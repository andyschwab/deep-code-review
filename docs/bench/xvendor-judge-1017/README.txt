Cross-vendor re-judge of the rival benches, 2026-10-17. Cited by docs/perun-vs-rivals.md H1g (and the cross-vendor values in H1 to H1f, H7, H7b).

Question: do the same-vendor judge results survive a judge from a different vendor? Judge: Qwen3.5-397B-A17B (OpenAI-compatible endpoint, temperature 0). Tiebreak: GLM-5.2 (non-thinking mode; thinking mode took 200+ s per call and returned empty content at the token cap), judged only where Qwen and the original judge disagreed. Spend: $0 (no claude -p call).

- xvendor.py: driver. `smoke|judge|review|tiebreak|report`. `judge` re-judges every original (first, second) order of the pairwise benches with the arm labels hidden and the call order shuffled with a fixed seed; the original judge prompt or protocol is used (docs/bench/*/bench.py, pos-1016/pos.py). `review` re-runs the per-finding verifier (scripts/bench_corpus.py VERIFY) on the H7 and H7b findings with the patch inlined. `tiebreak` asks GLM-5.2 about the items where Qwen and the original differ; the tiebreak result is the median of original, Qwen and GLM-5.2 where all three exist; a judgment with no original verdict (5 of 10 scenarios of H1e new vs rival) keeps Qwen's score alone. `report` recomputes results.json offline from the cached verdicts. A rerun resumes from the cache.
- verdicts-qwen.jsonl, verdicts-glm52.jsonl: raw verdicts, append-only, one JSON object per judgment (key, verdict, reason).
- results.json: original, Qwen and tiebreak win rates (95% CI, bootstrap 10000 resamples, seed 1, PASS only if the CI lower bound is above 0.5), per-judge first-slot counts ("position"), and the review precision, recall and F1 differences.
- scripts/test_xvendor_judge.py (offline): pins that the originals recompute exactly from the committed judge files, plus the scoring helpers.

Not re-judged: rows without committed raw outputs (the 2026-10-15 rubric runs, positioning, contribution, business-ops, H2 to H6).

Caveats: post-hoc, not preregistered. Qwen prefers the first-shown response (about 70% of the rubric-free judgments) and GLM-5.2 does too (75 of 102 tiebreak verdicts, 74%), so their order splits carry less signal than the originals and the tiebreak does not remove the bias (counts in results.json under position and position_total). Neither judge was calibrated against human labels. Generators and scenarios are unchanged (same-vendor), so only judge-family bias is removed. Not reproducible bit for bit: re-running the calls depends on the hosted models; `report` is deterministic. Needs PCSS_KEY (env or a .env above this folder) for judge, review and tiebreak; the key is never printed or committed.
