**Executive Summary: Prism App Usability Test Round 4**

**Recommendation:** Prioritize fixing the Export, Recurring Schedule, and Permissions flows for the November sprint. These three issues caused 64% of all task failures and represent critical blockers for core workflows.

**Key Findings**
Testing with 14 participants (Oct 7–18) revealed a sharp drop-off in success rates as task complexity increased. While project creation is seamless (93% success), downstream tasks are failing:

*   **Manage Permissions (36% success):** Users cannot distinguish between the four permission levels. The UI lacks definitions, causing 9 failures.
*   **Export Report (43% success):** Eight users failed; five could not locate the feature because it is hidden in the Analytics view rather than the Project view.
*   **Recurring Schedule (57% success):** Six users failed to find recurring options, which are buried inside an "Advanced" dropdown.
*   **Invite Team Members (71% success):** Users expect the invite button in the Team panel, but it is currently located in Settings.

**Severity Overview**
*   **Critical:** 3 issues (blocks completion)
*   **High:** 7 issues (significant frustration)
*   **Medium/Low:** 20 issues

**Sprint Capacity & Constraints**
The team has capacity for 5–6 medium-large items. The top three critical issues should consume the majority of this capacity.

**Open Questions for Decision**
1.  **Permissions:** We do not yet know if the four-role model is fundamentally flawed or if better labeling will suffice. The product team has debated simplifying to three roles, but this has not been tested.
2.  **Export Location:** Moving the export button to the Project view may conflict with the Analytics redesign already scheduled for Q1.

**Next Steps**
Approve the top three fixes for immediate development. Simultaneously, authorize a rapid labeling test for permissions and a technical feasibility check on the export button relocation to ensure no conflicts with Q1 plans.

*Note: Two existing users participated, which may have slightly inflated the success rate for Task 1. Screen-sharing lag occurred in three sessions but likely impacted time-on-task rather than success rates.*
