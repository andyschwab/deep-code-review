**Subject:** Remediation Strategy for Open Source License Audit (Jan–Sep 2024)

We scanned 4,218 components across GitHub and Bitbucket. While 74% carry permissive licenses (MIT, Apache, BSD), 198 components require immediate action to mitigate legal risk.

**Remediation Scope & Cost**
*   **High Risk (GPL/LGPL in production):** 6 components create copyleft obligations. Replacing or rewriting them costs $45K–$70K and takes 4–8 weeks. (12 additional GPL components are confined to test environments and pose lower risk.)
*   **Unknown Terms (No License File):** 124 components in transitive chains lack license files. Contacting maintainers or replacing them costs $30K–$55K and takes 6–12 weeks.
*   **Contract Conflicts:** 9 components conflict with customer contract terms. Resolution costs $20K–$35K and takes 3–6 weeks.

**Total Estimated Effort:** $95K–$160K over 12–20 weeks if executed in parallel.

**Critical Blocker: "streamflow-utils"**
One production component (GPL v3) has no identifiable owner. The repository is abandoned (last updated March 2022), the maintainer's profile is deleted, and no contact info exists. Because it is deeply embedded in our data pipeline, replacement alone costs ~$25K and takes 6+ weeks. We cannot currently determine copyright ownership or secure permission.

**Decisions Required**
1.  **Remediation Approach:** Do we authorize full parallel remediation ($160K/20 weeks), phase by risk category (starting with the 6 production GPL items), or accept risk for lower categories?
2.  **Ownership Strategy:** How should we proceed with "streamflow-utils"? Options include immediate replacement, engaging external counsel to attempt title clearance, or documenting an acceptance of risk given the inability to contact the owner.

Please advise on the preferred strategy so engineering can begin scoping.

---
*Audit covers Jan–Sep 2024; 47 internal-only tools without licenses were excluded from remediation costs.*
