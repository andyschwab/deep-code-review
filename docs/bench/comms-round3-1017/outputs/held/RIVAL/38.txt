**To:** Maria Santos, COO  
**From:** Fleet Strategy Team  
**Date:** October 24, 2024  
**Subject:** Recommendation on Fleet Electrification Phase 1

We recommend proceeding with the electrification of the 156 delivery vans, provided we immediately address route constraints for 23 vehicles.

The financial case is strong. The total upfront investment is $4.05M ($2.82M net vehicle cost after $1.24M in incentives, plus $1.23M for charging infrastructure). With projected annual savings of $1.52M in fuel and maintenance, the payback period is approximately 2.7 years. Given the December 1 deadline to secure 2025 delivery, delaying risks missing this window and facing uncertain battery pricing later.

However, three operational risks require mitigation before approval:
1.  **Range Limits:** 23 vans currently exceed the 150-mile electric range twice weekly. We must restructure these routes or budget for mid-day fast charging, though the impact on delivery times is currently unquantified.
2.  **Winter Performance:** Cold weather could reduce range by 20–30%, potentially affecting all vans from November through March. Contingency plans for peak winter days are necessary.
3.  **Asset Disposal:** The resale market for our diesel vans is soft. We may recover only $8K–$12K per unit versus the $18K book value, creating an unplanned write-down.

Infrastructure lead times (16–20 weeks) and unfixed utility rates add uncertainty. We should initiate utility negotiations immediately to lock in favorable time-of-use pricing.

**Decision Required:** Approve Phase 1 electrification and authorize a two-week sprint to finalize route adjustments for the 23 long-haul vans and secure utility terms.

*Note: This summary relies on current estimates; final savings depend on securing projected fuel/maintenance reductions.*
