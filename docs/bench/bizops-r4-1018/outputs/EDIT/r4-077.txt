**The Call**
You must recognize the $200,000 insurance recovery as taxable income in the year received; you cannot treat the loss and recovery as separate events to retain a net tax benefit. Under IRS rules, if you deducted the $150,000 operational loss in a prior year (or the same year), the recovery is taxable to the extent it provided a prior tax benefit, effectively neutralizing the deduction.

**The Numbers**
The governing principle is the **Tax Benefit Rule** (IRC § 111). The formula for the taxable portion of the recovery is:
$$ \text{Taxable Recovery} = \min(\text{Insurance Proceeds}, \text{Prior Deducted Loss that Reduced Tax}) $$

*   **Insurance Proceeds:** $200,000 (Given)
*   **Operational Loss Claimed:** $150,000 (Given)
*   **Excess Proceeds (Potential Gain):** $50,000 ($200k - $150k)

**Scenario A: Loss deducted in a prior year**
*   *Assumption:* The $150k loss was fully deducted last year and reduced your tax liability.
*   *Result:* The entire $150,000 recovery is **taxable income** this year. The remaining $50,000 is also taxable as it exceeds the loss basis (likely treated as ordinary income or gain depending on policy terms).
*   *Net Tax Impact:* You report $200,000 additional income.

**Scenario B: Loss and recovery in the same tax year**
*   *Assumption:* You have not yet filed the return for the year the loss occurred.
*   *Result:* You net the events on the current return.
*   *Calculation:* $200,000 (Income) - $150,000 (Expense) = **$50,000 Net Taxable Income**.
*   *Outcome:* You do not claim the $150k loss as a separate deduction; you only report the net $50k gain.

**What Flips It, and the Trigger**
The single input that changes the reporting method is the **filing status of the year in which the loss occurred**.
*   *Trigger:* If the return for the loss year is **already filed and accepted**, you must file an amended return (if within the statute of limitations) or recognize the full recovery as income in the current year under the Tax Benefit Rule.
*   *Rule of Thumb:* If the deduction lowered your tax bill in any prior year, the recovery is income now.
*   *Action Step:* If the loss year return is unfiled, net the $150k expense against the $200k income on the current draft; if filed, prepare to report $200k as "Other Income" on Form 1120 (or applicable entity form).

**Routing Note**
While the general rule (Tax Benefit Rule) is clear, the classification of the $50,000 excess proceeds and any potential Alternative Minimum Tax (AMT) adjustments require a licensed CPA. Specifically, ask your CPA: "Does the excess insurance recovery trigger any AMT preference items for our C-Corp/S-Corp status, and how do we characterize the $50k excess on Form 1120 Schedule M-1?" This is a binding tax determination that carries audit risk if misclassified.
