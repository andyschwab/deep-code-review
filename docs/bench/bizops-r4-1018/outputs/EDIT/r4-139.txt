**The Call**
Do not market the $100 premium badge as an "investment" or guarantee specific financial returns; reframe it immediately as an access fee for a service (network introduction) with no guaranteed outcome. While selling a service is not inherently a securities offering, promising "guaranteed interview opportunities" coupled with an expectation of profit from the efforts of others risks triggering the *Howey* test, potentially classifying the badge as an unregistered security.

**The Numbers**
*   **Current Model Revenue:**
    *   Formula: `(Base Price × Students) + (Premium Price × Conversion Rate × Students)`
    *   Assumption: 20% conversion to premium (industry standard for optional add-ons).
    *   Calculation: `($500 × 2,000) + ($100 × 0.20 × 2,000)` = `$1,000,000 + $40,000` = **$1,040,000**.
*   **Risk-Adjusted Scenario (If deemed a security offering):**
    *   Assumption: Legal remediation, potential fines, and refund liabilities consume 15% of gross revenue (conservative estimate for unregistered offering corrections).
    *   Calculation: `$1,040,000 × 0.15` = **$156,000** potential exposure.
    *   **Net Impact:** A 15% margin erosion on a model that currently looks low-risk.

**What Flips It, and the Trigger**
The single input that changes the call is the **wording of the promise**. If your marketing states that buyers will earn a return *primarily through the efforts of the Guild* (e.g., "We guarantee you a job"), it leans toward an investment contract. If the buyer must perform the work and the Guild merely provides a list of contacts, it remains a service.
*   **Trigger Rule:** If any marketing material uses the words "guarantee," "return," "income potential," or "passive," stop immediately.
*   **Step this week:** If your current copy promises "guaranteed interviews," consider replacing it with "priority access to our partner hiring network" and explicitly disclaiming employment guarantees in the terms of service.
