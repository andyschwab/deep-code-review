**The Call:** Your product remains a cosmetic provided your marketing claims strictly limit "relaxation" to a subjective feeling of calm and do not imply any physiological change or treatment of a medical condition; however, any claim suggesting the product treats insomnia, anxiety, or alters body function pushes it into drug territory, requiring immediate legal review before packaging finalization.

**The Regulatory Boundary & Criteria:**
Under the U.S. Federal Food, Drug, and Cosmetic Act (FD&C Act), the distinction between a cosmetic and a drug is defined by **intended use**, which regulators infer from your labeling, advertising, and consumer perception.
*   **Cosmetic:** Articles intended to be rubbed, poured, or sprinkled on the human body for cleansing, beautifying, promoting attractiveness, or altering appearance. Your vibrant coloring agents altering bathwater shade fit squarely here as "altering appearance."
*   **Drug:** Articles intended for use in the diagnosis, cure, mitigation, treatment, or prevention of disease, or to affect the structure or any function of the body.

**Impact of "Therapeutic Relaxation" Claims:**
Mentioning "relaxation" is a high-risk trigger.
*   **Safe Zone:** Claims like "helps you unwind," "creates a spa-like atmosphere," or "promotes a sense of calm" are generally accepted as cosmetic because they describe a subjective emotional state or ambiance.
*   **Danger Zone:** Claims like "therapeutic relaxation," "treats stress," "cures insomnia," "relieves muscle tension," or "reduces anxiety" imply a physiological effect or disease treatment. The word "therapeutic" is particularly dangerous as it explicitly suggests medical benefit. If your copy suggests the colorants themselves penetrate the skin to deliver a health benefit, you cross the line.

**Illustrative Cost Impact (Assumption-Based):**
*   **Cosmetic Path:** Compliance costs are limited to ingredient safety substantiation and proper labeling (GMPs). Estimated annual compliance cost: **$2,000–$5,000** (internal documentation + occasional consultant review).
*   **Drug Path:** If classified as an Over-the-Counter (OTC) drug, you face New Drug Application (NDA) requirements or strict adherence to an OTC Monograph.
    *   *Assumption:* Developing a new monograph or NDA requires clinical trials.
    *   *Result:* Costs typically exceed **$1M+** and take years, far surpassing your $40,000 revenue projection and rendering the business model unviable. Even fitting an existing monograph requires rigorous testing and facility registration that dwarfs your current scale.

**What Flips It & The Trigger:**
The single input that changes your classification is the **verb-adjective pairing** in your marketing copy.
*   *Trigger Value:* The presence of the word "therapeutic," "treat," "cure," "prevent," or "relieve" paired with a condition (stress, pain, sleeplessness).
*   *Stop/Pivot Rule:* If your draft packaging contains any claim implying a change in body function beyond temporary aesthetic enjoyment or subjective mood enhancement, **stop printing** and pivot to legal counsel.
*   *Actionable Step:* This week, audit your box copy and website. If "therapeutic" appears, replace it with "sensory" or "atmospheric" and remove all references to specific medical conditions.
