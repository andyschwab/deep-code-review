**The Call**
Treat the $3 jar deposits as a current liability on your balance sheet, not revenue, until the jar is returned or the obligation to return the funds is legally extinguished. Do not recognize unreturned deposits as revenue immediately based solely on an 85% return rate assumption; doing so risks misstating your financials and could trigger scrutiny during loan underwriting. You need a CPA experienced in retail liabilities to define your specific "breakage" recognition policy under GAAP and verify Washington state unclaimed property rules.

**The Numbers**
*Formula:* Total Deposit Liability = (Active Jars × Deposit Amount).
*Current State:* 1,500 jars × $3 = **$4,500 Liability**.
*Target State:* 5,000 jars × $3 = **$15,000 Liability**.

*Breakage Revenue Recognition (Illustrative Only):*
If you were permitted to recognize "breakage" (unreturned deposits) as revenue, the calculation would rely on your assumed return rate.
*Assumption:* 85% return rate (15% breakage).
*Scenario A (Conservative - No recognition until legal expiry):* Revenue recognized = $0. Liability remains $15,000.
*Scenario B (Aggressive - Recognizing estimated breakage immediately):* 5,000 jars × $3 × 15% = **$2,250 Potential Revenue**.
*Impact on Loan Metrics:* Under Scenario A, your cash asset ($60,000) is offset by a $15,000 liability, netting $45,000 equity impact. Under Scenario B, you show higher income but retain the cash; however, lenders typically add back non-cash liabilities only if the recognition policy is audited and GAAP-compliant. Premature recognition may be viewed as aggressive accounting, negatively affecting loan approval.

**What Flips It, and the Trigger**
The single input that changes the accounting treatment is the **legal threshold for "abandonment"** defined by Washington State unclaimed property laws.
*Trigger:* If a jar remains unreturned for the statutory period (commonly 3–5 years for consumer deposits, but varies by jurisdiction and contract terms), the liability converts to a payable to the state or recognizable revenue.
*Rule of Thumb:* Never recognize breakage revenue before the legal right to retain the funds is established.
*Action Step:* If your current policy assumes immediate breakage revenue, consider halting that practice this week and reclassifying those amounts as liabilities until a CPA confirms the allowable recognition timeline.
