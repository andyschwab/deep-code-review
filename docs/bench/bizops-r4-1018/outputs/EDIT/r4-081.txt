**The Call:** Do not attempt to "structure" the round to hide the concentration; instead, price the risk explicitly by offering a valuation discount or enhanced liquidation preferences, and make the Series A thesis strictly about using the $5M to diversify revenue away from the Department of Infrastructure within 18 months.

**The Numbers:**
Your current financial position creates a binary outcome for investors:
*   **Current Runway:** $0 cash (assumed pre-raise) / $45,000 monthly burn = **0 months runway**. You must close this round immediately to survive.
*   **Post-Money Burn Multiple:** If you raise $5M at a typical Series A pre-money of $15M (post $20M), your burn multiple is roughly 9x ($5M / $45k/mo / 12), giving you ~9 years of runway *if* revenue stays flat. However, the risk is not burn; it is revenue collapse.
*   **Concentration Metric:** 85% Revenue from 1 Client.
    *   *Base Case:* Contract renews, you diversify to 50% gov/50% private in 24 months.
    *   *Failure Case:* Contract lost, revenue drops 85%.
*   **Valuation Impact (Illustrative):** Investors will likely apply a "concentration discount." If a diversified SaaS comp trades at 10x ARR, a single-client dependent entity might only command 4–6x ARR until diversification is proven.

**What Flips It, and the Trigger:**
The deal flips from "uninvestable" to "high-risk opportunity" only if the use of funds is legally restricted or contractually committed to specific business development hires and product features required for private sector entry.
*   **Trigger:** If you cannot show a signed letter of intent (LOI) or a concrete pilot with a non-government entity within 6 months of closing, the concentration risk remains fatal.
*   **Rule of Thumb:** Investors accept concentration only when the capital is explicitly earmarked to fix it.
*   **Step this week:** Draft a "Use of Funds" slide that allocates at least 40% of the $5M specifically to sales/marketing for private sector acquisition, not general R&D or headcount.

**Routing Note (Tier 3 – Securities & Compliance):**
Selling equity or SAFEs is a securities offering; you cannot self-structure the disclosures. You must engage securities counsel immediately to draft the Risk Factors section of your Private Placement Memorandum (PPM) or Term Sheet. Specifically, counsel must articulate the "Customer Concentration Risk" and any potential conflicts regarding your federal contracting status (e.g., organizational conflicts of interest if selling to private competitors of your government client). Do not finalize term sheets without this review.

**Prioritized Terms/Disclosures:**
1.  **Explicit Risk Factor:** Lead with the 85% concentration in your deck. Hiding it destroys trust; owning it shows maturity.
