**The Call:** Proceed with dynamic pricing for your B2B API gateway, but only if you implement explicit, pre-contractual transparency regarding the algorithm's logic and caps; do not offer a fixed-price alternative as a legal requirement, though it remains a sound commercial strategy for risk-averse clients.

**The Numbers:**
*   **Current Revenue:** €600,000.
*   **Projected Uplift:** 20% during peak seasons.
*   **Formula:** `New Revenue = Base Revenue + (Peak Season Revenue × 0.20)`.
*   **Assumption (ASSUMED):** Peak seasons account for 30% of annual traffic volume.
    *   *Base Case:* €600k + (€180k × 0.20) = **€636,000** total annual revenue.
    *   *High Case (50% peak traffic):* €600k + (€300k × 0.20) = **€660,000**.
    *   *Low Case (10% peak traffic):* €600k + (€60k × 0.20) = **€612,000**.
*   **Result:** The strategy adds between €12k and €60k annually depending on traffic concentration.

**What Flips It, and the Trigger:**
The single input that changes the verdict from "compliant" to "high-risk" is **opacity in the pricing formula**. If a client cannot calculate their maximum potential cost before signing, the practice risks violating the Unfair Commercial Practices Directive (UCPD) and the Digital Services Act (DSA) principles on transparency.
*   **Trigger Value:** Any pricing tier where the multiplier exceeds **3x** the base rate without a hard cap disclosed upfront.
*   **Stop/Pivot Rule:** If customer churn exceeds **5%** within the first quarter of rollout due to "bill shock," immediately pivot to a "capped dynamic" model.
*   **This Week's Step:** If your current terms of service lack a specific "Variable Pricing Mechanism" clause, consider drafting an addendum that explicitly defines the traffic thresholds and multipliers before notifying existing clients.

**Routing Note:**
While B2B contracts generally allow freedom of pricing, the **EU Digital Services Act (DSA)** and **Unfair Contract Terms Directive** impose strict transparency duties if your service is deemed critical infrastructure or if clients are small businesses (SMEs) rather than large enterprises. You must route the final wording of your "dynamic pricing" disclosure clause to an EU-qualified technology counsel to verify it meets the "plain and intelligible language" standard required for SME protection.
