**The Call**
Reclassify the $300,000 in unused credits immediately as deferred revenue (a liability); recognizing it as earned revenue now is incorrect under standard accounting principles and creates a material risk of restatement that will damage credibility during fundraising.

**The Numbers**
*Formula:* Deferred Revenue Liability = Total Cash Received for Unused Credits − Revenue Recognized as Credits Are Redeemed.
*Current State (Incorrect):* $300,000 recognized as Revenue.
*Corrected State (Base Case):* $300,000 sits on the Balance Sheet as a Liability; $0 hits the P&L until redemption.
*Impact on Reported Revenue:* If you reclassify, your current month's reported revenue drops by the portion of that $300k you previously booked. Your cash flow from operations remains unchanged (cash is already in the bank), but your *reported* net income decreases, and your liability section increases by $300,000.
*ASSUMED:* You have not yet recognized any portion of this $300k as the service is delivered. If you have, the liability is $300k minus the redeemed amount.

**What Flips It, and the Trigger**
The single input that dictates the complexity is the **redemption rate**. If customers redeem credits slowly, the liability stays high, deferring revenue recognition. If they redeem quickly, revenue is recognized faster.
*Trigger:* If your historical redemption data shows >20% of credits expire unused, you may eventually recognize that portion as "breakage revenue," but only after the likelihood of redemption becomes remote.
*Rule of Thumb:* Treat 100% of prepaid credits as a liability until the service is performed.
*This Week's Step:* If your auditors have flagged this, consider halting any new bulk credit sales promotions until the ledger is corrected to avoid compounding the error before your next funding round.

**Routing Note**
This is a Tier 3 accounting determination involving IFRS 15 (applicable in Berlin/EU) or ASC 606 (if US investors require it). You must engage your CPA or external auditor to formally calculate the "breakage" model and execute the journal entries to restate prior periods if necessary. Do not attempt to self-certify the breakage percentage.

Offering prepaid units does not inherently complicate financial planning; it is a standard SaaS/infrastructure model (similar to AWS credits or Twilio prepay). The complication arises solely from misclassifying the cash. Investors prefer seeing the $300k as a liability because it represents guaranteed future work and sticky customer commitment; hiding it as current revenue looks like inflation of top-line growth and signals weak internal controls. Continuing your current method risks a "qualified" audit opinion, which can terminate term sheets instantly.
