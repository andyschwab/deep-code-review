**The Call**
Choose the **18% variable fee** to protect your $55,000 cash reserves. While the fixed rent offers slightly better margins in peak months, the variable model eliminates the risk of negative cash flow during slow seasons, which is critical given your limited runway. The fixed rent of $4,500 creates a dangerous deficit in slow months that your current reserves cannot sustain for long.

**The Numbers**
We compare the total monthly cost of each option against your contribution margin (Sales − COGS).
*   **COGS:** 25% of Sales.
*   **Contribution Margin (before rent/labor):** 75% of Sales.
*   **Fixed Costs (Labor):** $6,000/month.

*Break-Even Analysis (where Variable Fee = Fixed Rent):*
$0.18 \times \text{Sales} = \$4,500$
$\text{Sales} = \$25,000$

*Monthly Cash Flow Projection (Net of COGS, Labor, and Rent):*

| Scenario | Sales | Fixed Rent Option Net | Variable Fee Option Net |
| :--- | :--- | :--- | :--- |
| **Slow Month** | $14,000 | **-$1,500** (Loss) | **+$1,080** (Profit) |
| **Average Month** | $22,000 | +$4,500 | +$3,960 |
| **Peak Month** | $35,000 | +$14,250 | +$13,650 |

*Assumptions:* COGS remains constant at 25%; Labor is strictly fixed at $6,000 regardless of sales volume.

**What Flips It, and the Trigger**
The deciding input is **monthly sales volume**. The variable fee becomes more expensive than the fixed rent only when sales exceed **$25,000/month**.
*   **Trigger Rule:** If you are confident the new Dallas location will consistently exceed $25,000 in monthly sales from Month 1, the fixed rent saves you roughly $540–$600 per month in average/peak scenarios.
*   **Risk Reality:** However, your historical data shows slow months at $14,000. Under the fixed rent model, a single slow month generates a $1,500 loss. With only $55,000 in total reserves across three locations, absorbing recurring losses at a fourth location could deplete your runway in under 12 months if travel dips. The variable fee guarantees positive cash flow even in your worst historical month.
*   **Action Step:** If the airport authority insists on fixed rent, consider negotiating a "hybrid" cap (e.g., 18% up to $4,500, then fixed) or request a 3-month ramp-up period with variable fees to validate the new location's baseline without immediate fixed overhead.
