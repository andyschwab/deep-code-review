**The Call**
Proceed with the expansion only if you secure a working capital line of credit or external funding to cover the $43,200 incremental tax accrual gap; do not rely solely on your current $120,000 cash reserve, as federal bond requirements will likely force you to post additional collateral that ties up liquidity exactly when you need it for operations.

**The Numbers**
*Formula:* Monthly Excise Accrual = (Cases × Bottles/Case) × Tax Rate.
*Assumptions:* Standard craft distiller tax rate (first 100,000 proof gallons) is approx. $2.70 per proof gallon. Assuming 750ml bottles at 80 proof (0.4 proof gallons/bottle), tax is ~$1.08/bottle.
*Current State (500 cases):* 500 cases × 12 bottles × $1.08 = **$6,480/month** accrued tax liability.
*Expanded State (1,200 cases):* 1,200 cases × 12 bottles × $1.08 = **$15,552/month** accrued tax liability.
*Incremental Gap:* $15,552 - $6,480 = **$9,072/month** additional cash tied up in tax accrual before sale.
*Annualized Impact:* $9,072 × 6 months (average aging/bottling cycle) = **$54,432** in trapped working capital.
*Bond Cost Estimate:* If TTB requires a bond increase to cover the higher liability (e.g., raising coverage by $60k), the 2% premium costs **$1,200/year**.

**What Flips It, and the Trigger**
The deciding factor is your **cash conversion cycle length**. If your inventory sits for more than 4.5 months before sale, your $120,000 reserve will be insufficient to cover operating expenses plus the growing tax lien.
*Trigger:* If your average inventory age exceeds 150 days, pause expansion until a credit facility is secured.
*Rule of Thumb:* Maintain a cash buffer equal to 3 months of *expanded* tax accruals ($46,656) separate from operating funds.
*Next Step:* If your current inventory turnover is slower than 4 months, consider leasing the still instead of buying to preserve cash for the tax bond.
