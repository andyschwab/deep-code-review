**The Call**
Treat the $45,000 audit fee and the $20,000 internal CTO effort as immediate operating expenses for this year's tax filing unless your CPA identifies specific "implementation" activities that created a new, distinct asset; do not capitalize these costs without a formal review, as the risk of an IRS disallowance outweighs the benefit of deferring the deduction.

**The Numbers**
*Formula:* Tax Impact = (Total Cost × Marginal Tax Rate).
*Inputs:* Audit Fee ($45,000) + Internal Labor ($20,000) = **$65,000 Total Cost**.
*Assumption (ASSUMED):* Your effective combined federal and state marginal tax rate is **25%** (typical for small C-corps; verify your actual bracket).

**What Flips It, and the Trigger**
The single input that changes the call is the **nature of the work performed**. Under US GAAP (ASC 350/985) and IRS guidelines, costs incurred during the *preliminary project stage* (planning, evaluating vendors) and the *post-implementation stage* (training, maintenance, annual auditing) must be expensed. Only direct costs incurred during the *application development stage* (coding, configuring, testing specific new modules) may be capitalized.
*   **Trigger:** If your CTO's time was spent strictly on *preparing for the audit* (gathering evidence, fixing controls for the current period) rather than *building new software functionality*, the cost is an expense. Given this is an annual certification for an existing product ("CloudGuard"), 90%+ of these costs are likely compliance/maintenance.
*   **Rule of Thumb:** If the work maintains the current service level rather than adding significant new functionality, expense it.
*   **Step this week:** If your CTO's logs show >50% of time was spent on *new feature development* triggered by the audit requirements, consider capitalizing that specific portion; otherwise, expense the full $65,000.
