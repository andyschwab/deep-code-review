**The Call:** Use FIFO (First-In, First-Out) for your financial statements to align with GAAP and standard VC expectations for retail; do not use LIFO or mark-to-market, as they will either distort your margins or introduce unacceptable volatility that scares investors. Present your inventory at the lower of cost or market (LCM) with a clear, conservative write-down policy for slow-moving items, rather than highlighting unrealized gains on trending streetwear.

**The Numbers:**
Your current book value is $350,000. Under **FIFO**, you sell oldest stock first, keeping recent (potentially higher-cost or volatile) items in inventory.
*   *Formula:* Ending Inventory = Cost of Most Recent Purchases.
*   *Impact:* If 90s streetwear costs rose, FIFO shows higher ending inventory value and higher gross profit.
*   *Result:* Your $350k likely understates current replacement cost if trends are up, but it remains stable and auditable.

Under **LIFO** (Last-In, First-Out):
*   *Formula:* Ending Inventory = Cost of Oldest Purchases.
*   *Impact:* Matches recent high costs against revenue, lowering taxable income but also lowering reported inventory value on the balance sheet.
*   *Result:* For a growth-stage startup seeking VC, LIFO is rarely used (and prohibited under IFRS), making your metrics look artificially depressed and complicating comparisons with peers.

Under **Mark-to-Market** (Fair Value):
*   *Formula:* Ending Inventory = Current Estimated Market Price.
*   *Impact:* Recognizes unrealized gains/losses immediately.
*   *Result:* **ASSUMED** 20% volatility in your mix. A 40% drop in a segment could wipe $50k+ off your balance sheet in a month.
*   *Verdict:* Reject. GAAP generally prohibits writing inventory *up* above cost. Writing it down is required, but writing it up creates the "instability" you fear.

**What Flips It, and The Trigger:**
The trigger is a sustained market decline exceeding 15% across a major category for two consecutive quarters.
*   *Rule of Thumb:* If specific SKU groups (e.g., 90s tees) trade below your acquisition cost, you must write them down to Net Realizable Value (NRV) immediately under GAAP, regardless of the method.
*   *Action:* If your Q3 sell-through rate for streetwear drops below 20%, consider a targeted write-down of that specific cohort to show investors you are prudent, not optimistic.

**Documentation for Due Diligence:**
Auditors and VCs will not accept a spreadsheet total. They require:
1.  **Third-party appraisal or sold-comps log:** For high-value unique items (> $1,000 cost), maintain a file of recent eBay/StockX sold listings supporting your LCM assessment.
2.  **Aging report:** Breakdown of inventory by acquisition date. Anything >12 months old should be flagged for potential write-down.
3.  **Physical count sheets:** Signed records of the last physical inventory count reconciled to the general ledger.

**Routing Note:**
While FIFO is the standard recommendation for retail startups, the specific application of "Lower of Cost or Market" tests and the tax implications of any write-downs are binding accounting determinations. Engage a CPA specializing in e-commerce/retail to finalize your write-down policy and review your capitalization rules before sharing data rooms with VCs.
