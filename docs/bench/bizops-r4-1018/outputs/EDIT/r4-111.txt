**The Call**
Reject the price cut to $29; it mathematically destroys your unit economics and accelerates cash burn without solving the retention problem. At $29, your Lifetime Value (LTV) collapses below your current Customer Acquisition Cost (CAC), guaranteeing a loss on every new user unless churn improves drastically or CAC drops simultaneously.

**The Numbers**
*Current State:*
Formula: $LTV = \frac{Price \times Gross Margin}{Churn Rate}$ (Simplified for monthly subscription).
Assuming 100% gross margin on digital coaching for this model:
$LTV = \$79 / 0.12 = \$658$ (Theoretical max if churn is constant monthly).
However, you stated LTV is **$420**. This implies your effective churn or cost structure differs, or the 12% is specific to month 3+.
Current Ratio: $420 / 185 = 2.27x$. While investors call this "unsustainable" for high-growth SaaS, it is positive contribution margin.

*Proposed Scenario ($29 Price):*
To maintain current revenue, volume must increase by **172%** ($79/$29).
New LTV Projection (assuming churn stays at 12%):
$LTV_{new} = \$29 / 0.12 = \$241$.
New Ratio: $241 / 185 = 1.30x$.
*Result:* You are now earning only $56 profit per user before overhead, down from $235. If your marketing funnel cannot instantly triple volume at the same $185 CAC, your total revenue drops immediately.

*Break-Even Churn Requirement:*
To keep LTV at $420 with a $29 price:
Required Churn = $29 / 420 = 6.9%$.
You must reduce churn from 12% to under 7% *just to break even* on the current LTV metric, ignoring the volume ramp-up risk.

**What Flips It, and The Trigger**
The single input that changes this verdict is **CAC**. If dropping the price to $29 also lowers your CAC from $185 to below **$95** (due to easier conversion), the model might work.
*Trigger:* Run a low-budget A/B test on the $29 offer for 7 days. If CAC does not drop below $100 immediately, abort the pivot.
*Rule of Thumb:* Never lower price to fix retention; lower price only if it drastically lowers acquisition friction.
*This Week's Step:* If churn remains >10% at the $79 price point, consider adding value (e.g., "premium" add-ons) rather than cutting price, or test a $49 "starter" tier instead of a full drop to $29.
