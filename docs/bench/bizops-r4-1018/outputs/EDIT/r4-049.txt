**The Call:** Shift 60% of your budget ($4,800) to online acquisition immediately to capture the higher contribution margin, while allocating the remaining 40% ($3,200) to retail support strictly for securing new doors and in-store activations, not general brand awareness. Your current online unit economics ($43 contribution per bottle) vastly outperform the compressed margins of retail once marketing costs are layered on top.

**The Numbers:**
*   **Online Unit Economics:** Price (implied ~$22 CAC recovery + margin) minus CAC ($22) equals **$43 contribution** per bottle (derived from $65 LTV - $22 CAC).
*   **Retail Unit Economics:** A 40% retailer margin leaves you 60% of the wholesale price. If your online price is the ceiling, your wholesale revenue is significantly lower. Without your specific wholesale price, we assume a standard 50% gross margin on COGS before the retailer cut.
    *   *Formula:* (Wholesale Price × 0.60) - COGS - Allocated Marketing = Retail Contribution.
    *   *Assumption (Base):* If a bottle sells for $10 wholesale, you keep $6. If COGS is $3, your gross profit is $3. This is likely lower than the online $43 contribution unless volume is massive.
*   **Budget Split Scenario (60/40):**
    *   Online Spend: $4,800 / $22 CAC = ~218 new customers/month.
    *   Retail Spend: $3,200 used for trade spend (samples, slotting fees) to maintain 3 shops + add 1 new door.

**What Flips It, and The Trigger:**
The single input that changes this call is the **Retail Halo Lift Factor**. If you can prove that every $1 spent on retail presence generates more than $1.50 in *incremental* online sales (due to trust/visibility), you should shift funds back to retail.
*   **Trigger:** Track online sales velocity in zip codes where you just placed a new retail door. If online sales in that specific zip code do not increase by **15% within 45 days** of distribution, cut retail marketing spend for that region entirely.
*   **Action Step:** If your online CAC rises above $28 next month, consider pausing paid ads and reallocating that specific budget to influencer seeding which may lower blended CAC.

**Attribution & Testing Approach:**
To solve the "see in store, buy online" problem, do not attempt complex multi-touch modeling yet; it is too data-heavy for your current volume. Instead, use **Geo-Lift Testing**:
1.  **Control Group:** Pick 3 similar zip codes where you have *no* retail presence.
2.  **Test Group:** Pick 3 zip codes where you are actively marketing to get shelf space.
3.  **Measurement:** Compare online sales growth in Test vs. Control. The difference is your attributed retail lift.
4.  **Metrics to Track:**
    *   *Online:* CAC, LTV:CAC Ratio (target >3:1), Payback Period.
    *   *Retail:* Sell-through rate (units sold per store per week), Cost per New Door, Gross Margin Return on Inventory Investment (GMROII).
