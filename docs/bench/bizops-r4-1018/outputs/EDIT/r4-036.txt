**The Call:** Treat the off-season as a distinct period with separate unit economics; do not smooth the data. Your current cash reserves ($35k) cover less than one month of operations if churn spikes to 11% without immediate corrective action, making a "re-acquisition later" strategy financially dangerous. **The Numbers:**
*   **Baseline Monthly Revenue:** 2,500 subs × $45 = $112,500.
*   **Baseline Churn (4%):** 100 customers lost → Net loss of $4,500 MRR/month.
*   **Crisis Churn (11%):** 275 customers lost → Net loss of $12,375 MRR/month.
*   **Discount Scenario ($38 price, assumed churn reduction to 6%):**

    *   Churn: 150 customers lost → Net loss of $5,700 MRR/month.
    *   *Result:* Discounting saves $6,675 in MRR erosion compared to the crisis scenario but costs $17,500 in top-line revenue immediately.

**Runway Calculation (ASSUMED Operating Expenses):**
Since you did not provide monthly burn, we must derive the break-even constraint.
*   *Formula:* Runway (months) = Cash Reserves / (Operating Expenses - Net Revenue Change).
*   *Scenario A (No Discount, High Churn):* If your fixed costs exceed $100,125 ($112,500 revenue - $12,375 churn loss), you are burning cash.
*   *Scenario B (Discount, Lower Churn):* If your fixed costs exceed $89,300 ($95,000 revenue - $5,700 churn loss), you are burning cash.
*   *Critical Gap:* With only $35,000 cash, if your fixed costs are even $110,000/month, you have **less than 3 weeks** of runway during the off-season under Scenario A.

**What Flips It, and The Trigger:**
The deciding input is your **Customer Acquisition Cost (CAC)** versus the **Lifetime Value (LTV) lost** during the gap.
*   *Trigger Rule:* If CAC < $150, re-acquisition is cheaper than discounting. If CAC > $150, retention via discount is mandatory.
*   *Threshold:* Calculate the LTV of a retained customer: ($38 margin × 12 months) vs. ($45 margin × 8 months + $0 for 4 months).
*   *Actionable Step:* If your historical CAC is above $100, **consider** implementing the $38 "harvest pass" discount immediately for the upcoming off-season to stabilize cash flow, as re-acquiring 275 users will likely exceed your $35k cash reserve.
