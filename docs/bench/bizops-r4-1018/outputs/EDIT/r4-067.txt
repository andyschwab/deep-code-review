**The Call**
Adopt the hybrid model for cash flow, but treat the €5,000 fee as deferred revenue recognized over the contract term for accounting; do not book it immediately. For investor reporting, separate "one-time implementation" from "recurring subscription" to avoid distorting LTV and MRR growth metrics.

**The Numbers**
*Current Model:*
*   Monthly Recurring Revenue (MRR): €500 × 100 customers = **€50,000**.
*   Annual Contract Value (ACV): €500 × 12 = **€6,000**.
*   Lifetime Value (LTV) at 18 months: €500 × 18 = **€9,000**.

*Proposed Model (Hybrid):*
*   Upfront Fee: **€5,000** (One-time).
*   New MRR: €300 × 100 customers = **€30,000** (appears as a 40% drop in MRR if not contextualized).
*   Total First-Year Revenue per Customer: €5,000 + (€300 × 12) = **€8,600**.
*   LTV at 18 months (Cash Basis): €5,000 + (€300 × 18) = **€10,400**.
*   LTV at 18 months (Accrual/Reporting Basis): If the fee is recognized over 18 months, monthly recognized revenue = €300 + (€5,000 / 18) ≈ **€578**. Total LTV remains **€10,400**, but the recognition curve changes.

*Assumption:* Churn and customer count remain constant during the transition.

**What Flips It, and the Trigger**
The critical input is **churn rate**. If churn exceeds **33% within the first 6 months**, the upfront fee becomes a liability rather than an asset, as refunds or reputational damage may outweigh the cash benefit.
*Trigger:* If >15 customers cancel within 6 months of signing the new model, pause the implementation fee strategy.
*Rule of Thumb:* Implementation fees should cover <20% of the total first-year contract value to signal strong recurring value. Here, €5,000 is ~45% of the first-year cash collected (€5,000 + €3,600), which is high.
*Step this week:* Model a "blended MRR" metric for your board deck that adds 1/12th of the implementation fee to the subscription MRR to show true economic growth without violating accounting standards.

**Accounting and Valuation Implications**
*Accounting (Tier 2 Explanation):* Under IFRS 15 (applicable in Dublin/EU), revenue from implementation fees is generally recognized over the period the customer benefits from the service (the subscription term), not when cash is received. Booking it immediately would be non-compliant and require restatement. It sits on the balance sheet as "Deferred Revenue" and releases to the P&L monthly.
*Valuation/Investors:* Investors view large upfront fees as a double-edged sword. Positively, it extends your runway (€5,000 × 100 customers = €500,000 potential cash injection, doubling your current bank). Negatively, it can signal that the recurring product value is weak (hence the need to subsidize it with services). High services revenue multiples are lower than SaaS multiples. You must clearly distinguish "Professional Services Revenue" from "Subscription Revenue" in your data room.

**Routing Note**
The specific classification of your implementation deliverables (custom code vs. standard setup) determines the exact recognition schedule under IFRS 15; confirm the amortization period with your Irish qualified accountant before updating financial statements.
