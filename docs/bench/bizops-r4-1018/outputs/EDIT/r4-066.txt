**The Call:** Adopt a hybrid pricing model immediately: a base fee covering baseline usage plus a defined overage rate for traffic spikes. Do not absorb non-linear infrastructure costs into flat fees; this structure guarantees your 70% margin target at baseline while capping erosion during spikes.

**The Numbers:**
Your current baseline unit economics are strong, but spike scenarios destroy them.
*   **Formula:** Gross Margin % = (Revenue - Direct Cost) / Revenue.
*   **Baseline Scenario:** ($2,000 - $600) / $2,000 = **70% Margin**.
*   **Spike Scenario (Current Flat Fee):** ($2,000 - $1,500) / $2,000 = **25% Margin**.
*   **Risk Projection:** If you add 10 large customers who all hit peak spikes simultaneously under a flat-fee model, your blended margin could indeed collapse toward the 40% range you fear, depending on the mix of baseline vs. spiking accounts.

To fix this, we model a **Tiered + Overage** structure:
*   **Assumption (ASSUMED):** You set the base tier at $2,000 covering up to 1.5x baseline traffic (cost ~$900).
*   **Assumption (ASSUMED):** Overage is charged at $0.0X per GB or a fixed step-up fee that covers the marginal cost of the spike (the jump from $900 to $1,500).
*   **Result:** In a spike event, if the customer pays an additional $600 overage fee, revenue becomes $2,600. New Margin = ($2,600 - $1,500) / $2,600 = **42%**. This prevents the drop to 25% and protects the blended portfolio margin.

**What Flips It, and The Trigger:**
The single input that changes this recommendation is **Price Elasticity of Demand** (how many deals you lose by adding overage fees).
*   **Trigger Value:** If more than 20% of prospective enterprise deals walk away due to the removal of "unlimited" flat-rate ingestion, the model fails.
*   **Stop/Pivot Rule:** If you lose 2 out of the next 10 qualified opportunities specifically citing pricing complexity, pause the overage rollout and revert to a higher flat fee with a hard usage cap (throttling) instead of overages.
*   **Action This Week:** If your current churn is below 5%, consider drafting a "Fair Use Policy" addendum for new contracts that defines "baseline" vs. "spike" traffic, then test this language on your next 3 sales negotiations.

**Routing Note:**
While pricing strategy is operational, ensuring your new tiered terms do not inadvertently create ambiguous service level obligations (SLOs) regarding "spike handling" performance is a legal matter. Have Seattle counsel review the specific wording of the overage triggers to ensure they don't guarantee unlimited scalability, which could create breach-of-contract risk if your infrastructure throttles.
