**The Call**
Continue billing in local currencies to maximize conversion; do not delay invoicing or force USD billing due to accounting fears. Recognize revenue at the spot rate on the invoice date, and treat exchange differences as unrealized until you actually convert the cash or settle the receivable. Given your current volume ($23k/month), the complexity is manageable with standard software, but you should engage a CPA specializing in international tax immediately if foreign revenue exceeds 20% of total revenue or if you decide to hold foreign currency balances for more than 30 days.

**The Numbers**
Under US GAAP (ASC 830), foreign currency transactions are recorded at the spot rate on the transaction date.
*Formula:* `Reported Revenue = Foreign Invoice Amount × Spot Rate on Invoice Date`
*Formula:* `FX Gain/Loss = (Spot Rate at Settlement − Spot Rate at Invoice) × Foreign Amount`

*Worked Example (Illustrative):*
You invoice a Japanese customer ¥1,500,000 (approx. $10,000 at 150 JPY/USD) on Jan 1.
- **Jan 1 (Invoice):** Record $10,000 revenue. Accounts Receivable is $10,000.
- **Jan 31 (Month-end):** If rate moves to 145 JPY/USD, the receivable is now worth ~$10,345. You record an *unrealized* gain of $345 on the P&L.
- **Feb 15 (Payment/Conversion):** You convert the Yen to USD at 148 JPY/USD. You receive $10,135. You realize a total gain of $135 for the period (adjusting the previous unrealized gain).

*Assumptions:*
- **ASSUMED (Low):** You convert currency immediately upon receipt (minimizes balance sheet exposure, simplifies tracking).
- **ASSUMED (Base):** You hold receivables for 30 days before payment (standard net-30 terms).
- **ASSUMED (High):** You hold foreign cash balances for >90 days (increases exposure to rate swings and potential "functional currency" questions).

**What Flips It, and the Trigger**
The single input that changes the accounting burden is **holding foreign currency balances** rather than converting immediately.
*Trigger:* If your average foreign cash balance exceeds $50,000 or you hold funds for >60 days, the risk of material misstatement due to volatility increases, requiring more sophisticated hedging or treasury policies.
*Rule of Thumb:* If foreign revenue >20% of total revenue, the cost of a specialized international CPA is justified by the risk of errors in tax filings (Form 1118 for foreign tax credits, if applicable, or Subpart F considerations).
*Step:* If you are currently converting JPY/BRL to USD within 48 hours of receipt, consider setting up automatic conversion rules in your payment processor to eliminate manual tracking of unrealized gains/losses.

**Routing Note**
While the general rule (ASC 830) is standard, the determination of your "functional currency" and the specific tax treatment of realized vs. unrealized gains for a Delaware C-Corp with expanding global operations is a binding tax determination. Consult a CPA specializing in international taxation to confirm your specific reporting obligations under IRC Section 988 and to verify if your current volume triggers any state-level nexus complications in Japan or Brazil.
