**The Call:** Operate under the assumption that you are a Business Associate (BA) and do not sign new enterprise contracts with covered entities until a qualified healthcare attorney confirms your status; real-time processing without storage does not automatically exempt you from HIPAA if you are creating, receiving, maintaining, or transmitting Protected Health Information (PHI) on behalf of a covered entity.

**The Analysis:**
Under HIPAA, a "Business Associate" is defined not by how long you store data, but by whether you perform functions or activities on behalf of a covered entity (like your diabetes clinic clients) that involve the use or disclosure of PHI. Your clients are likely covered entities, and the food/exercise logs they transmit for specific patients constitute PHI. The fact that you process this in real-time and do not store it permanently is a risk-mitigation factor, but it is not a legal exemption. If your API is integrated into a workflow where a clinic sends patient data to you to get a result back for that patient's care, you are likely "maintaining" or "transmitting" PHI as a service provider.

**Specific Compliance Measures & Thresholds:**
If you are a BA, the following are mandatory, not optional:
1.  **Business Associate Agreement (BAA):** You must sign a BAA with every covered entity client before any data exchange occurs. Operating without one is a direct violation.
2.  **Encryption:** While HIPAA lists encryption as an "addressable" specification, for a cloud API transmitting PHI, it is effectively required. You must encrypt data in transit (TLS 1.2 or higher) and, if there is any temporary caching in memory or logs, data at rest. Failure to encrypt is a primary factor in penalty calculations during a breach.
3.  **Third-Party Audit:** HIPAA does not strictly mandate a third-party audit (like SOC 2 or HITRUST) by statute. However, enterprise health clients will almost certainly require a SOC 2 Type II report or HITRUST certification as a condition of signing a BAA. Without it, your sales cycle to legitimate clinics will stall.
4.  **Immediate Actions:** Cease marketing to covered entities as "HIPAA-ready" until you have a BAA template vetted by counsel. Implement strict logging policies to ensure no PHI is written to persistent application logs during your real-time processing.

**What Flips the Call:**
The determining factor is the **source and purpose** of the data. If your enterprise clients are sending data strictly for their own treatment/payment/operations purposes, you are a BA. If your clients are fitness apps where users self-report data without a provider involved, you are likely not a BA (though FTC Health Breach Notification Rule may apply).
*Trigger:* The moment you sign a contract with a clinic that specifies you are handling patient data for care coordination, you are a BA.
*Rule of Thumb:* If the data includes a patient identifier and comes from a provider, assume BA status.
