**The Call:** Do not pursue venture backing with this model; the math shows a $3.2M ceiling on a shrinking base, which fails the $10M target and the growth mandate of VC. Treat this as a profitable lifestyle business or a bootstrapped niche, not a venture-scale opportunity.

**The Numbers:**
Total Addressable Market (TAM) is calculated as: `Total Enterprises × Penetration Rate × Annual Spend`.
Using your figures:
*   Total Enterprises: 4,000
*   Penetration Rate (Fortune 500 users): 5% (0.05)
*   Annual Spend (Budget): $200,000

**Base Case Result:** 4,000 × 0.05 × $200,000 = **$40,000,000** (Total industry maintenance spend).

However, TAM is not revenue. To reach your **$10M Revenue Target**, you must capture a specific market share.
*   Required Market Share: $10M / $40M = **25%**.
*   Required Customer Count: $10M / $200,000 (assuming 100% budget capture, which is impossible) = **50 customers**.
*   Realistic Pricing Assumption (ASSUMED): You capture 20% of their maintenance budget ($40k/year).
*   Realistic Customer Count Needed: $10M / $40,000 = **250 customers**.

**The Constraint:** There are only 200 total potential customers globally (4,000 × 5%). Even if you captured 100% of the market at a $40k price point, revenue caps at **$8M**. To hit $10M, you must extract >$50k/year from every single remaining user on earth, assuming zero churn and zero competition.

**What Flips It, and The Trigger:**
The only variable that changes this verdict is **Price Expansion** (selling beyond monitoring into expensive consulting, migration, or compliance) or **Market Definition** (finding that the 5% figure is wrong and the actual user base is 25,000+).
*   **Trigger:** If primary research confirms the total install base is >5,000 enterprises (not 4,000) OR if you can prove an Average Revenue Per User (ARPU) of >$50,000.
*   **Decision Rule:** If the total addressable customer count is <500, reject the venture path.
*   **This Week's Step:** If the total customer count is confirmed under 500, consider pivoting to a "cash cow" strategy aiming for $2–3M profit with no external capital, rather than raising a round.

**Data Sources & Validation:**
