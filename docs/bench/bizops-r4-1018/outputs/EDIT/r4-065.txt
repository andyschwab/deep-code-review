**What Flips It, and the Trigger:**
The trigger is a **direct RFP (Request for Proposal) mandate**. Many large state research universities *require* SOC 2 for vendors holding sensitive student data, while community colleges often accept a partner's compliance umbrella.
*   **Rule of Thumb:** If the procurement office explicitly states "Vendor must possess independent SOC 2 Type II report" in the RFP, you cannot bypass it.
*   **Stop/Pivot Date:** If you cannot secure a signed Letter of Intent (LOI) via a partner channel within **45 days**, pause US expansion and focus on private institutions or Canadian markets until cash reserves allow for the audit.
*   **Action This Week:** If your target university has a public RFP, read the "Mandatory Qualifications" section; if SOC 2 is listed, contact a existing compliant partner immediately to propose a white-label or reseller agreement.

**Routing Note:**
US public university procurement is governed by state-specific statutes and the Federal Acquisition Regulation (FAR) only if federal funds are directly involved in the contract structure. Whether a specific contract triggers FedRAMP (federal cloud security) versus state-level data privacy laws (like FERPA handling) is a binding legal determination. Engage US counsel specializing in EdTech procurement to review the specific contract terms before signing, particularly regarding data residency and liability caps.

*Note: FedRAMP is generally for federal agencies, not state universities, but state equivalents exist. SOC 2 is an industry standard, not a government law, but often mandated by buyers.*
