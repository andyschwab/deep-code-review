**The Call**
Do not create separate SKUs or unilaterally adjust wholesale prices to cover the tax; instead, maintain your current SKU structure and shift the legal liability for remittance to your distributors via contract amendments, but you must engage a local tax professional before signing any distribution agreements in Seattle or Berkeley to confirm the "pass-through" legality.

**The Numbers**
Your current unit economics are:
*   **Price:** $2.50
*   **COGS:** $0.90
*   **Gross Margin:** $1.60 (64%)

If you absorb a hypothetical $0.18 tax (approx. 7.2% of retail price) to keep shelf price stable:
*   **New Margin:** $1.42
*   **Margin Compression:** 11.25%

If you pass the full $0.18 to the consumer (raising price to $2.68):
*   **Margin:** $1.60 (unchanged)
*   **Risk:** Volume churn (ASSUMED: 5-10% drop in price-sensitive markets like Berkeley).

*Formula for Break-Even Volume Drop if raising price:*
$$(\text{Old Margin} \times \text{Old Volume}) = (\text{New Margin} \times \text{New Volume})$$
Since margin per unit stays same if fully passed through, you only lose profit if volume drops. If you absorb the cost, you need a **12.7% volume increase** just to break even on total profit dollars ($1.42 \times X = $1.60 \times 15,000).

**What Flips It, and the Trigger**
The deciding factor is the **statutory incidence** of the tax.
*   **Seattle:** The Sweetened Beverage Tax is imposed on the *distributor*. If you self-distribute, you are the distributor. If you use a third-party distributor, they are legally liable, though they will contractually pass the cost to you.
*   **Berkeley:** The Measure D tax is imposed on *distributors*, but the definition of "distributor" can trap manufacturers who sell directly to retailers.
*   **Trigger:** If your expansion model involves selling directly to Seattle/Berkeley retailers (bypassing a licensed distributor), you become the liable entity immediately.
*   **Action:** If you plan to self-distribute even 10% of volume, halt and consult counsel. If using established distributors, the operational workaround is a "tax adder" line item on invoices, not a new SKU.

**Routing Note**
While the general rule allows passing costs via contract, the specific definition of "distributor" in Berkeley and Seattle varies based on where title transfers. You must ask a local tax attorney: "Does our specific wholesale agreement structure make CraftFizz the statutory 'distributor' liable for remittance, or does liability rest solely with our logistics partner?"
