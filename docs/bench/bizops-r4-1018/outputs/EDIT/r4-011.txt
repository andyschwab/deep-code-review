**The Call**
Switch to the co-manufacturer immediately; the arrangement is viable and improves your unit economics, provided you maintain the $6.50 retail price. Your current pilot kitchen model likely hides significant labor and overhead inefficiencies that the $1.20 fee replaces. At current volume, you will operate profitably on a contribution basis, but you must verify if the $18,000 fixed overhead includes the salaries of your three employees, as this drastically changes your break-even reality.

**The Numbers**
*Formula:* Contribution Margin (CM) = Retail Price − (Raw Materials + Co-manufacturing Fee).
*Inputs:* Price $6.50, Materials $2.10, Co-man Fee $1.20.
*Result:* **$3.20 CM per unit** (49% margin).

*Formula:* Break-Even Volume = Monthly Fixed Overhead / CM per Unit.
*Inputs:* Overhead $18,000, CM $3.20.
*Result:* **5,625 units/month** required to cover fixed costs.

*Analysis:* You currently sell 4,000 units. At this volume, your total contribution is $12,800 (4,000 × $3.20). Against an $18,000 overhead, you are operating at a **monthly loss of $5,200**. You need to increase sales by 41% (1,625 additional units) to reach break-even.

*Assumption Check:* If the $18,000 overhead *excludes* the three full-time employee salaries, your actual fixed costs are significantly higher, pushing the break-even point much further out. If the $18,000 *includes* salaries, the path to profitability is clearer but still requires growth.

**What Flips It, and the Trigger**
The single input that flips this from a "growth challenge" to a "viability crisis" is the **Retail Price**. If retailer slotting fees or promotional discounting force your net price below $5.46, your contribution margin drops below $2.16, and the break-even volume skyrockets beyond 8,300 units.
*Trigger:* If your net realized price averages below **$5.50** for two consecutive months, pause the co-manufacturer transition and renegotiate terms or reduce raw material costs.
*Rule of Thumb:* A healthy CPG startup targets a break-even volume at no more than 60–70% of current capacity. You are currently at 71% of the required volume (4,000/5,625), which is tight but manageable with aggressive sales efforts.
*This Week's Step:* If your sales team cannot commit to securing shelf space for 1,700 additional units within 90 days, consider a 5% price increase to $6.85, which would lower your break-even point to 5,180 units.

**Hidden Cost Factors**
1.  **Minimum Order Quantities (MOQs):** Co-manufacturers often require batch sizes that exceed your monthly sales, tying up cash in inventory.
2.  **Freight and Logistics:** The $1.20 fee likely covers production only; inbound material shipping and outbound distribution to grocery stores are now your explicit cost.
3.  **Slotting Fees:** Scaling to new stores often requires upfront payments to retailers, which are not reflected in unit economics but drain cash flow.
4.  **Labor Redundancy:** If the co-manufacturer handles production, do you still need three full-time employees? If yes, your overhead is bloated; if no, factor in severance or restructuring costs.
