**The Call**
Present the **strictly matched** gross margin figures alongside a clear explanation of the cost driver; do not smooth or average the costs to manufacture a steady 78% margin. While volatility feels risky, artificially stabilizing COGS obscures the unit economics of your heavy analysis jobs and risks being flagged as aggressive accounting by sophisticated Series A investors. The standard practice for variable cloud infrastructure tied to specific customer usage is to match the expense to the revenue period in which the consumption occurred.

**The Numbers**
Gross Margin = (Revenue – COGS) / Revenue.
Using your figures:
*   **Revenue:** $85,000 (steady).
*   **Scenario A (Low Compute):** COGS $4,000. Margin = ($85k - $4k) / $85k = **95.3%**.
*   **Scenario B (High Compute):** COGS $12,000. Margin = ($85k - $12k) / $85k = **85.9%**.
*   **Your "Smoothed" Figure:** You cited 78%, which implies an averaged COGS of ~$18,700, or you are including other direct costs not mentioned. If we strictly use your $4k-$12k range, the fluctuation is actually between 86% and 95%, not 65%-82%.
*   **ASSUMED:** The 65%-82% range you fear includes other variable direct costs (e.g., third-party data fees, support labor) that also fluctuate with job volume. If those exist, they must also be matched, not smoothed.

**What Flips It, and the Trigger**
The input that changes the recommendation is **causality**. If the $12,000 spend is purely discretionary R&D or platform optimization not tied to specific customer contracts, it moves from COGS to OpEx (R&D), stabilizing your gross margin.
*   **Trigger:** If >20% of the variable spend is not directly traceable to fulfilling a specific customer contract, reclassify that portion to OpEx.
*   **Rule of Thumb:** Investors prefer a volatile but honest gross margin over a smooth but misleading one. Volatility in COGS signals a variable cost structure (good for scaling), whereas smoothing signals potential manipulation.
*   **This Week:** Prepare a slide showing the margin trend *and* a waterfall chart explaining the variance (e.g., "Month X margin dipped due to 3 large migration jobs requiring extra compute").
