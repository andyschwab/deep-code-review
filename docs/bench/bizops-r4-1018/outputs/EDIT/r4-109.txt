**The Call:** Yes, adding disease risk predictions for Type 2 diabetes and heart conditions classifies your kit as a medical device requiring FDA clearance; you cannot legally market this feature in the US without it, and your three-month launch date is not feasible for a new submission. You must delay the launch of this specific feature until clearance is obtained or significantly narrow the claims to fall under existing enforcement discretion policies (which rarely cover specific disease risk probabilities).

**The Numbers & Regulatory Threshold:**
The determining factor is the "intended use" defined by your claims. Under the Federal Food, Drug, and Cosmetic Act, any product intended to diagnose, cure, mitigate, treat, or prevent disease is a medical device.
*   **Current State:** Ancestry/wellness traits = Generally non-device or low-risk (enforcement discretion often applies).
*   **Proposed State:** Predicting risk for specific diseases (Type 2 diabetes, heart conditions) = Medical Device.
*   **Classification:** Most genetic risk tests for common diseases are classified as **Class II** devices, requiring a **510(k) premarket notification** to demonstrate "substantial equivalence" to a legally marketed predicate device.
*   **Timeline (Typical):** The statutory review clock for a 510(k) is 90 days, but the *real-world* timeline including preparation, interactive review, and potential deficiencies is typically **6 to 12 months**.
*   **Cost (Typical):**
    *   FDA User Fee (FY2024 small business): ~$3,100 (standard: ~$12,400).
    *   Preparation costs (consulting, validation studies, documentation): **$150,000 – $500,000+** depending on data availability.
    *   *Assumption:* You do not yet have clinical validation data linking your specific DNA markers to disease incidence in a validated population. If you lack this, costs and time increase significantly.

**What Flips It & The Trigger:**
The single input that changes the outcome is the **specificity of the claim**.
*   **Trigger:** If you change the claim from "predicts risk for Type 2 diabetes" to general wellness information (e.g., "genetic variants associated with glucose metabolism") without mentioning the disease name or risk probability, you *might* fall under the FDA's current enforcement discretion for certain Low-Risk Genetic Health Risk (GHR) tests. However, the FDA has tightened this; explicit disease risk percentages almost always trigger device status.
*   **Decision Rule:** If the marketing material mentions a specific disease name + risk probability, it is a device.
*   **Immediate Step:** If your launch is in three months, **do not include the disease risk feature**. Launch only the ancestry/wellness components. Initiate a regulatory strategy assessment this week to determine if a predicate device exists for your specific claims; if no predicate exists, you may face the much longer and costlier De Novo classification pathway.
