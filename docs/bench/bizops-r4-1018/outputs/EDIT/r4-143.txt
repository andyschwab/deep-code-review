**The Call**
Forecast your tax liability using a 5-year straight-line method with zero salvage value as your base case; do not assume accelerated depreciation applies without a CPA confirming your eligibility under current federal bonus rules and California's specific conformity limits.

**The Numbers**
*Formula:* Annual Depreciation = (Cost Basis − Salvage Value) / Useful Life.
*Inputs:* Cost Basis = $900/unit; Total Basis = $180,000; Salvage Value = $0 (ASSUMED).
*Scenario A (Base Case - 5-Year Life):* $180,000 / 5 = **$36,000/year**.
*Scenario B (Aggressive - 3-Year Life):* $180,000 / 3 = **$60,000/year**.
*Scenario C (Conservative - 7-Year Life):* $180,000 / 7 = **~$25,714/year**.
*Note:* Software updates are generally expensed as incurred (R&D or operating expense) rather than capitalized into the hardware's useful life, unless they constitute a major upgrade extending the asset's life.

**What Flips It, and The Trigger**
The single input that changes your cash flow is the **applicable depreciation regime** (e.g., 100% Bonus Depreciation vs. MACRS vs. Straight-Line).
*Trigger:* If the federal bonus depreciation percentage drops below 100% or California decouples from the federal provision (which it historically often does), your current-year deduction could drop from ~$180,000 to ~$36,000 instantly.
*Rule of Thumb:* Assume California does not conform to federal accelerated rates unless verified; this creates a state/federal book-tax difference.
*Action:* If your CPA confirms California non-conformity for the current tax year, consider accelerating hardware purchases before year-end only if federal benefits outweigh the state deferral.
