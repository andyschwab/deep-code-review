**The Call**
Do not claim "carbon neutral" based solely on internal calculations and offset receipts; while US law does not mandate a specific accredited third-party *certification* seal, it strictly requires that any unqualified environmental claim be substantiated by "competent and reliable scientific evidence," which the FTC and Oregon AG typically interpret as requiring third-party verification of both the footprint calculation and the offset quality to avoid greenwashing penalties. Proceeding without external verification exposes ThreadPure to high regulatory risk given the $15,000 audit cost is manageable relative to your $570,000 revenue and the potential fines for deceptive trade practices.

**The Numbers**
*Risk Exposure vs. Verification Cost*
*   **Revenue at Risk:** $570,000 (brand reputation and potential disgorgement of profits).
*   **Proposed Offset Spend:** $10,000 (ASSUMED: covers scope 1-3 for 15k units).
*   **Verification Cost:** $15,000 (ASSUMED: one-time audit + methodology review).
*   **Potential Penalty:** Variable; FTC civil penalties can exceed $50,000 *per violation* (per ad/label instance), and Oregon's Unlawful Trade Practices Act allows for consumer class actions and attorney general enforcement.
*   **Result:** The cost of verification ($15k) is 1.5x the offset budget but represents only ~2.6% of annual revenue, acting as insurance against penalties that could exceed total annual profit. Without verification, the "competent and reliable" standard is likely unmet for a public claim.

**What Flips It, and the Trigger**
The deciding input is the **methodology transparency**. If you can publicly disclose the exact ISO standard (e.g., ISO 14064 or GHG Protocol) used for calculation and the specific registry (e.g., Verra, Gold Standard) where offsets are retired, you lower risk slightly, but the *verification* of that data remains the weak link.
*   **Trigger:** If the FTC updates its Green Guides (currently under review) to explicitly require third-party verification for "net zero" or "carbon neutral" claims, self-certification becomes indefensible.
*   **Rule of Thumb:** If the claim drives purchasing decisions (marketing campaign), the substantiation burden is highest.
*   **Step this week:** If you proceed without an audit, consider narrowing the claim from "Carbon Neutral Brand" to "Carbon Neutral Shipping" (if only shipping is offset) and explicitly state "based on internal estimates" while preparing for immediate scrutiny. However, the safer pivot is to allocate the $15,000 audit budget before launching the campaign.
