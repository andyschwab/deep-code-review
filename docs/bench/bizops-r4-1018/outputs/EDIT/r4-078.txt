**The Call**
Yes, bundling distinct performance obligations (software vs. services) in a single contract requires you to allocate the transaction price based on Standalone Selling Prices (SSP) under IFRS 15 (the standard applicable in Ireland). You do not need to halt sales, but you must formalize your SSP methodology and allocation logic immediately to avoid a qualified audit opinion. Engage your auditor now to validate your specific SSP evidence and allocation policy before closing more deals.

**The Numbers**
Under IFRS 15, you must separate the contract into distinct performance obligations and allocate the total price (€15,000) proportionally to the SSP of each component.

*Formula:*
Allocated Price = (Component SSP / Total SSP of all components) × Total Transaction Price

*Your Figures:*
*   Total Transaction Price: €15,000
*   Software SSP (assumed equal to list price): €10,000
*   Services SSP (assumed equal to list price): €5,000
*   Total SSP: €15,000

*Result (Base Case - No Discount):*
Since your bundle price (€15k) equals the sum of individual list prices (€15k), the allocation is 1:1.
*   Software Revenue: €10,000 (recognized ratably over 12 months ≈ €833/month).
*   Services Revenue: €5,000 (recognized upon completion/milestones).

*Result (Discount Scenario - ASSUMED):*
If you later offer the bundle for €13,500 (10% discount), you cannot recognize the full discount against services just because they are one-time. You must allocate the discount proportionally unless specific criteria allow otherwise.
*   Allocation Ratio: €13,500 / €15,000 = 90%
*   Software Revenue: €9,000 (recognized over time).
*   Services Revenue: €4,500 (recognized on completion).

**What Flips It, and the Trigger**
The complexity flips from "simple" to "high-risk" if you cannot evidence the SSP for the professional services. If your services are never sold separately, the €5,000 figure is an assumption, not an SSP.
*   **Trigger:** The moment you sell a bundle where the components are *not* sold separately with observable prices, or when you introduce variable pricing/discounts.
*   **Rule of Thumb:** If you cannot produce three recent standalone contracts for the service at €5,000, your SSP is "estimated" (using adjusted market assessment or expected cost-plus-margin), which requires rigorous documentation.
*   **Action:** If you have not sold the services standalone in the last 6 months, consider freezing the bundle price until you document an SSP estimation model (e.g., cost of delivery + target margin) and have it reviewed by counsel/auditor.

**Routing Note**
This is a binding accounting determination under IFRS 15. While the math above illustrates the mechanism, the specific evidence required to support your SSP (especially for services not sold standalone) and the precise timing of revenue recognition for your workshops must be validated by your external auditor. Bring them your draft "Revenue Recognition Policy" document specifically addressing multi-element arrangements before your next quarter-end close.
