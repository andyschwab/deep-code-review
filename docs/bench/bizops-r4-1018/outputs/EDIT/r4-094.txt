**The Call**
Proceed with the tiered revenue share, but strictly decouple payout volume from operational control; the classification risk stems from *how* you manage contributors, not the variable pay itself. Do not grant governance rights that dictate platform strategy or enforce work schedules, as these specific activities trigger employee reclassification under California's ABC test.

**The Numbers**
There is no statutory dollar threshold where a contractor becomes an employee; the test is qualitative (control), not quantitative (earnings). However, we can model the financial exposure of a misclassification event to understand the stakes.
*Formula:* `Potential Liability = (Back Taxes + Penalties + Unpaid Benefits) per Worker × Number of Reclassified Workers`
*User Figures:* 300 contributors; top 10% (30 users) earn >$3,000/mo.
*Assumptions (ASSUMED):* If 30 top earners were reclassified, average back-pay/benefit liability is estimated at 30% of gross earnings over a 3-year lookback (conservative estimate for CA).
*Result:*
*   Annual Gross per Top Earner: $36,000 (low) to $100,000+ (high).
*   Estimated Liability per Worker (3 yrs): $32,400 (low) to $90,000 (high).
*   Total Exposure (30 workers): **$972,000 to $2.7M**.
This illustrates that while the revenue share model is safe, the *cost* of getting the classification wrong is existential for a platform with your current transaction volume ($120k/year gross revenue).

**What Flips It, and the Trigger**
The single input that flips the classification from "contractor" to "employee" is **Behavioral Control**.
*Trigger Value:* If your new model requires contributors to attend mandatory meetings, adhere to specific working hours, use proprietary tools exclusively, or if their "governance" role involves voting on hiring/firing or core business strategy, they fail the "B" prong of the ABC test (work outside the usual course of business).
*Rule of Thumb:* Contractors control the *method* and *time* of work; employees are told *how* and *when*.
*Actionable Step:* If your new governance plan includes voting on platform fees or roadmap priorities, consider restructuring it to an "Advisory Council" with non-binding recommendations only, implemented by Q3.

**Routing Note**
California's *Dynamex* decision and AB5 statute enforce the strict "ABC test" for worker classification. Because you are operating in California and proposing changes to governance structures, you must have California employment counsel review the specific bylaws of your proposed "governance" model before implementation to ensure they do not constitute "control."
