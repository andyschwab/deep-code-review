**The Call**
Present a three-tiered market size (TAM, SAM, SOM) rather than a single discounted number; specifically, define your Serviceable Obtainable Market (SOM) by applying your historical conversion velocity and sales cycle constraints to the regulated subset, while keeping the Total Addressable Market (TAM) as the raw theoretical maximum to show ceiling potential. Do not discount the raw TAM directly, as this conflates market scope with execution risk; instead, isolate the "adoption velocity" penalty strictly within the SOM calculation to demonstrate to investors that you understand the friction but still see the large upside.

**The Numbers**
Use the standard funnel: TAM → SAM → SOM.
*Formula:* $SOM = (Target Segments \times Active Buying Rate) \times Average Contract Value (ACV)$.

Using your provided figures:
1.  **TAM (Raw):** 5,000 enterprises. (Do not apply rates here).
2.  **SAM (Serviceable):** The subset you can actually reach given your Toronto location and specific compliance focus. *Assumption:* If you target only North American banking/healthcare, SAM ≈ 5,000. If you exclude regions you cannot service, mark this **ASSUMED**.
3.  **SOM (Obtainable):** This is where you apply the friction.
    *   *Active Buyers per Year:* $5,000 \times 2\% = 100$ companies/year.
    *   *Your Share:* You currently have 40 customers. If your sales cycle is 12+ months, your "run rate" capture is limited by how many deals you can close simultaneously.
    *   *Revenue Projection:* $100 \text{ buyers} \times \text{Your Win Rate} \times \text{ACV}$.
    *   *Current ACV:* $\$1.2M / 40 = \$30,000$.
    *   *Illustrative SOM (Base Case):* If you capture 10% of the active buyers (10 deals) at current ACV: $10 \times \$30,000 = \$300,000$ *new* annual revenue potential from this specific churn/buy cycle, plus retention.

*Note on Inputs:* The 2% "buy security tools" figure is a broad proxy. **ASSUMED** that this applies to *your specific vault solution*; if your tool is niche, this rate may be lower (thin data).

**What Flips It, and the Trigger**
The single input that changes the narrative is the **Sales Cycle Length vs. Cash Runway**. If your sales cycle extends beyond 18 months, the 2% annual buying rate effectively drops for *your* specific pipeline because deals slip years.
*Trigger:* If your average time-to-close exceeds 14 months, pivot your investor story from "volume of enterprises" to "land-and-expand within existing regulated logos," as new logo acquisition becomes too capital-intensive.
*Rule of Thumb:* Investors accept a small SOM if the path to expanding ACV within won accounts is clear.
*Step this week:* If your current sales cycle > 12 months, consider piloting a "compliance-lite" tier for non-regulated tech firms to shorten feedback loops and cash flow while the enterprise deals mature.
