**The Call:** Shift your proposal structure from "technical migration" to "financial risk arbitration." Present a Total Cost of Ownership (TCO) comparison showing a 3-year break-even, explicitly quantifying the "cost of inaction" (maintenance + risk) versus the "cost of action," and split your deck into a technical appendix for the CTO and a one-page financial executive summary for the CFO.

**The Numbers:**
To justify the $120,000 upfront cost, you must model the **Payback Period** and **Net Present Value (NPV)** of the migration. Since you lack specific client maintenance data, use these labelled assumptions to build your template:

*Formula:* `Annual Savings = (Legacy Maintenance Cost + Downtime Risk Cost) - (New Cloud Run Cost)`
*Formula:* `Payback Period (Months) = Upfront Cost / (Annual Savings / 12)`

**Worked Example (Labelled ASSUMED inputs):**
*   **Upfront Cost:** $120,000 (Your data)
*   **ASSUMED Legacy Maintenance:** $80,000/year (Typical for mid-size COBOL systems)
*   **ASSUMED Risk Cost:** $40,000/year (Estimated compliance/outage exposure)
*   **ASSUMED New Cloud Run Cost:** $20,000/year
*   **Calculated Annual Savings:** ($80k + $40k) - $20k = $100,000
*   **Calculated Payback Period:** $120,000 / ($100,000 / 12) = **14.4 months**

*Scenario Analysis:*
*   **Conservative (High Cloud Cost/Low Risk):** If savings drop to $60k/year, payback extends to **24 months**.
*   **Aggressive (High Risk/Low Maintenance):** If savings rise to $140k/year, payback shrinks to **10.3 months**.

**What Flips It, and The Trigger:**
The single input that changes the CFO's decision is the **Legacy Maintenance Cost**. If this figure is lower than your assumption, the ROI collapses.
*   **Trigger Rule:** If the client's current annual maintenance spend is less than **$40,000**, the 3-year ROI turns negative under base assumptions.
*   **Actionable Step:** Before the next meeting, send a one-question email to your champion: *"To finalize the business case, can you confirm your current annual spend on mainframe licensing and specialized COBOL developer retention?"* If they cannot answer, the deal is not financially qualified.

**Strategy for Dual Stakeholders:**
1.  **For the CFO (The "Risk & Cash" Slide):** Lead with the **Cost of Inaction**. Do not start with technology. Start with a table showing: "Current State: $120k/year bleed (Maintenance + Risk)" vs. "Future State: $20k/year run cost + $120k one-time investment." Highlight that the $120k fee is capitalized (CapEx) while maintenance is operational (OpEx), which often appeals to finance teams managing EBITDA.
2.  **For the CTO (The "Enablement" Slide):** Keep the technical architecture deep-dive, but append a "Risk Mitigation" section that mirrors the CFO's language (e.g., "Reduced single-point-of-failure probability by X%").
3.  **The Bridge:** Create a shared "Value Realization Roadmap." Map every technical milestone (e.g., "Module X migrated") to a financial release (e.g., "Legacy server Y decommissioned, saving $Z/month immediately"). This proves to the CFO that the CTO's technical wins translate directly to cash flow.
