**The Call**
Do not defend the 10x multiple on current ARR alone; instead, reframe the valuation narrative around "Net Dollar Retention (NDR)-driven growth" and present a "churn-adjusted ARR" metric that isolates the high-value core, while immediately committing to a specific plan to reduce logo churn to unlock the full multiple.

**The Numbers**
Your current valuation ask relies on a standard SaaS multiple (10x) applied to a fragile top-line number.
*   **Current ARR:** $1.2M
*   **Gross Logo Churn:** 4% monthly (~40% annualized). This is the primary de-risking factor for investors.
*   **Net Dollar Retention (NDR):** 120%. This indicates your remaining customers are growing 20% net of contraction/churn.
*   **Burn Rate:** $100k/month ($1.2M/year).
*   **Runway:** With $0 new capital, your runway is effectively zero if you are burning $100k/mo against $100k/mo revenue (assuming ARR is collected evenly), but likely you have some cash balance not stated. If revenue covers burn exactly, runway is infinite only if growth stops; however, with 4% logo churn, you must replace 48% of your customer base annually just to hold steady, which drives up CAC.

*Assumption (ASSUMED):* You did not provide your CAC or the average contract value (ACV) of churned vs. expanding customers.
*   **Scenario A (Status Quo):** Investors apply a "distressed" multiple (4x–6x) due to 40% annual logo churn. Valuation: $4.8M – $7.2M.

If you can prove the churn is limited to low-ACV segments while high-ACV segments have <1% churn, you can argue for an 8x–9x multiple on the *projected* stable ARR. Valuation: $9.6M – $10.8M.

**What Flips It, and the Trigger**
The single input that changes the call is **Churn by Segment**.
*   **Trigger:** If you can demonstrate that the 4% monthly churn is concentrated entirely in customers with an ACV below $X (e.g., self-serve tier), while your enterprise/core tier churn is <1% monthly, the narrative flips from "leaky bucket" to "funnel optimization."
*   **Rule of Thumb:** SaaS companies with >120% NDR can command premium multiples *only if* gross revenue retention (GRR) is above 80%. Your current GRR is likely too low (~60% implied by 4% monthly churn without expansion offsetting it fully in dollar terms if the base is shrinking).
*   **Step this week:** Segment your churn data immediately. If core churn is high, pause the raise and fix retention; if core churn is low, build the "Two-Tier Business Model" slide for your deck.
