**What Flips It & The Trigger:**
The deciding factor is the **destination state's specific quarantine status for Solanaceae (tomato family)**.
*   **California:** Extremely strict. Often prohibits entry of nightshade family plants from almost all other states due to pest risks (e.g., tomato spotted wilt virus). Individual certificates may not even be sufficient; a specific import permit is usually required *before* shipping.
*   **Arizona & New York:** Generally require a **Nursery Stock Certificate** or **Phytosanitary Certificate** issued by your home state (Florida) verifying the plants are pest-free. Some states offer "blanket" permits for registered nurseries, allowing multiple shipments over a year for a single fee.
*   **Seeds vs. Live Plants:** Seeds generally only require declaration on the package and sometimes a germination test label; they rarely need phytosanitary certificates unless originating from a specific quarantined zone for a specific pest. Switching to seeds-only flips the model from "non-compliant/loss-making" to "compliant/profitable" immediately.
