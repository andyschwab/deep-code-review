**Regulatory Steps & Timeline (General Rule):**
Under the Dietary Supplement Health and Education Act (DSHEA), structure-function claims (e.g., "supports muscle recovery") do not require pre-approval by the FDA, but they do require a specific process:
1.  **Substantiation:** You must possess evidence that the claim is truthful and not misleading *before* making it.
2.  **The Disclaimer:** Your label **must** carry the exact statement: *"This statement has not been evaluated by the Food and Drug Administration. This product is not intended to diagnose, treat, cure, or prevent any disease."* This must be prominent and linked to the claim.
3.  **Notification Timeline:** You must notify the FDA of the claim **no later than 30 days after** you first market the product. You do *not* need to wait for FDA acknowledgment to sell, but if the FDA later determines the claim is actually a disease claim (e.g., implies treating a deficiency disease), the product is misbranded.

**What Flips It:**
The trigger is the specific wording of your claim relative to your ingredients. If "aids in energy metabolism" is interpreted by the FDA as implying treatment for fatigue associated with a specific disease (like anemia or thyroid issues), it becomes an unapproved drug claim.
*   **Trigger Value:** Any claim linking an ingredient to a disease state or class of diseases.
*   **Stop/Pivot Rule:** If your general counsel cannot explicitly cite the substantiation file for each claim against FDA guidance documents within 48 hours, **stop** and hire a specialist.

**Routing Note (High-Stakes):**
Determining whether your specific phrasing crosses the line from "structure-function" to "disease claim" is a binding legal determination (Tier 3). General counsel often lacks specific FDA regulatory affairs expertise for supplements.
*   **Action:** Engage a regulatory affairs consultant specializing in dietary supplements *before* finalizing label artwork.
*   **Question for Professional:** "Review our specific claim wording and substantiation file to confirm they meet 21 CFR 101.93 criteria and do not trigger disease-drug classification."
