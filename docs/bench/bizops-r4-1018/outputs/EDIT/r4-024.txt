**The Call:** Yes, moving honey across state lines immediately triggers federal FDA jurisdiction that does not apply to your current intrastate sales; you must register your facility with the FDA and comply with the Food Safety Modernization Act (FSMA) before shipping your first pallet to Illinois. You cannot legally distribute interstate with only Georgia state compliance.

**The Numbers & Requirements:**
Interstate commerce shifts your regulatory status from "local" to "federal." The following are mandatory thresholds and actions:
*   **Facility Registration:** Any facility manufacturing, processing, packing, or holding food for consumption in the U.S. must register with the FDA if the food crosses state lines.
    *   *Status:* **Required.** You currently lack this.
    *   *Action:* Submit FDA Form 3537. There is no fee, but it is illegal to operate without it once interstate shipping begins.
*   **FSMA Compliance (Preventive Controls):** Under the FSMA, most food facilities must implement a food safety plan.
    *   *Threshold:* Honey is often eligible for a "Qualified Facility" exemption if you sell directly to consumers or your annual sales are under ~$500k (you are at $85k).
    *   *Requirement:* Even if exempt from full Preventive Controls, you must submit an **attestation** to the FDA confirming your exemption status and affirming you follow basic food safety practices.
    *   *Labeling:* Federal labeling (21 CFR 101) supersedes state rules for interstate goods. You must ensure your label includes the name and place of business of the manufacturer, packer, or distributor, and a net quantity statement in both imperial and metric units.
*   **Grade A Packing License:** This is typically a USDA voluntary grading service or a specific state requirement for certain commodities. It is **not** a federal mandate for general interstate sale of honey, though some large distributors may request it as a commercial condition. Do not confuse this with the mandatory FDA registration.

**What Flips It, and The Trigger:**
*   **The Trigger:** The moment a single jar crosses the Georgia state line for commercial sale.
*   **The Risk:** Operating without FDA registration while shipping interstate can lead to your product being deemed "adulterated" and subject to seizure, detention at the border, or recall.
*   **Decision Threshold:** If you cannot complete FDA registration and update your labels to federal standards within the next 14 days, delay the Illinois shipment.
*   **This Week's Step:** If you plan to ship next month, consider hiring a food safety consultant immediately to draft your FSMA exemption attestation and verify your label compliance, as errors here cause customs/distributor rejections.
