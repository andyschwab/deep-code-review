**The Call:** Do not rely on a static monthly P&L; you must build a **weekly cash flow waterfall** that tracks deposits as liabilities until delivery. Based on your current numbers, a sudden surge to 40 suits creates an immediate cash spike of $24,000 (40 suits × $1,200 × 50%), but this is not profit—it is unearned revenue funding future labor. You likely do not need a line of credit for a one-month surge given your $60,000 buffer, but you will if high order volume persists for three consecutive months without corresponding deliveries.

**The Numbers:**
To model this, use the following weekly logic:
*   **Cash Inflow (Week $t$):** $(0.5 \times \text{Price} \times \text{New Orders}_t) + (0.5 \times \text{Price} \times \text{Deliveries}_t)$
*   **Cash Outflow (Week $t$):** $\text{Fixed Overhead} + (\text{Variable Cost per Suit} \times \text{Active Production Volume}_t)$
*   **Net Cash Position:** $\text{Opening Balance} + \text{Inflow} - \text{Outflow}$

*Assumptions (Labelled ASSUMED as you did not provide variable costs):*
*   **Variable Cost:** ASSUMED $400/suit (fabric + tailor labor).
*   **Fixed Overhead:** ASSUMED $10,000/month ($2,500/week).
*   **Production Flow:** Orders placed in Month 1 deliver in Month 2.5 (6 weeks).

*Scenario: Surge to 40 suits in Month 1, returning to 20 in Month 2.*
1.  **Month 1 (Order Spike):**
    *   Inflow: $24,000 (deposits) + $12,000 (final payments from prior month's 20 suits) = $36,000.
    *   Outflow: $10,000 (fixed) + $16,000 (40 suits in production × $400) = $26,000.
    *   **Net:** +$10,000. Ending Cash: $70,000.
2.  **Month 2 (High Production, Low Delivery):**

    *   Outflow: $10,000 (fixed) + $16,000 (completing Month 1's 40 suits) = $26,000.
    *   **Net:** -$14,000. Ending Cash: $56,000.
3.  **Month 3 (Delivery Spike):**
    *   Inflow: $12,000 (deposits) + $24,000 (final payments from Month 1's 40 suits) = $36,000.
    *   Outflow: $10,000 + $8,000 (20 suits) = $18,000.
    *   **Net:** +$18,000. Ending Cash: $74,000.

**What Flips It, and The Trigger:**
The single input that breaks your liquidity is **sustained growth**. If you hit 40 suits/month for three months straight, your outflows for labor/materials will compound before the first wave of deliveries clears.
*   **Trigger Value:** If your **Cash Balance drops below $30,000** (3 months of fixed overhead), activate a line of credit.
*   **Rule of Thumb:** Never spend more than 60% of *new* deposit cash on variable costs; reserve the rest for the "valley" before delivery.
*   **Action:** If your projected cash balance dips below $40,000 in any week of your 6-month forecast, consider securing a $25,000 revolving credit line now while your balance sheet is strong.

**Key Variables to Track:**
1.  **Unearned Revenue Liability:** Total deposits received minus costs incurred on those specific orders.
2.  **Production Pipeline Value:** The dollar value of suits currently in progress (future cash outflow obligation).
3.  **Weekly Burn Rate:** Distinct from monthly burn, this captures the timing mismatch of payroll.
