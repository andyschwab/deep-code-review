**The Call:** Absorb the $0.90 audit cost immediately; do not raise prices yet. Your unit economics remain positive with a healthy contribution margin, and the audit is a critical investment for a fair-trade brand's longevity. The risk lies in your total volume and fixed overhead coverage, not the per-unit profitability.

**The Numbers:**
*Formula:* Contribution Margin (CM) = Retail Price − (Variable COGS + Variable Fees).
*Inputs:* Price $6.50; Base COGS $2.10; Audit Cost $0.90 (ASSUMED: added to variable cost); Distributor Fee 15% of $6.50 ($0.975).

*Scenario A (Current State, no audit):*
$6.50 − ($2.10 + $0.975) = **$3.425 per bar**.

*Scenario B (With Audit):*
$6.50 − ($2.10 + $0.90 + $0.975) = **$2.525 per bar**.

*Annual Projection (50,000 units):*
Total Contribution = 50,000 × $2.525 = **$126,250**.
Annual Fixed Overhead = $12,000/month × 12 = **$144,000**.
Net Operating Result = $126,250 − $144,000 = **−$17,750**.

**What Flips It, and the Trigger:**
The decision to absorb the cost flips only if the audit fails to protect your brand premium or if volume drops significantly. However, your current projection shows a loss regardless of the audit due to fixed overhead, not the audit cost itself.
*Trigger:* You must sell **57,030 bars** annually (approx. 4,753/month) to break even with the audit cost included.
*Rule of Thumb:* If you cannot confidently reach 4,800 units/month in Year 1, the business model is unsustainable at this price point, audit or not.
