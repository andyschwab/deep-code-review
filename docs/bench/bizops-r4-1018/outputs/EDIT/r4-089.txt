**The Call**
Do not renegotiate your existing B2B Data Processing Agreements (DPAs); the shift to a B2C tier does not invalidate your current compliance framework for enterprise clients. Instead, treat the B2C launch as a distinct processing activity requiring a new, consumer-facing Privacy Notice and Terms of Service, while updating your internal Record of Processing Activities (RoPA) to reflect the new data scope. Your B2B contracts remain valid because the legal basis and data subjects for those 200 clients (corporate entities acting in a professional capacity) have not changed; you are simply adding a new customer segment with different rights, not altering the service delivered to existing business customers.

**The Numbers & Scope Impact**
*   **Current State:** 200 B2B clients × €150/mo = €30,000 MRR. Data subjects are primarily professional contacts; rights are managed via account managers or B2B support channels.
*   **Projected B2C State:** Assuming 1,000 initial users × €15/mo = €15,000 MRR. Data subjects are natural persons under GDPR Article 4(1).
*   **Compliance Overhead:**
    *   *B2B:* 200 relationships, low volume of Subject Access Requests (SARs), manual fulfillment acceptable.
    *   *B2C:* Potential for thousands of SARs, erasure requests, and portability demands. Manual fulfillment is mathematically unsustainable.
    *   *Assumption:* If even 1% of B2C users exercise rights monthly, that is 10 requests/month initially, scaling with user growth.
*   **Result:** You must build self-service tooling (tier 1 engineering task) to handle portability and erasure for the B2C tier to maintain margin. The cost of renegotiating 200 B2B contracts (legal fees + account management time) vastly exceeds the cost of drafting one new B2C agreement and building the self-service portal.

**What Flips It, and the Trigger**
The single input that changes this recommendation is if your B2B clients begin uploading **consumer data of their own customers** into HookHub for processing.
*   **Trigger Value:** If a B2B client uses your webhook to process PII of *their* end-users (not just their own employee data), your role shifts from a simple processor of business data to a processor of high-volume consumer data on their behalf.
*   **Pivot Date:** Before the B2C launch next month.
*   **Action:** If you detect B2B clients sending consumer data, you must amend *those specific* B2B DPAs to address sub-processor risks and higher liability caps, but you still do not need to renegotiate the entire fleet.
*   **Rule of Thumb:** If the data subject is an individual acting outside their trade/business, GDPR consumer rights apply. If the data subject is an employee acting in a professional capacity, B2B terms usually suffice (subject to local EU member state nuances).
*   **This Week's Step:** If your architecture allows logical separation of B2B and B2C data stores, consider isolating B2C data immediately to simplify future erasure requests without touching B2B databases.

**Routing Note**
While the strategy above follows general GDPR operational practices, the specific wording of your new B2C Privacy Notice and the "right to be forgotten" workflow must be reviewed by EU counsel to ensure alignment with the specific member states where your largest concentration of individual developers resides, as local implementations of GDPR vary.
