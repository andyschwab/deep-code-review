**The Call:** Calculate your TAM using a bottom-up "Annual New Buyer" model based on replacement cycles, not the total population, and treat retail partners as distribution channels for the same end-user demand rather than additive market segments to avoid double-counting.

**The Numbers:**
The standard formula for durable goods TAM is:
$$TAM = (\text{Addressable Population} \times \text{Annual Replacement Rate}) \times \text{Average Selling Price (ASP)}$$

*   **Addressable Population:** 330M (US) × 20% (Env. Conscious) = 66M people.
*   **Annual Replacement Rate (ASSUMED):** Since the bottle lasts years, users do not buy annually. If the average lifespan is 3 years, the annual replacement rate is ~33% (0.33). If lifespan is 5 years, the rate is 20% (0.20).
*   **ASP:** $35.

*Scenario A (3-year lifespan):*
$66,000,000 \times 0.33 \times \$35 = \mathbf{\$762.3M}$

*Scenario B (5-year lifespan):*
$66,000,000 \times 0.20 \times \$35 = \mathbf{\$462.0M}$

**What Flips It, and The Trigger:**
The single input that changes the call is the **Average Product Lifespan**. A shift from a 3-year to a 5-year lifespan reduces your TAM by nearly 40%.
*   **Trigger:** If customer surveys or warranty data indicate an average lifespan exceeding 4 years, your near-term revenue ceiling drops below $550M, which may impact seed valuation expectations.
*   **Rule of Thumb:** For durable goods, TAM is strictly a function of *new* buyers plus *replacements*, not total owners.
*   **This Week's Step:** If you lack hard data on lifespan, consider running a quick survey of 50 current users asking "When did you buy your last reusable bottle?" to validate the replacement rate assumption before finalizing the deck.

**Addressing the Distribution Concern:**
You do not add Direct-to-Consumer (DTC) and Retail volumes together. Both channels sell the *same* unit to the *same* 66M people.
*   **Correct Approach:** Calculate the total units the market absorbs (as done above). Then, model your *Share of Market* (SOM) by estimating what percentage of those units you can capture via DTC vs. Retail.
*   **Incorrect Approach:** Calculating TAM(DTC) + TAM(Retail). This counts the potential customer twice. The retail partner (e.g., Target) is simply a conduit for a subset of the 66M addressable people.
