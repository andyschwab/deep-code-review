**The Call:** Your current $250 per-patient fee structure creates a high-risk exposure to federal Anti-Kickback Statute (AKS) violations and potential Medicare hospice cap complications; you must immediately pause scaling and restructure your pricing model to a flat administrative subscription or cost-based fee that is decoupled from patient volume or referral success.

**The Numbers & Risk Mechanics:**
The issue is not a mathematical breach of the Medicare hospice cap (which limits the total reimbursement a *provider* receives from Medicare per beneficiary, currently approx. $34,000–$36,000 depending on the year), but rather the legal characterization of your fee under fraud and abuse laws.
*   **Current Model:** $250 × 300 patients = $75,000 revenue.
*   **The Violation Vector:** Under the AKS, offering or paying anything of value to induce referrals for services payable by a federal healthcare program (Medicare) is a felony. A "per-patient" fee directly correlates your compensation to the volume of referrals. Regulators often view this as a kickback because the provider effectively pays you a portion of their Medicare reimbursement for the privilege of receiving the patient.
*   **Cap Interaction:** While your fee doesn't mathematically alter the CMS cap limit, if a provider argues that your fee was a necessary cost of doing business that ate into their margin under the cap, it could trigger audits regarding "financial relationships" that compromise the integrity of the benefit.

**What Flips It, and The Trigger:**
The single input that changes this from "high risk" to "compliant" is the **pricing basis**.
*   **Trigger:** If your fee is calculated *per referral* or *per enrollment*, it is presumptively illegal under AKS safe harbors.
*   **Pivot:** If your fee is a **flat monthly software subscription** (e.g., $500/month for platform access) or a **fair-market-value (FMV) administrative fee** for specific, documented non-referral services (e.g., data reporting, credentialing verification) that does not vary with the number of patients, it may qualify for safe harbor protection.
*   **Rule of Thumb:** Compensation must never rise or fall based on the volume or value of referrals.
*   **This Week's Step:** If your current contracts tie fees to "enrollments" or "patients," consider drafting an amendment to convert all future billing to a flat monthly platform access fee immediately, regardless of patient count.
