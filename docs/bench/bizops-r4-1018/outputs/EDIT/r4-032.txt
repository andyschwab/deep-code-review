**The Call:** Do not raise prices yet; the 8% breakage rate is anomalously high and represents a solvable operational failure, not a structural margin issue. Prioritize immediate packaging redesign to target a <2% breakage rate before altering your $120 price point, as a price hike risks suppressing your already low customer lifetime value (LTV) without fixing the root cause of the bleed.

**The Numbers:**
*   **Current Unit Economics (Base Case):**
    *   Revenue per set: $120
    *   Direct Cost (Material + Labor): $45
    *   Packaging: $12
    *   *Expected* Breakage Cost (at 8%): $3,600 / 400 units = $9.00 per unit shipped.
    *   **Current Contribution Margin:** $120 - ($45 + $12 + $9) = **$54 per unit**.
    *   **Current Margin %:** 45%.

*   **Scenario A: Price Increase to $135 (Assuming 10% Volume Drop):**
    *   New Revenue: $135
    *   Costs remain identical (breakage is an operational flaw, not a fixed cost).
    *   New Contribution Margin: $135 - $66 = **$69 per unit**.
    *   *Risk:* If volume drops >22%, total contribution dollars decrease. With an LTV of only $210, your repeat purchase rate is likely low; acquiring new customers at a higher price point may further depress volume.

*   **Scenario B: Packaging Improvement (Targeting 2% Breakage):**
    *   Assumed new packaging cost: $16 (+$4 investment).
    *   New Breakage Cost (2% of $120 revenue replacement + shipping estimate): ~$2.50 per unit (estimated).
    *   New Total Cost: $45 + $16 + $2.50 = $63.50.
    *   **New Contribution Margin:** $120 - $63.50 = **$56.50 per unit**.
    *   *Result:* You gain $2.50/unit in margin *without* risking volume loss from a price hike, and you improve customer experience (fewer broken goods).

**What Flips It, and the Trigger:**
The decision flips if your breakage rate is actually industry-standard (typically 1–3% for well-packaged ceramics). An 8% rate suggests your current $12 packaging is insufficient for standard carriers.
*   **Trigger:** If a prototype test with upgraded packaging (e.g., molded pulp or double-boxing) reduces breakage to <3% for under $5 additional cost per unit, abandon the price increase.
*   **Rule of Thumb:** Breakage costs for fragile goods should rarely exceed 2% of revenue. At 8%, you are effectively giving away nearly one free set for every twelve sold.
*   **This Week's Step:** If your current breakage cost exceeds $5/unit, consider ordering sample high-performance packaging materials immediately and running a 50-unit stress test before the holiday rush.
