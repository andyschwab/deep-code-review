**The Call**
Proceed with the lease model only if you treat the transaction as a taxable rental in each target state; physical presence of the equipment in Texas, New York, and California almost certainly establishes a sales/use tax nexus regardless of your Delaware headquarters. Do not assume the lease avoids tax; in these jurisdictions, leasing medical equipment often triggers *higher* cumulative tax liability than an outright sale due to recurring taxation on rental receipts.

**The Numbers**
*Outright Sale Scenario (One-time):*
- Revenue: $60,000
- Estimated Sales Tax (approx. 8% blended rate): $4,800 (collected once from clinic).
- Net Cash Upfront: $60,000 (plus tax collected).

*Lease Scenario (36 Months):*
- Monthly Rent: $2,500
- Total Contract Value: $90,000 ($2,500 × 36).
- Estimated Tax Basis: In CA, NY, and TX, sales tax typically applies to *each* rental payment.
- Estimated Total Tax Collected (8% of $90,000): $7,200.
- **Variance:** The lease model generates $4,200 more in potential tax collection obligations over the term than the sale model, increasing the administrative burden and audit surface area.

*Assumption:* ASSUMED blended sales tax rate of 8% for illustration; actual rates vary by specific clinic zip code (e.g., NYC vs. upstate NY).

**What Flips It, and the Trigger**
The defining input is the legal classification of the contract: "True Lease" vs. "Conditional Sale."
- **True Lease:** Tax is due on every monthly payment. Nexus is established immediately upon the equipment entering the state because you are deriving income from property situated there.
- **Conditional Sale:** If the lease includes a $1 buyout clause or transfers title automatically at the end, states like Texas and California may reclassify the entire transaction as a sale. This could make the *full* sales tax due upfront on the total contract value ($90,000), creating a massive immediate liability mismatched with your cash flow.

*Trigger:* If your contract includes a bargain purchase option (e.g., buyout < 10% of FMV), stop and reclassify as a sale for tax purposes.
*Rule of Thumb:* If the lessee builds equity or owns the asset at term end, treat it as a sale.
*This Week's Step:* If your draft contract has a $1 buyout, consider rewriting it as a fair-market-value (FMV) lease with a genuine return option to maintain the rental tax treatment, then verify this specific wording with counsel.

**Routing Note**
The determination of whether your specific lease terms constitute a "true lease" or a "disguised sale" under Texas Tax Code §151.005, New York Tax Law §1101, and California Revenue & Taxation Code §6006 is a binding legal conclusion. You must engage a state-and-local-tax (SALT) attorney to review your master lease agreement before signing any clinic contracts, as misclassification triggers penalties and interest in all three states.
