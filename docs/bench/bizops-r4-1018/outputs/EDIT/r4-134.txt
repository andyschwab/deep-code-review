**The Call:** Pause the 5,000-unit production run immediately. FDA clearance for the medical device aspect does **not** exempt the Bluetooth radio component from FCC certification; you must obtain FCC approval (or validate an existing module certification) before shipping or even manufacturing units intended for sale in the US.

**The Numbers & Rules:**
*   **Regulatory Regime:** The FDA regulates safety and efficacy for medical use. The FCC regulates radio frequency (RF) interference for any device emitting radio waves (47 CFR Part 15). These are separate jurisdictions with no "medical exemption" for RF emissions.
*   **Certification Path:** Since you are integrating Bluetooth Low Energy (BLE):
    *   *Scenario A (Most Likely):* You used a pre-certified BLE module (e.g., from Nordic, Texas Instruments). You do not need full re-certification but **must** file a Class II Permissive Change (C2PC) or a Supplier's Declaration of Conformity (SDoC) depending on antenna changes, confirming the integration didn't alter RF parameters. This costs ~$2,000–$5,000 and takes 2–4 weeks.
    *   *Scenario B:* You designed the RF circuit from scratch. You require full FCC Certification (TCB review). This costs ~$10,000+ and takes 6–8 weeks.
*   **Penalties (47 U.S.C. § 503):** Shipping uncertified RF equipment can result in forfeiture penalties up to **$20,000 per day** for continuing violations and up to **$100,000 per single violation** (or per unit shipped). The FCC can also issue a stop-sale order and seize inventory.

**What Flips It & The Trigger:**
*   **The Flip:** The only variable is whether your BLE component is a fully pre-certified module with a valid FCC ID and if your antenna integration matches the module's strict guidelines.
*   **The Trigger:** If your engineering team cannot produce the FCC Grant of Equipment Authorization for the specific BLE module *and* a schematic proving the antenna gain/layout matches the original certification exactly, you are non-compliant.
*   **Decision Threshold:** Do not ship a single unit until a qualified RF test lab issues a formal test report or C2PC filing acceptance.
*   **This Week's Step:** If your BLE chip is a pre-certified module, contact an accredited RF test lab immediately to schedule a "modular integration" review; if it is a custom design, halt all tooling for the enclosure until the RF design is locked for testing.

**Routing Note:**
This is a binding regulatory determination under US federal law (Communications Act of 1934). Engage an FCC-recognized Telecommunications Certification Body (TCB) or a specialized regulatory counsel immediately to review your Bill of Materials (BOM) and antenna design. Do not rely on general corporate counsel for the technical filing; the liability rests on the grantee (BreathLink).
