**The Call**
Proceed with the sample pack promotion as a classic "loss leader" strategy; the risk of a successful predatory pricing claim against SpiceRoute is negligible given your <1% market share, short three-month duration, and lack of market power to recoup losses later.

**The Numbers**
*   **Unit Economics:**
    *   Sale Price: $5.00
    *   COGS: $7.50
    *   **Contribution Margin:** -$2.50 per pack (Loss)
    *   **Monthly Revenue Context:** $22,000 (approx. 2,315 standard jars).
*   **Assumption (Adoption Rate):** If 10% of monthly revenue volume converts to sample packs (232 packs):
    *   **Monthly Burn on Promo:** 232 packs × $2.50 = **$580 loss/month**.
    *   **Total 3-Month Cost:** **$1,740**.
*   **Break-Even Logic:** To justify this $1,740 expense, you need only ~184 new customers to convert to full-price buyers ($9.50 price - $7.50 COGS = $2.00 margin) within a reasonable timeframe.

**What Flips It, and the Trigger**
The single input that changes this from a safe marketing expense to a legal liability is **Market Power**. Predatory pricing requires the ability to raise prices later to recoup losses (the "recoupment" element).
*   **Trigger:** If SpiceRoute's market share in any defined geographic or product market exceeds ~30-40% (varies by jurisdiction) *and* you sustain losses long enough to drive competitors out.
*   **Rule of Thumb:** With <1% share, you cannot monopolize the spice market; therefore, you cannot legally "predate."
*   **Action This Week:** If your projected sample pack volume exceeds 20% of total units sold, consider capping the offer to ensure the loss remains a bounded marketing line item rather than a core business model shift.

**Routing Note**
While federal law (Sherman Act) and most state laws require proof of dangerous probability of recoupment (which you lack), a few states (e.g., California, Texas) have specific "below-cost" sales statutes that technically prohibit selling below cost with intent to harm competition. However, these almost universally include exemptions for "good faith" introductory offers or loss leaders.
*   **Question for Counsel:** "Does our specific 'new customer only' restriction and 3-month cap satisfy the 'introductory offer' exemption in [Specific State] unfair competition statutes?"
*   **Time-Sensitive:** Confirm this before launching ads in states known for aggressive plaintiff bars (CA, TX, FL) if you expect high volume there.
