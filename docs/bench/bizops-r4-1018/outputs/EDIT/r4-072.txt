**The Call:** Do not bind internal cost savings to customer performance credits; it creates unmanageable liability and conflates your cost structure with service delivery. Instead, guarantee specific performance metrics (e.g., scan completion time) with credits defined as a percentage of the subscription fee, unrelated to your internal costs.

**The Numbers:**
*   **Current Proposal Risk:** Liability = (Number of Clients) × (Frequency of Breach) × ($100/month credit).
    *   *Assumption (Base):* 10% of 300 clients breach the 2-hour threshold monthly due to external factors (e.g., PyPI outages, complex dependency graphs).
    *   *Result:* 30 clients × $100 = **$3,000/month direct revenue erosion** (7.5% of total MRR at current mix), uncapped if costs spike again.
*   **Standard Model:** Liability = (Number of Clients) × (Frequency of Breach) × (10% of Monthly Fee).
    *   *Assumption:* Same breach rate, but credit is capped at 10% of the $400 fee ($40).
    *   *Result:* 30 clients × $40 = **$1,200/month** (3% of MRR), predictable and contained.
*   **Flip Input:** The definition of "exceeds 2 hours." If this includes delays caused by third-party repositories (outside your control), your breach frequency assumption shifts from 10% to potentially 40%+, making the cost-savings model catastrophic.

**What Flips It, and the Trigger:**
The trigger is **force majeure and third-party dependency**. Your internal cost reduction (35%) is irrelevant to whether a scan finishes in 2 hours; scan time depends on network latency, repository availability, and project complexity.
*   *Rule of Thumb:* Never guarantee an outcome dependent on variables outside your sole control.
*   *Step this week:* If your scan engine relies on external APIs (PyPI, GitHub), consider rewriting the SLA to measure "uptime of the scanning service" rather than "completion time of a specific job," or explicitly exclude third-party delays from the credit calculation.
