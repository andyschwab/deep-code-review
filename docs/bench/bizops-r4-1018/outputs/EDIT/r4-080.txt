**The Numbers & Rules:**
*   **VAT Threshold:** The EU One-Stop-Shop (OSS) threshold for cross-border B2C services is €10,000 annually. Your €92,000+ monthly revenue from France, Italy, and Spain far exceeds this.
    *   *Result:* You are required to collect and remit VAT in the customer's location (France, Italy, Spain) at their local rates (approx. 20–22%).
    *   *Mechanism:* Instead of registering in three countries, you likely register for the **Non-Union OSS** in Estonia (your establishment). You file one quarterly return in Estonia covering all EU sales.
*   **Digital Services Tax (DST):** Unlike VAT, DST is not harmonized. France, Italy, and Spain have unilateral DST regimes (typically 3% on gross revenue) if global revenue >€750M and EU revenue >€50M.
    *   *Assumption:* If QueueFast is a startup below these global/EU revenue caps, you likely owe **$0** in DST currently.
    *   *Risk:* If you grow past these thresholds, DST applies based on "user participation" or data transmission within borders, regardless of physical office.

**What Flips It, and the Trigger:**
*   **The Flip:** The classification of your service. If tax authorities reclassify "data transfer" as a "telecommunications service" vs. an "electronically supplied service," the place of supply rules remain the same (customer location), but specific exemptions or reporting nuances may change.
*   **The Trigger:** Crossing the **€10,000** annual B2C threshold (already passed) mandates immediate VAT action. Crossing the **€50M EU revenue** mark triggers DST exposure.
*   **Step this week:** If your global revenue is under €750M, consider registering for the Non-Union OSS in Estonia immediately to cover VAT; if near the DST thresholds, pause and model the 3% gross impact.
