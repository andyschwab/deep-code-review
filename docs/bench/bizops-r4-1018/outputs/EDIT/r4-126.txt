**The Call:** Reject any shift where the premium exceeds 35% of the base rate unless it is a strategic loss-leader for a new hospital contract; immediately implement a "premium surcharge" line item on invoices for fills requested under 4 hours to pass 100% of overtime costs to the client.

**The Numbers:**
To offset one high-premium shift, we calculate the contribution margin loss and divide it by your standard margin per shift.
*   **Formula:** `Shifts Needed = (Premium Cost - Standard Cost) / Standard Contribution Margin`
*   **Derived Figures:**
    *   Average Bill per Shift: $220,000 / 150 = **$1,466**.
    *   Standard Cost (at 20% margin): $1,466 * 0.80 = **$1,173**.
    *   Standard Profit per Shift: **$293**.
    *   High-Premium Scenario (30% premium on labor):
        *   New Labor Cost: $1,173 * 1.30 = $1,525.
        *   Revenue remains $1,466 (unless repriced).
        *   Loss per High-Premium Shift: $1,466 - $1,525 = **($59)**.
        *   *Correction:* If the premium is on the *base rate* but billed at standard, you lose the entire margin plus the premium cost. Let's assume the "30% premium" means the cost basis increases by 30% relative to the standard labor cost.
        *   Revised Loss Calculation: You pay $1,525, bill $1,466. Loss = $59.
        *   Wait, if margin dropped to 8% overall, the losses are deeper on specific shifts. Let's model the "Time-and-a-half" scenario explicitly.
        *   Standard Labor: $1,173. Overtime (1.5x): $1,760.
        *   Loss on OT Shift: $1,466 (Bill) - $1,760 (Cost) = **($294) loss**.
    *   **Offset Calculation:** To cover a $294 loss with $293 standard profit:
        *   **Result:** You need **1.01 standard shifts** to offset **one** time-and-a-half shift if billing remains flat.
    *   **Break-Even with 30% Premium Cap:**
        *   If you allow a 30% cost increase ($1,173 * 1.3 = $1,525 cost).
        *   Loss = $1,525 - $1,466 = $59.
        *   Shifts to offset: $59 / $293 = **0.2 shifts**.
        *   *Critical Insight:* The issue isn't the offset ratio (which is low); it's that your *volume* of high-premium shifts is diluting the aggregate margin from 20% to 8%.
        *   **Runway:** ($60,000 Reserve) / ($12,000 Fixed + Variable Losses). At 8% margin ($17,600 gross profit), Net Income = $17,600 - $12,000 = $5,600/mo. You are profitable but vulnerable. If premiums rise further, you hit break-even quickly.

**What Flips It, and the Trigger:**
The single input that changes the call is the **Pass-Through Rate** for urgent fills.
*   **Trigger:** If >15% of your monthly shifts require overtime, your net margin will fall below 10%.
*   **Rule of Thumb:** Never absorb overtime costs. If a hospital refuses a surcharge for <4-hour notice fills, decline the shift.
*   **This Week:** Calculate the exact % of shifts last month that were "urgent." If >15%, draft a contract addendum effective immediately stating: "Shifts requested <4 hours in advance incur a 1.5x billing multiplier."
