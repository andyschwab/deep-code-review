**Regulatory Landscape Overview (General Rule):**
1.  **Singapore (Headquarters):** The Strategic Goods Control Act (SGCA) controls the export of dual-use items. Singapore aligns closely with the Wassenaar Arrangement. "Software" specifically designed for the development or production of controlled items is often controlled itself.
2.  **Destination Risks:**
    *   **China:** Highest risk. Strict end-user checks apply. Many AI and simulation technologies are subject to blanket denials or heavy scrutiny due to military-civil fusion concerns.
    *   **South Korea & Japan:** Generally allied nations, but specific high-end simulation tools for autonomous systems (which have direct military drone/vehicle applications) may still require licenses depending on resolution and fidelity specs.
3.  **Thresholds:** There is no public "safe harbor" number for data resolution you can rely on without legal counsel. Controls often hinge on the *capability* of the software to model complex environments for defense purposes, not just pixel count.
4.  **Due Diligence:** You must screen all customers against denied party lists (Singapore, US BIS, UN). Implementing geoblocking is a technical mitigation but does not replace the legal requirement for a license if the item is controlled.
5.  **Penalties:** Beyond fines, penalties include imprisonment for directors, loss of export privileges, and reputational destruction.
