**The Call:** Classify the $15,000 commission as **standard gross revenue**, not a contra-expense. You are selling a service (cost optimization) priced as a percentage of value delivered; the client's savings are the metric for your fee, not a reduction of your own costs.

**The Numbers:**
*   **Formula:** Gross Revenue = Commission Rate × Client Savings Achieved.
*   **Your Figures:** 30% × $50,000 (monthly savings) = $15,000 revenue.
*   **Annual Context:** 30% × $200,000 (total savings) = $60,000 total gross revenue.
*   **Assumption (ASSUMED):** Your Delaware entity is a C-Corp or S-Corp filing Form 1120 or 1120-S. If you are a single-member LLC, this flows to Schedule C, but the classification as revenue remains the same.
*   **Result:** The full $60,000 is top-line revenue. Your "cost of goods sold" (COGS) includes the engineering time and server costs to run CloudSaver, not the client's cloud bill (which you never paid).

**What Flips It, and the Trigger:**
The classification would only change to a "contra-expense" or "netted" item if you legally **paid the client's cloud bill directly** and then billed them for the net difference. Since the client pays their own AWS/Azure/GCP bill and pays you a separate fee, you cannot net their expense against your income.
*   **Trigger:** If your contract structure changes so that CloudSaver becomes the primary obligor paying the cloud provider, you would then report gross billings less pass-through costs.
*   **Rule of Thumb:** If the money never touches your bank account as an outgoing payment for the client's infrastructure, it is not your expense to net.
*   **This Week's Step:** If your current invoices list the $15,000 as "Commission on Savings," ensure your bookkeeping software codes this to an Income account (e.g., "Service Revenue"), not a negative Expense account.

**Routing Note (Tier 3 - Tax Determination):**
While the general rule for service commissions is gross revenue, the specific line-item placement on your federal return (e.g., Line 1c vs. Other Income) and any state-level gross receipts tax implications (Delaware does not have a general sales tax, but other states where you have nexus might) require a binding determination.
*   **Ask your CPA:** "Confirm that our value-based commission model is reported as gross service revenue on Form 1120/1120-S Line 1, and verify if any state nexus thresholds are triggered by $60k in revenue."
*   **Time-Sensitive:** Ensure this classification is locked before filing your quarterly estimated payments to avoid underpayment penalties if the revenue is recognized differently than expected.
