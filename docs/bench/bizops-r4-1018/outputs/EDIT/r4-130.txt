**The Numbers & Apportionment Logic:**
Sales tax applies to the specific taxable portion of a bundled transaction. You must establish a reasonable allocation method (e.g., itemized menu prices or cost-plus margins) to split the $2,200.
*   **California:** Lodging is taxable (Transient Occupancy Tax); prepared food is generally taxable; therapeutic services by licensed professionals are often exempt. *Risk:* If you cannot reasonably separate the charges, the entire bundle may be taxed.
*   **New York:** Lodging is taxable; food consumed on-premises is taxable; therapeutic services are generally exempt if performed by licensed professionals. *Crucial:* NY strictly requires the taxable and non-taxable charges to be separately stated on the invoice. If lumped together, the **entire** $2,200 becomes taxable.
*   **Florida:** Lodging is taxable (Tourist Development Tax + Sales Tax); meals are taxable; therapeutic services are generally exempt. Like NY, failure to separately state non-taxable services on the invoice can result in the full amount being subject to tax.

*Illustrative Impact (Assumed Split: $1,200 Lodging, $600 Food, $400 Therapy):*
*   **Current Method (Taxing $2,200):** You collect tax on $400 of exempt services. In NY/FL, this is over-collection requiring refund to customers or remittance as penalty.
*   **Correct Method:** Tax applies only to $1,800.
*   **Nexus Thresholds:** You currently have **physical nexus** in all three states because your team or operations are physically present there. Economic nexus thresholds (e.g., $100k sales or 200 transactions) are irrelevant since you already have physical presence.

**What Flips It & The Trigger:**
The trigger is your invoicing structure. If your current booking engine outputs a single line item ("Wellness Package: $2,200"), you are non-compliant in NY and FL immediately.
*   **Rule of Thumb:** If the invoice does not explicitly list the price of the therapy session separate from lodging/food, assume 100% of the revenue is taxable.
