**The Call:** Your current aggregation model likely remains a non-insured administrative service, but the risk of being deemed an unlicensed insurer rises sharply if your contracts guarantee specific outcomes, cap vendor costs without passing variance to the client, or imply you assume the financial risk of service utilization. You must immediately audit your master services agreements (MSAs) to ensure you are selling "access" and "coordination," not "risk transfer."

**The Numbers:**
There is no single formula for "insurance status," but regulators apply a risk-transfer test. We can model your exposure based on three contractual variables:
*   **Formula:** Risk Exposure = (Fixed Price to Employer) − (Variable Cost to Vendors) + (Guaranteed Outcomes Clause).
*   **Your Figures:** $45/employee/month (Fixed Revenue).
*   **Labelled Assumptions (ASSUMED):**
    *   *Scenario A (Safe):* You pay vendors per use (variable cost). If utilization spikes, you bill the employer extra or the contract allows price adjustment. **Result:** Low regulatory risk; you are a broker/aggregator.
    *   *Scenario B (Danger Zone):* You pay vendors a fixed fee or cap your costs to the employer regardless of usage. If 20% of employees use therapy vs. 2%, you absorb the loss. **Result:** High regulatory risk; this looks like underwriting.
    *   *Scenario C (Critical Failure):* Your marketing or contracts promise "resolved mental health issues" or specific financial outcomes. **Result:** Near-certain classification as an insurance product or guaranteed warranty, triggering licensing requirements.

**What Flips It, and The Trigger:**
The single input that changes the call is **who bears the utilization variance**. If your P&L absorbs the cost when employee usage exceeds actuarial expectations, you are transferring risk.
*   **Trigger Value:** If your gross margin on the $45 fee fluctuates inversely with claim volume (higher usage = lower margin), you are acting as an insurer.
*   **Stop/Pivot Trigger:** If your legal counsel confirms any clause guarantees a specific result (e.g., "debt reduced by 10%") or caps employer liability regardless of usage, **stop sales immediately** in Illinois until restructured.
*   **Rule of Thumb:** Insurance transfers risk of loss; service agreements transfer access to resources.
*   **This Week's Step:** If your MSAs contain "fixed fee" arrangements with vendors while you charge employers a flat rate, consider restructuring to a "pass-through" cost model or adding explicit "non-insurance" and "no guarantee of outcome" disclaimers to all client contracts.
