**The Call**
Do not convert the loan to equity immediately before pitching; instead, offer investors the option to assume the debt or convert it at the Series A price with a modest discount (10–20%) or a valuation cap set at the pre-money target of this round. Converting now without an agreed valuation looks like an unpriced insider transaction that complicates due diligence, whereas keeping it as debt (or converting it *as part of* the round) aligns your risk with the new capital and signals confidence in the upcoming valuation.

**The Numbers**
Your current financial position dictates urgency but does not determine the conversion price.
*   **Runway:** $30,000 (MRR) - $45,000 (Burn) = -$15,000 monthly net burn.
*   **Cash Position:** Not provided (ASSUMED: sufficient only for near-term ops given the raise urgency).
*   **Runway Duration:** If you have $X in cash, Runway = $X / $15,000 months. (e.g., if $45k in bank, runway is 3 months).
*   **Debt Obligation:** $150,000 principal + accrued interest (4% simple interest over 2 years ≈ $12,000). Total claim ≈ $162,000.
*   **Conversion Math:** If you convert now at an arbitrary valuation (e.g., $5M post-money), your $162k buys ~3.2% equity. If you wait and convert at the Series A price (e.g., $10M post-money), it buys ~1.6%.
*   **Fairness Assumption:** The "fair" cap is usually the Series A pre-money valuation discounted for the early risk you took. If you target a $8M pre-money, a $6M–$7M cap on your note rewards your early risk without punishing new money.

**What Flips It, and the Trigger**
The deciding input is the **investor's preference for cap table cleanliness versus pricing precedence**.
*   **Trigger:** If term sheets stall because investors refuse to underwrite a company with founder debt hanging over the assets, convert immediately at the *lowest* valuation you would accept for the equity round (the "floor").
*   **Rule of Thumb:** Early debt converts at a discount to the new money price, not at a premium. If you force a high conversion now, you signal that you believe the company is already worth more than the Series A investors will pay, which creates friction.
*   **Action This Week:** Draft a term sheet appendix showing two scenarios: (A) Debt remains and is repaid from proceeds, or (B) Debt converts at the Series A price minus a 15% discount. Present this to your co-founder and legal counsel to standardize the offer before meeting VCs.

**Routing Note**
Converting founder debt to equity is a securities transaction subject to state corporate law and IRS regulations (potential imputed interest or cancellation of debt income). You must consult a securities attorney to draft the conversion agreement and ensure the interest rate meets Applicable Federal Rates (AFR) to avoid tax penalties, and to confirm that the conversion does not trigger a "409A" valuation issue for future employee options.
