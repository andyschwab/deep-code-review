**The Call**
Your current model—taking custody of crypto assets even briefly to settle a fee—creates a high probability that you are acting as a money transmitter or money services business (MSB) in key jurisdictions, including the US and potentially Singapore. You must stop touching user funds immediately; restructure to a non-custodial model where the developer pays the auditor directly or uses a licensed third-party processor, while you invoice for the software service separately.

**The Numbers**
*   **Current Exposure:** 1,200 transactions/year × $150 = $180,000 processed volume.
*   **Regulatory Thresholds:** In the US, the Financial Crimes Enforcement Network (FinCEN) defines a money transmitter as anyone accepting and transmitting value, with **no minimum dollar threshold** for licensing requirements; one transaction can trigger the obligation. Singapore's Payment Services Act (PSA) similarly regulates "digital payment token services" involving transfer arrangements, though licensing exemptions exist for very small entities (which you may exceed depending on exact local interpretations of "facilitation").
*   **Cost of Non-Compliance:** Civil penalties for unlicensed transmission in the US often start at **$5,000 per violation** (per transaction), potentially totaling **$6M+** in theoretical exposure (1,200 × $5,000), plus criminal liability. Licensing costs (legal, bonding, state-by-state filings) typically range from **$50,000 to $250,000** initially, with ongoing compliance burdens.

**What Flips It, and the Trigger**
The single input that changes your status is **custody**. If your smart contract or wallet holds the user's crypto asset before forwarding it (even for seconds), you are transmitting. If the user sends crypto directly to the auditor's wallet, and you separately charge the auditor (or the user pays you via a non-crypto rail or a licensed processor), you are likely purely a software provider.
*   **Trigger:** The next transaction where your system takes custody of funds.
*   **Rule of Thumb:** "Touching the money" = Financial Intermediary. "Sending an invoice" = Software Vendor.
*   **Step this week:** If you are currently holding funds in a hot wallet or multi-sig before distribution, **freeze this flow immediately** and engage a payments lawyer to redesign the settlement layer to be non-custodial.
