**The Call**
Do not proceed with selling raw blanks as a regulatory loophole until counsel confirms that customer finishing does not trigger "substantial transformation" liability or negate the exemption; the potential $3,000 savings are likely outweighed by the risk of retaining manufacturer liability for a product you knowingly sold incomplete for a specific use.

**What Flips It, and the Trigger**
The deciding factor is the legal definition of "manufacturer" under the **Consumer Product Safety Act (CPSA)**. If the US Consumer Product Safety Commission (CPSC) views the blank as a "component part" intended for a finished good, you may still bear liability for the final product's safety, especially if you market it specifically for skateboarding.
*   **Trigger:** If legal counsel determines that selling a shape-specific blank constitutes placing a "consumer product" into commerce, the $3,000 savings vanish while liability remains.
*   **Rule of Thumb:** If the item has no reasonable use other than becoming the regulated product, regulators often treat it as the regulated product.
