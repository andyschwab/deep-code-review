**The Call**
Proceed with the free tier planning but engage EU antitrust counsel immediately before finalizing the feature gating or publishing the pricing page. Your stated 80% market share among European startups creates a high probability of "dominance" under EU law, triggering strict scrutiny on any pricing strategy that could be construed as exclusionary, regardless of your intent to attract users.

**The Rules and Factors**
Under Article 102 of the Treaty on the Functioning of the European Union (TFEU), the violation is not "having" dominance, but "abusing" it. Regulators and the European Commission generally assess two primary pillars in your scenario:

1.  **Definition of Dominance:** This is not solely about raw market share, though >50% (and certainly ~80%) creates a rebuttable presumption of dominance. Regulators will look at:
    *   **Barriers to Entry:** Can a competitor easily replicate OrbitContainer? If your open-source status creates a network effect or high switching costs that lock users in, dominance is reinforced.
    *   **Countervailing Buyer Power:** Do your startup customers have the leverage to demand better terms or switch quickly? If they are fragmented and dependent on your ecosystem, your power is higher.
    *   **Market Definition:** The regulator may argue your market is broader than "startup orchestration" (e.g., "all container orchestration" including Kubernetes directly or cloud-native alternatives). If the market is defined broadly, your 80% share shrinks. However, if the niche is defined narrowly as you described, the risk is acute.

2.  **Abuse via Pricing Strategies:** Once dominant, you cannot price like a non-dominant firm. The specific risks for a "free tier" include:
    *   **Predatory Pricing:** Selling below cost to eliminate competitors. Since the tier is "free," the price is below cost. The defense usually requires proving the strategy is a legitimate promotional launch for a new product, not an attempt to crush rivals who cannot match $0. If the free features are sufficient to satisfy the market and the paid features are non-essential, regulators may view this as squeezing competitors out of the revenue pool entirely.
    *   **Margin Squeeze:** If you also compete in the enterprise space against others who rely on your open-source core, pricing the core at $0 while charging high fees for integration tools can make it impossible for competitors to survive.
    *   **Tying and Bundling:** If the free tier forces users to accept your enterprise tools for basic functionality, or if the advanced capabilities are technically tied to the free core in a way that forecloses third-party add-ons, this is an abuse.

**What Flips the Decision**
The trigger for mandatory legal engagement is the combination of **confirmed >50% market share** in a defensibly narrow market and a **free tier that replicates the core value proposition** of paid competitors. If your free tier leaves competitors no viable "entry-level" product to sell, the risk shifts from theoretical to actionable.

**Routing Note**
You must engage an EU antitrust attorney to perform a formal market definition analysis and a "meeting competition" defense assessment. Specifically, ask them: "Given our ~80% share in [defined niche], does our proposed feature split between free and enterprise tiers constitute an exclusionary practice under Article 102 TFEU, and what documentation do we need to prove legitimate business justification?" Do not launch the pricing page until this specific question is answered in writing.
