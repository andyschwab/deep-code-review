**The Call**
Yes, you must recognize a liability and an associated expense now; do not wait until refunds are paid. Under standard accrual accounting (e.g., US GAAP/IFRS), the 8% claim rate creates a "probable" and "reasonably estimable" loss that requires an immediate reserve on your balance sheet to present clean books for your seed round.

**The Numbers**
The reserve is calculated as: `Active Customers × Monthly Fee × Estimated Claim Rate`.
Using your Q3 figures:
*   **Active Customers:** 500
*   **Monthly Fee:** $500
*   **Observed Claim Rate:** 8% (0.08)
*   **Monthly Exposure:** 500 × $500 × 0.08 = **$20,000 per month**.

*Assumption (Labelled ASSUMED):* The 8% rate applies to the current active base monthly. If the 8% represents a one-time churn over a specific period rather than a steady-state monthly run-rate, the monthly reserve would be lower. However, for conservative seed-round diligence, investors will expect you to reserve based on the worst-case observed run-rate until proven otherwise.

*Scenario Analysis (Monthly Reserve):*
*   **Base Case (Current Run-rate):** $20,000/month liability accrual.
*   **Optimistic (Rate drops to 4%):** $10,000/month.
*   **Pessimistic (Rate rises to 12%):** $30,000/month.

**What Flips It, and The Trigger**
The single input that changes the call is the **stabilization of the claim rate**. If you can demonstrate to auditors that the 8% was an anomaly caused by early bugs now fixed, you may lower the reserve.
*   **Trigger:** If the claim rate drops below 2% for two consecutive months, recalculate the reserve downward.
*   **Rule of Thumb:** Reserve the higher of (a) the actual claims paid in the prior month or (b) the rolling 3-month average claim rate applied to current revenue.
*   **This Week's Step:** If your claim rate exceeds 5% next month, consider pausing new sales of the "guaranteed" tier or tightening the eligibility criteria until the product stabilizes.
