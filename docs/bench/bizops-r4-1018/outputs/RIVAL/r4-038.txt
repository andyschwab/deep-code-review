Federal bond requirements limit your expansion speed by creating a mandatory cash reserve that grows in direct proportion to your production volume, effectively reducing the working capital available to purchase the new still or fund operations. Under current TTB regulations, you must maintain a surety bond (or cash collateral) covering your estimated tax liability for a specific period. As you increase production from 500 to 1,200 cases, your accrued excise tax liability spikes immediately upon distillation, even though you won't collect revenue until the gin is bottled, aged (if applicable), and sold months later.

This creates a "cash gap" where your tax obligations outpace your cash inflows. You do not necessarily need to post a larger surety bond immediately if your current bond covers the new liability threshold, but if your projected tax liability exceeds your current bond coverage, you must increase it. The cost of this bond is indeed approximately 1-2% annually of the bond amount, which is an operating expense. However, the more critical constraint is not the bond premium cost, but the liquidity requirement. The government does not hold a lien on your inventory that legally prevents you from using it as collateral for a loan; rather, the federal tax lien attaches to all your assets once the tax is due and unpaid. Banks are often hesitant to lend against inventory that is subject to such a superior federal tax lien, especially when the tax bill is accruing faster than sales.

Here is the cash flow impact of your expansion:
*   **Current State:** 500 cases/month. Tax accrues at a steady rate your current $120,000 cash position supports.
*   **Expanded State:** 1,200 cases/month. Your tax liability more than doubles immediately. If your current bond covers only the old volume, you must increase it. More importantly, the cash you need to keep liquid to ensure you can pay these taxes when due (or to cover the bond collateral if you choose cash collateral over surety) increases drastically.
*   **The Crunch:** With a $9 production cost and $28 sales price, your margin is healthy, but the timing is the killer. You are paying $9/bottle + taxes upfront, but waiting for the $28/bottle return. The bond requirement forces you to hold back a larger chunk of your $120,000 as a safety net for taxes, leaving less cash to buy the $85,000 still.

You likely do not need to slow growth if you secure external funding specifically designed for this gap. The bond itself is not the blocker; the lack of liquid cash to cover the accelerated tax cycle is. Seek a working capital line of credit or an equipment loan where the lender understands alcohol tax cycles. Do not use your limited cash reserves for the still if it leaves you under-bonded or unable to pay the imminent tax bill.

Note: Consult a TTB compliance specialist to calculate your exact required bond amount based on the 1,200-case projection before signing loan documents.
Note: Your bank may require a subordination agreement from the TTB to accept inventory as collateral, which is a standard but necessary administrative step.
