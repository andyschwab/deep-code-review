**General Rules on Physical Presence and Tax Nexus in Germany**

For a foreign tech firm like Acme Low-Code, the risk of creating a "permanent establishment" (PE) in Germany—and thus becoming liable for German corporate income tax and trade tax—depends on the nature, duration, and authority of your team's activities onsite. Under the Germany-France Double Taxation Treaty and German domestic law, the following principles apply:

**1. The Construction/Installation Project Threshold (12-Month Rule)**
Germany typically treats a construction, installation, or assembly project as a permanent establishment only if it lasts more than **12 months**. Your four-week onsite engagement for system configuration falls well below this threshold. Generally, short-term technical deployment of this nature does not automatically trigger a corporate PE solely based on the duration of the physical presence.

**2. The "Fixed Place of Business" Risk**
A PE can be created immediately if your employees have a **fixed place of business** at the client's disposal. If Acme's developers are given their own offices, keys, and exclusive workspaces within the Berlin manufacturing plant for the duration of the project, tax authorities *could* argue this constitutes a fixed place of business. However, if they are merely guest users of the client's facilities without exclusive control, this risk is significantly reduced.

**3. The "Dependent Agent" Risk (Highest Concern)**
The most significant risk for service firms is not the duration of the stay, but the **authority** of the personnel. A PE exists if your employees in Germany:
*   Have the authority to **conclude contracts** in the name of Acme Low-Code.
*   Habitually exercise this authority.
*   Perform core business activities (beyond auxiliary or preparatory work).

If your three developers are strictly configuring the system based on a contract already signed by your UK/France headquarters and have no power to negotiate or sign amendments, the "dependent agent" PE risk is low. If they are negotiating scope changes or signing deliverables that legally bind Acme, the risk increases substantially.

**4. VAT vs. Corporate Tax Distinction**
Do not confuse corporate tax nexus with VAT obligations. Even if you do not create a corporate tax PE, the supply of services to a German B2B client usually requires **VAT reverse charge** mechanisms. You must issue invoices without German VAT, stating the reverse charge provision, provided the client is a valid taxable entity in Germany. This is an administrative compliance issue, not a corporate tax liability issue.

**Specific Triggers to Avoid**
To minimize PE risk during your four-week engagement:
*   **Duration:** Keep the onsite period under 6 months (you are at 4 weeks, which is safe).
*   **Authority:** Ensure onsite staff explicitly **cannot sign contracts** or negotiate commercial terms. All contractual authority must remain with the UK/France entity.
*   **Facilities:** Avoid having a dedicated, exclusive office space labeled as "Acme Low-Code." Use shared client spaces or hot-desking arrangements.
*   **Nature of Work:** Ensure the work is framed as "technical implementation" rather than "management" or "core business operations" of the German client.

**Recommendation**
While a four-week technical configuration by non-signatory staff rarely triggers a corporate permanent establishment in Germany, the stakes (back taxes, penalties, and trade tax) are high. The specific wording in your Statement of Work regarding the authority of your onsite team is critical. Given the €250,000 contract value, the cost of a local tax attorney to review the SOW language and confirm your specific operational setup is a prudent investment before finalizing the agreement.
