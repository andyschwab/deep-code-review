Yes, bundling your software subscription with professional services introduces significant accounting complexity under revenue recognition standards (specifically IFRS 15, which applies in Ireland, and ASC 606 in the US). You are correct to be concerned: mixing these distinct performance obligations in a single contract generally requires you to allocate the total transaction price based on their relative Standalone Selling Prices (SSP).

**The Core Accounting Requirement**
Under IFRS 15, your bundle contains two distinct performance obligations:
1.  **Software Subscription:** A service provided over time (recognized ratably over 12 months).
2.  **Professional Services:** A service provided at a point in time or upon completion (recognized when the workshop/design is delivered).

Because you are selling them together for €15,000, you cannot simply recognize €10,000 as software revenue and €5,000 as service revenue unless those exact figures represent their valid Standalone Selling Prices. If you ever sell the software alone for €10,000 and the services alone for €5,000, your current allocation is defensible. However, if the €15,000 bundle represents a discount (e.g., if you normally charge €6,000 for services when sold separately), you must allocate the total €15,000 contract value proportionally based on the SSPs.

**Why This Matters for Your Audit**
If you fail to allocate correctly, you risk misstating revenue in specific periods. For example, if the bundle includes a hidden discount on the software to drive the service sale, recognizing the full software portion immediately or ratably without adjustment could violate the matching principle. Auditors will specifically look for:
*   **SSP Documentation:** Evidence supporting how you determined the standalone price for each component (e.g., historical sales data, competitor pricing, or cost-plus margins).
*   **Allocation Methodology:** A consistent formula applied to every bundled contract to split the €15,000 into two separate revenue buckets.
*   **Timing Differences:** Clear tracking of the allocated software revenue (deferred and released monthly) versus the service revenue (released upon milestone completion).

**Immediate Action Required**
You do not necessarily need to halt sales, but you **must engage your auditor or a qualified revenue recognition specialist immediately** before selling more bundles. With 25 deals already closed this quarter, you have a material volume of contracts that may require retroactive adjustment if your current booking method is non-compliant.

To prepare for this engagement, document the following:
1.  **Standalone Evidence:** Gather data proving the €10k and €5k price points are genuine standalone prices, not arbitrary split numbers.
2.  **Contract Language:** Ensure your contracts clearly distinguish the deliverables and payment terms for each component.
3.  **System Capability:** Verify your billing/ERP system can track two separate revenue schedules for a single invoice line item.

Attempting to self-diagnose the allocation model without external validation creates a high risk of audit qualification. Engaging an expert now to formalize your policy is far cheaper than restating revenue post-audit.
