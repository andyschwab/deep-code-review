### FrostyWheels Stress-Test Framework

**1. Current Unit Economics (Baseline)**
*   **Contribution Margin per Serving:** $5.30 ($8.50 Price - $3.20 Variable Cost)
*   **Daily Fleet Contribution:** $114,480 (12 trucks × 180 servings × $5.30)
*   **Monthly Fixed Costs:** $14,000
*   **Break-Even Volume:** ~2,642 servings/month (approx. 88 servings/day across the fleet).
*   *Observation:* Your current model is highly profitable on "good" days. The risk is entirely concentration-based (weather dependency).

**2. The "Three-Week Poor Weather" Stress Test**
Assuming a "poor weather" day cuts volume by 60% (dropping from 180 to 72 servings/truck):
*   **Daily Revenue (Poor Weather):** $73,440 (12 trucks × 72 servings × $8.50)
*   **Daily Variable Costs:** $27,648 (12 trucks × 72 servings × $3.20)
*   **Daily Contribution Margin:** $45,792
*   **Weekly Contribution (5 operating days):** $228,960
*   **3-Week Contribution:** $686,880
*   **Less 3-Week Fixed Costs:** $10,769 ($14,000 / 4.33 weeks × 3)
*   **Net Cash Flow (3 Weeks Poor Weather):** +$676,111

*Critical Insight:* Even with a 60% volume drop, your unit economics ($5.30 margin) are strong enough that you **do not go negative** in this specific scenario. You remain cash-flow positive because your fixed costs ($14k/mo) are low relative to your volume.

**However**, if your definition of "40% revenue drop" implies a deeper structural decline or if "poor weather" means near-zero sales (e.g., 90% drop), the math changes.
*   **Scenario B (Catastrophic Weather - 90% drop to 18 servings/truck):**
    *   Daily Contribution: $11,448
    *   3-Week Contribution: $171,720
    *   Net Cash Flow: Still positive (+$160k).

*Correction on your premise:* With an $8.50 ticket and only $3.20 variable cost, your margin (62%) is exceptionally high. Unless fixed costs are understated or "poor weather" means **zero** sales for 3 weeks, you do not need a massive cash buffer for survival based on these numbers alone. You need a buffer for **fixed cost coverage during total shutdowns**.
*   **Cash Buffer Needed for Total Shutdown (0 sales):** $10,769 (to cover 3 weeks of fixed costs).

**3. Daily Data Points to Track**
To dynamically adjust purchasing and staffing, track these three metrics daily by 10:00 AM:
1.  **Real-Time Weather Delta:** Compare forecasted temp/precipitation vs. actuals. If rain starts, trigger immediate reduction in dairy prep.
2.  **Hourly Sell-Through Rate:** Servings sold per hour vs. target. If <50% of target by 2:00 PM, halt mobile production and redirect staff to cleaning/maintenance (non-variable cost time).
3.  **Perishable Waste %:** Track dairy discarded due to melt or over-prep. Goal: <2%. If >5%, reduce next day's base prep by 15%.

**4. Operational Pause Threshold**
Pause operations when the **Projected Daily Contribution Margin < Daily Variable Labor Cost**.
*   Since your variable cost ($3.20) includes dairy/fuel, ensure your "staffing" cost is flexible. If staff are hourly and sent home early, your true variable cost drops.
*   **Hard Stop Rule:** If forecast predicts >80% chance of rain AND temperatures <60°F, deploy only 25% of the fleet (3 trucks) to test demand, keeping the rest docked to save fuel and labor. Do not run the full fleet unless the forecast confidence for sun is >70%.

*Note: Your high margin suggests your primary risk is inventory waste, not fixed cost coverage. Focus on just-in-time purchasing.*
*Ensure your "variable costs" truly include all hourly labor; if labor is fixed, your break-even volume rises significantly.*
