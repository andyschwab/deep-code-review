Your current flat per-employee model is unsustainable because it decouples your revenue from your highest cost driver: site remoteness. By charging a flat $25 while costs swing from $8 to $18, you are effectively subsidizing remote sites with profits from urban ones. On a remote job costing $18, your margin is only 28% ($7 profit); on an $8 job, it's 68% ($17 profit). This volatility prevents predictable scaling and crushes margins on smaller, remote contracts where the $1,500 minimum may not cover the fixed logistics overhead.

To stabilize margins at your target 30%, you must shift to a **Base Fee + Variable Rate** model. This separates your fixed operational costs (scheduling, travel logistics, admin) from the variable cost of the actual screening.

### The New Pricing Model Structure

**1. The Base Fee (Covers Fixed Costs & Margin)**
Instead of a hard "$1,500 minimum," implement a mobilization fee per site. This ensures every job contributes to overhead regardless of volume.
*   **Recommended Base Fee:** $600 per site.
*   **Rationale:** This covers the initial logistics dispatch. If a site is remote, this fee absorbs the travel burden before the first employee is even tested.

**2. The Variable Rate (Covers Test Cost + Margin)**
Charge a lower per-employee rate, as the base fee now covers the heavy lifting.
*   **Recommended Variable Rate:** $18 per employee.
*   **Rationale:** Even if your cost hits the maximum of $18, you break even on the variable portion. All profit comes from the Base Fee and the spread when costs are lower than $18.

### Break-Even and Margin Modeling

To maintain a **30% gross margin**, your formula for any given job is:
$$Revenue = Base Fee + (Rate \times Volume)$$
$$Target Cost = Revenue \times 0.70$$

**Scenario A: Small Remote Job (High Cost)**
*   *Context:* 40 employees, Remote location (Cost = $18/test).
*   *Old Model:* Rev $1,500 (min) | Cost $720 | Profit $780 (52% margin). *Wait, this looks profitable, but only because the minimum was high enough. If volume drops to 20?*
*   *Risk Case (20 employees, Remote):*
    *   Old Model: Rev $1,500 | Cost $360 | Profit $1,140. (Still high margin due to minimum).
    *   *Correction:* The issue arises when the $1,500 minimum isn't triggered or costs exceed assumptions on mid-sized jobs. Let's look at the volatility risk. If you have a job with 60 employees at $18 cost:
    *   Old Model: Rev $1,500 ($25x60) | Cost $1,080 | Profit $420 (28% margin). **Below Target.**
    *   **New Model:** Base $600 + ($18 x 60) = $1,680 Revenue.
    *   Cost: $1,080.
    *   Profit: $600.
    *   **Margin:** 35.7%. **Target Met.**

**Scenario B: Large Urban Job (Low Cost)**
*   *Context:* 200 employees, Urban location (Cost = $8/test).
*   *Old Model:* Rev $5,000 | Cost $1,600 | Profit $3,400 (68% margin).
*   **New Model:** Base $600 + ($18 x 200) = $4,200 Revenue.
*   Cost: $1,600.
*   Profit: $2,600.
*   **Margin:** 61.9%.
*   *Analysis:* You earn slightly less absolute dollars here, but you protect your downside on remote jobs. The volume discount is implicit in the removal of the high per-unit fee.

### Implementation Strategy

1.  **Reprice Immediately:** Move to **$600 Site Mobilization Fee + $19/employee**.
    *   At $19, you capture $1 profit per test even at max cost ($18), while the base fee secures the 30% overall margin.
2.  **Define "Site":** Clearly define what constitutes a separate site (e.g., >5 miles apart) to prevent customers from bundling remote locations to avoid multiple base fees.
3.  **Grandfathering:** Apply this only to new contracts. For the 120 existing sites, analyze their historical volume vs. location. If a client consistently runs small, remote jobs, they are currently unprofitable relative to risk; renegotiate or let them churn.

This model aligns your revenue with your cost structure: logistics are paid by the base fee, and testing is paid by the volume.

*Note: Ensure your contracts specify that the "Base Fee" is non-refundable after scheduling to protect against last-minute cancellations which spike effective costs.*
*Consider adding a fuel surcharge clause if gas prices volatility exceeds 15% to further insulate margins.*
