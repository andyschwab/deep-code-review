### Specific Risks in Your Current Setup

Your current model—a 15% facilitation fee taken from every session booked—presents significant exposure under the **Federal Anti-Kickback Statute (AKS)**. The core risk is that your fee could be interpreted as remuneration paid to induce referrals for services reimbursable by federal healthcare programs (Medicare/Medicaid).

1.  **The "Per-Unit" Problem:** Charging a percentage of the reimbursement amount (or a flat fee per visit) is often viewed as varying directly with the volume or value of referrals. If a therapist sends you more patients, they "earn" the platform's service, but legally, the arrangement looks like you are paying them (via the platform's existence and marketing) to generate billable events, or conversely, they are paying you a kickback to receive those referrals.
2.  **Lack of Safe Harbor Fit:** Most AKS safe harbors require compensation to be set in advance, not determined by the volume of referrals. A percentage-of-revenue model rarely fits the "Personal Services and Management Contracts" safe harbor because the total aggregate payment fluctuates based on referral volume.
3.  **Enforcement Threshold:** There is no specific dollar "threshold" where fees become problematic. Liability is intent-based. However, enforcement scrutiny increases when the compensation model creates a financial incentive to increase utilization of services rather than focusing on patient need. With $1.2M in the bank, a federal investigation alone could deplete your reserves through legal defense costs, regardless of the final outcome.

### Alternative Compensation Structures

To align with Safe Harbor provisions without becoming a direct employer, consider restructuring toward models where compensation is **fixed in advance** and **independent of referral volume**.

**1. Fixed Monthly Subscription (SaaS Model)**
Instead of a per-transaction fee, charge therapists a flat monthly fee for access to the platform's scheduling, billing, and marketing tools.
*   **Why it helps:** The fee is set in advance and does not change based on how many patients the therapist sees or refers through the platform. This aligns closer to the **Personal Services and Management Contracts Safe Harbor**, provided the aggregate compensation is set in advance, the term is at least one year, and the services are commercially reasonable.
*   **Implementation:** Calculate your average revenue per therapist ($30/session × avg sessions/month) and set a flat fee slightly below or equal to that value, ensuring it reflects fair market value (FMV) for the software/services provided, not the value of referrals.

**2. Fair Market Value (FMV) Administrative Fee**
Charge a flat fee per administrative task completed (e.g., $5 for scheduling, $10 for claims processing) rather than a percentage of the clinical session.
*   **Why it helps:** You are charging for specific, identifiable administrative services rendered, not for the referral itself. The key is that the fee must reflect the actual cost and FMV of the administrative work, not a share of the clinical reimbursement.
*   **Requirement:** You must document the time/cost required for these tasks to prove the fee is not a disguised kickback.

**3. Patient-Paid Convenience Fee**
Shift the fee entirely to the family/patient as a "convenience" or "booking" fee for using the platform to find and schedule care, rather than deducting from the therapist's reimbursement.
*   **Why it helps:** This removes the financial flow between the provider and the platform regarding the federal reimbursement. However, you must ensure this fee is not passed on to federal programs and is clearly disclosed to private-pay or commercially insured patients.

### Immediate Next Steps
*   **Stop Percentage Billing:** Move away from % of reimbursement immediately.
*   **Document FMV:** Engage a third-party consultant to document the Fair Market Value of your platform's services to justify any flat fees.
*   **Review Contracts:** Ensure all therapist agreements specify a term of at least one year and define services clearly, as required by safe harbors.
