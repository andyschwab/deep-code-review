### Regulatory Assessment

Based on your description, MenoCircle likely operates as a **service marketplace** rather than a money transmitter, but specific operational details determine this classification.

**Money Transmitter Laws:**
In the US, money transmission generally requires the "acceptance of value from one person for the purpose of transmitting it to another." However, most states (including Florida, Illinois, and Washington) provide exemptions for **agents of payees** or **platforms facilitating specific goods/services**. If your platform:
1. Does not take possession of funds (users pay directly via a third-party processor like Stripe Connect), OR
2. Holds funds only momentarily as an agent for the service provider (the advisor) before payout, AND
3. The transaction is strictly for a specific service (health advice) rather than general value transfer,

...you likely fall under the "agent of payee" exemption and do not need a money transmitter license. The $15 subscription fee you charge is clearly revenue for platform access, not money transmission.

**Key Risk Factors:**
- **Commingling Funds:** If you hold user funds in a corporate account before paying advisors, regulators may view this as transmission.
- **Discretion:** If you decide *when* or *if* to pay advisors (rather than automatic payouts upon service completion), this increases regulatory scrutiny.
- **Health Advice Regulation:** While not financial regulation, facilitating paid medical/health advice may trigger scrutiny from health boards or the FTC regarding unlicensed medical practice claims, especially if advisors claim to "treat" conditions.

**Transaction Volume Thresholds:**
There is **no safe harbor threshold** for money transmission registration. If your activity constitutes money transmission, you theoretically need licensing from day one, regardless of volume. However, the $4,500 monthly volume you described is generally below the radar for aggressive enforcement *if* your model fits the marketplace exemption. The federal MSB (Money Services Business) registration with FinCEN is required if you transmit over $1,000 in a day for one person, but this usually applies to currency exchange or pure money movement, not service marketplaces.
