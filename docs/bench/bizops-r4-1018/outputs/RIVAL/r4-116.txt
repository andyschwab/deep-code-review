**GDPR Compliance Analysis for SafeStay GPS Feature**

Adding GPS tracking for dementia patients in the EU does not provide legal exemptions; rather, it significantly elevates your regulatory risk profile. Geolocation data is classified as personal data, and when combined with health status (dementia), it constitutes "special category data" under Article 9 of the GDPR. This triggers strict prohibitions on processing unless specific, narrow exceptions apply.

**1. Legal Basis and Exemptions**
There is no blanket exemption for dementia care. You cannot rely on "consent" (Article 6/9) as a primary basis because residents with dementia often lack the legal capacity to give informed, specific, and freely withdrawable consent. While guardians can consent, EU regulators view consent in care settings as inherently imbalanced (coercive), making it a fragile legal foundation for high-risk features.

Your viable legal basis is likely **Article 9(2)(h)** (processing necessary for preventive medicine, medical diagnosis, or provision of health/social care) combined with **Article 9(2)(c)** (vital interests) for immediate safety. However, this requires the processing to be strictly necessary for the specific care objective, not just a "nice-to-have" premium add-on. If the feature is marketed as a €10 upsell, regulators may argue it is a commercial product rather than essential care, weakening the "vital interest" defense.

**2. Mandatory Steps Before Launch**
You cannot launch this feature without completing the following:

*   **Data Protection Impact Assessment (DPIA):** This is mandatory. Because you are systematically monitoring vulnerable individuals on a large scale (50 facilities) using new technology (GPS), you meet the threshold for a DPIA under Article 35. You must document the necessity, proportionality, and risks to residents' rights.
*   **Supervisory Authority Consultation:** If your DPIA indicates high residual risk that cannot be mitigated (e.g., potential for mass surveillance or data breaches), you must consult your lead Supervisory Authority (likely in Germany or France depending on your main establishment) *before* processing begins.
*   **Explicit Legal Grounds Documentation:** You must formally document which specific Article 9 exception applies per resident. Relying on "legitimate interest" is insufficient for special category data.
*   **Technical Safeguards:** Implement "Privacy by Design." This includes:
    *   **Geofencing:** Limit tracking to specific perimeters rather than continuous global tracking.
    *   **Data Minimization:** Store location history only as long as absolutely necessary (e.g., delete after 24 hours unless an incident occurs).
    *   **Access Controls:** Strictly limit which staff members can view real-time location data.

**3. Risk Assessment**
Classifying this as a "premium add-on" increases your liability. If a breach occurs, the fine calculation (up to 4% of global turnover) will consider that you introduced a high-risk feature for commercial gain without adequate safeguards. With €3M revenue, a maximum fine could be €120,000, but reputational damage could lose your contracts across Germany and France.

**Conclusion**
The feature is not impossible, but it is legally perilous without a completed DPIA and a shift from "consent-based" to "care-necessity" legal framing. Do not market this as a commercial upsell; frame it strictly as a clinical safety tool integrated into the care plan. Engage a specialized EU data protection officer immediately to draft the DPIA before writing a single line of GPS code.

*Note: This analysis is based on general GDPR frameworks and does not constitute legal advice. You must consult qualified EU legal counsel specializing in health data.*
