**Regulatory Reclassification and Tax Liability**
Yes, under current federal regulations enforced by the Alcohol and Tobacco Tax and Trade Bureau (TTB), if your product tests above 0.5% Alcohol By Volume (ABV), it is legally reclassified as an alcoholic beverage. The 0.5% threshold is a strict statutory limit for non-alcoholic classification, not a target with a functional tolerance for tax purposes. If a batch tests at 0.55% ABV, that entire batch is subject to federal excise taxes applicable to beer (or malt beverages), which are significantly higher than the negligible or non-existent taxes on non-alcoholic products.

**Retroactive Taxes and Penalties**
If the TTB determines via random testing or audit that your product exceeded 0.5% ABV at the time of sale, you are liable for the unpaid excise taxes on those specific batches retroactively. Because you sold $18,000 worth of product last month, and three batches tested at 0.55%, you face potential liability for the tax difference on those units. Furthermore, selling a product labeled as "non-alcoholic" that actually contains >0.5% ABV can be viewed as a labeling violation and tax evasion, potentially triggering civil penalties and fines in addition to the back taxes. There is no automatic "natural fermentation" exemption that waives tax liability once the threshold is crossed.

**Required Reporting Steps**
You do not simply "report" a variance on your next standard filing if you have already sold the product as non-alcoholic. The correct compliance path generally involves:
1.  **Immediate Cessation:** Stop distributing any inventory from the affected batches immediately.
2.  **Voluntary Disclosure:** Contact the TTB National Revenue Center or your local TTB investigator to disclose the testing results voluntarily before they discover it during an audit. Voluntary disclosure often mitigates penalty severity.
3.  **Amended Returns:** You will likely need to file amended excise tax returns (TTB Form 5000.24) for the periods in question, reclassifying the product as alcoholic and paying the due tax.
4.  **Labeling Correction:** You cannot sell the remaining inventory as "non-alcoholic." You must either destroy the product, re-label it as an alcoholic beverage (which requires formula approval and a different label), or blend it down (if technically feasible and approved) to meet the <0.5% standard before release.
