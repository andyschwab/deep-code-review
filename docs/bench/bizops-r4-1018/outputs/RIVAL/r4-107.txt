### Core Legal Principle: Location of the Patient
In almost all US jurisdictions, the practice of nursing is legally defined as occurring **where the patient is located**, not where the nurse is sitting. If your Ohio-based RN supervises care for a patient in Kentucky, the RN is legally "practicing nursing in Kentucky." Consequently, the Ohio RN generally must hold an active license in Kentucky to avoid charges of Unauthorized Practice of Nursing (UPN).

### Key Regulatory Hurdles by Concept

**1. Licensure Portability (The Compact Issue)**
*   **Ohio:** Member of the Nurse Licensure Compact (NLC).
*   **Kentucky:** Member of the NLC.
*   **Indiana:** Member of the NLC.
*   **West Virginia:** Member of the NLC.
*   **Pennsylvania:** **NOT** a member of the NLC.
*   **Implication:** Your Ohio RNs can likely supervise patients in KY, IN, and WV using their Ohio "multistate" license, provided they declare KY, IN, or WV as their primary state of residence if they move there, or adhere to specific telehealth registration rules if remaining in Ohio. However, for **Pennsylvania**, your Ohio RNs **must** obtain a specific single-state PA license. Practicing in PA with only an OH license is illegal.

**2. Supervision Ratios and "Telemetry"**
State boards often dictate maximum patient-to-nurse ratios for supervision. These ratios frequently tighten when the supervision is remote versus on-site.
*   **Remote Supervision Definitions:** Some states define "supervision" as requiring physical presence for certain tasks (e.g., initial assessment, care plan development), allowing remote check-ins only for ongoing monitoring.
*   **Telemetry/Data Review:** If your app transmits patient data for the RN to make clinical decisions, this is practice. If the RN is merely reviewing logs without intervening, some states may classify this differently, but most boards err on the side of caution: if clinical judgment is applied, a license in the patient's state is required.

**3. Corporate Practice of Medicine/Nursing (CPOM)**
Some states restrict non-nurses (or non-local entities) from employing nurses or directing clinical care. While less common for home care agencies than physician groups, you must verify if your Ohio entity can directly employ staff or direct care in PA, WV, KY, and IN, or if you need local professional entities.

### State-Specific Operational Playbook Considerations

*   **Pennsylvania (Highest Risk):** Since PA is not in the Compact, you cannot rely on multistate privileges. You must budget for and manage individual licensure for every RN supervising PA patients. PA also has strict rules regarding the frequency of supervisory visits (often requiring periodic on-site visits by the RN, not just remote app usage).
*   **Kentucky, Indiana, West Virginia:** While in the Compact, you must verify if these states have specific **telehealth registration** requirements for out-of-state providers even with a compact license. Some states require notification to the board before initiating telehealth services.
*   **Care Plan Authority:** Verify if an RN located outside the state can *initially* authorize a care plan. Many states require the initial assessment and plan sign-off to be performed by a nurse licensed in and physically present in the state.
