### Executive Analysis: Price Increase vs. Packaging Investment

**1. Breakage Rate Benchmark**
An **8% breakage rate is anomalously high**. For handmade ceramics shipped via standard carriers (UPS/FedEx) in the US, the industry standard for well-packaged goods is typically **1% to 3%**. At 8%, you are experiencing a critical operational failure, not a standard cost of doing business. This suggests your current $12 packaging solution is insufficient for the rigors of national logistics, regardless of the product's fragility.

**2. Financial Impact of Current Operations**
*   **Current Margin per Set:** $120 (Price) - $45 (COGS) - $12 (Packaging) = **$63**.
*   **Breakage Cost per Unit Sold:** With an 8% failure rate, you effectively lose the full cost of goods and shipping on 8 out of every 100 units. Your stated $3,600 loss on 400 units confirms an average loss of **$9 per unit sold** ($3,600 / 400).
*   **True Net Margin:** $63 (Gross Margin) - $9 (Breakage Loss) = **$54 per set**.
*   **Monthly Profit:** 400 units * $54 = **$21,600**.

**3. Scenario A: Raising Price to $135 (Operational Status Quo)**
If you raise the price but keep the 8% breakage rate:
*   **New Margin per Set:** $135 - $45 - $12 = **$78**.
*   **New True Net Margin:** $78 - $9 (Breakage) = **$69**.
*   **Volume Sensitivity:** To match your current total profit of $21,600, you would need to sell **313 units** ($21,600 / $69).
*   **Risk:** This allows for a **21.75% drop in sales volume** before you lose money compared to today. However, with an LTV of only $210, losing customers due to a 12.5% price hike is dangerous. If volume drops by more than 22%, this strategy fails. Furthermore, you are still shipping broken goods, damaging your brand reputation.

**4. Scenario B: Investing in Packaging (Price Holds at $120)**
Assume upgrading to premium packaging (e.g., molded pulp, double-boxing) costs **$18 per box** (+$6 increase) but reduces breakage to a standard **2%**.
*   **New Packaging Cost:** $18.
*   **New Margin per Set:** $120 - $45 - $18 = **$57**.
*   **New Breakage Cost:** At 2% failure, the loss drops to approx. **$2.25 per unit** (estimated based on proportional reduction of the $9 loss).
*   **New True Net Margin:** $57 - $2.25 = **$54.75**.
*   **Result:** You achieve a slightly higher net margin ($54.75 vs $54.00) *without* raising prices or risking volume loss. Additionally, you eliminate the reputational damage of sending broken items.

**Recommendation**
**Do not raise the price yet.** An 8% breakage rate is an operational emergency, not a pricing issue. Raising prices to subsidize poor packaging is a flawed strategy that risks churn in a low-LTV environment.

**Immediate Action Plan:**
1.  **Allocate Capital:** Use **$2,400** of your $20,000 working capital to immediately upgrade packaging materials (approx. $6 extra/unit for next month's volume).
2.  **Target Metric:** Aim to reduce breakage to <3%.
3.  **Re-evaluate:** Once breakage is under 3%, your margins will naturally expand. *Then* consider a price increase to $135 if demand remains strong, using the extra margin for marketing or profit.

Fix the leak in the bucket before trying to fill it faster.
