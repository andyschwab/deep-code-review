### Unit Economics Evaluation: HeartBeat Wearables

**Current Model Status: High Risk / Likely Unprofitable**

Based on your provided costs and churn assumptions, your current model faces a significant profitability gap before accounting for operating expenses.

**1. The Break-Even Lifespan**
To recover your $150 hardware subsidy, a customer must generate $17 in monthly gross margin ($25 revenue - $8 cloud cost).
*   **Months to break even on hardware:** $150 / $17 = **8.82 months**.
*   A customer must stay subscribed for **9 full months** just to return the cash spent on manufacturing their device. They generate $0 profit until month 10.

**2. Impact of Current Churn (6%)**
At a 6% monthly churn rate, your average customer lifespan is approximately **16.7 months** (calculated as 1 / 0.06).
*   **Total Gross Margin per Customer:** 16.7 months × $17 = **$283.90**.
*   **Net Profit per Customer:** $283.90 (margin) - $150 (hardware) = **$133.90**.

While this shows a positive unit contribution of ~$134, this margin is dangerously thin. It must cover all other costs (support, marketing, R&D, overhead) from a $750k runway. If your Customer Acquisition Cost (CAC) is even $100, your LTV:CAC ratio is barely above 1:1, which is unsustainable for a hardware startup. Furthermore, if actual lifespan drops to 12 months (common in wearables), your profit per unit collapses to $54, likely resulting in a net loss once operational costs are applied.

**3. Maximum Tolerable Churn Rate**
To determine when the model "breaks" (i.e., fails to recover hardware costs within a reasonable timeframe or yields negligible margin), we look at the lifespan required to generate a viable return.
*   If we define "breaking" as failing to generate at least $300 in total gross margin (a bare minimum to cover CAC and overhead):
    *   Required Lifespan: $300 / $17 = 17.6 months.
    *   **Max Churn Rate:** 1 / 17.6 = **5.7%**.
*   If we define "breaking" strictly as never recovering the hardware cost (Lifespan < 9 months):
    *   **Max Churn Rate:** 1 / 9 = **11.1%**.

**Verdict:** Your current 6% churn is just below the catastrophic failure point but leaves almost no room for error. You are operating in a "zombie" zone where you recover costs but struggle to scale.

**Strategic Recommendations**

1.  **Reduce Churn Immediately (Priority #1):** A drop from 6% to 4% increases average lifespan from 16.7 to 25 months. This nearly doubles your lifetime margin per user from $284 to $425, transforming your unit economics without changing prices.
2.  **Add a Hardware Markup:** Selling at cost is risky. A $50 markup ($200 price) reduces the subsidy recovery time to 6 months. This creates an immediate cash buffer and lowers the break-even lifespan, making the business resilient to higher churn.
3.  **Raise Subscription Price:** Increasing the subscription to $30/month raises monthly margin to $22. This reduces the hardware recovery time to 6.8 months and significantly boosts lifetime value.

**Conclusion:** Do not commit the full $750k to mass production yet. The 6% churn rate makes the "hardware at cost" strategy too fragile. Implement a $30–$50 hardware markup immediately to de-risk the cash flow, and focus your pre-launch efforts entirely on engagement features that drive churn below 4%.

*Note: These calculations assume constant churn and do not factor in discount rates or taxes. Actual cash flow timing may vary based on payment collection cycles.*
