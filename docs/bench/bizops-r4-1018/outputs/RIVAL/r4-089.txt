You do **not** need to renegotiate your existing Data Processing Agreements (DPAs) with your 200 B2B clients, nor does launching a B2C tier invalidate your current compliance framework for them.

Your existing DPAs govern the specific data processing relationship between HookHub (Processor) and your B2B clients (Controllers). These agreements remain valid because the nature of the data *they* send you has not changed; they are still sending business data under the original terms. The introduction of a new customer segment (individual developers) creates a separate, parallel data processing stream. The personal data belonging to B2C users is controlled by those individual users, not by your existing B2B corporate clients. Therefore, the rights and obligations defined in your B2B contracts are unaffected by the addition of a consumer product line.

Instead of renegotiation, you should execute the following three steps to maintain compliance:

1.  **Draft a Separate B2C DPA and Terms of Service:** Create a new legal framework specifically for the €15/month tier. This document must address consumer-specific GDPR rights, including self-service mechanisms for data portability, the right to be forgotten, and direct subject access requests. Since B2C users act as their own Data Controllers, this agreement must be click-wrap accessible during signup, distinct from your B2B negotiation process.
2.  **Update Your Internal Record of Processing Activities (RoPA):** Under GDPR Article 30, you must update your internal compliance documentation to reflect this new processing activity. You are now processing two distinct categories of data: B2B data (governed by existing DPAs) and B2C personal data (governed by the new consumer terms). Your RoPA must explicitly list the new purposes, data categories, and retention schedules for the consumer tier.
3.  **Review Privacy Policy Scope:** Update your public-facing Privacy Policy to clearly distinguish between how you handle data for business clients versus individual consumers. While the B2B DPA remains private between you and the corporate client, the Privacy Policy is the public notice that must cover all processing activities. Ensure it explicitly states that individual developers have direct rights to manage their data via a self-service dashboard, a feature not present in the B2B workflow.

Your compliance framework is modular, not monolithic. Adding a new module (B2C) does not break the existing ones (B2B) provided the data flows remain segregated and the specific contractual obligations for each controller type are met independently.

Note: Ensure your technical architecture logically separates B2B and B2C data to prevent accidental cross-application of data rights requests. Consult qualified EU legal counsel to finalize the specific wording of the new B2C agreements.
