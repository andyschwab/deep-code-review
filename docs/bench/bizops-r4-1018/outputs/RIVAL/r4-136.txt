You likely face immediate contract rejection if your current hosting agreement only specifies "AES-256" without explicitly stating "FIPS 140-2 validated modules." Specifying the algorithm (AES-256) is not legally equivalent to specifying the validation standard (FIPS 140-2) in government contracts.

You generally do **not** need to migrate your entire database to a new host. Most major cloud providers operating in Virginia (such as AWS, Azure, or Google Cloud) offer FIPS 140-2 validated endpoints as a configuration option or a specific compliance add-on within their existing infrastructure.

Here is the clarification on your status and next steps:

**1. The Compliance Gap**
Your current setup almost certainly fails the strict legal requirement. "AES-256" describes the mathematical encryption method, while "FIPS 140-2" certifies that the specific software/hardware module implementing that method has been tested and validated by NIST. Government mandates for health information exchanges (HIE) almost exclusively require the validation certificate, not just the algorithm. Without explicit written confirmation from your provider that your specific instances are using FIPS-validated modules, you are non-compliant.

**2. Migration vs. Configuration**
Migration is rarely necessary. Cloud providers typically host FIPS-validated modules on the same physical hardware as standard instances. The difference is usually a matter of:
*   **Enabling a specific endpoint:** Switching your application connection strings to a FIPS-specific URL provided by the host.
*   **Selecting a compliant instance type:** Changing your VM configuration to a specific series that runs FIPS-validated cryptography.
*   **Purchasing a compliance add-on:** Some providers bundle this under a "Government Cloud" or "High Compliance" tier.

**3. Immediate Action Plan**
Do not assume compliance. Contact your cloud provider's sales or compliance team immediately with this specific query: "Can you provide the FIPS 140-2 validation certificate number for the specific encryption modules used in our current Virginia region instances, and what configuration changes are required to activate them?"

If they can provide the certificate and a path to enable it via configuration, you can satisfy the HIE contract without data migration. If they cannot provide a valid certificate for their Virginia infrastructure, only then would a full migration to a different provider (or a specific government-cloud region) be required.

Note: Do not sign the HIE contract until you have the FIPS certificate in hand; verbal assurances are insufficient for legal indemnity.
Note: Engage your legal counsel to review the specific wording of the "exclusively" clause before making technical changes.
