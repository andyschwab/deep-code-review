Based on standard accounting principles (ASC 606 / IFRS 15), you generally **cannot** recognize the entire $12,000 upfront upon signing. Because your customers do not receive access to the software until the three-month implementation is complete, the performance obligation is not satisfied at the signing date.

Here is the correct treatment for your financial statements:

### 1. Timing of Recognition
Revenue must be **deferred** upon signing and recognized **ratably over the service period** during which the customer actually consumes the benefit.
*   **The Implementation Phase:** Since employees cannot log in during the three-month customization period, no software service is being delivered. Therefore, **$0 revenue** is recognized during these three months.
*   **The Service Period:** Revenue recognition begins only when the software goes live (likely January, given an October start). The $12,000 should be recognized evenly over the 12 months of actual access.
*   **Monthly Recognition Rate:** $1,000 per month ($12,000 / 12 months), starting from the go-live date.

For the five contracts signed this quarter totaling $60,000:
*   **Current Quarter (Oct–Dec):** $0 revenue recognized. The full $60,000 sits on your balance sheet as **Deferred Revenue** (a liability).
*   **Next Fiscal Year:** You will recognize revenue only for the months the system is active. If implementation finishes in January, you will recognize $12,000 per contract over the following 12 months.

### 2. Is Implementation a Separate Performance Obligation?
You asked if the setup service is a separate obligation requiring price allocation. Under ASC 606, activities are separate obligations only if the customer can benefit from them on their own or with other readily available resources.
*   **Likely Outcome:** In most SaaS models, customization is highly specific to your platform and provides no standalone value to the client without the software license. Therefore, the implementation is usually **not distinct**. It is considered a "fulfillment cost" or a setup activity required to make the software ready for use.
*   **Accounting Impact:** You do **not** need to allocate a portion of the $12,000 to the implementation service. Instead, the entire $12,000 is treated as the transaction price for the software access, recognized over the access period as described above.
*   **Costs:** The costs incurred by Sam's team during those three months (salaries, etc.) should be capitalized as "Contract Fulfillment Costs" on the balance sheet and amortized over the same period as the revenue, rather than expensed immediately.

### Summary for Your Board
*   **Q4 Revenue Impact:** $0 from these new deals.
*   **Balance Sheet:** $60,000 recorded as Deferred Revenue.
*   **Future P&L:** $1,000/month per contract recognized starting at go-live.
*   **Rationale:** Revenue is recognized when control transfers to the customer. Control transfers only when the system is live and usable, not when the contract is signed.

*Note: While this reflects standard GAAP/IFRS principles for SaaS, you should validate the specific "distinctness" of your implementation services with your external auditors, as highly modular setups can sometimes alter this treatment.*
