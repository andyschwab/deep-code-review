**The Accounting Reality**
When a customer purchases compute credits, you have received cash but have not yet delivered the service. In accounting terms, this creates a **performance obligation**. Until those build minutes are consumed or the credits expire, that money is not revenue; it is a **liability** known as "Deferred Revenue" (or Unearned Revenue).

By recognizing the $300,000 in unused credits as immediate revenue, you are currently:
1.  **Overstating Revenue:** Your top-line growth looks artificially inflated.
2.  **Understating Liabilities:** Your balance sheet hides a $300,000 debt owed in services.
3.  **Distorting Cash Flow:** While cash flow from operations increases (cash came in), the mismatch between recognized revenue and delivered service creates a dangerous illusion of profitability and sustainability.

**How Similar Companies Handle This**
SaaS and usage-based infrastructure companies (like AWS, Vercel, or Stripe) strictly follow the "consumption model" for prepaid credits:
*   **Sale Event:** Cash increases, Deferred Revenue (Liability) increases. No Revenue is recorded.
*   **Consumption Event:** As the customer runs a build and burns credits, you move the proportional amount from Deferred Revenue to Recognized Revenue.
*   **Expiration (Breakage):** If credits have an expiry date (your 18-month window), you can only recognize the value of unused expired credits as revenue *after* the likelihood of redemption becomes remote (usually at expiry), not upfront.
