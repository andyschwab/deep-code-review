### Financial Impact Analysis: Acme Roasters

**Current Unit Economics (Single-Bag Order)**
*   **Revenue:** $22.00 (Bean) + $6.00 (Shipping Charge) = **$28.00**
*   **Variable Costs:** $9.00 (COGS) + $8.50 (Actual Shipping) = **$17.50**
*   **Contribution Margin:** $28.00 - $17.50 = **+$10.50 per order**

**Verdict:** You are **not** losing money on single-bag shipments. Each order contributes $10.50 toward your fixed overhead and profit. The fear that shipping costs "outweigh" the margin is incorrect; while the shipping subsidy ($2.50 loss on the shipping line item) reduces your total margin, the transaction remains profitable.

**Monthly Profitability Snapshot**
Based on last month's volume (1,200 bags total):
*   **Single-bag orders (85%):** 1,020 orders × $10.50 margin = $10,710
*   **Multi-bag orders (15%):** 180 bags. Assuming these are mostly 2-bag orders (~90 orders):
    *   Revenue: (2 × $22) + $6 = $50
    *   Cost: (2 × $9) + ~$9.50 (estimated slightly higher shipping) = $27.50
    *   Margin: ~$22.50 × 90 orders = $2,025
*   **Total Contribution Margin:** ~$12,735
*   **Fixed Overhead:** -$4,000
*   **Estimated Net Profit:** **+$8,735**

Your current model is sustainable and profitable. The shipping subsidy is a drag on efficiency, not a cause of loss.

**Strategic Recommendations**
While you are profitable, you are leaving money on the table. The $2.50 subsidy per single-bag order cost you **$2,550 last month** (1,020 orders × $2.50). Capturing this would increase your net profit by nearly 30%.

1.  **Do not enforce a minimum order value yet.** Since single-bag orders are your volume driver (85%) and are profitable, a hard minimum could drastically reduce order volume and total profit.
2.  **Adjust Shipping Fees Immediately.** Increase flat-rate shipping to **$8.99** or **$9.99**.
    *   At $8.99, you cover costs and gain $0.49 per order.
    *   At $9.99, you generate $1.49 profit on the shipping line itself.
    *   Coffee customers often accept shipping fees up to $10 if the product quality is high.
3.  **Incentivize Volume Instead of Punishing Small Orders.** Keep single-bag availability but offer **free shipping on orders over $45** (3 bags). This encourages customers to add a second or third bag to "save" on shipping, increasing your average order value (AOV) and spreading the fixed shipping cost over more units.

**Conclusion:** Stop the bleeding by raising shipping rates to match reality, but do not block single-bag purchases. Your unit economics are positive; you simply need to capture the full margin you are currently subsidizing.
