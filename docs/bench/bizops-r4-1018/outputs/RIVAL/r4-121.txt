### Financial Viability Analysis

Based on the figures provided, your current growth strategy is **mathematically profitable but cash-flow dangerous** given your six-month runway.

**The Unit Economics (Long-Term View)**
Your Lifetime Value (LTV) and Customer Acquisition Cost (CAC) ratio looks healthy on paper:
*   **Monthly Margin:** $45 (Revenue) - $8 (Cost) = **$37** per user/month.
*   **Lifetime Value:** $37 × 14 months = **$518**.
*   **LTV:CAC Ratio:** $518 : $200 = **2.6x**.

An LTV:CAC ratio above 3.0x is ideal, but 2.6x is generally considered viable for a subscription business. In a vacuum, you are building value with every sale.

**The Cash Flow Reality (Immediate Risk)**
Your co-founder is focusing on total profit, while you are correctly identifying the **payback period** as the threat.
*   **Monthly Contribution:** $37.
*   **Payback Period:** $200 (CAC) ÷ $37 (Monthly Margin) = **5.4 months**.

It takes nearly **5.4 months** of a customer's subscription just to earn back the money you spent to acquire them. During those five months, that customer is costing you cash, not generating it.

With only **$150,000** in the bank and a **six-month runway**, you are in a "valley of death" scenario. If you spend your entire budget on ads today, you will run out of cash before the revenue from those new customers accumulates enough to cover the initial spend. You cannot afford to wait 5.4 months for ROI when your company might cease to exist in 6 months.

**Recommendation:**
Do not pivot the product, but **immediately restrict marketing spend**. You cannot scale acquisition at $200/CAC with your current cash reserves unless you have immediate access to more capital. Shift focus to:
1.  **Lowering CAC:** Test cheaper channels or organic growth strategies.
2.  **Improving Cash Flow:** Offer a significant discount for **annual prepayment** (e.g., $450/year instead of $45/month). This brings the cash in upfront, reducing the payback period from 5.4 months to immediate, extending your runway instantly.
3.  **Retention:** Increasing lifespan from 14 to 16 months drastically improves LTV without increasing CAC.

### Weekly Metrics to Track

To monitor this without a CFO, track these four numbers every Monday:

1.  **Cash Burn Rate:** Total cash spent this week. Compare this strictly against your remaining runway.
2.  **Blended CAC:** Total marketing spend ÷ new customers acquired *this week*. Ensure this doesn't creep above $200.
3.  **Active Subscribers & Churn Count:** Net new subscribers minus those who left. Watch for any spike in churn that shortens the 14-month average.
4.  **Cash Collection vs. Recognition:** Track actual cash entering the bank (especially if you launch annual plans) versus recognized monthly revenue. Cash in hand is your only lifeline right now.

*Note: Your unit economics work long-term, but your liquidity is the bottleneck. Prioritize cash collection over user growth until the runway extends.*
