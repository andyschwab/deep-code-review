For a pre-revenue profitability SaaS company like CodeNiche with $180k ARR, investor valuation is rarely a strict mathematical multiple of revenue. Instead, it is a negotiation based on growth potential, team quality, and market comparables.

**Valuation Benchmarks & Multiples**
In the current market, early-stage developer tools with $100k–$200k ARR typically see pre-money valuations between **$1.5 million and $3.5 million**.
*   **Revenue Multiples:** While mature SaaS companies might trade at 5x–10x ARR, seed-stage deals often effectively range from **8x to 15x ARR** if growth is strong. However, at your scale, investors often ignore the multiple and focus on the "price of the round."
*   **The "Standard" Seed:** Many seed rounds target a specific ownership percentage (usually 15%–20%) for a fixed investment amount (e.g., $500k–$750k). If an investor offers $600k for 20% equity, your post-money valuation is $3M, making your pre-money valuation $2.4M.
*   **Key Drivers:** Your negative cash flow is expected at this stage; investors are buying growth. To justify the higher end of the valuation range, you must demonstrate high month-over-month growth rates, low churn (critical for seat-based models), and a clear path to scaling beyond individual developers to teams or enterprises.

**Required Legal & Financial Documents**
Before discussing specific terms, you must prepare a "data room" with the following:
1.  **Financial Statements:** P&L, balance sheet, and cash flow statements for the last 12–24 months. Include a detailed financial model projecting the next 18–36 months showing how the new capital drives growth.
2.  **Cap Table:** A current, clean capitalization table showing all founders, employees with options, and any existing shareholders.
3.  **Corporate Formation Documents:** Certificate of Incorporation, bylaws, and board consents.
4.  **IP Assignment Agreements:** Proof that all code and IP created by founders and contractors are fully assigned to the company. This is critical for IDE plugins.
5.  **Customer Metrics Report:** A summary of MRR/ARR growth, churn rate, CAC (Customer Acquisition Cost), LTV (Lifetime Value), and cohort analysis.
6.  **Existing Contracts:** Copies of any outstanding debt, convertible notes, or SAFEs.

**Legal Counsel Requirement**
**Yes, you absolutely need a lawyer to review the term sheet before signing.**
*   **Why:** A term sheet sets the binding economic and control terms of the deal. Clauses regarding liquidation preferences, board composition, anti-dilution protection, and vesting schedules can have massive long-term consequences that are not obvious to non-lawyers.
*   **Timing:** Do not sign a term sheet without counsel. While you can negotiate business terms (valuation, amount) directly, legal counsel must vet the protective provisions. Many startup lawyers will work on a deferred fee basis until you close the round.

*Note: Valuation is highly subjective at the seed stage; focus on finding a partner who offers fair terms rather than maximizing the number. Ensure your IP assignments are watertight before due diligence begins.*
