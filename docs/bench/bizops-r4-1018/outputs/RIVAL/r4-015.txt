### Recommendation: Raise Prices Now with a Staggered Rollout

You should implement the price increase to €34 immediately, but **only for new customers** and via a staggered rollout for existing ones. Waiting risks running out of cash, which kills your Series A chances faster than a temporary churn spike. Investors prefer a company with strong unit economics and a clear path to profitability over one that burns cash to maintain an artificially low price.

#### Financial Impact Analysis

**Current State:**
*   **Monthly Revenue:** 2,500 subs × €29 = €72,500
*   **Monthly Burn:** €60,000
*   **Net Cash Flow:** +€12,500
*   **Runway:** €400,000 / €60,000 ≈ **6.7 months** (You are already tight for a 6-month fundraise timeline).

**Scenario A: Immediate Full Price Hike (€29 → €34) with 10% Churn Spike**
*   *Assumption:* 10% of base (250 customers) leave immediately; remaining 2,250 pay €34.
*   **New Revenue:** 2,250 × €34 = €76,500
*   **New Burn:** €60,000 (assuming variable costs drop slightly with fewer users, but keeping fixed costs constant for conservatism).
*   **Net Cash Flow:** +€16,500
*   **Runway:** €400,000 / €60,000 ≈ **6.7 months** initially, but the *rate* of cash accumulation improves.
*   *Correction on Runway Logic:* Runway is calculated by Cash / Net Burn. If you are profitable (positive cash flow), your runway is technically infinite until growth costs exceed revenue. The critical metric here is **cash accumulation rate**.
    *   Current accumulation: €12,500/mo.
    *   New accumulation: €16,500/mo.
    *   **Result:** You build cash reserves **32% faster**. This creates a stronger buffer for the Series A.

**Scenario B: Wait 6 Months**
*   **Revenue:** Stays at €72,500.
*   **Cash Accumulation:** €12,500/mo.
*   **Risk:** Any unexpected cost or slight increase in CAC pushes you into negative cash flow before the round closes. Running out of cash forces a "down round" or desperate terms.

**The Verdict on LTV:CAC:**
*   **Current LTV (simplified):** €29 / 0.03 (churn) = €966. LTV:CAC = 2.1x.
*   **New LTV (with price hike + churn spike):** €34 / 0.033 (3% + 10% relative increase) ≈ €1,030.
*   **New LTV:CAC:** €1,030 / €45 = **2.3x**.
*   **Outcome:** Your unit economics actually *improve*, making you more attractive to investors despite the churn.

#### Strategic Execution Plan
Do not shock all 2,500 users at once.
1.  **New Customers:** Price is €34 effective immediately.
2.  **Existing Customers:** Announce a "value update" coming in 60 days. Offer them a choice: lock in €29 for 6 months if they switch to annual billing now (injects immediate cash), or move to €34 next cycle.
3.  **Communication:** Frame the increase as necessary to maintain eco-quality standards, not just margin improvement.

#### Metrics Investors Scrutinize Pre-Fundraise
1.  **Net Revenue Retention (NRR):** Can you grow revenue despite churn? A price hike that stabilizes NRR above 100% is a strong signal.
2.  **Payback Period:** With higher ARPU, your €45 CAC is recovered faster. Show this improvement.
3.  **Churn Elasticity:** Investors want to see that you tested pricing and understand your customers' willingness to pay. A controlled churn spike proves you have pricing power.
4.  **Cash Flow Breakeven:** Showing you are near or at breakeven reduces the perceived risk of the investment.

*Note: The "10% churn increase" assumption is conservative; often, only 2-5% of loyal users leave due to modest price hikes in essential categories like baby products.*
*Ensure your data room clearly models the "Grandfathering" scenario vs. "Immediate Hike" to show strategic flexibility.*
