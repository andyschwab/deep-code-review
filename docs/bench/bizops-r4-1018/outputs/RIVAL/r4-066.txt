To model margin erosion and protect your unit economics, you must shift from a flat-fee model to a **hybrid pricing structure** that decouples baseline usage from volatile spikes. Your current flat fee of $2,000 creates a dangerous asymmetry: you capture fixed revenue while bearing non-linear infrastructure risk.

**1. Financial Modeling Framework for Investors**
Stop projecting margins based on "average" costs. Instead, build a **scenario-based model** with three distinct columns for your P&L:
*   **Baseline Scenario:** Costs at $600/customer (70% margin).
*   **Stress Scenario:** Costs at $1,500/customer during peak growth (25% margin).
*   **Blended Reality:** Weight these based on historical frequency of customer spikes.

Present this to investors as a "Margin Sensitivity Analysis." Explicitly show that under the current flat-fee model, adding 10 large customers in a high-growth phase drops aggregate gross margins to ~42%, burning through your $500k reserve faster than anticipated. This transparency demonstrates sophisticated unit economic understanding rather than hiding the risk.

**2. Recommended Pricing Structure: The "Base + Overage" Model**
Do not simply build a buffer into flat fees; this makes you uncompetitive against flat-rate rivals and leaves money on the table from stable customers. Instead, restructure your tiers to align price with your actual cost curve:

*   **Tiered Baseline (The "Flat" Competitor Match):** Set your base price (e.g., $2,000/mo) to include a specific ingestion volume (e.g., 50GB/day) that covers your $600 baseline cost comfortably. This keeps your headline price competitive.
*   **Dynamic Overage Rates (The Margin Protector):** Implement an automatic overage charge for traffic exceeding the baseline. Crucially, this rate should be **progressive**.
    *   *Example:* First 20% overage charged at 1.5x base rate per GB.
    *   *Spike Protection:* Traffic exceeding 50% over baseline charged at 3x base rate per GB.
    *   This ensures that when a customer's traffic doubles and your costs triple, your revenue also triples, preserving your margin percentage regardless of volume spikes.

**3. Implementation Strategy**
*   **Grandfathering:** Move existing customers to this model only upon contract renewal or significant volume increases. For new deals, make this the standard.
*   **Competitive Framing:** Position this as "Fair Usage Pricing." Explain to prospects that flat-rate competitors subsidize spiky users with stable users, whereas your model ensures they only pay for the massive scale they actually utilize.
*   **Forecasting Buffer:** In your financial model, assume a 15% "spike coefficient" on all new bookings. If a customer signs for $2k, model the revenue at $2.3k but the cost at the higher stress level until their usage patterns stabilize.

By adopting a hybrid model, you transform your cost structure from a liability into a scalable asset. You remain competitive on the headline number while ensuring that every dollar of additional infrastructure cost is matched by a corresponding dollar of revenue, securing your path to profitability even with aggressive growth.

*Note: Ensure your contracts clearly define "spike" vs. "sustained growth" to prevent billing disputes.*
*Consider offering pre-purchased volume bundles at a discount to smooth out cash flow for customers wary of variable overages.*
