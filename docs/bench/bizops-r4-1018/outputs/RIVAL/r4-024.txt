When moving honey from Georgia to Illinois (interstate commerce), you transition from solely state jurisdiction to federal jurisdiction under the **Food and Drug Administration (FDA)**. While honey is often exempt from Hazard Analysis and Critical Control Points (HACCP) regulations if it is raw and unprocessed, crossing state lines triggers specific mandatory federal requirements that do not apply to intrastate sales.

**1. Mandatory FDA Facility Registration**
You **must** register your processing facility with the FDA under the Bioterrorism Act of 2002. This is required for any facility that manufactures, processes, packs, or holds food for consumption in the United States if that food crosses state lines.
*   **Action:** Register online via the FDA Unified Registration and Listing Systems (FURLS).
*   **Timing:** This must be done before you ship any goods. It requires renewal every even-numbered year.
*   **Note:** There is no fee for registration, but failure to register can result in your product being detained at the border.

**2. Federal Labeling Compliance (FD&C Act)**
Your current Georgia-compliant labels may not meet federal standards. Interstate commerce requires adherence to the Federal Food, Drug, and Cosmetic Act.
*   **Statement of Identity:** Must use the common name "Honey."
*   **Net Quantity of Contents:** Must be declared in both fluid ounces (if liquid) and weight (grams/pounds), following specific font size requirements based on the package size.
*   **Ingredient Statement:** If pure honey, no ingredient list is needed. If additives exist, they must be listed.
*   **Allergen Warning:** While honey is not a major allergen, if processed in a facility with nuts or other allergens, advisory labeling ("May contain...") is strongly recommended to limit liability, though not strictly federally mandated unless cross-contact is unavoidable.
*   **Distributor Information:** Since you are selling to a distributor, the label must list the name and place of business of the manufacturer, packer, or distributor. If you are not the packer, you must use a qualifying phrase like "Manufactured for [Your Company]."
