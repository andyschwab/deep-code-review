I cannot provide legal advice or determine whether your digital badge system constitutes a security under federal law. That determination requires a qualified attorney to analyze the specific facts of your program against the *Howey Test* and relevant SEC guidelines.

However, I can highlight the specific marketing claims you mentioned that typically trigger regulatory scrutiny and suggest general principles for describing professional benefits without making financial promises.

**High-Risk Language to Avoid**
To minimize the risk of your offering being interpreted as an investment contract, you should generally avoid language that frames the purchase as a path to guaranteed financial return. Based on your description, the following phrases are particularly risky:

*   **"Guaranteed interview opportunities":** Regulators often view "guarantees" of future economic benefit (like job interviews leading to income) as a red flag. No third party can truly guarantee an employer's hiring decision.
*   **"Promises of future income potential":** Explicitly marketing the badge as an asset that will generate profit or income shifts the perception from "education/certification" to "investment."
*   **"Return on Investment (ROI) assurances":** Avoid calculating or promising specific payback periods (e.g., "Earn back your $100 fee in your first week").
*   **"Passive income" or "Appreciating asset":** Do not suggest the badge itself holds value that grows over time independent of the holder's labor.

**Safer Framing Principles**
When describing the value of the "premium badge," focus on **access**, **utility**, and **professional development** rather than financial outcomes.

*   **Focus on Access, Not Outcomes:** Instead of "guaranteed interviews," describe the feature as "priority access to our partner hiring network" or "direct submission channels to participating employers." This sells the *mechanism* (the network), not the *result* (the job).
*   **Emphasize Skill and Credibility:** Frame the $500 certification and $100 add-on as purchases of professional credibility, verified skills, and community membership. The value proposition should be "enhanced employability" through qualification, not "income generation" through asset ownership.
*   **Use Probabilistic Language:** If you share success stories, use clear disclaimers such as "Results vary," "Participation does not guarantee employment," and "Graduates are responsible for their own job search efforts."

**Immediate Recommendation**
Given that you have 2,000 students enrolled and are about to launch a monetized feature with potential legal exposure, you must pause any marketing claims regarding "guarantees" until your legal counsel explicitly approves the final wording. They may advise restructuring the "premium" tier entirely to ensure it functions strictly as a service fee for networking access rather than a purchase of expected financial returns.
