When users pay indirectly (through the facility's fee) or directly for a listing, they often infer a higher standard of vetting than exists in free directories. **Monetization vs. Neutrality**
While charging a flat listing fee is common in directory models, the legal distinction between a "passive host" and an "active participant" often hinges on the degree of control and curation exercised. If your fee is perceived as purchasing a "seal of approval" rather than just database inclusion, and a listed home causes harm, plaintiffs may argue your revenue model created an incentive to overlook red flags or that you held yourself out as a guarantor of quality.
