### Evaluation: Per-Week Subscription vs. Per-Meal Credit System

**Current State (Per-Week Subscription)**
*   **Unit Economics:** $60 revenue - $25 food cost (5 meals × $5) = **$35 Gross Profit/week/customer**.
*   **Waste Impact:** 8% waste on variable prep adds ~$1.00/meal in hidden costs, reducing gross profit to ~$30/week.
*   **Churn Impact:** 12% monthly churn limits Customer Lifetime Value (LTV). At $240/month revenue, high churn drastically increases the acquisition cost needed to maintain growth.
*   **Forecasting:** Weekly commitments allow for tighter inventory planning, keeping waste manageable despite the current 8% leak.

**Proposed State (Per-Meal Credit System)**
*   **Unit Economics:** If priced at $12/meal, the margin per unit remains similar ($7 gross profit before waste). However, without a weekly commitment, revenue becomes irregular.
*   **Waste Risk:** Your hypothesis is correct; flexibility usually increases waste in meal prep. Without fixed weekly counts, forecasting demand spikes becomes difficult. Waste could realistically jump from 8% to 15–20%, eroding margins to $4–$5 per meal or turning specific SKUs unprofitable.
*   **Churn Benefit:** Flexibility often reduces "subscription fatigue." Churn might drop from 12% to 6–8%, increasing LTV significantly if the customer continues ordering regularly.
*   **Cash Flow Risk:** Credits often lead to "breakage" (unused credits) which is pure profit, but they also lead to irregular ordering patterns that make covering the $8,000 fixed kitchen cost volatile.

**Recommendation: The Hybrid "Flex-Subscription" Model**
Switching strictly to per-meal credits is too risky for your food waste margins. Instead, adopt a **modified subscription** that balances flexibility with forecasting:

1.  **Keep the Weekly Commitment:** Require a minimum weekly spend (e.g., 3 meals/week) to maintain baseline volume for kitchen planning. This protects your ability to cover the $8,000 fixed cost.
2.  **Introduce Rollover Credits:** Allow users to skip a week without losing value by rolling credits forward. This addresses the churn driver (rigidity) without destroying your forecast accuracy.
3.  **Dynamic Pricing for Flexibility:** Charge a slight premium ($13/meal) for orders outside the standard weekly window or for one-off purchases, while keeping the $12/meal rate ($60/5 meals) for committed weekly subscribers. This incentivizes the behavior that lowers your waste.

**Financial Outlook**
The hybrid model aims to reduce churn to ~8% (increasing LTV) while keeping waste under 10% by maintaining a predictable baseline volume. A strict per-meal model risks pushing waste above 15%, which would require raising prices to $14+/meal to maintain profitability, potentially negating the churn benefit. Stick to a recurring revenue model but soften the cancellation policy rather than abandoning the subscription structure entirely.

*Note: Run a 4-week pilot offering "skip weeks" to 20% of users before fully restructuring pricing.*
*Monitor waste % daily during the pilot; if it exceeds 12%, revert to strict weekly commitments.*
