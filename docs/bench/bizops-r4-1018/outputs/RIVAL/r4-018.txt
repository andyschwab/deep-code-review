### Financial Analysis: Wholesale Distribution Viability

**Current Local Model (Baseline)**
*   **Selling Price:** $4.50
*   **Cost of Goods Sold (COGS):** $2.10
*   **Gross Margin per Loaf:** $2.40
*   **Monthly Volume:** 10,000 loaves
*   **Total Gross Profit:** $24,000
*   **Fixed Overhead:** $18,000
*   **Net Profit:** $6,000

**Proposed Distributor Model**
*   **Distributor Selling Price to Retailer:** $4.50
*   **Distributor Margin (25%):** $1.125 (25% of $4.50)
*   **Your Selling Price to Distributor:** $3.375 (Mathematically required for their 25% margin)
    *   *Note: The distributor's request of $3.20 implies they are calculating margin on cost or targeting a ~28.6% margin on the retail price. We will analyze their specific ask of $3.20.*
*   **Proposed Selling Price:** $3.20
*   **Current COGS:** $2.10
*   **New Gross Margin per Loaf:** $1.10
*   **Fixed Overhead:** $18,000

**Risk Scenario: 10% Flour Cost Increase**
*   **New COGS:** $2.31 ($2.10 × 1.10)
*   **New Gross Margin per Loaf:** $0.89 ($3.20 - $2.31)

**Break-Even Analysis**
To cover your $18,000 monthly overhead:
1.  **At Current Costs ($2.10):** You must sell **16,364 loaves** monthly ($18,000 / $1.10).
2.  **At Increased Costs ($2.31):** You must sell **20,225 loaves** monthly ($18,000 / $0.89).

**Capacity Comparison**
*   **Current Capacity:** 10,000 loaves.
*   **Gap (Current Costs):** You are short 6,364 loaves (64% increase in volume needed).
*   **Gap (Increased Costs):** You are short 10,225 loaves (102% increase in volume needed).

**Conclusion**
The proposed terms are **financially unsustainable**. Accepting the $3.20 price point requires you to more than double your production volume immediately just to break even, assuming no profit. If flour costs rise by 10%, you would need to triple your current output merely to cover overhead. Furthermore, this model eliminates your safety margin; any production inefficiency or unsold inventory results in an immediate net loss. You cannot absorb a 54% reduction in per-unit margin ($2.40 to $1.10) without a guaranteed volume commitment that exceeds your current capacity.

**Critical Contract Clauses to Watch**
In typical distribution agreements, be wary of these clauses that could further erode your margins:
1.  **Most Favored Nation (MFN) Clause:** Allows the distributor to demand the lowest price you offer any other customer, potentially forcing you to lower your local cafe prices to match the wholesale rate.
2.  **Slotting Fees & Pay-to-Play:** Requirements to pay upfront fees for shelf space or promotional placement, which act as hidden costs reducing your effective margin.
3.  **Return/Chargeback Rights:** Clauses allowing the distributor to return unsold or "damaged" inventory at your expense, shifting the risk of spoilage entirely to you.
4.  **Unilateral Price Reduction:** Language permitting the distributor to demand price cuts if market conditions change or if competitors lower prices.
5.  **Marketing Co-op Fees:** Mandatory contributions (often 2-5% of sales) to the distributor's marketing fund, further reducing your net revenue per loaf.

*Recommendation: Do not sign at $3.20. Counter with a price that maintains at least $1.80 margin per unit to cover overhead at realistic volumes, or negotiate a volume guarantee before committing to lower pricing.*
