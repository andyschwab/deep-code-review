### Executive Summary: Accounting & Valuation Implications

**1. Accounting Treatment: Do Not Book Immediately**
You cannot book the €5,000 implementation fee as immediate revenue. Under standard accounting principles (IFRS 15, applicable in Dublin, and ASC 606), this fee is **deferred revenue**.
*   **The Rule:** Revenue must be recognized as you deliver value. If the implementation is a one-time setup required to access the software, the fee is typically amortized (recognized) over the **expected customer lifespan** (your current 18 months) or the contract term, whichever is shorter.
*   **The Impact:** You will see a massive boost in **cash flow** immediately, but your **reported revenue** will only increase by ~€278/month per customer (€5,000 / 18 months) on top of the subscription. Booking it all upfront would be non-compliant and risky for due diligence.

**2. Impact on SaaS Metrics**
This model shift drastically alters your key metrics, creating a "split personality" in your data:
*   **LTV Distortion:** Your Lifetime Value will appear highly volatile.
    *   *Old Model (18 mo):* €500 × 18 = €9,000.
    *   *New Model (6 mo churn):* €5,000 + (€300 × 6) = €6,800.
    *   *New Model (3 yr stay):* €5,000 + (€300 × 36) = €15,800.
    *   *Result:* LTV becomes heavily dependent on early retention. If churn spikes in month 7, your LTV collapses because the upfront fee was already collected but the "value period" wasn't fully realized.
*   **MRR Growth:** Your Monthly Recurring Revenue (MRR) growth will **slow down**. You are swapping €200 of monthly recurring value for €5,000 of one-time cash. Investors scrutinize MRR growth as a proxy for product stickiness; reducing the recurring component makes the business look less predictable.
*   **Cash Flow vs. Profit:** Your cash runway will extend significantly (positive), but your GAAP/IFRS profitability will not improve proportionally in the short term.

**3. Investor Perception: Cash Boost vs. Weak Value**
Investors will view this through two conflicting lenses:
*   **The Positive:** It solves your immediate runway issue. Extending a 12-month runway to 18+ months via cash collection is a valid survival tactic. It proves customers are willing to pay for integration, validating the "service" aspect of your engine.
*   **The Negative:** It signals a potential weakness in **product-led value**. High upfront fees often suggest the software is not "plug-and-play" and requires heavy professional services to derive value. This lowers your valuation multiple because service revenue is valued lower (1x–2x) than pure SaaS recurring revenue (5x–10x).
*   **The Verdict:** Frame this as "accelerating time-to-value" rather than "charging for setup." If you position it as a premium onboarding package that guarantees compliance faster, it protects the narrative. If positioned as a barrier to entry because the tool is hard to use, it hurts valuation.

**Recommendation:** Proceed if you need the cash to survive the next 12 months, but maintain strict separation in your reporting between "One-time Implementation Revenue" and "Recurring Subscription Revenue" to avoid confusing investors about your true MRR trajectory.
