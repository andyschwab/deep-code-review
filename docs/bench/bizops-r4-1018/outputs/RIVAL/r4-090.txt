Your current pricing model is misaligned with your cost structure, and you are almost certainly losing money on your heaviest users while overcharging your lightest users.

**The Core Problem: Decoupled Value and Cost**
You are charging for **storage** (a static asset) but incurring costs primarily from **egress** (dynamic usage).
*   **Current State:** A light user stores 100GB ($5 revenue) and pulls rarely (negligible cost). A heavy user stores 100GB ($5 revenue) but pulls constantly, generating massive egress costs.
*   **The Subsidy:** Because 10% of users generate 70% of your egress traffic, your $12,000 infrastructure bill is heavily skewed toward serving that small group. If your storage revenue is pooled to pay this bill, the 90% of light users are subsidizing the 10% of heavy users.

**Financial Reality Check**
With $18,000 revenue and $12,000 costs, your margin is 33%. However, this is an average.
*   If egress constitutes the majority of that $12,000 bill (which is typical for registries with high pull rates), your heavy users likely have a negative contribution margin. You are paying more to serve their data than they are paying you.
*   Your light users are likely highly profitable, but their surplus is being drained to cover the losses from heavy usage.

**Strategic Recommendation: Unbundle Storage and Egress**
You must restructure your pricing to separate these two distinct value metrics. Do not change your storage rates yet; instead, introduce a distinct line item for data transfer.

1.  **Keep Storage Pricing:** Maintain your $0.05/GB (hot) and $0.01/GB (cold) model. This covers your disk costs and aligns with the value of retention.
2.  **Introduce Egress Pricing:** Charge explicitly for data pulled.
    *   **Option A (Direct Pass-through):** Charge $X per GB of egress. Calculate your blended cost per GB of egress from your $12k bill, add a 30-50% margin, and set this as your rate.
    *   **Option B (Tiered Allowances):** Bundle a generous amount of free egress into your storage tiers (e.g., "Includes 10GB/month free egress"), then charge overage fees. This protects light users from seeing a new fee while ensuring heavy users pay for their consumption.

**Immediate Action Plan**
*   **Calculate Unit Economics:** Isolate the exact egress cost generated by your top 10% of users. Compare this directly to the revenue they generate from storage fees alone. The gap is your current loss.
*   **Model the Shift:** Simulate revenue if you charged $0.02–$0.04/GB for egress. You will likely see total revenue increase significantly, but more importantly, the *profitability per user* will correct. Light users' bills stay flat; heavy users' bills rise to reflect their true cost-to-serve.
*   **Communicate the Change:** Frame this as "fairness." Explain that to keep storage costs low for the 90% of users who rarely pull, data transfer must be billed separately for those who do.

**Conclusion**
Yes, restructure immediately. Continuing to bundle these costs forces your light users to subsidize heavy infrastructure usage, creating a fragile business model where growth in usage actually decreases profitability. Unbundling ensures every customer pays for the specific resources they consume.

*Note: Consider offering "unlimited egress" only as a premium enterprise add-on priced significantly higher than your standard tiers.*
