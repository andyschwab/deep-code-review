**Executive Summary: Do Not Cut Price to $29**

Dropping your price from $79 to $29 will not fix your unit economics; it will likely accelerate cash burn and make your seed round impossible to close. Your core problem is **retention**, not price sensitivity. Lowering the price for a service that users already abandon after three months validates the perception that the service is low-value, potentially increasing churn further while destroying your revenue per user.

**Current Unit Economics (Status Quo)**
*   **Price:** $79/month
*   **Churn:** 12% monthly (implied average lifespan ~3.5 months)
*   **Current LTV:** $420 (as stated)
*   **CAC:** $185
*   **LTV:CAC Ratio:** 2.27:1
*   *Analysis:* While investors prefer 3:1, a 2.27:1 ratio is fundable if the trend is improving. The issue is the high churn, not the ratio itself.

**Projected Unit Economics (The $29 Pivot)**
To maintain your current revenue volume at $29/month, you would need **2.7x more customers**. Assuming your churn rate remains constant (optimistic) or worsens (likely, due to perceived lower value):

*   **New Price:** $29/month
*   **Projected LTV:** ~$154 (assuming same 3.5-month lifespan)
    *   *Calculation:* $29 × 3.5 months = $101.50 (conservative) to $154 (if lifespan extends slightly). Even at $154:
*   **New CAC Impact:** Your CAC is $185. If you charge $29, your LTV ($154) is now **lower than your CAC ($185)**.
*   **New LTV:CAC Ratio:** 0.83:1
*   **Result:** You lose $31 on every single customer acquired immediately. This is a "death spiral."

**The Break-Even Reality**
For the $29 model to work with your current $185 CAC, a customer would need to stay for **6.4 months** just to break even on acquisition costs (ignoring service delivery costs). Given that users currently leave after month 3, expecting them to stay twice as long solely because the price is lower contradicts behavioral data for coaching services. Sleep coaching is a high-stakes, outcome-based purchase; parents pay for results, not cheapness. A $29 price point signals "DIY ebook," not "expert coaching," attracting a more price-sensitive, less committed demographic that churns faster.

**Strategic Recommendation**
1.  **Reject the Price Cut:** Presenting a plan to sell below CAC to investors will signal a lack of unit economic understanding.
2.  **Fix Retention First:** Your LTV of $420 suggests some users stay longer, but the 12% monthly churn is the leak. Investigate *why* they leave after month 3. Is the program too long? Do they feel "cured"? Introduce a "graduation" milestone or a lower-cost "maintenance" tier ($49) instead of slashing the core price.
3.  **Increase Price, Not Decrease:** Consider raising the price to $99 or $129 and adding a "success guarantee" or enhanced onboarding. Higher prices often increase commitment and perceived value, reducing churn.
4.  **Address CAC:** $185 is high for a $79 product. Focus on organic channels, referrals, or partnerships with pediatricians rather than paid ads until LTV improves.

**Conclusion:** The math proves the $29 strategy creates negative unit economics (LTV < CAC). Your board needs a plan to extend customer lifespan or lower acquisition costs, not a race to the bottom on price.

*Note: This analysis assumes churn behavior remains correlated with value perception; lowering price often degrades perceived value in service industries.*
