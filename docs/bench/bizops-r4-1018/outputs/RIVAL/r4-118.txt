To secure a fair Series A valuation despite seasonal volatility, you must shift the investor narrative from "lumpy revenue" to "predictable cohort economics." Investors in maternal health expect seasonality; their concern is not the swing itself, but whether your underlying growth engine is broken. Here is how to normalize your financials and frame your pitch:

**1. Normalize ARR Using Trailing Twelve Months (TTM) and Seasonality Indices**
Do not present current monthly recurring revenue (MRR) annualized (e.g., $120k × 12), as this drastically undervalues you during off-peak months. Instead, present **TTM Revenue** divided by 12 to establish a baseline "average month."
*   **The Adjustment:** Create a "Seasonality Index" showing your historical ratio of peak-to-trough revenue (e.g., Summer is 1.8x Winter). Apply this index to your forward projections to demonstrate that the dip is a known, modeled variable, not a surprise.
*   **The Metric:** Highlight **Normalized ARR**, calculated as the average of the last 12 months, rather than the run-rate of the current month. This smooths the curve immediately on your summary slide.

**2. Pivot the Core Narrative to Cohort Retention, Not Top-Line Fluctuation**
Your $650 Lifetime Value (LTV) is your strongest asset. Seasonality affects *acquisition* volume, but it should not affect *retention* quality.
*   **Cohort Curves:** Display retention curves by birth quarter (e.g., "Q2 2023 Births" vs. "Q4 2023 Births"). If these curves overlap tightly, it proves that once a mother subscribes, her behavior is consistent regardless of when she joined. This isolates seasonality as a marketing funnel issue, not a product fit issue.
*   **Net Revenue Retention (NRR):** If your NRR is strong (>100% via upsells or extended care plans), emphasize this. It shows that the value of an existing customer grows independently of new birth rates.

**3. Reframe Unit Economics with "Cost per Acquired Subscriber" (CPAS) Variance**
Acknowledge that your Cost of Acquisition (CAC) likely fluctuates with demand.
*   **The Frame:** Show that while acquisition volume drops in winter, your **LTV:CAC ratio** remains healthy because competition for ad inventory decreases, potentially lowering costs, or because your organic referral rates remain stable.
*   **Contribution Margin:** Present contribution margin per subscriber rather than overall monthly profit. This demonstrates that every single unit sold is profitable, proving that scaling during peak seasons will yield exponential returns without breaking your unit economics.

**4. The "Capacity-Constrained" Growth Story**
Investors fear volatility implies instability. Reframe your troughs as **intentional capacity management**.
*   **The Narrative:** Argue that your lower winter numbers reflect a deliberate choice to maintain high-touch care standards (nurse-to-patient ratios) rather than an inability to sell. Position the Series A capital specifically as the tool to "flatten the curve" by building the operational infrastructure (hiring pools, telehealth scalability) needed to capture 100% of the summer demand without service degradation.

**5. Valuation Benchmarking**
When discussing multiples, benchmark against other **seasonal consumer subscriptions** (e.g., tax software, education tech, fitness) rather than flat-line B2B SaaS. These sectors are often valued on **TTM Revenue** or **Peak Season Run-Rate** with a discount applied for seasonality, rather than a penalty that assumes churn.

**Pitch Deck Specifics:**
*   **Slide 1:** Show TTM Revenue growth year-over-year (ignoring monthly dips).
*   **Slide 2:** Overlay acquisition cohorts to prove retention consistency.
*   **Slide 3:** Present a "Use of Funds" plan explicitly tied to smoothing operational capacity for peak seasons.

By proving that your unit economics are stable and your retention is immune to the calendar, you transform seasonality from a risk factor into a predictable operational rhythm that capital can solve.

*Note: Ensure your data room includes a sensitivity analysis showing valuation under conservative winter-growth assumptions to build trust.*
*Focus heavily on the "Q-over-Q" cohort overlap; if those lines cross, the seasonality argument is won.*
