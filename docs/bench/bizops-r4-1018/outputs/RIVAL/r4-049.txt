### Optimal Marketing Allocation Strategy for BlazeCraft

**1. Immediate Financial Baseline**
Before splitting the budget, clarify your unit economics.
*   **Online:** Price minus COGS minus $22 CAC. With an LTV of $65, your net profit per customer depends heavily on your bottle cost. If your margin is 50% on a $20 bottle ($10 gross), you are currently losing $12 on the first purchase ($10 - $22). You rely entirely on repeat purchases to hit the $65 LTV.
*   **Retail:** A 40% store margin is standard. If you sell to stores at $10 (wholesale) and your COGS is $5, your gross profit is $5/bottle. Retail has no CAC in the traditional sense; your "marketing cost" is the trade spend (discounts, samples, slotting fees) required to get and keep shelf space.

**2. The Attribution Solution: "Lift Studies" Not "Last Click"**
You cannot technically attribute a specific online sale to a specific store visit using standard software without expensive enterprise tools. Instead, use **Geo-Lift Testing**:
*   **Method:** Select two similar geographic markets (e.g., Austin vs. San Antonio).
*   **Action:** Aggressively fund retail activation (in-store demos, local radio, shelf talkers) in Market A. Pause all retail-specific marketing in Market B, keeping only baseline distribution.
*   **Measurement:** Track online sales velocity in *both* markets. If online sales rise in Market A significantly more than Market B, that delta is the "Retail Halo Effect."
*   **Allocation Rule:** Assign a portion of your online revenue in activation zones to the retail budget based on this lift.

**3. Testing Allocation Strategies**
Run a 90-day phased test with your $8,000 budget:
*   **Month 1 (60/40 Split):** $4,800 to Online (Paid Social/Search), $3,200 to Retail (Demos/Trade Spend). Measure total blended profit.
*   **Month 2 (40/60 Split):** $3,200 to Online, $4,800 to Retail. Focus retail spend on high-traffic demo days to drive immediate trial.
*   **Month 3 (Optimized):** Shift 100% of the *incremental* budget to the channel that produced the highest **Blended ROAS** (Total Revenue / Total Marketing Spend).

**4. Key Metrics to Track**
Stop looking at channel silos. Track these weekly:
*   **Blended CAC:** Total Marketing Spend / Total New Customers (Online + Retail).
*   **Contribution Margin:** Revenue minus COGS minus Marketing Spend. Do not optimize for revenue; optimize for cash left over.
*   **Retail Velocity:** Units sold per store per week. If velocity drops below a threshold, the shelf space is costing you money.
*   **Repeat Purchase Rate:** Critical for online. If Month 1 buyers don't return by Month 3, your $22 CAC is unsustainable.

**5. Hiring Recommendation**
Do not hire a generic "Financial Analyst." You need a **Growth Operator** or **Fractional Head of Growth** with specific experience in **CPG (Consumer Packaged Goods) Omnichannel Attribution**.
*   **Specific Profile:** Look for someone who has scaled a DTC brand into retail (or vice versa). They must understand "trade spend" accounting, not just digital ad pixels.
*   **Interview Question:** Ask them, "How do you model the halo effect of retail distribution on DTC LTV without using multi-touch attribution software?" If they suggest complex software immediately, they are not the right fit. If they suggest geo-lift testing or matched-market analysis, hire them.

*Note: Ensure your COGS are accurate before scaling; negative unit economics cannot be fixed by allocation alone.*
