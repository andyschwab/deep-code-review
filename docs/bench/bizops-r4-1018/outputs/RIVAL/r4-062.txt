Do not split the contract into two separate agreements. This practice, often called "contract splitting" or "salami slicing," is a red flag for auditors and procurement teams. It signals an attempt to bypass internal controls, which can destroy trust, trigger immediate compliance reviews, and potentially void the deal entirely. Given your six-month runway and the critical need for cash flow, the risk of delaying payment further due to an audit investigation is existential.

Instead, adopt a **Modular "Base + Add-on" Pricing Structure** within a single Master Services Agreement (MSA). This is a standard, accepted B2B practice that keeps the initial invoice under the £10,000 threshold while legally committing the customer to the full £20,000 value over time.

**Recommended Structure:**

1.  **Core License (The "Base"):** Price this at **£9,500/year**.
    *   Includes: Static analysis engine, standard rule sets, and up to 5 seats.
    *   *Result:* The initial Purchase Order (PO) is under £10k, bypassing the formal procurement review and allowing payment within 30 days.

2.  **Enterprise Modules (The "Add-ons"):** Price these as separate line items with **delayed billing triggers**.
    *   *Module A (Advanced Fintech Rules):* £5,250/year.
    *   *Module B (Unlimited Seats & SSO):* £5,250/year.
    *   *Contractual Mechanism:* The MSA states these modules are required for "Enterprise Compliance." However, the **invoice date** for these modules is set to "Day 90" or "Upon Completion of Onboarding."

**How this works in practice:**
You sign one contract for £20,000 total value. The customer's legal team approves the whole document once. However, the *financial* trigger for the bulk of the cost happens after the initial 30-day payment window for the base license has already cleared. By the time the add-on invoices arrive (e.g., Month 4), the tool is already embedded in their workflow, making cancellation painful and unlikely.

**Concrete Industry Examples:**

*   **Security & Compliance Tools (e.g., Vanta, Drata):** Often sell a "Platform Access" fee separately from "Audit Readiness Modules." The platform gets you in the door quickly; the heavy compliance features are billed once the customer realizes they need them for their own audit cycle, often 60–90 days later.
*   **Developer Tools (e.g., Snyk, SonarQube):** Frequently use a "Core Team" tier vs. "Enterprise Governance" add-ons. A startup can buy the Core tier instantly. The CISO later approves the Governance add-on as a separate operational expense once the security team proves value, avoiding the initial capital expenditure hurdle.
*   **Cloud Infrastructure (e.g., AWS/Azure Resellers):** Sell a base reservation upfront, with "overage" or "premium support" billed in arrears. This aligns cost with consumption and avoids large upfront approvals.

**Execution Strategy for SecureCode:**
Frame the £9,500 base as the "Fintech Starter Compliance Pack." Position the additional £10,500 not as hidden costs, but as "Phase 2: Full Enterprise Deployment" which naturally occurs after the initial integration period. This aligns with your customer's implementation timeline rather than looking like an evasion tactic.

This approach secures your immediate cash flow (£9,500 within 30 days) to extend your runway, while contractually locking in the full £20,000 value without triggering the four-month procurement delay.

*Note: Ensure your MSA clearly defines the billing triggers for add-ons to avoid disputes later. Consult a local UK contract lawyer to verify specific wording regarding "deferred billing" clauses.*
