EcoUtensil Co. is not automatically disqualified from municipal bidding based on your current size (six employees) or age (18 months). While some large federal contracts have strict tenure requirements, municipal contracts for parks and events often feature specific exemptions and pathways for small businesses.

**Pathways to Compliance and Exemptions:**

1.  **Small Business Set-Asides:** Most municipalities and states have "Small Business Enterprise" (SBE) or "Local Business Enterprise" (LBE) programs. These often reserve a percentage of contract value specifically for companies under a certain revenue threshold or employee count (often under 50 employees). Your six-person team likely qualifies you for these set-asides, effectively shielding you from competition with larger incumbents on specific bids.
2.  **Waivers for Tenure:** The requirement for a business to be established for several years is frequently waivable for new entities if the principals (owners) can demonstrate relevant industry experience. You can submit a "responsibility determination" package highlighting your team's collective experience rather than the corporate entity's age.
3.  **Sustainable Product Preferences:** Many cities have "Green Procurement" policies that mandate or give scoring preference to biodegradable products. This can override standard lowest-bidder rules. If a city has a zero-waste goal, your cornstarch cutlery may be the *only* compliant option, negating the need to compete on price alone against plastic manufacturers.

**"Buy American" and Supply Chain Clarification:**

"Buy American" statutes generally apply to **manufacturing location**, not just raw material sourcing. Since you manufacture the cutlery in Ohio, your product likely qualifies as "domestic" even if the raw cornstarch is imported, provided the manufacturing process in the U.S. adds significant value (which converting raw starch to finished cutlery does).
*   **The Threshold:** Typically, a product is considered "American-made" if the cost of domestic components and processing exceeds 55% of the total cost. Given your Ohio facility and labor, you likely meet this threshold.
*   **Action:** You must obtain a formal certification from your state's economic development agency or a legal opinion confirming your specific supply chain meets the local "domestic content" threshold before bidding.

**Strategic Recommendation to Protect Capital:**

Do not blindly respond to full Requests for Proposals (RFPs) yet. To avoid wasting your $40,000 working capital:
1.  **Request a "Pre-Bid Conference":** Attend these mandatory meetings to ask eligibility questions directly to the procurement officer before doing any work.
2.  **Submit a "Capability Statement":** Instead of a full bid, submit a one-page capability statement to the city's procurement office asking for a determination of eligibility based on your SBE status and green product classification.
3.  **Target "Micro-Purchases":** Look for contracts under $10,000–$25,000 (thresholds vary by city). These often require no formal bidding process, only a quote, allowing you to build a government sales history quickly.

Your corporate structure is an asset, not a liability, if positioned correctly under small business and green initiatives.
