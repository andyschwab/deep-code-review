### Financial Analysis: PopGourmet Dallas Expansion

**The Break-Even Point**
The variable fee (18% of sales) becomes more expensive than the fixed rent ($4,500) when your monthly gross sales exceed **$25,000**.
*   *Calculation:* $4,500 ÷ 0.18 = $25,000.
*   Below $25,000 in sales, the percentage model is cheaper.
*   Above $25,000 in sales, the fixed rent is cheaper.

**Scenario Comparison Based on Your Data**

| Scenario | Monthly Sales | Fixed Rent Cost | Variable Fee (18%) Cost | Financial Impact |
| :--- | :--- | :--- | :--- :--- |
| **Slow Month** | $14,000 | $4,500 | $2,520 | **Variable saves you $1,980** |
| **Average Month** | $22,000 | $4,500 | $3,960 | **Variable saves you $540** |
| **Peak Month** | $35,000 | $4,500 | $6,300 | **Fixed saves you $1,800** |

**Risk & Cash Flow Analysis**
Given your limited cash reserves ($55,000) and the seasonal nature of airport traffic, the **Variable Fee (18%) is the superior choice for minimizing downside risk.**

1.  **Cash Flow Protection:** In a slow month ($14k sales), choosing the Fixed Rent model forces you to pay $4,500 regardless of performance. The Variable model adjusts automatically to $2,520. This $1,980 difference is critical for preserving runway during travel dips.
2.  **Profitability Check:** Even in your slowest month ($14k sales), you remain profitable under the variable model:
    *   Sales: $14,000
    *   COGS (25%): -$3,500
    *   Labor: -$6,000
    *   Variable Fee (18%): -$2,520
    *   **Net Profit: $1,980**
    *   *Note:* Under the Fixed Rent model, your profit in a slow month drops to just $500, leaving almost no buffer for unexpected costs.
3.  **Upside Cost:** You only "overpay" with the variable model during peak months. At $35,000 sales, you pay $1,800 more than the fixed rent. However, your net profit at that volume is high enough ($8,450 vs $10,250) to absorb this extra cost easily. Paying a premium on high revenue is a healthy problem; paying fixed rent on low revenue is a survival threat.

**Strategic Recommendation**
Accept the **18% variable fee**. It aligns the airport authority's incentives with yours (they only make more if you make more) and acts as an automatic hedge against seasonal downturns. With only $55,000 in reserves, avoiding the rigid $4,500 obligation during potential slow months is essential for stability.

**Negotiation Tactic:** If you want to capture more upside later, propose a **hybrid cap**: "We accept 18% of gross sales, capped at a maximum of $5,500 per month." This protects you in slow months while limiting your exposure in banner months, though the pure variable model is already the safest bet for your current cash position.
