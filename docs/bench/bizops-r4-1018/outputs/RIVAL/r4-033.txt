### Standard Accounting Treatment for Aging Inventory

For a manufacturing business like GreenWhey Alternatives, the standard treatment under Generally Accepted Accounting Principles (GAAP) and IRS regulations (specifically IRS Publication 538) is to **capitalize all direct and indirect costs** necessary to bring the inventory to its current condition and location for sale. **3. Compliance and Tax Implications**
*   **Uniform Capitalization Rules (UNICAP):** The IRS generally requires businesses with inventory to capitalize direct costs and an allocable share of indirect costs (like electricity and labor) into inventory. Expensing these immediately would likely understate your assets and overstate your expenses, potentially leading to an audit adjustment if discovered.
*   **Matching Principle:** Proper capitalization ensures you match the revenue from the sale with the costs incurred to produce that specific revenue. If you expensed the labor and electricity in Q3 but didn't sell the cheese until Q4, your Q3 profits would be artificially low, and Q4 profits artificially high.
*   **Agricultural Exceptions:** While some cash-method farmers have exceptions for certain crops, processed food products like vegan cheese typically fall under standard manufacturing inventory rules rather than unprocessed agricultural exemptions.
