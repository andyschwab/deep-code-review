### Boundary Between Software Insights and Professional Advice

The distinction between providing a technology tool and offering professional services generally hinges on **discretion, judgment, and the explicit assumption of responsibility**.

*   **Technology Provider:** Your software aggregates data, applies predefined rules, and highlights anomalies based on objective criteria. The output is presented as "data points," "potential flags," or "informational findings" that require human interpretation.
*   **Professional Service:** A licensed professional (lawyer/CPA) applies judgment to specific facts, considers nuance/context, assumes responsibility for the conclusion, and provides a definitive recommendation on how to act.

To remain a technology provider, your product must function as a **decision-support tool**, not a **decision-maker**.

### Essential Language and Disclaimers

To mitigate the risk of being classified as practicing law or accounting without a license, your terms of service, user agreements, and report headers should clearly articulate the following:

1.  **Informational Purpose Only:** Explicitly state that all reports, findings, and scores are for **informational and educational purposes only**. They do not constitute legal, accounting, or professional advice.
2.  **No Attorney-Client/Professional Relationship:** Include a prominent clause stating that using the software or receiving a report **does not create an attorney-client, accountant-client, or any other professional fiduciary relationship** between Northwind Audit Tech and the user.
3.  **Requirement for Human Review:** Mandate that all findings **must be reviewed by qualified internal counsel or external professional advisors** before any action is taken. Frame the output as "preliminary indicators" rather than "final determinations."
4.  **Limitation of Scope:** Clarify that the software checks against a specific set of programmed rules which may not reflect the most current regulations, local nuances, or unique factual circumstances of the provider.
5.  **User Responsibility:** Assert that the **user retains sole responsibility** for compliance decisions and any consequences resulting from reliance on the software's output.

**Sample Disclaimer Language:**
*"Northwind Audit Tech provides automated data analysis tools. The reports generated are for informational purposes only and do not constitute legal, accounting, or professional advice. No professional relationship is formed by your use of this service. All findings should be verified by qualified legal or accounting professionals before making compliance decisions. Northwind Audit Tech is not responsible for any actions taken based on these reports."*

### Key Risks of Client Reliance Without Human Review

If a client relies solely on your automated findings to make major compliance decisions, Northwind Audit Tech faces several significant risks:

*   **Unauthorized Practice of Law/Accounting (UPL):** Regulators may argue that by issuing definitive "compliance findings," you are engaging in the practice of a licensed profession. Penalties can include cease-and-desist orders, fines, and criminal charges in some jurisdictions.
*   **Negligence and Liability:** If your software misses a critical gap (false negative) or flags a non-issue (false positive) and the client suffers financial loss, regulatory fines, or reputational damage, you could be sued for negligence. Without a licensed professional's oversight, your defense of "we are just a tool" weakens if your marketing or report language implies certainty.
*   **Breach of Contract/Consumer Protection:** If clients believe they purchased a "compliance guarantee" rather than a "scanning tool," they may claim false advertising or breach of contract when issues arise.
*   **Regulatory Scrutiny:** Healthcare is highly regulated. State medical boards or health departments may scrutinize your tool if it becomes evident that clinics are bypassing human expert review based on your automated outputs.

**Strategic Recommendation:** Before moving to an unlimited subscription model, ensure your Terms of Service are updated by counsel to strictly limit liability and define the scope of service. Consider adding a mandatory "acknowledgment" step where users must confirm they understand the reports are not professional advice before downloading them.
