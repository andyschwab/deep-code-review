**Series A Strategy: Mitigating Single-Customer Concentration Risk**

To secure your $5M Series A despite 85% revenue concentration, you must reframe the narrative from "dependency risk" to "validated beachhead strategy." Investors in B2G (Business-to-Government) understand that landing one major agency is harder than landing ten SMBs; your goal is to prove the Department of Infrastructure (DOI) is a replicable template, not an anomaly.

**1. Prioritize These Disclosures & Terms**

*   **Contract Durability & Stickiness:** Explicitly disclose the remaining term length, renewal history, and termination clauses of the DOI contract. If the contract is multi-year or has auto-renewal mechanisms, highlight this as "committed revenue" rather than "at-risk revenue." Provide a "survival analysis" showing that even if DOI churns tomorrow, your runway (based on current burn vs. cash on hand + raise) allows 18+ months to pivot.
*   **The "Replication Playbook" Metric:** Do not just present revenue charts. Present a **sales cycle comparison**. Show investors that the sales motion for the next agency is 40–50% faster than the first because compliance, security audits (FedRAMP), and legal frameworks are already solved. This proves the *marginal cost* of the next customer is significantly lower.
*   **Use of Proceeds Allocation:** Structure the raise so that 60–70% of the $5M is explicitly earmarked for **commercial diversification** (hunting private sector or non-federal public sector clients), while only 30% supports the existing DOI account. This shows you are actively using investor capital to solve the very risk they fear.
*   **Milestone-Based Tranches (Optional but Powerful):** If investors remain hesitant, offer a tranched deal. Release $3M immediately to fund operations and the first two new logos, with the remaining $2M released upon signing a second anchor customer (even if smaller). This reduces their downside exposure while giving you a clear target.

**2. Strategic Narrative Shifts**

*   **Reframe the "Zero Revenue" Scenario:** Acknowledge the risk candidly but counter it with **asset value**. Even without the DOI contract, CodeFlow retains its federal security authorizations, proprietary review models trained on government codebases, and a team cleared for federal work. These are high-barrier moats that a new entrant cannot replicate quickly. Argue that the *technology* has intrinsic value independent of the current payer.
*   **Leverage the "Reference Account" Power:** Position the DOI contract not as revenue, but as the ultimate **trust signal**. In the private sector (especially finance and healthcare), having a federal agency as a client validates your security and reliability more than any whitepaper. Frame the revenue concentration as the "cost of acquiring the ultimate case study."
*   **Target the Right Investor Profile:** Avoid generalist SaaS funds who demand immediate diversification. Target firms with a **B2G or Deep Tech thesis** (e.g., those who invested in Palantir, Anduril, or GovTech startups). They understand that government sales are "lumpy" and that concentration is a feature of the early stage, not a bug.

**3. Immediate Action Items for the Data Room**

*   **Churn Sensitivity Model:** Include a financial model showing the company's trajectory if DOI is lost today. Show exactly how the $5M raise extends the runway to acquire replacement revenue.
*   **Pipeline Validation:** Show letters of intent (LOIs) or advanced-stage discussions with at least two other agencies or private entities. Even non-binding interest proves the market exists beyond DOI.
*   **Unit Economics Separation:** Break out the unit economics of the DOI account vs. the projected economics of new customers. If the DOI account required heavy customization (low margin) but new customers will be standard SaaS (high margin), highlight this margin expansion potential.

By treating the DOI contract as a strategic asset that lowers customer acquisition costs for future deals, rather than a single point of failure, you align your risk profile with the high-reward potential of dominating the federal automation niche.

*Note: Ensure all claims about contract terms and security clearances are legally vetted before inclusion in investor materials.*
