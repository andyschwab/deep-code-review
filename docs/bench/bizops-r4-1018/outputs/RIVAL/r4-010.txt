To calculate a rigorous Total Addressable Market (TAM) for HydroLoop without double-counting, you must shift your mindset from "volume of water consumed" to "volume of bottles sold." Investors know that a durable good is a replacement market, not a recurring consumption market like bottled water.

Here is the logical approach to sizing your market using a bottom-up methodology:

**1. Define the Addressable Population (The "Who")**
Start with your specific constraint: the US population of 330 million. Apply your filter for environmentally conscious consumers (20%).
*   Calculation: 330,000,000 × 0.20 = **66 million addressable individuals**.

**2. Determine Penetration and Ownership Rate (The "How Many")**
Not every addressable individual needs a new bottle today. Many already own one. You must estimate the "replacement cycle" and "new adopter" rate.
*   Assume a conservative saturation where only 10% of your addressable market is in the market for a *new* premium bottle this year (either first-time buyers or replacements).
*   Calculation: 66,000,000 × 0.10 = **6.6 million potential units sold annually**.

**3. Apply Price Point (The "Revenue")**
Multiply the potential units by your Average Selling Price (ASP) of $35.
*   Calculation: 6,600,000 units × $35 = **$231 million TAM**.

**4. Resolve the Distribution Channel Fear (No Double-Counting)**
Your worry about double-counting DTC and retail is a common misconception. You do not add DTC potential customers to Retail potential customers.
*   **The Logic:** A customer is a customer, regardless of where they buy. If Jane buys a HydroLoop at Target, she is part of the 6.6 million units sold. If she buys it on your website, she is still just one unit sold.
*   **The Method:** Your TAM is the total revenue opportunity from those 6.6 million units. The *channel mix* (e.g., 40% DTC, 60% Retail) is a go-to-market strategy detail that dictates how you capture that TAM, not how you size it. You calculate the total pool of buyers first, then estimate what percentage you can capture through each channel.

**Summary for Investors:**
Present your TAM as: **"The annual revenue opportunity from US environmentally conscious consumers purchasing a premium reusable bottle."**
*   **Formula:** (Total US Pop × Target Segment % × Annual Purchase Rate) × Price Point.
*   **Result:** ~$231M (based on the 10% annual purchase assumption).

This approach demonstrates to investors that you understand the difference between a consumable market (bottled water) and a durable goods market (reusable bottles), and that you have a disciplined method for avoiding inflated numbers.

*Note: For Seed rounds, investors often prefer a "Bottom-Up" TAM (calculated above) over a "Top-Down" TAM (e.g., "The global water market is $300B, so 1% is ours"), as it proves you understand your specific unit economics.*
*Ensure you clearly state your assumptions regarding the "Annual Purchase Rate," as this is the variable investors will challenge most.*
