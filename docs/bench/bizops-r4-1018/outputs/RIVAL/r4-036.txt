### Seasonal Churn & Cash Flow Model for TropiBox

Do not smooth your projections; smoothing hides the cash cliff that kills subscription businesses. You must model the off-season as a distinct period with altered unit economics. Your baseline business (4% churn, $45 ARPU) and your off-season business (11% churn, potential $38 ARPU) are effectively two different companies operating on the same P&L.

**1. The Segmented Forecasting Framework**
Build a monthly cash flow model with three explicit variables that change only during the "lean months" (e.g., Months 6 and 7):
*   **Churn Rate:** Hard-code 11% for these months, not 4%.
*   **ARPU:** Model two scenarios: one maintaining $45 (high churn) and one at $38 (discounted retention).
*   **COGS:** Adjust for the cost difference between exotic imports and substitutes.

Calculate your "Cash Burn" specifically for these months. If your 2,500 subscribers drop by 11% (275 users) in one month, you lose $12,375 in recurring revenue immediately. If you discount to $38 to save half of them, you retain revenue but compress margin. Run the math: Is the cost of the discount ($7/user) lower than the Customer Acquisition Cost (CAC) required to replace the lost subscriber later? If your CAC is >$50, the discount is mathematically superior even if it hurts short-term cash flow.

**2. The "Bridge" Strategy vs. Re-acquisition**
With only $35,000 in reserves, you cannot afford a "churn and re-acquire" strategy unless your CAC is under $20. Re-acquiring 275 users at a standard $40 CAC costs $11,000, plus you suffer a revenue gap during the switch.
*   **Recommendation:** Treat the off-season as a "Retention Bridge." Offer a targeted "Harvest Pause" or "Variety Box" discount *only* to users identified as price-sensitive, rather than a blanket price drop. This keeps ARPU closer to $42 while mitigating the 11% spike.
*   **Cash Flow Guardrail:** Your $35,000 reserve must cover the *net* cash outflow of the lean months plus a 20% safety buffer. If the discounted scenario keeps you above a $10,000 minimum cash balance, take the discount. If the model shows you dipping below $5,000, you must secure a line of credit *before* the season starts, as marketing spend during a churn spike is inefficient.

**3. Marketing Spend Allocation**
Shift your marketing budget away from the lean months. Do not spend heavily on acquisition when your product is weakest. Instead, front-load acquisition in the high-availability months to build a larger base before the drop, and allocate lean-month funds to retention emails and community engagement rather than paid ads.

**Note:** Your primary risk is not the churn itself, but the timing of cash outflows (inventory purchases) against delayed revenue recovery.
**Note:** Validate your specific substitution costs immediately; if substitutes cost more than exotics, the discount strategy may render those months unprofitable regardless of retention.
