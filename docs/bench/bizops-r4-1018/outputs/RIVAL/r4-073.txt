For your Series A board presentation, you should **match variable cloud infrastructure costs strictly to the specific revenue period they occurred in**, rather than averaging or smoothing them artificially.

In SaaS accounting, particularly under UK GAAP (FRS 102) and IFRS standards relevant for Series A investors, Cost of Goods Sold (COGS) must reflect the direct costs incurred to deliver the service in that specific period. Your compute costs are a direct variable cost of delivering your migration assessments; therefore, they are recognized as expenses in the month the compute resource was consumed, regardless of when the cash was paid or how steady the revenue is.

**Accounting Implications of Each Method:**

1.  **Strict Matching (Recommended):** You recognize the $12,000 expense in the heavy month and $4,000 in the light month. This results in fluctuating gross margins (65%–82%).
    *   *Implication:* This is the most accurate representation of your unit economics. It shows investors the true variability of your delivery costs. Sophisticated Series A investors prefer this transparency because it allows them to model your scalability accurately. Hiding volatility can be seen as a red flag during due diligence.

2.  **Averaging/Smoothing (Not Recommended for External Reporting):** You average the costs over a quarter to present a steady 78% margin.
    *   *Implication:* While internal management accounts sometimes use smoothing for trend analysis, presenting smoothed margins as actual GAAP-compliant financials to investors can be misleading. It obscures the reality that your COGS are not perfectly linear with revenue. If discovered during due diligence, it may raise questions about the rigor of your financial controls.

**Addressing Investor Concerns:**
Do not fear the volatility. In the UK tech sector, investors understand that cloud-native tools often have "lumpy" infrastructure costs. Instead of smoothing the numbers to fake stability, use the board deck to **explain the variance**.
*   Present the strict matching numbers.
*   Add a commentary slide showing the *trend* of your gross margin over the last 6–12 months.
*   Highlight that while monthly margins fluctuate, the *average* margin is healthy and improving as you optimize your compute efficiency.
*   Frame the variability as a function of current usage patterns that will stabilize as your customer base grows and usage normalizes.

**Do you need a CPA sign-off?**
You do not strictly need a CPA to *sign off* on the methodology before sharing a draft deck with lead investors for a preliminary meeting. However, you absolutely must have your chosen methodology reviewed and validated by a qualified accountant (CPA/ACA) before the final due diligence phase or if you are formalizing audited financial statements.
*   **Immediate Action:** For next week's meeting, ensure your CFO or external accountant agrees that your "strict matching" approach aligns with your current accounting policies.
*   **Future Requirement:** As part of the Series A process, investors will require audited or reviewed financials. At that stage, an external auditor will verify that your COGS recognition matches revenue periods correctly. If you have been smoothing costs in your internal books, you will have to restate them anyway.

Present the real, volatile numbers with a clear narrative. Transparency regarding the "lumpiness" of cloud costs builds more trust than artificial smoothness, which can look like financial engineering.

*Note: This guidance reflects standard SaaS reporting practices but does not constitute formal legal or tax advice. Given the Series A context, a quick validation call with your external auditor is highly advisable before the meeting.*
