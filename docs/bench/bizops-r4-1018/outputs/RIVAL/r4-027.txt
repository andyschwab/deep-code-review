### Legal Requirements for "Carbon Neutral" Claims

**No Federal Mandate for Third-Party Verification:**
Currently, neither federal law nor Oregon state law explicitly *mandates* third-party verification from an accredited body to make a "carbon neutral" claim. You are legally permitted to self-certify based on internal calculations and offset purchases. However, the absence of a mandate does not eliminate liability. The Federal Trade Commission (FTC) and the Oregon Department of Justice enforce laws against deceptive practices, and the burden of proof lies entirely on you to substantiate your claim if challenged.

**The Substantiation Standard (FTC Green Guides):**
Under the FTC's Green Guides (16 C.F.R. Part 260), any environmental claim must be backed by "competent and reliable scientific evidence." For a "carbon neutral" claim, this generally requires:
1.  **Comprehensive Accounting:** You must calculate emissions across the specific scope you claim to cover (e.g., farming and shipping). If you omit significant sources (like manufacturing, dyeing, or retail operations) while implying total neutrality, the claim is deceptive.
2.  **Offset Quality:** The offsets purchased must be real, additional, permanent, and verified by a recognized standard (e.g., Verra, Gold Standard). Receipts alone are insufficient if the underlying project quality is dubious.
3.  **Clear Qualification:** If your calculation does not cover the entire lifecycle of the product, you must clearly qualify the claim (e.g., "Carbon neutral shipping" rather than "Carbon neutral product").

**Oregon Specifics:**
Oregon has aggressive consumer protection laws (ORS 646.605 et seq.) that often mirror or exceed FTC standards. The Oregon Attorney General has historically taken action against vague or unsubstantiated environmental claims. While they do not require a specific seal, they require that the claim not be misleading to a reasonable consumer.

**Risk Assessment of Self-Certification vs. Audit:**
*   **Self-Certification ($0 audit cost):** High legal risk. If the FTC or Oregon AG investigates, you must immediately produce your methodology and data. If your internal calculations are flawed or your offsets are criticized, you face fines, forced corrective advertising, and reputational damage. The $10,000 offset budget is irrelevant if the claim itself is deemed deceptive.
*   **Third-Party Verification ($15,000 cost):** Provides the "competent and reliable scientific evidence" the FTC expects. It acts as an insurance policy against regulatory fines and consumer lawsuits.

**Phrases to Avoid Without Certification:**
If you proceed without third-party verification, avoid absolute, unqualified terms that imply total lifecycle neutrality unless you have irrefutable data:
*   Avoid: "Carbon Neutral," "Net Zero," "Climate Positive," or "100% Carbon Free."
*   Safer Alternatives (if strictly accurate): "We purchase offsets for shipping emissions," "Carbon offsets included for transport," or "Supporting carbon reduction projects."
