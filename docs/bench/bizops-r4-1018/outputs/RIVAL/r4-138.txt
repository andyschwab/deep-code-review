### Unit Economics Impact Analysis

**Current State (Pre-Regulation)**
*   **Revenue per Patient:** $79/mo × 14 months = **$1,106**
*   **Cost per Patient:** $22/mo × 14 months = **$308**
*   **Gross Profit per Patient:** **$798** (72% margin)

**New State (Post-Regulation, Price Unchanged)**
*   **Revenue per Patient:** $79/mo × 14 months = **$1,106** (Assuming retention holds)
*   **Cost per Patient:** $35/mo × 14 months = **$490**
*   **Gross Profit per Patient:** **$616** (56% margin)
*   **Impact:** You lose **$182 in profit per patient** (-23% total profitability).

**The Retention Risk (Churn Sensitivity)**
Your current 4% monthly churn implies an average lifetime of ~25 months mathematically, but your actual observed lifetime is 14 months. This suggests early churn is higher than the steady-state 4%, or the 4% is rising.
*   If price friction increases churn from 4% to **6%**, average lifetime drops from 14 months to ~9 months.
*   **Scenario: 6% Churn @ $79/mo:**
    *   Lifetime Revenue: $711
    *   Lifetime Cost: $315 ($35 × 9)
    *   Gross Profit: **$396** (50% drop from original $798).

**Does the Visit Frequency Justify a Price Increase?**
Yes, fundamentally. The regulatory change altered your **value metric**.
*   **Old Value:** "Ongoing management" (90-day blocks).
*   **New Value:** "Monthly compliance and access" (30-day blocks).
*   You are now delivering 3x the number of touchpoints/consultations for the same therapeutic outcome. The "product" has shifted from a quarterly refill service to a monthly monitoring service.

**Recommendation: Raise Price to $99–$109**
You must pass on the $13/mo cost increase *and* capture value for the increased frequency.
1.  **Price to $99/mo:** Covers the $13 cost hike + $7 value capture.
    *   New Margin @ 14 mo life: ($99 - $35) × 14 = **$896 profit** (Restores and exceeds original profitability).
2.  **Churn Threshold:** You can afford to lose some customers. Even if churn rises to **5.5%** (lifetime ~10.5 months) at $99:
    *   Revenue: $1,039 | Cost: $367 | Profit: **$672**.
    *   This is still healthier than staying at $79 with unchanged churn ($616 profit).

**Strategic Verdict**
Do not absorb the cost. The mandatory follow-ups are a regulatory moat that increases your service intensity; pricing should reflect this "high-touch" compliance necessity. Frame the increase not as a rate hike, but as a plan restructuring to "Monthly Care Plans" necessitated by new safety regulations. Offer an annual pre-pay option at $990 (effectively $82/mo) to lock in cash flow and reduce churn sensitivity among price-elastic users.

*Note: Implement the price increase only for new patients initially to test churn elasticity before migrating existing users.*
*Consider tiering: A lower "refill-only" tier may emerge if competitors attempt to undercut on price, though regulatory overhead makes low-cost models difficult.*
