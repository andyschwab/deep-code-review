**Executive Summary: Transfer Pricing Risk Assessment for CollaborateSDK**

Based on the facts provided, your revenue share agreements with unrelated third-party integration partners generally **do not** trigger formal transfer pricing documentation requirements under OECD guidelines. Transfer pricing rules primarily govern transactions between **related parties** (e.g., a parent company and its subsidiaries, or entities under common control). Since your 40 partners are unrelated third parties acting as independent agents, payments to them are typically classified as standard sales commissions or business expenses rather than intercompany profit shifts.

**Specific Risks and Considerations**

While formal transfer pricing documentation (such as Master Files or Local Files) is likely unnecessary, you face three specific operational and compliance risks as you scale into the US and EU:

1.  **Permanent Establishment (PE) Risk:** This is your most significant exposure. If an integration partner in the US or EU has the authority to conclude contracts on behalf of CollaborateSDK, or if they appear to be a "dependent agent" rather than an independent broker, tax authorities in those jurisdictions may argue that CollaborateSDK has created a taxable presence (Permanent Establishment). This could subject your global revenue, not just the local portion, to corporate income tax in those countries. The 20% revenue share is high enough to attract scrutiny regarding whether these partners are economically dependent on you.
2.  **Withholding Tax Obligations:** Cross-border payments for services or commissions often attract withholding tax requirements in the source country (where the partner is located). Depending on the specific tax treaties between Singapore and the five countries where your partners operate, you may be legally required to withhold a percentage of the commission before payment. Failure to do so can result in penalties and interest liabilities for CollaborateSDK.
3.  **Substance Over Form Scrutiny:** If the "partners" are effectively controlled by your executives, former employees, or related entities despite being legally structured as third parties, tax authorities will reclassify them as related parties. This would immediately trigger full transfer pricing regulations and potential accusations of aggressive tax avoidance.

**Recommendation on Engaging a Specialist**

**Yes, you must engage a qualified international tax specialist before scaling further.**

While you likely do not need a full *transfer pricing policy* for unrelated parties, you urgently require a **cross-border commission compliance framework**. A specialist is needed to:

Attempting to scale this channel without verifying PE status and withholding obligations poses a far greater financial risk than the cost of specialized counsel.
