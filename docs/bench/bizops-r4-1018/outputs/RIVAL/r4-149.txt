Front-loading hardware costs does not automatically destroy fundraising potential, but your current plan creates a specific "cash valley of death" that investors will view as a critical risk. In hardware-heavy models, investors expect capital expenditure (CapEx), but they distinguish between "strategic deployment" and "cash burn without liquidity buffers."

Your current trajectory presents a mathematical red flag:
*   **Current Runway:** 9 months at $80k/mo burn = ~$720k cash on hand.
*   **Proposed Spend:** $600k over 6 months.
*   **Resulting Cash Balance:** If you spend $600k while burning $480k in operating expenses over those same 6 months, you will have spent $1.08M against $720k in cash. You will be insolvent in approximately 4–5 months, well before hitting your 30-unit milestone or your Series A target date.

Investors do not expect you to deplete 100% of your cash reserves to hit a milestone. A bank balance approaching zero signals distress, not growth. It suggests poor capital efficiency and leaves no room for error if deployment slows or technical issues arise. If you arrive at your Series A meeting with $0 in the bank and 30 units deployed, you lose all negotiating leverage; investors can dictate harsh terms because you have no alternative but to accept.

**Recommendation: Slow Down and Restructure**

You should absolutely slow down deployment to preserve a minimum 6-month cash buffer post-milestone. Here is the strategic adjustment:

1.  **Phased Deployment:** Instead of 50 units in 6 months, target 25–30 units. This reduces CapEx to ~$360k–$400k.
2.  **Preserve Liquidity:** By spending ~$400k on hardware and ~$480k on operations over 6 months, your total cash outflow is ~$880k. Against your $720k starting position, you still face a shortfall, indicating your current cash on hand is insufficient for *any* aggressive hardware rollout without additional bridge funding.
3.  **The "Bridge" Reality:** You likely need a small bridge round, convertible note, or equipment financing *now* to fund the hardware, rather than using operating cash. Hardware is an asset; try to finance it via debt or leasing so your equity cash runway covers only the operating burn.
4.  **Narrative Shift:** Do not pitch "we spent all our cash on boxes." Pitch "we leveraged debt/financing to deploy assets, preserving equity cash for growth and operations."

**Verdict:** Do not spend the cash as planned. It will leave you insolvent before the raise. Slow the rollout to match a sustainable burn rate, or secure non-dilutive hardware financing immediately. Investors prefer a founder who hits 30 units with 6 months of runway remaining over one who hits 30 units with $0 in the bank. The growth signal is valuable, but not if it kills the company before the check is written.

*Note: Your current cash position ($720k) is mathematically insufficient to cover both 6 months of operating burn ($480k) and significant hardware deployment without external financing.*
*Consider approaching equipment lessors or venture debt firms immediately to separate hardware CapEx from your operating runway.*
