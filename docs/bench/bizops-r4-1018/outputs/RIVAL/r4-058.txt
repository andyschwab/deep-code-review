### Market Sizing Framework for a Declining Niche

To determine if DBWatch can support a $10M revenue goal, you must shift from a "growth market" mindset to a "high-value extraction" mindset. In declining legacy markets, Total Addressable Market (TAM) is not about future expansion; it is about the immediate capture of existing, urgent spend.

**The Calculation Model**
Do not rely on top-down industry reports, which likely aggregate your niche into broader "database management" categories, diluting your specific opportunity. Instead, use a bottom-up approach based on your known constraints:

1.  **Define the Serviceable Available Market (SAM):**
    *   Total Enterprises: 4,000
    *   Target Penetration (Fortune 500 subset): 5% = 200 companies.
    *   *Correction:* Your prompt states 5% of Fortune 500 uses the stack. If assuming the 4,000 global enterprises includes non-Fortune 500 entities with similar stacks, clarify your total count. If the total global pool is strictly 4,000 companies, and you can reach all of them, your SAM is 4,000. If only 200 are viable targets, your SAM is 200.
    *   *Scenario A (Broad):* 4,000 companies × $200k current spend = $800M total maintenance spend.
    *   *Scenario B (Strict):* 200 companies × $200k current spend = $40M total maintenance spend.

2.  **Determine Your Price Point (Value Capture):**
    *   Customers currently spend $200k/year on maintenance (likely labor + legacy tools).
    *   SaaS monitoring typically replaces a fraction of this cost or prevents catastrophic failure.
    *   If you charge $50k/year (25% of their current spend), you offer immediate savings.
    *   If you charge $100k/year (50% of current spend), you must prove risk mitigation.

3.  **The $10M Revenue Test:**
    *   **Required Customers:** To hit $10M ARR at a $50k price point, you need **200 customers**.
    *   **Feasibility Check:**
        *   If your total global market is 4,000 companies: You need a 5% market share. This is highly achievable for a specialized vendor.
        *   If your total viable market is only 200 companies (the Fortune 500 subset): You need 100% market share at $50k, or 50% share at $100k. This is risky and likely insufficient for venture scale unless you expand use cases or raise prices significantly.

**Strategic Verdict**
If the total global count is truly only 4,000 companies, and only a fraction are Fortune 500 with big budgets, this is likely a **lifestyle business or private equity candidate, not a venture-backed hyper-growth play.** Venture capital usually requires a path to $100M+ ARR. To make this work for VC:
*   **Expand the definition:** Are there mid-market companies with the same critical need?
*   **Increase price:** Can you charge $150k+ by bundling compliance or emergency support?
*   **Adjacency:** Can the tech monitor *other* legacy systems?

**Data Sources & Validation**
*   **Trust:** Internal sales data, direct CIO interviews, and niche user group membership lists. These are more accurate than Gartner/Forrester for dying tech.
*   **Do Not Trust:** Broad "Database Market" reports; they will overestimate your reachable audience.
*   **Hiring a Firm:** **No.** Do not hire a expensive market research firm. Investors in niche B2B care about your *ability to close* the known 4,000 accounts, not a third-party validation of the market size. Instead, spend that budget on hiring two senior sales reps to attempt to close 10 deals. 10 closed deals at $50k ($500k ARR) validates the model better than any report.

*Note: If the math shows a ceiling below $20M ARR, pivot your pitch from "hyper-growth" to "high-margin cash flow" to attract different investor types.*
*Ensure your pricing reflects the critical nature of the system, not just the cost of the software.*
