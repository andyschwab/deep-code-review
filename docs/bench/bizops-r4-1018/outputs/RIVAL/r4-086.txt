### Blended CAC Calculation for PixelPerfect

To resolve the confusion and accurately reflect profitability, you must separate **variable media costs** from **fixed labor costs** and calculate CAC using two distinct lenses: **Paid CAC** (cash efficiency) and **Fully Loaded CAC** (true profitability).

**1. The Data Breakdown**
*   **Total New Customers:** 800 (400 Ads + 400 Conferences)
*   **Variable Media Spend:** $100,000 ($60k Ads + $40k Conferences)
*   **Fixed Labor Cost:** Let's assume the annual fully burdened cost for your two marketing staff is $X. (You did not provide this figure, so we will treat it as a variable).

**2. Method A: Paid CAC (Cash Efficiency)**
This metric tells you how much cash you burn to acquire a customer *before* overhead. It isolates channel efficiency.
*   **Formula:** Total Media Spend / Total New Customers
*   **Calculation:** $100,000 / 800 = **$125 per customer**
*   **Insight:** Your immediate return on ad spend (ROAS) is positive. You pay $125 to get $200 in Year 1 revenue. This confirms both channels are viable on a variable cost basis.

**3. Method B: Fully Loaded Blended CAC (True Profitability)**
This is the metric your sales team needs to understand true profitability. It answers: "After paying for ads, booths, *and* the team running them, how much did each customer actually cost?"
*   **Formula:** (Total Media Spend + Total Marketing Salaries) / Total New Customers
*   **Calculation:** ($100,000 + $X) / 800
*   **Example:** If your two marketers cost $160,000/year combined:
    *   ($100,000 + $160,000) / 800 = **$325 per customer**
*   **Insight:** In this scenario, you are losing $125 on every customer in Year 1 ($200 Revenue - $325 CAC). The business only becomes profitable if the Lifetime Value (LTV) exceeds $325 significantly or if retention extends beyond one year.

**4. How to Factor in Team Time (The Allocation Trap)**
You mentioned the numbers "swing wildly" based on how you allocate salaries. **Stop allocating fixed salaries to specific channels for CAC calculations.**
*   **Why:** Unless you are hiring/firing staff directly based on conference attendance vs. ad spend, their salaries are fixed costs of doing business. Arbitrarily splitting their time (e.g., "Marketer A spent 60% on ads") creates false precision and obscures the real issue: total headcount efficiency.
*   **The Solution:** Treat labor as a layer on top of the blended total.
    1.  Calculate **Channel-Specific Variable CAC** to judge media buys:
        *   Ads: $60k / 400 = $150
        *   Conferences: $40k / 400 = $100
        *   *Conclusion:* Conferences are more efficient on a cash basis.
    2.  Calculate **Total Fully Loaded CAC** using the aggregate method above to judge business health.

**Final Recommendation for Profitability View**
Do not try to assign specific salary dollars to the "conference customer" vs. the "ads customer." Instead, view your marketing team as an engine that drives the entire 800-customer volume.
*   **Step 1:** Confirm your total marketing headcount cost.
*   **Step 2:** Add that to your $100k media spend.
*   **Step 3:** Divide by 800.
*   **Step 4:** Compare this single number against your **Lifetime Value (LTV)**. If LTV > Fully Loaded CAC, you are profitable. If not, you must either increase prices, reduce headcount, or improve retention to extend LTV.

Your sales team should focus on the **$125 Paid CAC** for tactical decisions (e.g., "Should we buy another booth?") and the **Fully Loaded CAC** for strategic decisions (e.g., "Can we afford to hire a third marketer?").

*Note: Ensure you include payroll taxes and benefits in the "salary" figure for an accurate fully loaded cost. If your LTV is less than 3x your Fully Loaded CAC, your current growth model is unsustainable.*
