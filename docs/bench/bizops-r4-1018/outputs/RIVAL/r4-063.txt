### Immediate Reality Check
Hiring a single full-time employee residing and working in a state almost universally creates **income tax nexus** and **withholding obligations** in that state immediately. There is generally no "de minimis" threshold for employee count; one employee is sufficient to trigger filing requirements. The fact that they work from home rather than in a rented office does **not** exempt you; the employee's physical presence constitutes a business presence.

### State-Specific Risks & Requirements

1.  **California:** High risk. CA aggressively pursues nexus. One employee triggers immediate registration with the Franchise Tax Board (FTB) and Employment Development Department (EDD). You will owe state income tax withholding, unemployment insurance (SUI), and potentially a minimum franchise tax ($800/year) if registered as a foreign entity.
2.  **New York:** High risk. NY requires registration if you have employees in the state. You must withhold NY state and city taxes (if the employee lives in NYC/Yonkers/Albany). NY has strict "convenience of the employer" rules, though these primarily affect the employee's liability, your withholding obligation remains.
3.  **Washington:** Moderate/High risk. WA has no state *income* tax, so no income withholding is needed. However, you will likely owe **Workers' Compensation** premiums and **Business & Occupation (B&O) tax** on the payroll allocated to WA. You must register with the Department of Revenue and L&I.
4.  **Florida:** Low/Moderate risk. FL has no state *income* tax. Your primary obligations are **Reemployment Tax** (unemployment) and Workers' Comp registration. You generally do not need to register as a foreign entity solely for income tax purposes, but you must register for unemployment tax.
5.  **Illinois:** Moderate/High risk. IL requires withholding registration and unemployment insurance registration upon hiring the first employee. You may need to register as a foreign entity depending on the nature of the work performed, though some states allow limited registration just for tax purposes.

### Does "Work From Home" Change Nexus Rules?
**No.** For state income tax and withholding purposes, the employee's home is treated as a business location. Your company is considered to be "doing business" in that state because labor is being performed there. This creates a physical presence nexus regardless of lease agreements.

### Do De Minimis Thresholds Exist?
*   **Employee Count:** No. One employee triggers withholding nexus in all five states.
*   **Payroll Dollars:** Some states have thresholds for *corporate income tax* filing based on sales or property, but **withholding tax** obligations usually start with the first dollar of wages paid to a resident.
*   **Foreign Entity Registration:** This varies. Some states allow you to register solely for tax purposes without full foreign qualification if you have no other physical presence. However, CA and NY often require full foreign qualification if you have an employee performing core business functions (like software engineering) within their borders.

### Steps to Remain Compliant

1.  **Do Not Delay Registration:** Register for payroll tax accounts in each state *before* the first paycheck. Penalties for late registration and failure to withhold are severe.
2.  **Engage a PEO or EOR (Recommended):** Given your small team size (5 remote vs. current Texas team) and limited cash ($300k), the administrative burden and cost of registering in 5 states may exceed your $40k estimate. Consider using an **Employer of Record (EOR)** service (e.g., Deel, Remote, Rippling). They legally employ the workers in those states, handling all nexus, taxes, and compliance for a fee. This isolates TestCloud from direct nexus risk.
3.  **If Hiring Directly:**
    *   Register as a foreign entity (or tax-only agent) in CA, NY, IL, WA, and FL.
    *   Set up multi-state payroll processing immediately.
    *   Budget for Workers' Comp policies in each state.
    *   File annual reports and pay minimum franchise taxes where applicable (especially CA).

**Note:** Your $300k cash balance makes the $40k+ annual compliance hit significant; an EOR might cost more per employee but eliminates the need for 5 separate legal registrations and reduces legal liability.
**Note:** Failure to withhold correctly makes *your company* liable for the uncollected taxes plus penalties.
