### Unit Economics Viability Analysis: ChaosKit

Based on the figures provided, a pure sales-led motion is currently **mathematically unsustainable**, while a pure product-led growth (PLG) motion carries significant risk due to projected churn. A hybrid model is likely the only viable path, but it requires specific structural changes to the pricing and onboarding to work.

**1. The Sales-Led Math (The Problem)**
Your current unit economics break down immediately under a sales-led model.
*   **Lifetime Value (LTV):** $45,000
*   **Sales Rep Cost:** $120,000+ (base + commission)
*   **The Breakeven Reality:** To simply cover the cost of one sales rep (ignoring overhead, marketing, and R&D), that rep must close **2.7 deals per year** just to pay for their own salary.
*   **The Ratio Trap:** Healthy SaaS businesses target an LTV:CAC ratio of 3:1. To achieve a 3:1 ratio with a $45k LTV, your maximum allowable CAC is **$15,000**. Your sales rep costs $120,000. This means each rep must close **8 deals annually** just to hit a break-even LTV:CAC of 1:1, and **24 deals annually** to hit a healthy 3:1 ratio.
*   **Conclusion:** Given the long sales cycles in enterprise infrastructure, expecting 24 closed deals per rep per year is unrealistic. The human capital cost destroys the unit economics at your current price point.

**2. The Pure PLG Math (The Risk)**
*   **Churn Impact:** At 5% monthly churn, your average customer lifespan is only 20 months ($1 / 0.05$).
*   **LTV Erosion:** If churn remains at 5%, your $45,000 LTV projection is likely overly optimistic unless your Average Revenue Per User (ARPU) is very high ($2,250/mo). If ARPU is lower, the LTV collapses, making even low-cost acquisition unsustainable.
*   **Conclusion:** Without "hand-holding," the product fails to retain value, destroying the LTV before you can recover acquisition costs.

**3. The Hybrid Model Verdict**
The math only works if you **segment your motion** to align cost-to-serve with revenue potential. You cannot afford human sales for customers who generate less than ~$60k–$80k in LTV.

**Recommended Strategy:**
*   **Self-Serve (PLG) for SMB/Mid-Market:** Target accounts where LTV is < $50k. Invest the savings from not hiring reps into **product-led onboarding** (interactive tutorials, automated success playbooks) to reduce that 5% churn to <2%.
*   **Sales-Assisted for Enterprise:** Reserve human sales *only* for deals where the projected LTV is > $250k. Here, the $120k rep cost is justified because the LTV:CAC ratio remains healthy even with longer cycles.

**Specific Metrics to Model for Your Decision**

To finalize this strategy, build a model varying these four inputs:

1.  **Break-even ARPU for Sales:** Calculate the minimum monthly revenue required per account to justify a human touch. (Formula: *Sales Cost / Target Payback Period*). If your typical deal is below this number, it must be self-serve.
2.  **Churn Sensitivity Curve:** Model LTV at 1%, 2%, 3%, 4%, and 5% monthly churn. Determine exactly how much you can spend on "self-serve hand-holding" (e.g., customer success tools, better docs) to drop churn from 5% to 2% while staying cheaper than a $120k rep.
3.  **Sales Cycle vs. Quota Attainment:** Model how many deals a rep can actually close in 12 months given your specific enterprise cycle length (e.g., 6–9 months). If the math shows <8 deals/year, the role is unviable at current pricing.
4.  **Expansion Revenue (Net Dollar Retention):** In a hybrid model, PLG users should expand over time. Model the % of self-serve users who upgrade to a tier that triggers sales intervention. This "expansion efficiency" often saves the hybrid model.

**Final Note:** Do not hire generalist sales reps yet. Your pricing tiers likely need to increase for enterprise to support the sales cost, or your onboarding must be radically improved to support PLG without human intervention.
