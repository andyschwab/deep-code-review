To bridge the gap between your CTO champion and CFO approver, you must shift your pitch from "technical modernization" to "financial risk arbitration." The CFO does not care about cloud architecture; they care about cash flow, liability, and predictability. You need to present a **Total Cost of Ownership (TCO) vs. Risk-Adjusted Value** model that translates technical debt into line-item expenses.

**1. The Financial Model: The "Cost of Inaction" Calculator**
Stop selling the solution; sell the cost of the problem. Build a dynamic model for the CFO that quantifies the status quo. Your $120k fee should look small compared to the bleeding neck of their current state. Include these four specific line items:

*   **The "Bus Factor" Liability:** Calculate the fully loaded cost of their remaining COBOL developers. If they have three experts averaging $150k/year and one leaves, estimate the recruitment cost ($50k+) and the productivity ramp-up gap (6 months). Frame your platform as "insurance" against a $500k+ operational crisis.
*   **Maintenance Tax vs. Innovation Capex:** Show the ratio of their IT budget spent on "keeping the lights on" (legacy maintenance) versus new revenue-generating features. Legacy systems often consume 70-80% of IT budgets. Demonstrate how your $120k investment shifts $400k/year from maintenance to innovation within 18 months.
*   **Regulatory & Compliance Risk Exposure:** For banks/insurance, downtime or security gaps in legacy systems carry massive fines. Estimate the potential cost of a single compliance failure or outage hour. Position modernization as risk mitigation, not just an upgrade.
*   **Infrastructure Arbitrage:** Compare their current mainframe/hosting costs (often fixed and high) against the variable, scalable cost of the cloud architecture you enable. Show the monthly cash flow improvement.

**2. Dual-Stakeholder Messaging Strategy**
You cannot use one deck for both. You need a "Translator" document that sits between the technical proposal and the financial approval.

*   **For the CTO (The Champion):** Provide deep technical validation. Focus on code fidelity, migration speed, and architectural elegance. Give them the ammunition to prove *feasibility*.
*   **For the CFO (The Decider):** Create a one-page "Investment Memo." Do not mention COBOL, refactoring, or microservices. Use terms like "asset lifecycle extension," "operational leverage," and "liability reduction."
    *   *Bad:* "We refactor legacy code to reduce technical debt."
    *   *Good:* "We convert a depreciating liability into a scalable asset, reducing annual operational run-rate by 35%."

**3. Shortening the Sales Cycle**
The nine-month cycle suggests you are waiting for them to build the business case internally. You must build it *for* them.
*   **Pilot-to-Production Bridge:** Instead of a full $120k commitment upfront, offer a fixed-price "Risk Assessment & Roadmap" engagement for $15k–$20k. This delivers the exact financial model the CFO needs, funded by a smaller budget bucket that requires less approval. Once they pay for the roadmap, they are psychologically committed to the solution.
*   **Outcome-Based Guarantees:** If possible, tie a portion of your fee to specific milestones (e.g., "20% payable only after successful migration of Module X with zero downtime"). This aligns your risk with theirs, soothing CFO anxiety about upfront spend.

Your goal is to make the CFO feel that *not* buying is the riskier financial decision. By quantifying the hidden costs of legacy systems, you transform your platform from an IT expense into a strategic financial necessity.

*Note: Ensure your "Cost of Inaction" model uses conservative estimates; CFOs distrust overly optimistic projections. Use their own internal data for salary and infrastructure costs wherever possible to increase credibility.*
