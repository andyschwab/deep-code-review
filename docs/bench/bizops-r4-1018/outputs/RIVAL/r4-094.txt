In California, the classification of workers is primarily governed by the **ABC Test** (established by *Dynamex* and codified in AB5). To maintain independent contractor status, you must prove all three prongs: (A) the worker is free from control, (B) the work is outside the usual course of your business, and (C) the worker is customarily engaged in an independent trade.

Your proposed changes introduce specific risks to these prongs:

**1. The "Control" Risk (Prong A)**
Increasing financial stakes is generally safe; however, tying **platform governance** rights to revenue share is dangerous. If top contributors gain voting rights on platform rules, fee structures, or acceptance criteria for other developers, this may be interpreted as "control" over the business operations.
*   **Avoid:** Giving contributors veto power, managerial decision-making authority, or the ability to set policies for other users.
*   **Safe Approach:** Limit "governance" to advisory feedback loops (e.g., beta testing features) where the final decision rests solely with SnippetHub management.

**2. The "Usual Course of Business" Risk (Prong B)**
This is often the hardest prong for marketplaces. If SnippetHub's business is "selling code," and the contributors are selling code, they may fail Prong B.
*   **Mitigation:** You must argue your business is "operating a marketplace platform," not "writing/selling code."
*   **Avoid:** Structuring the revenue share so it looks like a salary or guaranteed draw. Ensure contributors can sell their code elsewhere (non-exclusivity is vital). If your new model requires exclusivity or prevents them from working with competitors, you risk failing Prong B and C.

**3. Payment Structure Risks**
Tiered revenue shares based on volume are generally acceptable as performance bonuses for contractors, provided they do not resemble wages.
*   **Avoid:**
    *   **Guaranteed Minimums:** Do not guarantee a specific income floor.
    *   **Hourly Equivalents:** Do not structure payouts that effectively calculate to an hourly wage.
    *   **Benefits:** Do not offer health insurance, paid time off, or equipment stipends to these contributors.
    *   **Integration:** Do not require them to attend mandatory meetings, use specific working hours, or follow an employee handbook.

**Specific Thresholds to Watch**
There is no specific dollar threshold where a contractor becomes an employee; the test is qualitative, not quantitative. However, risk increases significantly if:
*   The contributor derives **100% of their income** from you (suggests economic dependence).
*   You exert **disciplinary control** (e.g., suspending accounts for policy violations in a way that mimics employee discipline rather than platform terms of service enforcement).
*   The "governance" role allows them to hire, fire, or direct the work of other contributors.

**Recommendations for Your New Model**
1.  **Keep Governance Advisory:** Frame any "governance" as a "Top Contributor Council" that provides non-binding recommendations.
2.  **Maintain Non-Exclusivity:** Explicitly state in your terms that contributors are free to sell their code on other platforms or directly to clients.
3.  **Performance-Based, Not Time-Based:** Ensure the tiered revenue share is strictly tied to successful sales transactions, not time spent or activities performed.
4.  **Written Agreement:** Update your Independent Contractor Agreement to explicitly reflect that the revenue share is a commission on sales, not a wage, and reiterate their independence.
