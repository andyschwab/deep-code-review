**General Principles for Revenue Recognition with Usage-Based Pricing**

Under US GAAP (specifically ASC 606), revenue from usage-based pricing models where the final volume is unknown at the time of service delivery generally follows the "right to invoice" practical expedient. This allows you to recognize revenue only as the usage occurs, rather than estimating the total month-end volume in advance.

**Key Principles:**

1.  **Recognition Timing:** You recognize revenue in the period the usage actually happens. Since your customers are charged $0.50 per run, you record $0.50 of revenue for every workflow executed, regardless of when the invoice is generated. The 30% of runs occurring in the last three days of the month are recognized as revenue in the current month, even though the billing event (and cash collection) will happen next month.

2.  **No Deferral Required for Unknown Volume:** You do not need to defer revenue until the month ends or until the invoice is sent. The obligation is satisfied instantly upon each workflow run. The fact that the *total* monthly bill is unknown until day 30 does not prevent you from recognizing the revenue daily as the service is consumed.

3.  **Accrual Accounting:** While you recognize the revenue immediately, you must accrue the corresponding receivable. At month-end, you will have recognized revenue for all 200,000 runs ($100,000), but your accounts receivable will include an accrued amount for the unbilled 60,000 runs ($30,000) that occurred in the final three days. This accrued revenue is a standard balance sheet item, not a deferral.

4.  **Variable Consideration Constraint:** ASC 606 restricts recognizing variable consideration only if it is probable that a significant reversal will occur later. In pure usage-based models with a fixed per-unit price ($0.50) and no minimums or caps, the risk of reversal is negligible because the price is fixed and the usage is factual. Therefore, the constraint usually does not apply, and you can recognize revenue as usage accumulates.

**Regarding the External Auditor:**

You likely do not need an external auditor solely to close your next financial quarter if your accounting team understands accrual basics. The process involves:
*   Tracking daily usage logs.
*   Calculating the total runs for the month.
*   Recognizing revenue for the total count.
*   Booking an accrued receivable for the unbilled portion at month-end.

However, you should engage an auditor or specialized accountant if:
*   Your contracts include complex clauses like minimum commitments, tiered pricing that changes retroactively, or volume discounts that require estimating the final tier before the month ends.
*   Your internal team lacks experience with accrual accounting for unbilled services.
*   You are preparing for an imminent IPO or major funding round where audited financials are immediately required.

For a standard SaaS transition to simple per-unit pricing, establishing a robust internal process to capture end-of-month usage data and book the accrual is typically sufficient for the next quarter close.

*Note: This overview reflects general US GAAP principles for usage-based billing and does not constitute formal legal or certified public accounting advice.*
