The core issue is that "financial advice" implies a fiduciary duty and a guarantee of outcomes, which triggers regulation. "Strategic budgeting intelligence" or "board-ready forecasting" implies high-confidence data support for decision-making, which remains a SaaS utility.

To double your ARR from $1.5M without triggering compliance burdens, consider this strategic pivot for your premium tier:

1.  **Reframe the Output:** Instead of selling "advice," sell "audit-ready certainty." CFOs don't necessarily need you to be a registered advisor; they need defensible numbers they can present to a board without fear of being wrong. Market the premium tier as providing the *methodology documentation*, *variance analysis*, and *scenario modeling* required to defend a budget, rather than the advice itself.
2.  **Change the Value Metric:** Move away from a simple flat fee if you are offering significantly more value. Consider a tiered structure based on "spend under management" or "forecast complexity."
    *   **Standard ($299/mo):** Algorithmic predictions for engineering leads.
    *   **Executive ($1,500+/mo):** Board-ready packages including variance explanations, multiple scenario runs (best/worst/likely case), and executive summary narratives.
3.  **Language Guardrails:** Work with your counsel to draft specific "safe harbor" language. You can claim your tool provides "institutional-grade forecasting data" used *by* CFOs to *formulate* their advice, rather than providing the advice directly. The distinction lies in who makes the final judgment call. If your tool says "You should budget X," it risks being advice. If it says "Based on patterns, X is the 95% confidence interval," it remains data.

Your goal is to sell the *confidence* and *time savings* associated with high-stakes budgeting, not the regulatory status of a financial advisor. By packaging the deliverables as "Board Preparation Assets" rather than "Financial Guidance," you align the price with the high value CFOs place on risk mitigation without necessarily assuming the legal burden of a regulated entity.
