**Potential Compliance Risks and Professional Consultation**

Your proposed trade-in program introduces specific physical presence risks that differ from standard remote sales. While selling 2,000 units remotely generally triggers nexus only if you exceed state-specific economic thresholds (typically $100,000 in sales or 200 transactions), the act of **physically accepting returned hardware** from customers in various jurisdictions can create "physical nexus" instantly, regardless of your sales volume in those specific counties.

In many states, nexus is established not just by having a warehouse, but by any regular presence of company-owned property or agents. If your logistics partner or your own staff enters a state specifically to collect these used ovens, tax authorities could argue you have established a physical footprint. The risk is compounded if the returned units are temporarily stored, sorted, or consolidated in a third-party facility within a state before being shipped to your final recycling or refurbishment center. Even short-term storage of company-owned assets (the returned ovens) can trigger a filing obligation.

Furthermore, the structure of the discount affects how sales tax is calculated on the new unit. In some jurisdictions, sales tax is applied to the net price after the trade-in credit ($749), while others require tax on the full retail price ($899) regardless of the allowance. Misapplying these rules across different counties could lead to under-collection liabilities and penalties during an audit.

Because you are operating with limited cash reserves ($250,000), the cost of retroactive registration, filing back-taxes, and potential penalties in multiple jurisdictions could be financially devastating. The uncertainty of whether a specific county considers the "pickup" of a used appliance as creating nexus requires a definitive interpretation of local statutes that varies significantly by state.
