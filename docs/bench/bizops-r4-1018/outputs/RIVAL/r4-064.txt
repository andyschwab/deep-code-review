**Immediate Verdict: Your current model is not viable for a Series A raise and risks insolvency within seven months without immediate intervention.**

Your unit economics are fundamentally broken. With a €150 Customer Acquisition Cost (CAC) and only €25 Monthly Recurring Revenue (MRR), you are paying six months of revenue just to acquire a customer. Compounded by an 8% monthly churn rate, your average customer lifetime is only 12.5 months. However, because revenue is recognized monthly, the *actual* gross margin-adjusted lifetime value (LTV) is likely closer to €250–€280 (assuming ~80% gross margins), yielding an LTV:CAC ratio of roughly **1.8:1**.

**Investor Benchmarks vs. Your Reality**
European SaaS investors typically demand an LTV:CAC ratio of **3:1 or higher** for Series A consideration. A ratio below 2:1 is a severe red flag indicating that you are buying revenue at a loss. Furthermore, your "payback period" (time to recover CAC) is six months. While <12 months is the standard benchmark, this calculation assumes customers stay long enough to pay. With 8% monthly churn, nearly 40% of your customers will cancel before you recover the €150 you spent to acquire them. You are effectively lighting cash on fire to grow top-line revenue that evaporates before becoming profit.

**Strategic Recommendation: The Order of Operations**

Do not try to fix all three variables (Price, CAC, Churn) simultaneously. You must prioritize based on speed of impact.

1.  **Priority 1: Fix Churn Immediately (Critical)**
    An 8% monthly churn (92% retention) is catastrophic for a SaaS business; healthy benchmarks are 95–98%+ (5% or less churn). This suggests a product-market fit issue or a severe onboarding failure. No amount of pricing optimization saves a leaking bucket. You must diagnose why 8% of users leave every month. Until you get churn below 4-5%, your LTV ceiling is too low to support any CAC.

2.  **Priority 2: Increase Prices (High Impact)**
    Your pricing (€25/mo) is likely too low for a B2B GraphQL federation layer, which is critical infrastructure. Low prices attract low-quality, high-churn customers. Doubling your price to €50/mo instantly doubles your LTV and halves your payback period to 3 months, making the unit economics viable even with current churn. For B2B infrastructure, €25 is often perceived as "too cheap to be reliable."

3.  **Priority 3: Reduce CAC (Secondary)**
    Once churn is stabilized and prices are raised, your allowable CAC increases. Currently, you cannot afford €150 CAC. But if ARPU becomes €50 and churn drops to 4%, your LTV triples, and a €150 CAC becomes acceptable.

**Conclusion**
You cannot raise a Series A with these metrics. Investors will view the 8% churn and <2:1 LTV:CAC as fatal flaws. **Pivot immediately:** Pause aggressive paid acquisition, implement an immediate price increase for new customers (and grandfathering strategy for existing), and dedicate all engineering/product resources to stopping the churn.
