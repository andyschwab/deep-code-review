**What Flips It, and the Trigger**
The single input that changes this recommendation from "proceed" to "halt" is **B2C vs. B2B classification**.
*   **The Trigger:** If any portion of your €600k revenue comes from individual consumers (B2C) rather than registered businesses, EU unfairness standards tighten drastically under the Unfair Commercial Practices Directive.
*   **Rule of Thumb:** B2B contracts allow freedom of contract regarding price variability if agreed upon; B2C dynamic pricing requires rigorous justification and often real-time disclosure.
*   **Actionable Step:** If your client list contains any non-VAT-registered entities, consider Y: segment them immediately into a fixed-price tier before launching the algorithm.

**Regulatory Context & Pitfalls (Tier 2 Explanation)**
Under current EU frameworks (specifically the Digital Services Act and Unfair Commercial Practices Directive), dynamic pricing itself is not prohibited for B2B services. However, the **transparency obligation** is binding.
1.  **Disclosure:** You must clearly disclose the *methodology* of the price variation (e.g., "prices increase by X% when traffic exceeds Y requests/second") before the contract is signed. Hidden algorithms violate transparency norms.
2.  **Non-Discrimination:** In B2B, you may charge different rates based on objective metrics (traffic volume). You cannot discriminate based on protected characteristics or arbitrary factors unrelated to service cost.
3.  **Fixed Alternative:** There is no statutory requirement to offer a fixed-price alternative, but the absence of one may be scrutinized if the dynamic model creates "lock-in" effects during essential service usage.
