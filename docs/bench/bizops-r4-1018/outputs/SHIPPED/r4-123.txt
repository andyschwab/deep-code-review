**The Numbers**
*Formula:* Eligible Credit ≈ (Qualified Research Expenses [QREs]) × (Applicable Credit Rate).
*Your Figures:*
- Monthly Burn: $300,000 / 9 months = ~$33,333/month.
- Annualized Engineering Cost (Assumed 100% of burn for this model): ~$400,000.
- **ASSUMED (Low):** 40% of dev time qualifies as QREs (conservative estimate for dual-use). Result: $160,000 QREs × ~10% credit = **$16,000**.
- **ASSUMED (Base):** 65% of dev time qualifies (standard for deep-tech dual-use). Result: $260,000 QREs × ~10% credit = **$26,000**.
- **ASSUMED (High):** 85% of dev time qualifies (aggressive, high uncertainty). Result: $340,000 QREs × ~10% credit = **$34,000**.

*Note:* The "split" is not between medical/hospitality features, but between "routine development" (non-qualified) and "resolving technological uncertainty" (qualified). Both your medical compliance algorithms and complex hospitality scheduling logic can qualify if they required novel engineering solutions.

**What Flips It, and The Trigger**
The single input that changes the outcome is the **percentage of developer time spent on "technological uncertainty"** versus routine implementation.
*Trigger:* If your team cannot document specific technical hurdles (e.g., "integrating real-time HIPAA-compliant data streams with legacy facility hardware") distinct from standard feature building, your QRE percentage drops to near zero.
*Rule of Thumb:* If >50% of the sprint was solving a known problem using standard tools, it is likely non-qualified.
*This Week's Step:* If you have not already, instruct your lead engineer to draft a one-page memo per major feature from the last year identifying the specific *technical* uncertainty faced and the experiments run to resolve it; if this memo cannot be written, consider pausing the credit claim preparation until technical leads are aligned.
