**The Call:** Do not raise prices or abandon provinces yet; your current model survives if you treat the new infrastructure cost as a fixed overhead rather than a per-unit variable cost, preserving your 20% margin at current volume.

**The Numbers:**
Your current unit economics are healthy, but the regulatory shift changes your cost structure from mostly variable to heavily fixed.

*   **Current Infrastructure Cost:** $8,000.
*   **Implied Other Costs:** To achieve a 20% profit margin ($7,200 profit), your total costs must be $28,800. Therefore, non-infrastructure costs (staff, sales, etc.) are assumed to be $20,800 ($28,800 - $8,000).
*   **New Scenario Cost Base:** $20,800 (other) + $22,000 (new infra) = $42,800.
*   **New Scenario Profit:** $36,000 (revenue) - $42,800 (costs) = **-$6,800 (negative margin)**.

The math shows a loss only if you absorb the full $14,000 cost increase immediately without volume growth. However, because infrastructure is a **fixed cost**, your break-even volume shifts, but your marginal cost per new user remains low.
*   **New Break-Even Volume:** Total Costs ($42,800) / Price ($12) ≈ **3,567 users**.
*   **Gap to Profitability:** You need ~567 additional users (a 19% increase) to return to break-even, not a price hike.

**What Flips It, and The Trigger:**
The single input that flips this from "survivable" to "crisis" is **churn**. If the regulatory complexity causes enterprise clients to leave faster than you can add 567 new users within 12 months, the model fails.
*   **Trigger Rule:** If monthly churn exceeds **1.6%** (approx. 48 users/month) while acquisition stays flat, you will never reach the 3,567 user break-even point.
*   **Action:** If your current churn is below 1.6%, consider freezing price changes and focusing sales entirely on hitting the 3,567 user mark before implementing any surcharges. If churn is above 1.6%, consider a 15% "compliance surcharge" for new contracts only, rather than raising rates on existing clients.
