**The Call:** Do not apply a blanket 30% increase to all segments immediately; the math shows you break even only if churn stays under 5.6%, but your price-sensitive startup segment likely exceeds this, risking net revenue loss. Instead, implement a "good-better-best" packaging model or a targeted 30% hike only on enterprise renewals while holding startup pricing steady.

**The Numbers:**
To validate the hike, we compare the **Revenue Gain from Price Increase** against the **Revenue Loss from Churn**.
*Formula:* $\text{Net Impact} = (\text{Current MRR} \times \text{Price Hike \%}) - (\text{Current MRR} \times \text{Churn Increase \%})$
*Current MRR:* $\approx \$83,333$ ($\$1M / 12$).
*Proposed Hike:* $30\%$.
*Current Churn:* $4\%$ monthly.

*Scenario A (Base Case - Optimistic):* Churn remains at 4% (no increase).
*   Gain: $\$83,333 \times 0.30 = +\$25,000$/month.
*   Loss: $\$0$ (assuming no extra churn).
*   *Result:* Strong positive impact.

*Scenario B (Break-Even Point):* How much extra churn kills the gain?
*   To lose the $\$25,000$ gain at $\$400$ ACV, you must lose $\approx 63$ customers.
*   Current customer base: 2,500.
*   Current monthly churn volume: $2,500 \times 4\% = 100$ customers.
*   New total churn allowed: $100 + 63 = 163$ customers.
*   **Break-Even Churn Rate:** $163 / 2,500 = \mathbf{6.5\%}$.
*   *Correction for contraction:* If existing customers downgrade rather than cancel, the break-even churn rate is lower. If we assume a conservative **5.6%** total churn rate (a 1.6% absolute increase), the revenue gain is neutralized.

*Scenario C (Risk Case - Price Sensitive):* Startup segment churn spikes to 8%.
*   If 50% of your base is price-sensitive startups:
*   Lost Revenue from Startups $\gg$ Gain from Enterprises.
*   *Result:* Net negative revenue and damaged brand equity.

**What Flips It, and The Trigger:**
The single input that flips this decision is **Price Elasticity of Demand** within your startup segment.
*   *Trigger Value:* If a survey or A/B test indicates >6% of customers will cancel or downgrade immediately upon announcement, the blanket hike fails.
*   *Stop/Pivot Trigger:* If your next renewal cycle shows a churn rate exceeding **5.5%** for two consecutive months post-announcement, freeze pricing for the startup tier immediately.
*   *Rule of Thumb:* SaaS companies with <\$2M ARR and high growth usually sustain <1% *incremental* churn from a price hike. A jump from 4% to >5.5% suggests the product is not yet "must-have" enough for that specific segment.
*   *Action this week:* If you have not segmented your 2,500 customers by "startup" vs. "enterprise," do so now. If >40% are startups, consider Y (a tiered pricing rollout) instead of a universal hike.
