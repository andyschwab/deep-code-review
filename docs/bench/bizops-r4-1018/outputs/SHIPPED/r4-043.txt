**The Call:** Do not add the $0.90 audit cost at the current $6.50 price; it flips your unit economics from a modest positive contribution to a loss on every bar sold. You must either secure non-dilutive funding (grants) to cover the audit before implementation or raise the retail price to at least $7.65 to maintain your current margin profile.

**The Numbers:**
*Formula:* Contribution Margin per Unit = Retail Price − (Distributor Fee + COGS + Audit Cost).
*Inputs:* Price $6.50; Distributor Fee 15% ($0.975); Base COGS $2.10; Audit Cost $0.90 (ASSUMED).

*Scenario A: Current Model (No Audit)*
$6.50 − ($0.975 + $2.10) = **$3.425 per bar**.
Annual Contribution (50k units): $171,250.
Less Annual Overhead ($12k/mo): $144,000.
*Projected Operating Profit:* **$27,250**.

*Scenario B: With Audit Cost (No Price Change)*
$6.50 − ($0.975 + $2.10 + $0.90) = **$2.525 per bar**.
Annual Contribution (50k units): $126,250.
Less Annual Overhead: $144,000.
*Projected Operating Loss:* **($17,750)**.

*Scenario C: With Audit Cost & Price Increase to $7.65*
$7.65 − ($1.1475 [15%] + $2.10 + $0.90) = **$3.5025 per bar**.
This restores your margin to slightly above Scenario A.

**What Flips It, and The Trigger:**
The single input that changes the call is the **audit cost absorption method**. If you cannot secure a grant covering at least $45,000 (the total annual audit cost), the trigger to raise prices is immediate.
*Rule of Thumb:* If your price elasticity allows a 17.7% price increase without dropping volume below 42,000 units, raise the price. If volume drops below 42,000 at the higher price, the business model fails with the audit included.
*This Week's Step:* If your current retailer agreements allow price adjustments, draft a communication justifying a $1.15 increase based on "enhanced ethical verification"; if contracts are fixed, pause the audit contract and apply for grants immediately.

**Routing Note:**
Regarding grants and tax credits: Specific eligibility for supply chain auditing offsets depends on federal (IRS) and Oregon-state statutes which change annually.
*Action:* Speak to a **CPA with expertise in Oregon manufacturing credits** first to quantify potential tax offsets; they can often identify specific line items for "business development" or "sustainability" that a general grant specialist might miss.
