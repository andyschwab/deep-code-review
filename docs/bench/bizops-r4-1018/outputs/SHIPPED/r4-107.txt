**The Numbers & Jurisdictional Reality:**
There is no arithmetic formula to solve this; the "input" is the specific statutory text of each state's Nurse Practice Act (NPA) and Board of Nursing administrative code. The "result" of ignoring these inputs is binary: compliance or enforcement action (fines, cease-and-desist, license loss).

In many jurisdictions, "supervision" requires the supervisor to be physically present in the state or licensed within that state, regardless of where the patient is located.

**What Flips It, and The Trigger:**
The single input that changes the verdict is **Multi-State Licensure Status** (via the Nurse Licensure Compact or individual endorsements) and the specific **definition of "practice location"** in each target state's NPA.
*   **The Trigger:** The date you onboard your first patient in a new state. If your RNs are not explicitly authorized to practice *in that state* (which often includes the act of supervising care delivered there), you are operating illegally from day one.
*   **Rule of Thumb:** The law of the state where the *patient receives care* and where the *aide performs tasks* typically governs the supervision requirements, not just where the RN sits. However, some states assert jurisdiction over the RN's physical location too.
