**The Call**
Continue billing in local currencies to maximize conversion; do not delay invoicing or force USD billing due to accounting fears. Recognize revenue at the spot rate on the invoice date, and realize foreign exchange (FX) gains or losses only when the cash is actually converted back to USD or when you revalue unpaid foreign-currency receivables at month-end. Your current volume ($23k/month) is well below the threshold where FX complexity outweighs the sales benefit, but you should engage a CPA specializing in international SaaS once foreign revenue consistently exceeds 20% of total revenue or if you begin holding foreign cash balances for operational expenses.

**The Numbers**
*Formula:* `Reported Revenue (USD) = Invoice Amount (Foreign) × Spot Exchange Rate (Invoice Date)`
*Formula:* `Realized FX Gain/Loss = (Conversion Rate − Invoice Rate) × Amount Converted`

*Current Month Illustration (Labelled Illustrative):*
- **Japan:** $15,000 JPY equivalent invoiced.
  - *Assumption (ASSUMED):* Spot rate on invoice date was 145 JPY/USD.
  - *Result:* $15,000 recognized immediately as USD revenue.
- **Brazil:** $8,000 BRL equivalent invoiced.
  - *Assumption (ASSUMED):* Spot rate on invoice date was 5.0 BRL/USD.
  - *Result:* $8,000 recognized immediately as USD revenue.
- **Total Recognized Revenue:** $23,000 USD.

*FX Impact Scenario (Labelled Illustrative):*
If you hold the JPY receipt for 30 days and the yen weakens to 150 JPY/USD before you convert:
- *Input:* $15,000 value at 145 rate = ~2,175,000 JPY.
- *Conversion:* 2,175,000 JPY / 150 rate = $14,500 USD.
- *Result:* $500 Realized FX Loss recorded in the month of conversion.

**What Flips It, and the Trigger**
The single input that changes the recommendation is **volatility combined with cash retention**. If you start keeping large balances in JPY or BRL to pay local vendors (instead of converting immediately to USD), your balance sheet becomes exposed to unrealized FX swings that must be marked-to-market monthly.
*Trigger:* If foreign currency holdings exceed $50,000 cumulatively or foreign revenue surpasses 20% of total ARR, the accounting burden shifts from "simple monthly adjustment" to "complex hedge accounting consideration."
*Rule of Thumb:* Treat FX variance as noise until it exceeds 5% of your monthly burn; then treat it as a risk line item.
*Step this week:* Configure your payment processor (e.g., Stripe/Paddle) to auto-convert to USD upon receipt to eliminate the need to track foreign cash balances, limiting your exposure to the gap between invoice date and settlement date only.

**Routing Note**
While US GAAP (ASC 830) dictates the functional currency rules described above, the specific treatment of "unrealized" gains/losses on open invoices and any potential state-level tax implications of foreign sourcing require a binding determination. Consult a CPA with international SaaS experience to confirm your "functional currency" status and set up the correct journal entries for month-end revaluation if you choose to hold foreign balances.
