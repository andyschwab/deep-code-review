**The Call**
Do not hire full-time specialized sales reps yet; the math fails because a single rep requires closing ~3.4 deals annually just to cover their cost, which contradicts your long sales cycle reality. Instead, pursue a "high-touch PLG" hybrid: invest in self-serve onboarding to lower the 5% churn, and use founder-led sales or contractors for the first 10 enterprise deals to validate the cycle length before committing to $120k fixed costs.

**The Numbers**
We analyze the break-even point for a sales rep versus the impact of churn on Lifetime Value (LTV).

*Formula:* Annual Rep Cost / Gross Margin = Revenue Required to Cover Rep.
*Formula:* LTV = (Average Revenue Per Account × Gross Margin) / Monthly Churn Rate.

*Inputs & Assumptions:*
- **Rep Cost:** $120,000 base + commission (ASSUMED: total cost $160,000/year including 33% commission/benefits).
- **Gross Margin:** 80% (User provided).
- **LTV (Current):** $45,000 (User provided).
- **Churn Rate:** 5% monthly (User provided, marked HIGH RISK).
- **Sales Cycle:** "Long" (Qualitative, implies >6 months).

*Computed Scenarios:*
1.  **Rep Viability:** To cover a $160k fully loaded rep cost at 80% margin, you need $200,000 in annual revenue per rep. With a $45k LTV, you must close **4.4 new enterprise deals per year** (one every ~2.7 months) just to break even on the salary, ignoring CAC payback period. If the sales cycle is 6+ months, the rep will be cash-flow negative for the first year.
2.  **Churn Sensitivity (The Killer):** At 5% monthly churn, average customer lifespan is 20 months.
    - *Current LTV:* $45k (implies ~$1,800/mo revenue per customer).
    - *If Churn drops to 2% (via better onboarding):* Lifespan extends to 50 months. LTV jumps to **~$90,000** (assuming same monthly revenue).
    - *Impact:* Halving churn doubles your LTV, making the business viable for PLG without needing immediate high-cost sales intervention.

**What Flips It, and The Trigger**
The deciding input is **Monthly Churn**. The high cost of human capital destroys unit economics only if churn remains at 5%.
- *Trigger Threshold:* If you cannot reduce churn below **3% monthly** within 6 months via product improvements, the PLG motion fails regardless of sales staff.
- *Rule of Thumb:* In enterprise infrastructure, >3% monthly churn indicates the product is not "sticky" enough for self-serve; <2% indicates PLG viability.
- *Actionable Step:* If churn is >3% next month, consider pausing enterprise sales hiring and allocating 100% of engineering resources to building guided onboarding flows and in-product education.
