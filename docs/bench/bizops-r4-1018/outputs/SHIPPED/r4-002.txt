**The Numbers & Requirements:**
Alcohol distribution is a "permit-first" regime; operating without the specific state license is not a tax error but an illegal shipment.
*   **Utah:** Operates under a unique state-controlled model (DABC). You generally cannot ship directly to a private wholesaler without navigating the state's specific franchise and listing requirements. *Assumption:* You need a "Non-Resident Seller" license and must list products with the DABC.
*   **Nevada:** Requires a "Certificate of Compliance" from the state where you are licensed (Colorado) plus a Nevada Wholesaler permit application filed by your distributor, but you often need a corresponding "Non-Resident License." *Assumption:* Your distributor must be licensed in NV before you can ship.
*   **Arizona:** Requires a "Non-Resident License" from the Arizona Department of Liquor Licenses and Control. You must also ensure your distributor holds an active wholesale license.
*   **Tax:** Excise tax is typically paid by the wholesaler upon removal, but you must be registered to ensure the tax chain is unbroken. *Assumption:* Failure to register means the wholesaler cannot legally take possession, stalling payment.

**What Flips It, and The Trigger:**
The trigger is the **first bill of lading**. If you ship before the state issues your specific non-resident license number, the shipment is contraband.
*   **Flip Input:** The relationship with your distributor. In many states (like NV and AZ), the distributor files key parts of the permit application. If your distributor is not fully licensed in the target state, your application cannot be completed.
*   **Rule of Thumb:** If the state does not issue you a license number before the truck leaves Colorado, the goods will be seized or rejected.
