**The Call**
Reject the client's argument that your proactive detection creates a new legal duty of care; your existing Terms of Service (ToS) limiting liability to three months of fees is the primary shield, but you must immediately verify that your contract explicitly defines the service as "detection-only" and not a guarantee of security or a managed security service.

**The Numbers**
*Current Exposure Cap:* $1,000 (monthly fee) × 3 months = **$3,000 per client**.
*Total Theoretical Cap (if all 150 clients sue simultaneously):* $3,000 × 150 = **$450,000**.
*Assumption:* This calculation assumes a court upholds your limitation of liability clause. In many jurisdictions, clauses attempting to limit liability for gross negligence or willful misconduct are unenforceable (marked ASSUMED: enforceability varies by state/country).
*Illustrative Risk:* If a court deems your service a "guarantee" due to marketing language or specific SLAs, the cap could be pierced, exposing you to the full cost of a data breach (often millions), which is not computed here due to lack of breach cost data.

**What Flips It, and the Trigger**
The one input that changes the call is the **specific wording of your Service Level Agreement (SLA) and marketing materials**. If you promise "100% detection," "complete security," or imply a duty to *prevent* breaches rather than *scan* for them, a court may find an implied warranty that overrides your liability cap.
*Trigger:* If your marketing or contracts use the words "guarantee," "prevent," or "managed security" instead of "scanning," "detection," or "advisory," consider revising your legal counsel engagement immediately.
*Rule of Thumb:* Courts generally distinguish between a tool that provides information (scanner) and a service that assumes responsibility for outcomes (managed security).
*Step this week:* If your ToS does not explicitly state "Service provides detection alerts only and does not guarantee the absence of vulnerabilities," consider adding this disclaimer to your next contract renewal or click-wrap update.

**Routing Note**
This is a jurisdiction-specific determination of tort law (negligence) and contract law (enforceability of liability caps). You must consult qualified legal counsel in your primary operating jurisdiction and the jurisdictions of your largest clients to confirm whether your specific ToS language holds up against a negligence claim, especially regarding "gross negligence" which often cannot be contractually limited.
