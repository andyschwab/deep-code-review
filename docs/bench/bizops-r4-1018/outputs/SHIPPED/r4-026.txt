**The Call**
Adopt a blended annual average cost for internal unit economics and pricing decisions to prevent seasonal distortion, but maintain specific monthly actuals for cash-flow management and tax filings. Do not attribute the full summer spike solely to summer units sold if those units are held in inventory for later sale; instead, treat storage as an overhead absorbed by all units produced or sold during the year.

**The Numbers**
To model this, calculate your Blended Monthly Storage Cost and apply it to your production volume.

*Formula:*
`Blended Monthly Cost = (Sum of 12 Months Storage Costs) / 12`
`Cost Per Pound (Storage) = Blended Monthly Cost / Monthly Production Pounds`

*User Figures & Assumptions:*
*   **Winter Cost:** $2,100 (Given)
*   **Summer Cost:** $3,500 (Given)
*   **Seasonality Assumption:** Assuming a standard 4-month summer peak and 8-month off-peak cycle based on "significant" seasonal fluctuation description.
*   **Annual Storage Total:** ($3,500 × 4) + ($2,100 × 8) = $14,000 + $16,800 = $30,800.
*   **Blended Monthly Cost:** $30,800 / 12 = **$2,567**.
*   **Production Volume:** 5,000 lbs/month.

*Results:*
*   **Summer-Specific Cost Per LB:** $3,500 / 5,000 = **$0.70/lb** (Distorts margin if used alone).
*   **Winter-Specific Cost Per LB:** $2,100 / 5,000 = **$0.42/lb**.
*   **Blended Cost Per LB (Recommended):** $2,567 / 5,000 = **$0.51/lb**.

Using the blended rate ($0.51/lb) stabilizes your unit economics. If you use the summer rate ($0.70/lb) for pricing decisions, you risk overpricing during winter or panicking about summer profitability unnecessarily.

**What Flips It, and The Trigger**
The decision flips if your inventory turnover slows significantly (e.g., you produce in summer but don't sell until winter).
*   **Trigger:** If inventory days on hand exceed 60 days, you must shift from a production-based absorption to an inventory-based allocation where storage costs capitalize into the inventory asset value on the balance sheet rather than expensing immediately to COGS.
*   **Rule of Thumb:** If you are selling what you make within 30 days, the blended production method above holds.
*   **Step this week:** If your inventory turnover is under 30 days, update your pricing model to use the $0.51/lb storage figure immediately; if over 60 days, consult your accountant about capitalizing storage costs into inventory valuation.

**Routing Note**
For investor reports and tax purposes, the binding determination of whether to capitalize storage costs into inventory (GAAP/IRS rules) versus expensing them as period costs depends on your specific inventory flow and accounting method (cash vs. accrual). Bring your monthly production logs and inventory aging report to a CPA to finalize the treatment for official filings.
