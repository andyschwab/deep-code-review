**The Call**
Yes, recognizing $4M as a deferred revenue liability rather than earned income artificially inflates your leverage ratios and suppresses your apparent liquidity, which is a standard red flag for debt lenders; however, this is a routine accounting presentation in subscription healthcare, not a deal-breaker. You must present an "adjusted" financial view that reclassifies this liability as operational cash flow available for debt service, rather than trying to change the GAAP treatment.

**The Numbers**
Lenders typically assess risk using the **Current Ratio** (Current Assets / Current Liabilities) and **Debt-to-Equity**. Under strict GAAP:
*   **Current Liabilities:** Inflated by $4M (the full unearned balance).
*   **Reported Equity:** Suppressed by $4M.
*   **Formula for Adjustment:** `Adjusted Liquidity = Cash on Hand + (Deferred Revenue × Service Completion Rate)`.

*Assumptions (marked ASSUMED):*
1.  **Service Completion Rate:** Since delivery is gradual over 12 months, assume a straight-line recognition. If you are mid-year, ~50% is effectively "earned" operationally even if not yet booked as revenue.
2.  **Cash Burn Rate:** ASSUMED low, as costs (blood panels, consults) are variable per member visit rather than fixed upfront.

*Illustrative Impact:*
If the lender sees $4M in liabilities against $2M in requested debt, your leverage looks high. If you present a schedule showing that $3.3M of that liability (representing 10 months of future service) is backed by cash already in the bank and requires no immediate outflow, the *effective* liability drops drastically. The "risk" is not the liability itself, but the lender's fear that you cannot refund members if you fail. Your mitigation is proving the cash is already there to cover the service, not a refund.

**What Flips It, and the Trigger**
The single input that changes the lender's mind is the **Quality of Earnings (QoE) Adjustment Schedule**.
*   **Trigger:** If you submit your application without a QoE addendum explicitly normalizing EBITDA and adjusting working capital to add back deferred revenue as "cash available for debt service," the algorithmic credit score will likely reject the deal or price it prohibitively.
*   **Rule of Thumb:** Lenders in recurring revenue healthcare typically apply a 0.8x to 1.0x multiplier to Annual Recurring Revenue (ARR) for borrowing base calculations, ignoring the GAAP liability line if the churn is low (<5% annually, ASSUMED).
*   **Actionable Step:** If your current financial package only includes standard GAAP balance sheets, consider commissioning a "Pro Forma Adjusted Balance Sheet" from your CPA this week that explicitly lists "Deferred Revenue" as a separate line item with a footnote stating: "Non-cash liability representing prepaid services; cash retained for operational deployment."

**Routing Note**
While the accounting principle (ASC 606) mandates deferred revenue recognition for upfront fees, the specific **covenant calculations** and **borrowing base eligibility** for that $2M facility are binding legal and financial determinations. You must engage a CPA with healthcare SaaS/subscription experience to draft the Quality of Earnings report and a finance attorney to negotiate the definition of "Indebtedness" in the credit agreement to ensure deferred revenue is excluded from leverage covenants.
