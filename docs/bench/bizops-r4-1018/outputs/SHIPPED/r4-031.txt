**The Call**
Do not create separate SKUs or unilaterally adjust your wholesale price by a flat $0.18; instead, maintain a single SKU for now but contractually shift the tax liability and remittance obligation to your distributors or retailers via written agreements, and defer hiring a specialized tax attorney until you have signed letters of intent from partners in those specific jurisdictions.

**The Numbers**
Your current unit economics show a gross margin of $1.60 per can ($2.50 price - $0.90 cost).
*Formula:* New Margin = Current Margin - (Tax Burden Absorbed by Manufacturer).
*Assumption (Low):* Tax is fully passed to the consumer; Manufacturer Burden = $0.00. Result: $1.60 margin.
*Assumption (Base/Your Plan):* You absorb a flat $0.18 to simplify pricing. Result: $1.42 margin (11% margin compression).
*Assumption (High):* Seattle/Berkeley rates differ or apply to volume tiers, and you misestimate by 20%. Result: Variable margin erosion + potential penalty risk.
*Runway Impact:* At 15,000 units/month, absorbing $0.18/can costs $2,700/month. Over a 6-month rollout, this consumes $16,200 of your $45,000 reserve (36% of expansion capital) purely on a tax guess.

**What Flips It, and the Trigger**
The one input that changes this call is the **specific statutory definition of the "taxpayer"** in the Seattle and Berkeley ordinances.
*Trigger:* If the ordinance legally defines the *manufacturer* or *distributor* as the liable party (rather than the retailer), your current plan to "let retailers handle remittance" fails without a contract.
*Rule of Thumb:* Never absorb a variable municipal tax into a fixed wholesale price; it destroys margin predictability.
*Step this week:* Draft a "Tax Pass-Through" clause for your distributor agreements stating that any applicable local beverage tax is added to the invoice as a separate line item and is the legal responsibility of the downstream seller to remit, then pause final pricing until a local distributor confirms their willingness to sign it.

**Routing Note**
While the operational workaround is contractual shifting, the binding determination of whether Berkeley's or Seattle's specific ordinance *allows* the liability to be contractually transferred to the retailer, or if it strictly binds the first distributor/manufacturer regardless of contract, is a tier-3 legal question. Bring the draft "Tax Pass-Through" clause to a general business attorney in Oregon or Washington for a one-hour review before signing any distribution deals; do not engage a specialized tax litigator yet.
