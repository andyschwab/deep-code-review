**The Numbers & Rules (Illustrative):**
Sales tax on bundled services depends on whether the state views the "true object" of the transaction as the taxable item (lodging) or the service (therapy), or if it requires strict apportionment.
*   **California:** Generally requires apportionment if items are separately stated. If you sell a single lump sum without breaking out the cost of the room vs. the therapy, the entire amount may be taxed as the predominant item (likely lodging/hotel occupancy).
    *   *Taxable:* Lodging (occupancy tax + sales tax), Food (if prepared/meals).
    *   *Non-Taxable:* Therapy services (professional services are generally exempt).
    *   *Risk:* If you don't separate the $2,200, you may owe tax on the therapy portion you didn't collect, or owe penalties for collecting tax where none was due if the state deems the service dominant.
*   **New York:** Strict apportionment rules apply. If the taxable and non-taxable components are not separately stated on the invoice, the entire receipt is often subject to tax.
    *   *Taxable:* Lodging (hotel occupancy tax), Food (prepared meals).
    *   *Non-Taxable:* Therapy (if performed by licensed professionals).
    *   *Nexus Threshold:* Economic nexus is $500,000 in sales AND 100 transactions. You are currently below the sales threshold ($350k), but physical presence (owning/leasing property or having employees) creates immediate nexus regardless of sales volume.
*   **Florida:** Similar to NY, if the price is not broken out, the entire bundle is taxable.
    *   *Taxable:* Lodging (transient rental tax), Food (meals).
    *   *Non-Taxable:* Therapy services.
    *   *Nexus Threshold:* Physical presence creates nexus immediately. Economic nexus is $100,000 in taxable sales.

**What Flips It:** The presence of a detailed invoice breaking out the specific dollar amount for lodging, meals, and therapy. Without this line-item separation, states default to taxing the full bundle.
