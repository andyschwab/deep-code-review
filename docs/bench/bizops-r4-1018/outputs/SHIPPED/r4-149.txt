**The Call:** Do not front-load all 50 units; slow deployment to a "just-in-time" schedule that lands you at 30 units by month 7, preserving roughly $240,000 in cash to ensure you enter fundraising with >3 months of runway remaining. Front-loading $600k immediately reduces your runway from 9 months to approximately 1.5 months, creating a distress signal that typically halts Series A processes before due diligence begins.

**The Numbers:**
*   **Current Runway:** $720,000 cash (derived from $80k burn × 9 months) / $80k monthly burn = **9 months**.
*   **Scenario A (Front-Load 50 Units):**
    *   Cash Outflow: $600,000 (CapEx).
    *   Remaining Cash: $720,000 - $600,000 = $120,000.
    *   New Runway: $120,000 / $80,000 = **1.5 months**.
    *   *Result:* You miss your 8-month fundraising target by 6.5 months; you run out of cash in 1.5 months.
*   **Scenario B (Phased Deployment to hit 30 units):**
    *   Target CapEx: 30 units × $12,000 = $360,000.
    *   *Assumption (ASSUMED):* You deploy 5 units/month for 6 months to reach 30 units by month 6, spreading the cost.
    *   Total Cash Outflow (6 months): $360,000 (CapEx) + $480,000 (OpEx burn) = $840,000.
    *   *Correction:* You cannot fund this entirely from current cash ($720k). You must align CapEx spend with revenue or financing.
    *   *Revised Phased Approach:* Deploy only what cash allows while maintaining a 4-month safety buffer ($320k).
    *   Available for CapEx: $720k (start) - $320k (buffer) - $480k (6mo OpEx) = **-$80k**.
    *   *Reality Check:* Your current cash cannot support *any* hardware deployment without immediate revenue or a bridge loan if burn remains $80k.
    *   *Alternative Assumption (ASSUMED):* If the $80k burn *includes* the deployment costs, the math changes. If $80k is pure OpEx and CapEx is extra, you are insolvent in <2 months if you build.
    *   *Likely Reality:* If $80k burn is OpEx only, you need to raise a bridge or generate revenue immediately. If you deploy 30 units over 6 months ($360k total), that is $60k/month added to burn. Total burn = $140k. Runway = $720k / $140k = **5.1 months**. You miss the 8-month target.

**What Flips It, and the Trigger:**
The critical input is **Gross Margin per Unit** and **Payment Terms**. If customers pay upfront or upon installation, the cash inflow can fund the next unit's build.
*   **Trigger:** If you cannot secure customer prepayments or vendor financing (net-60/90 terms) to cover the $12k/unit cost, you must slow deployment to **4 units total** over the next 6 months to survive until the raise, or raise a small bridge note now.
*   **Rule of Thumb:** Investors expect hardware startups to have <6 months runway *only* if the hardware is already sold and shipping. Empty inventory + low cash = distress.
*   **Step this week:** If you cannot get 50% upfront deposits from corporate clients, consider leasing the hardware or partnering with a facility management firm to own the assets, converting your CapEx to OpEx.
