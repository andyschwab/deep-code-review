**The Call**
Do not set aside a separate cash pool; instead, record an accrued liability on your income statement based on the probable refund amount. Your current data (8% claim rate) provides a reliable basis for this estimate, which is required to present "clean books" under standard accrual accounting principles.

**The Numbers**
Under accrual accounting (e.g., US GAAP ASC 450 or IFRS IAS 37), you must recognize a liability when a loss is *probable* and *reasonably estimable*. A money-back guarantee tied to performance metrics creates a variable consideration that reduces revenue or creates an expense in the period the service is delivered, not when the refund is paid.

*Formula:*
`Monthly Accrual = (Active Customers × Monthly Fee) × Historical Claim Rate`

*Your Figures:*
*   Active Customers: 500
*   Monthly Fee: $500
*   Historical Claim Rate: 8% (0.08)
*   Total Monthly Revenue at Risk: $250,000

*Computed Result:*
`$250,000 × 0.08 = $20,000 per month`

*Assumptions & Sensitivity:*
*   **Base Case (8%):** You accrue **$20,000/month**. Over a quarter, this matches your actual cash outflow ($60,000), smoothing your P&L.
*   **Low Case (5%):** If optimization improves, accrual drops to **$12,500/month**.
*   **High Case (12%):** If new customers have harder-to-tune databases, accrual rises to **$30,000/month**.

**What Flips It, and the Trigger**
The single input that changes the accounting treatment is the **reliability of the 8% estimate**. If your customer base or database types shift significantly (e.g., you pivot from e-commerce to fintech with stricter latency needs), the historical rate may no longer be predictive.
*   **Trigger:** If the claim rate deviates by >2% from the 8% baseline for two consecutive months, recalculate the accrual rate immediately.
*   **Rule of Thumb:** Treat the 8% as a running average, not a fixed constant.
*   **This Week's Step:** If your claim rate holds steady, consider tightening the guarantee terms (e.g., extending the window to 90 days or adding specific exclusion criteria for legacy schemas) to lower the probability factor.

**Routing Note**
While the math above dictates the *method*, the specific classification (contra-revenue vs. operating expense) and the sufficiency of your data to meet the "probable" threshold for your auditors is a Tier 3 determination. Bring your quarterly claim logs and customer contract language to your CPA to finalize the journal entries before closing your seed round due diligence.
