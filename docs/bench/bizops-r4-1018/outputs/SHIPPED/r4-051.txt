**The Numbers**
We analyze viability using **Contribution Margin** and **Break-Even Volume**.

*Formula:*
`Contribution Margin per Unit = Price - Variable Cost`
`Total Contribution = Contribution Margin × Volume`
`Net Profit = Total Contribution - Fixed Costs`

*Current State (Base Case):*
*   Price: $0.0020
*   Variable Cost: $0.0008
*   **Margin/Unit:** $0.0012
*   Volume: 50M
*   **Total Contribution:** $60,000
*   Fixed Costs: $15,000
*   **Net Profit:** $45,000

*Proposed Tier (Acme Scenario - ASSUMED Acme moves entire 50M to new tier for worst-case):*
*   Price: $0.0012
*   Variable Cost: $0.0008
*   **Margin/Unit:** $0.0004 (66% reduction in margin)
*   Volume: 50M
*   **Total Contribution:** $20,000
*   Fixed Costs: $15,000
*   **Net Profit:** $5,000

*Sensitivity (If volume drops 10% or variable costs rise 10%):*
*   If Volume = 45M: Contribution = $18,000 → Net Profit = $3,000.
*   If Var Cost = $0.0009: Margin = $0.0003 → Contribution = $15,000 → **Net Profit = $0 (Break-even)**.
*   **Break-Even Volume at $0.0012 price:** $15,000 / $0.0004 = **37.5 million requests**. You must process at least 37.5M requests just to cover costs; anything below this loses money.

**What Flips It, and the Trigger**
The decision flips on **Variable Cost Stability**. Your margin per unit shrinks to $0.0004. If your infrastructure costs rise by just $0.0001 per unit (to $0.0009), your profit vanishes.
*Trigger:* If your average variable cost exceeds **$0.0009** in any month where Acme is on the discounted tier, you are effectively working for free.
*Rule of Thumb:* Never discount below 3x your variable cost unless volume is guaranteed to offset the margin loss. Here, $0.0012 is only 1.5x your variable cost.
