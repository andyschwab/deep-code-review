**The Call:** Do not hire a local firm in each country immediately; your primary obligation is likely a single EU-wide VAT registration (via the OSS scheme) rather than three separate local filings, but you must verify if your specific "data transfer" classification triggers Digital Services Tax (DST) in France, Italy, or Spain, as DST rules differ from VAT.

**The Numbers & Rules:**
*   **VAT Nexus:** In the EU, B2B (Business-to-Business) digital services generally follow the "reverse charge" mechanism where the customer accounts for VAT, requiring no registration from you if you only serve businesses. However, if you serve B2C (consumers) in France, Italy, or Spain, you have an immediate VAT obligation.
    *   *Threshold:* The EU-wide distance selling threshold is €10,000. Your €100,000 revenue exceeds this.
    *   *Mechanism:* Instead of registering locally in three countries, you typically register for the **One Stop Shop (OSS)** in your home country (Estonia). You file one quarterly return in Estonia covering all EU B2C sales, paying the respective VAT rates (France ~20%, Italy 22%, Spain 21%) into the Estonian system, which distributes them.
    *   *Assumption:* This assumes your customers are a mix of B2B and B2C. If 100% B2B, you may need zero VAT registration, only valid VAT IDs from customers.
*   **Digital Services Tax (DST):** This is distinct from VAT. It is a tax on *revenue* (not value added) for specific digital activities.
    *   *France:* Has an active DST (3%) on digital intermediation and targeted advertising. Data transmission *might* be excluded depending on the specific legal characterization of "queue messaging" vs. "digital interface."
    *   *Italy & Spain:* Have enacted DST laws, but enforcement and specific scope regarding "data transmission fees" vary and are subject to ongoing international trade disputes and exemptions for smaller entities (global revenue thresholds often apply, e.g., €750M global turnover).
    *   *Computed Figure:* If DST applies in France at 3% on your €100k (unlikely due to global revenue thresholds usually required, but theoretically): €3,000 liability. *Note: Most DSTs require significant global revenue (€750M+) to trigger, which a startup likely does not meet.*

**What Flips It:**
The classification of your customer base (B2B vs. B2C) and your total *global* annual revenue.
*   *Trigger:* If >90% of your customers are businesses with valid VAT IDs, your immediate filing requirement drops to near zero (reverse charge). If you have significant B2C volume, you must register for OSS in Estonia within 30 days of crossing the threshold.
*   *Stop/Pivot:* If your global revenue exceeds €750 million, DST becomes a critical Tier-3 issue. Until then, DST is likely not applicable due to minimum revenue thresholds in those jurisdictions.
