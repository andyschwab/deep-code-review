**The Call:** Do not register for VAT in every EU country immediately; instead, utilize the EU-wide €10,000 distance-selling threshold to simplify your launch, but you must register for the One Stop Shop (OSS) scheme before your first sale to legally collect and remit VAT.

**The Numbers:**
Your projected first-year EU revenue is **€130,000** (2,000 units × €65 AOV).
*   **EU Wide Threshold:** €10,000 (applies to total cross-border B2C sales of goods within the EU).
*   **Your Status:** You are **€120,000 over** the threshold immediately upon hitting your projection.
*   **ASSUMED:** Your 2,000 units are sold to consumers (B2C), not businesses (B2B). If any sales are B2B, the threshold calculation changes, but the obligation to charge VAT generally shifts to the customer via reverse charge.

Because your projected volume (€130k) vastly exceeds the €10,000 threshold, you cannot rely on the "home country VAT rule" (charging only US/Vermont taxes or a single EU rate without registration). Under current EU rules (post-2021), once you exceed €10,000 in total cross-border sales to EU consumers, you must charge the specific VAT rate of the **customer's country** for every transaction.

**What Flips It, and the Trigger:**
The trigger is your **first sale** to an EU consumer if you anticipate exceeding €10,000 in the calendar year. Since your projection is €130,000, you trigger the obligation to charge destination-country VAT rates immediately.
*   **Pivot Point:** If you could guarantee staying under €10,000 total EU sales for the entire calendar year, you would only need to account for VAT in one jurisdiction (often simplified).
*   **Rule of Thumb:** The €10,000 limit is a hard regulatory cliff, not a soft target. Exceeding it by €1 requires full compliance.
*   **This Week's Step:** If your launch date is within 30 days, **consider hiring a VAT compliance specialist or using a Merchant of Record (MoR)** immediately, as manual OSS filing across 27 countries is administratively prohibitive for a small finance team.
