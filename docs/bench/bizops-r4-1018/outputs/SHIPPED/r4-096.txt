**The Call**
Reclassify the $300,000 in unused credits immediately as deferred revenue (a liability); recognizing it as earned revenue now is incorrect under standard accounting principles and creates a material risk of restatement that will damage credibility during due diligence for your next funding round.

**The Numbers**
*Formula:* Deferred Revenue Liability = Total Cash Received for Unused Credits − Revenue Recognized as Credits Are Redeemed.
*Current State (Incorrect):* $300,000 recognized as Revenue.
*Corrected State (Illustrative):*
*   **Liability Increase:** +$300,000 (Balance Sheet).
*   **Revenue Reduction:** −$300,000 (Income Statement, cumulative).
*   **Cash Flow Impact:** $0 change to *operating* cash flow (cash was received), but a reclassification from "Cash from Operations" to "Change in Deferred Revenue" within the operating section.
*   **ASSUMED (Low/Base/High):** The redemption rate over the next 18 months. If 10% redeem monthly, you recognize $30k/month. If 2% redeem, you recognize $6k/month. The liability persists until redemption or expiration.

**What Flips It, and the Trigger**
The single input that dictates your P&L shape is the **redemption velocity**.
*   **Trigger:** If redemption averages <5% per month, your recognized revenue drops sharply compared to your current model, potentially showing a loss instead of profit.
*   **Rule of Thumb:** Revenue is recognized only when the service is delivered (build minutes consumed), not when cash is collected.
*   **Step this week:** If your current financials show profitability solely due to the $300k inclusion, consider pausing aggressive hiring until the organic $45k/mo (growing at 15%) proves sufficient runway, as the "profit" was an accounting artifact.

**Routing Note**
This is a binding accounting determination under IFRS 15 (applicable in Berlin/EU) and likely US GAAP if raising US capital. You must engage your auditor or a qualified CPA to formally book the journal entry reclassifying the $300k from Retained Earnings/Revenue to Deferred Revenue. Do not attempt to structure the expiration terms of these credits to accelerate revenue recognition without legal counsel, as consumer protection laws in the EU may restrict forfeiture of prepaid funds.

Offering prepaid units is standard in SaaS and infrastructure (e.g., AWS credits, Twilio prepay) and does not complicate planning if modeled correctly; the complexity arises only from misstating the liability. Continuing your current method risks a "qualified" audit opinion or a requirement to restate prior periods during investor due diligence, which often kills early-stage rounds. Investors prefer seeing the liability clearly on the balance sheet as it represents a future obligation you have already been paid to fulfill.
