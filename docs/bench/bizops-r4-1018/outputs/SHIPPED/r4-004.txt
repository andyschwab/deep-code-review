**The Call**
Stay with the weekly subscription for now; switching to a per-meal credit system is likely to worsen your unit economics by increasing food waste beyond your current 8% and eroding contribution margin, unless you can prove churn drops below 6% monthly to offset the operational inefficiency. The fixed cost coverage ($8,000/month) is currently precarious under both models without volume growth, but the subscription model provides the forecasting stability required to keep waste contained.

**The Numbers**
*Current Weekly Model (Per Customer/Month):*
- Revenue: $60/week × 4.3 weeks = $258
- Variable Food Cost: 5 meals/week × 4.3 weeks × $5/meal = $107.50
- Waste Cost (8%): $107.50 × 0.08 = $8.60
- Total Variable Cost: $116.10
- **Contribution Margin (CM): $141.90 per customer/month**

*Hypothetical Per-Meal Credit Model:*
- Assumption: Price per meal remains $12 (no discount for flexibility).
- Assumption: Waste increases to 15% due to irregular forecasting (industry standard for on-demand vs. subscription).
- Assumption: Order volume drops 10% initially as users "skip" weeks they previously paid for (behavioral friction).
- Adjusted Revenue: $258 × 0.90 = $232.20
- Adjusted Food Cost: $107.50 × 0.90 = $96.75
- Waste Cost (15%): $96.75 × 0.15 = $14.51
- Total Variable Cost: $111.26
- **Projected CM: $120.94 per customer/month**

*Break-Even Analysis (Fixed Costs $8,000):*
- Weekly Model Customers Needed: $8,000 / $141.90 ≈ **57 customers**
- Per-Meal Model Customers Needed: $8,000 / $120.94 ≈ **67 customers**

**What Flips It, and the Trigger**
The single input that changes this call is **Churn Rate**. If switching to per-meal credits reduces your monthly churn from 12% to below **6%**, the increased Customer Lifetime Value (LTV) could outweigh the immediate margin compression from higher waste and lower weekly revenue.
*Trigger:* Run a 4-week A/B test with 20% of your list offered the credit option. If their waste-adjusted margin exceeds $141.90 *and* retention improves by 50% relative to the control group by next month, consider pivoting. Until then, the "flexibility tax" (waste + skipped meals) is too high.
