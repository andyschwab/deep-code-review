**The Call:** Pause the 5,000-unit production run immediately. FDA clearance for the medical function does **not** exempt the Bluetooth radio from FCC certification; you must obtain FCC approval (or validate an existing module certification) before shipping or even importing the devices into the US.

**The Numbers & Regulatory Reality:**
There is no "medical exemption" for radio frequency interference under FCC rules (47 CFR). The FDA regulates safety and efficacy for patients; the FCC regulates electromagnetic interference to ensure your device doesn't disrupt other communications.
*   **Cost of Compliance (Estimated):** If you use a pre-certified Bluetooth module correctly, validation costs are typically $2,000–$5,000. If you designed the radio circuitry yourself, full certification testing ranges from $8,000 to $15,000.
*   **Penalty Exposure:** The FCC base forfeiture penalty for marketing or importing non-compliant equipment is currently **$19,693 per violation** (per unit). On a run of 5,000 units, theoretical maximum exposure exceeds **$98 million**, though settlements often occur in the hundreds of thousands plus mandatory recall costs.
*   **Assumption:** You are importing or selling in the US interstate commerce. If true, the rule binds you regardless of FDA status.

**What Flips It & The Trigger:**
The only factor that allows you to proceed without *new* full testing is if your engineering team integrated a **FCC-granted modular approval** Bluetooth chip exactly according to the manufacturer's stipulations (antenna gain, shielding, no modifications).
*   **Trigger:** If your Bill of Materials (BOM) lists a Bluetooth module with an FCC ID and your team made no circuit changes affecting the RF path, you may only need a "Class II Permissive Change" or simple declaration ($2k–$5k cost, 2-week timeline).
*   **Stop Rule:** If you designed the radio on your own PCB or modified a module's antenna, you cannot ship. The trigger to resume is the receipt of your own FCC Grant of Equipment Authorization.
*   **This Week's Step:** If your BOM uses a pre-certified module, have your lead engineer verify the integration against the module's "integration manual" today; if not, contact an FCC-recognized Telecommunication Certification Body (TCB) immediately to schedule testing.
