**The Call**
Use **Lower of Cost or Market (LCM)** applied to individual items (or specific identification) rather than FIFO, LIFO, or broad mark-to-market; this satisfies GAAP for unique vintage goods while allowing you to present a "normalized" inventory schedule to investors that separates volatile streetwear from stable core stock.

**The Numbers**
*Current Snapshot:*
*   **Book Inventory:** $350,000
*   **Monthly Revenue:** $85,000
*   **Gross Margin:** 55% (Implies COGS is 45% of revenue)
*   **Inventory Turnover (Annualized):** ~2.9x ($85k * 12 * 0.45 / $350k)

*Valuation Method Impact (Illustrative Assumptions):*
Assume 40% of inventory ($140k cost) is volatile 90s streetwear. Half doubled in value, half dropped 40%.
1.  **FIFO/LIFO (Cost Basis):** Reports inventory at **$350,000**.
    *   *Risk:* Overstates assets if the 40% drop items are unsold; auditors may force a write-down anyway under LCM rules.
2.  **Broad Mark-to-Market:** Reports inventory at **~$392,000** (assuming gains offset losses).
    *   *Risk:* GAAP generally prohibits writing *up* inventory above cost. This creates an audit failure and signals aggression to VCs.
3.  **LCM (Specific Identification - Recommended):**
    *   Stable items (60%): Held at cost ($210,000).
    *   Volatile winners (20%): Held at cost ($70,000) – *gains unrecognized until sale*.
    *   Volatile losers (20%): Written down to market ($70k * 0.6 = $42,000).
    *   **Reported Value:** **$322,000**.
    *   *Result:* A conservative $28,000 write-down now prevents a larger surprise later and demonstrates financial discipline to investors.

**What Flips It, and The Trigger**
The deciding factor is the **sell-through rate of the volatile segment**. If the 90s streetwear sell-through drops below **15% per month** (based on your current velocity), the risk of further obsolescence spikes.
*   **Trigger:** If streetwear inventory aging exceeds **90 days**, consider marking it down an additional 10% in your internal models to stress-test your runway.
*   **Action:** This week, segment your inventory list into "Core" and "Trend" buckets and apply a 40% markdown reserve only to the "Trend" items older than 6 months to create a conservative pro-forma balance sheet for the pitch deck.

**Routing Note**
While LCM is the standard GAAP approach for retail, the specific application of "market" value for unique vintage items and the tax implications of write-downs (IRC §471) require a **CPA** to finalize your year-end filings. Additionally, disclosing asset valuation risks in a **securities offering** (your seed round) requires review by **securities counsel** to ensure your pitch deck disclosures meet SEC/state anti-fraud standards.
