**The Call**
Present the **strictly matched** gross margin figures alongside a clear narrative explaining the variable nature of your compute costs; do not smooth or average these costs to artificially stabilize the margin. While smoothing creates a prettier 78% line, it obscures the unit economics of your heavy analysis jobs and risks credibility with sophisticated Series A investors who expect transparency into cost drivers. In the UK context, adhering to the matching principle (accrual accounting) is the standard requirement for board reporting; smoothing costs across periods where the revenue was not generated violates this principle and misrepresents profitability per period.

**The Numbers**
Gross Margin is calculated as: `(Revenue - Cost of Goods Sold) / Revenue`.

*   **Your Inputs:**
    *   Monthly Revenue: $85,000 (Steady)
    *   Variable COGS (Compute): $4,000 (Low) to $12,000 (High)

*   **Scenario A: Strict Matching (Recommended)**
    *   *Low Cost Month:* ($85,000 - $4,000) / $85,000 = **95.3% Margin**
    *   *High Cost Month:* ($85,000 - $12,000) / $85,000 = **85.9% Margin**
    *   *Note:* Your stated fluctuation of 65%-82% implies other direct costs (e.g., third-party data fees, support labor) totaling roughly $15k-$22k are also being matched. If we include an assumed $18,000 in other fixed COGS:
        *   Low Month: ($85k - $4k - $18k) / $85k = **74.1%**
        *   High Month: ($85k - $12k - $18k) / $85k = **64.7%**
    *   *Result:* Accurate reflection of periodic performance.

*   **Scenario B: Averaged/Smoothed Costs (Not Recommended for GAAP/IFRS)**
    *   *Average COGS:* ($4,000 + $12,000) / 2 = $8,000 (plus other COGS)
    *   *Smoothed Margin:* ($85k - $8k - $18k) / $85k = **69.4%** (Constant)
    *   *Result:* Hides the cost volatility associated with specific analysis jobs.

**What Flips It, and the Trigger**
The deciding factor is **investor due diligence depth**. If your lead investors perform a line-item audit of your P&L (standard for Series A), discovering that you smoothed costs to hide volatility will trigger a loss of trust and questions about other potential accounting irregularities.
*   **Trigger:** If your variable compute costs are directly tied to specific customer usage events (even if billed monthly), they must be recognized in the same period as the revenue.
*   **Rule of Thumb:** Volatility in margins is acceptable for infrastructure-heavy SaaS if explained; volatility caused by accounting method choices is a red flag.
*   **Step this week:** If your high-cost months correlate with specific large customer onboardings or analysis runs, prepare a slide showing "Margin per Job Type" to prove that high-cost months are actually high-value months, turning the volatility narrative into a growth narrative.
