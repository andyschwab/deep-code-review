**The call:** Treat this as a serviceable niche, not a broad TAM play; the math supports a $10M revenue target if you capture just 12.5% of the remaining global install base, but venture scalability depends entirely on proving you can extract >$25k/year per account without churning as the stack dies.

**The numbers:**
*   **Total Install Base:** 4,000 enterprises × 5% adoption = **200 target companies**.
*   **Current Spend Pool:** 200 companies × $200,000 avg spend = **$40M total market value**.
*   **Revenue Target:** $10M.
*   **Required Market Share:** $10M / $40M = **25% of total spend**.
*   **Required ARPA (Average Revenue Per Account):** $10M / 200 companies = **$50,000/year**.
*   **Assumption (Low):** If adoption is actually 3% (120 companies), required ARPA jumps to **$83,333**.
*   **Assumption (High):** If you can expand beyond Fortune 500 to mid-market (assuming 2,000 additional smaller firms at $50k spend), the pool grows, lowering required ARPA to **~$14,000**.

**What flips it, and the trigger:**
The single input that changes the call is **annual churn rate of the underlying technology**. If the legacy stack loses >15% of its users annually due to cloud migration, your organic growth must exceed that just to stand still.
*   **Trigger:** If you cannot secure 10 pilot customers willing to sign multi-year contracts (3+ years) at >$40k ACV within 6 months, the niche is likely too small for venture returns.
*   **Rule of Thumb:** In a declining market, LTV must be realized quickly; aim for <12 month payback.
*   **Step this week:** If you have <5 qualified leads, consider pivoting the tech to adjacent legacy stacks or positioning as a "migration bridge" service rather than a pure monitoring SaaS.

**Data sources and validation:**
Do not hire a expensive market research firm yet; the sample size (200 companies) is small enough for manual verification.
1.  **Trust:** Direct outreach (interviews), legacy vendor earnings calls (mentioning install base decline), and specialized IT procurement reports (e.g., Gartner/Forrester only if they cover this specific legacy niche).
2.  **Avoid:** Generic TAM reports that extrapolate broad database trends; they will miss the nuance of this specific shrinking stack.
3.  **Investor Validation:** Investors in this stage care more about your *capture rate* and *pricing power* than third-party TAM validation. A spreadsheet showing 20 named targets with confirmed budget is worth more than a $50k report.
