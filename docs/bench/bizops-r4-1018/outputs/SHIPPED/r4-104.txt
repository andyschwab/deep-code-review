**The Call**
Do not recognize the $60,000 upfront. Under standard accounting principles (ASC 606 / IFRS 15), you must defer the revenue associated with the software license and recognize it ratably over the 12-month access period. The three-month implementation phase delays the *start* of that recognition clock; it does not create a gap in the middle of the year. Unless your implementation service is distinct and sold separately, it is likely bundled into the license, requiring the entire fee to be recognized only as the customer consumes the software benefit.

**The Numbers**
The core formula for ratable recognition is:
`Monthly Revenue = Total Transaction Price / Service Period Months`

*   **Total Contract Value:** $60,000 (5 clients × $12,000)
*   **Service Period:** 12 months (access duration)
*   **Implementation Lag:** 3 months (Oct, Nov, Dec)

**Scenario A: Implementation is NOT a separate performance obligation (Most Likely)**
*Assumption:* The customization is highly integrated with the software and not sold standalone. The "performance obligation" is the single promise of providing access to the customized platform.
*   **Recognition Start Date:** January 1 (when access begins).
*   **Monthly Recognition:** $60,000 / 12 = **$5,000/month**.
*   **Q4 (Oct-Dec) Revenue:** $0.
*   **Next Year Revenue:** $60,000 recognized evenly Jan–Dec.

**Scenario B: Implementation IS a separate performance obligation (Less Likely)**
*Assumption:* You sell standalone implementation services, and the client could benefit from the setup alone or via a third party. You must allocate price based on Standalone Selling Price (SSP).
*   *Hypothetical SSP:* If implementation usually sells for $3,000 and software for $9,000.
*   **Allocation:** $15,000 to Setup, $45,000 to Software.
*   **Setup Recognition:** Recognized upon completion (end of December) = $15,000 in Q4.
*   **Software Recognition:** $45,000 / 12 months = $3,750/month starting January.

**What Flips It, and the Trigger**
The deciding factor is whether the implementation service is **"distinct."**
*   **The Test:** Can the client benefit from the implementation service on its own (or with readily available resources), and is your promise to transfer the service separately identifiable from the software license?
*   **The Trigger:** If Sam's work involves deep, unique customization that fundamentally alters the software functionality specifically for that client, regulators often view this as a single, integrated project. In this case, **no revenue is recognized until go-live**.
*   **Rule of Thumb:** If you do not itemize implementation fees on your invoice or sell them as a standalone SKU, treat the whole contract as one obligation.
*   **This Week's Step:** If your contracts do not explicitly separate the implementation fee with a standalone price, prepare your board deck showing $0 revenue for Q4 and a straight-line recognition schedule starting in January.

**Routing Note**
Determining whether your specific implementation activities constitute a "distinct" performance obligation under ASC 606 is a binding accounting judgment that affects audit outcomes. Bring your specific Statement of Work (SOW) language and pricing history to a CPA to finalize the allocation method before filing financial statements.
