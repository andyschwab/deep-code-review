**The Call**
Do not renegotiate your existing B2B Data Processing Agreements (DPAs); the shift to a B2C tier does not invalidate your current compliance framework for enterprise clients. Instead, treat the B2C launch as a distinct processing activity requiring a new, separate consumer-facing privacy notice and terms of service, while updating your internal Record of Processing Activities (RoPA) to reflect the new data scope.

**The Numbers & Scope**
Your compliance obligation scales with the data subject type, not the contract count.
*   **Current State:** 200 B2B clients × €150/mo = €30,000 MRR. Data subjects are corporate entities; DPAs cover business data flows.
*   **Projected State:** +Thousands of B2C users × €15/mo. Data subjects are natural persons (EU consumers).
*   **Assumption (ASSUMED):** Your technical architecture logically separates B2B client data from B2C end-user data. If data commingles without segregation, the risk profile for B2B clients increases, potentially triggering DPA amendments regarding sub-processor risks or data mixture liabilities.

**What Flips It, and the Trigger**
The single input that changes this recommendation is **data commingling**. If your system architecture forces B2B client data to mix with B2C personal data in a way that prevents distinct deletion or portability requests, your existing B2B DPAs may be breached due to inability to guarantee specific security or isolation standards.
*   **Trigger:** If your engineering team cannot guarantee logical separation of B2B and B2C data stores by the launch date, pause the B2C launch and consult counsel immediately.
*   **Rule of Thumb:** Regulatory regimes (GDPR) apply per data subject type. B2B contracts govern commercial relationships; B2C interactions trigger direct statutory obligations to individuals. One does not automatically void the other unless the risk profile of the latter materially impacts the former.
*   **Step this week:** If your database schema supports multi-tenancy with strict isolation, proceed with drafting a standalone B2C Privacy Policy; if not, consider Y (architectural refactor) before legal drafting.

**Routing Note**
While the strategic call is to keep agreements separate, the **specific wording** of your new B2C privacy notice (covering right to erasure, portability, and lawful basis for marketing) and the **update to your RoPA** are binding legal determinations under the EU GDPR. You must route the final draft of the consumer-facing documents and the updated RoPA to an EU-qualified data privacy counsel to ensure the "self-service" mechanisms you build actually satisfy the statutory timeline and format requirements for consumer rights requests.
