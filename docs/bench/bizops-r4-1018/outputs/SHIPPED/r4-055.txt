**The Call**
Do not fix a pre-money valuation multiple yet; instead, pitch a seed round based on your team, product traction (1,500 active users), and roadmap, letting market demand set the price, while preparing standard seed documents (pitch deck, data room) and engaging a startup lawyer before signing any term sheet.

**The Numbers**
Valuation for pre-profit SaaS is not a pure math function of revenue; it is a market negotiation. However, we can bracket your position using typical early-stage benchmarks.
*   **Formula:** Implied Valuation = ARR × Revenue Multiple.
*   **Your Figures:** $180,000 ARR.
*   **Assumptions (Market Multiples):** Early-stage developer tool multiples vary wildly based on growth rate and team pedigree.
    *   *Low (Slow growth/No VC interest):* 3× ARR → $540,000 valuation.
    *   *Base (Moderate growth/Standard seed):* 8× ARR → $1.44M valuation.
    *   *High (Hyper-growth/Hot sector):* 15×+ ARR → $2.7M+ valuation.
*   **Result:** Your implied valuation likely sits between **$540k and $2.7M**, but because you are cash-flow negative (-$40k) and seeking seed capital, investors will likely ignore the revenue multiple and value you based on the "cost to replace" the team and product, often resulting in a standard seed cap ($2M–$4M) regardless of the $180k ARR.

**What Flips It, and the Trigger**
The single input that changes the call is your **year-over-year growth rate**. If you are growing >100% YoY, you command the high multiple; if flat or declining, you fall to the low end or face rejection on revenue grounds.
*   **Trigger:** If your month-over-month growth is <5% by next quarter, pivot your pitch from "revenue traction" to "technical moat/user loyalty."
*   **Step:** If you have not yet incorporated as a C-Corp (Delaware), consider doing so immediately before issuing any term sheets, as investors rarely invest in LLCs for seed rounds.

**Routing Note**
Fundraising via equity or SAFEs is a securities offering; you must engage a startup attorney to draft/review the term sheet and ensure compliance with federal (SEC) and state blue sky laws. Specifically, ask counsel: "Which exemption (e.g., Reg D 506(b) or 506(c)) fits our investor list, and what specific disclosures are required to avoid future liability?" Do not sign a term sheet without this review.
