**The Call**
Do not enter serious negotiations until you have executed written "work-for-hire" assignments and IP transfer agreements for all three contractors; without these, the compression algorithm is likely not fully owned by Acme Logic, creating a material defect that will either kill the deal or force a significant valuation discount and escrow holdback.

**The Factors & Risks**
Acquirers value certainty of ownership above all. In the US (including Delaware), the default legal rule is that the creator of a work owns the copyright, even if paid to build it, unless a written agreement explicitly states otherwise.
*   **Employees:** Your nine full-time staff likely signed employment agreements containing automatic IP assignment clauses. Their work is presumptively yours ("work made for hire" under specific conditions + contract assignment).
*   **Contractors:** For the three external builders, no automatic transfer exists without a signed document. If you only have a services agreement or invoice receipts, the contractors may legally retain ownership of the compression algorithm.
*   **Valuation Impact:** An acquirer will not pay a full multiple (e.g., 8x–12x ARR) for core IP you might not own. They will view this as a "title defect." The risk is not just a lower price; it is the potential for the sellers (the contractors) to sue the acquirer post-close or demand licensing fees. This usually results in the acquirer demanding:
    1.  A reduction in purchase price to cover legal risk.
    2.  A large portion of the payout held in escrow (12–24 months) to indemnify against IP claims.
    3.  In worst cases, termination of the deal if the IP cannot be cleanly assigned.

**What Flips It, and the Trigger**
The single input that changes the outcome is the existence of a **signed, written IP Assignment Agreement** specifically covering the compression algorithm for each contractor.
*   **Trigger:** If you cannot produce these signed documents before the Letter of Intent (LOI) stage, assume the deal structure will shift from a clean asset sale to a high-risk transaction.
*   **Action:** If you do not have these agreements signed *today*, consider pausing all substantive due diligence discussions until counsel drafts and you execute immediate retroactive assignments.
