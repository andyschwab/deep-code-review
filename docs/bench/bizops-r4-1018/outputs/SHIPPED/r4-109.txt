**The Call:** You must delay the launch of the disease-risk feature; providing probability percentages for Type 2 diabetes and heart conditions classifies your product as a medical device requiring FDA clearance, and proceeding without it carries significant enforcement risk that outweighs any potential discretion.

**The Numbers & Regulatory Threshold:**
Under the Federal Food, Drug, and Cosmetic Act, a product intended to diagnose, cure, mitigate, treat, or prevent disease is a medical device. Your current ancestry product relies on "enforcement discretion" because it provides non-health-related information. However, adding specific disease risk predictions shifts the "intended use" squarely into medical device territory.
*   **Classification:** Most genetic risk tests for common diseases (like Type 2 diabetes or coronary heart disease) are classified as **Class II medical devices**.
*   **Pathway:** This typically requires a **510(k) premarket notification** to demonstrate substantial equivalence to a legally marketed predicate device.
*   **Timeline:** The statutory review clock is 90 days, but real-world timelines including preparation, interactive review, and potential deficiencies average **4 to 7 months**.
*   **Cost:** While FDA user fees for small businesses are reduced (approx. $5,000–$10,000 depending on current fiscal year adjustments), the total cost including third-party testing, clinical data validation, and regulatory consulting typically ranges from **$150,000 to $500,000+**.

**What Flips It, and the Trigger:**
The single input that changes this assessment is the **claim type**. If you remove specific disease names and probability percentages, reverting to general wellness information (e.g., "genetic markers associated with metabolic health" without linking to a diagnosed condition), you might remain under enforcement discretion.
*   **Trigger:** Marketing materials or software outputs stating "risk for Type 2 diabetes" or specific percentages triggers Class II status immediately.
*   **Decision Rule:** If the output informs a medical decision or diagnoses a condition, it is a device.
*   **Next Step:** If your launch date is fixed in three months, **consider** pivoting the feature to "general wellness" only or delaying the launch to initiate the 510(k) process immediately, as the timeline exceeds your current runway.
