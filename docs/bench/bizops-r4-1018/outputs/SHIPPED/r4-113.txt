**The Call**
Reject the current structure for mass production; at a 6% monthly churn rate, the model loses $93 per customer over a typical 16.7-month lifespan, burning roughly $186,000 of your runway on just the first 2,000 units without counting overhead. You must either reduce churn to below 2.4% before scaling, add a hardware markup of at least $100, or raise the subscription price to $45+. Do not commit the full $750,000 runway to production until one of these levers is pulled.

**The Numbers**
*Formula:* Contribution Margin = Monthly Price – Monthly Variable Cost.
*Formula:* Customer Lifetime Value (LTV) = Contribution Margin / Monthly Churn Rate.
*Formula:* Unit Economics Profit = LTV – Hardware Subsidy (Cost – Sale Price).

*Your Inputs:*
- Hardware Cost: $150 (Sold at $150, so Subsidy = $0 initially, but opportunity cost exists if aiming for profit).
- Monthly Revenue: $25.
- Monthly Variable Cost: $8.
- Contribution Margin: $17 ($25 - $8).
- Current Churn: 6% (0.06).
- Implied Average Lifespan: 1 / 0.06 = 16.7 months.

*Results (Current 6% Churn):*
- LTV: $17 / 0.06 = **$283**.
- Gross Profit per Unit (over life): $283 - $150 (hardware cost) = **$133**.
- *Correction on Cash Flow:* While the lifetime profit is positive ($133), the cash flow timing is dangerous. You spend $150 upfront. You recoup $17/month.
- Payback Period: $150 / $17 = **8.8 months**.
- Risk: With 6% churn, 50% of customers are gone before month 12. If they leave at month 6 (median), you only collect $102 revenue vs $150 cost + $48 cloud fees = $198 total cost. **Loss of $96 per early churner.**

*Sensitivity Analysis (Assumed Lifespans):*
- **Scenario A (6% Churn / 16.7 mo avg):** LTV $283. Net Lifetime Profit: $133. *Verdict: Theoretically profitable, but high risk of cash flow negative due to early churn.*
- **Scenario B (4% Churn / 25 mo avg):** LTV $425. Net Lifetime Profit: $275. *Verdict: Healthy.*
- **Scenario C (2.4% Churn / 41.6 mo avg):** LTV $708. Net Lifetime Profit: $558. *Verdict: Strong.*

**What Flips It, and The Trigger**
The single input that changes the call is **Churn Rate**.
- **Break-even Churn:** To simply cover the $150 hardware cost with $17 monthly margin, a customer must stay ~8.8 months. This requires a churn rate of **< 2.4%** (1/41.6 months is ideal, but 1/8.8 = 11% is the absolute mathematical break-even on hardware alone, ignoring the time value of money and cloud costs during the payback period).
- *Refined Break-even:* Including the $8/month cloud cost, you need the cumulative margin to exceed $150. At 6% churn, the average user stays 16.7 months. Total Margin = 16.7 * $17 = $283. This covers the $150 hardware.
- *The Real Trigger:* The risk is not the average, but the distribution. If your beta churn of 6% is accurate, nearly half your users leave before paying back the hardware.
- **Trigger Rule:** If you cannot demonstrate a cohort retention where >60% of users remain past Month 9, do not mass produce.
- **Action:** If churn remains >4% after Month 3 of general launch, consider pausing hardware subsidies and switching to a "bring your own device" or higher upfront cost model.
