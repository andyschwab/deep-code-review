**The Call**
Choose the **18% variable fee** to protect your $55,000 cash reserves. While the fixed rent offers slightly better margins in peak months, the variable model eliminates the risk of negative cash flow during slow seasons, which is critical given your limited runway. The fixed rent creates a dangerous "dead zone" where you lose money every month sales dip below $28,000.

**The Numbers**
We compare the monthly cost of both options against your contribution margin.
*   **Fixed Rent:** $4,500/month.
*   **Variable Rent:** 18% of Gross Sales.
*   **Your Margin:** Sales - 25% COGS - $6,000 Labor = 75% of Sales - $6,000.

*Break-Even Analysis (Where Variable Cost = Fixed Cost):*
$4,500 = 0.18 × Sales
Sales = $4,500 / 0.18 = **$25,000**

*Monthly Cash Flow Projection (Net Cash after COGS, Labor, and Rent):*

| Scenario | Sales | Fixed Rent Option | Variable Rent Option |
| :--- | :--- | :--- | :--- |
| **Slow Month** | $14,000 | **-$1,000 (Loss)** | **+$1,920 (Profit)** |
| **Average Month** | $22,000 | +$6,000 | +$5,280 |
| **Peak Month** | $35,000 | +$15,750 | +$14,850 |

*Assumptions:* COGS remains strictly 25%; Labor is fixed at $6,000 regardless of sales volume; no other variable operating expenses exist.

**What Flips It, and the Trigger**
The deciding input is **monthly sales volume**.
*   **The Threshold:** At **$25,000** in sales, both options cost exactly $4,500.
*   **The Risk:** Below $25,000, the variable fee is cheaper. Above $25,000, the fixed rent is cheaper.
*   **The Reality Check:** Your "slow months" are $14,000. Under the fixed rent model, you operate at a loss of $1,000/month during these periods. With only $55,000 in reserves, five consecutive slow months (or a mix of slow/average months with unexpected costs) under the fixed model could deplete your capital before peak season arrives. The variable model guarantees a profit even in your worst historical month.
*   **Action Step:** If you project average monthly sales to consistently exceed $28,000 (providing a 12% safety buffer above the mathematical break-even), consider negotiating a "hybrid" cap (e.g., 18% up to $30k, then fixed thereafter). Until then, stick to variable.
