**The Call:** Do not claim "carbon neutral" based solely on internal calculations and offset receipts; the legal risk of an FTC or Oregon Attorney General action for unsubstantiated environmental marketing is high without third-party verification. While no federal or Oregon statute explicitly mandates a specific accredited body for *all* claims, the burden of proof required to avoid penalties effectively necessitates independent, competent, and reliable scientific evidence that self-certification rarely satisfies for broad "carbon neutral" assertions.

**The Numbers:**
*   **Potential Revenue at Risk:** $570,000 (annual revenue).
*   **Cost of Compliance (Audit):** $15,000 (one-time/annual).
*   **Cost of Offset Budget:** $10,000.
*   **Risk Exposure:** FTC civil penalties can exceed $50,000 *per violation* (per ad impression or label), plus reputational damage and potential class-action litigation under state consumer protection laws.
*   **Assumption (ASSUMED):** The $10,000 offset budget covers 100% of Scope 1, 2, and relevant Scope 3 emissions (farming + shipping). If your actual footprint exceeds $10,000 worth of credits, the claim is false regardless of verification.
*   **Result:** Spending $15,000 on verification protects $570,000 in revenue and brand equity against disproportionate fines. The ratio of compliance cost to potential penalty makes the audit the economically rational choice.

**What Flips It, and the Trigger:**
The single input that changes this recommendation is the **scope of the claim**. If you narrow the claim from "Carbon Neutral Brand" (which implies total net-zero operations) to a specific, limited claim like "We purchased carbon offsets for shipping emissions," the substantiation burden lowers, potentially allowing internal data if robust.
*   **Trigger Value:** If your internal Life Cycle Assessment (LCA) cannot definitively quantify *all* emissions sources (including cotton farming variations) with peer-reviewed methodology, the trigger to hire an auditor is hit immediately.
*   **Decision Threshold:** As a rule of thumb, if the claim appears on the primary product label or homepage hero section (high visibility), external verification is required. If it is buried in a sustainability report with detailed methodology links, internal substantiation *might* suffice but remains risky.
*   **Step this week:** If you cannot produce a full Scope 1-3 inventory calculated via ISO 14064 or GHG Protocol standards internally, consider pausing the "carbon neutral" label and switching to "climate conscious" while commissioning a limited-scope audit.

**Routing Note:**
Environmental marketing claims are governed by the **FTC Green Guides** (16 C.F.R. Part 260) and the **Oregon Unlawful Trade Practices Act**. The FTC does not "certify" companies but prosecutes deceptive acts; the "exact threshold" is whether a reasonable consumer would be misled and whether you possess competent and reliable scientific evidence. Determining if your specific internal calculations meet this federal evidentiary standard and navigating Oregon's specific consumer protection statutes is a binding legal determination. You must consult an attorney specializing in environmental marketing law to review your specific LCA methodology and offset contracts before publishing the claim.
