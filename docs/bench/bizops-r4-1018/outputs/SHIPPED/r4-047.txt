**The Call:** Your product remains a cosmetic provided your marketing claims strictly limit "relaxation" to a subjective feeling of calm and do not imply any physiological change or treatment of a condition; however, any claim suggesting the colorants themselves provide a therapeutic benefit or alter bodily function risks reclassification as a drug, which would be financially catastrophic for your current revenue model.

**The Regulatory Boundary & Criteria:**
Under US FDA regulations (which govern New York), the distinction between a cosmetic and a drug hinges entirely on **intended use**, determined by your labeling, advertising, and consumer perception.
*   **Cosmetic:** Articles intended to be rubbed, poured, or introduced into the human body for cleansing, beautifying, promoting attractiveness, or altering appearance. Your "visual immersion" and water-coloring agents fit here if the claim is purely aesthetic.
*   **Drug:** Articles intended for use in the diagnosis, cure, mitigation, treatment, or prevention of disease, or to affect the structure or any function of the body.
*   **The "Relaxation" Trap:** Mentioning "relaxation" is a high-risk gray area. If you claim the product "relieves stress" (implying a physiological effect on the nervous system) or "treats anxiety," it becomes a drug. If you claim it "creates a relaxing atmosphere" or "helps you unwind" via the *experience* of the scent/color (aromatherapy/ambiance), it generally stays cosmetic. The regulator looks at whether the *colorant* is claimed to have the effect. If the blue water itself is marketed as "healing" or "calming nerves," that is a drug claim. If the *bath experience* is relaxing, that is cosmetic.

**What Flips It & The Trigger:**
The single input that changes your classification is the **causal link** established in your copy between the *ingredient* and a *bodily function*.
*   **Rule of Thumb:** If the claim suggests the product changes how the body *works*, it is a drug. If it suggests the product changes how the body *looks* or the *environment* feels, it is a cosmetic.
