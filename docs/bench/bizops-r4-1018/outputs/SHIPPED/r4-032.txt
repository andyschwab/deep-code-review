**The Call**
Do not raise prices yet; the 8% breakage rate is anomalously high and represents a solvable operational failure, not a pricing problem. Prioritize immediate packaging redesign to target a <2% breakage rate, as the current loss magnitude ($9 per unit sold) erodes more profit than a $15 price increase could safely recover given your low customer lifetime value (LTV).

**The Numbers**
*Current Unit Economics (per shipped set):*
*   Revenue: $120
*   Direct Cost (Material + Labor): $45
*   Packaging: $12
*   Expected Breakage Cost: $9.00 (Calculated: $120 replacement value × 8% rate)
*   **Contribution Margin:** $54.00 ($120 - $45 - $12 - $9)
*   **Margin %:** 45%

*Scenario A: Price Increase to $135 (assuming 5% volume drop due to elasticity):*
*   New Volume: 380 sets
*   New Revenue/set: $135
*   Breakage Cost/set: $10.80 ($135 × 8%)
*   New Contribution Margin/set: $65.20 ($135 - $45 - $12 - $10.80)
*   Total Monthly Contribution: $24,776 (vs. current $21,600)
*   *Risk:* If volume drops >12%, total contribution falls below current levels. With an LTV of only $210, you have little room to acquire new customers to replace lost volume.

*Scenario B: Packaging Improvement (Targeting 2% breakage, cost +$3/box):*
*   New Packaging Cost: $15
*   New Breakage Cost: $2.40 ($120 × 2%)
*   New Contribution Margin/set: $57.60 ($120 - $45 - $15 - $2.40)
*   Total Monthly Contribution (at 400 sets): $23,040
*   *Result:* Higher total profit than the price hike scenario, with no volume risk and improved customer satisfaction.

**What Flips It, and the Trigger**
The decision flips if packaging upgrades cannot reduce breakage below 5%.
*   **Trigger:** If a $5 packaging upgrade trial (costing ~$2,000 of your $20k capital) over the next 2 weeks does not reduce breakage to <4%, reconsider the price increase.
*   **Rule of Thumb:** For fragile direct-to-consumer goods, a >3% breakage rate indicates a packaging failure, not a market condition. Industry standard for well-packaged ceramics is typically <2%.
*   **Action This Week:** If breakage >5% in a 50-unit test batch with reinforced corners, consider raising the price to $135 immediately to fund premium freight or specialized carriers.
