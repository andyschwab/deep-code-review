**The Call**
Do not pursue FedRAMP or SOC 2 immediately; instead, target the $150k quarterly goal by selling as a sub-contractor through an established education technology partner or reseller who already holds these certifications. FedRAMP is unnecessary for your segment, and SOC 2, while increasingly requested by large state universities, is not an absolute legal barrier to entry if you leverage a partner's compliance umbrella. Pursuing certification now would consume ~$75k (over 50% of your implied quarterly burn) and take 6–9 months, directly jeopardizing your 9-month runway before recognizing the first dollar of that specific revenue stream.

**The Numbers**
*Decision Logic:* Compare the cost of compliance against the risk of lost revenue.
*Formula:* `Net Value = (Potential Revenue × Win Rate without Cert) - (Cert Cost + Delay Cost)`
*Inputs:*
*   Target Revenue: $150,000 (this quarter).
*   Estimated Cert Cost: $75,000/year (user provided).
*   Runway: 9 months.
*   Team: 10 people.

*Scenarios (Labelled Assumptions):*
1.  **Partner Route (Base Case):**
    *   *Assumption:* Partner takes 20% margin; Win Rate remains high due to their existing trust.
    *   *Result:* Revenue $120,000 net; $0 upfront compliance cost; Revenue recognized this quarter.
2.  **Direct Certification Route (High Risk):**
    *   *Assumption:* SOC 2 Type I takes 3 months, Type II takes 6+ months; Win Rate drops to 0% during procurement review without cert.
    *   *Result:* Revenue $0 (this quarter); Cash outflow $18,750 (prorated 3-month spend); Runway reduced from 9 to ~8.5 months purely on cash burn, plus opportunity cost of delayed sales.
3.  **Direct Sales without Cert (Low Case):**
    *   *Assumption:* Large research universities require SOC 2 in RFPs; Community colleges may waive it for low-cost tools. Win Rate 20%.
    *   *Result:* Expected Revenue $30,000; $0 compliance cost.

**What Flips It, and the Trigger**
The single input that changes this call is the **procurement threshold** of the specific university. Large state research universities often mandate SOC 2 for vendors handling student data directly, whereas community colleges frequently exempt vendors under certain contract values or data-handling scopes.
*Trigger:* If a specific RFP explicitly states "SOC 2 Type II required for primary vendor" and forbids sub-contracting, you must pause that specific deal.
*Rule of Thumb:* If the deal size is under $250k, the administrative burden of direct certification usually outweighs the margin benefit for a 10-person team.
*Action:* This week, identify three existing EdTech platforms (e.g., LMS integrators or campus portal providers) already selling to your target US states and propose a reseller agreement where you are the "technology provider" behind their compliant front.

**Routing Note**
While SOC 2 is an industry standard rather than a federal law, US state procurement rules vary; consult a US-based education counsel to confirm if any specific state (e.g., California or New York) has statutory data-residency or certification mandates that cannot be satisfied via a reseller agreement before signing any US contracts.
