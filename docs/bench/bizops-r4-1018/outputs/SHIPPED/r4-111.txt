**The Call:** Reject the price cut to $29; it mathematically destroys your unit economics and accelerates cash burn without solving the retention problem. **The Numbers:**
*Formula:* $LTV = \frac{ARPA \times Gross Margin}{Churn Rate}$ (simplified for monthly subscription).
*Assumptions:* Gross Margin is assumed at 90% (typical for digital coaching); Churn remains constant at 12% monthly for the projection (though price cuts often fail to fix structural churn).

*Current State ($79/mo):*
*   ARPA: $79
*   Monthly Contribution: $71.10 ($79 × 0.90)
*   Estimated LTV: $592.50 ($71.10 / 0.12)
*   CAC: $185
*   **LTV:CAC Ratio:** 3.2x (using simplified LTV) or ~2.3x (using your provided $420 LTV).
*   *Verdict:* Healthy ratio, but absolute LTV is too low relative to CAC payback period.

*Proposed State ($29/mo):*

*   *Volume Requirement:* To maintain current revenue of ~$420/user lifetime value equivalent, you need ~2.7x more users. If you have 1,000 users now, you need 2,700. With a fixed marketing funnel, this is impossible immediately.

**What Flips It, and The Trigger:**
The single input that changes this call is **Churn Rate**. For the $29 model to match your current $420 LTV, your churn must drop from 12% to roughly **5.5% monthly**.
*Trigger:* If you cannot demonstrate a sustained churn rate below 6% within 30 days via product changes (not just price), the price cut will fail.
*Rule of Thumb:* A price cut only works if the elasticity of demand (volume increase) and retention improvement outpace the revenue loss per user. Here, the math requires a >50% reduction in churn to break even on LTV.
*Step this week:* If your churn data shows users leaving due to *price* (survey exit reasons), consider a "good-better-best" tier at $49 rather than a blanket drop to $29. If they leave due to *lack of results*, price is irrelevant; fix the coaching outcome.
