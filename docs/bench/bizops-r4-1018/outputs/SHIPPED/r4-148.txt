**The Call**
Your monetization model alone does not automatically transform SoberPath into a broker or guarantor of quality under California law, but charging fees while marketing "verification" or implying endorsement significantly increases your liability exposure. To mitigate risk immediately, you must explicitly disclaim any warranty of licensure or quality in your terms of service and on the listing pages, clarifying that the fee covers directory inclusion only, not vetting, unless you actually perform and document that vetting.

**The Analysis**
Under general legal principles applicable to directories and online platforms, liability often hinges on whether the platform holds itself out as a verifier of information.
1.  **Passive Directory vs. Active Broker:** Courts typically distinguish between passive conduits of information and active participants who recommend or guarantee services. Collecting a fee for *listing* is common for directories (like Yellow Pages or Yelp) and usually insufficient on its own to create a duty of care for the listed entity's negligence. However, if your marketing materials, site copy, or interactions with families imply that you "verify," "vet," or "recommend" these homes because they pay, you may be creating an implied warranty of safety or licensure.
2.  **The Duty of Care:** A duty of care generally arises if you voluntarily undertake a duty (e.g., claiming to check licenses) and perform it negligently, or if a special relationship exists where users reasonably rely on your specific expertise. If families ask about verification and you remain ambiguous or imply verification without a rigorous process, a court could find you liable for negligent misrepresentation if a user relies on that implication and suffers harm.
3.  **California Context:** California courts scrutinize consumer protection closely. If your platform targets vulnerable populations (families seeking sober living), the expectation of trust is higher. Statutory protections for online platforms (like Section 230 of the CDA) generally protect against liability for *third-party content*, but they do not shield you from liability for your own negligent conduct, such as falsely claiming to verify licenses or making specific safety guarantees.

**What Flips It**
The critical input that changes your risk profile is your **public representation of verification**.
*   **Trigger:** If your website, ads, or support staff state or strongly imply "verified," "checked," or "safe" without a documented, rigorous audit process for every listing, you flip from a low-risk directory to a high-risk guarantor.
*   **Stop/Pivot Rule:** If you cannot verify 100% of licenses monthly, remove all language suggesting verification.
*   **Actionable Step:** This week, audit your homepage, FAQ, and sales scripts. If the word "verify" appears regarding licensure, replace it with "self-reported" or add a bold disclaimer: "SoberPath does not verify licensure; users must confirm status with the California Department of Health Care Services."
