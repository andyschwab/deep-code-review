**What Flips It, and the Trigger:**
The specific wording of the claim flips the product from "food" to "drug."
*   **Trigger Phrase:** "Fight off," "cure," "treat," "prevent," or "diagnose" a disease (e.g., "common cold").
*   **The Rule:** Under the Federal Food, Drug, and Cosmetic Act (FD&C Act), any article intended for use in the diagnosis, cure, mitigation, treatment, or prevention of disease is a **drug**. The "common cold" is a disease. Claiming to "fight it off" is a drug claim.
*   **Permissible Alternative:** "Structure-function" claims (e.g., "supports immune health") are allowed for foods *if* they describe the role of a nutrient in affecting normal structure or function, *do not* reference a disease, and include the disclaimer: *"This statement has not been evaluated by the Food and Drug Administration. This product is not intended to diagnose, treat, cure, or prevent any disease."*
*   **Decision Threshold:** If the claim implies disease intervention, it is prohibited.
