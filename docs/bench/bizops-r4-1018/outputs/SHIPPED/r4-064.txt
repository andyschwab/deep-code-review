**The Call:** Your current model is not viable for a Series A; with an 8% monthly churn, you lose money on every customer before recovering acquisition costs. You must prioritize reducing churn immediately, as no pricing increase or marketing efficiency gain can fix a business where customers leave faster than you can earn back their cost.

**The Numbers:**
*   **Payback Period:** €150 CAC / €25 ARPU = **6 months** (ignoring churn).
*   **Customer Lifetime:** At 8% monthly churn, average lifetime is ~12.5 months (1 / 0.08).
*   **Lifetime Value (LTV):** €25 × 12.5 months = **€312.50** (gross, ignoring discount rates).
*   **LTV:CAC Ratio:** €312.50 / €150 = **2.08x**.
*   **Runway:** €400k reserves / €60k burn = **~6.7 months**.

*Assumptions:* LTV calculation assumes constant ARPU and churn; it does not account for cost of goods sold (COGS), which would lower the actual margin and LTV further.

**What Flips It, and The Trigger:**
The single input that changes the call is **Churn**. At 8% monthly churn, your effective LTV is too low to support a €150 CAC once operating expenses are considered. Investors typically expect an LTV:CAC ratio of **3:1 or higher** for Series A, and a payback period of **<12 months** (ideally <6 months) on a *net* basis. Your current 2.08x ratio is a red flag because it leaves insufficient margin to cover R&D, G&A, and the 8% loss of asset value monthly.
*   **Trigger:** If you cannot reduce monthly churn to **<4%** within the next 90 days, the unit economics will remain negative on a cash-flow basis relative to growth spend.
*   **Step:** If churn remains above 5% next month, consider pausing paid acquisition entirely and reallocating the €150/user budget to customer success interventions to extend lifetime value.
