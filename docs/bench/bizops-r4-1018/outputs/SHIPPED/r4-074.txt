**What Flips It, and the Trigger**
The single input that changes this from a "standard commission" to a "profit shifting risk" is the **lack of documented service equivalence**. If tax authorities in the US or EU determine that the 20% payout exceeds what an unrelated third party would charge for identical referral services in that local market, they may reclassify the payment as a disguised dividend or profit shift.
*   **Trigger:** The moment you sign a partner in a high-scrutiny jurisdiction (e.g., Germany, France, or specific US states) where the local market rate for similar referrals is demonstrably lower than 20%, or if the partner performs additional functions (like implementation) not covered in the contract.
*   **Rule of Thumb:** If the commission rate exceeds the local industry standard for pure referrals by more than 5 percentage points, documentation requirements spike.
*   **Step this week:** If you have partners in the EU, consider auditing one existing agreement to ensure it explicitly lists "referral services" as the sole deliverable to justify the 20% fee under local VAT and withholding tax rules.

**Routing Note**
While payments to unrelated third parties generally fall outside strict "controlled transaction" transfer pricing rules (which apply to related entities), the OECD and local authorities still require proof that the price is "arm's length." You must engage a cross-border tax specialist to confirm: (1) whether withholding tax applies to these commissions in the five specific countries you operate in, and (2) if your volume (40 partners) triggers a requirement for a master file or local file documentation in those jurisdictions.
