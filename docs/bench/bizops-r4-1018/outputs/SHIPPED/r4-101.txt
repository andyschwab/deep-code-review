**The Rules and Landscape:**
In the United States, the standard legal rule for telehealth is that the practice of medicine occurs where the **patient is physically located** at the time of the consultation, not where the doctor sits. Therefore, your Texas doctors are legally practicing medicine in Florida when treating your Florida patients.

Florida law generally requires any physician treating a patient in Florida to hold a full, active Florida medical license. While Florida has created specific pathways for telehealth, they are not "loopholes" for immediate, unregistered practice:
1.  **Telehealth Provider Registration:** Florida Statute § 456.47 allows out-of-state physicians to register to provide telehealth services in Florida without obtaining a full Florida license, *but* this requires an application to the Florida Board of Medicine, payment of fees, and approval. It is not instantaneous. You cannot start next week without this registration being active.
2.  **No "Temporary" Permits for General Care:** There is no generic "temporary permit" that allows a doctor to start working next week while a full license processes, unless they have already filed for the specific telehealth registration and are operating under strict statutory constraints during the pendency (which still requires filing first).
3.  **The "Consultation" Exception is Narrow:** Exceptions usually apply only to formal "consultations" between a Florida-licensed treating physician and an out-of-state specialist, not to direct-to-consumer urgent care visits where the out-of-state doctor is the primary provider.

**Penalties for Non-Compliance:**
Practicing medicine without a license in Florida is a **third-degree felony**. Penalties include:
*   Criminal prosecution (up to 5 years in prison).
*   Significant fines (up to $5,000 per incident).
*   Permanent injunctions against your company and the individual doctors.
*   Exclusion from federal healthcare programs (Medicare/Medicaid), which can destroy a telehealth business model.
