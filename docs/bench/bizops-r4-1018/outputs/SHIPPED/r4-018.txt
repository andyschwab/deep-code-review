**The Call**
Reject the contract as structured; at $3.20 per loaf, a 10% cost increase pushes your contribution margin to near zero, requiring a production volume of 22,500 loaves just to cover overhead—more than double your current 10,000-loaf capacity.

**The Numbers**
*Formula:* Break-Even Volume = Fixed Overhead / (Price per Unit – Variable Cost per Unit).

*Current Local Model (Baseline):*
*   Price: $4.50
*   Cost: $2.10
*   Margin: $2.40
*   Break-Even: $18,000 / $2.40 = **7,500 loaves**. (You are currently profitable).

*   Margin: $1.10

*   *Result:* This exceeds your current 10,000-loaf capacity by 63%. You would operate at a loss.

*Proposed Wholesale Model (Stress Test: +10% Flour Cost):*
*   Assumed New Cost: $2.10 × 1.10 = $2.31 (ASSUMED: flour is the sole variable cost driver).
*   Price: $3.20
*   Margin: $0.89
*   Break-Even: $18,000 / $0.89 = **20,225 loaves**.
*   *Result:* This requires doubling your current output. If "flour costs rise" implies total variable costs rise similarly, the margin collapses further.

**What Flips It, and The Trigger**
The single input that changes this call is the **wholesale price**. To maintain your current break-even safety margin (producing 10,000 loaves and covering $18k overhead) under the stress test cost of $2.31, you must charge the distributor **$4.11 per loaf**, not $3.20.
*Trigger Rule:* If your variable costs rise above $2.25, do not sign any deal with a price below $3.50.
*Action Step:* If the distributor refuses a price above $3.50, consider a "cost-plus" clause where the wholesale price floats with a certified flour index, rather than a fixed $3.20.
