**The Call**
Proceed with the tiered revenue share, but strictly decouple payout volume from operational control; classification risk turns on behavioral control and integration, not compensation levels. Do not grant governance rights or exclusivity, as these are the primary triggers that flip independent contractors to employees under California's ABC test.

**The Numbers**
There is no statutory dollar threshold where a contractor becomes an employee; the test is qualitative. However, we can model the financial exposure of a misclassification event to understand the stakes.
*Formula:* Potential Liability = (Back Wages + Penalties) × Number of Affected Workers.
*User Figures:* 300 contributors; top 10% (30 users) earn >$3,000/mo.
*Assumptions (ASSUMED):* If reclassified, assume minimum wage + overtime differential averages $5/hour per worker over a 2-year lookback (conservative estimate for high earners who may have worked irregular hours), plus statutory penalties (PAGA) of $100–$200 per violation per employee.
*Illustrative Result:* For just the top 30 earners, a 2-year lookback could expose the platform to roughly $75,000–$150,000 in wage differentials alone, potentially tripling with penalties. This illustrates why the *structure* of the relationship matters more than the *amount* paid.

**What Flips It, and the Trigger**
The single input that changes the call is **behavioral control**, specifically the right to direct *how* the work is performed or integrating the worker into the core business such that they are indistinguishable from staff.
*Trigger Value:* If your new model requires contributors to attend mandatory meetings, adhere to fixed working hours, use specific non-public tools mandated by you, or grants them voting rights on platform strategy (governance), the risk shifts from low to critical immediately.
*Rule of Thumb:* Under California's ABC test, a worker is presumed an employee unless you prove they are free from your control, perform work outside your usual business, and are customarily engaged in an independent trade. Since selling code is your "usual business," Prong B is your hardest hurdle; high integration (governance) fails Prong A.
*Step this week:* Draft the new tiered agreement explicitly stating that higher revenue shares are automatic based on sales volume algorithms, not managerial discretion, and explicitly exclude any right to participate in platform management or decision-making.

**Routing Note**
California's ABC test (Dynamex/AB5) is fact-specific and aggressive; before launching the governance component or finalizing the tiered contract language, engage California employment counsel to review the specific "governance" mechanics to ensure they do not constitute "control" or create a joint venture implication.
