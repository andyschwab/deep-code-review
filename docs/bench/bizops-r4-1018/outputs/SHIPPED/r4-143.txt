**The Call**
Adopt a 5-year straight-line depreciation schedule with zero salvage value for your initial tax forecast, but immediately engage a CPA to evaluate eligibility for Section 179 expensing or bonus depreciation, which could allow you to deduct the entire $180,000 capital outlay in the current year rather than spreading it out.

**The Numbers**
*Formula:* Annual Depreciation = (Cost Basis − Salvage Value) / Useful Life.
*Your Figures:* Cost Basis = $180,000 (200 units × $900); Salvage Value = $0 (assumed for conservative forecasting).
*Assumptions & Results:*
1.  **Standard Straight-Line (5-year class life):** $180,000 / 5 = **$36,000/year** deduction.
2.  **Accelerated (Section 179/Bonus - Illustrative Only):** If fully eligible, **$180,000** deduction in Year 1, $0 thereafter.
3.  **Software Impact:** Software updates are typically treated as operating expenses (R&D or COGS) deducted immediately, not added to the hardware's depreciable basis, unless they materially extend the hardware's physical life (unlikely for VR headsets).

**What Flips It, and The Trigger**
The single input that changes your cash flow projection is the **current year's federal and California state limits on Section 179 and bonus depreciation percentages**.
*Trigger:* If your taxable income exceeds the deduction limit or if California decouples from federal bonus depreciation rules (which it frequently does), your state tax liability will be higher than your federal projection.
*Rule of Thumb:* Assume federal acceleration is available but California conformity is partial or non-existent until verified.
*Next Step:* If your CPA confirms California does not conform to the current federal bonus depreciation rate, consider accelerating equipment purchases before year-end if federal limits allow, or adjust your state tax accruals upward by the difference between the federal and state depreciation amounts.
