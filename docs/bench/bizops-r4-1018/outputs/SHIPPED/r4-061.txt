**The Call:** Classify the $15,000 commission as **standard gross revenue**, not a contra-expense. You are selling a service (cost optimization) priced as a percentage of value delivered; the client's savings are the metric for your fee, not a reduction of your own costs.

**The Numbers:**
*   **Formula:** Gross Revenue = Commission Rate × Client Savings Achieved.
*   **Your Figures:** 30% × $50,000 (monthly savings) = $15,000 revenue.
*   **Annual Context:** 30% × $200,000 (total savings) = $60,000 total gross revenue.
*   **Assumption (ASSUMED):** Your contract explicitly states you invoice the client for this amount and they pay it from their operating budget (which you confirmed). If you were deducting this from a bill you paid *on their behalf* (e.g., you pay AWS directly), the accounting might differ, but your description confirms a direct service fee.
*   **Result:** The full $60,000 is top-line revenue. Your "cost of goods sold" (COGS) includes your server costs, labor, and tools, but not the client's cloud bill (since you never paid it).

**What Flips It, and the Trigger:**
*   **The Flip:** The classification would only change to a "contra-expense" or "netted" item if you acted as a **principal** who purchased the cloud resources yourself and then billed the client for the net difference. Since you act as an **agent** advising the client who pays AWS directly, the "gross" method applies.
*   **Trigger:** If your contract structure changes so that CloudSaver receives the full cloud bill from the client, pays AWS, and keeps the difference, you must switch to gross reporting of the total bill (revenue) and the AWS payment (COGS).
*   **Rule of Thumb:** If you don't hold the liability for the underlying cloud bill, you don't net it.
*   **Next Step:** This week, verify your invoices explicitly label the $15,000 as "Professional Services" or "Performance Fee" rather than "Reimbursement" to align with the revenue classification.
