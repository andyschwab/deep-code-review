**The Call:** Operate under the assumption that you are a Business Associate (BA) and do not process any further data from covered entities until a qualified healthcare attorney confirms your status; the "real-time only" argument is a high-risk interpretation that rarely shields API providers handling patient-specific data for treatment purposes.

**The Rules and Thresholds:** Under HIPAA, the threshold for becoming a Business Associate is not based on data volume, storage duration, or revenue, but on **function**. If you create, receive, maintain, or transmit Protected Health Information (PHI) on behalf of a Covered Entity (your clinic clients) to perform a function regulated by HIPAA (such as treatment support or care coordination), you are a BA. The fact that your clients treat diabetes and obesity confirms they are Covered Entities handling PHI. Your argument that ephemeral processing exempts you is legally fragile; HHS guidance explicitly states that transmission alone can trigger BA status if the data is PHI. There is no "safe harbor" volume threshold; one record transmitted for a covered purpose triggers the obligation.

**Illustrative Compliance Measures (If BA Status Applies):**
If counsel confirms you are a BA, the following are standard requirements (illustrative, not exhaustive):
1.  **Business Associate Agreement (BAA):** You must sign a BAA with every Covered Entity client before exchanging data. Operating without one is a direct violation.
2.  **Encryption:** While HIPAA is technology-neutral, the "Security Rule" requires encryption of ePHI in transit and at rest as an "addressable" (effectively mandatory) safeguard. TLS 1.2+ for transit and AES-256 for any temporary logging/cache are industry standards.
3.  **Audit Controls:** You must implement hardware/software to record and examine activity. This does not automatically mandate a *third-party* audit (like SOC 2 or HITRUST) unless your contracts require it, but you must perform internal risk analyses and maintain logs.
4.  **Breach Notification:** You must have a protocol to notify clients within 60 days of discovering a breach.

**What Flips the Call:** The specific nature of the data fields. If your API only processes anonymized, aggregated statistical data (no dates, no zip codes, no specific patient IDs linked to individuals), you may fall outside HIPAA. However, if the input includes any of the 18 HIPAA identifiers linked to health conditions, you are likely a BA.
*Trigger:* If your JSON payload contains any user identifier combined with a health condition (e.g., "Patient X has Type 2 Diabetes"), stop and route to counsel immediately.
