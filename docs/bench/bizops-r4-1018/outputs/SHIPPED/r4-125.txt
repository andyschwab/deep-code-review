**The Numbers**
*   **Current Volume:** $4,500/month ($54,000 annualized).
*   **Regulatory Thresholds:** There is **no federal safe harbor** based on transaction volume for Money Services Business (MSB) registration if you are deemed a money transmitter.
    *   *Federal (FinCEN):* Registration is required if you accept and transmit value, regardless of volume. However, the "payment processor exemption" often applies if you facilitate payments for *goods/services* without taking custody of funds.
    *   *State (FL, IL, WA):* These states aggressively enforce MTL laws.
        *   **Washington:** Strict licensing; often no de minimis exemption for transmitters.
        *   **Florida & Illinois:** Licensing thresholds vary, but "facilitating" payments often triggers requirements once you touch the funds.
*   **Assumption (UNVERIFIED):** You are currently taking custody of the $4,500 (holding it before paying the advisor). If you simply pass data to Stripe/PayPal Connect and they settle directly to the advisor, your risk drops significantly but does not vanish.

**What Flips It, and The Trigger**
*   **The Flip Input:** **Custody of Funds.** If MenoCircle's bank account touches the user's money before it reaches the advisor, you are almost certainly a money transmitter. If funds settle directly from payer to payee via a licensed third-party (e.g., Stripe Connect), you may qualify for an exemption.
*   **The Trigger:** **Immediate.** The moment you facilitated the first $4,500 transaction with a fee, you potentially triggered state licensing obligations in WA, FL, and IL.
*   **Rule of Thumb:** If you take a cut of a peer-to-peer transfer, regulators assume you are transmitting money unless a specific exemption (like the "payment processor" exemption) is legally validated for your flow.
