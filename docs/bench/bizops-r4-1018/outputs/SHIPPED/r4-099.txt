**The Numbers:**
To justify the $120,000 deal size, you must model the **Payback Period** and **3-Year Net Savings**. Since you lack specific client data, use these formulas with labelled assumptions to build your calculator:

1.  **Annual Legacy Burden (ALB)** = (Annual Maintenance Contracts) + (Internal Dev Hours × $150/hr [ASSUMED avg loaded cost]) + (Estimated Downtime Risk Cost).
    *   *Assumption:* If a bank spends 20 hours/week on patches at $150/hr, ALB labor = $156,000/year.
2.  **Payback Period (Months)** = $120,000 / ((ALB - Annual Cloud Run Rate) / 12).
    *   *Scenario A (Low Efficiency):* If migration saves only $80k/year, Payback = 18 months.

3.  **3-Year Net Value** = (ALB × 3) - ($120,000 + (Annual Cloud Run Rate × 3)).

**What Flips It, and The Trigger:**
The single input that changes the CFO's mind is the **Internal Labor Cost of Maintenance**. Technical benefits flip to financial wins only when you assign a dollar value to the hours engineers spend keeping the lights on.
*   **Trigger Rule:** If your calculated Payback Period exceeds 24 months, the deal will likely stall.
*   **Actionable Step:** Before your next meeting, audit one pilot client's ticketing system to count exact hours spent on legacy patches last quarter. If that number exceeds 15 hours/week, consider leading your pitch with a "Labor Reclamation" slide rather than a "Cloud Architecture" slide.
