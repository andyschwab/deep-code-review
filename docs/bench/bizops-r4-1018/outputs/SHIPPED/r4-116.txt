**What Flips It & The Stop Trigger:**
The single input that changes the verdict is **valid legal basis and capacity to consent**.
*   **Assumption:** Dementia patients often lack the legal capacity to provide GDPR-compliant "explicit consent."
*   **The Rule:** Under GDPR Article 35, processing that involves systematic monitoring of vulnerable persons on a large scale requires a DPIA *before* processing begins. If the DPIA indicates high residual risk, you must consult the supervisory authority (Article 36) prior to launch.
*   **The Exemption Myth:** There is no blanket "dementia care exemption" in GDPR that waives the need for a legal basis. While "vital interests" (Article 6(1)(d)) can sometimes justify processing to protect someone's life (e.g., immediate fall response), *continuous* preventive tracking for a premium feature usually fails this test and reverts to requiring consent or a specific national health-law mandate.
*   **Trigger Date:** Immediate. Do not collect a single GPS coordinate until the DPIA is signed off.

1.  **Conduct a DPIA:** You must document the necessity, proportionality, and risk mitigation of GPS tracking. This is not optional for this use case.
