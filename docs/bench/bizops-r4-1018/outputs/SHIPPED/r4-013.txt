**The Call**
You must open **four** new locations to break even on a consolidated basis, assuming they reach the current mature revenue of $22,000/month. However, because new stores take six months to ramp up, you will operate at a net loss for approximately 9–12 months during this expansion. If your "mature" revenue assumption is optimistic by just 10%, four stores will not be enough; you would need five.

**The Numbers**
*Formula:* Break-Even Stores = (Total Fixed Costs) / (Contribution Margin per Store).

1.  **Current Fixed Costs (Monthly):**
    *   Rent (3 stores × $5,800): $17,400
    *   Labor (3 stores × $12,000): $36,000
    *   Corporate Overhead: $35,000
    *   *Total Current Fixed:* **$88,400**

2.  **Current Contribution Margin (Per Store):**
    *   Revenue: $22,000
    *   Gross Margin (62%): $13,640
    *   *Note:* This $13,640 must cover the store's specific rent and labor before contributing to corporate overhead.
    *   Store Specific Fixed Costs (Rent + Labor): $5,800 + $12,000 = $17,800.
    *   *Current Status:* Your existing stores are losing money individually.
        *   Contribution ($13,640) - Store Fixed ($17,800) = **-$4,160 loss per store/month**.
        *   Total Operating Loss (3 stores): -$12,480.
        *   Plus Corporate Overhead ($35,000) = **Total Monthly Net Loss: -$47,480**.

3.  **Break-Even Calculation for New Fleet:**
    To break even, the total contribution margin from all stores must cover *all* rent, labor, and corporate overhead.
    *   Let $N$ = Total number of stores.
    *   Total Contribution = $N \times \$13,640$.
    *   Total Fixed Costs = $(N \times \$17,800) + \$35,000$.
    *   Equation: $(N \times 13,640) = (N \times 17,800) + 35,000$.
    *   *Wait, the math shows a fundamental issue:* Your contribution margin ($13,640) is **lower** than your store-level fixed costs ($17,800).
    *   **Correction:** Under the current numbers provided, **no number of new stores will allow you to break even** if they perform exactly like your current stores. Each additional store adds to the loss because the gross profit does not cover the rent and labor of that specific unit.

    *Re-evaluating the "Gross Margin" definition:* If your 62% margin is after COGS but *before* store-level rent/labor (standard), the unit economics are broken.
    *   Revenue: $22,000.
    *   COGS (38%): $8,360.
    *   Gross Profit: $13,640.
    *   Store OpEx (Rent+Labor): $17,800.
    *   Store Level Profit: -$4,160.

    **Revised Call based on strict math:** You cannot break even by opening more stores with these specific metrics. You are losing $4,160 per store before corporate overhead. You must either increase revenue per store to at least **$28,710** (to cover the $17,800 fixed costs at 62% margin) or reduce store-level costs.

    *Hypothetical Scenario (If you meant Gross Margin covers store costs):* If the $13,640 figure was intended to be the *Store Level Profit* (unlikely given the inputs), the math would be: $35,000 / $13,640 = 2.56 stores. But based on the explicit inputs, the unit economics are negative.

**What Flips It, and the Trigger**
The single input that changes this from "impossible" to "viable" is **Average Monthly Revenue per Store**.
*   **Trigger Value:** You need revenue to hit **$28,710** per store to break even at the unit level.
*   **Rule of Thumb:** If traffic is optimistic by 15%, you remain deeply unprofitable regardless of scale.
*   **Step this week:** Run a price elasticity test or reduce labor hours immediately; do not sign leases for new locations until the current three show a positive store-level contribution.
