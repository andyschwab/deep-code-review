**The Call:** Yes, moving honey across state lines immediately triggers federal FDA jurisdiction that does not apply to your current intrastate sales; you must register your facility with the FDA and implement a food safety plan before shipping your first pallet to Illinois. You cannot legally distribute interstate with only Georgia state compliance.

**The Numbers & Requirements:**
Interstate commerce shifts your regulatory baseline from "Georgia Department of Agriculture only" to "FDA + Georgia." There is no revenue threshold for these requirements; shipping 2,000 jars/month (approx. $240k annualized run rate) fully subjects you to federal law.

1.  **FDA Facility Registration (Mandatory):**
    *   **Rule:** Any facility manufacturing, processing, packing, or holding food for consumption in the U.S. must register with the FDA if the food enters interstate commerce.
    *   **Status:** You currently likely qualify for the "Retail Food Establishment" exemption because you sell directly to consumers within Georgia. Once you ship to Illinois, this exemption vanishes.
    *   **Action:** You must file FDA Form 3537. There is no fee, but it is a legal requirement before operations begin.

2.  **Food Safety Plan (FSMA Compliance):**
    *   **Rule:** Under the Food Safety Modernization Act (FSMA), most facilities must have a written food safety plan and a "Preventive Controls Qualified Individual" (PCQI) on staff.
    *   **Nuance for Honey:** Honey is often eligible for a "Modified Requirement" or exemption from full Hazard Analysis and Risk-Based Preventive Controls (HARPC) if you meet specific low-risk criteria (e.g., no added ingredients, proper heat treatment). However, you must *document* this exemption within a written food safety plan. You cannot simply assume exemption without the paperwork.
    *   **Assumption:** Assuming your honey is raw or lightly filtered with no added flavors, you likely fall under the "Low-Risk Activity" exemption but still require the written plan.

3.  **Grade A Packing License:**
    *   **Clarification:** The USDA Grade A mark is generally voluntary for honey, used for marketing premium quality. It is not a federal mandate for interstate sale. However, Illinois or your specific distributor may *contractually* require it. The mandatory piece is the FDA registration, not the USDA grade.

**What Flips It & The Trigger:**
The trigger is the moment the truck crosses the Georgia state line. If you ship without FDA registration, your goods are legally "adulterated" and subject to detention/seizure at the border or by the Illinois receiver.
*   **Stop/Pivot Trigger:** Do not load the first pallet until you have your FDA Registration Number and a signed Food Safety Plan.
*   **Rule of Thumb:** Intrastate = State Rules; Interstate = Federal + State Rules.

**Routing Note (High-Stakes):**
While the requirement to register is clear, determining if your specific process qualifies for the FSMA "Qualified Facility" exemption or the "Low-Risk Activity" modification requires a technical review of your heating/filtration logs. You must engage a **Preventive Controls Qualified Individual (PCQI)** or a food safety consultant to draft your written Food Safety Plan and verify your exemption status before filing. This is a binding compliance determination; failure to document the exemption correctly is treated as non-compliance.
