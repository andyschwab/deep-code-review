**The Call:** Do not enforce the global cap yet; this is a high-risk change that requires a local legal expert to review your Terms of Service and onboarding flow before implementation. While identity verification for abuse prevention is a legitimate aim, conditioning core free-tier functionality on it without clear, upfront disclosure and proportional alternatives risks violating EU unfair commercial practices and German transparency rules.

**The Analysis (Tier 2 Explanation):**
Under the EU Unfair Commercial Practices Directive (UCPD) and German law (UWG), a practice is unfair if it materially distorts consumer behavior through deception or aggressive omission. Your proposed cap creates two specific friction points:
1.  **Transparency & Expectation:** If users signed up expecting 500 virtual users based on prior marketing or terms, retroactively capping them at 100 unless they provide ID may be seen as a "bait-and-switch" or a hidden condition. EU regulators scrutinize "dark patterns" where essential service access is gated behind unnecessary data collection (identity verification) that feels disproportionate to the risk (running a load test).
2.  **Proportionality (GDPR & Digital Services):** While you have a right to prevent abuse, the measure must be proportional. Forcing identity verification (processing special category or high-risk personal data) to access a *free* tier feature can be viewed as excessive if less intrusive methods (rate limiting, CAPTCHA, email verification) exist. In Germany, the principle of "data minimization" is strictly applied; gating functionality behind ID verification often triggers skepticism from consumer protection agencies unless the abuse risk is catastrophic and proven.

**Illustrative Risk Scenario:**
If a German consumer group argues that 100 virtual users is insufficient for any meaningful testing (rendering the free tier useless) and that ID verification is a barrier to entry not clearly stated at sign-up, you could face:
*   **Cease-and-desist orders** (Abmahnung) under German competition law, which are common and costly even for first-time offenders.
*   **Fines** under the UCPD or GDPR if the data collection is deemed disproportionate to the stated goal.

**What Flips the Call:**
The single factor that changes this from "high risk" to "acceptable" is **explicit, pre-contractual consent**. If your current Terms of Service explicitly state: *"We reserve the right to limit free-tier resources to 100 virtual users for unverified accounts to prevent abuse, and full access (500 users) requires identity verification,"* and the user agreed to this *before* signing up, the risk drops significantly. If this clause is missing or buried, the risk is critical.

*   **Trigger:** If your current Terms do not explicitly reserve the right to cap free-tier usage based on verification status, or if the cap renders the free tier functionally useless for its advertised purpose.
