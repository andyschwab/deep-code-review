**The Call:** Proceed with caution; while your 2% market share significantly lowers the risk of a formal antitrust investigation, the mere use of an algorithm that ingests real-time competitor data to set prices creates a "hub-and-spoke" collusion risk that is not automatically safe due to size. You must architect the algorithm to react only to public, historical, or aggregated market signals, never to private or real-time feeds that could facilitate tacit coordination.

**The Numbers & Risk Factors:**
Antitrust liability under EU law (Article 101 TFEU) does not rely on a specific revenue threshold or a "safe harbor" market share percentage for cartel behavior; even small players can be liable if they participate in coordination.
*   **Current Status:** €600k revenue, ~2% market share.
*   **Risk Variable:** The input data source for your pricing algorithm.
*   **Assumption (Low Risk):** Algorithm uses only your internal inventory, cost data, and publicly available, delayed competitor pricing (e.g., weekly averages).
*   **Assumption (High Risk):** Algorithm ingests real-time, non-public, or granular competitor price feeds via a third-party software provider used by multiple competitors.
*   **Result:** If the high-risk assumption holds, the European Commission may view the software provider as a "hub" facilitating collusion among "spokes" (you and your competitors), regardless of your individual market share.

**What Flips It, and the Trigger:**
The single input that changes the call from "manageable risk" to "high violation probability" is **real-time data symmetry**. If your algorithm and your competitors' algorithms are reacting to the same instantaneous price changes via a shared data vendor, authorities may infer a concerted practice.
*   **Trigger Value:** Any data feed latency of <24 hours combined with coverage of >30% of the relevant market by the data vendor.
*   **Stop/Pivot Rule:** If your legal counsel cannot verify that your data vendor has strict "clean room" protocols preventing cross-client data leakage, do not deploy the real-time feature.
*   **Actionable Step:** If you proceed, consider implementing a "randomization buffer" (e.g., price changes occur at random intervals within a 4-hour window rather than instantly) to break the signaling loop, and document this design choice as an antitrust compliance measure this week.
