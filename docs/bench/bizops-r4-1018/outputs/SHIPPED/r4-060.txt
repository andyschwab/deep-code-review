**The Call**
Do not recognize revenue for the 30% of usage occurring in the final three days until the measurement period closes; defer this portion to the next period. You likely do not need an external auditor immediately to close your next quarter, but you must document your revenue recognition policy and apply it consistently.

**The Numbers**
Under US GAAP (specifically ASC 606), revenue for usage-based fees tied to a distinct service period is generally recognized as the usage occurs, provided the price is fixed per unit and the outcome is known. However, if the total volume is not finalized until the period ends, you cannot recognize the uncertain portion.

*   **Total Runs:** 200,000
*   **Rate:** $0.50/run
*   **Potential Gross Revenue:** $100,000
*   **Deferred Volume (Last 3 Days):** 60,000 runs (30% of 200k) → **ASSUMED** based on your report.
*   **Recognizable Revenue (Current Month):** 140,000 runs × $0.50 = **$70,000**
*   **Deferred Revenue (Liability):** 60,000 runs × $0.50 = **$30,000** (Recognize in the following month when the run completes and billing is triggered).

*Assumption:* The "last three days" usage is genuinely unbilled and unmeasurable until the month closes. If you have real-time tracking that finalizes the count before books close, the deferral window shrinks.

**What Flips It, and the Trigger**
The deciding factor is **measurement certainty**. If your system can definitively count and validate the 60,000 runs before you issue financial statements, you may recognize them. If the count relies on a month-end batch process that hasn't run, you must defer.
*   **Trigger:** If your month-end close process takes >3 days, you will almost always have a deferral lag.
*   **Rule of Thumb:** If the variance between estimated and actual usage exceeds 5%, tighten your real-time tracking or defer more aggressively.
*   **Step this week:** If your engineering team can expose a real-time "confirmed runs" API endpoint, consider automating the revenue calculation daily to reduce the month-end crunch.

**Routing Note**
While the general principle (recognize as usage occurs if measurable) is standard, the specific application to your contract terms and the materiality of the $30,000 deferral relative to your total financials is a binding accounting determination. Consult a CPA to validate your specific policy documentation for ASC 606 compliance before filing your next quarterly report.
