**The Call**
Proceed with the leasing model if your unit economics support the cash-flow delay, but treat the creation of sales tax nexus in Texas, New York, and California as a near-certainty under either the lease or sale model; the physical presence of your equipment in a clinic for more than a few days typically triggers nexus regardless of your Delaware headquarters. Do not let tax fear drive the pricing model, but do budget for registration and collection in all three states immediately upon deploying units.

**What Flips It, and The Trigger**
The decision flips if your cost of capital or default rate exceeds the extra $30,000 gross profit per unit generated by leasing.
*   **Trigger:** If clinic churn exceeds **2.5% per month** or your cost of capital makes waiting 10 months for break-even untenable, revert to outright sales.
*   **Nexus Trigger:** In Texas, New York, and California, the "trigger" for sales tax liability is generally the **delivery of tangible personal property** into the state for use. Whether labeled a "lease" or "conditional sale," the physical presence of the robot in a clinic creates a taxable event.
*   **Rule of Thumb:** If the equipment sits in the state for >30 days, you likely owe tax.
*   **This Week:** If you plan to deploy units next month, consider engaging a multi-state tax specialist to register for seller's permits in CA, NY, and TX before the first delivery.

**Routing Note (Tier 3)**
The determination of whether your specific contract language constitutes a "true lease" (taxed on rental payments) versus a "conditional sale" (taxed on full sales price upfront) is a binding legal and tax conclusion that varies by state statute. You must ask a licensed CPA or tax attorney: "Does our specific lease agreement structure create immediate nexus and liability on the full fair market value in CA, NY, and TX, or only on monthly rentals?" This is time-sensitive before signing the first clinic contract.
