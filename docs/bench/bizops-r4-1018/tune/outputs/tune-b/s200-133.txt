**The Call:** Retain the per-session model (£12) as your primary path for insurance reimbursement in the UK and Germany; a flat £45 unlimited subscription will likely be rejected by private insurers as a non-billable "wellness" bundle rather than a medically necessary treatment.

**The Numbers:**
*   **Per-Session Revenue:** £12.00
*   **Variable Cost:** £0.50 per session
*   **Contribution Margin:** £11.50 per session (95.8%)
*   **Break-even Utilization for Subscription:** To match the revenue of a user having just **4 sessions** a month (£48), the subscription is viable. However, if a user has **1 session**, the subscription yields £45 vs £12 (win), but if the user has **20 sessions**, the subscription yields £45 vs £240 (loss).
*   **Reimbursement Probability (Estimated):** Per-session aligns with fee-for-service codes (high probability); Subscription aligns with capitation/wellness (low probability for acute CBT reimbursement).

**What Flips It, and The Trigger:**
The deciding input is the **insurer's definition of a "billable event."** In both the UK and Germany, private health insurance typically reimburses specific, documented therapeutic interventions (CPT/OPS codes) rather than unlimited access.
*   **Trigger:** If an insurer explicitly states they only reimburse "per unit of service" or require "session logs" for claims, the subscription model fails for reimbursement purposes.
*   **Rule of Thumb:** If the product is marketed as "therapy," it must look like therapy to the payer (discrete events). If marketed as "wellness," subscription works, but reimbursement vanishes.
*   **This Week's Step:** If your target insurers require itemized claims, consider a hybrid: charge the patient a subscription for "unlimited chat," but generate a separate, itemized invoice for specific, clinician-reviewed CBT modules that can be submitted for reimbursement.

**Documentation & Routing:**
To prove medical necessity under a per-session model, you typically need:
1.  **Session Logs:** Timestamp, duration, and specific CBT module completed.
2.  **Clinical Oversight:** Evidence that a licensed professional reviewed or supervised the bot's output for that specific session.
3.  **Diagnosis Code:** A linked ICD-10/ICD-11 code provided by a referring practitioner or initial assessment.
For a subscription model, documentation usually shifts to "membership benefits," which rarely qualifies for acute care reimbursement in these jurisdictions.
