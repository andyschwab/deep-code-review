**The Call:** Reject the price cut; choose the packaging upgrade. Lowering the price to $24 anchors LuxGlow as a mid-tier commodity, likely validating customer complaints about quality and failing to fix the "subtle scent" perception. In luxury goods, price signals quality; a discount suggests the product was overpriced, not that expectations were mismanaged. Upgrading packaging reinforces the $32 price point, aligning perceived value with cost and addressing the root psychological disconnect causing returns.

**The Numbers:**
*Current Baseline:*
Revenue: $32 × 1,500 = $48,000
COGS: $9 × 1,500 = $13,500
Shipping: $6 × 1,500 = $9,000
Returns (22%): 330 units lost revenue + lost COGS/Shipping on those units.
*Net Revenue (after returns):* $48,000 - (330 × $32) = $37,440
*Total Variable Cost (on sold units only, assuming return shipping/replacement costs equal original ship + COGS for simplicity of net impact):*
Actually, let's look at Contribution Margin (CM) per *successful* sale minus the cost of failures.
Simplified Net CM = (Price - COGS - Ship) × (1 - Return Rate).
Current CM per unit sold initially: $32 - $9 - $6 = $17.
Effective CM per unit launched: $17 × (1 - 0.22) = $13.26.
Monthly Net CM: $13.26 × 1,500 = **$19,890**.

*Scenario A: Price Drop to $24 (Assumption: Returns drop to 15% due to lowered expectations, volume constant).*
New CM per unit: $24 - $9 - $6 = $9.
Effective CM: $9 × (1 - 0.15) = $7.65.
Monthly Net CM: $7.65 × 1,500 = **$11,475**.
*Result:* You lose $8,415/month. The margin compression outweighs the return reduction.

*Scenario B: Keep $32, Add $4 Packaging (Assumption: Returns drop to 10% due to enhanced perceived quality).*
New COGS: $9 + $4 = $13.
New CM per unit: $32 - $13 - $6 = $13.
Effective CM: $13 × (1 - 0.10) = $11.70.
Monthly Net CM: $11.70 × 1,500 = **$17,550**.
*Result:* You lose $2,340/month compared to a hypothetical 0% return world, but you gain significantly over Scenario A and are closer to current performance with a stronger brand position. If the packaging drives returns below 8%, you beat current net CM.

**What Flips It:** The return rate reduction. For Scenario B to beat the *current* $19,890 net CM, returns must drop below **12.6%**. If the new packaging only reduces returns to 13% or higher, you are bleeding more cash than the status quo.
*Trigger:* Run a 30-day test. If returns do not fall below 13% by [Date 30 days from now], pivot to reformulating the scent concentration instead of altering packaging.

**Routing Notes:**
1.  **Shipping Tiers:** Heavier packaging may push units into the next weight bracket (e.g., 1 lb to 2 lb), potentially doubling shipping costs. Check your carrier's specific weight breakpoints immediately; if shipping jumps to $9+, Scenario B fails.
2.  **A/B Testing & Legal:** Rotating packaging for an A/B test generally does not require specific consumer protection disclosures if the core product (scent/volume) remains unchanged and no false claims are made on the box. However, if the "premium" packaging implies a different ingredient list or volume that isn't there, that is deceptive. No formal legal review is typically needed for simple packaging variance tests, but ensure label compliance (weight, ingredients) remains exact.
