**What Flips It, and The Trigger:**
The single input that changes this from "illegal" to "potentially permissible" is whether the data meets the strict statutory definition of **de-identified** (HIPAA) or **anonymized** (CCPA/CPRA).
*   **The Trigger:** Under HIPAA, removing names is not enough. You must remove 18 specific identifiers, including dates (other than year), geographic subdivisions smaller than a state, and **any unique identifying number, characteristic, or code** (which includes device IDs unless they are truly random and cannot be linked back to the user). Under California law, "anonymized" means information that cannot reasonably identify, relate to, describe, or be linked to a particular consumer. A static device ID linked to hydration patterns likely fails this test because it can be re-identified when combined with other datasets.
*   **Rule of Thumb:** If a data broker or insurer could theoretically match the device ID to a specific person (even without a name), it is not anonymous.
*   **Step this week:** If you cannot mathematically prove re-identification is impossible (not just unlikely), consider halting the sales plan and commissioning a formal re-identification risk assessment.
