**The Call:** Yes, renting commercial kitchen space in Austin, Denver, and Seattle almost certainly creates a corporate income tax and sales tax filing obligation (nexus) in Texas, Colorado, and Washington, regardless of your revenue level or the number of hours rented. You must register and file in these states immediately upon starting operations; do not wait for a threshold to be met.

**The Numbers & Rules:**
*   **Physical Nexus Rule:** In state taxation, "physical presence" is generally binary, not proportional. Leasing space, even for limited hours (6 hours/day), constitutes a physical footprint.
    *   **Texas:** Leasing real property creates nexus for both franchise tax and sales tax. The $200,000 revenue is irrelevant for physical nexus; it only matters for *economic* nexus if you had no physical presence.
    *   **Colorado:** Maintaining an office or place of business (including a rented kitchen) triggers income tax nexus. Colorado also has strict sales tax collection rules for remote sellers, but physical presence supersedes economic thresholds.
    *   **Washington:** Having a physical location triggers Business & Occupation (B&O) tax registration and sales tax collection duties. Washington's economic nexus threshold ($100k+ revenue) is moot because you have physical presence.
*   **Cost Impact (Illustrative):**
    *   **New Fixed Costs:** 3 states × ~$500–$1,000/year per state for minimum franchise/B&O taxes (varies by entity type) + CPA fees for 3 additional state returns (est. $1,500–$2,500 per state annually).
    *   **Cash Reserve Risk:** The primary drain is not the tax itself (likely low on $200k revenue apportioned to these states) but the **penalties** for failing to file from day one. Penalties can range from 10% to 25% of tax due plus interest.

**What Flips It & The Trigger:**
*   **The Trigger:** The date you sign the lease or store inventory/equipment in the new kitchens. This is the moment nexus attaches.
*   **The Flip:** The only way to avoid this is if the rental agreement is structured strictly as a "license to use" without exclusive possession or if you use a third-party fulfillment center that commingles goods (though baking on-site usually defeats this). However, standard commercial kitchen rentals typically confer exclusive possession during rented hours, creating nexus.
*   **Rule of Thumb:** If you hold the keys or have exclusive rights to the space during your slot, you have nexus.
*   **This Week's Step:** If you have signed leases, consider halting shipments from those locations until a tax professional confirms your registration status, or immediately engage a CPA to file "initial" returns to stop penalty accrual.
