**The Call**
Your current model carries a **high risk** of instructor reclassification in Ontario if your "quality standards" dictate *how* lessons are taught or if "dispute resolution" routinely overrides instructor scheduling autonomy; you should pause the Quebec expansion until you audit and decouple these controls, as the potential liability ($180k+ in back taxes/fines) outweighs the speed of entry.

**The Numbers**
The financial exposure is binary based on the classification verdict.
*   **Formula:** `Total Exposure = Back Payroll Taxes + Penalties + Legal Costs`
*   **User Figures:** $180,000 (processed payments subject to reclassification).
*   **Assumed Variables (Illustrative Only):**
    *   *Payroll Tax Rate (Employer portion + unremitted employee portion):* **ASSUMED 20%** (blended CPP, EI, Income Tax).
    *   *Penalty Rate:* **ASSUMED 10–20%** of unpaid amounts (varies by CRA discretion).
*   **Computed Results:**
    *   *Base Case (20% tax + 10% penalty):* $180,000 × 30% = **$54,000**.
    *   *High Case (20% tax + 20% penalty + interest):* **~$75,000+**.
    *   *Note:* This excludes the cost of retroactive benefits (vacation, holiday pay) which could double the exposure.

**What Flips It, and The Trigger**
The single input that changes the call is the **degree of control over "means and methods."**
*   **The Trigger:** If your platform mandates specific teaching curricula, sets fixed lesson times that instructors cannot change without penalty, or unilaterally refunds students without instructor consent, you cross the line from "facilitator" to "employer" under the *Ontario Employment Standards Act* and common law tests (control, ownership of tools, chance of profit, risk of loss).
*   **Rule of Thumb:** If an instructor can say "no" to a student's time request or "yes" to their own teaching method without fear of de-platforming, the risk drops significantly.
*   **Actionable Step:** If your current Terms of Service allow you to override an instructor's cancellation policy or dictate lesson content, **consider** immediately revising these terms to explicitly grant instructors final authority over pedagogy and scheduling before processing any Quebec transactions.

**Routing Note**
Worker classification is a binding legal determination (Tier 3). You must engage an Ontario employment lawyer to review your specific dispute resolution logs and instructor contracts. Ask them: *"Do our current quality assurance protocols and refund policies constitute 'control' sufficient to trigger an employment relationship under the recent Ontario gig-worker guidelines, and what specific contractual language must change before we operate in Quebec?"* Do not expand to Quebec until this review is complete, as Quebec's *Act respecting labour standards* has distinct, often stricter, tests for dependent contractors.
