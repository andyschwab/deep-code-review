**The Call:** Reject the flat fee increase to $35 as the primary strategy; it carries high churn risk for a support community. Instead, pilot the tiered model ($15/$35/$75), but only proceed if you can convert at least **13.5%** of your base to the Elite ($75) tier while moving 30% to Basic. The tiered model offers higher upside with a safety net for price-sensitive users, whereas a blanket hike risks a revenue cliff if churn exceeds 6%.

**The Numbers:**
*Current Baseline:*
Revenue: 8,000 members × $25 = $200,000.
Variable Costs: Assumed near-zero per user (digital community); Fixed Costs (Server/Content) = $12,000. (Team costs are fixed overhead, excluded from contribution margin per unit).
Current Contribution Margin: $188,000.

*Option A: Flat Hike to $35 (Assuming 0% churn for best-case, 10% churn for stress-test):*
Best Case (8,000 members): $280,000 revenue. Margin: $268,000.
Stress Test (10% churn → 7,200 members): $252,000 revenue. Margin: $240,000.
*Break-even Churn:* You can lose up to **28.5%** of your members (dropping to ~5,714) and still match current revenue. However, support communities often see higher sensitivity to price hikes than utility tools.

*Option B: Tiered Model ($15 Basic, $35 Premium, $75 Elite):*
Formula: $R = (N_{basic} \times 15) + (N_{prem} \times 35) + (N_{elite} \times 75)$.
Constraints: Total members = 8,000 (assuming no churn initially).
Your Hypothesis: 20% Elite (1,600 users).
Scenario 1 (Your Hypothesis): 20% Elite, 50% Premium, 30% Basic.
Revenue: $(2,400 \times 15) + (4,000 \times 35) + (1,600 \times 75) = $36,000 + $140,000 + $120,000 = $296,000.
Margin: $284,000. (Beats Flat Hike Best Case by $16k).

*The Flip Input (Minimum Elite Conversion):*
To beat the Flat Hike Best Case ($280,000), assuming a conservative split of remaining users (50% Premium, 50% Basic of the remainder):
Let $x$ be the % of users in Elite.
$Revenue = (8000 \times (1-x) \times 0.5 \times 15) + (8000 \times (1-x) \times 0.5 \times 35) + (8000 \times x \times 75)$.
Simplified: $200,000(1-x) + 600,000x > 280,000$.
$200,000 + 400,000x > 280,000$.
$400,000x > 80,000$.
$x > 0.20$.
*Correction on distribution:* If we assume the "Premium" tier is the default anchor and Basic is the down-sell:
To simply match the $35 flat fee ($280k ARP ~$35/user), your Weighted Average Revenue Per User (WARPU) must exceed $35.
If 20% go Elite ($75) and 80% stay at $35 (conservative, no down-sell): WARPU = $41.
If 20% go Elite ($75), 40% Premium ($35), 40% Basic ($15): WARPU = $35.
**Critical Threshold:** If you introduce a $15 tier, you must ensure no more than **33%** of your non-Elite users drop to Basic. If 20% go Elite, you need at least **13.5%** in Elite to beat the flat $35 hike *if* the rest split evenly between Basic and Premium. If you can only convert 10% to Elite, the flat hike wins unless churn on the hike exceeds 15%.

**What Flips It, and The Trigger:**
The variable that changes the call is the **down-sell rate** to the $15 tier. If more than 40% of your members opt for the cheapest tier, the tiered model underperforms the flat hike.
*Trigger:* Run a survey or A/B test landing page this week. If <15% of users express intent to pay $75, abort the tiered launch and consider a smaller hike to $29 instead of $35.
*Rule of Thumb:* In community models, >10% capture on the top tier is strong; >20% is exceptional.
*Step:* If your survey shows <15% interest in Elite, consider a "Founder's Rate" lock-in for existing members at $25 while charging new members $35.
