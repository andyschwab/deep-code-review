**The Call**
Do not rely on invoice structure to exclude shipping fees from California's economic nexus threshold; treat the $5 fee as part of your gross receipts for nexus determination. While separating shipping *might* alter whether the shipping portion itself is subject to *sales tax*, California's economic nexus statute (Cal. Rev. & Tax. Code § 6203) defines the threshold based on "gross receipts" from sales into the state, which generally includes all amounts received for the sale of tangible personal property, including mandatory or optional shipping charges if they are part of the sale transaction. With $576,000 in base revenue, adding $192,000 in shipping fees ($5 × 3,200 subscribers × 12 months) pushes your total to $768,000, far exceeding the $500,000 threshold regardless of classification. You have already established economic nexus based on your base revenue alone if the $576,000 figure represents gross receipts from California sales; if that figure is net of something, the addition of shipping certainly crosses the line. The decision to separate shipping is a pricing strategy, not a nexus-avoidance tactic.

**The Numbers**
*   **Current Base Revenue:** $576,000 (Given).
*   **Potential Shipping Revenue:** $5/user/month × 3,200 users × 12 months = $192,000.
*   **Total Gross Receipts (Baked or Separate):** $576,000 + $192,000 = $768,000.
*   **California Economic Nexus Threshold:** $500,000 in gross receipts.
*   **Status:** **Exceeded.** Even if shipping were somehow excluded (unlikely for nexus purposes), your base revenue of $576,000 already exceeds the $500,000 threshold.
*   **Assumption:** The $576,000 figure represents total gross receipts from California customers for the toothbrushes themselves. If this number is net of discounts or excludes some customers, the conclusion holds even more strongly once shipping is added.

**What Flips It, and the Trigger**
The only input that changes the call is if your $576,000 "base revenue" figure is incorrect and actually falls below $500,000 *and* California explicitly excludes separately stated shipping from the "gross receipts" definition for *nexus* (not just taxability). However, under current California law and the South Dakota v. Wayfair precedent adopted by California, "gross receipts" for nexus is broad.
*   **Trigger:** You are already over the threshold based on the provided numbers. The trigger for registration was crossed when your California sales exceeded $500,000.
*   **Rule of Thumb:** If your total inflow from CA customers > $500k, you have nexus. Invoice line items rarely change the definition of "gross receipts" for the threshold test, even if they change the tax base.
*   **Step this week:** If your CA sales are >$500k, register with the California Department of Tax and Fee Administration (CDTFA) immediately to avoid penalties for late registration.
