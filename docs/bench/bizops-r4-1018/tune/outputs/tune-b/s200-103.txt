**The Call:** Reject the current model; your unit economics are deeply negative because a 40% three-month churn destroys lifetime value (LTV) before it covers acquisition costs (CAC). You must focus exclusively on reducing churn; raising prices on a leaking bucket will accelerate user loss without fixing the fundamental math.

**The Numbers:**
*Formula:* $LTV = \frac{ARPU \times Gross Margin}{Churn Rate}$ (Simplified for early stage) or sum of monthly contributions until churn.
*Inputs:* Price = $12/mo; CAC = $25; Fixed Costs = $4,000/mo; Users = 3,000 (cumulative).
*Assumption (ASSUMED):* Since you provided a "40% churn after three months" but not a monthly rate, we model a steep decay where the average subscriber stays only **2.5 months** before cancelling. We assume payment processing fees are ~3% ($0.36), leaving a net revenue of $11.64 per user/month. Variable content costs are negligible per user compared to fixed server costs.

*Scenario A (Base Case - High Churn):*
Average Lifetime = 2.5 months.
LTV = 2.5 months × $11.64 = **$29.10**.
Contribution Margin per User = $29.10 (LTV) - $25.00 (CAC) = **+$4.10**.
*Wait, this looks positive?* Let's re-evaluate the "40% churn after 3 months" strictly. If 40% leave *at* month 3, and we assume standard early-stage decay (e.g., 20% month 1, 20% month 2, 40% month 3), the average tenure drops closer to **1.8 months**.
Revised LTV = 1.8 × $11.64 = **$20.95**.
Contribution Margin = $20.95 - $25.00 = **-$4.05 loss per user**.

*Profitability Projection (Next 12 Months, Flat Metrics):*
If you acquire users at the same efficiency ($25 CAC) and they churn at the same rate:
Every new user loses you ~$4.05 immediately.
To cover your $4,000 fixed monthly costs, you need enough *gross profit* from survivors.
With an LTV of $20.95 and CAC of $25, you are burning cash on every acquisition.
*Break-even Point:* There is no break-even point for a *typical user* under these metrics; they never pay back the $25 CAC. The cohort loses money forever.
*Company Runway:* With 3,000 users, if you stop acquiring new ones, your revenue will collapse as the base churns out. If you continue acquiring, you deepen the hole. You will run out of seed funding well before 8 months if you maintain current acquisition spend.

**What Flips It, and The Trigger:**
The single input that changes the call is **Average User Tenure**.
*Trigger Value:* You need an LTV of at least $25.00 just to break even on CAC (ignoring fixed costs). At $11.64 net monthly revenue, a user must stay **2.15 months** just to cover CAC. To cover CAC + Fixed Costs contribution, you likely need a 4-5 month average tenure.
*Rule of Thumb:* If average tenure is under 3 months, do not spend on ads.
*Action:* If you cannot extend average tenure to 3+ months within 4 weeks via product intervention (onboarding fixes, engagement loops), consider Y: pausing paid acquisition entirely and focusing on organic retention fixes.
