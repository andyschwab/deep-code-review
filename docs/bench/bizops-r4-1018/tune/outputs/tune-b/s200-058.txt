**The Call**
Do not rely on contract clauses alone; a disclaimer stating "no custody" is insufficient to prevent money transmitter classification if your technical architecture allows you to alter balance data or if your operational role facilitates settlement. You must technically restrict your system to read-only access for balance fields and ensure your contracts explicitly limit your role to passive data hosting without any settlement, reconciliation, or instruction capabilities.

**What Flips It, and The Trigger**
The single input that changes the call is **technical write-access to balance fields** or **operational involvement in transaction reconciliation**.
*   **Trigger:** If your database schema or API allows your internal admin tools (or your staff) to manually credit/debit user balances, or if your system automatically updates balances based on external payment instructions you control, regulators will likely deem you a transmitter regardless of contract language.
*   **Rule of Thumb:** Money transmission laws generally hinge on "accepting value for the purpose of transmitting it." If you only store the *record* of a transaction that occurred elsewhere, you are likely safe. If you are the mechanism that *effects* the change in ownership, you are likely a transmitter.
*   **Action This Week:** If your current architecture allows any non-audit write access to balance tables, **consider** immediately refactoring to a write-once, read-many (WORM) log structure for transaction records and stripping all balance-update permissions from your application layer, delegating actual balance authority strictly to the regulated banks or processors your customers use.
