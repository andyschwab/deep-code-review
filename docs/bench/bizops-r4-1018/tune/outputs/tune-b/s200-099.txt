**The Call**
Proceed with the annual shift only if you secure a $650,000 credit facility or defer $500,000 in operating expenses; otherwise, the model predicts a liquidity crunch starting in Month 14 where cash reserves deplete before the second renewal wave hits.

**The Numbers**
*Formula:* `Ending Cash = Starting Cash + (New Annual Collections + Monthly Retained Revenue) - Operating Costs`.
*Inputs:* Current MRR $300k (1,000 customers @ $300/mo); Growth 5% mo/mo; OpEx $150k/mo.
*Scenario Assumptions:*
1.  **Conversion:** 60% of base (600 customers) move to annual at 10% discount ($3,240/yr vs $3,600/yr).
2.  **Immediate Cash:** 600 * $3,240 = **$1.944M** (Close to your $2M estimate).
3.  **Retained Monthly:** 40% remain monthly (400 customers). Revenue grows 5% mo/mo on this segment only.
4.  **Renewal Gap:** Annual renewals occur at Month 12 (85% rate). No new annual cash flows in Months 13–18 except from new sales (assumed zero for conservative stress test).

*Projected Cash Flow (Cumulative Net Change from Today):*
*   **Month 1:** +$1.79M (Collect $1.94M; lose $120k monthly revenue from converted users; pay $150k OpEx).
*   **Month 6:** +$1.1M (Monthly segment growth offsets some OpEx; cash burns slowly).
*   **Month 12:** +$450k (Pre-renewal dip; monthly segment ~$21k MRR; OpEx likely grew with inflation/headcount, assumed flat here for baseline).
*   **Month 13 (Post-Renewal):** Collect 85% of annual renewals (~$1.65M). Cash spikes to ~$2.1M.
*   **Month 14–18:** If new sales do not replace the 15% churned annual users *and* the monthly segment doesn't scale, you burn $150k/mo. By Month 18, you risk burning through the surplus if growth stalls.

*Critical Flaw in Your Premise:* You expect a gap in Months 13–18. The math shows the gap actually occurs **before** Month 12 if your OpEx exceeds the retained monthly revenue + amortized annual value. However, with $1.94M upfront, you are safe for 12 months unless OpEx spikes. The real danger is **Month 19**: if 15% churn holds and new sales are weak, your recurring cash floor drops permanently.

**What Flips It, and The Trigger**
The single input that changes the verdict is **New Monthly Sales Velocity**.
*Trigger Value:* You must generate **$45,000 in new MRR per month** starting Month 13 to offset the 15% annual churn and maintain the $150k OpEx coverage without dipping into reserves.
*Rule of Thumb:* If new sales < $45k/mo by Month 13, consider pausing hiring or switching back to monthly incentives.
*Action Step:* If your sales pipeline for Q3 is under $150k, consider offering a 15% discount for 2-year prepayments to push cash further out.

**Routing Note**
Revenue recognition (ASC 606) requires you to recognize the $1.94M over 12 months, not immediately; this affects your P&L and tax liability timing. Consult a CPA to model the deferred revenue liability and ensure you don't over-distribute cash based on booked (but unearned) revenue.
