**The Call**
Do not bundle the hardware and subscription into a single, unallocated line item if your goal is to minimize sales tax; in Texas, commingling tangible personal property (the mirror) with a taxable data service without a separate stated price typically causes the **entire** transaction to be taxed at the higher tangible property rate. To preserve your margins, you must explicitly allocate the revenue between the hardware and the service on the invoice, even if the customer pays one total sum upfront.

**The Numbers**
*Current Model (Separate Charges):*
- Hardware Price: $1,200 (Taxable as tangible property).
- Subscription: $40/mo (Generally exempt or differently taxed if sold separately as a data service).
- *Assumption:* Texas sales tax rate is 8.25% (Austin base + local).
- Tax per unit upfront: $1,200 × 8.25% = **$99.00**.

*Proposed Bundled Model (Single Line Item):*
- Total Price: $1,680.
- Risk: If not allocated, Texas Comptroller rules often treat the entire amount as the sale of tangible personal property.
- Tax per unit upfront (Risk Scenario): $1,680 × 8.25% = **$138.60**.
- **Margin Impact:** An extra **$39.60** in tax cost per unit immediately, reducing your effective margin on the hardware sale.

*Allocated Bundled Model (Recommended Execution):*
- You charge $1,680 total but list: "$1,200 Hardware" and "$480 Prepaid Service."
- Tax applies only to the $1,200 portion.
- Tax per unit upfront: **$99.00** (Same as current model).

**What Flips It, and the Trigger**
The single input that changes the outcome is **invoice line-item allocation**.
- **Trigger:** If your checkout system or invoice generates a single SKU description like "FitReflect Bundle" for $1,680 without breaking out the service value, the default tax engine will likely apply the hardware rate to the full amount.
- **Rule of Thumb:** In Texas, if a taxable item (mirror) and a potentially exempt item (data service) are sold together without a separate charge for each, the entire receipt is taxable.
- **Action Step:** If you launch next week, ensure your e-commerce platform (Shopify, Stripe, etc.) is configured to map the $480 portion to a non-taxable or separately taxed product code before going live.

**Routing Note**
Texas tax law regarding "true object" tests and specific exemptions for data services is fact-specific and subject to Comptroller interpretation. You must confirm with a Texas CPA or tax attorney whether your specific streaming content qualifies for the "data service" exemption and verify the exact documentation required to sustain the allocation during an audit.
