**The Call:** Elect accelerated depreciation (likely Section 179 or Bonus Depreciation) to maximize immediate tax shields and improve current cash flow, provided your business is currently profitable. This strategy front-loads deductions when your tax rate matters most, deferring tax payments until later years when expansion slows.

**The Numbers:**
*   **Asset Basis:** $300,000 (Medical equipment, typically 5-year MACRS property).
*   **Current Annual Revenue:** $540,000 ($45k/mo).
*   **Annual Loan Payment:** $96,000 ($8k/mo). *Note: Principal repayment is not an expense; only interest is deductible.*

*Scenario A: Accelerated Depreciation (Year 1)*
*   **Assumption:** 100% Bonus Depreciation or Section 179 expensing (subject to current IRS limits and taxable income caps).
*   **Deduction:** $300,000.
*   **Taxable Income Impact:** Reduces taxable income by $300,000 immediately.
*   **Estimated Tax Savings (Assumed 21% Fed + 0% TX State):** ~$63,000 cash retained in Year 1.
*   **Future Years (2–5):** $0 depreciation deduction for this asset. Higher taxable income relative to Scenario B.

*Scenario B: Straight-Line Depreciation (5-Year)*
*   **Annual Deduction:** $60,000 ($300k / 5 years).
*   **Year 1 Taxable Income Impact:** Reduces taxable income by $60,000.
*   **Estimated Tax Savings:** ~$12,600 cash retained in Year 1.
*   **Future Years (2–5):** Continues to deduct $60,000 annually, smoothing tax liability.

**What Flips It, and the Trigger:**
The decision flips if your business reports a **net loss** before depreciation. Accelerated deductions cannot create or increase a Net Operating Loss (NOL) for certain entities without carryback/carryforward complexities; if you are not profitable, the deduction is wasted now.
*   **Trigger:** If (Revenue - Operating Expenses - Interest) < $0, reconsider acceleration.
*   **Rule of Thumb:** Use acceleration if marginal tax rate today > expected marginal tax rate in 5 years.
*   **Step:** If your Year 1 projected net income exceeds $300,000, instruct your accountant to elect Section 179 immediately.

**Routing Note:**
Texas has no state corporate income tax, but federal rules (IRC Section 179 limits, Bonus Depreciation phase-downs starting in 2023) and "taxable income limitations" are binding. You must confirm the exact interest portion of your $8,000 payment with your loan amortization schedule, as only interest is deductible against revenue before applying depreciation.

**Financial Implications:**
Choosing accelerated depreciation does not change total tax paid over the asset's life, only the *timing*. By deducting $300,000 now, you effectively receive an interest-free loan from the government equal to the tax saved ($63k vs $12.6k). You can reinvest this cash into operations. When expansion slows in Year 4 or 5, your depreciation deductions will be lower (or zero), resulting in higher taxable income then. This is beneficial if you expect lower revenue or lower tax brackets in the future. However, if you plan to sell the equipment before 5 years, accelerated depreciation increases "recapture" tax upon sale.
