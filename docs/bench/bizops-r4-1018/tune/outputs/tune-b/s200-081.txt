**The Call:** Unbundle professional services immediately; do not raise the base subscription price yet. Your current model hides a $2,400 annual cost increase per customer that destroys contribution margin. Treat the $2,500 fee as pure software revenue and charge separately for implementation to restore unit economics visibility.

**The Numbers:**
*Current State (Bundled):*
*   **Monthly Revenue:** $2,500
*   **COGS (Hosting/Support @ 28%):** $700
*   **New Service Cost:** 20 hours × $150/hr = $3,000/year ($250/month)
*   **True Monthly Contribution Margin:** $2,500 - $700 - $250 = **$1,550** (62% margin, down from 72%)
*   **Customer Lifetime (L):** 1 / 0.03 churn = 33.3 months
*   **Lifetime Value (LTV):** $1,550 × 33.3 = **$51,615**
*   **Payback Period:** If CAC is $10,000 (assumed), payback is 6.5 months. If CAC is higher, payback extends dangerously.

*Option A: Increase Base Price to $3,000 (Absorb Cost)*
*   **New Margin:** $3,000 - $700 - $250 = $2,050
*   **Impact:** Restores dollar margin but risks churn if value perception doesn't match. LTV rises to ~$68,000, but only if churn holds at 3%.

*Option B: Unbundle Services (Recommended)*
*   **Structure:** Keep subscription at $2,500; charge $3,000 one-time for onboarding.
*   **Software LTV:** ($2,500 - $700) × 33.3 = **$60,000** (Restores 72% margin on recurring revenue).
*   **Service Revenue:** $3,000 one-time covers the $3,000 cost exactly (break-even service line).
*   **Result:** Recurring metrics reflect pure software health; service costs no longer drag down monthly margins.

*Option C: Reduce Included Hours to 5 (Cap at old level)*
*   **Cost Saved:** 15 hours × $150 = $2,250/year saved.
*   **Remaining Deficit:** Customers still need 20 hours. If they pay extra for the remaining 15 hours, this mimics Option B. If they fail to onboard due to lack of support, churn will spike above 3%, collapsing LTV.

**What Flips It, and The Trigger:**
The deciding input is **Churn Sensitivity**. If unbundling causes churn to rise from 3% to 4%, your LTV drops from $60,000 to $45,000, negating the margin gain.
*   **Trigger Rule:** If churn exceeds 3.5% within 60 days of unbundling, revert to a "concierge" tier or subsidize the first 10 hours.
*   **This Week's Step:** If your sales team reports >20% of prospects objecting to a separate implementation fee, consider a "Good-Better-Best" packaging model where the top tier includes 20 hours, priced at $3,200/month.

**Metrics to Track Weekly:**
1.  **Net Revenue Retention (NRR):** Ensure expansion revenue isn't masking churn caused by service friction.
2.  **Service Utilization Rate:** Hours consumed per client vs. cap. If average drifts to 25 hours, your $3,000 unbundled price is underpriced.
3.  **Time-to-Value:** Days from contract to first active dashboard. Rising time indicates onboarding inefficiency, not just volume.
