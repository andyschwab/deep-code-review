**The Call**
Keep your current ratable recognition policy; do not accelerate revenue to improve reported financials for your Series A. Under US GAAP (ASC 606), revenue for SaaS subscriptions must be recognized over the period the service is provided, regardless of when cash is received or when implementation occurs. Accelerating revenue based on upfront effort or cash collection would likely constitute a material misstatement, creating severe compliance risks and potential liability during investor due diligence.

**The Numbers**
Your current financial position relies on the distinction between *bookings* (cash/contract value) and *revenue* (recognized income).
*   **Current Runway:** $1,200,000 Cash / $80,000 Monthly Burn = **15 months**.
*   **Revenue Recognition Impact:**
    *   *Scenario A (Current Policy):* $135,000 contract / 36 months = **$3,750/month** recognized revenue per deal.
    *   *Scenario B (Proposed Acceleration):* If you attempted to recognize 50% upfront due to implementation: **$67,500** in month one.
    *   **Result:** Scenario B artificially inflates top-line revenue by ~18x for that month without changing cash flow or unit economics.
*   **Assumption (ASSUMED):** Your implementation costs are expensed as incurred (standard for SaaS unless specific capitalization criteria are met, which is rare for general onboarding).

**What Flips It, and the Trigger**
The only factor that changes this recommendation is if your contract structure fundamentally shifts from "access to software" to "distinct deliverables."
*   **The Trigger:** If your contract explicitly separates the "implementation" as a distinct performance obligation with standalone value (e.g., a custom integration sold separately that the customer could buy without the subscription), *that specific portion* might be recognized upon completion.
*   **Rule of Thumb:** If the implementation is necessary to make the software functional and is not sold separately, it is bundled with the subscription.
*   **Action Step:** If your legal team can restructure contracts to unbundle implementation as a distinct, standalone service with observable standalone pricing, consider piloting that structure on *new* deals only; do not retroactively change existing contracts.

**Routing Note**
Revenue recognition under ASC 606 is a binding accounting determination. Before finalizing your Series A audit or changing any policies, you must consult your external auditor or a CPA specializing in SaaS. Specifically, ask them: "Does our specific implementation workflow constitute a distinct performance obligation separable from the subscription license under ASC 606-10-25?"
