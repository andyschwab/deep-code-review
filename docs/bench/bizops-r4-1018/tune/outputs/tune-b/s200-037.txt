**The Call:** Your quiz-based customization likely keeps you in the dietary supplement category *only if* you strictly avoid disease treatment claims and stay within established nutrient dosage limits; however, the moment your algorithm recommends specific nutrient combinations to treat, cure, or prevent a diagnosed condition, you cross into "unapproved new drug" territory, which requires clinical trials and would halt operations. You must treat your recommendation engine as a high-risk compliance boundary, not a standard feature.

**The Regulatory Line & Criteria:**
The FDA distinguishes supplements from drugs based on **intended use**, determined by your labeling, marketing, and recommendation logic, not just the physical act of mixing.
1.  **Disease Claims (The Primary Trigger):** If your quiz asks about symptoms (e.g., "Do you have joint pain?") and your pack is formulated to address that specific ailment, the FDA views this as a drug claim. Supplements can only support general structure/function (e.g., "supports joint health") without referencing a disease.
    *   *Red Flag:* Any output suggesting the pack treats, mitigates, or prevents a specific disease (arthritis, diabetes, hypertension).
2.  **Dosage & Ingredients:** You must use only ingredients listed in the FDA's "Dietary Supplement Ingredient Database."
    *   *Red Flag:* Recommending dosages exceeding the Tolerable Upper Intake Levels (ULs) set by the Food and Nutrition Board, or including prescription-only substances.
3.  **The "Diagnosis" Risk:** If your quiz collects medical history to generate a regimen tailored to a user's specific pathology, regulators may argue you are practicing medicine or compounding a drug without a license.
    *   *Red Flag:* Algorithms that output a "treatment plan" rather than a "wellness suggestion."

**Illustrative Risk Assessment (Tier 2 Explanation):**
*   **Safe Zone (Supplement):** Quiz asks lifestyle goals ("Want more energy?"); Pack contains standard vitamins at ≤100% DV; Label says "Supports energy metabolism."
*   **Danger Zone (Drug):** Quiz asks medical symptoms ("Do you feel fatigued due to anemia?"); Pack contains high-dose iron + B12; Label/Email says "Treats fatigue associated with low iron."
*   **Compounding Issue:** While mixing is allowed, creating unique potencies for individual patients based on medical need mimics "compounding," which generally requires a pharmacy license and a prescriber's order.

**What Flips It & The Trigger:**
The single input that changes your status from viable business to shutdown risk is **the specific wording of your quiz questions and the resulting marketing claims**.
*   *Trigger Value:* If >5% of your marketing copy or quiz logic references a specific disease state or symptom relief, you are likely operating as an unapproved drug manufacturer.
*   *Stop/Pivot Rule:* If your legal review identifies any claim linking a nutrient dose to a disease outcome, pause all marketing using that language immediately.
