**The Call:** Proceed with the trade-in program structured explicitly as a **purchase price discount**, not a barter exchange. This approach minimizes income recognition complexity and likely reduces sales tax liability in most states, but you must engage a CPA to confirm nexus-specific rules before launch.

**The Numbers:**
Your unit economics for the trade-in cohort rely on the net cash collected versus the cost of goods sold (COGS) and refurbishment.
*   **Net Revenue per Unit:** $800 (List Price) - $200 (Credit) = **$600**.
*   **Refurbishment Cost:** $50 per unit (Given).
*   **Gross Margin Impact:** The $50 refurbishment cost is an expense against the $600 revenue.
*   **Quarterly Volume:** 500 units.
*   **Total Refurbishment Exposure:** 500 units × $50 = **$25,000** (ASSUMED: all 500 units are accepted and require standard refurbishment).
*   **Total Net Revenue:** 500 units × $600 = **$300,000**.

*Formula for Sales Tax Liability (Illustrative):*
If treated as a **Discount**: Taxable Base = $600.
If treated as **Barter**: Taxable Base = $800 (in many jurisdictions).
*Assumption:* Washington State and typical nexus states allow trade-in credits to reduce the taxable sales price.
*Result:* On $300,000 net revenue vs $400,000 gross value, at a hypothetical 10% sales tax rate, structuring as a discount saves **$20,000** in collected/remitting tax complexity and customer upfront cost.

**What Flips It, and the Trigger:**
The single input that changes the recommendation is the **specific state statute regarding "trade-in allowances."**
*   **Trigger Value:** If any state where you have nexus (e.g., CA, NY, FL) does *not* allow third-party or non-like-kind trade-ins to reduce the sales tax base, you must collect tax on the full $800.
*   **Stop/Pivot Date:** Before the marketing announcement (Target: Q1 Launch).
*   **Rule of Thumb:** If the state treats the old machine as "consideration" rather than a "discount," your invoicing system must split the transaction: $200 barter income + $800 sales price.
*   **This Week's Step:** If you sell to customers in more than three states, consider hiring a sales tax specialist to map your nexus footprint against trade-in laws immediately.
