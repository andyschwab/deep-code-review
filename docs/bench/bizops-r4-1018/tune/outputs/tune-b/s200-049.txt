**The Call**
Do not include the opportunity cost of aging inventory in your headline runway calculation for a Series A pitch; present the standard cash runway of 40 months based on actual cash burn, but add a separate "Working Capital Intensity" note to frame the 18-month cycle as a strategic moat rather than an inefficiency. Including implicit costs in the primary burn rate confuses investors by mixing accounting economics with cash liquidity, making your financials look non-standard and potentially obscuring your true ability to pay bills.

**The Numbers**
*Formula:* Runway = Cash Balance / Monthly Net Cash Burn.
*User Figures:* Cash Balance = $1,200,000; Operating Costs = $30,000/month.
*Assumption:* We assume the $30,000 covers all cash outflows (COGS purchases, salaries, rent) and that revenue from existing sales is either negligible or already netted out of this burn rate for this specific projection. If revenue exists, the runway extends further.

*Scenario A: Standard Cash Runway (Recommended)*
*   **Input:** $1,200,000 / $30,000
*   **Result:** **40 months** of operations.

*Scenario B: Adjusted for Inventory Opportunity Cost (Your Co-founder's View)*
*   **Input:** Inventory = $500,000. Annual Opportunity Cost = 8%.
*   **Calculation:** ($500,000 × 0.08) / 12 months = ~$3,333/month implicit cost.
*   **Adjusted Burn:** $30,000 (cash) + $3,333 (implicit) = $33,333.
*   **Result:** $1,200,000 / $33,333 = **36 months**.

**What Flips It, and the Trigger**
The input that changes the narrative is not the math, but the **investor's lens on capital efficiency**. If your gross margins are below 60%, the 18-month tie-up may signal a broken model; if margins are >75%, it signals a premium barrier to entry.
*Trigger Rule:* If your gross margin (Price - Variable Cost) is less than 3x your cost of capital tied up in inventory, the aging process is destroying value.
*Action Step:* This week, calculate your fully loaded gross margin per bottle; if it exceeds 70%, update your deck to explicitly label the 18-month cycle as "Aging Moat" and keep the 40-month cash runway prominent.

**Routing Note**
While the math above is straightforward, the presentation of "working capital constraints" in a securities offering (Series A) touches on how you represent risk factors to potential shareholders; ensure your final deck's discussion of inventory liquidity aligns with your legal counsel's drafting of the risk factors in your subscription documents.
