**The Call:** Do not print "human-grade" on your packaging or website until you have engaged legal counsel to verify your supply chain and labeling against AAFCO and FDA definitions; using this term without strict adherence triggers immediate regulatory risk that standard pet food claims do not.

**The Numbers & Rules:**
There is no mathematical formula here, but there is a binary compliance state.
*   **Standard Pet Food:** Regulated primarily as animal feed. Requires facility registration with the FDA under the Bioterrorism Act and adherence to Good Manufacturing Practices (GMPs). Labels must meet state adoption of AAFCO (Association of American Feed Control Officials) model regulations.
*   **"Human-Grade" Claim:** This is not a standard marketing term; it is a legal definition. For a product to be labeled "human-grade," **every** ingredient must be edible for humans, and the **entire** product must be manufactured, packed, and held in a facility inspected and approved for human food production (21 CFR Part 117).
*   **The Gap:** If your Ohio suppliers provide ingredients fit for humans, but your co-packer (or your own facility) is only licensed for pet food, the final product **cannot** be labeled "human-grade." It becomes "misbranded" under the Federal Food, Drug, and Cosmetic Act.

**Assumptions & Triggers:**
*   **Assumption (UNVERIFIED):** You intend to use a standard pet-food co-packer or a kitchen not currently permitted for human food canning.
*   **Result:** If this assumption is true, your claim is illegal immediately upon printing.
*   **Trigger:** The moment you design the label. If the facility holding the final product lacks a human-food Safe Food for Humans Act (FSMA) preventive controls plan and inspection history, the claim fails.
*   **Flip Input:** The certification status of your manufacturing facility. If they are not USDA/FDA inspected for *human* canned goods, you must drop the claim.

**What Flips It & The Trigger:**
The single input that changes your path is the **facility certification**.
*   **Value Needed:** A current letter of guarantee or inspection report from your manufacturer confirming eligibility for human-food production.
*   **Stop Trigger:** If your manufacturer cannot provide documentation proving the *finished* cans are produced in a human-edible facility, stop the print run.
*   **Rule of Thumb:** "Human-grade" applies to the whole chain, not just the ingredients. Sourcing local Ohio beef is irrelevant if the canning line is for dog food only.
*   **Step This Week:** Contact your potential co-packers and ask: "Are you certified and inspected for producing canned goods for human consumption?" If the answer is no, consider pivoting your marketing to "high-quality ingredients" instead of "human-grade."

**Routing Note (Tier 3 - Binding Determination):**
This is a high-stakes regulatory determination involving federal misbranding laws and state consumer protection statutes. You must consult with an attorney specializing in FDA/AAFCO compliance before finalizing packaging. Specifically, ask counsel: "Does our specific supply chain and manufacturing partner meet the full definition of 'human-grade' under 21 CFR 101.100 and AAFCO guidelines, and what specific letters of guarantee must we retain from our Ohio suppliers to defend this claim during an inspection?"

**Compliance Steps & Documentation:**
1.  **Verify Facility:** Confirm the manufacturing site is registered for human food, not just animal feed.
2.  **Supply Chain Audit:** Obtain signed Letters of Guarantee from every ingredient supplier stating the ingredients are fit for human consumption at the time of receipt.
3.  **Record Keeping:** Maintain these documents on-site for inspectors; absence of proof equals a violation.
4.  **Label Review:** Have legal counsel review the exact phrasing to ensure it doesn't inadvertently trigger drug claims (e.g., implying health benefits).
