**The Call:** Treat every resale certificate as transaction-specific until a tax professional confirms your states accept a "blanket" certificate; do not rely on a one-time collection for ongoing orders across multiple jurisdictions. You are liable for uncollected sales tax plus penalties if you accept an invalid or expired certificate, regardless of whether the designer is in New York or another state.

**The Numbers & Liability Exposure:**
*   **Average Order Value:** $2,500
*   **Discount:** 40% (Reseller price: $1,500)
*   **Potential Tax Exposure per Order:** If sales tax (avg. 8%) was required but not collected due to an invalid certificate, the exposure is ~$120 per order.
*   **Monthly Exposure (50 orders):** ~$6,000 in uncollected tax.
*   **Annual Exposure:** ~$72,000 plus accrued interest and penalties (which can range from 10% to 50% of the tax due depending on the state).
*   **Assumption:** This calculation assumes an average combined state/local tax rate of 8%. Your actual exposure varies by the specific destination state's rate.

**What Flips It & The Trigger:**
The deciding factor is whether a specific state accepts a "blanket" resale certificate (one document covering all future transactions) versus requiring a certificate for every invoice.
*   **Trigger:** If any of your five target states mandate per-transaction verification or have strict expiration rules on blanket certificates, your current "collect once" plan fails immediately.
*   **Rule of Thumb:** Most states allow blanket certificates, but they often require re-verification every 3–5 years or if the buyer's status changes. However, accepting a certificate from an out-of-state buyer often triggers different nexus rules.
*   **Action This Week:** If you have more than 3 states involved, consider implementing a digital validation workflow immediately rather than manual filing.

**Protocol & Cross-Border Nuance:**
1.  **Verification Requirement:** You must verify the *validity* of the certificate. This means checking that the form is fully completed, signed, and matches the buyer's legal name and address. Simply having a piece of paper is insufficient if the data is wrong.
2.  **Out-of-State Designers:** Yes, the requirement changes. If a designer is outside New York, you must determine if you have "nexus" in their state. If you do, you must hold a valid resale certificate *for that specific state*. A New York resale certificate generally does not exempt sales shipped to California if you have nexus in California.
3.  **Liability:** If you accept an invalid certificate, the state tax authority will view the sale as taxable to you (the seller). You will owe the tax out of pocket, plus penalties for failure to collect.

Sales tax exemption rules are strictly jurisdiction-specific.
