**The Call**
Adopt the 15% success-fee model but treat it as variable consideration; you must estimate and constrain revenue at the point of placement rather than recognizing the full amount immediately. This does not require deferring *all* revenue, but it does mandate a specific accounting policy for refunds that standard legal templates cannot solve—you need a specialized finance advisor or CPA to set the reserve methodology before your Series A due diligence.

**The Numbers**
*Current Model:* £3,000 flat × 60 placements = £180,000/quarter (stable).
*Proposed Model Base Case:* £6,000 avg fee × 60 placements = £360,000/quarter (theoretical gross).
*Volatility Calculation (20% refund rate):*
Assumption: A "partial refund" equals a 50% clawback of the fee (£3,000) for failed guarantees.
Formula: `Net Revenue = (Total Placements × Avg Fee) - (Placements × Refund Rate × Refund Amount)`
Calculation: `(60 × £6,000) - (60 × 0.20 × £3,000)` = £360,000 - £36,000 = **£324,000 net**.
*Volatility Range:* If refund rates fluctuate between 10% and 30% month-to-month, your reported revenue swings by **£36,000 per month** purely on adjustment timing. Under accounting standards (IFRS 15, applicable in UK/Ireland), you must recognize only the amount "highly probable" not to reverse. If your historical data shows a 20% failure rate, you should arguably recognize only 80-90% of the fee upfront and hold the rest as a liability (refund reserve) until the guarantee period expires.

**What Flips It, and the Trigger**
The single input that changes the audit complexity is the **length of the guarantee period relative to your reporting cycle**. If the guarantee period (3 months) spans multiple reporting periods, the risk of material misstatement increases.
*Trigger Rule:* If your monthly refund adjustments exceed 5% of total revenue, auditors will flag your revenue recognition policy as a "significant estimate" requiring enhanced disclosure in your Series A data room.
*Action:* If your projected refund rate exceeds 15%, consider shortening the guarantee to 60 days or splitting the fee (e.g., 50% on start, 50% after 30 days) to reduce the contingent portion.
