**The Numbers**
For a single-purchase model, the relevant metric is not LTV:CAC, but **Contribution Margin per Acquisition** and **Payback Period**.

*Formula:*
`Contribution Margin = Revenue per Student - CAC - Variable Costs`
`ROI per Student = (Revenue per Student - CAC) / CAC`

*Your Figures:*
*   Revenue per Student: $2,500
*   CAC: $450
*   Variable Costs (Instructor ratio, materials, venue per head): **ASSUMED** $0 (Base case, likely underestimated) to $1,000 (High case).

*Results:*
1.  **Base Case (Ignoring variable costs):**
    *   Contribution: $2,500 - $450 = **$2,050 per student**.
    *   ROI: ($2,050 / $450) = **4.5x**.
    *   *Interpretation:* You make $4.50 for every $1.00 spent on ads before paying fixed overhead.

2.  **Conservative Case (Assuming $800 variable cost/student):**
    *   Contribution: $2,500 - $450 - $800 = **$1,250 per student**.
    *   ROI: ($1,250 / $450) = **2.7x**.
    *   *Interpretation:* Still highly profitable on a per-unit basis.

*Why Standard LTV Fails:*
Standard LTV = `(ARPA * Gross Margin) / Churn`.
If Churn = 100% (1.0), LTV = Revenue of one period.
If you force a "lifetime" of 3 years based on hope, you are fabricating data. Investors in seasonal businesses expect LTV to equal **One-Time Revenue + Verified Referral Value**.
