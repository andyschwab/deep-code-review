**The Call**
Reject the pure leasing pivot as currently structured; it collapses your immediate cash inflow by ~82% per unit, accelerating your runway depletion despite recurring revenue. To extend your runway and delay fundraising, you must retain a hybrid model (high upfront + lower monthly) or secure a debt facility against lease receivables; pure leasing forces an earlier raise.

**The Numbers**
*Current State (Outright Sale):*
Margin per unit = Price ($2,800) - Cost ($950) = **$1,850**.
Monthly Contribution (150 units) = 150 × $1,850 = **$277,500**.
Net Burn = Operating Burn ($185,000) - Contribution ($277,500) = **-$92,500** (Net Positive Cash Flow).
*Runway:* $1.2M / -$92.5k = **Negative** (You are currently cash-flow positive and theoretically have infinite runway without new hires/spend increases).

*Proposed State (Pure Lease):*
Month 1 Cash per unit = Setup ($500) - Cost ($950) = **-$450** (Immediate cash loss per acquisition).
Monthly Recurring = $120.
Churn = 4% monthly.
*Month 1 Impact:* 150 units × -$450 = -$67,500 cash drag from hardware alone.
*Recurring Revenue (Steady State Approximation):* With 4% churn, the stable subscriber base from 150 monthly adds is roughly 150 / 0.04 = 3,750 units (theoretical cap), but in early months, revenue lags significantly.
*Month 1 Net Cash:* -$67,500 (hardware drag) + (150 × $120 recurring) - $185,000 burn = **-$234,500 Net Burn**.
*Runway:* $1.2M / $234.5k ≈ **5.1 months**.

*Break-Even Analysis:*
Leasing only matches the current $277,500 monthly contribution if the Lifetime Value (LTV) of a lease exceeds $2,800 significantly to offset the upfront loss.
LTV Formula: (Monthly Price × (1/Churn)) + Setup - COGS.
LTV = ($120 × 25 months) + $500 - $950 = $3,000 + $500 - $950 = **$2,550**.
Since $2,550 (Lease LTV) < $2,850 (Sale Margin + potential service), and critically, the *timing* of cash is delayed, leasing destroys immediate liquidity. There is no break-even point where pure leasing improves *immediate* runway compared to your current cash-positive sales model; the current model already delays fundraising indefinitely unless burn increases.

**What Flips It, and The Trigger**
The **upfront cash collected per unit** is the flip input. You need to collect at least $950 upfront to cover COGS immediately.
*Trigger:* If you cannot secure a debt line of credit covering 80% of lease receivables within 30 days, do not pitch pure leasing.
*Rule of Thumb:* If Upfront Cash < COGS, you are financing your customers' hardware with your runway.
*Action:* If your board demands recurring revenue, consider a "Rent-to-Own" model with a $1,500 down payment and $80/month, keeping immediate cash flow positive.

**Routing Note**
**Securities & Debt Financing:** Structuring lease receivables as collateral for a debt facility or selling equity/SAFEs to cover the cash drag constitutes a securities offering and regulated lending activity. Consult counsel to draft the term sheet for asset-backed lending and verify usury/leasing laws in your target states before presenting to the board.

**Uncertain Assumptions Requiring CFO Review**
1. **4% Monthly Churn:** This is an unverified guess for a medical device; if actual churn is 2%, LTV doubles, but if it is 6%, the model collapses. A CFO must stress-test this against industry benchmarks.
2. **Debt Availability:** The model assumes you can borrow against future lease income; banks may not lend to pre-Series A hardware startups without personal guarantees or warrants.
3. **Tax Treatment:** The shift from sales tax (one-time) to lease tax (recurring) and depreciation schedules (CAPEX vs. OPEX) changes your taxable income and cash timing unpredictably.
