**The Numbers**
Texas Business Personal Property (BPP) tax applies to tangible property used to produce income, including raw materials and supplies, regardless of whether finished goods exist.
*   **Current Estimated Taxable Value:** $8,000 (Raw Materials).
*   **Projected Taxable Value:** $25,000 (Raw Materials + increased supplies).

**What Flips It, and the Trigger**
The single input that changes your status is holding **completed unsold sneakers**. If you produce a pair before a confirmed sale, that unit becomes "finished goods inventory."
*   **Trigger:** If finished goods inventory value exceeds $0 for any part of the tax year, you must report it, though the primary liability driver remains your raw materials.
*   **Rule of Thumb:** Texas taxes property owned on January 1st. If you hold raw materials on this date, you owe BPP tax on them.
*   **This Week's Step:** If your raw material value exceeds $2,500 (the typical Texas BPP exemption threshold), consider filing Form 50-144 (Business Personal Property Rendition) immediately to avoid penalties, even if you believe you owe little.

**Documentation & Filing Route**
To prove made-to-order status during an audit, maintain: (1) dated sales receipts showing payment prior to production start, (2) production logs linking specific job IDs to customer orders, and (3) inventory ledgers distinguishing raw materials from work-in-progress.
*   **Routing Note:** While you can likely file the initial rendition yourself via the Texas Comptroller's online portal or local appraisal district website, **consult a local Texas tax specialist** to confirm if your specific raw materials qualify for any manufacturing exemptions or freeport exemptions (goods destined for immediate out-of-state shipment) and to verify the correct exemption certificates for your jurisdiction. Do not rely on general guidance for the final filing of exemption certificates.
