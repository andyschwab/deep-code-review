**The Call**
Proceed with the $200/listing fee but treat the projected $100,000 as potentially taxable Unrelated Business Income (UBI) until a tax professional confirms it qualifies as "related" income; do not assume the activity is exempt solely because it funds your mission. The risk to your 501(c)(3) status is low at this revenue scale ($100k vs. $300k grants), but the liability for filing Form 990-T and paying corporate tax on that specific income is high if the "relatedness" test fails.

*   **Projected Commercial Revenue:** 500 listings × $200 = $100,000 (ASSUMED: You convert 1/3 of current 1,500 free postings or find new volume; if volume stays 1,500, revenue = $300,000).
*   **Total Projected Revenue:** $300,000 (Grants) + $100,000 (Fees) = $400,000.

    *   *Result:* Your $100,000 vastly exceeds the $1,000 filing threshold.
*   **Tax Liability Estimate (Illustrative):** If deemed unrelated, tax ≈ $100,000 × 21% (flat corporate rate) = **$21,000** (ASSUMED: No offsetting direct expenses allocated to this activity).
*   **Subsidiary Cost Benchmark:** Setting up/maintaining a taxable C-Corp subsidiary typically costs $2,000–$5,000 annually in legal/accounting fees.
    *   *Decision:* If tax liability > subsidiary maintenance cost, a subsidiary becomes mathematically attractive, but only if the activity is definitively unrelated.

**What Flips It, and the Trigger**
The single input that changes the call is the **nexus between the fee and the charitable mission**.
*   **The Rule of Thumb:** If the job board is "substantially related" to your mission (e.g., the primary purpose is placing disabled workers, not just selling ad space), the income is related and tax-free. If it functions like a generic job board (Craiglist/Monster) where the disability aspect is incidental, it is unrelated.
*   **The Trigger:** If the IRS or state auditor determines <50% of your operational effort directly furthers the placement of disabled individuals versus general administrative/commercial sales, the income flips to taxable UBI.
*   **Actionable Step:** If your commercial revenue exceeds 20% of total revenue (you are at 25%), consider engaging a CPA this week to draft a "relatedness memo" documenting how every fee-based listing directly advances your specific charitable exemption purpose.
