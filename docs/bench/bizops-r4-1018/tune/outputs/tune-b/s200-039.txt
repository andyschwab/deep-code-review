**The Call:** Do not create separate SKUs solely for tax reporting; manage this through retailer-level price adjustments and backend tax remittance. The $10,000 annual logistics cost of new SKUs likely outweighs the administrative burden of variable pricing, provided your distributors can handle location-based tax mapping.

**The Numbers:**
*   **Current Monthly Revenue (Tax Cities):** 50,000 cans × $2.00 = $100,000.
*   **Scenario A (Absorb Tax):** Margin drops from 40% to 32%.
    *   Lost Margin per can: 8% of $2.00 = $0.16.
    *   Monthly Impact: 50,000 × $0.16 = **$8,000 loss/month** ($96,000/year).
*   **Scenario B (Pass Tax):** Price increases to $2.15.
    *   Revenue per can: $2.15.
    *   Break-even Volume Drop: To match the $8,000 margin loss from Scenario A, you can afford to lose ~3,720 cans/month (assuming the new margin holds). If volume drops >7.4%, absorbing the tax is financially superior to passing it on *if* the alternative is zero sales, but passing it on preserves gross margin dollars if volume stays stable.
*   **SKU Complexity Cost:** **$10,000/year** fixed cost.
*   **Comparison:** The $96,000 annual margin erosion from absorbing the tax dwarfs the $10,000 SKU cost, *but* creating SKUs is the wrong tool for the job. The correct comparison is between the cost of *software/distributor configuration* for variable pricing (likely <$10k) vs. the $96k margin hit.

**What Flips It, and the Trigger:**
The deciding factor is **distributor capability**. If your distributors cannot map a single UPC to different tax rates based on the ship-to zip code, you are forced into separate SKUs.
*   **Trigger:** Ask your distributor partner: "Can you apply Philadelphia/Boulder specific sugar tax rates to UPC [X] based on delivery location without a distinct manufacturer SKU?"
*   **Rule of Thumb:** If the answer is "No," the $10,000 SKU cost is justified only if the tax rate differential persists and volume remains stable at the higher price. If the answer is "Yes," the SKU cost is wasted capital.
*   **Step this week:** If your distributor confirms they can handle location-based tax logic, consider implementing a price-zone strategy in your ERP rather than physical SKU proliferation.

**Operational & Compliance Considerations:**
1.  **Retailer Acceptance:** Major retailers generally accept single SKUs with variable pricing managed at the point of sale (POS) or distributor invoice level. They resist unique SKUs for the same physical product as it complicates their shelf sets and planograms.
2.  **Tax Remittance Liability:** In most sugar-tax jurisdictions (like Philadelphia), the liability often falls on the *distributor* or *retailer* to collect and remit, not the manufacturer, unless you are selling direct-to-consumer. If you sell via distributors, your "compliance" is often just ensuring your invoices allow them to calculate the tax. Creating a new SKU does not automatically solve tax reporting if the underlying transaction data doesn't flag the location.
3.  **Inventory Fragmentation:** Separate SKUs prevent inventory pooling. If Boulder sales dip and Philadelphia spikes, you cannot easily shift stock without a formal transfer, increasing the risk of expiration (critical for craft soda).

**Routing Note:** Tax liability (who legally owes the money) and nexus requirements in Philadelphia and Boulder are jurisdiction-specific determinations. Confirm with a tax professional whether FizzCraft has a direct remittance obligation or if it flows entirely through the distributor.
