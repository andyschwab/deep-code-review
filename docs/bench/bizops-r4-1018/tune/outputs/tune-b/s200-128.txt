**The Call**
Pass the 8% tax to the consumer in New York immediately; absorbing it destroys unit economics and creates an unsustainable subsidy from rural bookings to urban ones. Do not absorb the cost unless urban cancellation rates exceed 15% in a controlled test.

**The Numbers**
*Current Monthly Gross Booking Value (GBV):* 400 bookings × $1,200 = $480,000.
*Split:* 240 Urban (NY), 160 Rural (VT).
*Current Revenue (12% commission):* $57,600 total ($34,560 NY, $23,040 VT).

*Scenario A: Absorb the Tax (Margin Hit)*
If ZenBook pays the 8% tax on the $1,200 base to keep the consumer price flat:
Tax Cost per NY booking = $1,200 × 8% = $96.
Monthly Cost = 240 bookings × $96 = **$23,040**.
*Result:* Your NY revenue drops from $34,560 to $11,520. Your effective commission rate in NY falls from 12% to 4%. You lose 67% of your urban revenue.

*Scenario B: Pass the Tax (Price Increase)*
Consumer price rises to $1,296 ($1,200 + $96).
*Assumption (Labelled):* Price elasticity of demand is -1.5 (standard for discretionary travel). A 8% price increase causes a 12% volume drop.
New Volume = 240 × (1 - 0.12) = 211 bookings.
New GBV = 211 × $1,296 = $273,456.
New Revenue (12% of GBV) = **$32,815**.
*Result:* Revenue drops only 5% ($34,560 → $32,815) compared to a 67% drop if absorbed.

**What Flips It, and the Trigger**
The deciding input is the **cancellation/volume drop rate**.
*Trigger:* If passing the tax causes urban booking volume to drop by more than **33%**, then absorbing the tax becomes mathematically preferable to losing the volume entirely.
*Rule of Thumb:* Most travel platforms see <15% volume loss on mandatory tax pass-throughs because competitors face the same law.
*Step this week:* Run a geo-targeted A/B test or soft-launch with the tax included for 14 days. If cancellations or cart abandonment in NY spikes >15%, consider a temporary 4% marketing subsidy (splitting the pain) rather than full absorption.
