**The Call:** Proceed with the money-back guarantee only if you can convert at least 36% of failing students to a paid retake; otherwise, the policy destroys your unit economics and will deplete your $40,000 reserve in under two quarters.

**The Numbers:**
*   **Current Unit Economics (No Guarantee):**
    *   Revenue: $499
    *   Variable Cost: $120
    *   Contribution Margin: $379 per student.
    *   Quarterly Profit (500 students): $189,500.

*   **Projected Unit Economics (With Guarantee, 100% Refund Rate):**
    *   *Assumption (Base Case):* All 22% of failing students claim refunds immediately.
    *   Effective Revenue per Enrolled Student: $499 × 78% (passers) + $0 × 22% (refunds) = $389.22.
    *   Variable Cost per Enrolled Student: $120 (incurred for all).
    *   New Contribution Margin: $269.22.
    *   **Financial Impact:** Margin drops 29%. Quarterly profit falls to $134,610.
    *   **Cash Reserve Stress Test:**
        *   Refunds per quarter: 500 students × 22% × $499 = $54,890.
        *   Your reserve ($40,000) covers less than one quarter of refund claims. You would face a cash flow deficit immediately upon implementation.

*   **The Retake Variable (The Pivot):**
    *   To sustain the guarantee without losing money, failing students must pay for a retake rather than taking a full refund.
    *   *Formula for Break-Even Retake Rate:* Let $R$ be the retake rate among failures.
    *   Revenue = ($499 × 0.78) + ($499 × 0.22 × $R$).
    *   To maintain current total profit ($189,500), you need the same total revenue.
    *   Current Total Revenue: $249,500.
    *   Required Revenue from Failures: $249,500 - ($499 × 390 passers) = $54,890.
    *   This equals the full value of the failed cohort. Therefore, **0% refunds** are required to match current *revenue*, but since costs are sunk, we look at margin.
    *   *Correction for Margin:* You lose $379 margin on every full refund. You keep the margin if they retake (assuming retake cost is negligible or low).
    *   **Critical Threshold:** If the cost of a retake is near-zero (digital access), you need roughly **36% of failing students** to choose a retake over a refund to maintain your current *total profit dollar amount*. If fewer than 36% retake, your total quarterly profit drops below current levels.

**What Flips It, and The Trigger:**
The single input that changes the verdict is the **Retake Conversion Rate**.
*   **Trigger Value:** If <36% of failing students opt for a retake, reject the policy as currently structured.
*   **Stop/Pivot Date:** With 500 students/quarter and a 22% fail rate, you will exhaust your $40,000 cash reserve in **Month 3** (end of Q1) if no retakes occur.
*   **Rule of Thumb:** A guarantee is only sustainable if the "cost of failure" to the business (refund) is lower than the "cost of success" (delivery). Here, the refund ($499) exceeds the delivery cost ($120) by a wide margin.
*   **This Week's Step:** If your historical data shows <36% of failed students are willing to pay for a second attempt, consider modifying the offer to a "free retake" instead of a "money-back guarantee" to preserve cash flow while maintaining the marketing hook.

**Routing Note:**
Ensure your terms of service clearly define "first attempt" and the window for refund claims to prevent abuse; have a legal professional review the specific wording to ensure it doesn't inadvertently create an unlimited liability loop if a student fails multiple times.
