**The Call**
Changing your billing from per-transaction to a subscription model does **not** alter your legal classification as a Consumer Reporting Agency (CRA) under the Fair Credit Reporting Act (FCRA) or New York state law, nor does it reclassify you as a "data broker" solely due to the payment structure. Your compliance obligations are triggered by the *nature of the data* (consumer reports for employment/tenancy decisions) and the *act of assembling* that data, not by how you charge for it. However, shifting to "unlimited" access for agencies introduces significant operational risks regarding "permissible purpose" verification and data minimization that could lead to violations if not technically enforced.

**The Numbers & Operational Impact**
*   **Current Volume:** 2,000 checks/month × $40 = $80,000 monthly revenue.
*   **Subscription Break-Even:** To match current revenue at $300/month, an agency must utilize the service for roughly 7–8 checks monthly ($2,400 total spend).
*   **Risk Assumption (ASSUMED):** Under an unlimited model, agencies may run "speculative" checks on individuals without a specific, immediate hiring need to "use up" the subscription value.
*   **Compliance Trigger:** If an agency runs 50 checks/month but only hires 5, you are facilitating 45 potential FCRA violations (lack of permissible purpose) unless your system blocks them.

**What Flips It, and The Trigger**
The factor that changes your liability is not the invoice format, but whether your technical controls enforce **permissible purpose** for every single report generated.
*   **The Trigger:** If an agency subscriber generates a report without a documented, active employment application or hiring intent for that specific individual, you are furnishing a report without permissible purpose.
*   **Rule of Thumb:** The FCRA requires a permissible purpose for *each* report, regardless of whether the customer paid $40 or $300.
*   **Actionable Step:** If you launch the subscription, consider implementing a mandatory "certification of permissible purpose" checkbox or API hook for *every* individual report request, even within the unlimited plan, to ensure the agency attests to a valid reason for that specific pull.

**Routing Note (Tier 3)**
While the pricing model itself is not a legal trigger, the "unlimited" nature may attract regulatory scrutiny regarding your reasonable procedures to ensure customers only obtain reports for valid purposes (15 U.S.C. § 1681e). You must consult with counsel specializing in FCRA compliance to draft updated Terms of Service that explicitly prohibit speculative searching and to design audit logs that demonstrate you are monitoring for abuse of the unlimited tier. Additionally, verify if New York's specific background check statutes (Article 23-A of the Correction Law) impose distinct data retention limits that conflict with a "store everything" approach often paired with unlimited subscriptions.
