**The Call:** Do not raise the price to $14 yet; the math indicates this move would likely destroy value by accelerating churn and reducing conversion faster than revenue per user increases. Your immediate priority is lowering the $45 acquisition cost or extending user lifetime, as your current unit economics are negative.

**The Numbers:**
We evaluate the decision using Lifetime Value (LTV) compared to Customer Acquisition Cost (CAC).
*Formula:* $LTV = \frac{ARPA}{Churn}$ (simplified, ignoring discount rates for this snapshot).
*Current State:*
- ARPA (Average Revenue Per Account): $9
- Churn: 8% (0.08)
- **Current LTV:** $9 / 0.08 = **$112.50**
- **Current LTV:CAC Ratio:** $112.50 / $45 = **2.5x** (Healthy, but fragile).

*Proposed State ($14 Price):*
You project a 30% drop in conversion (fewer new users) and fear higher churn. Even if we assume churn stays flat at 8% (optimistic):
- New ARPA: $14
- **Projected LTV:** $14 / 0.08 = **$175.00**
- **Projected LTV:CAC:** $175 / $45 = **3.9x**

*The Flip:* The math only supports the hike if churn remains near 8%. However, in K-12 edtech, an 8% monthly churn implies an average lifespan of just 12.5 months. A 55% price increase ($9 to $14) typically triggers significant volume loss. If churn rises to just **12%** (a common reaction to sharp price hikes in price-sensitive segments like parents/schools), the new LTV drops to $14 / 0.12 = **$116.66**. While technically still above CAC, you lose 30% of your trial conversions, drastically slowing growth. If churn hits **15%**, LTV falls to **$93.33**, barely covering the $45 CAC once operational costs are factored in, making the business unsustainable.

**What Flips It, and The Trigger:**
The single input that changes the call is **Price Elasticity of Churn**.
*Trigger:* If a small-scale test (5% of users) shows churn exceeding **10.5%** at the $14 price point, abort the full rollout immediately.
*Rule of Thumb:* In subscription models, if a price increase >20% causes churn to rise by more than half the percentage of the price hike, the move is value-destructive.
*Action this week:* Run an A/B test on 300 existing users or new trials offering the $14 tier. If the 30-day retention rate drops by more than 4 percentage points compared to the control group, consider keeping the price at $9 and focusing on ad creative optimization to lower CAC.

**Data Points Needed:**
To finalize the LTV model, you must gather:
1.  **Gross Margin per User:** You cannot calculate *profit* LTV without knowing server, content, and support costs per student. If margins are below 60%, your current $112.50 revenue LTV is misleadingly high.
2.  **Cohort-Based Churn:** Is the 8% churn driven by users leaving after 1 month or 12 months? Early churn suggests a product-market fit issue that price will exacerbate; late churn suggests value fatigue.
3.  **Refund Rate vs. Voluntary Churn:** Distinguish between credit card failures (involuntary) and cancellations (voluntary). A price hike often spikes involuntary churn due to card declines, which is fixable, versus voluntary cancellations, which are fatal.
