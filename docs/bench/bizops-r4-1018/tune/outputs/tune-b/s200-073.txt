**The Call:** Do not sign the "guaranteed zero breaches" clause as written; it creates an uninsurable, existential risk that likely voids standard coverage and exceeds your capacity to pay. Accepting unlimited liability for a flaw inherent to all software (false negatives) contradicts the fundamental principle of cyber insurance, which covers *negligence*, not *absolute guarantees*. You must negotiate this down to a liability cap and a "reasonable efforts" standard before proceeding.

**The Numbers:**
*   **Current Exposure:** The client demands coverage for "all damages." In a financial services breach, this could easily exceed £10M–£50M+ (regulatory fines, customer restitution, forensic costs).
*   **Your Capacity:** $2M (approx. £1.6M) insurance cap.
*   **The Gap:** If a breach causes £5M in damages, you are personally liable for the £3.4M difference. With $400k revenue, a single event bankrupts the company immediately.

**What Flips It, and The Trigger:**
The trigger is the word "guarantee" or "warranty of fitness for a particular purpose" regarding security outcomes.
*   **Flip Input:** Changing "guarantee zero breaches" to "perform services with reasonable professional skill and care."
*   **Trigger Rule:** Never accept liability caps exceeding 12–24 months of fees paid under the contract, or your insurance limit, whichever is lower.
*   **This Week's Step:** If the client insists on "zero breaches," consider walking away; if they accept "reasonable efforts," counter with a liability cap of £500k (approx. 3x annual contract value) tied strictly to direct damages, excluding consequential losses.
