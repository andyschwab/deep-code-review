**What Flips It, and The Trigger:**
The single input that changes this call is **Price Elasticity of Demand**.
*   **Trigger:** If your historical data or A/B testing shows that a $4 price increase reduces conversion by less than 20%, then raise the price to $18 immediately.
*   **Rule of Thumb:** In mass-market CPG, a >15% price increase often triggers a disproportionate volume drop unless the brand has strong loyalty.
*   **Actionable Step:** This week, run a split test on your landing page or Amazon listing: show half the traffic $14 and half $18. If the $18 cohort converts at >60% of the $14 cohort's rate, consider the premium tier.

**Analysis of Logistics vs. Perception:**
Customers rarely pay a premium *because* a product is lighter; they pay because the *total cost to them* is lower or the value proposition is clearer. Your 0.9 lb shipping saving (approx. $3–$5 in carrier costs) is a backend benefit. Passing this entirely to the consumer as a lower shelf price ($14 vs competitors' $18 effective cost) drives volume. Positioning as "Premium" ($18) requires a brand narrative shift (luxury ingredients, spa experience) that weight alone does not support. Given your $60k inventory and need for speed, the volume play at $14 minimizes the risk of stranded stock.

**Risks:**
*   **Premium Play:** High risk of missing the 10,000 unit target, leaving you with cash flow issues despite higher per-unit margin.
*   **Volume Play:** Lower margin per unit, but ensures inventory turnover and market share acquisition.
