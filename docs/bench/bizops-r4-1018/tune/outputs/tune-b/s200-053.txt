**The Call**
Do not reduce free compute by 20% yet; the required conversion lift (from 1.2% to ~1.9%) assumes zero drop in viral sign-ups, which is unlikely given your high viral coefficient (1.8). Instead, model the "break-even elasticity" where the revenue gain from higher conversion exactly offsets the loss of future users from reduced virality.

**The Numbers**
*Current State:*
- Monthly Recurring Revenue (MRR): 450 seats × $29 = **$13,050**.
- Net Burn: $8,000 (costs) - $13,050 (revenue) = **-$5,050** (You are cash-flow positive).
- Runway: $95,000 / $5,050 surplus = **Indefinite** (at current burn).

*Scenario: Reduce Free Compute by 20%*
To justify this friction, the new conversion rate ($C_{new}$) must generate enough revenue to cover the loss of future users.
Formula for Revenue Neutral Conversion Lift (assuming static user base for simplicity):
$C_{new} = C_{current} \times (\frac{\text{Target Revenue}}{\text{Current Revenue}})$

However, the real constraint is the viral loop.
- Current Free Users: 12,000.
- Current Conversions: 12,000 × 1.2% = 144 users/month.
- Current Viral Sign-ups: 12,000 × 1.8 invites = 21,600 potential invites (gross).

*Assumption (Labelled ASSUMED):* A 20% feature cut reduces the viral coefficient ($k$) by 10% (from 1.8 to 1.62) due to reduced utility/sharing incentive.
- New Viral Sign-ups: 12,000 × 1.62 = 19,440 (Loss of 2,160 potential users/month).
- Lost Future Conversion Potential (at 1.2%): 2,160 × 1.2% = ~26 lost paid seats/month.
- Revenue Loss from Virality Drop: 26 × $29 = **$754/month**.

To offset this $754 loss purely via conversion lift on the *remaining* 12,000 users:
- Required Additional Revenue: $754.
- Required Additional Paid Seats: $754 / $29 ≈ 26 seats.
- New Total Paid Seats Needed: 144 (current) + 26 (offset) = 170.
- **Required Conversion Rate:** 170 / 12,000 = **1.42%**.

*Result:* If virality drops 10%, you need conversion to rise from 1.2% to **1.42%** just to break even on monthly revenue. If virality drops 20% ($k$ falls to 1.44), the required conversion rate jumps to **1.68%**.

**What Flips It, and The Trigger**
The single input that changes the call is the **elasticity of the viral coefficient** relative to feature restrictions.
- **Trigger:** If an A/B test shows the viral coefficient drops by more than 15% when compute is limited, abort the restriction.
- **Rule of Thumb:** For every 1% drop in viral coefficient, conversion must increase by at least 0.15 percentage points to maintain growth-adjusted revenue.
- **Step this week:** Run a "soft friction" test on 5% of free users (e.g., delay compute rather than cut it) to measure the change in invite rate before applying a hard 20% cut.
