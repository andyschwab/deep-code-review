**The Call**
Recognize the full $4,500 per bike as revenue at the point of sale (delivery), provided the sale meets standard criteria for transfer of control. The fact that you are subsidizing the interest via a third-party lender does not defer revenue recognition; instead, the cost of that subsidy is recorded as an expense (likely sales and marketing or interest expense) in the same period.

**The Numbers**
*Formula:* Total Quarter Revenue = (Units Sold × Price Per Unit)
*Your Figures:* 200 bikes × $4,500
*Result:* **$900,000** recognized in Q1.

*Formula:* Subsidy Expense = (Units Sold × Imputed Interest Cost Per Unit)
*Missing Input:* The actual interest rate the lender charges you to fund the 0% offer (ASSUMED: 6% annualized for illustration).
*Illustrative Calculation:*
If the lender charges you 6% annually on the $4,500 balance over 12 months:
Approximate interest cost per bike ≈ $270.
Total Subsidy Expense = 200 × $270 = **$54,000**.
*Net Impact on Q1 Income:* $900,000 Revenue - $54,000 Expense = $846,000 Gross Impact (before COGS).

**What Flips It, and the Trigger**
The single input that changes this treatment is **who holds the receivable risk**.
*Trigger:* If your agreement with the lender is "recourse" (you must buy back the loan if the customer defaults) AND the terms effectively make you the primary lender rather than a guarantor, auditors may argue the sale isn't complete.
*Rule of Thumb:* If the lender assumes credit risk and you receive cash (or a guaranteed receivable) immediately upon delivery, revenue is recognized at delivery.
*Action Step:* If the lender requires you to repurchase defaulted loans after 90 days, consider consulting your auditor before booking the full $900k, as this may indicate retained risk.

**Specific Criteria & Subsidy Mechanics**
Under current standards (ASC 606 / IFRS 15), revenue is recognized when control of the goods transfers to the customer. The financing arrangement is a separate transaction.
1.  **Transfer of Control:** The customer takes the bike home.
2.  **Fixed Price:** The $4,500 is fixed and non-refundable (assuming standard return policies).
3.  **Subsidy Treatment:** Because you are paying the lender to offer 0% to the customer, this is effectively a discount on the sale price or a marketing cost to acquire the sale. It is an expense incurred to generate the revenue, not a reduction of the revenue top-line itself, unless the "discount" is so implicit that the $4,500 price is not the standalone selling price. Since you sell other bikes at $4,500 without financing, the $4,500 is your valid standalone price.

**Routing Note**
While the general rule is immediate recognition, the specific accounting for the subsidy (whether to classify it as Interest Expense or Sales/Marketing Expense) and the assessment of "recourse" provisions in your lender contract are binding accounting determinations. Bring your specific lender agreement to your CPA to confirm the expense classification and ensure the "true sale" criteria are met for your audit.
