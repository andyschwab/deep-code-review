**The Call**
Do not recognize the $250,000 as immediate revenue; under standard accounting principles (US GAAP), you must amortize these upfront fees over the term of the agreement (or the period you provide substantial services), as the fee pays for the right to operate over ten years, not a one-time transaction at signing. Recognizing it all now creates a high audit risk and misstates your profitability by inflating current earnings while ignoring the ongoing obligation to support these sub-franchisees.

**The Numbers**
The accounting treatment follows the revenue recognition principle where upfront fees are deferred and recognized ratably over the contract life if no distinct initial service is performed.

*Formula:* Annual Revenue Recognition = Total Upfront Fees / Contract Term (Years)

*User's Figures:*
*   Total Fees: $250,000
*   Contract Term: 10 years
*   Assumption (ASSUMED): No distinct, billable initial training or site-selection service is separated in the contract; the fee covers the 10-year license and ongoing support.

*Results:*
*   **Immediate Recognition (Incorrect):** $250,000 revenue in Year 1; $0 in Years 2–10.
*   **Amortization (Standard Practice):** $25,000 revenue per year for 10 years.

*Impact on Current Fiscal Year:*
Choosing amortization reduces your reported revenue from this deal by $225,000 in the current year compared to immediate recognition. However, this matches the revenue to the period in which you earn it, preventing a "cliff" in future years where you show revenue without corresponding active contracts.

**What Flips It, and the Trigger**
The one input that changes the call is the existence of **distinct initial services**. If your contract explicitly separates a non-refundable fee for specific, completed initial services (e.g., site selection, intensive initial training) that have a standalone value to the franchisee, that specific portion *might* be recognized upfront.
*   **Trigger:** If the fair value of distinct initial services exceeds $0, allocate that portion to immediate revenue and amortize the remainder.
*   **Rule of Thumb:** If the "initial services" are just general setup or brand access already covered by the ongoing royalty, the entire fee is deferred.
*   **Step this week:** If your contract lumps all fees together, consider drafting a service schedule that explicitly values any one-time setup tasks separately; if the value is negligible, proceed with full amortization.
