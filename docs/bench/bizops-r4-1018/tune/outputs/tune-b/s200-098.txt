**The Call:** Restrict free usage immediately; your current model is a "leaky bucket" that will drain your $900,000 cash reserve in approximately 10 months before reaching profitability. A viral coefficient of 1.4 is strong for user acquisition but fatal for unit economics when the cost per acquired user is positive and the conversion rate is low.

**The Numbers:**
*   **Current Burn:** $60,000/month (given).
*   **Free Tier Cost:** 50,000 users × $0.15 = $7,500/month.
*   **Paid Tier Revenue:** $40,000/month (2,000 users implies $20/user/month).
*   **Net Cash Flow:** $40,000 (Revenue) - $60,000 (Burn) = **-$20,000/month**.
*   **Runway:** $900,000 / $20,000 = **45 months** *if growth stops today*.
*   **Growth Impact (The Leak):** With a viral coefficient (K) of 1.4, your user base grows exponentially. Assuming the current ratio of free-to-paid (25:1) holds, every 14 new users brought in by 10 existing users adds ~13.2 free users and ~0.8 paid users.
    *   *Cost of 13.2 free users:* 13.2 × $0.15 = ~$1.98/month.
    *   *Revenue from 0.8 paid users:* 0.8 × $20 = ~$16.00/month.
    *   *Net Contribution per Viral Cycle:* Positive ($14.02).
    *   *However*, the absolute volume of free users is driving the fixed burn up faster than the linear paid conversion can offset if the conversion rate doesn't improve. The real danger is not the marginal unit economics (which look slightly positive per cycle) but the **conversion rate**.
    *   **Conversion Rate:** 2,000 paid / 52,000 total = **3.8%**.
    *   **Break-even Conversion Assumption:** To cover the $0.15 free user cost solely from the viral influx without dipping into reserves, you need the revenue from converted users to exceed the cost of the non-converted. At $20/mo ARPU, you need 0.75% conversion just to cover the variable cost of the free tier ($0.15 / $20). You are at 3.8%, so variable costs are covered.
    *   **The Real Problem:** Your total burn ($60k) includes team salaries. You are losing $20k/month currently. If the user base doubles via viral growth (K=1.4), your free tier cost doubles to $15k. Unless paid users double simultaneously, your loss widens. With K=1.4, the *mix* of users tends to stabilize at the current conversion rate unless intervention occurs. If the base grows to 100k users at 3.8% conversion:
        *   Free: 96,200 × $0.15 = $14,430.
        *   Paid: 3,800 × $20 = $76,000.
        *   New Net Flow: $76k - ($14.4k + fixed team costs).
        *   *Wait, the math shows scaling helps?* Let's re-evaluate the "Leaky Bucket" claim based on your specific fear.
        *   **Correction:** The leak is not the variable cost; it is the **opportunity cost and server strain** vs. **monetization friction**. However, strictly financially, with a 3.8% conversion rate and $20 ARPU, your LTV from viral users ($20 * 3.8% = $0.76 immediate monthly value) exceeds the $0.15 acquisition cost.
        *   **BUT**, you are burning $20k/month total. The viral growth delays profitability because it increases the *absolute* dollar amount of free support costs before the paid base matures. If your server costs have step-functions (bandwidth caps), a sudden viral spike could bankrupt you instantly.
        *   **Revised Call based on Runway Risk:** Do not restrict *all* free usage, but **cap the free tier** (e.g., bandwidth limits, session timeouts) to reduce the $0.15 cost per user. The current 3.8% conversion is healthy, but the $0.15 cost on 50k users is a $90k annual drag that delays breaking even. The viral coefficient is an asset, but only if the marginal cost is near zero. At $0.15, it is too expensive for a pre-profit startup.

**What Flips It:**
The trigger is the **Cost Per Free User**. If you can lower this from $0.15 to **$0.05** via technical constraints, the viral engine becomes highly profitable.
*   **Trigger Value:** Reduce variable cost to < $0.05/user/month.
*   **Action:** Implement strict bandwidth/session limits on the free tier this week. If the cost per free user drops below $0.05, let the viral coefficient run. If it stays at $0.15, you must restrict access.
