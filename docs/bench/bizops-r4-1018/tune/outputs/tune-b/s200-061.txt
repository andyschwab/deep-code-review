**The Call:** Present a three-layer TAM model (Core, Adjacent, Total) in your pitch deck rather than choosing a single definition; this satisfies conservative due diligence while capturing the aggressive growth narrative investors expect.

**The Numbers:**
You currently serve 300 developers at 4M functions/day.
*   **Current Revenue Run Rate:** 4M functions/day × $0.02/M × 365 days = ~$10,950/year.
*   **Growth Context:** At 15% monthly growth, your volume doubles roughly every 5 months.

Apply the three-layer framework to your market sizing:
1.  **Core TAM (Strict Definition):** Event-driven, no provisioned servers.
    *   *Basis:* Your current $2B figure.
    *   *Usage:* Use this for "Serviceable Obtainable Market" (SOM) near-term revenue projections. This is your credible floor.
2.  **Adjacent TAM (Hybrid Definition):** Core + Containerized microservices managed via serverless interfaces.
    *   *Basis:* The gap between $2B and $12B (~$10B).
    *   *Usage:* Label this explicitly as "Expansion Opportunity." State the assumption: "If 40% of container workloads migrate to serverless orchestration." This shows you understand the shift without claiming it as current revenue.
3.  **Total TAM (Broad Definition):** All cloud compute moving toward abstraction.
    *   *Basis:* The $12B figure.
    *   *Usage:* Use only for the "Vision" slide to show the ceiling if the industry fully converges.

**What Flips It, and the Trigger:**
The input that changes investor perception is not the total number, but the **conversion rate assumption** between Core and Adjacent segments.
*   *Trigger:* If your internal logs show >20% of your current users are attempting to run containerized workloads on your platform today, upgrade the "Adjacent" segment from "Vision" to "Near-term Roadmap."
*   *Rule of Thumb:* Investors accept broad TAMs only when paired with narrow, specific beachhead strategies. A $12B claim with a $10k revenue base triggers skepticism; a $2B core with a clear path to capture 5% of the adjacent $10B triggers interest.
*   *Step this week:* Segment your 300 active developers by workload type using your logs. If fewer than 10 are asking for container support, keep the $12B figure strictly in the "Long-term Vision" section. If more than 30 are asking, move the "Adjacent" definition into your 18-month product roadmap.

While market sizing is strategic, ensure your pitch deck's forward-looking financial statements based on these TAM percentages include safe-harbor disclaimers regarding securities regulations; have counsel review the specific language defining "market opportunity" vs. "revenue guidance" before filing any Series A materials.
