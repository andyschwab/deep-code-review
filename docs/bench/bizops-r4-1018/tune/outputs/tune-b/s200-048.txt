**The Call:** Do not lower your price; invest the $5,000 immediately in better photography and sizing tools. A 22% return rate driven by "size mismatches" is a product-information failure, not a pricing problem. Lowering the price to $150 will likely degrade your luxury brand perception without fixing the root cause of the returns, while improving information clarity directly targets the stated reason for the bleed.

**The Numbers:**
Your current unit economics are distorted by the return rate. We must calculate the *Effective Contribution Margin* per unit sold, accounting for the cost of goods sold (COGS) on returned items which you cannot resell as new without refurbishment costs (assumed zero here for a baseline, making this a conservative estimate).

*Formula:*
`Effective Margin = (Price × (1 - Return Rate)) - (COGS × Units Produced per Sale)`
*Note:* To sell 1 net unit, you must produce/sell `1 / (1 - Return Rate)` gross units.
`Gross Units Needed = 1 / (1 - 0.22) = 1.28 units`
`COGS per Net Sale = $60 × 1.28 = $76.80`
`Revenue per Net Sale = $180 × (1 - 0.22) = $140.40` (Wait, this logic assumes you refund the full price. Let's simplify to standard P&L flow).

*Corrected Standard Flow:*
1. **Gross Sales per Month:** $90,000
2. **Units Sold Gross:** $90,000 / $180 = 500 units
3. **Returns (22%):** 110 units
4. **Net Revenue:** $90,000 - (110 × $180) = $70,200
5. **Total COGS (on all 500 units):** 500 × $60 = $30,000 (Assuming returned units are total loss or high cost to restock; if they can be resold, margin improves, but cash flow is hit).
6. **Gross Profit:** $70,200 - $30,000 = $40,200
7. **Marketing Spend:** $15,000
8. **Net Contribution:** $25,200
9. **Effective CAC (per net customer):** Net Customers = 390. $15,000 / 390 = **$38.46**
10. **Effective Margin per Net Unit:** $40,200 / 390 = **$103.08**

*Scenario A: Lower Price to $150 (Assumption: Returns stay at 22%)*
- Gross Units needed to hit same $90k revenue increases, or revenue drops. Let's assume volume stays 500 units.
- Gross Revenue: $75,000
- Returns (22%): 110 units → Refund $16,500
- Net Revenue: $58,500
- COGS: $30,000
- Gross Profit: $28,500 (Down from $40,200)
- *Verdict:* You lose $11,700/month in gross profit instantly with no fix to the return rate.

*Scenario B: Invest $5,000 (One-time) to reduce returns to industry standard ~5% (ASSUMED)*
- Gross Revenue: $90,000 (500 units)
- Returns (5%): 25 units → Refund $4,500
- Net Revenue: $85,500
- COGS: $30,000
- Gross Profit: $55,500
- Increase in Monthly Gross Profit vs Current: **+$15,300**
- Payback Period on $5k investment: **< 1 week** of improved margins.

**What Flips It, and the Trigger:**
The decision flips only if the returns are *not* actually due to size mismatches but are a cover for "product feels cheap."
*Trigger:* If, after implementing the new sizing tools and photography, the return rate remains above **15%** after 30 days, then the issue is perceived value/quality, and a price adjustment or product reformulation must be considered.
*Rule of Thumb:* In luxury goods, a >10% return rate due to "fit/size" is almost exclusively a content failure, not a price failure.
