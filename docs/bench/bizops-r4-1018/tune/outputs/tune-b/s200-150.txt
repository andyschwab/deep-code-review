**The Call:** Do not pivot to an insurance-heavy model yet; the math shows a 60% revenue collapse if you simply swap rates while losing half your volume. Instead, adopt a hybrid "good-better-best" packaging strategy immediately: retain a premium cash tier for high-touch/long-duration care (tier 1) while introducing a limited, lower-margin insurance track strictly for volume stabilization (tier 2).

**The Numbers:**
Current Monthly Revenue = 400 patients × $150/session = **$60,000**.
Projected Hybrid Scenario (50% switch to insurance, 50% stay cash):
*   *Cash Segment:* 200 patients × $150 = $30,000.
*   *Insurance Segment:* 200 patients × $60 (ASSUMED reimbursement rate) = $12,000.
*   *Total New Revenue:* **$42,000** (a 30% drop).

To break even with the insurance mix, you must solve for volume ($V$) in the insurance tier:
$30,000 (cash) + ($60 × V) = $60,000 (target).
$60V = $30,000 → **V = 500 insurance patients**.
*Result:* You would need to more than double your total patient load (from 400 to 700) just to match current revenue, assuming the cash patients do not also churn.

**What Flips It, and The Trigger:**
The single input that changes this verdict is the **elasticity of demand** for your premium cash services. If you can increase the average cash session price to $200 (via longer duration or bundled outcomes) and retain just 40% of your base (160 patients), revenue becomes $32,000 cash + $12,000 insurance = $44,000, requiring far less volume growth to break even.
*Trigger Rule of Thumb:* If cash patient churn exceeds 45% within 90 days of introducing insurance billing, the brand dilution is too high; revert to cash-only or raise prices further.
