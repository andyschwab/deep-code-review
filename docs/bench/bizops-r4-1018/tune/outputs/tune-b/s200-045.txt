**The Call**
Yes, shifting to 80% direct-to-consumer (DTC) sales significantly increases your risk of triggering economic nexus obligations in more states compared to relying on wholesale; wholesale transactions generally do not count toward economic nexus thresholds if valid resale certificates are collected, whereas DTC sales almost always do. You should prioritize implementing a robust resale certificate collection system for your gallery partners immediately to insulate those sales from your nexus calculations.

**The Numbers**
To calculate exposure, you must separate "gross sales" from "taxable sales" used for nexus thresholds. Most states define economic nexus based on gross revenue from *retail* sales delivered into the state or the number of *retail* transactions.

*   **Current State (Year 1):**
    *   **DTC Revenue:** $400,000 (Counts toward nexus thresholds).
    *   **Wholesale Revenue:** $600,000 (Typically excluded from nexus counts *if* resale certs are held).
    *   **Effective Nexus Exposure:** ~$400,000 distributed across customer locations.
*   **Projected State (Year 2, 80% DTC Shift):**
    *   **Total Revenue Goal:** $1,000,000 (maintaining current scale for example).
    *   **DTC Revenue (80%):** $800,000 (Counts toward nexus).
    *   **Wholesale Revenue (20%):** $200,000 (Excluded with certs).
    *   **Effective Nexus Exposure:** ~$800,000 distributed across customer locations.

**Assumptions:**
1.  **ASSUMED:** Your wholesale buyers provide valid, state-specific resale certificates. Without these, states may treat the sale as a retail transaction, counting the full $600k (or $200k) toward your threshold.
2.  **ASSUMED:** Your DTC sales are distributed relatively evenly or concentrated in high-population states (CA, NY, TX, FL).
3.  **ASSUMED:** The standard threshold applies ($100k revenue OR 200 transactions), though some states have moved to revenue-only thresholds (e.g., California is $500k, Texas is $500k, while others remain at $100k).

**What Flips It, and the Trigger**
The single input that changes the call is the **validity of resale certificates** for your wholesale channel.
*   **Trigger:** If you fail to collect or renew resale certificates from your NY and CA galleries, those wholesale sales suddenly count as retail sales.
*   **Impact:** In the current model, missing certs could push your counted revenue from $400k to $1M, instantly triggering nexus in nearly every state with a $100k threshold.
*   **Rule of Thumb:** Treat any sale without a resale certificate as a retail sale for nexus counting purposes.
*   **Actionable Step:** If you do not have a centralized digital repository for resale certificates, consider implementing a compliance automation tool (like Avalara or TaxJar) this week to validate and store these documents before your next wholesale shipment.

**Routing Note**
Sales tax nexus is a binding legal determination that varies by state statute (e.g., California's $500k threshold vs. Colorado's remote seller notification requirements). You must consult a CPA or sales tax attorney to confirm which specific states your current DTC volume has already triggered and to validate your resale certificate processes for NY and CA, as penalties for non-compliance can be severe.
