**The Call**
Proceed with the sliding-scale model immediately; formalizing income-based discounts is a standard charitable activity that strengthens, rather than jeopardizes, your current tax-exempt status, provided the criteria are objective and applied uniformly. The compliance risk lies not in offering discounts, but in failing to document the charitable class served or applying the policy inconsistently, which could trigger "private benefit" concerns. Do not wait for the conversion to for-profit status to implement this; doing so now validates your community benefit mission, whereas delaying it offers no protective advantage and may weaken your narrative for a smooth transition later.

**The Numbers**
Your current revenue mix implies a gross potential revenue of approximately $3.0 million against realized revenue of $2.1 million, indicating a $900,000 annual charitable subsidy (43% of gross).

*   **Result:** Formalizing the scale likely *reduces* the total dollar value of uncompensated care while expanding access, improving revenue stability without sacrificing charitable intent.

**What Flips It, and the Trigger**
The single input that changes this recommendation is the **criteria definition**. If the sliding scale is based on arbitrary discretion by staff rather than objective federal poverty guidelines or documented income thresholds, it flips from a charitable program to a potential private benefit liability.
*   **Trigger:** If more than 5% of discounted slots are granted without written income verification or a standardized application, pause implementation.
*   **Rule of Thumb:** Charitable classes must be indefinite (open to all who qualify), not a closed group of specific individuals.
*   **Step this week:** Draft a written "Financial Assistance Policy" tying discount tiers to specific percentages of the Federal Poverty Level (e.g., <200% FPL gets 50% off) and apply it to all 3,500 families uniformly.
