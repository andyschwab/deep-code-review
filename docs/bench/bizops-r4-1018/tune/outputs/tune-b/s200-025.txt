**The Call**
Recognize the $120 hardware revenue immediately upon delivery and the $35 subscription revenue monthly as earned; the 20% bundle discount requires you to allocate the reduced total price proportionally between the two components based on their standalone selling prices, not simply defer everything or recognize it all upfront.

**The Numbers**
*Standalone Values:* Hardware ($120) + Subscription Month 1 ($35) = $155 Total Standalone.
*Bundle Price:* $155 × 0.80 (20% discount) = $124 Bundle Price.
*Allocation Formula:* (Component Standalone / Total Standalone) × Bundle Price.
*Hardware Allocation:* ($120 / $155) × $124 = **$96.00** (Recognize immediately).
*Subscription Allocation:* ($35 / $155) × $124 = **$28.00** (Recognize over the month).
*Assumption (ASSUMED):* You have 100 customers who purchased the bundle this year.
*Result:* Instead of $12,000 hardware revenue and $2,800 subscription revenue from these users, you recognize $9,600 hardware and $2,800 subscription (earned monthly). The $2,400 difference is the discount absorbed proportionally.

**What Flips It, and the Trigger**
The trigger is the **materiality of the discount** and **state nexus**. If your bundle discount exceeds 30% or if you sell into states with specific "true object" tests for sales tax (where the primary intent determines the tax rate for the whole bundle), your allocation method may face scrutiny.
*Rule of Thumb:* If the discount alters the economic substance of the deal (e.g., selling hardware at a loss to secure the subscription), auditors may reclassify the arrangement.
*Step this week:* If your discount structure changes, consider running a "standalone price" validation with your CPA to ensure your allocation ratios defensibly reflect fair market value.

**Routing Note**
Sales tax on bundled tangible goods (collar) and services/subscription (treats) varies significantly by state; Colorado (your home state) and destination states may tax the entire bundle at the tangible goods rate or require split taxation. You must consult a CPA or tax attorney to determine the specific "true object" test application in your nexus states and to finalize the revenue recognition schedule for your $90,000 filing.
