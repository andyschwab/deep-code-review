**The Call:** Do not assume your early contracts are sufficient; the highest risk to your $12M deal is not your full-time engineers, but the undocumented or loosely documented work of your short-term contractors. Prioritize an immediate, targeted audit of contractor agreements over renegotiating employee contracts.

**The Numbers & Risk Assessment:**
*   **Employee Contracts (8 engineers):** Standard California employment agreements from two years ago typically include robust IP assignment clauses covering "work made for hire" and future inventions. The risk here is **Tier 2 (Low/Medium)**: usually manageable if signed, provided there are no gaps in "prior invention" disclosures.
*   **Contractor Agreements (Unknown count):** This is your **Tier 3 (Critical)** failure point. In the US, contractors retain copyright to their work unless a written agreement *explicitly* assigns it to the company. A simple "work for hire" clause often fails for software code if the contractor relationship is misclassified or if the specific statutory language is missing.
*   **Financial Constraint:** With $50k reserves, a blanket renegotiation is inefficient. Focus legal spend on the highest probability of deal breakage.

**What Flips the Deal & The Trigger:**

If you cannot produce a signed assignment agreement for any of them within 48 hours, consider pausing deal talks to remedy this, as it will be a primary leverage point for price reduction or indemnity demands.
