**The Call:** Do not spend the $250,000 on the pilot study yet; wait until after Series A. Spending now reduces your runway from 20 months to roughly 9.5 months, creating a "death valley" where you run out of cash before achieving the FDA clearance milestone needed to justify a higher valuation. Investors are hesitant due to regulatory risk, but they are equally risk-averse to a company with less than 12 months of runway and no path to profitability or next funding.

**The Numbers:**
*   **Current Runway:** $900,000 cash / $45,000 monthly burn = **20 months**.
*   **Scenario A (Spend Now):** ($900,000 - $250,000) / $45,000 = **~9.5 months** of runway.
*   **Scenario B (Wait):** **20 months** of runway (assuming burn remains flat).
*   **Assumption (Pilot Timeline):** You noted uncertainty here. For software-only digital therapeutics (SaMD), typical FDA clearance (510(k) or De Novo) often takes 12–18 months *after* study completion, not including the pilot itself. A pilot study alone typically takes 6–9 months.
*   **Result:** If you spend now, you have ~9.5 months of cash. Even if the pilot finishes in 6 months, you will have only ~3.5 months of cash left while entering the longer regulatory review phase. This forces a distressed raise or shutdown before clearance is granted.

**What Flips It, and the Trigger:**
The decision flips if you can secure a **non-dilutive grant** (e.g., NIH SBIR/STTR) or a **strategic partnership** that funds the $250,000 pilot without touching your operating cash.
*   **Trigger Value:** If you cannot secure external pilot funding within **60 days**, you must formally shelve the FDA pilot to preserve the 20-month runway required to close Series A.
*   **Rule of Thumb:** Never reduce runway below 12 months for a pre-revenue hardware/regulatory play unless the spend immediately (within 3 months) unlocks a valuation step-up large enough to recapitalize the company.
*   **Step this week:** If you cannot identify two specific grant opportunities or a clinical partner willing to co-fund the pilot by next Friday, consider pausing all FDA-related expenditures and focusing entirely on product metrics that de-risk the business for Series A without regulatory clearance (e.g., user engagement, retention, preliminary efficacy data).

**Routing Note:**
The determination of whether your specific software intervention requires a 510(k), De Novo, or PMA pathway, and the exact design of a pilot study acceptable to the FDA, is a binding regulatory strategy question. You must engage a regulatory affairs consultant or health-law attorney to define the "specific category" timeline before committing any funds later. Additionally, the terms of your SAFE or equity instrument regarding the valuation cap and potential down-round protections if you miss milestones should be reviewed by securities counsel before signing the term sheet.
