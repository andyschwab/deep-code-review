**The Call**
Reject immediate recognition; amortize the $75,000 implementation fees over the three-year contract term. Because you stated the implementation services are **not distinct** from the software license, they form a single combined performance obligation under GAAP (ASC 606). Recognizing them upfront is non-compliant and creates severe audit and fundraising risks.

**The Numbers**
*Formula:* Annual Recognized Revenue = (Total Contract Value) / (Contract Term in Years).
*User Figures:* License = $250,000/year; Implementation = $75,000 one-time; Term = 3 years.
*Assumption:* The "not distinct" assessment is accurate (ASSUMED).

| Scenario | Total Contract Value (3 yrs) | Annual Recognized Revenue | Q1 Cash Impact (Reported) |
| :--- | :--- | :--- | :--- |
| **Correct (Amortized)** | $825,000 ($750k + $75k) | **$275,000** | $675k cash, ~$68.75k revenue |
| **Proposed (Upfront)** | $825,000 | $325,000 (Year 1 only) | $675k cash, $750k revenue |

*Runway Check:* Cash $150,000 / Burn $40,000 = **3.75 months**. Immediate revenue recognition does not increase cash; it only changes the income statement. Your runway remains critical regardless of accounting treatment.

**What Flips It, and the Trigger**
The single input that changes this call is **distinctness**. If the implementation service is **distinct** (the customer can benefit from it on its own or with readily available resources, and it is separately identifiable in the contract), you could recognize the $75,000 upon completion of that specific service.
*Trigger:* If your legal counsel and auditors sign off on a "distinct" assessment before the audit fieldwork closes, you may separate the obligations.
*Rule of Thumb:* If the software is useless without your specific setup, it is not distinct.
*Step:* If the services are truly integrated, consider restructuring future contracts to sell a standardized, low-touch "self-serve" implementation that customers can use independently, potentially creating a distinct obligation for future deals.

**Routing Note**
This is a binding accounting determination under US GAAP (ASC 606). You must finalize the "distinct vs. combined" performance obligation analysis with your external auditors before filing financials for Series B. Misstating revenue to secure funding can constitute securities fraud. Bring the specific statement of work (SOW) language to your CPA immediately to document the single performance obligation conclusion.
