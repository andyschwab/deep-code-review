**The Call:** Do not lower prices; the 8% churn signals a product-market fit gap in integration capabilities, not a pricing failure. Pull the product lever first by building the top two requested integrations, as discounting a deficient product accelerates cash burn without fixing retention.

**The Numbers:**
Your current unit economics are fragile. With $200k ARR, your Monthly Recurring Revenue (MRR) is ~$16,667.
*   **Current Margin:** $16,667 (Revenue) - $3,000 (Server Costs) = $13,667 Gross Profit/month.
*   **Churn Impact:** At 8% monthly churn, you lose ~$1,333 in MRR every month before adding a single new customer. You must replace 8% of your base just to stand still.
*   **Discount Scenario (Tier 1 Compute):** If you cut prices by 20%:
    *   New Estimated MRR: $16,667 * 0.80 = $13,334.
    *   New Gross Profit: $13,334 - $3,000 = $10,334.
    *   **Result:** You sacrifice $3,333 in monthly gross profit (a 24% reduction in cash flow) to potentially solve a problem price didn't cause. If churn remains at 8% because the product still lacks integrations, you have simply reduced your runway while keeping the same leak.

**Assumptions:**
*   **ASSUMED (Low):** Churn drops to 4% immediately after price cut. (Unlikely without product fixes).
*   **ASSUMED (Base):** Churn stays at 8% because the core value prop (integrations) is missing.
*   **ASSUMED (High):** Server costs scale linearly with user count (currently low at ~18% of revenue, so margin exists, but cash is king for seed rounds).

**What Flips It, and The Trigger:**
The single input that changes this call is **integration adoption**. If building the missing integrations does not reduce churn below 5% within 60 days, *then* you may have a pricing/willingness-to-pay issue.
*   **Trigger Rule:** If churn remains >6% two months after shipping the top two requested integrations, reconsider pricing tiers or target market.
*   **Rule of Thumb:** SaaS startups targeting developers (startups) typically tolerate higher prices if workflow integration is seamless; they churn instantly if the tool creates manual work.
*   **This Week's Step:** If 40% of departing customers cited "missing integrations," consider halting all price discount experiments and committing engineering resources to ship one high-demand integration as a beta for current at-risk customers.
