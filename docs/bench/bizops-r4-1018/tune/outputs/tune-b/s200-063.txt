**The Analysis:**
Under US federal law (HIPAA), there are two primary paths to de-identify Protected Health Information (PHI) so it is no longer subject to the rule:
1.  **Safe Harbor Method:** Removal of 18 specific identifiers (names, dates, geographies smaller than a state, etc.). While your output rows do not match single individuals, the "Safe Harbor" rule is strict about the *input* data handling and the potential for re-identification if any of the 18 categories remain or can be reconstructed.
2.  **Expert Determination Method:** A qualified statistician certifies that the risk of re-identification is "very small." This is likely the path your algorithm attempts to satisfy. However, the recent audit flagging "combinability with public datasets" directly attacks this certification. If your synthetic data preserves high-fidelity statistical correlations from 1 million real charts, advanced linkage attacks using public census or health data could theoretically re-identify cohorts or individuals, violating the "very small" risk threshold.

**Typical Safeguards & Industry Practice:**
Companies monetizing synthetic health data in the US typically implement the following to secure legal cover:
*   **Differential Privacy (DP):** Injecting mathematical noise into the generation process to guarantee that the inclusion or exclusion of any single individual's data in the training set does not significantly affect the output distribution. This is increasingly becoming the gold standard for defending against linkage attacks.
*   **Re-identification Testing:** Running active "red team" attacks where third parties attempt to link the synthetic data back to the source using known public datasets before release.
*   **Data Use Agreements (DUAs):** Even if data is de-identified, contracts often explicitly forbid recipients from attempting re-identification or combining the data with other specific datasets.
