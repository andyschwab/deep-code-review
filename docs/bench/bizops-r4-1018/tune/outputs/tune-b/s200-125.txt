**The Call:** Do not proceed with the "franchisee" reclassification as described; requiring an upfront fee and royalty while exerting control over methods almost certainly triggers mandatory franchise registration in Florida and Georgia, exposing you to severe penalties and rescission rights before you collect a dollar. The liability shift you seek is illusory without a properly registered franchise disclosure document (FDD), and the tax reporting change from 1099 to franchise royalty reporting is secondary to the securities-like regulatory breach of selling an unregistered franchise.

**The Numbers:**
*   **Current Model (Affiliates):** 50 coaches × $300/package × 20% commission = $3,000/coach annual payout (variable cost). Revenue share: $360,000 total. Tax form: 1099-NEC.
*   **Proposed Model (Franchisees):** 30 converts × $15,000 fee = $450,000 immediate cash inflow. Ongoing: 5% royalty on $300 packages = $15/coach.
*   **The Trigger Cost:** Franchise registration and FDD creation typically costs $25,000–$50,000 in legal fees upfront per state, plus state filing fees (~$500–$1,000).
*   **Net Immediate Cash:** $450,000 (fees) - $40,000 (estimated legal/filing) = ~$410,000, *only if* the model legally qualifies as a franchise and is registered. If unregistered, you face fines up to $10,000 per violation and forced refunds.

**What Flips It, and the Trigger:**
The single input that forces registration is the combination of **(1) a required payment** ($15,000 fee + 5% royalty) and **(2) significant control** over the coach's method of operation (standardizing sleep methods). Under the FTC Franchise Rule and both FL/GA state laws, if you mandate a specific operational system and charge a fee, you are selling a franchise regardless of the "affiliate" label.
*   **Trigger Threshold:** If you require the $15,000 fee AND dictate the "SleepNest Method" (scripts, protocols, branding usage), you have crossed the legal definition.
*   **Stop/Pivot Date:** Immediately. Do not collect the $15,000 fee or sign new agreements until counsel reviews the structure.
*   **Rule of Thumb:** Payment + Control + Trademark License = Franchise. Removing the fee keeps you in affiliate territory; removing control makes the business model unviable for standardization.
*   **This Week's Step:** If you must standardize methods, consider a "licensed consultant" model with no upfront fee, only a royalty, but verify if the royalty alone triggers state-specific thresholds (some states have minimum payment thresholds, e.g., $500/year, which you would exceed).

**Routing Note:**
You must engage a franchise attorney licensed in Florida and Georgia to draft a Franchise Disclosure Document (FDD) and handle state registrations before offering the $15,000 package. Specifically, ask them: "Does our proposed 'standardized method' combined with the $15,000 fee and 5% royalty meet the definition of a franchise under FL Stat. 556.10 and GA Code Title 10, and what are the specific rescission risks if we proceed without registration?"
