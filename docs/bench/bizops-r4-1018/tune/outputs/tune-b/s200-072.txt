**The Call**
Stick with the **seat-based model ($49/seat)** for now, but introduce a hybrid "enterprise tier" cap rather than switching fully to instance-based pricing. The instance model creates a 2.5x price jump in your example that acts as a adoption tax, likely stalling the internal expansion required to maximize Lifetime Value (LTV) in enterprise accounts.

**The Numbers**
*Current State:* 160 seats × $49 = **$7,840 MRR** (aligns with your ~$8k).
*Burn:* $35,000/mo.
*Runway:* $7,840 / $35,000 ≈ **0.22 months** of revenue coverage (you are pre-profitability; growth speed is critical).

*Scenario A (Seat Model - Expansion):*
Enterprise grows from 200 to 300 developers (50% team growth).
Revenue: 300 × $49 = **$14,700**.
*Growth Driver:* Frictionless addition of new users/tools.

*Scenario B (Instance Model - Value Capture):*
Enterprise runs 50 apps. Price set at $500/app.
Revenue: 50 × $500 = **$25,000**.
*Churn Risk:* If the customer hesitates to spin up App #51 due to the $500 marginal cost, your Net Revenue Retention (NRR) drops.

*ASSUMED (Base Case):* Enterprise customers typically expand seat count by 20-30% annually but add application instances sporadically.
*ASSUMED (High Risk):* Instance pricing reduces the velocity of new app deployment by 40% due to internal budget approval friction.

**What Flips It, and The Trigger**
The deciding input is **Marginal Deployment Friction**. If your sales data shows customers delaying new micro-frontends because of per-app costs, the instance model is destroying LTV.
*Trigger Threshold:* If the average revenue per account (ARPA) under the instance model exceeds **1.8x** the projected seat-model revenue (adjusted for organic seat growth), reconsider the hybrid cap.
*Rule of Thumb:* In platform infrastructure, pricing should feel like a "utility" (pay for users) rather than a "toll booth" (pay per transaction/app) during the adoption phase.
*This Week's Step:* If your pilot customers have not yet deployed >10 apps each, **do not switch**. Instead, propose a "Platform Cap" to your Fortune 500 pilots: "$49/seat, capped at $20k/month regardless of app count." This tests their willingness to pay for volume without penalizing experimentation.

**Data to Gather for Definitive Decision**
To move from assumption to fact, instrument these three metrics immediately:
1.  **Deployment Velocity:** Track the time between "decision to build" and "production deploy" for your pilots. If this slows as app count rises, instance pricing is the culprit.
2.  **Seat-to-App Ratio:** Calculate the average number of developers per application instance across your 12 pilots. If this ratio is stable (e.g., always ~4 devs per app), instance pricing is mathematically safe. If it varies wildly, seat pricing is safer.
3.  **Budget Approval Cycle Length:** Survey your champions on whether a $500 line-item requires a different sign-off level than a $49 line-item. Enterprise sales cycles often break at specific procurement thresholds.
