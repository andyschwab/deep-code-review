**The Call:** Register for a Canadian GST/HST account immediately; you likely owe tax from the first sale to Canada because your US-based sales count toward the $30,000 CAD "small supplier" threshold, and you must collect province-specific rates for Ontario, British Columbia, and Quebec at checkout.

**The Numbers & Rules:**
Canada's federal threshold for mandatory registration is $30,000 CAD in worldwide taxable supplies over four consecutive quarters. As a non-resident seller, your US sales count toward this limit. If your global revenue exceeds $30,000 CAD, you must register. Even if under, voluntary registration is often required to collect tax legally in some provinces if you have a "nexus" (though physical presence is the primary trigger, digital services and specific provincial rules for non-residents can complicate this; for physical goods shipped from the US, the primary trigger is the federal small supplier limit).

*   **Ontario:** Harmonized Sales Tax (HST) of 13% applies. You collect this if registered.
*   **British Columbia:** Provincial Sales Tax (PST) of 7% + GST 5%. Non-residents selling tangible personal property into BC generally must register for PST immediately upon making sales, regardless of the federal $30k threshold, unless specific exemptions apply (which rarely cover general furniture).
*   **Quebec:** QST of 9.975% + GST 5%. Quebec requires non-resident sellers to register for QST if they make taxable supplies in Quebec, often with no minimum threshold for remote sellers of tangible goods.

**Furniture Specifics:** Furniture is generally a taxable good. The $450 average order value does not trigger an exemption; low-value import exemptions typically apply to duties, not sales tax collection obligations for registered vendors. There are no special reduced rates for flat-pack furniture in these provinces.

**What Flips It:** The trigger is not a future Canadian revenue target, but your *global* revenue exceeding $30,000 CAD or the specific provincial rules for BC and Quebec which often require immediate registration for non-residents selling tangible goods.
*   **Trigger:** First sale to BC or Quebec (likely immediate registration required); First sale after global revenue >$30k CAD (federal/GST/HST).
*   **Action:** If you process 50 orders ($22,500 USD ≈ $30,500 CAD) next month, you will almost certainly breach the small supplier threshold or hit provincial mandatory registration rules.

**Steps to Take:**
1.  **Register:** Apply for a GST/HST account with the CRA. Simultaneously apply for PST (BC) and QST (Quebec) accounts.
2.  **Configure Checkout:** Update your e-commerce platform to calculate and collect 13% for ON, 12% total (5+7) for BC, and ~15% total (5+9.975) for QC based on the shipping address.
3.  **Fiscal Representative:** You generally do not *need* a local fiscal representative to register, but you must file returns. Consider a Canadian tax agent for filing if you lack local expertise.
4.  **Duties:** Inform customers they may owe duties upon import, though this is separate from your sales tax collection.

**Routing Note:** This involves binding cross-border tax obligations and specific provincial registration statutes for non-residents. You must confirm your exact "nexus" status and registration deadlines with a Canadian CPA or tax lawyer specializing in US-Canada cross-border commerce before shipping the first unit.
