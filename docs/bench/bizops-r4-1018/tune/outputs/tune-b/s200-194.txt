**What Flips It, and the Trigger:**
The determining factor is the **Howey Test**, specifically the expectation of profits derived from the efforts of others. Because students pay $0 upfront and repay only upon success, regulators may view the $12,000 tuition as an "investment" by Apex Prep into the student, where Apex's return (the 8% share) depends entirely on the student's future labor and Apex's placement efforts.
*   **Trigger:** Offering contracts where repayment is contingent on future income rather than a fixed debt obligation.
*   **Stop Rule:** If legal counsel cannot structure this explicitly as a "Service Agreement with Contingent Fees" rather than an "Investment Contract," do not proceed.
*   **This Week's Step:** If you intend to pursue this, consider hiring a securities attorney immediately to draft a "no-action" letter request or opinion before accepting a single student under these terms.

**Routing Note (Critical):**
This is a **Tier 3** determination. The question of whether an Income Share Agreement (ISA) constitutes a security under the Securities Act of 1933 is a binding legal verdict, not a math problem. You must ask a licensed securities attorney: *"Does our specific ISA structure, where tuition is deferred entirely and repayment is a percentage of future income, satisfy the prongs of the Howey Test in our jurisdiction?"* Do not rely on general rules; the SEC and state regulators actively scrutinize ISAs. Launching without registration if deemed a security creates unlimited liability for rescission (refunding all payments) and penalties, which will trigger immediate due diligence failures with any future venture capital firm.
