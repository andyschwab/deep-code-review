**The Call**
Your shift to leasing likely reclassifies your revenue from a one-time sale of tangible personal property to a recurring taxable lease of tangible personal property in most US states, and yes, physically placing equipment in a state almost certainly creates sales tax nexus where you previously had none. You must register and collect sales tax in every state where a leased unit sits, rather than treating this as a non-taxable service or a single point-of-sale event.

**The Numbers**
*Current Monthly Revenue:* $10,000.
*Estimated Tax Exposure (Illustrative):* Assuming an average combined state/local sales tax rate of **8%** (ASSUMED, varies by jurisdiction):
*   **Monthly Liability:** $10,000 × 0.08 = **$800/month**.
*   **Annual Liability:** $800 × 12 = **$9,600/year**.
*   *Sensitivity:* If your average rate is 6% (low), liability is $7,200/year; if 10% (high), liability is $12,000/year.
*Note:* Unlike a one-time sale where tax is collected on the full $40,000 unit price upfront, leasing typically requires collecting tax on each monthly rental payment. However, some states may tax the full fair market value upfront upon lease inception; this varies by statute.

**What Flips It, and The Trigger**
The specific definition of "tangible personal property" vs. "service" in each state flips the taxability. While most states tax hardware leases as tangible property, a few may exempt certain educational or aviation-specific equipment, or treat "full-service leases" (where you maintain the software/hardware) differently.
*   **Trigger:** The moment a lease agreement is signed and equipment is shipped to a new state.
*   **Rule of Thumb:** Physical presence of inventory/equipment = Nexus. Do not wait for a revenue threshold; physical presence usually establishes immediate nexus.
*   **Action This Week:** If you have units in more than one state, list every state where a unit is currently located. For each, check the state Department of Revenue website for "tangible personal property lease taxation." If any state requires registration, file immediately to avoid penalties on back-taxes.

**Routing Note**
This is a high-stakes, jurisdiction-specific determination. You must engage a **CPA or sales tax attorney** specializing in multi-state equipment leasing.
*   **Exact Question:** "Does our specific lease structure (hardware + software + maintenance) constitute a taxable lease of tangible personal property or a non-taxable service in [State X], and do we owe use tax on the inventory we moved there prior to signing leases?"
*   **Time-Sensitive:** Registration should ideally happen before the next filing deadline to mitigate penalty risks for prior months.
