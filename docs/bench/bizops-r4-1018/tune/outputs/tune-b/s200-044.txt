*Valuation Impact:*
VCs value scalability. A market capped at 500 units reduces the Total Addressable Market (TAM) for Chicago by 90%. If Chicago was projected to be 20% of your growth story, that narrative collapses. The risk profile shifts from "execution risk" to "regulatory ceiling risk," likely compressing your valuation multiple unless you prove the cap can be lifted via performance metrics.

**What Flips It, and The Trigger**
The single input that changes the call is the **variable cost ratio** in the capped market. If you can operate the 500-scooter fleet with less than 25% of your current fixed overhead (e.g., by using third-party logistics rather than dedicated staff), the unit economics remain attractive.
*Trigger:* If your localized operating costs exceed $135,000/month (50% of current OpEx) while revenue stays under $300,000, **pause the dedicated Chicago hire** and switch to a lean contractor model immediately.
*Rule of Thumb:* Do not deploy full-time local infrastructure until the permit allows >1,500 units.
*This Week's Step:* If the permit application fee exceeds $10,000 or requires a bond > $50,000, consider delaying the application until you have secured a "performance-based expansion" clause in the city code.
