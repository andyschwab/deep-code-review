**The Call**
Offer exactly **20%** equity to the investor; this matches the $1M investment on a $4M pre-money valuation. Do not alter this percentage to account for the $50k debt in the cap table math, as debt is a liability settled by company assets, not a direct reduction of founder ownership percentage, though it must be disclosed during due diligence. To retain control, ensure your founding group retains 80% combined, preserving your 60-20-20 split relative to each other within that remaining block.

**The Numbers**
*Formula:* Investor % = Investment / (Pre-Money Valuation + Investment).
*Inputs:* $1,000,000 / ($4,000,000 + $1,000,000) = 20%.
*Post-Money Valuation:* $5,000,000.
*Founder Dilution:* All existing shareholders are diluted by a factor of 0.8 (100% - 20%).
*Resulting Ownership:*
*   **Investor:** 20.0%
*   **Founder 1 (was 60%):** 48.0% (60% × 0.8)
*   **Founder 2 (was 20%):** 16.0% (20% × 0.8)
*   **Founder 3 (was 20%):** 16.0% (20% × 0.8)
*   **Total Founders:** 80.0%

*Debt Treatment:* The $50,000 supplier debt does not change these percentages. It reduces the company's net asset value and must be serviced from the new $1M cash, but it is not an equity instrument.

**What Flips It, and the Trigger**
The one input that changes your control is the **option pool shuffle**. If the investor requires a 10-15% employee option pool to be created *pre-money* (out of the founders' share rather than the investor's), your effective ownership drops further.
*Trigger:* If the term sheet states "Option pool to be established pre-money," your 80% collective ownership could drop to ~68-72% immediately.
*Rule of Thumb:* Fight to create the pool post-money or ensure the pre-money valuation is increased to compensate for the pool size.
*Step this week:* If the term sheet includes a pre-money option pool clause, consider negotiating a higher pre-money valuation or reducing the pool size to maintain your >50% collective control threshold.

**Routing Note**
While the dilution math above is fixed arithmetic, the **legal terms** governing this equity are binding determinations requiring counsel. Specifically, ask a securities lawyer: (1) Does the "liquidation preference" include a participation feature (double-dipping) that effectively lowers our economic ownership despite the 20% headline number? (2) Are there anti-dilution provisions (full-ratchet vs. weighted-average) that could drastically alter these percentages in a down round? (3) Does the $50k debt contain conversion rights or covenants that trigger upon this equity issuance?
