**The Call**
Accept the term sheet only if your growth targets require capital you cannot raise later at better terms; the deal implies **20% dilution**, which sits at the high end of typical Series A ranges (15–25%), leaving you with 80% of the post-money entity. While the math is clean, the reasonableness depends entirely on whether this cash extends your runway sufficiently to hit the milestones needed for a Series B at a significantly higher valuation.

**The Numbers**
Dilution is calculated as the investment amount divided by the post-money valuation.
*Formula:* `Dilution % = Investment / (Pre-money Valuation + Investment)`
*Your Figures:* €4M / (€16M + €4M) = €4M / €20M = **20%**.

*Ownership Impact (Assuming you currently own 100% for illustration; adjust for your actual cap table):*
*   **Pre-round:** 100% ownership.
*   **Post-round:** 80% ownership.
*   **Given Up:** 20% of the company.

Assuming "burn" means *net cash loss* of €120k):

**What Flips It, and the Trigger**
The single input that changes the call is your **ability to reduce churn below 3.5%** while scaling. At 3.5% monthly churn, you lose ~147 students/month; aggressive acquisition must exceed this significantly to justify the increased burn.
*   **Trigger:** If your Customer Acquisition Cost (CAC) in new markets exceeds €250 (approx. 3 months of revenue per user) within the first 6 months post-close, **pause the aggressive spend** and pivot to retention.
*   **Rule of Thumb:** Series A dilution above 25% is often a signal of distress or weak negotiating power; below 15% is exceptional. 20% is standard but demands strong execution.
*   **This Week's Step:** If your projected CAC for SE Asia/LatAm is unknown, **run a small-scale test campaign (€5k budget)** immediately to validate unit economics before committing the full €4M burn rate.

**Routing Note**
While the dilution arithmetic is straightforward (Tier 1), the **term sheet itself is a securities offering**. Before signing, you must engage qualified counsel to review liquidation preferences, anti-dilution provisions, board composition, and voting rights, as these terms can materially alter the economic value of your remaining 80% stake beyond the simple percentage count.
