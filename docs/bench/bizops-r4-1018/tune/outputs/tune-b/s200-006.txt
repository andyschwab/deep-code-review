**The Call**
A $200M TAM does not make a $10M post-money valuation impossible, but it caps your narrative; you must justify the round by proving you can capture a dominant share (20%+) of this niche quickly, rather than relying on massive market expansion. Reject the premise that small TAM automatically demands excessive dilution; instead, frame the raise as buying dominance in a profitable, defensible segment where unit economics outperform mass-market competitors.

**The Numbers**
*Equity Dilution Calculation:*
Formula: `Equity % = Raise Amount / Post-Money Valuation`
Your Figures: $1.5M Raise / $10M Post-Money = **15% Equity**.

*Sensitivity on Valuation (if investors push back on TAM):*

- **Downside Case (TAM Discount):** If investors value you at $7.5M Post due to niche constraints → $1.5M / $7.5M = **20%** dilution.
- **Upside Case (Premium Justification):** If brand strength commands $12.5M Post → $1.5M / $12.5M = **12%** dilution.

**What Flips It, and the Trigger**
The single input that changes the call is **Market Share Velocity**, not total TAM size. Investors accept small TAMs if the path to 20–30% share is clear and capital-efficient.
- **Trigger:** If you cannot demonstrate a path to $5M–$8M ARR (approx. 2.5%–4% of your stated TAM) within 18 months of the raise, the $10M valuation becomes difficult to defend, pushing dilution toward 20–25%.
- **Rule of Thumb:** In niche hardware, own the category or fail. If your current run rate is below $1M ARR, consider delaying the raise or lowering the valuation ask to secure terms.
- **Step this week:** If your current repeat purchase rate is below 15%, consider launching a subscription or accessory bundle immediately to lift LTV before entering term sheet negotiations.

**Metrics to Highlight Against Small TAM**
To counteract the $200M cap, highlight these specific metrics during negotiations:
1.  **Category Dominance Rate:** Your current % share of the *premium* segment (not total yoga mats). If you hold >10% of the high-end niche, you are the category king.
2.  **Net Revenue Retention (NRR):** In hardware, this is often <100%. If yours is >100% (via accessories/refills), it proves you can grow without new customer acquisition, mitigating TAM limits.
3.  **Capital Efficiency:** Ratio of revenue growth to cash burned. Show that your $1.5M raise gets you to profitability faster than a mass-market competitor burning cash on cheap customer acquisition.
