**The Call**
Do not launch with a "25-year shelf life" claim based solely on internal formulation data; the regulatory risk and potential retailer rejection outweigh the cash savings. You must either delay launch to conduct accelerated aging tests (accepting the $40k burn) or immediately revise your packaging to remove the specific year claim in favor of a "best by" date derived from shorter-term data.

**The Numbers**
*   **Current Runway:** $200,000 cash / $40,000 extra burn = 5 months of runway lost if you delay.
*   **Delay Cost:** $40,000 (explicitly stated).
*   **Unit Margin:** $14 (price) - $5 (COGS) = $9 contribution per unit.
*   **Break-even on Delay:** To recover the $40,000 delay cost, you must sell 4,445 units ($40,000 / $9) post-launch.
*   **Assumption (ASSUMED):** Your monthly burn rate excluding this test is roughly $30k–$40k given a 5-person team in Boulder; a 6-week delay consumes ~1.5 months of operating capital plus the test cost.

**What Flips It, and the Trigger**
The single input that changes this call is **retailer requirement**. If you are selling Direct-to-Consumer (DTC) only, you *might* survive on a conservative "best by" date without third-party verification initially. However, if you intend to sell to retailers (REI, Whole Foods, etc.), they will almost certainly require validated stability data for a 25-year claim before listing.
*   **Trigger Rule:** If your go-to-market strategy includes *any* wholesale partners, you cannot launch without the data.
*   **Pivot Step:** If you cannot afford the 6-week delay, strip the "25-year" claim from the initial batch. Print "Best By: [Date 2 years out]" based on standard industry assumptions for freeze-dried goods, and run the accelerated aging tests in parallel to validate the long-term claim for Batch 2.

**Routing Note**
Food labeling claims fall under FDA jurisdiction (21 CFR) and state consumer protection laws. Making a specific "25-year" claim without substantiation risks being deemed "misbranded." You must consult a food regulatory attorney or a food safety consultant to confirm the specific substantiation standards for "shelf life" vs. "best by" dates in Colorado and for your target retail channels.

Making a specific longevity claim without validated data exposes you to false advertising lawsuits and retailer chargebacks that exceed the $40,000 testing cost.
