**The Numbers**

*   **Deductible Impact:** You pay the first €50,000 of any claim. If a claim is <€50k, insurance pays nothing; if >€50k, insurance covers up to €1M.
*   **Scenario Analysis (Data Breach Claim):**

    *   *Base (Significant loss):* €600k claim → You pay €50k (deductible); Insurance pays €550k. **Current cap (€24k) fails here**, exposing you to €526k in personal/corporate liability beyond the cap if the clause is challenged or if the cap doesn't align with insurance triggers.
    *   *High (Catastrophic/Gross Negligence):* €2M claim → You pay €50k; Insurance pays €1M; **Uncovered €950k**. If gross negligence is proven, the liability cap may be voided entirely under German law, exposing all assets.

**What Flips It, and the Trigger**
The single input that changes your risk profile is the **legal characterization of the fault**.
*   **Trigger:** If a court or arbitrator determines "gross negligence" (*grobe Fahrlässigkeit*) or intent, German courts (§ 309 No. 7 BGB) typically void clauses excluding or capping liability for personal injury and often for property/data damage in standard business terms (AGB).
*   **Rule of Thumb:** Excluding consequential damages is standard and generally enforceable for ordinary negligence in B2B SaaS in the EU. However, attempting to cap liability for gross negligence is frequently overturned.
*   **Actionable Step:** If your prospect demands unlimited liability, counter with: "We align our liability cap with our professional indemnity insurance limit of €1M for data breaches, while excluding consequential damages for ordinary negligence."

**Routing Note**
German law on standard business terms (*Allgemeine Geschäftsbedingungen*) is highly specific regarding what can be excluded. You must ask German counsel: "Does our current AGB draft survive a *grobe Fahrlässigkeit* challenge for data loss, and is a tiered liability cap (higher for data breaches, lower for service downtime) enforceable under § 307 BGB?"
