**The Call**
It is not impossible to use FIFO for unique, sporadically acquired items; in fact, **specific identification** is likely the superior and more defensible method for your business model, though FIFO remains acceptable if your record-keeping tracks acquisition dates rigorously. The investor's concern is valid only if your current records are disorganized, not because the inventory is unique.

**The Numbers**
Your current unit economics show a healthy margin, but the accounting method affects how that profit is reported on paper.
*   **Formula:** Gross Profit = Total Revenue − Cost of Goods Sold (COGS).
*   **User Figures:**
    *   Units Sold: 320
    *   Average Sale Price: $450
    *   Total Revenue: $144,000 (320 × $450)
    *   Current Cash: $15,000
*   **COGS Calculation (Illustrative):**
    *   *Scenario A (FIFO):* Assumes the first 320 items bought (likely the cheaper estate sale finds early in the quarter) were sold first. If early costs averaged $150, COGS = $48,000. Gross Profit = $96,000.
    *   *Scenario B (Specific Identification):* Matches the exact cost of the 320 specific pieces sold. If you sold higher-cost items ($400 avg cost) first, COGS = $128,000. Gross Profit = $16,000.
    *   *ASSUMED:* The average cost of goods sold varies significantly based on which specific units left the floor. Without a log linking each sold item to its purchase receipt, these figures are estimates.

**What Flips It, and the Trigger**
The deciding factor is not the accounting method itself, but the **granularity of your source documentation**.
*   **The Trigger:** If you cannot link a sold item's ID (e.g., a tag number) to a specific purchase receipt with a date and cost within 24 hours, an auditor will flag your COGS as unreliable regardless of whether you claim FIFO or Specific Identification.
*   **Rule of Thumb:** Unique items demand unique tracking. FIFO works on batches; Specific Identification works on serial numbers or tags.
*   **Actionable Step:** If you currently track inventory only by total count or broad categories, consider implementing a simple tagging system (e.g., "Estate-A-001") immediately that links the physical item to its digital receipt file.

**Routing Note**
While Specific Identification is often preferred for unique goods (like art or custom furniture) under US GAAP, the final determination of which method best suits your tax position and audit readiness in Oregon is a binding accounting decision. Consult a CPA to formalize your inventory policy election and ensure your tracking system meets IRS substantiation requirements before the next audit cycle.
