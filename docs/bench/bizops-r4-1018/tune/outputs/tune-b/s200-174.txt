**The Call:** Do not form three separate LLCs; the maintenance costs and operational complexity will outweigh the marginal liability reduction for your current scale. Instead, operate under a single holding LLC with strong, specific disclaimers and professional liability insurance, as separate entities rarely shield against claims of professional negligence or outdated content if the same team creates the material for all three.

**The Numbers:**
*   **Current State:** 1 Entity vs. 3 Entities.
*   **Estimated Annual Cost per LLC:** $500 (state fees) + $1,200 (registered agent/tax prep) = ~$1,700/base.
*   **Total Additional Cost for 2 Extra LLCs:** ~$3,400/year minimum, excluding doubled legal/accounting retainer fees (estimated +$4,000/year).
*   **Total Incremental Cost:** ~$7,400/year.
*   **Revenue Impact:** $0 direct increase; potential decrease due to administrative distraction.
*   **Liability Coverage Gap:** A single LLC with $2M errors & omissions (E&O) insurance typically costs $3k–$5k/year. Three LLCs may require three policies or a complex master policy, potentially doubling insurance costs to ~$8k–$10k.
*   **Net Financial Drag:** Creating three entities could increase your annual fixed overhead by ~$12,000–$15,000 (legal + admin + insurance), representing ~3.5% of your $350k revenue, without guaranteeing asset protection if a court "pierces the veil" due to common management or content creation.

**What Flips It, and the Trigger:**
The decision flips if you expand into **high-risk clinical training** (hands-on patient care simulation) rather than exam prep, or if annual revenue exceeds **$2M**, making the cost of separate entities a smaller percentage of revenue while the risk profile increases.
*   **Trigger Value:** If you add hands-on clinical components or revenue hits $2M.
*   **Stop/Pivot Date:** Re-evaluate this structure in 18 months or upon launching non-digital services.
*   **Rule of Thumb:** Separate entities are justified when distinct business lines have uncorrelated risks; here, the risk (outdated info) is identical across all three states.
*   **This Week's Step:** If your current Terms of Service do not explicitly limit liability to the refund of the purchase price and exclude consequential damages (like failing the exam), consult a lawyer to draft these specific clauses immediately.

**Routing Note:**
The determination of whether a single LLC adequately shields assets from "professional negligence" claims in education is a jurisdiction-specific legal conclusion. You must consult a business attorney licensed in your primary state of operation to review your specific Terms of Service and insurance coverage; they will confirm if your content creation process creates a "unity of interest" that could pierce the corporate veil even with separate LLCs.
