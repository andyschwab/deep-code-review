**The Call**
Amortize the $84,000 upfront slotting fees over the 24-month contract term to match expenses with the revenue they generate; expense the $2,000 annual marketing fees immediately as incurred. This approach prevents distorting your unit economics and presents a clearer picture of operational profitability to investors.

**The Numbers**
*Formula:* Monthly Amortization = Total Upfront Fees / Contract Months.
*Your Figures:* $84,000 / 24 months = **$3,500/month** reported expense.
*Comparison:*
*   **Immediate Expensing (Accountant's suggestion):** Month 1 hit = $84,000. Reported profit shows a massive loss, obscuring the fact that you are selling 9,600 bottles monthly (12 stores × 800 bottles) with a $4 gross margin ($38,400 monthly gross profit).
*   **Amortization (Recommended):** Monthly hit = $3,500. Net contribution after slotting costs = $38,400 (gross profit) - $3,500 (amortization) = **$34,900/month**.
*   **Marketing Fees:** $2,000/store/year × 12 stores = $24,000/year. Monthly impact = **$2,000/month** (expensed as paid or accrued monthly).
*   **Total Monthly Burn Impact:** Under amortization, these fees add **$5,500/month** to your operating expenses ($3,500 + $2,000), rather than an $84,000 cliff in month one.

**What Flips It, and the Trigger**
The deciding factor is the **contract renewal term and refundability**. If the contract allows for a full refund of slotting fees upon early termination or lacks a defined benefit period (e.g., it renews automatically without new fees), the "24-month" assumption fails.
*   **Trigger:** If the contract language states fees are non-refundable and strictly tied to the initial 24-month placement, proceed with amortization. If fees are refundable or the term is indefinite, you may be forced to expense immediately or amortize over a different period.
*   **Rule of Thumb:** Match the expense to the period the shelf space is secured.
*   **Step this week:** If the contract lacks explicit non-refundable language for the 24-month period, consider having counsel add a clause defining the fee as non-refundable consideration for the specific term to solidify the amortization schedule.

**Routing Note**
While your internal bookkeeper can record the entries based on your policy election, the **materiality** of $84,000 relative to your total revenue likely requires **external auditor sign-off** if you plan to raise institutional capital or undergo a financial audit soon. Auditors often challenge capitalization policies for slotting fees if the contract terms regarding renewal or refundability are ambiguous. Bring the signed contracts and your proposed amortization schedule to your CPA for formal approval before closing the books.
