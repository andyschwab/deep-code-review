**The Numbers**
To clarify the relationship for acquirers, compute the following standard SaaS metrics using your provided figures:

1.  **Annual Recurring Revenue (ARR):** This normalizes your run-rate regardless of billing frequency.
    *   *Formula:* Recognized Revenue / (1 - Churn Rate adjustment if needed, but here simply annualized recognized revenue).
    *   *Your Figure:* Since you recognized $900k over 12 months with steady delivery, your current ARR is **$900,000**.
    *   *Assumption (ASSUMED):* We assume the $900k represents a steady state of delivered service over the year. If the $1.8M collections included significant one-time setup fees, the pure recurring ARR might be slightly lower, but for this model, we treat the service component as the core.

2.  **Deferred Revenue Liability:** This is the cash collected but not yet earned.
    *   *Formula:* Total Cash Collections - Recognized Revenue.
    *   *Calculation:* $1,800,000 - $900,000 = **$900,000**.
    *   *Interpretation:* You have $900k in "banked" revenue that will flow to the income statement over the next 12 months assuming zero new sales.

3.  **Net Revenue Retention (NRR):** This proves the quality of revenue despite the accounting lag.
    *   *Formula:* (Starting ARR + Expansion - Churn) / Starting ARR.
    *   *Your Figure:* With a 5% annual churn rate and no expansion mentioned, your NRR is **95%**.
    *   *Context:* Acquirers value a 95% NRR highly; it confirms the "modest" income statement is a timing artifact, not a retention failure.
