**The Call**
Classifying the nutrient pods as a "subscription" versus a "recurring order" generally **does not** change your sales tax collection obligation, rate, or filing frequency in California, Texas, New York, Florida, or Illinois. The taxability is driven by the physical nature of the goods (tangible personal property), not the billing cadence or the digital management of the account. Your advisors are likely conflating sales tax on goods with income tax nexus or digital-product taxes; for physical plant nutrients, the "subscription" label is a billing preference, not a tax classification that alters your liability in these jurisdictions.

**The Numbers & Rules**
Since you provided no sales volume figures, I cannot compute your specific nexus status. However, the rule for all five states is that **Tangible Personal Property (TPP)** is taxable regardless of whether it is sold one-off or via subscription.
*   **Tax Rate:** Determined by the *ship-to* address (destination-based sourcing) in all five states. A subscription does not trigger a different rate than a single purchase.
*   **Filing Frequency:** Determined by your total taxable sales volume in the state once registered, not by the fact that you have recurring charges.
*   **Nexus Thresholds (Remote Seller Registration):** You must register if you exceed these economic nexus thresholds (based on gross sales or transaction count in the prior 12 months). The "subscription" model does not lower these thresholds.

| State | Economic Nexus Threshold (Remote Seller) | Does "Subscription" Change Taxability? |
| :--- | :--- | :--- |
| **California** | $500,000 in gross receipts | No. TPP is taxable; billing method is irrelevant. |
| **Texas** | $500,000 in gross receipts | No. TPP is taxable; recurring orders are treated as continuous sales of TPP. |
| **New York** | $500,000 in gross receipts AND 100 transactions | No. TPP is taxable. Digital management does not alter the physical good's status. |
| **Florida** | $100,000 in taxable sales | No. TPP is taxable. |
| **Illinois** | $100,000 in gross receipts OR 200 transactions | No. TPP is taxable. |

**What Flips It, and the Trigger**
The one input that changes this analysis is the **nature of the item sold**. If you were selling *only* digital software to manage the garden, the "subscription" label might trigger different rules (some states tax SaaS, some don't). However, because you are selling physical nutrient pods, the trigger for registration is strictly hitting the dollar/transaction thresholds listed above.
*   **Trigger:** If your cumulative sales into any of these states exceed the listed threshold in the previous 12 months, you must register immediately.
*   **Step:** If your current annualized run-rate into Texas is >$400k, consider pausing marketing there until you confirm your exact trailing 12-month total, as crossing the $500k line creates an immediate filing obligation.

**Routing Note**
While the general rule for physical goods is consistent, the specific application of "bundling" (hardware + pods) at the point of sale can vary if shipped together versus separately. Furthermore, determining your exact "gross receipts" for nexus thresholds requires accounting for returns and exemptions. Bring your trailing 12-month sales ledger by state to a CPA to confirm the exact date your nexus triggered and to handle the registration filings.
