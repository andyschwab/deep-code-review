**The Call**
Do not book the $50,000 immediately; you must recognize it ratably over the 12-month service period. Booking it all upfront would materially misstate your financial performance, inflate your Monthly Recurring Revenue (MRR), and create a mismatch between reported revenue and actual service delivery.

**The Numbers**
The governing principle is the **Revenue Recognition Principle** (ASC 606 in the US, IFRS 15 internationally), which mandates that revenue is recognized when (or as) performance obligations are satisfied, not when cash is received.

*   **Formula:** Monthly Revenue Recognition = Total Contract Value / Contract Duration (Months)
*   **Your Figures:** $50,000 / 12 months
*   **Result:** **$4,166.67 per month**

*Journal Entry Logic:*
1.  **Upon Cash Receipt (January):**
    *   Debit Cash: $50,000
    *   Credit **Deferred Revenue** (Liability): $50,000
    *   *Note: This sits on the Balance Sheet, not the Income Statement.*
2.  **Monthly Adjustment (End of Jan, Feb, etc.):**
    *   Debit Deferred Revenue: $4,166.67
    *   Credit **Revenue**: $4,166.67
    *   *Note: Only this portion hits the Income Statement.*

**What Flips It, and The Trigger**
The only input that changes this treatment is the **service delivery model**. If your contract stipulated that the entire value was delivered instantly (e.g., a one-time software download with no ongoing support), immediate recognition might apply. However, since you deliver workshops and coaching continuously, the "over time" criterion is met.
*   **Trigger:** If you book the full $50,000 as January revenue, your MRR metric becomes unreliable for investors, and you risk an audit adjustment for overstating income.
*   **Rule of Thumb:** Cash flow $\neq$ Revenue. If the service spans multiple periods, the revenue must span multiple periods.
*   **This Week's Step:** If your current ledger shows the full $50,000 as January revenue, consider reclassifying the unrecognized portion ($45,833.33) to a "Deferred Revenue" liability account immediately before the audit.

**Routing Note**
While the mechanical application of ratable recognition is standard GAAP/IFRS practice, the specific tax treatment of deferred revenue (whether your jurisdiction allows you to defer taxable income or requires taxation upon receipt) varies by location and entity type. Bring the question "Does our local tax code conform to GAAP revenue recognition for multi-year service contracts, or is cash-basis taxation required?" to your CPA to finalize your tax filings.
