**What Flips It, and the Trigger:**
The single input that breaks this model is the **Watt-hour (Wh) rating** of your battery.
*   **Trigger:** If your battery exceeds **100 Wh**, you move from "Section II" (lighter regulation) to fully regulated Class 9 Dangerous Goods, requiring specialized cargo aircraft, drastically higher rates, and potentially banning passenger-aircraft transport (which limits flight frequency to Japan/EU).
*   **Action:** Check your battery spec sheet today. If >100Wh, you must pivot to sea freight for bulk or renegotiate battery chemistry before shipping.

**Routing Note:**
While the general rule (UN Manual of Tests and Criteria, Section II) allows smaller lithium batteries on passenger aircraft with specific labeling (Class 9 hazard label, "Cargo Aircraft Only" if >100Wh or specific packing instructions), the **binding determination** of your battery's exact UN number, the validity of your packaging certification for EU/Japan customs, and the final classification rests with a licensed **Dangerous Goods Safety Advisor (DGSA)** or a certified freight forwarder. Do not self-certify; a misdeclaration can result in seized goods and heavy fines in the EU.
