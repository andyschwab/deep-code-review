**The Call**
Do not restructure the initial fee as a "marketing fund contribution" to alter tax exposure without a binding opinion from a Texas tax attorney; the Texas Franchise Tax is generally based on total revenue, and recharacterizing fees carries a high risk of being disregarded by the state while triggering securities or franchise-registration complications. Proceed with the standard $45,000 initial fee and 6% royalty structure for your financial modeling, but treat any alternative structuring as a tier-3 legal determination, not an internal accounting decision.

**The Numbers**
The Texas Franchise Tax is computed on "total revenue," which broadly includes all revenue reported on your federal tax return, with limited specific deductions.
*   **Formula:** Taxable Margin = Initial Fees + Ongoing Royalties + Other Revenue – (Allowed Deductions, if any).
*   **Your Figures (Base Case):**
    *   Initial Fee: $45,000 (one-time)
    *   Annual Royalty: $320,000 (projected volume) × 6% = $19,200
    *   **Total Revenue per Unit (Year 1):** $45,000 + $19,200 = **$64,200**
*   **Hypothetical Restructure (Illustrative Only):**
    *   If you split the $45,000 into $20,000 Fee + $25,000 "Marketing Fund":
    *   **Risk:** Unless the $25,000 meets strict statutory exclusions (e.g., held in a separate trust, spent solely on behalf of franchisees with no benefit to the franchisor), the Texas Comptroller will likely include the full $45,000 in your taxable revenue regardless of the label.
    *   **Result:** $0 reduction in taxable base if recharacterization fails; potential penalties if deemed an aggressive avoidance scheme.

**What Flips It, and the Trigger**
The single input that changes the outcome is the **legal substance of the "marketing fund" arrangement**, not the label. If the funds are commingled with operating accounts or provide any indirect benefit to the franchisor, the "flip" occurs: the state includes 100% of the funds in your revenue base.
*   **Trigger:** The moment you draft language suggesting the marketing fund is discretionary, refundable, or beneficial to the franchisor's overhead, you cross from general planning into mandatory legal review.
*   **Rule of Thumb:** If the money touches your general operating account or pays for your corporate-level marketing, it is taxable revenue.
*   **This Week's Step:** If you intend to pursue a separate marketing fund, consider drafting a "separate entity" flowchart and pause any LOI finalization until counsel confirms the fund's exclusion status under Texas Tax Code §171.101.
