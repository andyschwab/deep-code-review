**The Call:** Structure your promotion as a post-sale manufacturer rebate; in California and most key markets, you must collect and remit sales tax on the full $110 selling price, not the net $85, because the rebate is issued after the transaction closes. Do not attempt to reduce your sales tax base; the cash flow cost of remitting tax on the unreduced amount is the compliance price for this promotion type.

**What Flips It, and the Trigger:**
The single input that changes the tax treatment is the **timing of the price reduction**. If you change the promotion to an "instant discount" (customer pays $85 at the register), the sales tax base drops to $85 immediately.
*   **Trigger:** If your cash flow cannot absorb the ~$5,400 extra tax remittance required by the rebate model, switch to an instant discount before the first sale.
*   **Rule of Thumb:** Rebates are marketing expenses; instant discounts are price reductions. Tax authorities generally tax the price paid at the moment of sale.
*   **This Week's Step:** If you proceed with the rebate, ensure your POS system prints the full $110 receipt and segregate the $25 rebate checks/mailings as a separate liability account, not a reduction of sales revenue, to satisfy audit trails.
