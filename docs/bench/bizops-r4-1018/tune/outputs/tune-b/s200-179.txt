**The Call:** Change payment terms to collect 100% upfront immediately upon enrollment; do not raise the base price or add a rush fee yet. This captures the cash needed to cover holding costs without triggering price sensitivity or perceived unfairness, preserving your 20% margin structure while fixing the liquidity squeeze.

**The Numbers:**
Your current unit economics per student:
*   **Revenue:** $4,500
*   **Margin (20%):** $900
*   **Cost of Goods Sold (COGS):** $3,600

The operational drag creates a monthly fixed cost increase of $1,500. To break even on this new cost solely through volume at current margins, you need ~1.7 extra students/month, which is inefficient. The core issue is the timing of cash inflow versus the extended 9-week visa wait.

*Scenario A: Increase Base Price to $4,800 (+$300)*
*   New Margin: $1,200 (25% margin).
*   **Risk:** High. A 6.7% price hike in a competitive travel market may reduce conversion rates. If volume drops by >15%, total profit falls.
*   **Result:** Covers admin costs only if volume remains stable.

*Scenario B: Add $300 "Rush/Processing" Fee (Optional)*
*   **Risk:** Medium-High. Customers perceive this as a penalty for external government delays. Low uptake expected if not truly "rushable."
*   **Result:** Unreliable revenue stream; does not solve the cash flow timing gap for the 80% who don't pay it.

*Scenario C: Shift to 100% Upfront Payment (Recommended)*
*   **Mechanism:** Require full $4,500 at booking (previously likely a deposit).
*   **Cash Impact:** Immediate access to the full $3,600 COGS portion + $900 margin.
*   **Financial Benefit:** Eliminates the need to fund the 9-week hold from operating reserves. The interest/opportunity cost of holding that cash for 6 extra weeks is effectively recovered.
*   **Assumption:** If you previously collected 50% ($2,250) upfront, you now gain $2,250/student in immediate liquidity. At 10 students/month, this frees up $22,500 in working capital, easily absorbing the $1,500 admin hike and buffering against further delays.

**What Flips It, and The Trigger:**
The single input that changes this call is **conversion rate elasticity**. If requiring 100% upfront causes your booking conversion to drop below **85% of current levels**, the lost volume outweighs the cash flow benefit.
*   **Trigger Rule:** If bookings drop >15% in the first 30 days of the new policy, revert to a 75% upfront / 25% pre-departure split.
*   **Action Step:** If your deposit-to-booking conversion holds steady after implementing full upfront payment next week, consider adding a small ($150) "early-bird" discount for payments made >6 months out to further smooth cash flow, rather than raising prices.

**Routing Note:**
Review your terms of service regarding refund policies if visas are denied after full payment collection; this shifts liability and requires clear legal wording to avoid consumer protection disputes in the EU/US.
