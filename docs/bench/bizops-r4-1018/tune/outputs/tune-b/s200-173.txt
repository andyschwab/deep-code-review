**The Call**
Your recurring revenue structure justifies targeting a 6x–8x revenue multiple, significantly higher than the 1x–2x typical of traditional toy hardware companies, provided you can prove churn is low and unit economics are healthy. Do not lead with the 4% net margin; lead with the Lifetime Value (LTV) to Customer Acquisition Cost (CAC) ratio and the stability of the £144k Monthly Recurring Revenue (MRR).

**The Numbers**
*Current Snapshot:*
- **MRR:** £144,000 (3,200 users × £45).
- **Annual Recurring Revenue (ARR):** £1.728M (£144k × 12).
- **CAC:** £60.
- **Gross Margin Assumption:** Hardware subscriptions often carry 40–50% gross margins after COGS and shipping. We will assume **45%** for this model (ASSUMED).
- **Churn Assumption:** To sustain 8% MoM growth with current acquisition, net churn must be low. We assume **5% monthly churn** (ASSUMED) based on typical hobbyist box retention.

*Valuation Multiples:*
- **Traditional Toy Hardware:** Typically sells for **1x–2x** revenue due to episodic purchases and inventory risk.
- **Subscription Box (Proven Retention):** Typically sells for **4x–8x** ARR.
- **Your Valuation Range (at 6x–8x ARR):**
  - 6x Multiple: £1.728M × 6 = **£10.37M**
  - 8x Multiple: £1.728M × 8 = **£13.82M**
  - *Comparison:* At a traditional 1.5x multiple, you would value at only £2.59M. The recurring model theoretically adds **£7.8M–£11.2M** in enterprise value.

*Unit Economics Check:*
- **LTV Formula:** (ARPA × Gross Margin) / Monthly Churn
- **Calculation:** (£45 × 0.45) / 0.05 = £20.25 / 0.05 = **£405 LTV**.
- **LTV:CAC Ratio:** £405 / £60 = **6.75x**.
- *Benchmark:* A ratio above 3.0x is considered excellent for acquisition. Your 6.75x (based on assumed churn/margin) signals high efficiency.

**What Flips It, and the Trigger**
The single input that collapses your multiple from 8x to 3x is **churn**. If your monthly churn exceeds **8%**, your LTV drops to ~£113, reducing your LTV:CAC to <2.0x, which scares acquirers fearing a "leaky bucket."
- **Trigger:** If your actual cohort retention shows >10% of users cancelling within the first 3 months, pause valuation discussions and fix onboarding before selling.
- **Rule of Thumb:** Acquirers pay for *predictability*. If your revenue is growing 8% MoM but driven entirely by new ads rather than retention, the multiple compresses.
- **This Week's Step:** If your 3-month retention rate is >75%, consider building a "cohort longevity" slide for your data room highlighting that specific metric over your net margin.

**Routing Note**
While the math supports a higher multiple, the final valuation depends on the legal structure of the subscription contracts (auto-renewal terms, cancellation rights) and GDPR compliance for your EU user base. Bring your customer terms of service and data processing agreements to M&A counsel to ensure no "change of control" clauses void the recurring revenue stream upon sale.
