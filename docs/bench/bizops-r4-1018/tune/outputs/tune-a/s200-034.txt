**The Call**
Do not launch the $50/month for 14 months program until counsel confirms the structure avoids "truth in lending" classification; your current draft likely triggers secured loan regulations in California because the total payment ($700) exceeds the cash price ($600) and includes an automatic or nominal transfer of title, creating a de facto finance charge.

**The Numbers**
*   **Cash Price:** $600
*   **Lease-to-Own Total:** $50 × 14 months = $700
*   **Implied Finance Charge:** $700 - $600 = $100
*   **Implied APR (Approximate):** ~24% (varies by exact fee treatment)
*   **Inventory Value:** 1,000 units × $600 = $600,000 (asset base)
*   **Burn Rate:** $40,000/month
*   **Runway Impact:** If legal reclassification forces a pause or refund reserve, runway decreases immediately.

**What Flips It, and the Trigger**
The single input that changes the call is the **end-of-term purchase option price**.
*   **Trigger:** If the customer must pay a nominal fee (e.g., $1) or nothing to own the chair after 14 months, regulators and courts typically view the transaction as a secured loan, not a true lease.
*   **Rule of Thumb:** In many jurisdictions, if the lessee has no meaningful economic choice but to purchase (because the option price is trivial compared to the item's value), the "lease" is recharacterized as a loan.
*   **Action:** If your contract sets the final purchase price below fair market value (e.g., <$50 for a used chair), consider restructuring to a genuine rental with a fair-market-value buyout or partnering with a licensed third-party lender.

**Routing Note**
This is a Tier 3 (Route) issue. You must engage a consumer finance attorney licensed in California immediately.
*   **Exact Question for Counsel:** "Does our 14-month payment plan with a nominal buyout option constitute a 'consumer credit contract' under California Civil Code § 1802.12 or trigger Truth in Lending Act (TILA) disclosure requirements due to the creation of a finance charge?"
*   **Time-Sensitive Element:** Do not sign any customer agreements or market the program until you have a written opinion on whether you need a lender's license or must register as a financing entity, as unlicensed lending can result in voided contracts and statutory penalties.
