**The Call:** Do not proceed with hiring internal legal staff or marketing a bundled "legal and mental health" package until a California-licensed attorney provides a written opinion on your specific triage workflow; the risk of unauthorized practice of law (UPL) by non-lawyer care coordinators is high enough to threaten your entire business model, outweighing the projected $40/user margin gain.

**The Numbers:**
*   **Current Model:** 12,000 employees × $250 = $3,000,000 annual revenue.
*   **Proposed Model:** (12,000 + 1,800 new) employees × $220 = $3,036,000 projected revenue.
*   **Margin Impact:** You anticipate saving $40 per user on legal costs.
    *   *Base Case:* 13,800 users × $40 = $552,000 annual cost savings.
    *   *Risk Adjustment:* If UPL findings trigger fines, injunctions, or client churn, the loss of even 5% of clients (600 employees) negates ~$132,000 in revenue, plus legal defense costs which often exceed $50,000 for UPL investigations.
*   **ASSUMED:** The $40 saving represents the full cost of the outsourced partner; internal hiring costs (salary, benefits, malpractice insurance for employed counsel) are not deducted here but must be verified against the $40 figure.

**What Flips It, and The Trigger:**
The single input that changes this from "high risk" to "manageable" is the **definition of "triage"** performed by your care coordinators.
*   **Trigger Value:** If "triage" involves *any* assessment of the legal merit of a claim, recommendation of a specific legal strategy, or interpretation of statutes for a specific fact pattern, you are likely engaging in UPL.
*   **Stop/Pivot Rule:** If your care coordinators cannot be restricted to purely administrative routing (e.g., "Here is a list of three licensed attorneys; you choose one") without discussing case details, you must pivot to keeping legal entirely separate or outsourced.
*   **This Week's Step:** If your current triage script asks clients "What happened?" regarding their legal issue, consider halting script usage immediately and drafting a "purely administrative referral" protocol for legal review.

**Regulatory Red Flags & Required Routing:**
In California, the unauthorized practice of law is strictly enforced. Bundling creates two primary red flags:
1.  **Implied Representation:** Marketing a single "legal and mental health" brand may lead consumers to believe the non-lawyer coordinators are agents of the legal service, creating an implied attorney-client relationship that the firm cannot legally sustain without lawyers overseeing every touchpoint.
2.  **Fee Splitting/Corporate Practice:** California rules generally prohibit non-lawyers from sharing legal fees or controlling the professional judgment of lawyers. A bundled invoice where the legal portion is not clearly distinct can be construed as improper fee sharing or the corporation practicing law through non-lawyer employees.

**Activities Care Coordinators Must Avoid (Tier 2 Explanation):**
To remain compliant, non-lawyer staff must strictly avoid:
*   Assessing the validity or strength of a legal claim.
*   Recommending a specific course of legal action (e.g., "You should sue for wrongful termination").
*   Interpreting laws or contracts for the client.
*   Drafting or reviewing legal documents, even informally.
*   Discussing the specifics of a case with the intent to provide legal guidance.
*   *Permitted:* Collecting contact info, explaining the *process* of how to access the lawyer (not the law), and scheduling appointments.

**Routing Note (Tier 3 - Mandatory):**
You absolutely require an opinion from a California-licensed attorney specializing in professional responsibility before hiring internal staff. Specifically, ask counsel: (1) Does our proposed bundled invoice structure violate California Rules of Professional Conduct regarding fee sharing with non-lawyers? (2) Can our non-lawyer care coordinators legally perform the proposed "triage" without triggering UPL statutes, and if so, what exact script limitations must be codified? (3) Does employing lawyers in-house change the entity's liability exposure compared to the current outsourced model?
