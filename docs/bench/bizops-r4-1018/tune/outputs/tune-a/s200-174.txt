**The Call:** Do not form three separate LLCs; the administrative cost and complexity will likely outweigh the marginal liability protection gained for content-related risks. Instead, maintain a single holding LLC with robust insurance and strengthened contractual disclaimers, as forming separate entities rarely shields a parent company from claims of negligence or defective content creation when the same team produces the materials.

**The Numbers:**
*   **Current Revenue:** $350,000/year.
*   **Estimated Cost of 3 Additional LLCs:** ~$1,500–$3,000/year in state filing fees and registered agent costs (ASSUMED: $500–$1,000 per state annually for maintenance/franchise taxes).
*   **Legal/Accounting Overhead:** ~$2,000–$4,000/year for separate tax filings and compliance (ASSUMED: $750 per entity for basic compliance).
*   **Total Additional Annual Cost:** ~$3,500–$7,000 (1%–2% of current revenue).
*   **Liability Coverage Gap:** If a single content error affects users in all three states, a plaintiff's attorney will likely "pierce the veil" between entities by arguing common control and identical content creation processes, rendering the separation ineffective for this specific risk.

**What Flips It, and the Trigger:**
The decision flips only if you expand into high-risk clinical training (hands-on patient care) or if annual revenue exceeds $2M, making the cost of separation negligible relative to asset exposure.
*   **Trigger:** If you begin offering *clinical placement services* or *guaranteed pass refunds* exceeding $50k in potential liability per state, reconsider entity separation.
*   **Rule of Thumb:** Separate entities make sense for distinct operational risks (e.g., a delivery fleet vs. a software platform), not for geographic variations of the same digital product.
*   **Step This Week:** If your current Terms of Service do not explicitly limit liability to the refund amount of the course and exclude consequential damages (like lost wages from failing an exam), consult a lawyer to draft these specific clauses before spending money on new entities.
