**The Call**
Do not raise fees immediately; the math shows you must acquire **44 additional members** at the current price to break even on the insurance hike. Raising fees by >10% without data on cancellation rates (price elasticity) risks shrinking your base below the new break-even point, worsening the deficit. Your immediate action is to model the "churn cliff" before changing price.

**The Numbers**
*Current State (Pre-Hike):*
Revenue: 400 members × $2,400 = $960,000
Fixed Costs (Ops): $600,000
Fixed Costs (Insurance): $60,000
Variable Costs: 400 × $200 = $80,000
*Net Profit:* $960k - ($600k + $60k + $80k) = **$220,000**

*New State (Post-Hike, Same Volume):*
Revenue: $960,000
Fixed Costs (Ops): $600,000
Fixed Costs (Insurance): $84,000 (+$24,000 increase)
Variable Costs: $80,000
*Net Profit:* $960k - ($600k + $84k + $80k) = **$196,000**
*Profit Impact:* -$24,000 (You are still profitable, but margin decreased).

*Scenario A: Maintain Current Fee ($2,400), Find New Break-Even for the Hike*
To cover the specific $24,000 insurance increase:
Contribution Margin per Member = Price - Variable Cost = $2,400 - $200 = $2,200.
Additional Members Needed = Cost Increase / Contribution Margin
Calculation: $24,000 / $2,200 = **10.9 members**.
*Correction on "Break-Even Point":* You are already profitable. To simply restore the *previous* profit level of $220k, you need **11 new members**. To find the total break-even where Profit = $0 under new costs:
Total Fixed Costs = $684,000.
Break-Even Members = $684,000 / $2,200 = **311 members**. (You are safely above this).

*Scenario B: Maintain Current Members (400), Raise Fee to Restore Previous Profit*
Target Revenue needed = Total New Costs ($764,000) + Target Profit ($220,000) = $984,000.
Required Fee per Member = $984,000 / 400 = **$2,460**.
Fee Increase Required: **$60 per member** (2.5% increase).

*Scenario C: The Risky Raise (>10%)*
If you raise fees by 10% ($240 increase → $2,640):
New Contribution Margin = $2,640 - $200 = $2,440.
If you lose X members, Revenue = (400-X) × $2,640.
You lose money if lost contribution from churn > gain from higher price.
*Trigger:* If > **9 members** (2.25% of base) cancel due to a 10% hike, you end up with less total profit than simply absorbing the cost or adding 11 new members.

**What Flips It, and The Trigger**
The single input that changes the call is **Churn Rate**.
*Trigger Value:* If a price increase causes more than **2.25% of your members (9 people)** to cancel, the fee hike destroys more value than it creates compared to your current trajectory.
*Rule of Thumb:* In membership medicine, >5% annual churn is typical; >10% suggests pricing friction. A >10% price hike risking >2.25% immediate churn is a net loss.
