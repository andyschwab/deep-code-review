**The Call**
Do not implement a blanket 20% compute reduction yet; the math requires your conversion rate to nearly double (to ~2.3%) just to maintain current revenue, which risks collapsing the viral loop that supplies your zero-CAC growth. Instead, model this as a threshold test: only apply friction if you can isolate a segment where conversion elasticity exceeds churn elasticity, or pivot to gating *team* features rather than individual compute limits.

**The Numbers**
We compare your current baseline against the proposed 20% friction scenario.

*Current Baseline:*
*   **Monthly Revenue:** 450 seats × $29 = **$13,050**.
*   **Net Burn:** $8,000 (costs) - $13,050 (revenue) = **-$5,050** (You are cash-flow positive).
*   **Runway:** $95,000 / -$5,050 = **Infinite** (until growth stalls).
*   **Viral Input:** 12,000 free users × 1.8 invites = 21,600 potential new reaches (gross).

*Proposed Scenario (20% Compute Cut):*
To offset a potential drop in new sign-ups (the viral engine), the revenue from increased conversion must equal or exceed current revenue.
*   **Formula:** $Revenue_{new} = (Users_{free} \times Rate_{conv\_new}) \times Price$
*   **Target Revenue:** Must remain $\ge$ $13,050 to maintain current surplus.
*   **Required Conversion Rate:** $13,050 / (12,000 \times \$29) = **3.75%**.
    *   *Correction:* This assumes free user count stays static. If the 20% cut reduces viral invites by even 10% (a conservative estimate for friction), your free base shrinks, pushing the required conversion rate higher.
    *   **Realistic Threshold (ASSUMED 10% viral drop):** If free users drop to 10,800, required conversion = $13,050 / (10,800 \times 29) = **4.16%**.
    *   **Gap:** You must move from 1.2% to ~4.2%. That is a **250% increase** in conversion efficiency.

**What Flips It, and The Trigger**
The single input that flips this decision is the **Elasticity of Conversion vs. Elasticity of Virality**.
*   **Trigger Value:** If a 20% feature cut yields less than a **3.0 percentage point** increase in conversion (moving you from 1.2% to >4.2%), the move destroys value.
*   **Rule of Thumb:** In freemium models, conversion elasticity rarely exceeds 2x without causing >30% viral decay. You are betting on a >3x conversion lift.
*   **Step this week:** Run an A/B test on 5% of traffic. Apply the 20% limit to Group A. Measure two metrics: (1) Conversion rate lift and (2) Invite rate decay. **If** conversion lift < 2.0x the invite decay, **consider** gating collaborative "team" features instead of individual compute, as this targets high-intent users without punishing the viral spreaders.
