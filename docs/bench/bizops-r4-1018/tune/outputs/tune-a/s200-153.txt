**The Call**
Do not raise the price to $14 yet; the math indicates this move destroys value under your current assumptions. With an 8% monthly churn, your customers stay only ~12.5 months on average. At $9/month, Lifetime Value (LTV) is ~$112, yielding a healthy 2.5x return on your $45 Acquisition Cost (CAC). Raising the price to $14 while simultaneously increasing churn (as higher prices often do) and slashing conversion by 30% creates a "double penalty" that likely shrinks total revenue and extends payback periods dangerously. Focus first on lowering the $45 CAC or reducing the 8% churn before attempting a price hike.

**The Numbers**
*Formula:* LTV = Price / Monthly Churn Rate.
*Current State:* $9 / 0.08 = **$112.50 LTV**.
*Unit Economics:* $112.50 LTV - $45 CAC = **$67.50 Contribution Margin** per user.
*LTV:CAC Ratio:* 2.5x (Healthy benchmark is >3x, but >2x is viable for early stage).

*Scenario: Price Hike to $14 (Illustrative Assumptions)*
You noted a 30% drop in *conversion*, but the critical missing variable is the *new churn rate*.
*Assumption A (Churn stays 8%):* $14 / 0.08 = $175 LTV. (Theoretically better, but unrealistic; price hikes usually spike churn).
*Assumption B (Churn rises to 12% - ASSUMED):* $14 / 0.12 = **$116.67 LTV**.
*Result:* You gain only ~$4 in LTV per user while losing 30% of new signups. Total net value drops significantly.
*Assumption C (Churn rises to 15% - ASSUMED):* $14 / 0.15 = **$93.33 LTV**.
*Result:* LTV falls below current levels, and the ratio against $45 CAC drops to ~2.0x, endangering runway.

**What Flips It, and The Trigger**
The single input that changes this call is **Post-Hike Churn Rate**.
*Trigger:* If you can prove via a small A/B test that churn remains below **9.5%** after the price increase to $14, the higher ARPU (Average Revenue Per User) may justify the volume loss.
*Rule of Thumb:* If LTV:CAC drops below 2.0x, stop the price increase immediately.
*Action This Week:* Run a "fake door" or limited cohort test offering the $14 plan to 5% of new traffic. Measure the *actual* conversion drop and the *actual* churn of those who buy. If conversion drops >20% or churn exceeds 9%, abandon the hike and focus on optimizing ad creatives to lower the $45 CAC.

**Data Points Needed**
To finalize this decision, you must gather:
1.  **Price Elasticity of Demand:** Specifically, how much does churn increase *per dollar* of price increase for your specific demographic?
2.  **Segmented Churn:** Is the 8% churn driven by low-income users who would leave at $14 anyway, or power users who will stay?
3.  **Payback Period:** How many months does it currently take to recover the $45 CAC? (Current: ~5 months). If the price hike extends this beyond 6-7 months due to volatility, it is too risky.
