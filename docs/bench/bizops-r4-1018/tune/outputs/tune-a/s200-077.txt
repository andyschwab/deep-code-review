**The Call**
Proceed with the 20% contingency model but decouple your fee calculation from the client's financial audit by contractually defining "Verified Savings" as an operational metric derived from a pre-agreed baseline, not an accounting adjustment. **The Numbers**
*Formula:* Monthly Revenue = (Client Baseline Spend − Actual Spend) × Fee %.
*User Figures:* Baseline = $100,000; Savings = $20,000; Fee % = 20%.
*Result:* $20,000 × 0.20 = **$4,000/month per enterprise client**.
*Path to Profitability:* You need 5 closed deals × $4,000 = **$20,000 Monthly Recurring Revenue (MRR)**.
*Assumption (ASSUMED):* This calculation assumes "Verified Savings" equals the simple arithmetic difference between a static baseline and actual spend. If the baseline adjusts dynamically or if "savings" excludes one-time credits, the $4,000 figure is **thin** and must be recalculated based on the contractual definition.

**What Flips It, and the Trigger**
The single input that changes the call is the **definition of the baseline**. If the contract allows CloudSaver to unilaterally adjust the baseline spend to inflate savings, auditors will likely flag this as a conflict of interest compromising data integrity.
*Trigger Rule:* If the contract does not mandate that the baseline be fixed for the audit period or validated by a neutral data source (e.g., raw exporter logs vs. billed amounts), consider pausing the deal signing.
*This Week's Step:* If your current draft defines savings solely based on your dashboard's internal calculations, consider adding a clause requiring client sign-off on the baseline data export before the performance period begins.
