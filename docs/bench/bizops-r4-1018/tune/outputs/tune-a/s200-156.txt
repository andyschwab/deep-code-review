**The Call**
Do not assume you are safe; your current practice of separately stating a charge for tangible personal property (workbooks) in states where you physically deliver them creates a high probability of a sales tax filing obligation, regardless of your total revenue. While bundling *might* shift the entire transaction to a taxable "service" or exempt "educational" classification depending on the state, unbundling explicitly isolates the workbook as a taxable good, triggering nexus rules based on physical presence (the event) rather than just economic thresholds. You should treat the workbook revenue in Florida, Nevada, and Washington as potentially taxable immediately and engage a tax professional to confirm the specific "true object" test in each jurisdiction before your next event.

**What Flips It, and the Trigger**
The single input that changes your liability is the **state-specific definition of the "true object" of the transaction.**
*   **Trigger:** If a state determines the customer's primary goal is the *education* (service), bundling might make the whole thing non-taxable. If they determine the primary goal is the *materials* (or if the state taxes the service regardless), you owe tax.
*   **Threshold Rule of Thumb:** In many states, physical presence for even one day creates nexus. Do not rely on the $100k/$200k economic nexus thresholds; your physical delivery of goods overrides those safe harbors for the specific transaction.
*   **Actionable Step:** If you have events scheduled in the next 90 days, consider temporarily bundling the cost into a single "Tuition" line item *only after* counsel confirms this shifts the tax characterization in those specific states, or begin collecting tax on the $300 portion immediately as a conservative measure.
