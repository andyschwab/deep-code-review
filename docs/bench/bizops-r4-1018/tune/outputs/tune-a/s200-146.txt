**The Call:** Do not frame the nonprofit tier as "zero revenue"; frame it as a verified B2B2C distribution channel that lowers your blended CAC and validates engagement, but you must present a clear path to monetizing the remaining 60% or an adjacent paid tier to satisfy Series A revenue growth mandates. Investors will view 40% zero-revenue users as a liability only if you cannot prove they drive paid conversion or reduce acquisition costs; otherwise, it looks like a charity, not a venture-scale business.

**The Numbers:**
*   **Runway:** $600,000 cash / $30,000 monthly burn = **20 months** of runway.
*   **Series A Timing:** You plan to raise in 12 months. This leaves an **8-month buffer** post-raise for due diligence and closing, which is tight but feasible if growth metrics spike in months 6–9.
*   **Unit Economics Impact (Illustrative):**
    *   *Scenario A (Current Plan):* 40% of users generate $0 ARPU. To hit a $2M ARR target (typical for a $4M raise at 2x revenue multiple), the remaining 60% must generate **$3.33M in annual revenue**.
    *   *Scenario B (Strategic Framing):* If the 40% free users refer paid users at a 10% conversion rate with a $50/month seat, the "free" tier effectively generates **$20 LTV per free user** in attributed revenue.
    *   *Assumption (ASSUMED):* Your paid ARPU is sufficient to cover the burden of the free tier. If your paid ARPU is low, the 40% drag makes unit economics negative unless the free users drastically lower marketing spend.

**What Flips It, and The Trigger:**
The deciding factor is **Blended CAC vs. Organic Growth**. If your 40% nonprofit users bring in new paid customers organically (viral coefficient > 0.5), the model is an asset. If you have to pay ads to acquire the paid 60% while giving the product away to the 40%, the model destroys value.
*   **Trigger:** By Month 6 of your runway (approx. 6 months from now), if your **Paid Conversion Rate** from the total user base is below 5% OR your **Blended CAC** has not dropped by 20% compared to a all-paid model, you must pivot to a "freemium-lite" (capped features) for nonprofits or introduce a corporate sponsorship tier where employers pay for the nonprofit access.
*   **Actionable Step:** If your current data shows nonprofits are not referring paid individuals, consider piloting a "Sponsored by Employer" model this quarter where corporations pay for the nonprofit access as part of their ESG benefits, turning the $0 line item into B2B revenue.
