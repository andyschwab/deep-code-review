**The Call:** Choose the nonprofit structure only if you can reliably secure the additional $100,000 in grants/donations; otherwise, the C-corp is the solvent choice. Based on your base numbers, the nonprofit's $20,000 compliance cost turns your thin $20,000 operating surplus into a deficit unless the extra funding materializes.

**The Numbers:**
*Formula:* Net Position = (Base Revenue + Additional Funding) – (Operating Expenses + Compliance Costs).
*Inputs:* Base Revenue $300k; OpEx $280k; Nonprofit Compliance $20k; Additional Funding (Nonprofit only) $100k.

| Scenario | Revenue | Expenses | Net Result | Status |
| :--- | :--- | :--- | :--- :--- |
| **C-Corp (Base)** | $300,000 | $280,000 | **+$20,000** | Solvent |
| **Nonprofit (No Grants)** | $300,000 | $300,000 ($280k + $20k) | **$0** | Break-even |

*Assumption (ASSUMED):* The $100,000 in grants/donations is not guaranteed and requires dedicated fundraising effort.
*Assumption (ASSUMED):* C-corp corporate income tax on $20k profit is negligible (~$3,000 at 15% bracket) compared to the $20k compliance cost difference.

**What Flips It, and the Trigger:**
The single input that changes the call is **Grant/Donation Acquisition**.
*Trigger:* If you cannot secure at least **$25,000** in tax-deductible funding within the first 12 months, the nonprofit structure yields a lower net position than the C-corp due to the higher compliance burden.
*Rule of Thumb:* Nonprofit viability here depends on funding covering 125% of the incremental compliance cost.
*Action Step:* If you do not have existing relationships with grantors or a donor pipeline by next month, consider starting as a C-corp (or an LLC taxed as one) to validate the revenue model before incurring the $20k compliance overhead.
