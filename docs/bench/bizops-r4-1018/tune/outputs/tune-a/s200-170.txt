**The Call**
Reject the €45,000 fixed-fee model as currently structured; it caps your upside and exposes you to catastrophic margin erosion if a client scales beyond 1,125 employees. Stick to per-seat pricing for now, or introduce a fixed fee only with a hard seat cap (e.g., up to 500 seats) and an overage rate thereafter.

**The Numbers**
We compare Contribution Margin (Revenue minus Variable Costs) for both models.
*Current Data:* Price/seat = €150; Cost/seat = €40; Contribution/seat = €110.
*Fixed Fee Proposal:* Revenue = €45,000; Cost/seat = €40 (variable).

*Scenario A: Average Client (250 seats)*
*   **Per-Seat:** (250 × €110) = **€27,500** contribution.
*   **Fixed-Fee:** €45,000 - (250 × €40) = **€35,000** contribution.
*   *Result:* Fixed fee wins by €7,500 per client at average volume.

*Scenario B: Scaled Client (1,000 seats)*
*   **Per-Seat:** (1,000 × €110) = **€110,000** contribution.
*   **Fixed-Fee:** €45,000 - (1,000 × €40) = **€5,000** contribution.
*   *Result:* Per-seat wins by €105,000. You effectively pay the client to train their staff.

*Break-Even Analysis (The Flip Point)*
To find where Fixed-Fee contribution equals Per-Seat contribution:
€45,000 - (€40 × Seats) = €110 × Seats
€45,000 = €150 × Seats
**Seats = 300**

At exactly 300 seats, both models yield €33,000 in contribution.
*   **Below 300 seats:** Fixed fee is more profitable.
*   **Above 300 seats:** Per-seat is more profitable.

Since your average client is 250 seats, the fixed fee looks attractive initially. However, your risk is not the average; it is the variance. If a client grows from 200 to 1,000, your margin collapses from €37,000 to €5,000, whereas per-seat revenue would have tripled.

**What Flips It, and The Trigger**
The single input that flips the decision is **Client Headcount Growth**.
*   **Trigger Value:** 300 employees per client.
*   **Rule of Thumb:** If you expect >20% of your enterprise clients to exceed 300 seats in a contract year, the fixed-fee model destroys value.
*   **Actionable Step:** If you must offer fixed fees to close enterprise deals, consider a "capped fixed fee" structure: €45,000 covers up to 300 seats, with a €120/seat rate for every employee above that threshold. This secures the baseline revenue while protecting your downside.

**Routing Note**
While this is a pricing math problem, switching to annual fixed-fee contracts in Germany may alter your VAT treatment timing (accrual vs. cash basis) and create specific obligations under German contract law regarding "unlimited" service definitions. Confirm the contract wording for "unlimited seats" with a German commercial lawyer to ensure it doesn't inadvertently classify as an open-ended service commitment that is difficult to terminate or adjust.
