**The Call:** Do not print "human-grade" on your packaging or website until you have confirmed your entire supply chain and manufacturing process meets the strict legal definition; using this term without full compliance triggers immediate regulatory risk and potential enforcement action that standard pet food claims do not.

**The Numbers & Rules:**
There is no arithmetic formula here, but there is a binary compliance gate. Under current FDA and Association of American Feed Control Officials (AAFCO) guidelines, "human-grade" is not a marketing flourish; it is a legal claim requiring that **every** ingredient and the **final product** be stored, handled, processed, and packaged in a facility inspected and approved for human food consumption.
*   **Standard Pet Food:** Requires facility registration as an animal food facility; ingredients must be "generally recognized as safe" (GRAS) or approved food additives for animals.
*   **"Human-Grade" Claim:** Requires your Ohio facility to be registered and inspected as a **human food facility** under the FDA Food Safety Modernization Act (FSMA). If your co-packer or your own facility is only licensed for animal food, the claim is illegal.
*   **Documentation:** You must maintain a paper trail proving every single ingredient (down to the vitamin premix) was sourced from a human-food supply chain and transported in human-food compliant vehicles. A supplier invoice stating "high quality" is insufficient; you need certificates of analysis and supply chain affidavits specifically validating human-edible status.

**What Flips It, and The Trigger:**
The single input that changes your path is your **manufacturing location's certification status**.
*   **Trigger:** If your production facility (or co-packer) holds a valid human food facility registration and passes human food Good Manufacturing Practice (GMP) inspections, you may proceed with the claim *provided* your ingredients match.
*   **Stop Condition:** If your facility is registered only for animal food, or if even one ingredient (e.g., a specific mineral supplement) is sourced from a feed-grade supplier, you cannot legally use the term.
*   **Rule of Thumb:** If you cannot serve the finished can to a human legally, you cannot label it "human-grade."
*   **This Week's Step:** If your co-packer cannot immediately produce their human-food facility registration number and a signed affidavit confirming your specific batch will be run under human-food GMPs, consider pivoting your marketing to "premium ingredients" or "locally sourced" while you audit the supply chain.

**Routing Note:**
This is a high-stakes regulatory determination involving FDA FSMA compliance and Ohio state agriculture laws. You must engage a food regulatory attorney or consultant before printing packaging. Specifically, ask them: "Does our specific co-packing arrangement and ingredient sourcing map strictly to the AAFCO 'human-grade' definition, and what specific language must appear on our label to avoid misbranding charges?" Do not rely on general business advice for this; a misstep here can result in product seizure and fines.
