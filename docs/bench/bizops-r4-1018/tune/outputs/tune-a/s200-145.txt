**The Call:** Treat the £80,000 software improvement and associated cloud simulation costs as highly likely eligible R&D, while the £150,000 formula development requires a strict split between "qualifying scientific uncertainty" (eligible) and "routine formulation/tasting" (ineligible); do not claim the full £230,000 without segregating the nutritionists' routine quality control time, as blending them triggers the highest audit risk.

**The Numbers:**
Under current UK rules (pre-April 2024 rates for context, though rates have merged/simplified for SMEs), the recoverable amount depends on the "qualifying portion" of your spend.
*Formula:* `Recoverable Cash = (Qualifying Staff Costs + Qualifying Cloud/Software) × Enhancement Rate`
*   **Software (£80k):** If the algorithm solves a technical uncertainty (e.g., predicting bioavailability where no off-the-shelf model exists), assume **100% eligibility**.
    *   *Base Case:* £80,000 × ~18-20% (effective cash credit rate for loss-making SMEs) = **~£14,400 - £16,000**.
*   **Formulas (£150k):** This is the variable. Routine blending is 0% eligible; testing new chemical interactions for stability/absorption where the outcome is uncertain is eligible.
    *   *Conservative Assumption (ASSUMED):* 30% of nutritionist time is genuine R&D vs. 70% routine QC.
    *   *Qualifying Spend:* £150,000 × 30% = £45,000.
    *   *Recoverable:* £45,000 × ~18-20% = **~£8,100 - £9,000**.
*   **Cloud Costs:** Directly attributable cloud compute for the R&D software team is eligible. If this is included in the £80k, the above holds. If separate, add it to the software pool.
*   **Total Estimated Recoverable (Base Case):** **£22,500 - £25,000** (approx. 10-11% of total spend).
*   **Aggressive Case (ASSUMED):** If 60% of formula work is deemed experimental. Total recoverable rises to **~£35,000**.

**What Flips It, and the Trigger:**
The single input that changes the call is the **technical narrative for the vitamin formulas**. If you cannot document a specific scientific hypothesis that failed or required iteration (e.g., "Compound X degraded at pH Y, requiring a novel encapsulation method"), the entire £150,000 shifts to "routine product iteration" (0% recoverable).
*   *Trigger Rule:* If your documentation does not explicitly record a failed experiment or an unresolved scientific contradiction for a specific batch, consider that cost routine QC.
*   *Action this week:* If your nutritionists' logs show only "tasting" or "standard mixing," consider reclassifying that time immediately to avoid inflating the claim; if they show "failed stability tests," document those specific failures as the core of your claim.
