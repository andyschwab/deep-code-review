**The Call**
Opening a staffed buying office in Quito creates a **high probability** of establishing a Permanent Establishment (PE) in Ecuador, exposing only the **profits attributable** to that office (not your global profit) to Ecuadorian corporate income tax. The $120,000 operational cost does not reduce the *risk* of creating a PE, but it directly reduces the *taxable profit* attributed to Ecuador if a PE is established.

**The Numbers & Criteria**
Under Ecuadorian domestic law (LORTI) and the US-Ecuador Income Tax Treaty (Article 5), a PE is typically triggered by:
1.  **Fixed Place of Business:** Maintaining an office, branch, or facility where business is conducted.
2.  **Dependent Agent:** Staff habitually exercising authority to conclude contracts or negotiate terms binding the head office.

*Attribution Rule:* If a PE exists, Ecuador taxes only the net income **attributable** to that office.
*Illustrative Calculation (ASSUMED):*
*   **Revenue Attributable:** If the office negotiates purchase prices that save CocoaCraft $500,000 annually compared to arm's-length market rates, this "savings" may be treated as deemed revenue.
*   **Deductible Expenses:** The $120,000 operating cost (rent, salaries, utilities) is deductible.
*   **Taxable Base:** $500,000 (Attributed Benefit) - $120,000 (Expenses) = $380,000.
*   **Estimated Tax:** Applied at Ecuador's corporate rate (currently 25%, **UNVERIFIED**, confirm with CPA).
*   **Result:** You pay tax on the $380k profit slice, not your Vermont processing profits.

**What Flips It & The Trigger**
The triggering input is **contractual authority**. If your Quito staff merely inspect quality and transmit data, but all purchase contracts are signed electronically in Vermont by US officers, you may avoid "Dependent Agent" status. However, the physical office itself ("Fixed Place") remains a risk if core business activities occur there.
*   **Trigger:** Staff signing purchase orders or negotiating final prices locally.
*   **Stop/Pivot Rule:** If local staff negotiate >10% of contract terms, consider routing final approval strictly to Vermont.
*   **This Week's Step:** Draft a "Limitation of Authority" clause for local employment contracts explicitly stating they cannot bind CocoaCraft to purchase agreements.
