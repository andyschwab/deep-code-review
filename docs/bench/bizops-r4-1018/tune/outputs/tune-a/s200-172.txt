**The Call:** Your current flow—holding funds in a merchant account before disbursing to tutors—almost certainly triggers money transmitter licensing (MTL) obligations in California, New York, and Texas, regardless of the 24-hour hold time. You must cease this specific fund-flow model immediately or engage specialized fintech counsel to structure a compliant exemption or partnership.

**The Numbers & Mechanics:**
The regulatory trigger is not your commission rate (15%) or transaction volume ($240k/month), but the legal definition of "receiving money for transmission."
*   **Your Model:** Parent pays Platform → Platform holds funds → Platform pays Tutor.
*   **The Risk Factor:** By taking custody, even briefly, you become the principal obligor to the tutor until payment is made. Most state statutes define this as "money transmission."
*   **The "24-Hour" Myth:** There is no universal federal or state exemption based solely on a <24-hour hold period. While some states have narrow exemptions for "agents of the payee" or specific low-value thresholds, these are fact-specific legal determinations, not automatic safe harbors.
*   **Jurisdiction Exposure:**
    *   **California:** The Department of Financial Protection and Innovation (DFPI) aggressively enforces MTL laws. Facilitating payments for third parties without a license is a criminal misdemeanor in CA.
    *   **New York:** The NYDFS requires a BitLicense or MTL for any virtual or fiat money transmission. The "limited purpose trust company" route is complex and expensive.
    *   **Texas:** The Department of Savings and Financial Institutions requires licensing if you receive money for transmission. Texas has limited exemptions for "payment processors," but they strictly require that you *never* take possession of the funds (i.e., funds must go directly from payer to payee via a third-party processor).

**What Flips It & The Trigger:**
The single input that changes your risk profile is **custody**.
*   **Current State:** You have custody. Risk = High (Cease and Desist orders, fines, potential criminal liability).
*   **Target State:** You have *no* custody. Risk = Low (Likely exempt as a technology platform).
*   **The Pivot:** Restructure your payment stack to use a "marketplace payout" solution (e.g., Stripe Connect, Adyen for Platforms) where the funds settle directly from the parent to the tutor, with your 15% fee split automatically by the processor *before* the tutor receives their net share. In this model, the funds never legally enter your balance sheet.
