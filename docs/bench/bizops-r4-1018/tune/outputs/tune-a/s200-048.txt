**The Call**
Do not lower your price; invest the $5,000 immediately in better photography and sizing tools. A 22% return rate driven by "size mismatches" is a product-information failure, not a pricing problem. Lowering the price to $150 will likely degrade your luxury brand perception without fixing the root cause of the returns, whereas clarifying the product description directly addresses the stated reason for returns.

**The Numbers**
Your current unit economics are distorted by the return volume. We must calculate the *Effective Contribution Margin* per unit sold to see the true bleed.

*Formula:*
`Effective Margin = (Price - COGS) - (Return Rate × (Price + Reverse Logistics Cost))`
*Note: Since you did not provide a reverse logistics/restocking cost, I will assume $0 for this baseline calculation to show the minimum impact. In reality, this cost is positive, making the situation worse.*

*Current Scenario ($180 Price):*
*   Gross Margin per unit: $180 - $60 = $120
*   Return Impact: 22% of units return.
*   Revenue lost per 100 units sold: 22 units × $180 = $3,960
*   COGS recovered (assuming full recovery): 22 units × $60 = $1,320
*   Net Loss on Returns (minimum): $3,960 - $1,320 = $2,640 per 100 units.
*   **Effective Margin per 100 units:** ($12,000 Gross Profit) - $2,640 (Return Loss) = $9,360.
*   **Effective Margin per Unit:** $93.60.

*Proposed Price Cut Scenario ($150 Price):*
*   Assumption: Price cut does *not* reduce the 22% return rate (since the cause is sizing confusion, not value expectation).
*   Gross Margin per unit: $150 - $60 = $90
*   Return Impact: 22% of units return.
*   Revenue lost per 100 units: 22 × $150 = $3,300
*   COGS recovered: 22 × $60 = $1,320
*   Net Loss on Returns: $1,980.
*   **Effective Margin per 100 units:** ($9,000 Gross Profit) - $1,980 = $7,020.
*   **Effective Margin per Unit:** $70.20.

*Investment Scenario (Keep $180, Spend $5k):*
*   Assumption: Improved assets reduce return rate from 22% to an industry-standard 5% (ASSUMED).
*   New Return Loss per 100 units: 5 × ($180 - $60) = $600.
*   **Effective Margin per 100 units:** $12,000 - $600 = $11,400.
*   **Effective Margin per Unit:** $114.00.
*   **Payback Period:** The improvement yields an extra $20.40 per unit ($114 - $93.60). With monthly revenue of $90k (~500 units), monthly gain is ~$10,200. The $5,000 investment pays back in **< 2 weeks**.

**What Flips It, and the Trigger**
The single input that changes this call is the *cause* of the return. If customer surveys reveal that returns are actually due to "foam quality" or "comfort" rather than "size," then the information fix will fail.
*   **Trigger:** If, after implementing the new sizing guide, the return rate remains above 15% after 30 days, pivot to investigating product quality or genuine price-to-value mismatch.
*   **Rule of Thumb:** Information fixes address "mismatch" returns; price cuts address "regret" returns. Your data says "mismatch."
