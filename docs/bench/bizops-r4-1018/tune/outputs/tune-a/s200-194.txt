**The Call**
Do not launch this specific income-share structure without securities counsel; while selling an educational service is not inherently a security, structuring repayment strictly as a percentage of future income with no fixed principal often triggers the "investment contract" test (Howey), creating a high risk of unregistered securities issuance.

**The Numbers**
There is no formula to compute "security status" because it is a legal determination, not a mathematical one. However, we can model the financial exposure based on your provided figures:
*   **Current Runway:** $150,000.
*   **Estimated Compliance Cost (if deemed security):** >$200,000.
*   **Deficit if triggered:** -$50,000 (insolvency risk).
*   **Student Volume:** 200 students.
*   **Churn:** 5% per cohort (ASSUMED: applies to enrollment, not repayment duration).
*   **Revenue Scenario (Base Case):** If 100 students qualify for repayment (earning >$80k), paying 8% of $80k ($6,400/year) for 3 years = $19,200/student. Total potential book value = $1.92M.
*   **Liability Exposure:** If classified as an unregistered security, regulators can demand rescission (returning all "investment" capital plus interest) and impose penalties. If 50 students demand rescission of the $12,000 value, immediate cash liability = $600,000 (4x your runway).

**What Flips It, and the Trigger**
The determining factor is whether the student is purchasing *education* (a service) or investing in *your school's success* with an expectation of profit derived from your efforts.
*   **Trigger:** If the marketing emphasizes the "return on investment" or if the repayment terms lack a fixed cap or principal amount, the SEC is more likely to view this as an investment contract.
*   **Rule of Thumb:** If the obligation looks like debt (fixed amount) or a service fee (fixed price), it is safer. If it looks like equity (percentage of upside forever or until a vague cap), it is riskier.
*   **Action:** If you cannot structure this as a fixed principal loan with income-contingent *repayment scheduling* (rather than income-contingent *obligation*), consider pausing launch until counsel reviews the contract language.

This is a Tier 3 (Route) issue. You must consult a securities attorney specializing in Income Share Agreements (ISAs).
*   **Exact Question:** "Does our specific ISA structure (0% payment below $80k, 8% above, 36-month term) constitute an 'investment contract' under the *SEC v. W.J. Howey Co.* test or fall under the 'consumer credit' exemption?"
*   **Time-Sensitive:** Do not sign a single student to this plan until you have a written legal opinion. Launching first and asking later risks your ability to raise venture capital, as VFs will not invest in a company with potential unregistered securities liabilities.
