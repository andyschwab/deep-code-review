**The Call**
Your donation and grant revenue is likely tax-exempt if you hold 501(c)(3) status, but the $12,000 interest and specific corporate sponsorship terms may trigger Unrelated Business Income Tax (UBIT) or require careful reporting; you must file Form 990 annually to maintain compliance, and you must immediately verify your exemption status and sponsorship contracts with a tax professional before assuming any income is free from tax or restriction.

**The Numbers**
*Total Revenue:* $180,000 (Individual) + $50,000 (Corporate) + $12,000 (Interest) = **$242,000**.
*Tax Treatment Illustration (Assuming 501(c)(3) Status):*
*   **Individual Donations ($180k):** Generally excluded from taxable income as charitable contributions.
*   **Corporate Sponsorship ($50k):**
    *   *Scenario A (Unconditional Gift):* $0 taxable income.
    *   *Scenario B (Quid Pro Quo/Advertising):* If sponsors receive advertising, logos, or exclusive benefits, this portion is **ASSUMED** to be unrelated business income. If 100% is deemed commercial, taxable income = $50,000.
*   **Interest Income ($12k):**
    *   *Standard Rule:* Usually passive investment income (exempt).
    *   *UBIT Trigger:* If funds are debt-financed or part of a larger commercial activity, a portion could be taxable. *Illustrative UBIT exposure:* $0 to $12,000 depending on source.
*   **Filing Threshold:** With $242,000 in gross receipts, you generally must file **Form 990-EZ** or the full **Form 990** (not the postcard Form 990-N), as gross receipts exceed $200,000.

**What Flips It, and the Trigger**
The single input that changes your tax liability is the **nature of the benefits provided to corporate sponsors**. If your agreements promise advertising, endorsements, or exclusive access in exchange for the $50,000, that revenue shifts from "exempt contribution" to "unrelated business income," potentially creating a tax bill and endangering your exempt status if it becomes a substantial part of your activities.
*Trigger:* Review all sponsorship contracts signed in the last 12 months. If any clause mentions "advertising," "logo placement," or "marketing deliverables," consider reclassifying that revenue as taxable immediately.
*Action Step:* If your sponsorship agreements include marketing deliverables, consider separating the "donation" portion from the "advertising purchase" portion in your bookkeeping this week.
