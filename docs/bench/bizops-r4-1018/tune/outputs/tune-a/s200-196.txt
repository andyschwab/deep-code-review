**The Call**
Reject the current launch plan; with $60k cash and $90k sunk infrastructure plus $4k/month burn, you are insolvent before acquiring the first user. You cannot reach break-even without immediate additional capital or a fundamental restructuring of costs. The high initial fixed cost creates a "debt hole" that standard SaaS unit economics cannot climb out of at your projected volume.

**The Numbers**
*Formula:* Break-Even Users (Month 1) = (Upfront Cost + Monthly Burn) / (Cert Fee + Maint Fee).
*Your Figures:* Upfront = $90,000; Monthly Burn = $4,000; Cert = $200; Maint = $20.
*Assumptions:*
1.  **Churn:** ASSUMED 0% for this static snapshot (optimistic).
2.  **Acquisition Speed:** ASSUMED linear growth to 500 users over 6 months (~83 users/month).
3.  **Server Scaling:** ASSUMED flat $4k/month (likely false if growth accelerates).

*Projection (Cumulative Cash Flow):*
*   **Month 0:** -$90,000 (Infra) - $60,000 (Reserves) = **-$30,000 Deficit immediately.**
*   **Month 1 Revenue:** 83 users * ($200 + $20) = $18,260.
*   **Month 1 Net:** $18,260 - $4,000 (server) = +$14,260.
*   **Cumulative End Month 1:** -$30,000 + $14,260 = -$15,740.
*   **Month 2 Revenue:** 166 users * $220 = $36,520. Net: +$32,520.
*   **Cumulative End Month 2:** -$15,740 + $32,520 = +$16,780 (Theoretical Profit).

*Reality Check:* You need $90k + $4k to start Month 1. You have $60k. You stop operations before launch.
*Break-Even Volume:* To cover just the $90k sunk cost with $220/user contribution, you need **409 users** immediately. To cover ongoing burn ($4k), you need **19 users/month** recurring.

**What Flips It, and the Trigger**
The **Upfront Infrastructure Cost** is the killer. In a standard SaaS model, infra scales with revenue; here, you prepaid for capacity you don't have.
*Trigger:* If you cannot raise $35k+ immediately to cover the gap between reserves ($60k) and required startup capital ($94k for Month 1), the venture fails.
*Rule of Thumb:* Never spend >50% of cash reserves on fixed assets before validating revenue.
*Step this week:* If you cannot secure a bridge loan or investor check for $40k by Friday, consider pivoting to a usage-based cloud model (pay-as-you-go) to eliminate the $90k upfront and reduce monthly fixed costs, even if variable costs rise.

*Note: Server scaling is a critical unknown; if 500 users require more than $4k/month in compute, your break-even month pushes back significantly.*
