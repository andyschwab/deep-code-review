**The Call**
Your current classification of coaches as independent contractors carries high risk of reclassification by the IRS and state agencies; do not rely on this structure to avoid payroll liabilities without immediately restructuring your operational control. The 70% revenue share and average $50/hr fee suggest a contractor model is *possible*, but your platform's likely control over scheduling and coaching methods pushes these workers toward employee status under federal guidelines.

**The Numbers**
*Current Exposure Estimate (Illustrative Only):*
If reclassified, you owe back payroll taxes on wages paid.
*   **Formula:** `Total Coach Payouts × (Employer Tax Rate ~7.65% + Unemployment Insurance ~0.6% + Potential Penalties)`
*   **User Figures:** $80,000 monthly revenue × 70% payout = $56,000 monthly coach payout.
*   **Annualized Payout:** $56,000 × 12 = $672,000.
*   **Estimated Liability (Base):** $672,000 × 8.25% ≈ **$55,440/year** in unpaid employer taxes alone.
*   **Penalties & Interest:** Can range from 20% to 100% of the unpaid tax depending on intent and duration.
*   **Result:** A single audit could generate a six-figure liability ($150k+), exceeding your current $50,000 cash reserve and threatening runway.

**What Flips It, and the Trigger**
The single input that changes the call is **Behavioral Control**.
*   **The Trigger:** If your platform mandates specific session times, provides mandatory training scripts, or disciplines coaches for rejecting bookings, the IRS will likely deem them employees.
*   **Rule of Thumb:** Contractors control *how* and *when* the work is done; employees are told *when* to show up and *how* to perform.
*   **Step this week:** Audit your coach onboarding docs and Terms of Service. If you see language requiring "minimum hours," "approved coaching methods," or "exclusive availability," consider removing those clauses immediately to align with contractor status, or prepare to withhold taxes.
