**The Call**
Do not bundle the hardware and subscription into a single unallocated line item yet; in Texas, commingling tangible personal property (the mirror) with a taxable service (the streaming content) without a separate stated price for each typically causes the **entire** transaction to be taxed at the higher tangible property rate. To preserve your margins, you must explicitly allocate the $1,680 bundle price into two distinct line items on the invoice ($1,200 hardware, $480 prepaid service) so the service portion can potentially be treated according to its specific taxability rules, rather than having the whole sum taxed as a physical good.

**The Numbers**
Texas sales tax rates vary by location (Austin is approx. 8.25%), but the *tax base* is the variable that flips your margin.
*   **Current Model (Separated):** Tax applies to the $1,200 hardware. The $40/mo service may be exempt or taxed differently depending on specific characterization (data processing vs. cable/satellite), but the hardware tax is certain.
    *   *Tax Base:* $1,200 per unit.
*   **Proposed Bundle (Unallocated):** If sold as a single "Smart Mirror Package" for $1,680 without breakdown:
    *   *Tax Base:* $1,680 per unit.
    *   *Assumption:* Texas Comptroller treats the dominant purpose as the tangible item if not separated, taxing the full amount at ~8.25%.
    *   *Impact:* You pay tax on an extra $480 per unit.
    *   *Cost Increase:* $480 × 8.25% = **$39.60 extra tax per unit**.
    *   *Margin Hit:* Your current hardware margin is $550 ($1,200 - $650). An extra $39.60 in tax (if passed to you) or friction (if passed to customer) reduces effective margin by **7.2%**.

**What Flips It, and the Trigger**
The trigger is **invoice allocation**. If your checkout system prints a single line item "FitReflect Bundle: $1,680," the tax engine defaults to taxing the whole amount as tangible property. If the system prints two lines ("Hardware: $1,200" and "12-Mo Prepaid Subscription: $480"), you isolate the tax base.
*   *Rule of Thumb:* In Texas, if a taxable item and a potentially non-taxable (or differently taxed) service are sold for a single price without allocation, the entire receipt is taxable.
*   *Action Step:* If your e-commerce platform cannot enforce distinct line-item allocation for the bundle, **do not launch the bundle** next week; keep the transactions separate until the billing logic is fixed.
