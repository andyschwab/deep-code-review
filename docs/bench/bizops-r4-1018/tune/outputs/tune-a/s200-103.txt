You must prioritize reducing churn over raising prices, as no realistic price increase can offset losing 40% of your base quarterly while paying $25 to acquire them.

**The Numbers:**
*   **LTV Formula:** $ARPU \times (1 / Monthly Churn Rate)$.
*   **Inputs:** ARPU = $12. Monthly Churn is derived from your 40% quarterly retention loss. Assuming a constant decay, this approximates to ~17% monthly churn ($1 - 0.60^{1/3}$).
*   **LTV Calculation:** $12 \times (1 / 0.17) = **$70.59** (Base Case).
*   **CAC:** $25 (Given).
*   **Gross Margin per User:** $12/month - ($4,000 fixed costs / 3,000 users) = $10.67 contribution margin initially, dropping as users churn but fixed costs remain.
*   **Payback Period:** $25 CAC / $12 ARPU = **2.1 months**.
*   **The Failure Point:** While payback looks fast (2.1 months), your average user lifespan is only ~5.9 months ($1/0.17$). After paying back CAC, you only collect ~3.8 months of pure profit ($45.60) per user. However, your fixed costs ($4,000/mo) are not covered by the shrinking active base.

**What Flips It, and The Trigger:**
The single input that kills this model is if the 40% churn is not a one-time drop but a *continuous* monthly rate. If 40% leave *every* month, LTV drops to $30, barely covering CAC.
*   **Trigger:** If your Month 4 retention is not >60% of Month 3, your LTV collapses.
*   **Rule of Thumb:** LTV:CAC must be >3:1 for safety. Yours is ~2.2:1.
*   **Step:** If churn remains >15% monthly next month, consider pausing paid acquisition and focusing entirely on engagement loops to extend user life to 8+ months.
