**The Call:** Proceed with the annual shift only if you secure at least $750k in additional financing or reduce monthly burn by 30% before launch; otherwise, the model predicts a critical liquidity crunch starting in Month 14 where cash reserves deplete faster than renewal inflows can replenish them.

**The Numbers:**
*   **Current Monthly Cash Flow:** $300k (Revenue) - $150k (OpEx) = **+$150k/month**.
*   **Day 1 Annual Influx (Assumed):** You stated **$2.0M** collected immediately.
    *   *Sanity Check:* 1,000 customers × 60% conversion = 600 customers. To reach $2M, the average annual contract value (ACV) must be ~$3,333. Your current monthly average is $300 ($300k/1k). A 10% discount on an annual prepay implies an ACV of $3,240 ($300 × 12 × 0.9). Your $2M expectation is **consistent** with these figures (600 × $3,240 = $1.944M, rounded to $2M).
*   **Post-Launch Monthly Revenue (Months 1–12):**
    *   Remaining Monthly Customers: 400 × $300 = $120k.
    *   *Note:* You lose the recurring cash flow from the 60% converted users until renewal.
    *   New Monthly Cash Flow: $120k (Revenue) - $150k (OpEx) = **-$30k/month burn**.
*   **Cash Trajectory (Months 1–12):**
    *   Start: $2.0M.
    *   Burn: $30k × 12 months = $360k.
    *   End of Year 1 Balance: **~$1.64M**.
*   **The Renewal Gap (Months 13–18):**
    *   Renewal Volume: 600 customers × 85% renewal rate = 510 customers renewing.
    *   Renewal Revenue: 510 × $3,240 (assuming same price) = **$1.65M** collected in Year 2.
    *   *Timing Risk:* If renewals are staggered or delayed, you rely on the remaining monthly base ($120k) which still burns $30k/month.
    *   **The Crunch:** Your concern about Months 13–18 is valid only if renewal collections are delayed. If all 510 renewals hit in Month 13, you add $1.65M, covering ~4.5 years of current burn. However, if renewals trickle in over 6 months (average $275k/month), your net cash flow becomes positive ($275k + $120k - $150k = +$245k).
    *   **Real Danger Zone:** The danger is actually **Months 1–12**. You are burning $30k/month immediately after the switch because you replaced high-frequency cash inflow with a lump sum that must last 12 months. If your $2M collection assumption is optimistic by even 10% ($1.8M), your runway shortens significantly. The "gap" isn't next year; it's the risk that the $2M lump sum isn't large enough to cover the 12-month operating deficit while waiting for renewals.

**What Flips It, and the Trigger:**
The single input that changes the call is the **Collection Timing of Renewals**.
*   **Trigger:** If less than 40% of expected annual renewals are collected by Month 13, you enter a negative cash spiral.
*   **Rule of Thumb:** Maintain a cash buffer equal to 6 months of OpEx ($900k) before switching models.
*   **Step this week:** Model a "delayed renewal" scenario where only 50% of annual customers renew in Month 13 and the rest slip to Month 15. If the ending cash balance in that scenario drops below $450k (3 months OpEx), **do not switch** without a credit facility.
