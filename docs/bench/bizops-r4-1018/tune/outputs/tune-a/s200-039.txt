**What Flips It, and The Trigger:**
The deciding factor is **retailer system capability**. If your distributors (who likely sell to Philly/Boulder retailers) cannot map one UPC to two different prices based on the store's zip code, you are forced into the new SKU.
*   **Trigger:** If your primary distributor confirms they cannot support "zone pricing" on a single UPC by **next reorder cycle**, you must proceed with the new SKU despite the $10k cost, as the $96k margin loss from absorbing the tax is the greater financial hit.
*   **Rule of Thumb:** If the price elasticity of demand for craft soda in these cities is higher than 1.0 (a 7.5% price hike causes >7.5% volume drop), absorbing the tax or delaying expansion is better than passing it on.

**Routing Note:**
Sugar tax ordinances (like Philadelphia's Beverage Tax) impose strict **reporting and remittance obligations** on the "dealer" or distributor, not always the manufacturer. You must confirm with a **tax professional** whether FizzCraft is legally defined as the "dealer" liable for filing and paying the tax directly in these municipalities, or if the liability sits entirely with your distributors. This determines if you need to collect tax data from retailers or simply adjust your wholesale price.

Operational challenges include managing "price leakage" (product bought in low-tax areas and diverted to high-tax areas) and ensuring retailer shelf tags match the scanned price to avoid consumer fraud complaints.
