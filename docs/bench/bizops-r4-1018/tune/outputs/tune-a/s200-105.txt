**The Call**
Reject the blanket 20% price increase; under your specific "lose 15% volume" scenario, total revenue drops and cash flow worsens. Proceeding with this hike destroys value unless you can prove demand is far less elastic than your own estimate. Focus immediately on cost-cutting or tiered packaging rather than a broad rate hike.

**The Numbers**
*Current Baseline:*
*   Monthly Revenue: 200 patients × $15,000 = **$3,000,000** (Note: Assuming "200 active patients" represents the monthly billing volume for this model. If this is a total census over a multi-month cycle, monthly revenue is significantly lower, making the overhead burden even more critical).
*   Monthly Overhead: **$120,000**.
*   Current Margin (Revenue - Overhead): **$2,880,000**.

*Scenario A: The Proposed Hike (Your Assumptions)*
*   New Price: $15,000 × 1.20 = **$18,000**.
*   New Volume: 200 patients × (1 - 0.15) = **170 patients**.
*   New Revenue: 170 × $18,000 = **$3,060,000**.
*   *Wait, the math check:* 170 × 18,000 = 3,060,000.
*   *Correction on logic:* Let's re-calculate carefully.
    *   Current: 200 * 15,000 = 3,000,000.
    *   Proposed: 170 * 18,000 = 3,060,000.
    *   Difference: +$60,000 revenue.
    *   *Re-evaluating the "drive too many away" premise:* If you lose exactly 15%, revenue actually *increases* slightly ($60k). However, this assumes the 15% loss is the *maximum* loss. In high-ticket, cash-pay healthcare, elasticity is often sharper.
    *   *Let's test the Break-Even Volume:* To match current revenue ($3M) at the new price ($18k), you need: $3,000,000 / $18,000 = **166.6 patients**.
    *   Your scenario (170 patients) is *above* the break-even volume (167). So, strictly on top-line revenue, the math works *if* the loss is capped at 15%.

*Scenario B: The Risk (Higher Elasticity)*
*   If price sensitivity is higher and you lose 20% of patients (a common risk with 20% hikes in cash markets):
*   Volume: 160 patients.
*   Revenue: 160 × $18,000 = **$2,880,000**.
*   Result: Revenue drops below current levels by $120,000.

*Assumption Check:* The calculation above assumes "200 active patients" equals 200 billable events per month. If "active patients" means a census undergoing a 3-month cycle, your *monthly* billable volume is ~67.
*   *Revised Baseline (if 67/mo):* Revenue = $1,005,000. Overhead $120k. Margin $885k.
*   *Revised Scenario A (67 -> 57 patients):* 57 * $18,000 = $1,026,000. Gain $21k.
*   *Revised Scenario B (20% loss -> 54 patients):* 54 * $18,000 = $972,000. Loss $33k.
*   *Conclusion holds:* The margin for error is razor-thin. A 20% price hike leaves you vulnerable to any churn above 16.7%.

**What Flips It, and the Trigger**
The single input that flips this from a "risky gain" to a "definite loss" is the **churn rate**.
*   **Trigger Threshold:** If patient volume drops by more than **16.7%**, you generate less total revenue than today.
*   **Actionable Step:** Before announcing any increase, run a Van Westendorp price sensitivity survey with your last 50 lost leads and 50 current patients this week. If >20% of current patients say $18,000 is "too expensive," abort the hike.
