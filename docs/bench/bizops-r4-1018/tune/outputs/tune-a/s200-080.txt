**What Flips It, and the Trigger:**
The single input that changes the outcome is the **status of written IP assignments from non-employee contractors**.
*   **The Rule of Thumb:** In California, work created by contractors is *not* automatically owned by the company unless there is a written agreement explicitly stating it is a "work made for hire" *and* containing a present assignment of future rights. If even one critical module in your schema registry was written by a contractor without this specific language, the acquirer's counsel will flag a "cloud on title."
*   **The Trigger:** If you cannot produce a signed IP assignment agreement for every single line of code not written by your eight full-time engineers, you must pause negotiations to remedy this.
*   **This Week's Step:** If you have any contractor invoices or Slack history indicating code delivery, consider drafting a "Confirmation of IP Assignment" letter for your attorney to review immediately, rather than waiting for the acquirer's due diligence list.
