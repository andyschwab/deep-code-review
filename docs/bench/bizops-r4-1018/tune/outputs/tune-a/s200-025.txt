**The Call:** Recognize the $45 hardware cost and allocated revenue immediately upon shipment, but recognize the treat subscription revenue ratably over the service period; the 20% bundle discount requires you to allocate the total transaction price between the two performance obligations (collar and treats) based on their relative standalone selling prices, not simply apply the discount to one item.

**The Numbers:**
*   **Standalone Prices:** Collar ($120) + Treats ($35) = $150 Total.
*   **Bundle Price:** $150 × 0.80 = $120.
*   **Allocation Ratio:**
    *   Collar Share: $120 / $150 = 80%.
    *   Treats Share: $35 / $150 = 23.3% (Note: Ratios sum to >100% due to rounding in explanation; mathematically: Collar gets 80% of the *discounted* price, Treats get the remainder).
    *   *Correct Allocation Math:*
        *   Collar Allocation: ($120 / $150) × $120 Bundle Price = **$96** (Recognize immediately).
        *   Treats Allocation: ($35 / $150) × $120 Bundle Price = **$24** (Recognize over the month).
*   **Impact:** For a bundled sale, you book $96 hardware revenue Day 1, and $24 subscription revenue over 30 days. Your COGS ($45 collar, $12 treats) matches these respective revenue recognition events.

**What Flips It, and The Trigger:**
The trigger is the **standalone selling price (SSP)**. If you frequently sell the collar alone for $100 instead of $120, your SSP changes, altering the allocation ratio.
*   *Rule of Thumb:* If your discount depth varies wildly or you lack a clear SSP for one component, the IRS may challenge your allocation method as arbitrary.
*   *This Week's Step:* If your discount strategy changes next month, recalculate the allocation ratio before issuing invoices to ensure consistent revenue recognition.
