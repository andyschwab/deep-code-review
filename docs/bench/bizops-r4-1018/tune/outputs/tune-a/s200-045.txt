**The Call**
Yes, shifting to 80% direct-to-consumer (DTC) sales significantly increases your risk of triggering economic nexus obligations in more states compared to relying on wholesale galleries. Wholesale sales to resellers generally do not count toward economic nexus thresholds if valid resale certificates are collected, whereas DTC sales to end-users almost always count fully toward both revenue and transaction thresholds.

**The Numbers**
To calculate exposure, you must separate "Gross Sales" from "Taxable Sales for Nexus Thresholds."

*   **Current State (Last Year):**
    *   Total Revenue: $1,000,000 ($400k DTC + $600k Wholesale).
    *   *Assumption (ASSUMED):* All wholesale buyers provided valid resale certificates.
    *   **Nexus Counting Revenue:** ~$400,000 (DTC only).
    *   **Nexus Counting Transactions:** ~2,667 units ($400k / $150) + *Unknown Wholesale Transaction Count*.
    *   **Result:** You are likely under the $100k revenue threshold in most states, but the transaction count depends on your wholesale volume.

*   **Projected State (80% DTC Shift):**
    *   Target Total Revenue: $1,000,000 (held constant for comparison).
    *   New Mix: $800,000 DTC / $200,000 Wholesale.
    *   **Nexus Counting Revenue:** ~$800,000.
    *   **Nexus Counting Transactions:** ~5,333 units ($800k / $150) + *Reduced Wholesale Count*.
    *   **Result:** You now exceed the $100,000 revenue threshold in any state where you generate just 12.5% of your total DTC revenue ($100k / $800k).

**Formula & Assumptions**
*   **Threshold Test:** `(State Specific DTC Revenue > $100,000) OR (State Specific DTC Transactions > 200)`
*   **Critical Assumption (ASSUMED):** Wholesale transactions are excluded from the count *only* if you hold a valid resale certificate for that buyer in that state. If missing, the full $90/unit counts toward the threshold.
*   **Critical Assumption (ASSUMED):** Shipping costs are excluded from the revenue threshold calculation in most, but not all, jurisdictions.

**What Flips It, and the Trigger**
The single input that flips your compliance status is the **validity of resale certificates** for your wholesale clients.
*   **Trigger:** If you fail to collect a resale certificate from a gallery in a specific state (e.g., New York), that entire $90/unit sale counts toward the economic nexus threshold.
*   **Pivot Point:** If your DTC revenue in any single state exceeds **$100,000**, you have a filing obligation in that state regardless of physical presence. With an $800k DTC target, hitting this trigger requires only 12.5% of your online customers to be in one state.
*   **Rule of Thumb:** High-volume, low-transaction wholesale is safer for nexus thresholds than high-volume, high-transaction DTC.
*   **This Week's Step:** If your DTC sales in any single state are approaching $80,000 annually, consider implementing geofenced marketing caps or preparing a sales tax registration plan for that specific jurisdiction.

**Routing Note**
Sales tax nexus determinations and the validity of resale certificates are binding legal interpretations that vary by state (e.g., California and New York have specific nuances on shipping and marketplace facilitator laws). You must consult a CPA or sales tax attorney to verify your resale certificate files and determine exactly which states you are currently obligated to register in before changing your revenue mix.
