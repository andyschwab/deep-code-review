**The Call**
Offer exactly **20%** equity to the investor; this matches the $1M investment on a $4M pre-money valuation. Do not offer more to "secure" the deal unless the valuation drops. Your current 60-20-20 split among founders will dilute proportionally to 48-16-16 post-money, preserving your relative control structure. The $50,000 debt is a liability on the balance sheet and **does not** alter the equity percentage calculation or the cap table math for this round.

**The Numbers**
*Formula:* Investor % = Investment / (Pre-Money Valuation + Investment).
*Inputs:* $1,000,000 / ($4,000,000 + $1,000,000) = 20%.
*Post-Money Valuation:* $5,000,000.
*Founder Dilution:* Each founder retains 80% of their original holding.
*   You: 60% × 0.80 = **48%**
*   Co-founder A: 20% × 0.80 = **16%**
*   Co-founder B: 20% × 0.80 = **16%**
*   Investor: **20%**
*   Total: 100%
*Assumption:* The $50k debt is standard accounts payable or a convertible note not triggering immediate conversion; if it converts now, it would increase the share count and slightly alter percentages (marked ASSUMED not impacting this basic math).

**What Flips It, and the Trigger**
The one input that changes your control is a **down-round valuation** or **excessive option pool creation** before the investment. If the pre-money valuation drops below $3M, your ownership falls below 40%, potentially risking majority control depending on future rounds.
*Trigger:* If the investor demands >25% equity for the same $1M, pause signing.
*Rule of Thumb:* Founders should generally retain >50% combined after a seed round to maintain morale and control.
*Step this week:* If the investor pushes for >20%, consider asking for a higher valuation based on your $675k revenue traction rather than conceding equity.

**Routing Note**
While the dilution math is straightforward, the **legal terms** attached to that 20% are binding and high-stakes. You must consult a securities lawyer to review:
1.  **Liquidation Preferences:** Ensure the investor does not demand a "2x participating" preference, which could wipe out your payout in an exit.
2.  **Valuation Caps/Conversion:** If any debt converts, the math changes; a lawyer must verify the cap table fully diluted count.
3.  **Securities Compliance:** Issuing equity is a securities offering; you must file the correct exemptions (e.g., Reg D in the US) to avoid illegal fundraising penalties.
