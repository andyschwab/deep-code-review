**The Call**
Changing your billing from per-transaction to a subscription model does not, by itself, alter your legal classification as a Consumer Reporting Agency (CRA) or trigger new data broker registration requirements under current New York or federal laws. Your compliance obligations are driven by the *nature of the data* you collect and the *purpose* for which you provide it (employment/screening), not by your revenue recognition method. However, shifting to "unlimited" access for agencies introduces significant operational risk under the Fair Credit Reporting Act (FCRA) regarding "permissible purpose" verification for every single pull, which is a higher compliance burden than your current model.

**The Numbers & Operational Impact**
*   **Current Volume:** 2,000 checks/month.
*   **Current Revenue:** 2,000 × $40 = $80,000/month.
*   **Subscription Break-Even:** To match current revenue, an agency must utilize the subscription at a rate where $300 covers their usage. If an agency runs 8 checks ($320 value), you break even on that client compared to the old model.
*   **Risk Multiplier:** Under a subscription model, if an agency runs 500 checks in a month, your revenue is capped at $300, but your liability exposure scales with every report generated.
*   **Assumption (ASSUMED):** The "unlimited" model encourages agencies to run checks on candidates they might not have previously screened due to cost, increasing the volume of data processing without a proportional increase in revenue to fund compliance audits.

**What Flips It, and The Trigger**
The factor that changes your compliance posture is not the price, but the **frequency of access**. The FCRA requires a "permissible purpose" for *each* report furnished.
*   **Trigger:** If an agency uses your API to run automated checks without re-verifying the specific candidate's consent and the agency's permissible purpose for *every* transaction, you are likely violating the FCRA.
*   **Rule of Thumb:** Billing frequency $\neq$ Compliance frequency. You cannot bundle consent for "unlimited future checks" into a single monthly subscription agreement; each background check generally requires a standalone disclosure and authorization.
*   **Action This Week:** If you proceed with the subscription model, consider implementing a technical gate that forces the agency to attest to permissible purpose and upload fresh consent for every individual report request, regardless of their payment status.

**Routing Note**
While the pricing model does not change your CRA status, the "unlimited" access structure may attract scrutiny from the New York Department of Financial Services (if handling certain data types) or the FTC regarding reasonable security measures for high-volume data access. You must consult with counsel specializing in FCRA and New York privacy law (such as SHIELD Act implications) to draft the specific "permissible purpose" workflows and data retention schedules required for high-frequency API access before launching the beta.
