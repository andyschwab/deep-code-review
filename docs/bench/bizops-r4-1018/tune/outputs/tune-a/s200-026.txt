**The Call:** Do not raise the price to $18 yet; keep it at $14 to secure the 10,000-unit volume needed to clear inventory and cover your burn, but explicitly market the "free shipping" or "lower total cost" advantage derived from your logistics savings rather than absorbing them as pure margin.

If this price hike causes your sales volume to drop by more than **22%** (the point where total gross profit dollars equal the current scenario), you will generate less cash overall despite the higher margin.
*   *Trigger:* If you test $18 and weekly sales volume drops below ~770 units (assuming a steady 1,000/week baseline), immediately revert to $14.

**Logistics vs. Price Perception:**
Your shipping weight reduction (1.2 lbs to 0.3 lbs) saves roughly $4–$6 in carrier fees per unit depending on the zone. However, consumers rarely pay a $4 premium *just* for a lighter package; they pay for outcomes (hair health, zero waste).
*   **Strategy:** Do not bury the shipping savings in the shelf price. Instead, keep the shelf price at $14 (competitive with premium drugstore brands) and use the $5 logistics saving to offer **free shipping** or a "buy 2, get 1 free" promo. This increases conversion velocity, which is critical given your $60,000 inventory overhang and 6-month runway at current burn.

**Risk Assessment:**
Positioning as a premium item ($18) risks slowing inventory turnover. With $60k in stock and a $10k/month burn, you have 6 months of runway *if* you stop all other spending, but you need cash flow now. A volume play at $14 clears inventory faster, reduces storage risk, and builds the customer base required for future upsells.
