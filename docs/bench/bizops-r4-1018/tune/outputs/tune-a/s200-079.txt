**The Call:** Do not abandon usage-based pricing; instead, adopt a hybrid "committed spend" model with overage rates. Pure variable billing will likely extend your four-month sales cycle in the US financial sector due to budget rigidity, but a tiered commitment structure resolves the predictability objection while retaining your unit-economic alignment.

**The Numbers:**
Your current baseline is **$48,000/year** per enterprise deal.
To model the hybrid approach, we define the commitment tier as a percentage of historical or projected usage, billed upfront or monthly, with a discounted overage rate for variable consumption.

*Formula:* Annual Contract Value (ACV) = (Commitment Tier × Base Rate) + (Projected Overage Minutes × Overage Rate)

*Scenario Analysis (based on your $48k average):*
1.  **Pure Usage (Status Quo Proposal):** $0 commitment, $X/minute.
    *   *Result:* High procurement friction. Budget holders cannot cap liability.
    *   *Risk:* Deals stall awaiting "worst-case" cost modeling.
2.  **Hybrid Model (Recommended):**
    *   **Assumption (ASSUMED):** Enterprise commits to 80% of expected volume ($38,400) annually.
    *   **Assumption (ASSUMED):** Overage rate is 10% higher than the blended committed rate to protect margin.
    *   *Result:* CFO sees a fixed $38,400 line item (predictable). The variable portion is treated as operational contingency.
    *   *Impact on Pipeline:* Of your $1M pipeline (20 deals), converting 100% to pure usage risks stalling ~40-50% in procurement. A hybrid model reduces this stall risk to <10%, preserving roughly $400k–$500k of at-risk revenue.

**What Flips It, and The Trigger:**
The single input that changes the call is the **Commitment Discount Depth**. If your discount for the committed portion exceeds 20% off your equivalent flat rate, you erode margin without gaining significant procurement speed.
*   *Trigger Rule:* If the sales cycle extends beyond **5 months** (a 25% increase from your current 4-month baseline) during pilot negotiations, the pricing structure is too complex or risky for the buyer.
*   *Action:* If a prospect demands a hard cap on total spend without a minimum commitment, consider a "true-up" clause where they prepay a block of minutes that expires in 12 months, rather than lowering rates.

**Anticipated Finance Objections & Structures:**
1.  **"We cannot approve open-ended liabilities."**
    *   *Solution:* Offer **Tiered Commitment Bands** (e.g., Silver: $25k/yr, Gold: $50k/yr, Platinum: $100k/yr). Each band includes a bucket of minutes; overages are billed monthly. This creates a fixed "license-like" cost base.
2.  **"Forecasting variable OpEx is difficult."**
    *   *Solution:* Provide a **Cost Calculator Tool** embedded in the proposal that allows them to model spikes. Offer a "soft cap" alert system that pauses non-critical jobs when 90% of the budget is consumed.
3.  **"Audit and reconciliation will be a nightmare."**
    *   *Solution:* Guarantee **Granular Reporting** tagged by department/project code, essential for financial sector chargebacks.

**Routing Note:**

Have counsel review your Master Services Agreement (MSA) to ensure the hybrid billing terms comply with standard procurement policies in the banking sector before sending proposals.
