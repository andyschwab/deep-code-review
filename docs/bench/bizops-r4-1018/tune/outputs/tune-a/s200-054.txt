**The Call:** Your current liability cap (€24k) creates an existential gap against your €1M insurance and potential breach costs; you must raise the cap to match your insurance limit (€1M) for data breaches specifically, rather than accepting unlimited liability or relying on a low cap that invites litigation.

**The Numbers & Risk Gap:**
*   **Current Exposure Cap:** €24,000 (12 months fees).
*   **Insurance Coverage:** €1,000,000 limit.
*   **Deductible:** €50,000.
*   **The Mismatch:** A claim between €24,001 and €50,000 is entirely uninsured and exceeds your contractual cap, forcing you to pay out of pocket. A claim of €2M (like your competitor's) leaves you exposed for €1.95M after insurance, but your contract attempts to limit you to €24k.
*   **The Reality:** If a court finds "gross negligence" (e.g., a known misconfiguration ignored), EU courts (including Germany) frequently invalidate liability caps entirely. In that scenario, your cap offers zero protection, and you are liable up to your assets and insurance limits.

**What Flips It & The Trigger:**
The trigger is the legal characterization of the fault.
*   **Standard Negligence:** Your cap likely holds.
*   **Gross Negligence/Willful Misconduct:** Caps are generally unenforceable under German law (§ 309 No. 7 BGB) and many EU jurisdictions.
*   **Decision Threshold:** If the potential loss exceeds your insurance deductible (€50k), your current cap is dangerous because it creates a "dead zone" where you pay fully while the insurer does not.
*   **Actionable Step:** This week, amend your Data Processing Addendum (DPA) or main agreement to set a **super-cap** for data breaches and security incidents at €1M (matching your insurance), while keeping the €24k cap for general service failures. Explicitly exclude liability for indirect/consequential damages (lost profits), which is standard and generally enforceable for startups, provided gross negligence is not involved.
