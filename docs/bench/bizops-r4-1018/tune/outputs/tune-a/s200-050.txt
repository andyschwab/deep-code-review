**The Call**
Do not claim your device "helps allergy sufferers" or "removes 99% of pollen/dander" in marketing materials yet; these are high-risk medical device claims under FDA interpretation that would likely force an immediate sales halt pending clearance. Instead, pivot your messaging to "general wellness" and "air quality" (e.g., "reduces airborne particulates," "freshens cabin air") which typically falls outside medical device regulation, allowing you to continue selling while you validate the regulatory boundary with counsel.

**The Numbers & Risk Assessment**
*   **Current Unit Economics:**
    *   Price: $120
    *   COGS: $45
    *   Gross Margin: $75 per unit (62.5%)
    *   Total Gross Profit (1,000 units): $75,000
*   **Regulatory Cost Assumption (ASSUMED):** FDA 510(k) clearance for a Class II medical device often costs $50,000–$150,000+ in consulting/testing fees and takes 3–6 months minimum, excluding clinical trials if required.
*   **The Trigger:** Using the words "allergy," "pollen," or "pet dander" in the context of *benefit* ("helps," "relieves") shifts the product from a consumer appliance to a device intended to diagnose, cure, mitigate, treat, or prevent disease (allergic rhinitis).
*   **The Consequence:** If the FDA determines you are marketing an uncleared medical device, they can issue a Warning Letter demanding immediate cessation of sales and recall of distributed units until clearance is obtained. Your current $75k gross profit buffer is likely insufficient to cover the legal and testing costs of a reactive compliance scramble.

**What Flips It, and The Trigger**
The single input that changes this call is the **specific wording of the claim**.
*   **High Risk (Medical Claim):** "Removes 99% of pollen to help allergy sufferers." (Implies treatment/mitigation of a disease state).
*   **Lower Risk (General Wellness/Consumer Claim):** "Captures 99% of airborne particles as small as 0.3 microns for cleaner cabin air." (Focuses on mechanical performance and general environment, not health outcomes).

**Decision Threshold & Next Step:**
If your marketing copy mentions a specific medical condition (allergies, asthma, hay fever) or a biological agent (pollen, dander, viruses) as the *target of relief*, consider that a red flag requiring legal review before publication.
*   **Action this week:** Draft two versions of your ad copy. Version A uses your proposed "allergy" language. Version B uses only "particulate/air quality" language. Take both versions to a regulatory attorney specializing in FDA consumer products for a definitive "freedom to operate" opinion before launching the campaign. Do not rely on internal interpretation for this boundary.
