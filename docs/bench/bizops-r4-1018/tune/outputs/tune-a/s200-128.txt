**The Call**
Pass the 8% tax to the consumer in New York immediately; absorbing it destroys unit economics and creates an unsustainable cross-subsidy from your rural business. Do not absorb the cost unless urban cancellation rates exceed 15% in a controlled test.

**The Numbers**
*Current Monthly Gross Booking Value (GBV):* 400 bookings × $1,200 = $480,000.
*Current Monthly Revenue (12% commission):* $480,000 × 0.12 = $57,600.

*Urban vs. Rural Split:*
*   Urban (60%): 240 bookings, $288,000 GBV, $34,560 revenue.
*   Rural (40%): 160 bookings, $192,000 GBV, $23,040 revenue.

*Scenario A: Absorb the Tax (Urban Only)*
If you absorb the 8% tax on the $1,200 base, the cost per booking is $96.
*   Monthly Cost: 240 urban bookings × $96 = **$23,040 loss**.
*   New Urban Revenue: $34,560 - $23,040 = $11,520.
*   **Total Company Revenue:** $11,520 (Urban) + $23,040 (Rural) = **$34,560**.
*   **Revenue Impact:** A **40% drop** in total monthly revenue ($23,040 lost). Your effective commission rate in NYC drops from 12% to 4%.

*Scenario B: Pass the Tax to Consumer*
*   New Consumer Price: $1,200 + ($1,200 × 0.08) = $1,296.
*   Revenue per booking remains $144 (12% of $1,200 base) assuming volume holds.
*   Risk: If price sensitivity causes a 10% volume drop (24 fewer bookings):
    *   Lost Revenue: 24 × $144 = $3,456.
    *   Net Position: Still $19,584 better than absorbing the tax.

**What Flips It, and the Trigger**
The decision flips only if the price increase causes urban booking volume to drop by more than **16%** (the point where lost commission equals the tax cost).
*   **Trigger:** Monitor urban conversion rates weekly for 30 days post-implementation. If urban bookings fall below 201/month (a >16% drop), consider a temporary 4% subsidy while re-evaluating pricing strategy.
*   **Step this week:** Update checkout UI to clearly line-item the "NY Occupancy Tax" as a government mandate separate from your service fee to reduce friction, then A/B test messaging on tax transparency.

**Routing Note**
New York's law designates you as a "Marketplace Facilitator," creating a binding obligation to register, collect, and remit sales/occupancy taxes for NY transactions, even if partners are rural or urban. Vermont currently lacks this specific mandate for your model, but nexus thresholds may apply.
*   **Action:** Engage a **CPA or Tax Attorney specializing in Multi-State Marketplace Facilitator Laws**.
