**The Call:** Treat the $350 last-mile logistics cost as **Cost of Goods Sold (COGS)**. Classifying it as Sales & Marketing (S&M) inflates your gross margin artificially and obscures the true unit economics of your "white-glove" promise, leading to flawed scalability assumptions for your next hire plan.

**The Numbers:**
To determine the impact on your contribution margin, we must calculate the Gross Margin under both scenarios using your provided figures ($2,500 Price, $350 Logistics). Note: Your base manufacturing cost is missing; I will label this **$M (ASSUMED)** to demonstrate the structural difference.

*Formula:*
Gross Margin = Revenue - COGS
Contribution Margin (Unit) = Gross Margin - Variable S&M

*Scenario A: Logistics in COGS (Recommended)*
*   Revenue: $2,500
*   COGS: $M + $350
*   **Gross Margin:** $2,150 - $M
*   *Result:* Your margin directly reflects the cost to fulfill the premium promise. If $M is $1,000, your Gross Margin is $1,150 (46%).

*Scenario B: Logistics in S&M (Incorrect for this model)*
*   Revenue: $2,500
*   COGS: $M
*   **Gross Margin:** $2,500 - $M
*   *Result:* If $M is $1,000, your Gross Margin appears to be $1,500 (60%).
*   *Distortion:* This creates a $350/unit illusion of profitability that vanishes once you pay for delivery. It makes the core product look more scalable than it is, risking over-hiring based on fake efficiency.

**What Flips It, and The Trigger:**
The only input that changes this classification is if the delivery becomes an optional, separate line-item charge paid directly by the customer *after* the base sale, rather than a bundled inclusion.
*   **Trigger Value:** If you unbundle delivery and charge the customer >$350 specifically for it, you could potentially net the revenue against the cost or treat it as a separate service line.
*   **Current Reality:** Since delivery is "included in the $2,500 price point," it is a fulfillment cost required to realize the revenue.
*   **Rule of Thumb:** If the customer cannot receive the product without this cost, it is COGS.
*   **This Week's Step:** If your blended Gross Margin (with logistics in COGS) drops below 50%, consider testing a tiered pricing model where "standard freight" ($150) is the base and "white-glove" is a $250 add-on to see if volume holds.
