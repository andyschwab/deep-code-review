**What Flips It, and the Trigger:**
The single input that flips this from a business experiment to a legal liability is **geographic correlation with protected classes**.
*   **The Risk:** Under the Fair Housing Act (FHA) and California's Fair Employment and Housing Act (FEHA), practices that appear neutral but result in a disproportionate adverse effect on protected groups (race, color, religion, sex, disability, familial status, national origin) are illegal ("disparate impact").
*   **The Mechanism:** If your algorithm charges higher fees for homes in "high-demand" areas, and those areas statistically correlate with lower minority populations (or conversely, penalizes homes in diverse areas by lowering fees below market viability), you may be steering tenants or pricing homes out of specific neighborhoods based on protected characteristics.
*   **Data Points to Avoid:** Do not use zip codes, census tract data, school district ratings, crime statistics, or any proxy that correlates with race or national origin as direct inputs for your pricing logic. Even "demand" metrics derived from tenant application density can be tainted if the applicant pool itself reflects historical segregation.
*   **Trigger:** If your legal counsel cannot mathematically prove that your pricing variables have **zero correlation** with protected class demographics, you must stop.

**Routing Note (Tier 3 - Binding Determination):**
You require a formal **disparate impact audit** by a fair housing specialist before writing a single line of pricing code. The specific question for counsel is: *"Does our proposed algorithm's use of geographic and demand-based variables create a statistically significant disparate impact on protected classes under California FEHA and the federal FHA, and does charging the provider rather than the tenant shield us from liability?"* (Note: Charging the provider does **not** automatically grant immunity if the effect restricts housing access). Do not rely on general business advice for this; the penalty for violation includes punitive damages and loss of licensure.
