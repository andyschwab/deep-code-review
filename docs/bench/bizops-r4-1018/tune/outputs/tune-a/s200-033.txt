**The Call**
Yes, renting commercial kitchen space in Austin, Denver, and Seattle almost certainly creates a corporate income tax and sales tax filing obligation (nexus) in Texas, Colorado, and Washington, regardless of your $200,000 revenue level. You must register and file in these states immediately upon establishing these locations; do not wait for an audit.

**The Numbers & Rules**
Your fear of cash drain is valid, but the risk of penalties for non-compliance is higher. Nexus (the obligation to file/pay) is triggered by two distinct mechanisms:

1.  **Physical Presence Nexus:** This is the primary trigger for you.
    *   **Rule:** Leasing or renting real property (including commercial kitchen space) for business operations constitutes a physical presence.
    *   **Application:** Paying $3,000/month in each city establishes a clear physical footprint. The duration (6 hours/day) or the specific type of rental agreement (license vs. lease) rarely negates this; the act of occupying space for production is the key factor.
    *   **Result:** You now have nexus in TX, CO, and WA. This obligates you to file corporate income tax returns (even if you owe $0 due to apportionment) and sales tax returns.

2.  **Economic Nexus:** This is secondary but relevant for sales tax.
    *   **Rule:** States require remote sellers to collect sales tax once they exceed specific revenue or transaction thresholds *into* that state.
    *   **Thresholds (Approximate):**
        *   **Texas:** $500,000 in revenue into TX.
        *   **Colorado:** $100,000 in revenue into CO.
        *   **Washington:** $100,000 in revenue into WA.
    *   **Application:** With $200,000 *total* national revenue, you likely haven't hit the economic nexus threshold for *remote* sales alone. However, because you now have **physical presence**, these economic thresholds no longer protect you. Physical presence overrides the higher economic limits; you must collect sales tax on *all* sales shipped to customers in those states, regardless of the amount.

**What Flips It & The Trigger**
*   **The Flip:** The only way to avoid this obligation is if the rental agreement is structured strictly as a service fee where you have *no* exclusive right to occupy the space (e.g., a pure "co-working" membership where you cannot store inventory or leave equipment). If you store flour, ovens, or finished goods there, it is physical presence.
*   **The Trigger:** The date you signed the lease or moved equipment in.
*   **Action Step:** If you have already signed or occupied, consider halting shipments to those states temporarily until registered, or budget immediately for retroactive filing.

**Routing Note**
This is a binding tax determination requiring a licensed professional.
*   **Who to hire:** A CPA or tax attorney specializing in **multi-state sales and use tax** and **state corporate income tax**.
*   **Exact Question:** "We have established physical presence in TX, CO, and WA via commercial kitchen rentals. Please perform a nexus study, register us for sales and income tax in these jurisdictions, calculate any retroactive liability from the lease start date, and advise on apportionment factors to minimize double taxation."
*   **Time-Sensitive:** Penalties and interest accrue monthly from the date nexus was established. Voluntary Disclosure Agreements (VDAs) may limit penalties if acted upon before the state contacts you.
