**The Call**
Do not fabricate a multi-year LTV; your model is a single-season transactional business, not a recurring subscription. Present your unit economics as **Contribution Margin per Student** and **Payback Period**, not LTV:CAC. Reject adding assumed referral or merchandise revenue to the core LTV unless you have historical data proving those margins exceed your acquisition cost; doing so without data will erode investor trust. Your current unit economics are negative on a standalone basis ($2,500 revenue vs. $450 CAC leaves $2,050 for all delivery costs), so the investment thesis must rely on operational leverage (lowering variable costs) or verified viral loops, not extended lifetime math.

**The Numbers**
Since "Lifetime" = 1 session, the standard LTV formula ($ARPU \times \frac{1}{Churn}$) collapses. Instead, use **Unit Contribution**:

$$ \text{Unit Contribution} = \text{Price} - \text{CAC} - \text{Variable Delivery Cost} $$

*   **Price:** $2,500 (Given)
*   **CAC:** $450 (Given)
*   **Variable Delivery Cost:** **ASSUMED UNKNOWN** (Instructor wages, materials, software licenses per student).
*   **Result:** You retain **$2,050** per student to cover all instruction, facility, and overhead costs before breaking even.

*Scenario Analysis on "Extended" Value:*
If you attempt to force an LTV by adding ancillary revenue, you must bracket these as **ASSUMED**:
*   **Base Case (Current):** LTV = $2,500. Ratio (LTV:CAC) = 5.5x. *However*, this ignores the 90%+ cost of goods sold (COGS) for running the camp.
*   **Referral Hypothesis:** If 10% of students refer a new student (who pays full price) and you give a $250 credit:
    *   Added Revenue: $250 (net of credit).
    *   New Effective LTV: $2,750.
    *   *Verdict:* Only valuable if the referral requires $0 additional ad spend.
*   **Merchandise Hypothesis:** If average merch profit is $50/student.
    *   New Effective LTV: $2,550.
    *   *Verdict:* Negligible impact on the $450 CAC justification.

**What Flips It, and The Trigger**
The single input that changes your viability is **Variable Delivery Cost per Student**.
*   **Trigger:** If your cost to instruct one student (wages + materials) exceeds **$2,050**, you lose money on every attendee regardless of marketing efficiency.
*   **Rule of Thumb:** In service businesses, CAC should be <33% of *margin*, not just revenue. If your delivery cost is $1,500, your margin is $550. A $450 CAC against a $550 margin is unsustainable (1.2x return).
*   **Actionable Step:** If your calculated delivery cost per student is >$1,600, consider pivoting your pricing model to a tiered "Good-Better-Best" structure (e.g., $2,500 standard, $3,500 premium with 1:1 mentoring) to increase margin before spending more on ads.
