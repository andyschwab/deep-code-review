**What Flips It, and The Trigger:**
The trigger is **control**. In both Florida and Georgia, a "franchise" is legally defined by three elements: (1) a trademark license, (2) a required fee (your $15k), and (3) a "marketing plan" or substantial control over operations. Your plan to "standardize methods" likely satisfies element three. If an attorney determines you exert significant control (e.g., mandating specific sleep protocols, scripts, or hours), you are operating an unregistered franchise. The threshold is not a dollar amount but the degree of operational mandate. If you dictate *how* the service is performed, not just the *outcome*, you cross into franchise territory.
*   **Stop Trigger:** If your operations manual dictates specific step-by-step service delivery rather than general brand standards, halt immediately.
*   **Next Step:** If you intend to standardize methods, consider hiring a franchise attorney this week to draft a Franchise Disclosure Document (FDD) or restructure as a licensing model with zero operational control.
