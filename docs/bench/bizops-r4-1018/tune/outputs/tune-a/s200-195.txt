**The Analysis (Factors & Risks):**
Courts weigh four statutory factors. In your specific scenario, the math and facts tilt heavily against you:
1.  **Purpose and Character:** While your content is tutorial-based, your business is a for-profit subscription service. The 20% sign-up spike directly links the copyrighted images to commercial gain. Courts rarely accept "educational" defenses when the copyrighted material is the primary draw for paying customers.
2.  **Nature of the Copyrighted Work:** Cartoon characters are highly creative, fictional works, which receive the strongest legal protection. Using them is riskier than using factual or news-based imagery.
3.  **Amount and Substantiality:** You used recognizable images of the characters. Even if the clip is short, using the "heart" of the character (their distinct visual identity) counts against you.
4.  **Effect on the Market:** This is the most dangerous factor for you. If rights holders license their characters for educational materials or official tutorials, your free-riding undermines that potential market. The fact that these tutorials are your "top-performing" drivers suggests you are capturing value the owner could otherwise monetize.

**Potential Damages:**
If sued, you face statutory damages ranging from $750 to $30,000 per work infringed, which can jump to $150,000 per work if the infringement is deemed willful. With three tutorials using two distinct characters, you are exposed to multiple counts. Additionally, you could be liable for the plaintiff's legal fees and any profits you attributed to the infringement (the revenue linked to that 20% spike). A judgment could easily exceed your $25,000 defense budget, potentially threatening the solvency of the business.

**Unknown Precedents:**
There is no secret precedent where a commercial site successfully used iconic characters to drive subscriptions under a fair use defense. Cases involving "educational" sites often fail when the content is behind a paywall or when the use serves as a marketing hook rather than critical commentary or parody. The "educational" label does not grant immunity to commercial enterprises selling access to copyrighted IP.

**Immediate Next Step:**
Stop creating content with these characters immediately. For the existing three tutorials, consider geo-blocking them in jurisdictions with strict IP enforcement or replacing the character images with generic or licensed alternatives.
