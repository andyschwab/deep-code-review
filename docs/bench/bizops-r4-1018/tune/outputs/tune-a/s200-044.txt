**The Call**
Proceed with the Chicago expansion but reframe the narrative for investors: the 500-scooter cap does not break unit economics, but it extends the time-to-scale, requiring a "cluster-density" strategy rather than a "flood-the-zone" approach. The regulatory hurdle increases execution risk but does not invalidate the business model if you demonstrate higher utilization rates per asset in the constrained zone.

**The Numbers**
*Current Miami Unit Economics (Unrestricted):*
*   **Revenue per Ride:** $1.00 (unlock) + ($0.35 × 18 mins) = **$7.30**
*   **Monthly Revenue (Est.):** Assuming 2 rides/scooter/day × 5,000 scooters × 30 days = 300,000 rides.
    *   Total Revenue: 300,000 × $7.30 = **$2,190,000**
*   **Monthly OpEx:** **$180,000** (Note: This implies an incredibly low cost base of $0.60 per ride or significant unlisted costs like COGS/depreciation. For this analysis, we treat $180k as the *variable* operating cost provided).
*   **Contribution Margin (per ride, excluding CapEx):** $7.30 - ($180,000 / 300,000 rides) = **$6.70**

*Chicago Scenario (Capped at 500 Scooters):*
*   **Constraint:** 10% of fleet size (500 vs 5,000).
*   **Projected Revenue (Same Utilization):** 30,000 rides × $7.30 = **$219,000/month**.
*   **Allocated OpEx:** If fixed costs (software, management) remain high, margins compress. If OpEx scales linearly with fleet (charging/maintenance staff), costs drop to ~$18,000.
    *   *Scenario A (Linear OpEx):* Profit = $219k - $18k = **$201,000/month**.
    *   *Scenario B (High Fixed OpEx):* If 50% of the $180k is fixed overhead, Chicago absorbs $90k + $18k variable = $108k. Profit = **$111,000/month**.
*   **Valuation Impact:** VCs value scalability. A hard cap limits Total Addressable Market (TAM) capture in that specific city. However, if Chicago yields **$201k/month** profit on a small asset base, the Return on Assets (ROA) is significantly higher than Miami, potentially increasing valuation multiples based on efficiency rather than sheer volume.

**What Flips It, and the Trigger**
The deal flips if the **rides-per-scooter-per-day** in Chicago drops below **0.8** due to poor placement or weather, making the fixed cost of compliance (permit fees, legal) outweigh the limited revenue.
*   **Trigger Threshold:** If average daily rides per scooter < 1.0 for three consecutive months, the constrained model fails to cover allocated overhead.
*   **Action:** If utilization in the first 60 days is < 1.5 rides/day, consider reallocating the 500 permitted scooters to high-demand micro-zones only, rather than spreading them city-wide.
