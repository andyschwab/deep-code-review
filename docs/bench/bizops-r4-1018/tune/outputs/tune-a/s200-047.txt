**The Call**
Proceed with the trade-in program structured explicitly as a **sales price discount** rather than a barter exchange; this is the standard industry practice that minimizes reporting complexity. Do not halt the launch for a specialized accountant, but schedule a one-hour review with your current CPA to confirm the specific sales tax treatment in your nexus states before the first invoice is issued.

**The Numbers**
The financial impact relies on treating the $200 as a reduction of the taxable sales price, not as income from selling used goods.

*   **Gross Revenue per Unit:** $800 (List Price)
*   **Trade-in Credit:** $200 (Treated as Discount)
*   **Net Cash Collected:** $600
*   **Refurbishment Cost:** $50 (ASSUMED per your input)
*   **Net Realizable Value per Trade-in:** $550 ($600 cash - $50 cost, ignoring inventory asset value of the core)

*Scenario Analysis on Taxable Basis:*
*   **If treated as a Discount (Recommended):** Sales tax is collected only on the $600 net price.
    *   *Result:* Lower sales tax liability; no income recognized for the old machine.
*   **If treated as Barter (Avoid):** You recognize $800 revenue + $200 fair market value (FMV) of old machine as income.
    *   *Result:* Higher income reporting complexity; sales tax potentially due on full $800 depending on state; requires tracking FMV of 500 distinct used units.

**What Flips It, and the Trigger**
The single input that changes this recommendation is a **state-specific statute** that prohibits deducting trade-in values from the sales price for *commercial* or *non-automotive* equipment.
*   **Trigger Rule:** If any state where you have nexus (e.g., WA, CA, NY) explicitly excludes "home embroidery machines" from their trade-in tax exemption laws, you must collect tax on the full $800 in that state.
*   **Actionable Step:** Before announcing the promotion, check the "Trade-in Exemption" clause in the sales tax code for your top 3 nexus states. If the law limits exemptions to vehicles or electronics only, **consider** restricting the promo to those states or absorbing the tax difference to keep the customer price at $600.

**Routing Note**
While the federal income tax treatment is generally clear (discount vs. barter), sales tax rules on trade-ins are highly jurisdiction-specific. Bring this exact question to your CPA: "Does [State X] extend sales tax trade-in exemptions to home embroidery machines, or is tax due on the pre-credit price?" This is time-sensitive before your first quarterly filing.
