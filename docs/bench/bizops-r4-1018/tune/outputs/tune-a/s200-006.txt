**The Call**
A $200M TAM does not make a $10M post-money valuation impossible, but it caps your narrative; you must justify the round by proving you can capture a dominant share of this niche quickly, not by promising mass-market scale. Reject the premise that small TAM forces excessive dilution; instead, frame the round as buying dominance in a high-margin, defensible segment where a 5–10% market share yields a profitable, sustainable business, even if it isn't a unicorn.

**The Numbers**
*   **Current Unit Economics:**
    *   Price: $80
    *   Gross Margin: 40% → $32 contribution per unit.
    *   *Assumption (ASSUMED):* To support a $10M post-money valuation at seed, investors typically look for a path to $2M–$3M ARR within 18–24 months.
    *   Required Volume: ~$2.5M / $80 = ~31,250 units/year globally.
    *   Market Share Required: $2.5M / $200M = 1.25% of TAM.
*   **Dilution Analysis:**
    *   Raise: $1.5M
    *   Target Post-Money: $10M
    *   **Equity Sold:** $1.5M / $10M = **15%**.
    *   *Scenario A (Standard Seed):* Investors accept 15–20% dilution for strong unit economics in a niche.
    *   *Scenario B (TAM Discount):* If investors fear the ceiling, they may demand a lower pre-money (e.g., $6M), pushing dilution to **20%** ($1.5M / $7.5M pre + $1.5M).
    *   *Result:* The "small TAM" risk likely shifts your realistic equity give-up range from **15% to 22%**, not higher, provided your margins hold.

**What Flips It, and the Trigger**
The single input that changes the call is **LTV:CAC ratio**. If your Lifetime Value (driven by retention/repeat purchases or accessories) is <3x your Customer Acquisition Cost, the small TAM becomes fatal because you cannot afford to grow.
*   **Trigger:** If your current repeat purchase rate is <15% or LTV:CAC is <3.0, investors will treat this as a lifestyle business and demand >25% equity or refuse the round.
*   **Rule of Thumb:** In niche hardware, high margin (40%+) must be paired with high retention to justify valuation.
*   **Step this week:** Calculate your exact LTV based on 12-month cohort data; if LTV:CAC > 4.0, lead negotiations with "capital efficiency in a dominated niche" to anchor the $10M valuation.
