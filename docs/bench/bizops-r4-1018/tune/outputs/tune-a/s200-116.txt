**The Call**
Do not finalize your landed cost model or margin targets yet; the duty liability for this $85,000 shipment is a tier-3 binding determination that requires a licensed customs broker to classify each ingredient under its specific HTS code and verify current trade agreement eligibility (e.g., GSP) for India, Peru, and Vietnam. Consolidating ingredients into a single SKU does not automatically create a single duty rate; US Customs typically assesses duty based on the classification of the individual components or the "essential character" of the blend, which a broker must determine to prevent costly penalties.

**What Flips It, and The Trigger**
The single input that flips this call is the **HTS Classification and Country of Origin Certification**. If your broker determines the blend takes the highest duty rate of its components, or if you fail to secure valid Certificates of Origin to claim preferential rates (like GSP for India/Peru), your costs jump to the "High" scenario immediately.
*Trigger:* If the broker cannot confirm GSP eligibility for the Indian and Peruvian components before the Bill of Lading is issued, **stop** and consider splitting the shipment or renegotiating Incoterms to shift duty liability to the supplier until verified.
*Rule of Thumb:* Assume no trade agreement applies until documentation is physically in hand; budget for the "High" scenario to protect cash flow.
