**The Call:** Retain the per-seat model ($49/seat) as your primary offering for now, but introduce the instance-based model ($500/app) as a high-volume "Enterprise Unlimited" tier. Switching entirely to instance pricing risks stalling adoption of new internal tools, which contradicts your goal of becoming a standard framework within large organizations. The seat model captures value from team growth, while the instance model captures value from scale; you need both to maximize Lifetime Value (LTV) without triggering churn from perceived "taxes on innovation."

**The Numbers:**
Your current unit economics show a clear baseline:
*   **Current MRR:** $8,000 (160 seats × $49).
*   **Hypothetical Scenario (200 devs, 50 apps):**
    *   *Seat Model:* 200 × $49 = **$9,800/mo**.
    *   *Instance Model:* 50 × $500 = **$25,000/mo**.
    *   *Gap:* The instance model yields **2.55× more revenue** in this specific high-density scenario.
*   **Runway Check:** With a $35,000 monthly burn and $8,000 MRR, your net burn is $27,000. You are not yet cash-flow positive.
*   **Break-even Analysis:** To cover the $35,000 burn solely on the seat model, you need ~715 active seats (assuming no churn). On the instance model (assuming 50 apps/customer), you would need only ~1.4 such customers.

**Assumptions (Labelled ASSUMED):**
*   **Churn Rate:** ASSUMED low (<2% monthly) due to Fortune 500 pilot stickiness, but unverified.
*   **Expansion Revenue:** ASSUMED that enterprises will add seats organically as teams grow (seat model benefit).
*   **Adoption Friction:** ASSUMED that charging per instance will reduce the number of apps deployed by 30-50% (the "discouragement" factor mentioned), effectively capping your upside in the instance model.

**What Flips It, and The Trigger:**
The decision flips if your customers' **developer-to-application ratio** drops significantly. If enterprises begin consolidating many developers onto very few massive applications, the seat model under-monetizes.
*   **Trigger Metric:** Monitor the "Seats per App" ratio. If the average across your top 5 customers exceeds **10 developers per application**, the instance model becomes dangerously under-priced relative to value, or conversely, if it drops below **2 developers per application**, the instance model becomes a barrier to entry.
*   **Rule of Thumb:** If adding a new internal tool costs the customer more than 5% of their total software budget for that department, they will halt deployment.
*   **Step this week:** If your pilot data shows customers deploying fewer than 3 new apps quarter-over-quarter, consider capping the instance fee or hybridizing the model.

**Data to Gather for a Definitive Decision:**
To move from hypothesis to certainty, you must instrument the following in your next pilot review:
1.  **Shadow IT Velocity:** How many *potential* internal tools are currently being built on competing frameworks or spreadsheets because they are "too small" to justify your instance fee?
2.  **Seat Expansion Rate:** In the last 6 months, did your pilot customers add seats organically without a sales push? (Validates the seat model's organic growth).
3.  **Budget Code Attribution:** Ask your Fortune 500 contacts whether "infrastructure/app fees" (instance model) come from a different, harder-to-access budget than "developer tooling/licenses" (seat model). Enterprise sales cycles often stall on CapEx (instances) vs. OpEx (seats) distinctions.
