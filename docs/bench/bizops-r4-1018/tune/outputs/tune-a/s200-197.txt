**The Call:** Accept the $150,000 grant immediately; the capital extends your runway by six months, and the reporting burden can be managed by your current team without hiring dedicated compliance staff or sacrificing product agility.

**The Numbers:**
*   **Runway Extension:** Grant Amount ($150,000) ÷ Monthly Burn ($25,000) = **6 months** of additional operating time.
*   **Cost of Compliance (Estimated):** Assuming a lean internal effort of 10 hours/month at a blended rate of $50/hour (existing staff), the monthly compliance cost is **$500**.
*   **Net Benefit:** $150,000 capital - ($500 × 12 months projected admin) = **$144,000** net value in Year 1.
*   *Assumption (ASSUMED):* You utilize existing staff for reporting rather than hiring externally; reporting frequency is quarterly (standard for such grants).

**What Flips It, and the Trigger:**
The deal turns negative only if the grant mandates **real-time data integration** or **external third-party audits** costing more than $12,500 per quarter (50% of your monthly burn).
*   **Trigger:** If the signed agreement explicitly requires "independent annual financial audits" or "live API data sharing," pause signing and negotiate terms.
*   **Rule of Thumb:** Government education/accessibility grants typically require quarterly narrative and financial reports, not real-time operational control.
*   **Step this week:** Draft a one-page "Reporting Protocol" proposing quarterly PDF summaries of veteran enrollment and completion rates; submit this to the grant officer as your proposed compliance method before signing. If they reject this summary format in favor of complex data feeds, reconsider the fit.

**Routing Note:**
While general grant management is operational, the specific legal interpretation of "clawback" clauses and the binding definition of "allowable expenses" under your specific government jurisdiction is a Tier 3 legal determination. Have a contract attorney review the indemnification and termination sections of the agreement to confirm exactly which failures trigger fund recovery before you sign.

Accepting this funding creates a six-month buffer that outweighs the minor administrative friction of quarterly reporting. The fear of losing agility is valid but manageable; you do not need to build a compliance department. Instead, treat reporting as a lightweight product feature: automate the extraction of "veterans trained" metrics from your existing database once a quarter. This approach satisfies typical government requirements for outcome tracking (did the veterans learn?) without impeding your ability to pivot curriculum content based on user feedback. The trade-off is favorable: you exchange roughly 40 hours of administrative work per year for half a million dollars in annualized runway value.
