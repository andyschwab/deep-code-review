**The Call:** Do not sign the "guaranteed zero breaches" clause as written; it creates an uninsurable, existential risk that likely voids standard cyber liability coverage and exceeds your capacity to pay. You must negotiate this down to a "commercially reasonable efforts" standard with a liability cap tied to your insurance limits or contract value, not unlimited damages.

**The Numbers & Risk Profile:**
*   **Exposure:** The client demands unlimited damages for a breach. A single financial services data breach can easily exceed $10M–$50M in regulatory fines, forensic costs, and customer restitution.
*   **Your Capacity:** $2M general liability cap (likely excludes cyber incidents specifically) vs. potential unlimited liability.
*   **Revenue Concentration:** This deal is 40% of your projected revenue ($400k annual implies ~$160k from this deal). Losing the deal hurts, but signing it risks 100% of the company's future equity and assets.
*   **Insurance Reality:** Standard cyber policies exclude liability assumed under contract that exceeds what you would owe by law (i.e., "assumed liability"). Guaranteeing a result ("zero breaches") rather than a service level ("uptime" or "scan frequency") is typically deemed a "business risk" excluded from coverage.

**What Flips It & The Trigger:**
The deal flips from "walk away" to "negotiate" only if the liability clause is changed from **strict liability for results** to **liability for negligence** with a **financial cap**.
*   **Trigger Value:** Cap liability at the greater of (a) your available insurance proceeds ($2M) or (b) 12 months of fees paid. If they refuse any cap below $5M, the risk/reward math fails for a $160k deal.
*   **Decision Rule:** If the client insists on "indemnify all damages" without a cap, consider the deal toxic regardless of revenue size.
*   **This Week's Step:** If the client pushes back on removing the guarantee, consider offering a service credit model (e.g., 100% refund of monthly fees for a missed critical vulnerability) instead of cash indemnity for downstream damages.
