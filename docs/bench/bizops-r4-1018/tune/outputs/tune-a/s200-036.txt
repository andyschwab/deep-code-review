**The Call**
Do not proceed with the $15,000 certification investment based solely on the current math; the price hike covers the variable cost increase immediately without it, and the certification requires selling an additional ~3,846 units just to break even on the fee, which likely exceeds your 12-month organic growth capacity.

**The Numbers**
*Current State (No Certification):*
- Revenue per unit: $35
- Cost per unit: $12 (increased from $10)
- Margin per unit: $23
- Total Margin (5,000 units): $115,000

*Proposed State (With Certification & Price Hike):*
- Revenue per unit: $42
- Cost per unit: $12 (material) + $3.00 (amortized certification fee per unit over 5,000 units) = $15 effective cost
- Margin per unit: $27
- Total Margin (5,000 units): $135,000
- *Net Gain from Strategy:* $20,000 ($135k - $115k)

*The Break-Even on Certification Fee:*
To cover the $15,000 fixed fee purely through the $7 price premium:
$15,000 / $7 = **2,143 units** must be sold at the new price just to pay for the certificate, before accounting for any potential drop in volume due to price elasticity.
If demand drops by more than 43% (2,143 lost sales out of 5,000), you are worse off than staying at $35 without the label.

*Assumptions (ASSUMED):*
- You maintain 100% of your current 5,000 customer base at the higher price (optimistic).
- The $15,000 is a one-time sunk cost within the 12-month runway.
- No additional marketing spend is required to communicate the new certification value.

**What Flips It, and The Trigger**
The single input that changes this call is **Price Elasticity of Demand**. If your loyal customer base values the *verified* GOTS label enough to absorb the 20% price increase without churning, the strategy works.
*Trigger:* If you cannot pre-sell or commit 2,150 units at $42 within 60 days of announcement, abort the certification spend.
*Rule of Thumb:* In saturated "organic" markets, unverified claims often convert at 80-90% of verified ones for loyal niches, but verified labels can command a 15-25% premium *if* trust is the bottleneck.
*Step this week:* Survey your 5,000 customers with a Van Westendorp price sensitivity meter specifically asking about the value of "GOTS Certified" vs. "Organic Cotton" to quantify the willingness-to-pay delta before spending the $15,000.

**Routing Note**
While the financial math is tier 1, the legal use of the term "organic" on clothing labels in the US is regulated by the USDA National Organic Program (NOP) if you make specific claims; verify with a compliance professional whether your current "organic" marketing language already exposes you to risk without the GOTS certification, as this could force the decision regardless of margins.
