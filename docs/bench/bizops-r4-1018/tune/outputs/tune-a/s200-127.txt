**The Regulatory Landscape & Risk:**
In the U.S., the regulation of veterinary medicine is state-specific, not federal. The core issue is whether recommending a proprietary supplement constitutes the "practice of veterinary medicine."
*   **Strict States:** Approximately 20–25 states legally require an in-person physical exam to establish a VCPR. In these jurisdictions, recommending a specific product to a patient without that prior physical exam is often interpreted as practicing without a license.
*   **The Risk:** If your platform allows a vet in State A (telehealth-friendly) to recommend supplements to a dog in State B (strict VCPR), you are likely violating State B's veterinary practice act. Penalties include cease-and-desist orders, fines ranging from $1,000 to $10,000 per violation, and potential disciplinary action against the recommending veterinarian's license.
*   **High-Risk States (Strict Enforcement):** California, Texas, Florida, New York, and Illinois are known for strict VCPR enforcement requiring physical exams. Other states with historically rigid interpretations include Pennsylvania, Ohio, and Georgia. *Note: This list changes; it is an illustrative baseline, not a real-time compliance map.*
