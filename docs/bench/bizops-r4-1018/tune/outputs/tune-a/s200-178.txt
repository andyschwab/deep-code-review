**The Call**
Do not recognize the $250,000 as immediate revenue; under standard accounting principles (US GAAP), you must amortize these upfront fees over the term of the agreement (10 years) because your material obligations (support, brand licensing, territory protection) continue throughout that period. Recognizing it all now creates a high audit risk and misstates your profitability by inflating current income while ignoring future service costs.

**The Numbers**
The accounting rule requires matching revenue to the period in which the service is performed. Since you retain obligations to the sub-franchisees for 10 years, the fee is earned ratably over that time.

*Formula:* Annual Revenue = Total Upfront Fees / Contract Term (Years)

*Inputs:*
*   Total Fees: $250,000 (Given)
*   Term: 10 Years (Given)
*   *Assumption (ASSUMED):* No significant upfront costs were incurred that exceed the fee (net deferral method).

*Results:*
*   **Immediate Recognition (Incorrect):** $250,000 revenue in Year 1; $0 in Years 2–10.
*   **Amortization (Correct):** $25,000 revenue per year for 10 years.

*Impact on Current Fiscal Year:*
Choosing amortization reduces your reported revenue from this deal by $225,000 in the current year compared to immediate recognition. However, this accurately reflects the liability you carry to support these franchises over the decade. The 4% ongoing royalty is recognized as earned each month and is unaffected by this decision.

**What Flips It, and the Trigger**
The only scenario where immediate recognition might be permissible is if you can prove you have **no** material continuing obligations after the signing (e.g., you sold the rights entirely and provide zero support, training, or brand access).
*   **Trigger Value:** If your legal counsel confirms your post-signing obligations are "perfunctory" (insignificant), you could recognize revenue earlier.
*   **Rule of Thumb:** If you provide an operations manual, training, or territorial exclusivity, you have continuing obligations.
*   **Step this week:** If your franchise agreement lists any ongoing support duties, consider drafting a memo to your accountant detailing those specific clauses to justify the 10-year amortization schedule before closing the books.

**Routing Note**
While the general rule (ASC 606 / FASB Topic 606) strongly favors amortization for franchise fees with continuing obligations, the exact amortization period and treatment of initial direct costs are binding accounting determinations. Bring your signed franchise agreement and a list of all post-signing services you provide to your CPA to finalize the revenue recognition policy and ensure compliance with US GAAP.
