**The Call:** Your current TAM is inflated because you are counting "potential users" (developers) while selling to "paying entities" (organizations); you must reframe your TAM from "number of developers" to "number of developer-seats sold to organizations" or "average revenue per organization," as investors buy revenue logic, not headcount logic.

**The Numbers:**
Your current calculation assumes a 1:1 relationship between a developer and a subscription, which contradicts your own data showing 30% of users span multiple stacks.
*   **Current Flawed TAM:** 2,500,000 developers × $40/month × 12 months = **$1.2 Billion**.
*   **The Distortion:** If 30% of developers overlap across stacks/organizations, counting them as unique seats double-counts the *addressable* subscription slots if your pricing is per-seat, or misrepresents the market if your pricing is per-organization.
*   **Corrected Approach (Seat-Based):** If you sell per-seat, the overlap matters less *if* the organization pays for every seat. However, if one developer uses two stacks within one org, they are still one seat. If they work for two orgs, they are two seats (two customers).
*   **Corrected Approach (Organization-Based - Recommended):** Investors prefer Top-Down TAM based on *companies*, not individuals.
    *   *Assumption (ASSUMED):* Average organization size for your target segment is 20 developers.
    *   *Adjusted Market:* 2,500,000 developers / 20 devs per org = 125,000 organizations.
    *   *Average Contract Value (ACV):* 20 seats × $40 × 12 = $9,600/year.
    *   **Revised TAM:** 125,000 orgs × $9,600 = **$1.2 Billion** (Mathematically similar, but logically defensible).
*   **Unit Economics Check:** With a CAC of $120 and Price of $40/month, your payback period is 3 months ($120 / $40). This is excellent, but only if churn is low. If the "overlap" means one person cancels two stacks, your churn risk is higher.

**What Flips It, and The Trigger:**
The factor that changes the call is your **pricing model definition**. If you charge per *repository* or per *language stack* rather than per *developer seat*, the 30% overlap is actually a revenue multiplier, not a distortion.
*   **Trigger:** If your pricing is per-seat, the 30% overlap is irrelevant noise (one dev = one seat). If your pricing is per-stack, the TAM is potentially *larger* than calculated, not smaller.
*   **Rule of Thumb:** Investors penalize "developer count" TAMs for B2B SaaS because companies, not individuals, write checks.
*   **Step this week:** If your sales contracts are signed by organizations, re-calculate TAM as "(Total Target Organizations) × (Average Seats per Org) × $480". If you cannot find the "Total Target Organizations" number, state your TAM as a percentage of the total software development spend in North America rather than a raw user count.
