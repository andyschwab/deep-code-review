**The Call**
Do not include the opportunity cost of aging inventory in your headline runway calculation for a Series A pitch; doing so will likely signal financial confusion rather than sophistication. Present your cash runway based on actual cash burn ($30k/month), which yields 40 months, and address the inventory capital constraint separately as a working capital metric. Investors evaluate runway as "months until we run out of cash," not "months until we run out of cash plus imputed costs." Mixing non-cash opportunity costs into burn rate obscures your true liquidity position and complicates comparability with peer startups.

**The Numbers**
*Formula:* Runway = Cash Balance / Monthly Cash Burn.
*Your Figures:* $1,200,000 Cash / $30,000 Monthly Ops Cost = **40 months**.

*Co-founder's Proposal (Implicit Cost Inclusion):*
1.  **Inventory Value:** $500,000.
2.  **Opportunity Cost Rate:** 8% annually.
3.  **Annual Implicit Cost:** $500,000 × 0.08 = $40,000.
4.  **Monthly Implicit Cost:** $40,000 / 12 = ~$3,333.
5.  **Adjusted Monthly Burn:** $30,000 (Ops) + $3,333 (Implicit) = $33,333.
6.  **Adjusted Runway:** $1,200,000 / $33,333 = **36 months**.

*Comparison:*
*   Standard Cash Runway: 40 months.
*   "Economic" Runway (with opportunity cost): 36 months.
*   Difference: 4 months.

**What Flips It, and the Trigger**
The input that changes the recommendation is the **investor's specific focus on working capital efficiency** versus **survival time**.
*   *Trigger Rule:* If your pitch narrative relies on proving you can survive >36 months without further funding, the 4-month difference is material. However, if your narrative is about scaling production, the $500k inventory is an asset building future value, not a burn item.
*   *Decision Threshold:* In early-stage SaaS or product pitches, deviating from standard cash-burn runway definitions is rarely rewarded unless the business model is capital-intensive finance or lending. For a CPG brand, inventory is expected.
*   *Actionable Step:* This week, create two slides: Slide A shows "Cash Runway: 40 Months" based on actual burn. Slide B (in the appendix or operations section) shows "Working Capital Cycle," highlighting the 18-month aging process and the $500k tied up, explicitly stating: "We manage an 18-month production cycle requiring $500k in working capital." This demonstrates sophistication without muddying your liquidity story.

**Routing Note**
While this is a strategic presentation choice, ensure your financial model's cash flow statement correctly treats the initial purchase of ingredients/barrels as cash outflows in the month they occur (which you have already done by reflecting the $500k inventory and reduced cash balance). No legal or tax routing is required for how you present this in a pitch deck, but a CPA should verify that your inventory valuation method (FIFO/LIFO/Weighted Average) aligns with how you present asset growth to investors.
