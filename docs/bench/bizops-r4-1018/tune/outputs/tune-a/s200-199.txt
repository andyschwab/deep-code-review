**The Call:** Do not size your market at 5,000 farms; your immediate realistic TAM is the subset with reliable bandwidth, likely 30–50% of the total, unless you pivot to a hybrid model using local hubs. Without adapting delivery, you face a structural revenue leak where 40% of enrolled students generate negative contribution margin due to support costs.

**The Numbers:**
*   **Naive TAM:** 5,000 farms × $2,500 = $12.5M.
*   **Adjusted TAM (Connectivity Constrained):** Let $P$ be the percentage of farms with sufficient bandwidth.
    *   Formula: $TAM_{adj} = 5,000 \times P \times \$2,500$.
    *   *Scenario A (Conservative, 30% connectivity):* 1,500 farms × $2,500 = **$3.75M**.
    *   *Scenario B (Optimistic, 50% connectivity):* 2,500 farms × $2,500 = **$6.25M**.
*   **Revenue Loss & Cost Exposure (if staying online-only):**
    *   Assumed Enrollment: 1,000 students (for illustration).
    *   Dropouts (40%): 400 students.
    *   Lost Revenue: 400 × $2,500 = $1.0M.
    *   Sunk Support Cost: 400 × $300 = **$120,000** (pure loss).
    *   *Unit Economics Impact:* If you refund dropouts, the loss is total. If you don't, the $300 support cost erodes margin from successful students.

**What Flips It, and the Trigger:**
The single input that changes the call is the **availability of offline hubs** (co-ops, community centers, equipment dealers).
*   **Trigger:** If you can secure **3 partner locations** within the target region that offer reliable Wi-Fi and space for proctored downloads/exams, your accessible market ($P$) shifts from ~40% to ~85%.
*   **Rule of Thumb:** If the cost to onboard a hub is less than $5,000, the expansion of TAM justifies the pivot immediately.
*   **Step this week:** Contact the top 5 agricultural co-ops in your target counties; if X (at least 2) express interest in hosting a "SkyAgri Download Station," consider Y (pausing pure online marketing to build a hybrid pilot).
