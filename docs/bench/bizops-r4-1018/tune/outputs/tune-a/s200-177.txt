**The Call**
Your shift to leasing likely reclassifies your revenue from a one-time taxable sale of tangible personal property to a recurring taxable lease of tangible personal property in most US states, but it significantly increases your risk of creating "physical nexus" in every state where a simulator sits, triggering immediate sales tax registration and filing obligations in those jurisdictions.

**The Numbers & Mechanism**
*   **Revenue Character:** Under a sales model, you collect sales tax once on the $40,000 unit price at the point of delivery. Under a lease model, most states (including major aviation hubs like Texas, Florida, and California) treat the monthly lease payment ($10,000 total / 25 units = $400/unit/month) as a recurring taxable event. You must collect sales tax on *every* monthly payment from the lessee, not just the initial delivery.
*   **Nexus Trigger:** Previously, nexus (the obligation to collect tax) likely existed only where you had an office or employees. By placing physical equipment in a state for lease, you have established "physical presence" nexus in every state hosting a unit.
    *   *Assumption (ASSUMED):* Your 25 units are distributed across multiple states. If even one unit is in a state where you have no other presence, that state now has jurisdiction over your tax obligations.
*   **Impact:** Instead of one filing per state where you sell, you now potentially owe monthly or quarterly filings in every state where a unit is located. Failure to register before placing the equipment can result in back taxes and penalties.

**What Flips It, and the Trigger**
*   **The Flip Input:** The specific statutory definition of "tangible personal property" vs. "service" in the destination state. While rare, a few jurisdictions might exempt certain educational or flight-training equipment leases, or treat "true leases" differently from "conditional sales."
*   **The Trigger:** The date the first lease unit was physically installed in a new state.
*   **Rule of Thumb:** If you have hardware in a state, you almost certainly have nexus there.
*   **This Week's Step:** If you have units in more than three states, consider halting new deployments until a tax professional maps your current footprint against state-specific "flight simulator" exemptions.

**Routing Note (High-Stakes / Jurisdiction-Specific)**
This is a Tier 3 determination requiring a licensed CPA or tax attorney. You must answer: "In which specific states do our 25 leased units create physical nexus, and do any states offer exemptions for aviation training devices under lease?" Do not attempt to self-register without confirming if your leases are classified as "true leases" or "financing arrangements," as this changes the tax base. The time-sensitive element is the potential accrual of penalties for failing to register immediately upon placing equipment in a state.
