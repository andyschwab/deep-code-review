**The Call**
Do not alter your fee structure, but immediately implement explicit, multi-point disclosure statements on your submission portal and in author agreements; the risk is reputational and governed by self-regulatory codes (COPE, DOAJ) rather than a specific EU statute banning APCs, provided peer review remains blind to payment status.

**The Numbers**
Your current unit economics show a healthy surplus, meaning financial pressure to accept papers for revenue is mathematically unnecessary, which supports your integrity defense.
*   **Monthly Revenue:** 50 submissions × €1,200 APC = €60,000 (ASSUMED: 100% acceptance/conversion for top-line potential; if acceptance is lower, revenue scales down).
*   **Monthly Operating Cost:** €25,000 (Given).
*   **Monthly Surplus:** €60,000 - €25,000 = €35,000.
*   **Break-even Submissions:** €25,000 / €1,200 ≈ 21 submissions/month.
*   **Analysis:** You only need 21 accepted papers to cover costs. With 50 submissions, you can reject up to 58% of papers and remain profitable. This mathematical buffer is your primary evidence against "pay-to-publish" accusations.

**What Flips It, and the Trigger**
The single input that flips this from "standard business" to "predatory behavior" is the **correlation between payment ability and acceptance rate**.
*   **Trigger:** If your acceptance rate for funded authors exceeds your acceptance rate for unfunded (or waiver-seeking) authors by more than 15%, or if editors have access to payment status *before* making an acceptance decision.
*   **Rule of Thumb:** Reputational damage occurs when the market perceives that €1,200 buys acceptance, not just open access.
*   **Actionable Step:** If you have not already, implement a "firewall" in your editorial management system this week so that editors and reviewers never see an author's payment status until *after* the final acceptance decision is recorded.

**Routing Note**
While no specific EU *statute* mandates a particular disclosure text for APCs, the **European Code of Conduct for Research Integrity** and guidelines from the **Committee on Publication Ethics (COPE)** function as the binding standard for legitimacy.
*   **Question for Counsel/Compliance Officer:** "Does our current waiver policy and disclosure language meet the specific criteria for DOAJ (Directory of Open Access Journals) inclusion and Plan S compliance, given our German jurisdiction?"
*   **Time-Sensitive:** If you seek Plan S compliance (required for many EU grants), your disclosure and waiver policies must be updated immediately to avoid disqualifying your authors from funding.

**Specific Disclosure Obligations**
You must explicitly state:
1.  **Separation of Duties:** "Payment is requested only after final acceptance; editors are blinded to payment ability during review."
2.  **Waiver Policy:** Clear criteria for fee waivers for authors without funding (mandatory for ethical standing).
3.  **Usage of Funds:** A brief statement that APCs cover editorial management, hosting, and indexing, not the "sale" of the paper.

Current self-regulatory codes are sufficient *if* strictly followed; statutory scrutiny arises only if practices drift into deceptive trade practices under German/EU consumer law by implying a service (acceptance) is guaranteed by payment.
