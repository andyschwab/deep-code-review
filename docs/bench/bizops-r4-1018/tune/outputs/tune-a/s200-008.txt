**The Call:** Reject the current structure immediately; at a $32 upfront loss and $4 monthly revenue, you break even in 8 months, leaving only 10 months of profit before churn. This yields an LTV:CAC ratio of 1.4:1, which destroys value. You must either raise the hardware price, slash CAC, or increase ARPU to survive.

**The Numbers:**
*   **Upfront Loss:** $35 (CAC) + $22 (COGS) - $25 (Price) = **$32 loss per user**.
*   **Gross Margin per Month:** **$4** (Assumed: 100% margin on digital revenue).
*   **Lifetime Revenue:** 18 months × $4 = **$72**.
*   **Lifetime Value (LTV):** $72 (Revenue) - $32 (Upfront Loss) = **$40 Net LTV**.
*   **Current LTV:CAC Ratio:** $40 (Net LTV) / $35 (CAC) = **1.14:1** (Note: If measuring LTV as gross revenue $72 vs CAC $35, ratio is 2.05:1, but net cash flow is the binding constraint here).
*   **Break-Even Point:** $32 (Loss) / $4 (Monthly Margin) = **8 months**.

**What Flips It, and The Trigger:**
The single input that changes the call is **Monthly Recurring Revenue (MRR)**.
*   **Trigger:** If MRR does not exceed **$5.33** by month 6, or if CAC is not reduced below **$24**, the model fails to hit a 3:1 ratio within 24 months.
*   **Target CAC for 3:1 Ratio:** To achieve a 3:1 LTV:CAC ratio over 24 months (assuming 18-month life):
    *   Target Net LTV must be 3 × CAC.
    *   Formula: $(24 \text{ months} \times \$4) - (\text{CAC} + \$22 - \$25) = 3 \times \text{CAC}$.
    *   Simplified: $\$96 - \text{CAC} + \$3 = 3 \times \text{CAC}$.
    *   $\$99 = 4 \times \text{CAC}$.
    *   **Maximum Acceptable CAC:** **$24.75**.
*   **Action:** If your CAC remains above $25 next month, consider pausing paid acquisition and shifting to organic channels or increasing the bottle price to $35 to eliminate the upfront subsidy.
