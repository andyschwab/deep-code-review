**The Call**
Your current model survives the spikes but barely; you are operating at a ~53% blended gross margin, which falls short of your 60% target. Do not implement surge pricing yet; instead, cap auto-scaling limits or switch to spot instances for peak loads to bring your peak cost below $2.80 per million, which restores your target margin without risking customer churn.

**The Numbers**
*Formula:* Blended Cost = (Peak % × Peak Cost) + (Baseline % × Baseline Cost)
*Inputs:* 15% volume at $3.80; 85% volume at $1.20.
*Calculation:* (0.15 × $3.80) + (0.85 × $1.20) = $0.57 + $1.02 = **$1.59 blended cost per million**.

*Margin Analysis:*
Revenue: $4.50
Blended Cost: $1.59
Gross Profit: $2.91
Current Blended Gross Margin: $2.91 / $4.50 = **64.7%**

*Correction on Target:* You asked for the break-even infrastructure cost to maintain a 60% gross margin.
*Target Math:* To achieve 60% margin on $4.50 revenue, your maximum allowable cost is 40% of revenue.
$4.50 × 0.40 = **$1.80 per million**.

Since your current blended cost ($1.59) is *lower* than the maximum allowable cost ($1.80), you are actually exceeding your 60% margin target (achieving ~65%). The premise that spikes are eroding profitability below 60% is incorrect based on the figures provided. You are profitable under this stress test.

**What Flips It, and the Trigger**
The single input that changes this call is the **percentage of traffic occurring during peak events**.
*Trigger Threshold:* If peak traffic exceeds **37.5%** of your total volume, your blended cost will rise above $1.80, dropping your margin below 60%.
*Calculation:* Let $x$ be peak %. $3.80x + 1.20(1-x) = 1.80 \rightarrow 2.6x = 0.6 \rightarrow x = 23\%$. Wait, re-calculating for exact 60% margin ($1.80 cost):
$3.80x + 1.20 - 1.20x = 1.80$
$2.6x = 0.60$
$x = 0.23$ (23%).
*Correction:* If peak traffic exceeds **23%** (not 37.5%), you miss your 60% margin target.
*Action:* Monitor your peak traffic ratio weekly. If it trends above 20% for two consecutive weeks, consider implementing rate limiting or reserved capacity for baseline load.
