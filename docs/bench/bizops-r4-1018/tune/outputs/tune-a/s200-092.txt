**The Call:** Do not settle immediately for the full $450,000; your $50,000 liability cap provides a strong initial defense in a UK court, but its enforceability hinges entirely on whether a judge deems the cap "reasonable" under the Unfair Contract Terms Act 1977 given the specific negligence alleged. The fact that the experiment was scheduled and approved by the client's CTO significantly strengthens your position that the risk was accepted, but it does not automatically immunize you against a finding of gross negligence or a ruling that the cap is unreasonable for the service provided.

**The Numbers:**
*   **Exposure Gap:** $450,000 (Claim) - $50,000 (Cap) = **$400,000 at risk**.
*   **Runway Impact:** $90,000 monthly burn. A $400,000 loss equals **4.4 months of runway**, effectively ending operations if paid.
*   **Contract Ratio:** Cap ($50k) is **3x** Annual Contract Value (implied ~$16.7k).
*   **ASSUMED:** Legal defense costs for a UK commercial trial are **ASSUMED** to be between £150,000–£300,000 ($190k–$380k) based on typical complex commercial litigation, which alone could consume 2–4 months of your runway even if you win on the cap.

**What Flips It, and the Trigger:**
The single input that flips the call from "fight" to "settle" is the **specific nature of the negligence**. If the client can prove "gross negligence" (a fundamental breach of duty beyond simple error) or if the court rules the 3x ACV cap is "unreasonable" for a service managing production stability, the cap fails.
*   **Trigger:** If preliminary legal counsel advises that the specific failure mode (e.g., a known bug ignored, vs. an edge-case failure) rises to gross negligence, **pivot immediately to settlement negotiations** before discovery costs mount.
*   **Rule of Thumb:** In UK B2B contracts, caps between 1x–2x contract value are common; 3x is favorable to you but not immune to challenge.
*   **Step this week:** If the CTO's written approval explicitly acknowledges the risk of outage, **consider sending a formal letter reiterating that acceptance** to frame the narrative before lawyers exchange pleadings.
