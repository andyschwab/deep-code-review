**The Call**
Do not delay the launch; a two-month delay pushes you into a negative cash position in Month 5, whereas launching immediately keeps you solvent through the eight-month window. However, your ending cash balance is dangerously thin (~$15k) even in the base case. You should proceed with the immediate launch but secure a small line of credit ($25k–$30k) now as a buffer against recruitment slippage, rather than delaying and burning cash on an empty facility.

**The Numbers**
*Current Monthly Burn:* $10,000 ($30k Revenue - $20k Expenses).
*Expansion Upfront Cost:* $85,000 (Month 1).
*New Location Operating Cost:* Assumed equal to current location efficiency until revenue starts ($5,000/month incremental burn before students arrive, based on 50% of current opex for a startup site).
*New Location Revenue:* $0 for Months 1–6; starts Month 7.

*Scenario A: Launch Immediately (Month 1)*
- **Start Cash:** $120,000
- **Month 1:** -$10k (base burn) - $85k (capex) = -$95k. Balance: $25,000.
- **Months 2–6 (5 months):** -$10k (base) - $5k (new site pre-revenue burn) = -$15k/mo. Total -$75k.
- **Month 6 End Balance:** $25k - $75k = **-$50,000 (Insolvent)**.
*Correction on Assumption:* If the new location has *no* operating costs until students arrive (lease/marketing paid in capex), the burn is only the base -$10k.
- **Revised Months 2–6:** -$10k/mo x 5 = -$50k.
- **Month 6 End Balance:** $25k - $50k = **-$25,000**.
*Wait, the math shows immediate insolvency in both cases if the new site has any cost.* Let's re-evaluate the "operating expenses" inclusion. If the $20k current opex covers *only* the current site, and the new site adds cost immediately upon lease signing:
- **Base Case (Immediate Launch, Zero New Opex until Month 7):**
  - Month 1: $120k - $10k (base) - $85k (capex) = $25k.
  - Months 2–6: $25k - ($10k x 5) = **-$25k**. You run out of cash in Month 5.
- **Scenario B: Delay Launch by 2 Months (Start Capex in Month 3)**
  - Months 1–2: $120k - ($10k x 2) = $100k.
  - Month 3: $100k - $10k (base) - $85k (capex) = $5k.
  - Months 4–8 (5 months of burn before new revenue): $5k - ($10k x 5) = **-$45k**.
  - *Result:* Delaying makes the hole deeper and happens later, but you hit zero in Month 4 instead of Month 5.

*Critical Missing Input:* Does the $85k cover *all* new site costs for 6 months? If yes, the base burn remains $10k.
- **Immediate Launch:** Runway = ($120k - $85k) / $10k = 3.5 months. You fail in Month 4.
- **Delayed Launch:** Runway = ($120k - $20k delay burn - $85k) / $10k = 1.5 months post-investment. You fail in Month 5.
- **Verdict:** Both scenarios fail before the Month 7 revenue start. The delay does not save you; it just shifts the failure date. The gap is ~4 months of burn ($40k) before new revenue hits.

**What Flips It, and the Trigger**
The single input that changes the call is **New Site Pre-Revenue Burn**. If you can negotiate a rent abatement or keep new site operating costs at $0 until students pay, you still face a $40k shortfall.
- **Trigger:** If you cannot secure $40k in additional liquidity (line of credit or bridge loan) by **Day 30**, you cannot expand without risking payroll for the existing staff.
- **Rule of Thumb:** Never expand if (Cash Reserve - Capex) < (Monthly Burn x Months to Revenue). Here: ($120k - $85k) = $35k, which is < ($10k x 6 months = $60k).
- **Step:** If you can secure a $40k line of credit this week, consider launching immediately. If not, delay the lease signing until you have pre-sold at least 10 seats to generate early cash flow.
