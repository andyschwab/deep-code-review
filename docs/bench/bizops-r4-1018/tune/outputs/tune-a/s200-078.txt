**The Call:** Reject the $25M cap as currently structured; at 4% monthly churn and slowing growth, your implied LTV:CAC is likely below the 3:1 threshold required for Series A, making the valuation mathematically unsupported without immediate churn reduction or proof of superior net revenue retention.

**The Numbers:**
To defend a valuation, you must prove unit economics, not just top-line growth.
*   **Annual Churn:** 4% monthly $\approx$ 39% annual churn ($1 - 0.96^{12}$).
*   **Implied Average Lifespan:** $1 / 0.04$ months = 25 months (approx. 2 years).
*   **LTV Formula:** $(\text{ARPA} \times \text{Gross Margin}) / \text{Monthly Churn}$.
    *   *Assumption (ASSUMED):* Gross Margin is 80% (standard for SaaS).
    *   *Assumption (ASSUMED):* ARPA is derived from $800k ARR / 12 months. If you have ~50 customers, ARPA $\approx$ $1,333.
    *   *Calculation:* $(\$1,333 \times 0.80) / 0.04 = \$26,660$ LTV per customer.
*   **The Benchmark:** Series A investors typically require an LTV:CAC ratio of >3:1 and payback periods <12 months.
    *   *Implication:* Your CAC must be under ~$8,800. If your sales cycle involves heavy engineering proof-of-concepts (common in distributed tracing), your CAC likely exceeds this, breaking the model.
*   **Runway Check:** $800k ARR $\approx$ $67k MRR. Burn is $90k. Net burn is $23k/month *if* growth covers new costs, but with slowing growth, you are likely burning closer to the full $90k against fixed costs. $90k burn / $67k MRR indicates you are not yet covering cost of revenue + OpEx, validating the 6-month runway claim only if cash on hand is ~$540k.

**What Flips It, and the Trigger:**
The single input that changes the call is **Net Revenue Retention (NRR)**. If your expansion revenue (upsells/cross-sells) from existing customers exceeds the 4% logo churn, your dollar-based retention could still be >100%, which justifies a higher cap.
*   **Trigger:** If NRR < 110% by your next board meeting (30 days out), the $25M cap is unreachable in the current market.
*   **Step:** This week, segment your churn. If the 4% churn is concentrated in small accounts while large accounts expand, model a "tiered churn" scenario to present to investors. If churn is uniform across all tiers, consider YC's rule of thumb: do not raise until monthly churn is <2%.
