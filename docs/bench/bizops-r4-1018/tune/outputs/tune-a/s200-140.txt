**The Call**
Adopt the 15% success-fee model but implement a "net revenue" recognition policy with an explicit refund reserve; do not defer all revenue, but do not recognize gross fees without an offset. This approach satisfies auditors for Series A while capturing upside, provided you formalize the refund probability in your accounting policies. Standard legal templates are insufficient for the *accounting* treatment; you require a specialized finance advisor or CPA to set the reserve methodology, though lawyers can draft the refund clauses.

**The Numbers**
*Current Baseline:* £3,000 flat fee × 60 placements = £180,000/quarter (£60,000/month).
*Proposed Model (Gross):* £6,000 avg fee × 60 placements = £360,000/quarter potential.
*Refund Scenario (20% incidence):*
Assumption: A "partial refund" implies returning 50% of the fee on failed guarantees (standard industry practice for partial failure).
Refund Cost = 60 placements × 20% failure rate × (£6,000 × 50% refund) = £36,000/quarter.
*Net Recognized Revenue:* £360,000 (Gross) - £36,000 (Reserve) = £324,000/quarter (£108,000/month).
*Volatility Impact:* If refund rates fluctuate by ±5% (15% to 25% failure), the quarterly reserve swings by £18,000 (£6,000/month). This creates a ±5.5% variance in reported net revenue, which is manageable but must be disclosed.

**What Flips It, and the Trigger**
The critical input is the **refund percentage defined in the contract**, not just the failure rate. If contracts mandate 100% refunds on any guarantee claim rather than partial, the reserve requirement doubles to £72,000/quarter, reducing net revenue to £288,000 and increasing volatility.
*Trigger:* If your actual refund rate exceeds 25% for two consecutive months, pause the 15% model and revert to a hybrid (lower base + lower percentage) to stabilize cash flow.
*Action:* This week, draft a "Revenue Recognition Policy" memo estimating the refund reserve based on your historical 20% failure rate and present it to your auditor before signing new employer contracts.

**Routing Note**
Determining the exact reserve percentage under UK GAAP (FRS 102) or IFRS 15 is a binding accounting determination; bring your historical refund data to a UK-qualified CPA to finalize the "variable consideration" constraint. Legal templates can define the refund *obligation*, but only a finance advisor can dictate how to *reserve* for it on the balance sheet.
