**The Call**
Your recurring revenue structure justifies targeting a 6x–8x revenue multiple, significantly higher than the 1x–2x typical of traditional toy hardware companies, provided you can prove churn is low and lifetime value (LTV) exceeds 3x your acquisition cost. Do not anchor negotiations on the multiple alone; anchor them on the predictability of your £144,000 Monthly Recurring Revenue (MRR) and your path to expanding margins beyond the current 4%.

**The Numbers**
*Current Snapshot:*
*   **MRR:** £144,000 (3,200 users × £45).
*   **Annual Recurring Revenue (ARR):** £1,728,000.
*   **Customer Acquisition Cost (CAC):** £60.
*   **Gross Margin Estimate:** *ASSUMED* at 50% (typical for hardware subs with shipping), implying £72,000 monthly gross profit.
*   **Net Margin:** 4% (£5,760/month), indicating high operating leverage potential.

*Valuation Scenarios (Revenue Multiple Approach):*
*   **Traditional Hardware Multiple (1.5x):** £2.59M valuation. (Baseline if viewed as a toy seller).
*   **Target SaaS/Subscription Multiple (6x):** £10.37M valuation.
*   **Aggressive Subscription Multiple (8x):** £13.82M valuation.

*Unit Economics Health Check:*
*   **LTV:CAC Ratio:** To justify a 6x+ multiple, investors typically expect an LTV:CAC ratio > 3.0.
    *   Required LTV: £180+ (£60 CAC × 3).
    *   Implied Minimum Lifespan: 4 months (at £45 revenue/month, before margin). With a 50% margin, you need a customer to stay ~8 months just to return 3x gross profit relative to CAC.
    *   *Assumption:* If your monthly churn is under 5% (average lifespan >20 months), your LTV is ~£900 revenue, creating an LTV:CAC of 15x, which strongly supports an 8x multiple. If churn is >15%, the multiple collapses toward the hardware baseline.

**What Flips It, and the Trigger**
The single input that changes the call from "premium valuation" to "commodity hardware" is **Churn Rate**.
*   **Trigger:** If your monthly churn exceeds 8% (average customer life <12.5 months), the acquirer will discount the multiple toward 3x–4x because the revenue is not "stable."
*   **Rule of Thumb:** Subscription multiples compress rapidly when net revenue retention falls below 100%.
*   **Actionable Step:** If your churn is currently unknown or untracked, calculate the cohort retention for the last 6 months this week; if it is below 90% monthly retention, prioritize a "win-back" campaign before entering term sheet negotiations to stabilize the metric.

**Routing Note**
While the math supports a higher multiple, the legal structure of the sale (asset vs. share deal) and the treatment of existing subscriber contracts under UK/EU consumer protection laws during a transfer of ownership require specific legal counsel to ensure the "recurring" nature of the revenue survives the acquisition intact.
