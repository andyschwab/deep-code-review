**The Call**
Recognize the full $4,500 per bike as revenue at the point of sale (delivery), not over the 12-month financing period. The interest subsidy you absorb is recorded as a sales and marketing expense (or a reduction of revenue, depending on specific policy election), but it does not defer the recognition of the underlying product revenue.

**The Numbers**
*   **Total Contract Value:** $4,500 per unit.
*   **Volume:** 200 units in Q1.
*   **Gross Revenue Recognition (Q1):** $4,500 × 200 = **$900,000**.
*   **Interest Subsidy Cost (ASSUMED):** Since the specific market interest rate is not provided, we label this *ASSUMED*. If the lender's standard rate is 8% APR, the subsidy cost is approximately $360 per bike ($4,500 × 8% × 1 year).
    *   *Low Estimate (5% rate):* ~$225/unit → $45,000 total expense.
    *   *Base Estimate (8% rate):* ~$360/unit → $72,000 total expense.
    *   *High Estimate (12% rate):* ~$540/unit → $108,000 total expense.
*   **Net Impact on P&L:** You book $900,000 in revenue immediately. You simultaneously book the calculated subsidy amount as an expense in the same quarter.

**What Flips It, and the Trigger**
The only factor that changes this call is if the financing arrangement includes a separate, distinct service obligation (e.g., free maintenance for 12 months bundled inseparably with the loan) that has standalone value.
*   **Trigger:** If the contract explicitly allocates a portion of the $4,500 to a future service rather than the bike itself, you must defer that specific portion.
*   **Rule of Thumb:** If the customer could buy the bike without the service but cannot get the service without the bike, the entire $4,500 is likely product revenue.
*   **Action Step:** If your legal team confirms the contract separates the bike sale from any ongoing services, consider updating your revenue policy memo this week to explicitly classify the interest subsidy as a "sales incentive expense" to ensure consistent board reporting.

**Routing Note**
While the general rule under ASC 606 (US GAAP) and IFRS 15 is clear regarding point-of-sale recognition for goods, the specific classification of the interest subsidy (contra-revenue vs. operating expense) and the precise calculation of the fair value of the financing benefit require a binding determination by your CPA. Bring the draft financing agreement to your auditor to confirm the journal entry structure before the board meeting.
