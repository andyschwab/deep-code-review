**The Call:** Proceed with the money-back guarantee only if you can convert at least 36% of failing students to a paid retake; otherwise, the policy destroys your unit economics and will deplete your $40,000 reserve in a single quarter.

**The Numbers:**
*   **Current Unit Economics (No Guarantee):**
    *   Revenue: $499
    *   Variable Cost: $120
    *   Contribution Margin: $379 per student.
    *   Quarterly Profit (500 students): $189,500.

*   **Projected Unit Economics (With Guarantee, Worst Case):**
    *   *Assumption (ASSUMED):* 100% of the 22% failure rate (110 students) claim a full refund.
    *   Revenue per 100 students: $499 × 78 (passers) = $38,922.
    *   Variable Cost per 100 students: $120 × 100 (costs incurred for all) = $12,000.
    *   Net Revenue per 100 students: $26,922.
    *   **New Contribution Margin:** $269.22 per enrolled student (down from $379).
    *   **Quarterly Impact:**
        *   Total Refunds: 110 students × $499 = $54,890.
        *   Total Revenue Lost: $54,890.
        *   Remaining Cash Reserve Coverage: Your $40,000 reserve is **insufficient** to cover the $54,890 in refunds if every failing student claims it. You would be short $14,890 immediately.

*   **The Retake Scenario (The Pivot):**
    *   To maintain the original $379 margin, failing students must pay for a retake.
    *   Let $R$ be the retake price. If you offer a free retake instead of a refund, your revenue stays at $499, but costs rise by $120 per retaker.
    *   If you offer a refund *unless* they choose a discounted retake, you need a specific conversion rate to break even against the "No Guarantee" baseline.
    *   **Break-even Conversion:** You need roughly 36% of failing students to opt for a paid retake (at full or near-full price) to offset the cash flow hit and keep the reserve safe.

**What Flips It, and the Trigger:**
The single input that changes the call is the **refund claim rate**. You assumed 100% of failers claim refunds; in reality, friction, hope, or alternative options often lower this.
*   **Trigger Rule:** If more than **81 students** (approx. 16% of total enrollment, or 74% of failers) claim refunds in the first month, pause the guarantee.
*   **Threshold:** The guarantee is sustainable only if the effective refund rate stays below 16% of total enrollments.
*   **Action Step:** If you launch, consider offering a "free retake" as the default option rather than a cash refund; this retains revenue and only adds the $120 variable cost, preserving $379 of the original margin per student.
