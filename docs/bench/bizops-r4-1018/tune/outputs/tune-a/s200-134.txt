**The Call:** Do not pivot to the government contract model with your current cash position; you face a near-certain liquidity crisis within 90 days. You require **$140,000** in immediate additional working capital just to survive the standard transition, and your current runway is insufficient to absorb even a single delayed payment cycle.

**The Numbers:**
*   **Current Cash on Hand:** $35,000 (burn) × 6 (months) = **$210,000**.
*   **Transition Gap Calculation:**
    *   New Monthly Burn (ASSUMED constant): **$35,000**.
    *   Financing Period Required: **3 months** (to cover operations before first payment).
    *   Total Cash Needed for Transition: $35,000 × 3 = **$105,000**.
    *   *Correction for Revenue Timing:* Since revenue jumps to $140k but arrives at Net-90, you generate $0 cash inflow for the first quarter.
    *   **Total Working Capital Gap:** You need to fund 3 months of burn ($105k) PLUS maintain enough buffer to avoid insolvency if the first payment is late.
    *   **Immediate Shortfall:** You have $210k. You need $105k for the first quarter. This leaves $105k buffer. However, you must also fund the *second* quarter's operations before the *second* payment arrives if the first is delayed.
    *   **Refined Gap:** To safely bridge 3 months of zero inflow: **$105,000** is the absolute minimum.
    *   **Risk Scenario (20% delay chance):** If payment arrives at Day 120 (Net-90 + 30 delay), you need 4 months of burn coverage.
    *   4 Months Burn: $35,000 × 4 = **$140,000**.
    *   **Additional Capital Needed for Risk Buffer:** $140,000 (required for delay) - $105,000 (base need) = **$35,000 extra**.

**What Flips It, and the Trigger:**
The single input that changes this from "risky" to "fatal" is the **payment realization date**. Your break-even on cash flow occurs only if payments arrive exactly on Day 90.
*   **Trigger:** If the government payment cycle exceeds **95 days**, your remaining cash buffer ($210k start - $105k burn = $105k left) will be consumed by the delay, leaving you unable to pay staff or suppliers in Month 4.
*   **Rule of Thumb:** Never accept Net-90 terms if your cash runway is less than 4.5x your monthly burn.
*   **Step this week:** If your current line of credit limit is under **$150,000**, consider pausing the pivot and negotiating 50% upfront milestone payments with the procurement office instead of pure Net-90.

**Routing Note:**
Government procurement contracts often contain specific "assignment of proceeds" clauses that may restrict your ability to use invoices as collateral for bridge financing; bring your draft contract to a commercial finance lawyer to verify if your line of credit can legally be drawn against these specific receivables before approaching lenders. A forensic accountant is not required for the projection itself, but a CPA should validate the cash flow model for the lender's due diligence package.
