**The Call**
Reject the pure leasing pivot as structured; it collapses your immediate cash inflow by ~82% per unit, cutting your runway from ~6.5 months to under 3 months unless you secure upfront lease financing. The recurring revenue model improves long-term valuation but creates an immediate cash trough that contradicts your goal of delaying fundraising.

**The Numbers**
*Current Model (Outright Sale):*
Margin per unit = $2,800 (Price) - $950 (Cost) = **$1,850**.
Monthly Cash Inflow (150 units) = 150 × $2,850 = **$277,500**.
Net Monthly Burn = $185,000 (OpEx) - $277,500 (Inflow) = **-$92,500** (Net Cash Positive).
*Result:* You are currently cash-flow positive; runway is effectively infinite until volume drops or costs rise.

*Proposed Model (Lease):*
Month 1 Cash per unit = $500 (Setup) - $950 (Cost) = **-$450** (Immediate cash loss per acquisition).
Recurring Margin = $120/mo (Revenue) - $0 (variable cost assumed) = $120.
Churn Impact: With 4% monthly churn, the average customer lifetime is ~25 months.
Lifetime Value (LTV) ≈ $120 × 25 = $3,000.
*Cash Flow Reality:* In Month 1, acquiring 150 patients costs you **$67,500** in hardware cash drag (150 × -$450) before collecting recurring fees. Even with full recurring revenue from a growing base, the initial hardware subsidy creates a massive hole.

*Break-Even Analysis:*
Leasing only matches the current model's immediate cash generation if the **Setup Fee ≥ $2,800** (impossible for a lease) or if you sell the lease receivables (factoring) at ~90% value immediately. Without factoring, you do not break even on cash flow; you accelerate cash consumption.

**What Flips It, and the Trigger**
The single input that changes this call is **Access to Debt Financing (Lease Factoring)**. If you can sell the lease contracts to a bank for 85%+ of their face value upfront, you can recoup the hardware cost.
*Trigger:* If you cannot secure a debt facility covering at least $950 per unit within 30 days, **do not switch models**.
*Rule of Thumb:* Never subsidize hardware with equity cash unless your LTV:CAC > 5x and you have non-dilutive debt available.
*This Week's Step:* If your cost of capital (debt rate) is <12%, consider a pilot with 10 units funded by a line of credit; if >12% or unavailable, stick to outright sales.

**Routing Note**
Presenting lease receivables as "recurring revenue" without disclosing the hardware subsidy and churn risk in a pitch deck triggers securities disclosure obligations. Before pitching this to the board or investors, consult a **CFO or fundraising advisor** to model the "cash trough" depth and verify if the lease structure qualifies as a security or requires specific debt covenants.
