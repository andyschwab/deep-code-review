**The Call:** You must register for and collect Canadian GST/HST and applicable provincial sales tax (PST/QST) immediately upon making your first taxable sale to a Canadian consumer; there is no revenue threshold or "small supplier" exemption for non-resident sellers of tangible goods like furniture. Do not wait for 50 orders or a specific dollar amount to begin compliance, as liability attaches from the first transaction.

**The Numbers & Rules:**
Your $450 average order value (AOV) does not trigger an exemption; in fact, it confirms every shipment is a taxable supply. For non-residents selling tangible personal property (like chairs and tables) delivered into Canada, the "small supplier" registration threshold (currently $30,000 CAD over four quarters) generally does not apply to you in the same way it does for domestic Canadian businesses once you are engaged in commercial activity in Canada. Most provinces and the federal government require non-residents to register before the first sale if they are carrying on business in Canada, which includes soliciting orders and delivering goods.

*   **Ontario:** Requires collection of Harmonized Sales Tax (HST) at 13%. This is a federal/provincial combined tax. You must register for a GST/HST account with the Canada Revenue Agency (CRA).
*   **British Columbia:** Requires collection of GST (5%) plus Provincial Sales Tax (PST) at 7%. These are often administered separately; you must register for GST with the CRA and PST with the BC Ministry of Finance.
*   **Quebec:** Requires collection of GST (5%) plus Quebec Sales Tax (QST) at 9.975%. You must register for both with the CRA and Revenu Québec. Note that Quebec has strict rules requiring non-resident vendors to register for QST if they make taxable supplies in the province.

**Furniture Specifics:** Furniture is generally a standard-rated taxable good in all three provinces. Your $450 price point does not qualify for any low-value import exemptions for B2C sales where the vendor is responsible for collection at checkout.

**What Flips It:** The only variable that changes this call is if you use a marketplace facilitator (like Amazon.ca) that is legally mandated to collect and remit the tax on your behalf. If you sell directly via your own website (ModularLiving.com), the obligation is yours. The trigger is the **first sale**, not a revenue volume.

**Immediate Steps for 50 Orders:**
1.  **Register Immediately:** Apply for a GST/HST account with the CRA and separate PST/QST accounts for BC and Quebec before shipping the first of the 50 orders.
2.  **Configure Checkout:** Update your e-commerce platform to calculate and collect the specific tax rates for ON (13%), BC (12% total), and QC (14.975% total) based on the shipping address.
3.  **Fiscal Representative:** You likely do not need a local fiscal representative for GST/HST, but you must post security (a deposit) with the CRA if you are a non-resident registrant, unless you meet specific criteria. Quebec may have different security requirements.

**Routing Note:** This is a binding tax compliance determination involving cross-border registration and potential security deposits. You must engage a Canadian CPA or international tax attorney to finalize your registration filings, confirm security deposit requirements, and ensure your terms of sale correctly transfer import duties (if applicable) to the customer.
