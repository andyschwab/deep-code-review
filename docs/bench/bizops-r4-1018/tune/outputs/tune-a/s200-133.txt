**The Call:** Do not switch to a flat £45 unlimited subscription if insurer reimbursement is your primary revenue goal; the per-session (£12) model is the only structure that aligns with current UK and German private insurer requirements for "billable medical events," whereas unlimited bundling will likely categorize your service as non-reimbursable wellness.

**The Numbers:**
*   **Per-Session Model:** Revenue = £12 × Sessions. Cost = £0.50 × Sessions. Margin = £11.50 per session.
    *   *Reimbursement Viability:* High. Matches fee-for-service codes.
*   **Subscription Model:** Revenue = £45/month. Cost = £0.50 × Sessions.
    *   *Break-even Usage:* 90 sessions/month (£45 / £0.50).
    *   *Reimbursement Viability:* Low/Zero. Insurers cannot audit individual "medical necessity" within an unlimited bundle.
*   **Assumption (ASSUMED):** Average user frequency is 4 sessions/month.
    *   *Per-Session Revenue:* £48/month.
    *   *Subscription Revenue:* £45/month.
    *   *Result:* Per-session yields higher top-line revenue *and* qualifies for reimbursement; subscription caps revenue and disqualifies reimbursement.

**What Flips It, and The Trigger:**
The single input that changes this call is the **insurer's definition of "bundled preventive care."** If UK/German regulators issue a new directive allowing fixed-fee "chronic care management" bundles for digital therapeutics (DTx), the subscription model becomes viable.
*   **Trigger:** Monitor the UK's NICE (National Institute for Health and Care Excellence) and Germany's BfArM (Federal Institute for Drugs and Medical Devices) for "DiGA" or DTx bundle approvals.
