**The Call:** Immediately restrict the free tier via credit limits or hard caps; the current model is mathematically insolvent because your infrastructure cost per user vastly exceeds any realistic lifetime value (LTV) from conversion. You are subsidizing non-paying load testers, not acquiring qualified leads.

**The Numbers:**
Your unit economics are currently inverted.
*   **Revenue:** $3,500/mo (120 users × $29).
*   **Cost:** $30,000/mo.
*   **Net Burn:** -$26,500/mo (excluding other opex).
*   **Cost Per Free User (ASSUMED):** With 4,880 free users (5,000 total - 120 paid), if we attribute the majority of the $30k bill to free-tier load testing, your cost to serve a free user is roughly **$6.15/month** ($30,000 / 4,880), while they pay $0.
*   **Break-Even Conversion Requirement:** To cover just the $30,000 infrastructure bill at $29/mo, you need **1,035 paid users**. You currently have 120. You would need to convert **21%** of your entire user base immediately, whereas your current conversion rate is **2.4%** (120/5,000).
*   **LTV vs. Cost to Serve:** Even assuming a generous 24-month lifespan for a paid user, LTV is $696 ($29 × 24). If a free user costs you $6.15/month, they cost $73.80/year. You would need a free user to stay on the free plan for nearly 10 years without paying to "break even" on their infrastructure cost alone, which is impossible. More critically, every month a free user runs a load test, they burn cash that cannot be recovered by future conversion unless the conversion probability is near 100% and immediate.

**What Flips It, and The Trigger:**
The single input that changes this call is the **attribution of infrastructure cost**. If you can prove that 90% of the $30,000 bill is generated by the 120 *paid* users (highly unlikely given "large-scale load tests" are typically free-tier behavior), the model might hold.
*   **Trigger:** Implement a hard cap of **$5 equivalent compute credits per month** for free users immediately.
*   **Decision Rule:** If conversion volume drops by more than 50% after capping, your "leads" were primarily free-loaders, not prospects. If conversion stays stable or rises (due to forced upgrades for heavy users), the cap was the correct filter.
*   **This Week's Step:** If your average free-user session exceeds 5 minutes of compute time, consider implementing a hard daily request limit (e.g., 100 requests/day) to stop load testing abuse instantly.
