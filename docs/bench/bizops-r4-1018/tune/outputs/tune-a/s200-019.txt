**The Call:** Reject the price cut; adopt the premium packaging upgrade while holding the $32 price point. Lowering the price to $24 anchors LuxGlow as a mid-tier commodity, likely validating customer complaints about "subtle" scent and failing to solve the root perception issue. In the luxury sector, price cuts signal lower quality, whereas enhanced packaging reinforces the premium narrative required to justify the scent profile and reduce returns.

**The Numbers:**
*Current Baseline (per unit):*
Price ($32) - COGS ($9) - Shipping ($6) = **$17 Contribution Margin**.
*Adjusted for Returns (22%):* Effective revenue drops as 22% of units are refunded but costs are incurred.
Net Contribution ≈ ($17 * 0.78) - ($15 * 0.22) [assuming full cost loss on return] = **$9.96 effective margin per unit sold**.

*Scenario A: Price Cut to $24 (Assuming returns drop to 15% due to lowered expectations):*
Price ($24) - COGS ($9) - Shipping ($6) = **$9 Gross Margin**.
Net Contribution ≈ ($9 * 0.85) - ($15 * 0.15) = **$5.40 effective margin**.
*Result:* Margin collapses by ~46%.

*Scenario B: Premium Packaging at $32 (Assuming returns drop to 10% due to perceived quality):*
Price ($32) - COGS ($9) - New Packaging ($4) - Shipping ($6 + **ASSUMED** $1 dimensional weight increase) = **$12 Gross Margin**.
Net Contribution ≈ ($12 * 0.90) - ($19 * 0.10) = **$8.90 effective margin**.
*Result:* Margin remains robust, nearly doubling Scenario A, while preserving brand equity.

**What Flips It, and the Trigger:**
The decision hinges on the **shipping cost increase** for the heavier package. If the dimensional weight push pushes shipping costs up by more than **$3.10 per unit** (eroding the margin advantage over Scenario A), the packaging strategy fails financially.
*Trigger:* Request a rate card quote from your carrier for the new dimensions immediately. If the increase is under $3, proceed with packaging.
*Step this week:* If the shipping quote is favorable, order a small batch of premium boxes and run a 500-unit A/B test against current stock.

**Routing Note:**
*Shipping Tiers:* Carrier dimensional weight thresholds are contract-specific; verify the exact cubic pricing tier with your account rep before committing to new box sizes.
*Legal/Compliance:* Rotating packaging for an A/B test generally does not require specific consumer protection disclosures if the product contents (scent/wax) remain identical and no false claims are made on the box. However, if the test involves different pricing displayed to different users without clear justification, or if "premium" claims are added that cannot be substantiated, consult counsel regarding unfair/deceptive trade practices. For now, a simple packaging swap without price discrimination is typically low-risk, but formal legal review is the binding determination for your specific jurisdiction.
