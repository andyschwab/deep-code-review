**The Call**
Changing your billing from per-transaction to a flat-rate subscription does **not** alter your legal classification as a Consumer Reporting Agency (CRA) under the Fair Credit Reporting Act (FCRA) or New York state law, nor does it reclassify you as a "data broker" solely due to the payment structure. Your compliance obligations are triggered by the *nature of the data* (consumer reports for employment/tenancy decisions) and the *act of assembling* that data, not by whether the customer pays $40 once or $300 monthly. However, an "unlimited" model introduces significant operational risk: it may incentivize customers to run excessive, non-permissible checks, increasing your liability for FCRA violations if you fail to audit their "permissible purpose" certifications.

**The Numbers & Risk Exposure**
*   **Current Volume:** 2,000 checks/month × $40 = $80,000 monthly revenue.
*   **Subscription Break-Even:** To match current revenue at $300/month, an agency must utilize the service for ~6.7 checks/month ($80,000 / $300 ≈ 267 agencies needed if all switch, or existing agencies must average >6.7 checks).
*   **Risk Assumption (UNVERIFIED):** If an agency subscribes for $300 and runs 500 checks (costing them $0.60/check vs. your $40 cost basis), your margin collapses, and the likelihood of them running checks without valid "permissible purpose" (a federal violation) statistically increases.
*   **Trigger:** If a single subscriber exceeds 50 checks/month, flag for manual review of their permissible purpose logs.

**What Flips It, and The Trigger**
The factor that changes your legal exposure is not the price, but the **loss of granular transactional friction**. Per-check pricing acts as a natural brake on unnecessary searches; unlimited access removes this.
*   **The Flip:** If your Terms of Service do not explicitly cap "unlimited" usage to bona fide permissible purposes per check, or if you lack automated logging to prove *why* each of the 2,000+ potential monthly checks was run, you risk regulatory action for facilitating non-compliant background checks.
*   **Actionable Step:** This week, draft a "Fair Usage Policy" addendum stating that "unlimited" refers to volume capacity, not a waiver of FCRA permissible purpose requirements, and implement a log requiring the subscriber to certify the specific role/need for every report generated, even in a subscription flow.

**Routing Note**
While the pricing model itself is neutral, the *implementation* of an unlimited model requires a review of your user agreements and audit trails by an attorney specializing in FCRA compliance to ensure your "reasonable procedures" (15 U.S.C. § 1681e) scale to prevent misuse by high-volume subscribers.
