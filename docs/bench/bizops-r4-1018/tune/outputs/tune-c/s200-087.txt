**The Call:** Raise the $750,000 seed extension immediately; bootstrapping this specific build creates a cash deficit that threatens solvency before the product ships, making the "safety" of no dilution an illusion.

**The Numbers:**
Your current annual revenue is $600k. Assuming a standard SaaS gross margin of 80%, your gross profit is $480k/year or ~$40k/month.
*   **Bootstrap Scenario:** You need $450k for development over 9 months ($50k/month burn just for dev). This exceeds your entire monthly gross profit ($40k), leaving $0 for existing operating expenses (salaries for 8 people, rent, tools).
    *   *Result:* You run out of cash in <1 month unless you cut existing ops by >80%, which contradicts your goal of maintaining growth.
    *   *Assumption (UNVERIFIED):* Current operating burn is not already exceeding revenue. If you are currently cash-flow positive, the math still fails because the $50k/mo dev cost alone exceeds total gross profit.
*   **Raise Scenario:** Raising $750k covers the $450k build cost plus ~$300k for 6 months of operating runway.
    *   *Dilution Math:* If your pre-money valuation is $3M (conservative "funding winter" estimate), $750k buys 20% of the company. If you wait and valuation drops to $2M later due to lost market share, that same $750k costs 27%.
    *   *Result:* Paying a 7% premium in equity now buys survival and growth; waiting likely costs more equity later or results in zero value.

**What Flips It, and the Trigger:**
The single input that changes this call is **Current Net Cash Flow**. If your existing business is generating *more* than $50k/month in free cash flow (after all 8 salaries and expenses), then bootstrapping becomes viable.
*   *Trigger:* If your monthly bank balance does not increase by at least $55k this month, you must initiate the fundraise.
*   *Rule of Thumb:* Never fund a discrete capital project (like a $450k rebuild) that exceeds 50% of your annual gross profit without external capital.
