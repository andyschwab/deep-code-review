**The Rules and Usual Practice:**
Insurance licensing is strictly line-of-authority specific.
1.  **Line of Authority:** P&C licenses cover property damage and liability (e.g., trip cancellation due to weather, slip-and-fall liability). They do not cover "accident and health" (A&H) benefits, which pay for medical expenses or accidental death/disability. Bundling a P&C product with an A&H product typically requires the agent to hold *both* lines of authority.
2.  **Duration Irrelevance:** The five-day duration of the retreat does not exempt the product from health insurance regulations. Short-term travel health policies are still classified as health insurance. There is no general regulatory exemption for policies under seven days that waives licensing requirements.
3.  **State Specifics (General Rule):**
    *   **California:** The Department of Insurance strictly separates lines. Selling health benefits without the specific license is a misdemeanor and grounds for administrative penalty.
    *   **Texas:** The Department of Insurance requires a separate A&H license. Penalties for acting without a license can include cease-and-desist orders and significant administrative fines per violation.
    *   **Florida:** The Office of Insurance Regulation mandates distinct licensing. Unlicensed activity can result in fines up to $50,000 per violation and potential criminal charges.

**What Flips It:**
