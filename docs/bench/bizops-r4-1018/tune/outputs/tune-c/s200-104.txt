**What Flips It, and the Trigger:**
The single input that changes the call is the **legal status of your anonymization method**. If counsel determines your data is "de-identified" under the HIPAA Safe Harbor method (removal of 18 specific identifiers) or Expert Determination method, costs stay lower. If the data is deemed "Limited Data Set" or still PHI (Protected Health Information), the compliance burden and liability skyrocket, flipping the decision to **Reject**.
*   **Trigger:** If the initial legal opinion (costing ~$5k) states that re-identification risk is non-negligible or that state laws (CCPA/CPRA) impose stricter standards than HIPAA, stop immediately.
*   **Step this week:** Engage a health-law attorney for a fixed-scope "De-identification Assessment" before budgeting for audits or technical builds.

**Routing Note:**
Selling genetic data touches HIPAA (if covered entity/business associate rules apply), the Common Rule (if research), and strictly the CCPA/CPRA (California consumer rights). Only a licensed attorney can determine if your specific anonymization technique meets the "Expert Determination" standard required to safely sell the data without patient consent. This is a binding legal determination, not a math problem.

**Risks of Non-Compliance:**
*   **HIPAA Violations:** Fines ranging from $100 to $50,000 per violation, up to $1.5 million per year per category.
*   **CCPA/CPRA Violations:** Statutory damages of $100–$750 per consumer per incident in private actions; administrative fines up to $7,500 per intentional violation.
*   **Reputational Collapse:** Loss of consumer trust in a DNA testing brand is often fatal.

**Mandatory Compliance Steps (General Framework):**
1.  **Legal Classification:** Obtain written Expert Determination that data is not PHI.
2.  **Data Use Agreements:** Draft strict contracts limiting partner usage and prohibiting re-identification.
3.  **Technical Safeguards:** Encryption at rest/transit, strict access controls, and immutable audit logs.
4.  **Administrative Safeguards:** Workforce training, incident response plan, and designated privacy officer.
