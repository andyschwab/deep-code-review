**The Call**
Reject immediate recognition; amortize the $75,000 implementation fees over the three-year contract term. Because you stated the implementation services are **not distinct** from the software license, they form a single combined performance obligation under GAAP (ASC 606). Recognizing them upfront is non-compliant and creates severe audit and fundraising risks.

**The Numbers**
*Formula:* Annual Recognized Revenue = (Total Contract Value) / (Contract Term in Years).
*User Figures:* License = $250,000/year; Implementation = $75,000 one-time; Term = 3 years.
*Assumption (Marked ASSUMED):* The $75,000 fee is fixed and non-refundable, and the "not distinct" assessment is accurate per your facts.

| Scenario | Total Contract Value (3 yrs) | Annual Recognized Revenue | Q1 Recognized (Cash Collected) | Q1 Recognized (GAAP) |
| :--- | :--- | :--- | :--- | :--- |
| **Your Hope (Immediate)** | $825,000 | $300,000 | $75,000 (impl.) | **$0** |
| **GAAP Treatment (Amortized)**| $825,000 | $275,000 | $0 | **$6,250** |

*Calculation:* ($250,000 × 3) + $75,000 = $825,000 Total.
$825,000 / 3 years = $275,000 annual revenue.
Quarterly revenue = $275,000 / 4 = $68,750.
Of that $68,750, only $6,250 relates to the implementation fee amortization ($75k / 12 quarters). The rest is license revenue.
*Cash Reality:* You have $150,000 cash / $40,000 monthly burn = **3.75 months runway**. Immediate recognition adds $225,000 to *revenue* but $0 to *cash*. It does not extend your runway.

**What Flips It, and the Trigger**
The single input that changes this call is the **distinctness** of the service. If the implementation is highly customized such that the customer cannot benefit from the software without it, they are not distinct.
*Trigger:* If an independent third party could perform the implementation using standard skills (not proprietary to you), you *might* argue distinctness.
*Rule of Thumb:* If the service significantly modifies or customizes the software, it is one obligation.
*Step this week:* If your implementation is standard configuration, document the "standalone value" test immediately; if it requires custom coding, prepare the amortization schedule for the auditors.
