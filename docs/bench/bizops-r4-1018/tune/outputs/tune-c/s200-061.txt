**The Call:** Present a three-tiered TAM in your pitch deck: a core "Strict Serverless" number (event-driven only), an expanded "Cloud-Native Compute" number (including containers), and a "Serviceable Obtainable Market" (SOM) based on your current geography and ideal customer profile. Do not choose one definition; explicitly show the bridge between them to demonstrate both market discipline and growth vision.

**The Numbers:**
*Formula:* TAM = (Total Addressable Units) × (Average Selling Price).
*Your Data:* 4M functions/day × 365 days = 1.46B annual requests. At $0.02/M, your current implied revenue run-rate from this volume is ~$29k (illustrative of unit price, not total company revenue).
*Market Segmentation Assumptions:*
1.  **Core TAM (Strict):** Event-driven, no provisioned servers. Use the $2B figure. *Assumption:* This matches your current product exactly.
2.  **Expanded TAM (Broad):** Includes containerized microservices. Use the $12B figure. *Assumption:* This represents the adjacent market you could capture with future product iterations.
3.  **SAM (Serviceable Available Market):** Developers in North America/Europe likely to migrate to serverless in 18 months. *Assumption:* Roughly 20% of the Core TAM based on typical cloud adoption curves (UNVERIFIED, label as internal estimate).

**What Flips It, and The Trigger:**
The input that changes investor perception is not the total dollar amount, but the **conversion logic** between the tiers. If you present only the $12B number without explaining the inclusion of containers, credibility flips to "dishonest." If you present only $2B, the narrative flips to "limited upside."
*Trigger:* When your monthly active developers exceed 1,000 or you launch a container-support feature, shift your primary narrative focus from the Core TAM to the Expanded TAM. Until then, anchor on the Core $2B to prove product-market fit, using the $12B strictly as a "future optionality" slide.
*Rule of Thumb:* Investors accept broad TAMs only when the path to capturing a slice of the *adjacent* market is technically feasible within 24 months.
*This Week's Step:* If your product roadmap includes container support within 18 months, consider adding a "Product Evolution" timeline slide that visually maps your entry from the $2B core into the $12B expanded market.
