**The Call**
Proceeding with your beta launch as described carries significant legal risk; do not assume US "fair use" or EU exceptions automatically protect the indexing and display of copyrighted code snippets without a specialized legal opinion. Your $150,000 runway is insufficient to defend a copyright infringement lawsuit, making pre-launch counsel engagement a critical survival step, not an optional expense.

**The Legal Landscape (General Rules & Usual Practices)**
*   **US Fair Use:** This is a fact-specific affirmative defense, not a blanket rule. Courts weigh four factors: (1) purpose (transformative vs. superseding), (2) nature of the work (functional code vs. creative expression), (3) amount used (three lines may be de minimis, but if it's the "heart" of the function, it matters), and (4) market effect (does your tool replace the need to visit the original repo?). *Usual practice:* Search engines often rely on this, but code is distinct from text/images; no definitive Supreme Court precedent exists specifically for code snippet search engines displaying executable logic.
*   **EU Copyright:** The EU lacks a direct "fair use" equivalent. Exceptions for "text and data mining" (TDM) exist under the DSM Directive (Art. 3 & 4), but they often require lawful access and may not cover public display of snippets in a commercial search result. Displaying even small portions can infringe the reproduction and communication-to-the-public rights. *Usual practice:* EU-based services often implement strict opt-out mechanisms or limit displays to non-functional metadata to mitigate risk.
*   **Opt-Out Mechanisms:** Implementing an opt-out is generally *not* an admission of liability; it is a standard risk-mitigation strategy (similar to `robots.txt` or DMCA safe harbor practices). However, its effectiveness depends on jurisdiction and whether you qualify for safe harbor protections (which usually require responding to takedown notices, not just offering opt-out).

**What Flips the Risk & The Trigger**
The single factor that flips this from "manageable risk" to "existential threat" is **receiving a cease-and-desist letter from a major rights holder (e.g., a large tech corporation or open-source foundation) before you have legal counsel retained.**
*   **Trigger:** If you launch without counsel and receive such a letter within the first 30 days, your runway could be depleted by initial legal fees alone.
*   **Rule of Thumb:** If your dataset includes code from repositories with restrictive licenses (e.g., GPL, AGPL) or proprietary code accidentally scraped, your risk profile spikes immediately.
*   **This Week's Step:** If you cannot secure a retainer with a specialized IP attorney before launch, consider delaying the beta or restricting the display to metadata (file names, languages, star counts) only, withholding actual code snippets until cleared.

**Routing Note: Specific Questions for Specialized Counsel**
You must engage an attorney specializing in software copyright and internet law. Bring these exact questions:
1.  Does our specific implementation (indexing 2M repos, displaying 3-line contextual snippets) qualify as "fair use" under current US case law, considering the functional nature of code?
2.  Do the EU Text and Data Mining exceptions (DSM Directive Art. 4) apply to our commercial search engine, and do they permit public display of snippets, or only internal analysis?
3.  Does implementing an opt-out mechanism weaken our "fair use" argument in the US or constitute an admission of infringement in the EU?
4.  What specific license types (MIT, Apache, GPL, proprietary) in our dataset pose the highest litigation risk, and should we filter them pre-launch?
5.  What steps are required to qualify for DMCA safe harbor protections, and is an opt-out mechanism sufficient or do we need a formal takedown process?
