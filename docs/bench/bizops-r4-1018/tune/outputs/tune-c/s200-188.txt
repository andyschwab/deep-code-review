**The call.** Reject the dedicated sales team; the market math cannot support $180k in fixed labor costs. Proceed with a self-serve online model to preserve runway.

**The numbers.**
Total Addressable Revenue (TAR) = Total Candidates × Conversion Rate × Price.
Using your figures: 1,200 candidates × 5% conversion × $1,500 price = **$90,000 maximum annual revenue**.

*Scenario A: Self-Serve Model*
*   Revenue: $90,000 (assuming 100% market capture at your estimated conversion).
*   Operating Costs: $10,000/month × 12 = $120,000/year.
*   Net Result: -$30,000 annual loss.
*   Runway: With $100k funding and a $30k annual burn (once revenue ramps), you have ~3 years. If revenue is zero initially, burn is $120k/year, giving <1 year runway.

*Scenario B: Sales Team Model*
*   Revenue: $90,000 (capped by total market size).
*   Operating Costs: $120,000 (base) + $180,000 (sales team) = $300,000/year.
*   Net Result: -$210,000 annual loss.
*   Runway: $100k funding / ($25k monthly burn rate) = **4 months**. Even if the sales team doubled conversion to 10% (highly optimistic for a niche exam), revenue would only reach $180,000, still resulting in a $120,000 annual loss and a runway of less than 10 months.

**What flips it, and the trigger.**
The single input that changes this call is the **Total Addressable Market (TAM)** size. Your current estimate of 1,200 candidates caps revenue below the cost of the sales team alone.
*   **Trigger:** If verified data shows the actual annual candidate pool exceeds **4,800 students** (assuming 5% conversion) or you can prove a conversion rate above **20%** on the current 1,200, reconsider a sales motion.
*   **Rule of Thumb:** In B2C education, a dedicated sales team usually requires an Average Revenue Per User (ARPU) >$5k or a TAM >$1M to justify fixed salaries.
*   **Step this week:** If your verified candidate count remains near 1,200, consider pivoting the product to a lower-cost, automated webinar series ($299 price point) to attempt volume scaling without increasing headcount.
