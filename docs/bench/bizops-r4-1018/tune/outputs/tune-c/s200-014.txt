**The Call**
Your made-to-order model likely exempts you from Texas finished-goods inventory tax, but your raw material stock ($8,000 now, projected $25,000) remains taxable business personal property; scaling materials increases your liability but does not change your tax status or exemption eligibility for finished goods.

**The Numbers**
Texas Business Personal Property (BPP) tax applies to tangible property used to produce income.
*   **Formula:** `Taxable Value = (Raw Materials + Supplies + Equipment) - Exemptions`. Finished Goods held for sale are often treated differently or exempt if never held as "inventory" in the traditional sense, but raw materials are standard BPP.
*   **Current State:** $8,000 (Raw Materials). *Assumption:* No equipment value provided (marked UNKNOWN).
*   **Projected State:** $25,000 (Raw Materials).
*   **Result:** You will file a BPP rendition on the higher value next year. If the local tax rate is ~2.5% (illustrative, UNVERIFIED), your tax bill could rise from ~$200 to ~$625 annually on materials alone. The "made-to-order" argument protects the *finished* sneaker line item (which is zero), not the filament on the shelf.

**What Flips It, and The Trigger**
The distinction that flips the liability is **title transfer timing**. If you purchase materials only *after* the customer pays and you have a binding contract, some jurisdictions allow those specific materials to be excluded as "goods in transit" for a specific customer, but generally, stockpiling filament for anticipated demand (even if justified by past sales) counts as taxable inventory.
*   **Trigger:** If your raw material value exceeds the local county's freeport exemption threshold (often $0 for small businesses, but varies by jurisdiction) or if you hold materials for more than a specific period (e.g., 90 days) without a specific customer order.
*   **Step:** If you plan to hold >$25,000 in filament, consider implementing a "just-in-time" purchasing policy where bulk buys are timed to coincide with confirmed orders to minimize the snapshot value on January 1st (the typical tax date).

**Documentation & Filing Route**
To prove made-to-order status during an audit, maintain: (1) dated sales invoices showing payment prior to production start, (2) production logs linking specific batch numbers to customer order IDs, and (3) purchase orders for materials dated after or concurrent with sales orders.
*   **Routing Note:** Texas BPP renditions are filed locally with the county appraisal district, not just the state. While the standard online portal (e.g., Harris County Appraisal District or similar) allows self-filing, **consult a local Texas tax specialist** to confirm if your specific raw materials qualify for any "freeport" or goods-in-transit exemptions and to verify the correct classification code before submitting your first rendition with the increased valuation.
