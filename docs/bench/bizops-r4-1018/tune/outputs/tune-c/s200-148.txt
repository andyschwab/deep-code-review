**The Call:** Do not mix bulk powders into capsules on-site; this activity almost certainly classifies as "manufacturing" under FDA guidelines, triggering facility registration, GMP compliance, and potential enforcement that threatens your core business. Proceed only by selling pre-manufactured, third-party produced bottles where you act strictly as a retailer, or halt the venture until a specialized regulatory attorney confirms your specific formulation's status.

**The Numbers:**
*   **Projected Revenue:** 50 bottles/week × $45/bottle × 52 weeks = **$117,000/year**.
*   **Risk Exposure:** While revenue is modest, FDA warning letters, seizure of product, and injunctions carry non-monetary costs that can force a temporary or permanent shutdown of the entire facility.
*   **Assumption (UNVERIFIED):** That your current back-room space lacks the separate ventilation, sanitation, and documentation systems required for Current Good Manufacturing Practices (cGMP).
*   **Break-even on Compliance:** The cost to retrofit a space and register as a food/supplement manufacturing facility typically exceeds $50k–$100k initially, far outweighing the $117k projected top-line revenue for a single side product.

**What Flips It, and The Trigger:**
The single input that changes this from "retail" to "manufacturing" is **mechanical alteration**.
*   **Trigger:** The moment you open bulk raw ingredient containers and perform any processing (mixing, encapsulating, blending, repackaging from bulk to consumer units) on-site.
*   **Rule of Thumb:** Retailers sell sealed units made elsewhere; manufacturers create the unit. If you touch the bulk powder, you are a manufacturer.
*   **Step this week:** If you intend to sell supplements, **consider** sourcing fully finished, labeled bottles from a certified contract manufacturer (CMO) who handles the compliance, and resell those sealed units only. Do not open bulk powders in your lab.

**Routing Note:**
You must consult a regulatory attorney specializing in FDA dietary supplement law (21 CFR Part 111) before purchasing any bulk ingredients. The specific question to bring is: "Does our proposed back-room encapsulation process trigger 'facility registration' and 'cGMP' requirements under the Dietary Supplement Health and Education Act (DSHEA), and what are the immediate cessation risks if we proceed without it?" This is time-sensitive because enforcement can begin upon the first sale of an unregistered manufactured product.
