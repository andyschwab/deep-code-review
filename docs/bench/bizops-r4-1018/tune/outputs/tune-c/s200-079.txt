**The Call:** Do not switch to a pure variable model; it will likely stall procurement in the US financial sector. Instead, adopt a **committed-use tiered model** (e.g., "Good-Better-Best" bundles with overage rates) to guarantee budget predictability while retaining usage flexibility. Pure variable pricing introduces uncapped liability, a primary dealbreaker for enterprise CFOs managing fixed OpEx budgets.

**The Numbers:**
*   **Current Baseline:** $48,000/year average deal size ($4,000/month).
*   **Pipeline Exposure:** 20 prospects × $48,000 = $960,000 potential annual revenue.
*   **Risk Factor:** In a pure usage model, a 20% spike in testing volume (common during release cycles) creates a 20% budget overrun.
*   **Proposed Structure (Illustrative):**
    *   *Tier 1 (Commit):* $4,000/month for up to 1,000 execution minutes (matches current average).
    *   *Overage Rate:* $5/minute for usage above 1,000 minutes.
    *   *Cap Option:* Hard cap at $6,000/month (150% of commit) with service throttling rather than billing shock.
*   **Assumption (ASSUMED):** Current $48k deals represent a stable usage baseline; volatility is driven by release schedules, not organic growth.

**What Flips It, and the Trigger:**
The deciding input is the **variance in monthly execution minutes**. If your data shows month-over-month usage variance exceeds ±15%, pure usage billing will trigger procurement stalls.
*   **Trigger Rule:** If >30% of pilot customers project >20% budget variance under a variable model, revert to committed tiers immediately.
*   **Actionable Step:** This week, survey your top 5 enterprise prospects: "Would a hard monthly cap with overage fees resolve your budget approval concerns?" If the answer is yes, draft the tiered contract immediately.

**Anticipated Finance Objections (US Financial Sector):**
1.  **Uncapped Liability:** CFOs cannot approve contracts where the final invoice is unknown. They require a "maximum foreseeable cost."
2.  **OpEx vs. CapEx Rigidity:** Financial institutions often have strict quarterly OpEx limits. Unexpected overages may require re-approval workflows that delay payment or halt service.
3.  **Audit Complexity:** Variable line items complicate internal chargeback models for different engineering teams. Fixed tiers simplify internal accounting.
