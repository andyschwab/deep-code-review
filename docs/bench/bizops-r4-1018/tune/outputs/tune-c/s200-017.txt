**The Call:** Collecting a resale certificate once per customer is generally insufficient for high-volume, multi-state sales; you must verify the certificate's validity for each transaction or establish a robust, documented periodic re-verification protocol to avoid liability. You are legally exposed to uncollected sales tax plus penalties if you accept an invalid certificate, regardless of the designer's location relative to your New York warehouse. Do not onboard your first ten partners without a tax professional defining your specific verification workflow; software alone cannot replace the initial legal setup for multi-state nexus.

**The Numbers & Rules:**
*   **Liability Exposure:** If a certificate is invalid, the state treats the sale as retail. You owe the sales tax on the $2,500 average order value.
    *   *Formula:* Tax Due = Order Value × State Rate.
    *   *Example (NY 8%):* $2,500 × 0.08 = $200 per order.
    *   *Monthly Exposure (50 orders):* $10,000 in tax + potential penalties (often 10–50% of tax due) + interest.
*   **Verification Standard (Tier 2):** Most states (including NY) require "good faith" acceptance. This means the certificate must be properly filled out, but "good faith" often fails if you ignore obvious errors or fail to check if the buyer is actually registered in that state's database.
    *   *Assumption:* You currently have no automated validation system (ASSUMED).
    *   *Practice:* Manual collection is risky across 5 states. The "usual treatment" for multi-state sellers is to use an automated service that validates the ID number against state databases in real-time.
*   **Nexus Impact:** The designer's location matters. If they are in a state where you have "nexus" (physical presence, economic threshold), you must collect their state's tax unless the certificate is valid *for that state*. A NY certificate does not automatically cover a sale shipped to California if you have CA nexus.

**What Flips It & The Trigger:**
*   **Flip Input:** The strictness of the "good faith" defense in your specific operating states. Some states void the defense entirely if the ID number is fake, even if you didn't know.
*   **Trigger:** Your first audit or a single invalid certificate detection.
*   **Rule of Thumb:** If you process >$10k/month in exempt sales across multiple states, manual verification is statistically likely to fail an audit.
