**The Call**
Do not aggressively shift revenue to direct channels before your Series A if it materially slows growth; instead, frame the marketplace strategy as "capital-efficient distribution validation" while presenting a concrete, low-cost plan to diversify. Investors prefer a narrative of *strategic leverage* backed by early direct-channel proof over a forced pivot that sacrifices growth rate for the appearance of sales capability. Your current data shows direct sales are not yet product-market fit for your motion ($30k vs $160k), and burning cash to fix a broken sales engine before raising is a stronger negative signal than channel concentration.

**The Numbers**
*   **Concentration Risk:** 20% of ARR ($160,000) is tied to marketplaces.
    *   *Assumption (ASSUMED):* You stated 20% of $800k comes from these channels. If this implies a single dominant partner, risk is high; if split across AWS/Azure/GCP, risk is moderate.
*   **Sales Efficiency Comparison:**
    *   Marketplace Revenue: $160,000 (handled by partner, likely lower margin but zero CAC).
    *   Direct Sales Revenue: $30,000 (incurred full salary/overhead cost).
    *   *Implied Efficiency:* Marketplace generated 5.3x more revenue than the direct team last year.
*   **Runway:** $800k ARR implies ~$67k MRR. With $45k monthly burn, you are likely near cash-flow neutral or slightly profitable on an operating basis (assuming no other massive COGS), giving you strong leverage.
    *   *Formula:* Runway = Cash on Hand / Monthly Burn. (Cash on hand not provided).
*   **Scenario Analysis (Shifting to Direct):**
    *   *Base Case (Status Quo):* Grow at current pace via marketplaces; invest 10% of eng time in direct enablement. Result: High growth, 20% concentration.
    *   *Pivot Case (Aggressive Shift):* Reallocate resources to direct sales. *Assumption (ASSUMED):* Direct sales cycle is 6-9 months. Result: Growth slows 30-50% in short term; concentration drops to 10% over 18 months.
    *   *Verdict:* The Pivot Case lowers valuation multiples (growth is king in Series A) more than the concentration risk does, provided the concentration isn't >50% from a single source.

**What Flips It, and the Trigger**
The single input that changes this call is **Single-Partner Concentration**. If >15% of your total revenue (i.e., nearly all that marketplace revenue) comes from *one* specific marketplace (e.g., only AWS), the risk becomes unignorable.
*   **Trigger Value:** If any single partner exceeds 15% of total ARR.
*   **Stop/Pivot Trigger:** If you enter Series A negotiations and one partner represents >20% of revenue without a multi-year contract or technical integration lock-in, investors will discount your valuation by 20-30% for "key person/platform risk."
*   **Rule of Thumb:** Diversification is required only when a single channel exceeds 20-25% of total revenue.
*   **This Week's Step:** If your marketplace revenue is split across two or more providers, consider building a "Direct Connect" landing page and tracking inbound leads from marketplace users who want to bypass fees, rather than hiring expensive AEs.

**Routing Note**
Review your marketplace partner agreements with counsel to identify termination clauses and data ownership rights; specifically ask, "If delisted, do we retain customer billing relationships and data access?" This legal determination dictates whether the risk is temporary (revenue loss) or existential (customer loss).
