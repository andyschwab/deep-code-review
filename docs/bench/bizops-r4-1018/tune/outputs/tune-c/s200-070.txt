Do not hide the upfront cash; instead, highlight it as proof of strong customer commitment and low churn risk, while strictly separating cash flow from performance metrics.

**The Numbers:**
To clarify the relationship for acquirers, you must report three distinct lines derived from your figures:
1.  **Cash Collections (Bookings):** $1.8M (The cash actually received).
2.  **Recognized Revenue (GAAP):** $900k (The value earned this period).
3.  **Deferred Revenue Liability:** $900k (Calculated as Collections minus Recognized Revenue).

*Formula:* `Ending Deferred Revenue = Beginning Deferred + New Bookings - Recognized Revenue`.
*Assumption:* Assuming a steady state where beginning and ending deferred balances are similar due to your 5% churn, your annual bookings ($1.8M) should roughly equal your recognized revenue ($900k) plus the net increase in deferred revenue. Since your collections are double your recognized revenue, this implies your contract duration or prepayment terms create a significant cash buffer.

**Illustrative Unit Economics Impact:**
*   **Annual Recurring Revenue (ARR):** ~$900k (based on recognized run-rate).
*   **Cash Flow from Operations:** +$1.8M (before expenses).
*   **Retention Signal:** Your 5% churn rate applied to the $900k deferred liability means only $45k of that obligation is at risk of non-renewal, securing $855k of next-year's top line before you sell a single new contract.

**What Flips It, and the Trigger:**
The metric that changes the buyer's perception is the **Deferred Revenue to Annual Revenue Ratio**.
*   **Current Ratio:** $900k (Deferred) / $900k (Recognized) = 1.0x.
*   **The Trigger:** If this ratio drops below 0.5x in future periods, it signals a shift away from upfront payments or worsening renewal terms. If it stays at or above 1.0x, it validates your "annual upfront" model as a competitive moat providing interest-free working capital.
*   **Action Step:** If your data room shows high cash but low revenue growth, immediately add a "Sources of Cash" footnote explaining that 50% of this year's cash is legally obligated to be delivered next year, effectively pre-funding next year's operations.
