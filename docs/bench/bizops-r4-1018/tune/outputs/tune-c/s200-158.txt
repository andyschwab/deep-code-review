**The Call**
Offer exactly **20%** equity to the investor; this matches the $1M investment on a $4M pre-money valuation. Do not alter this percentage to account for the $50k debt, as debt affects enterprise value, not the share count issued in a standard equity round. To retain control, ensure your founder group retains 80% post-money, but immediately consult counsel regarding liquidation preferences, which can effectively override your ownership percentage in an exit.

**The Numbers**
*Formula:* Investor % = Investment / (Pre-Money Valuation + Investment).
*Inputs:* $1M Investment / ($4M Pre + $1M New Cash) = $1M / $5M = **20%**.

**What Flips It, and the Trigger**
The **liquidation preference** is the single input that can flip your effective ownership. If the investor demands a "2x non-participating" or "participating" liquidation preference, they could receive more than their 20% share of proceeds in a sale, reducing your actual payout.
*Trigger:* If the term sheet includes any liquidation preference greater than **1x non-participating**, stop and renegotiate.
*Rule of Thumb:* Standard seed terms are 1x non-participating. Anything else significantly alters the economic deal.
*Step:* If the term sheet arrives with complex preference language, consider hiring a startup lawyer for a fixed-fee review before signing.

**Routing Note**
This transaction is a **securities offering**. You must engage a securities attorney to draft the Stock Purchase Agreement and finalize the cap table. Specifically, ask them: "Does the $50k supplier debt need to be converted to equity or paid down prior to closing to satisfy investor due diligence, and how do we structure the option pool (typically 10-15%) so it dilutes founders rather than the new investor?"
