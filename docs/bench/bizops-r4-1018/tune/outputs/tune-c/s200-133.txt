**The Call:** Retain the per-session model (£12) as your primary path for insurance reimbursement in the UK and Germany; a flat unlimited subscription (£45) will likely be rejected by insurers as a non-billable wellness bundle rather than a medically necessary service.

**The Numbers:**
*   **Per-Session Revenue:** £12/session.
*   **Variable Cost:** £0.50/session.
*   **Contribution Margin:** £11.50/session (95.8%).
*   **Break-even Usage for Subscription:** At £45/month, a user must consume **4 sessions** to match the revenue of the per-session model (£45 / £12 = 3.75).
*   **Risk Assumption (ASSUMED):** If subscription users average >4 sessions/month, your margin compresses below the per-session baseline. If they average <4, revenue drops.
*   **Reimbursement Impact:** Insurers typically reimburse discrete, coded events (e.g., CPT/OPCS codes). A bundled fee lacks the granularity to map to specific billable events, often resulting in a **0% reimbursement rate** for the subscription portion attributed to "unlimited/unmonitored" access.

**What Flips It, and the Trigger:**
The deciding factor is the **definition of a "billable event"** in UK (NHS/private) and German ( statutory/private) payer contracts.
*   **Trigger:** If a target insurer explicitly requires "fee-for-service" coding per interaction (standard for psychotherapy), the subscription model fails immediately.
*   **Rule of Thumb:** Medical reimbursement generally requires proof of *specific* intervention duration and provider oversight per instance. Unlimited access obscures this data.
*   **This Week's Step:** If your target insurers' provider manuals list "monthly unlimited access" as an excluded benefit category, consider hybridizing: keep per-session for reimbursement and offer a separate, self-pay-only subscription for between-session support.

**Documentation & Routing:**
To prove medical necessity under a per-session model, you typically need:
1.  **Session Logs:** Timestamped duration, therapist/AI intervention type, and patient ID.
2.  **Clinical Codes:** Mapping of each chat to standard diagnostic/procedure codes (e.g., ICD-10/11 for diagnosis, OPCS-4 in UK or OPS in Germany for procedures).
3.  **Outcome Measures:** Pre/post-session psychometric scores (e.g., PHQ-9, GAD-7) linked to specific sessions.

For a subscription model, documentation requirements shift to **population health outcomes** (aggregate reduction in claims), which is a much higher bar for initial approval.

General market data is insufficient because reimbursement hinges on specific statutory frameworks (e.g., Germany's DiGA process or UK's NICE evidence standards) and individual payer contract language.
