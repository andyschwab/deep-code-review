*   **Projected Revenue:** 500 scooters × 2.5 rides/day × 30 days × $7.30 = **$273,750**.
*   **Local Margin:** $273,750 - $60,000 = **$213,750**.
*   **Efficiency Drop:** Revenue per scooter remains similar, but *return on invested capital (ROIC)* for the market entry drops because the fixed cost of entry (permits, legal, setup) is amortized over 10x fewer assets.

*Valuation Impact:*
VCs value scalability. A market capped at 500 units signals a "lifestyle business" ceiling in that region unless the cap is lifted.
*   **Base Case (Unrestricted):** Market valued at ~3-5x revenue potential.
*   **Capped Case:** The Chicago segment contributes <10% of potential volume. If this pattern repeats in other major cities (NYC, SF), your Total Addressable Market (TAM) shrinks by ~40-50%, directly compressing valuation multiples from "hyper-growth" to "steady utility."

**What Flips It, and the Trigger**
The single input that changes the call is the **permit escalation clause**. Many cities grant initial caps (e.g., 500) with performance-based expansions (e.g., +500 upon 95% uptime/safety compliance).
*   **Trigger:** If the Chicago permit includes a written pathway to expand beyond 500 units within 6 months based on performance metrics, the risk profile reverts to Tier 1 growth.
*   **Rule of Thumb:** If the cap is static for >12 months, treat Chicago as a marketing expense, not a profit center.
*   **Step this week:** If the permit draft lacks an expansion clause, consider negotiating a "pilot-to-permanent" conversion clause before signing, or deprioritize Chicago for a city with scalable zoning.
