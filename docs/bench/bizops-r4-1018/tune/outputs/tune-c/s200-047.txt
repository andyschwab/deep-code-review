**The Call:** Proceed with the trade-in program structured explicitly as a **purchase price discount** rather than a barter exchange; this avoids recognizing income on used goods and simplifies sales tax collection in most jurisdictions, though you should engage a CPA to confirm specific nexus rules before launch.

**The Numbers:**
Your unit economics for the trade-in cohort rely on the net effective price and refurbishment margin.
*   **Formula:** `Net Revenue = List Price - Trade-in Credit`; `Gross Margin per Unit = Net Revenue - (COGS + Refurb Cost)`.
*   **User Figures:** List Price ($800), Credit ($200), Refurb Cost ($50).
*   **Computed Result (Base Case):**
    *   Net Revenue per unit: $800 - $200 = **$600**.
    *   Gross Profit contribution (excluding machine COGS): $600 - $50 = **$550** per unit.
    *   Total Q1 Refurb Liability: 500 units × $50 = **$25,000**.
*   **Assumption (MARKED ASSUMED):** You can resell refurbished units or parts for at least $50 to break even on refurbishment costs. If resale value is $0, your margin drops by $50/unit.

**What Flips It, and The Trigger:**
The single input that changes the tax treatment is **how the invoice is written**. If the invoice shows a separate "sale" of the old machine to StitchHome for $200, it risks being classified as a barter transaction (requiring FMV reporting). If the invoice shows a "$200 discount" applied to the $800 price contingent on returning the old unit, it is treated as a discount.
*   **Trigger Rule:** If any state where you have nexus mandates that sales tax be calculated on the *pre-discount* price for trade-ins (rare for business-to-consumer but possible), your cash flow impact increases by the local tax rate × $200 × 500 units.
*   **Actionable Step:** This week, draft the sales invoice template to display the transaction as "Price: $800, Less Trade-in Allowance: $200, Total Due: $600" and have a local tax professional review this specific line-item phrasing for Washington B&O tax and remote nexus states.

**Routing Note:**
While the general rule treats contingent discounts as price reductions (simplifying income recognition), the binding determination of whether Washington State or other nexus states require sales tax collection on the full $800 versus the net $600 is a jurisdiction-specific filing requirement. Bring your draft invoice and nexus map to a CPA to confirm the sales tax base before announcing the promotion.
