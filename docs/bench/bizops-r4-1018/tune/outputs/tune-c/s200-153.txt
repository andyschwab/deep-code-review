**The Call**
Do not raise the price to $14 yet; the math indicates this move would likely destroy value by accelerating cash burn unless you can prove the higher price significantly extends user lifetime. With an 8% monthly churn, your current unit economics are already negative (Lifetime Value < Acquisition Cost), so a price hike that further suppresses conversion or accelerates churn will worsen the deficit. Prioritize lowering your $45 acquisition cost or reducing the 8% churn before attempting a price increase.

**The Numbers**
We evaluate the decision using Lifetime Value (LTV) vs. Customer Acquisition Cost (CAC).
*Formula:* $LTV = \frac{ARPA}{Churn}$ (simplified, ignoring discount rates for this horizon).
*Current State:*
- ARPA (Average Revenue Per Account): $9
- Churn: 8% (0.08)
- **Current LTV:** $9 / 0.08 = **$112.50**
- **Current CAC:** $45
- **Current Margin:** $112.50 - $45 = **$67.50** (Positive unit economics, but thin margin for reinvestment).

*Proposed State ($14/mo) with Assumptions:*
You projected a 30% drop in conversion. We must assume how this impacts the *effective* ARPA and Churn.
- **Assumption A (Optimistic):** Price rises to $14, churn stays at 8%, volume drops 30% immediately but stabilizes.
  - New LTV: $14 / 0.08 = $175.
  - New CAC per *remaining* user: If ad spend is fixed but conversions drop 30%, effective CAC rises to ~$64 ($45 / 0.7).
  - New Margin: $175 - $64 = $111. (Theoretically better, but relies on churn not spiking).
- **Assumption B (Realistic/Stress):** Price rises to $14, but price sensitivity causes monthly churn to rise to 12% (common when price jumps >50% in K-12).
  - New LTV: $14 / 0.12 = $116.66.
  - New Effective CAC: ~$64.
  - New Margin: $116.66 - $64 = $52.66. (Worse than current state).

**What Flips It, and The Trigger**
The single input that changes the call is **Post-Hike Monthly Churn**.
- **Trigger Value:** If raising the price causes monthly churn to exceed **10.5%**, the LTV drops below $133, and combined with the higher effective CAC from lower conversion, your payback period extends dangerously.
- **Decision Rule:** If you cannot validate via a small A/B test that churn remains under 10% at the $14 price point, do not roll out the increase.
- **Step this week:** Run a "fake door" or limited A/B test on 5% of new traffic offering the $14 plan. Measure the *actual* trial-to-paid conversion drop and the *early* churn signal. If conversion drops >30% *and* early cancellation signals rise, abort the hike.

**Data Points Needed**
To finalize the LTV model, you must gather:
1.  **Cohort-based churn curves:** Does the 8% churn happen mostly in Month 1 or Month 6? High early churn makes CAC recovery impossible regardless of price.
2.  **Price elasticity by segment:** Do parents of high-schoolers tolerate $14 better than elementary parents? Aggregate data hides this.
3.  **Refund/Chargeback rates:** Higher prices often trigger more chargebacks, which act as instant churn + fees.
