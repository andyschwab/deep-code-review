**The Call**
Recognize the full $4,500 per bike as revenue at the point of sale (delivery), provided the sale meets standard criteria for transfer of control. The fact that you are subsidizing the interest via a third-party lender does not defer the revenue recognition of the bike itself; instead, the cost of that subsidy is recorded as an expense (likely marketing or interest expense) in the same period.

**The Numbers**
*Formula:* Total Q1 Revenue = (Units Sold × Price Per Unit)
*Your Figures:* 200 units × $4,500 = $900,000.
*Assumption (ASSUMED):* All 200 bikes are delivered and accepted by customers within Q1. If delivery slips to Q2, that portion of revenue slides to Q2.
*Result:* $900,000 recognized in Q1 revenue.
*Cost Impact:* The "0% financing" cost you pay to the lender is a separate cash outflow. If the market rate is 6% and you buy down the rate to 0% for 12 months on $900,000 of principal, you might incur ~$27,000–$54,000 in subsidy costs (illustrative, UNVERIFIED rate). This reduces your *net income*, but not your *top-line revenue*.

**What Flips It, and the Trigger**
The single input that changes this call is **transfer of control**. Under ASC 606 (the prevailing US GAAP standard), revenue is recognized when the customer obtains control of the asset.
*Trigger:* If your terms are "bill and hold" or if you retain title/risk until the financing term ends, you must defer revenue.
*Rule of Thumb:* If the customer can ride the bike home today and bears the risk of loss/theft, control has transferred.
*Step:* Before the board meeting, confirm with your logistics lead that the "delivery date" for all 200 projected units falls strictly within the Q1 calendar window. If any unit ships in April, move $4,500 per unit out of the Q1 forecast.

**Routing Note**
While the general rule (ASC 606) points to point-of-sale recognition, the specific accounting entry for the subsidy (whether to net it against revenue or book it as a sales expense) and the exact contract wording with the third-party lender require a review by your CPA to ensure compliance with ASC 842 (leases) or ASC 606 variable consideration rules.
