**The Call**
Do not proceed with the "franchisee" reclassification as described; requiring an upfront fee and ongoing royalty while exerting control over methods almost certainly triggers mandatory franchise registration in Florida and Georgia, exposing you to rescission lawsuits and state penalties before you collect a dollar. The liability shift you seek is illusory without a properly registered franchise disclosure document (FDD), and the tax reporting change from 1099 to franchise royalty reporting introduces immediate compliance risks that outweigh the cash reserve benefit.

**The Numbers**
*Current Model (Affiliates):*
Revenue: $1.8M
Coach Payout: 20% of $300 = $60 per package.
Annual Coach Cost: ~$360,000 (assuming volume holds).
Tax Reporting: Form 1099-NEC for payments >$600.

*Proposed Model (Unregistered "Franchise"):*
Upfront Cash Inflow: 30 coaches × $15,000 = $450,000 (one-time).
Recurring Royalty: 5% of $300 = $15 per package.
Annual Royalty Revenue (on converted base): ~$45,000 (assuming same volume).
**Risk Exposure:** Potential refund of all $450,000 plus legal fees if found to be an unregistered franchise offering.

*Assumptions:* Volume remains constant; all 30 targets convert immediately.

**What Flips It, and the Trigger**
The single input that changes the call is the **degree of control** you exert over the coaches' operations. Under the FTC Franchise Rule and both FL/GA state laws, a relationship is legally a franchise if three elements exist: (1) payment of a fee ($15k + 5% royalty), (2) trademark association (SleepNest brand), and (3) **significant control** or assistance in marketing/operations.
*Trigger:* If you mandate specific sleep methods, scripts, pricing, or marketing materials, you have crossed the threshold. You cannot avoid registration by calling them "affiliates" or "licensees" if these three elements are present. The moment you collect the first $15,000 fee without a registered FDD, you are in violation.

**Routing Note**
You must engage a franchise attorney in Florida and Georgia to draft and register a Franchise Disclosure Document (FDD) before soliciting any fees. Specifically, have counsel review your proposed Operations Manual and Affiliate Agreement to determine if your current level of oversight triggers the "significant control" test under FL Stat. § 556.10–17 and GA Code § 10-1-410. Do not file any 1099s or collect fees until counsel confirms the structure or completes registration.
