**The Call:** Reject the $25M cap as currently structured; at 4% monthly churn and slowing growth, your implied LTV:CAC is likely below the 3:1 threshold required for Series A, making the valuation indefensible without immediate proof of churn reduction or a clear differentiation moat.

**The Numbers:**
To defend a valuation, you must prove unit economics support hyper-growth.
*   **LTV Formula:** $ARPA \times (1 / Monthly Churn) \times Gross Margin$.
    *   *Inputs:* $ARPA$ (derived from $800k ARR / customer count, unknown), Churn = 4% (0.04), Margin (ASSUMED 80% for SaaS).
    *   *Result:* LTV $\approx$ $ARPA \times 20 \times 0.8$. A 4% churn caps your customer lifetime at ~25 months. If your CAC exceeds 8 months of gross margin contribution, your LTV:CAC falls below 3:1.
*   **Runway Formula:** $Cash Balance / Monthly Burn$.
    *   *Inputs:* Cash (ASSUMED $540k based on 6 months runway), Burn = $90k.
    *   *Result:* 6 months. This is critically low for a Series A process, which typically takes 6–9 months.
*   **Growth Efficiency:** 15% MoM is strong but decelerating. Investors will model this flattening to <10% MoM quickly if churn remains at 4%.

**What Flips It, and The Trigger:**
The single input that changes the call is **Net Revenue Retention (NRR)**. If you can demonstrate an NRR >120% (expansion revenue offsetting the 4% logo churn), the valuation becomes plausible.
*   *Trigger:* If next month's churn remains $\ge$ 4% while growth stays $\le$ 15%, your effective growth rate will approach zero within 6 months.
*   *Step:* If you cannot show expansion revenue covering >50% of churned revenue by next board meeting, consider pivoting the raise narrative to a "turnaround" story with a lower cap ($12M–$15M) or extend runway by cutting burn to $60k immediately.
