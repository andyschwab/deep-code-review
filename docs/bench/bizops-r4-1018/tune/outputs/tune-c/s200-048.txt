**The Call**
Do not lower your price; invest the $5,000 immediately in better photography and sizing tools. A 22% return rate driven by "size mismatches" is a product-information failure, not a pricing problem. Lowering the price to $150 will likely degrade your luxury brand perception without fixing the root cause of returns, whereas clarifying sizing directly addresses the stated reason for customer dissatisfaction.

**The Numbers**
Your current unit economics are distorted by the return rate. We must calculate the *Effective Contribution Margin* per unit sold, accounting for the cost of goods sold (COGS) on returned items and the lost revenue.

*Formula:*
`Effective Margin = (Price × (1 - Return Rate)) - (COGS × Total Units Shipped)`
*Note: We assume returned units have zero salvage value for this conservative estimate.*

*Current Scenario ($180 Price):*
*   Monthly Units Sold (Revenue/Price): $90,000 / $180 = 500 units.
*   Units Returned (22%): 110 units.
*   Units Kept: 390 units.
*   Total COGS Incurred (500 units × $60): $30,000.
*   Net Revenue (390 units × $180): $70,200.
*   **Net Contribution:** $70,200 - $30,000 = **$40,200**.
*   **Effective Margin per Unit Sold:** $40,200 / 500 = **$80.40**.

*Hypothetical Price Cut ($150 Price, assuming same 22% return rate):*
*   To maintain $90k revenue, you would need to sell 600 units (unlikely without demand elasticity data, so we compare per-unit economics).
*   Net Revenue per 100 units shipped: 78 kept × $150 = $11,700.
*   COGS per 100 units shipped: 100 × $60 = $6,000.
*   **Net Contribution:** $5,700.
*   **Effective Margin per Unit Sold:** **$57.00**.
*   *Result:* Lowering price reduces your buffer to absorb returns by ~29% without solving the return volume.

*Investment Scenario ($180 Price, Return Rate reduced to 8% industry standard):*
*   ASSUMED: Better tools reduce returns from 22% to 8%.
*   Units Returned (8% of 500): 40 units.
*   Units Kept: 460 units.
*   Net Revenue: 460 × $180 = $82,800.
*   Total COGS: 500 × $60 = $30,000.
*   **Net Contribution:** $52,800.
*   **Gain vs. Current:** $52,800 - $40,200 = **+$12,600/month**.
*   **Payback Period:** $5,000 investment / $12,600 monthly gain = **< 2 weeks**.

**What Flips It, and the Trigger**
The single input that changes this call is the *cause* of the return. If customer surveys reveal that returns are actually due to "poor foam quality" or "comfort" rather than "size mismatches," then the information fix will fail.
*   **Trigger:** If, after implementing new sizing tools, the return rate remains above 15% after 30 days, pause further marketing spend and investigate product quality or genuine price-value mismatch.
*   **Rule of Thumb:** In luxury goods, a return rate >10% usually signals a friction point in the buying journey (information), while <5% signals product-market fit.
*   **Step this week:** If your current product page lacks a "measure your dog" video or interactive guide, consider adding it before running any new ad campaigns.
