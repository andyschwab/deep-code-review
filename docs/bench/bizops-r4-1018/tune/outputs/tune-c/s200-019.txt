**The call:** Reject the price cut; keep the $32 price and invest in premium packaging. Lowering the price to $24 anchors LuxGlow as a mid-tier commodity, likely increasing return rates among luxury buyers who equate low price with low quality. Upgrading packaging reinforces the luxury positioning, addressing the "subtle scent" complaint by elevating perceived value without eroding margin.

**The numbers:**
*Current Baseline (per unit):*
Price $32 – COGS $9 – Shipping $6 – Returns Cost (22% of $32 = $7.04) = **$9.96 Net Contribution**.
*(Note: Returns cost assumes full refund loss on returned units; actual recovery may vary slightly).*

*Scenario A: Price Cut to $24 (Assumption: Return rate drops to 15% due to managed expectations, but volume stays flat).*
Price $24 – COGS $9 – Shipping $6 – Returns Cost (15% of $24 = $3.60) = **$5.40 Net Contribution**.
*Result:* Margin collapses by 46%.

*Scenario B: Keep $32, Add $4 Packaging (Assumption: Return rate drops to 10% due to enhanced perceived quality).*
Price $32 – COGS $13 ($9+$4) – Shipping $6 – Returns Cost (10% of $32 = $3.20) = **$9.80 Net Contribution**.
*Result:* Margin remains nearly identical to the distressed baseline, but brand equity is preserved. If the return rate drops to 8% (plausible for luxury fixes), contribution rises to **$10.44**.

**What flips it, and the trigger:**
The decision hinges on the **return rate sensitivity**. If the packaging upgrade fails to reduce returns below 11%, the price cut becomes mathematically safer on pure cash flow, though brand-damaging.
*Trigger:* Run a 30-day test. If returns do not fall below 11% with the new packaging, reconsider the pricing strategy or product formulation.
*Step this week:* If you can source the premium packaging for under $4.50, consider locking that vendor immediately to protect the margin buffer.

**Routing note:**
*Shipping Tiers:* Heavier/bulkier packaging often triggers carrier dimensional weight (DIM) pricing or tier jumps. You must measure the new package dimensions and weigh them against your carrier's specific rate card; do not assume the $6 cost holds.
*Legal/Compliance:* Rotating packaging for an A/B test generally does not require specific consumer protection disclosures if the product contents (scent/wax) remain identical and no false claims are made on the box. However, if the "premium" packaging implies a different product size or composition that isn't true, it risks deceptive trade practice claims.
