You must modify labels to use neutral descriptors (e.g., "Plant-Based Mince") before shipment. **The Numbers & Regulatory Landscape:**
There is no "fine calculator" without a specific violation event, but non-compliance risks include product seizure, mandatory recalls, and fines scaling with revenue.
*   **United Kingdom:** The use of "meat" descriptions for non-meat products is restricted under retained EU law (Regulation (EU) No 1308/2013). While "dairy" terms (milk, cheese) face strict crackdowns, meat terms like "sausage" or "burger" are often permitted *only* if clearly qualified (e.g., "Vegan Sausage"), but specific animal names like "Beef" are generally prohibited for plant products.
    *   *Status:* High risk. "Beef-Style" is likely non-compliant.
    *   *Pre-approval:* No formal pre-approval for labels, but Trading Standards can act post-market.
    *   *Local Rep:* Not strictly required for general food, but an Importer/Operator established in the UK is mandatory post-Brexit.
*   **Canada:** The *Food and Drugs Act* and *Safe Food for Canadians Regulations* prohibit labels that are "false, misleading or deceptive." Using "Beef" for a plant product is considered misleading. Terms like "meat" are reserved for animal flesh.
    *   *Status:* High risk. "Beef-Style" is non-compliant.
    *   *Pre-approval:* No pre-approval for standard labels, but the Canadian Food Inspection Agency (CFIA) conducts surveillance.
    *   *Local Rep:* An imported food requires a Canadian importer who holds a Safe Food for Canadians (SFC) license.
*   **Singapore:** The *Sale of Food Act* and *Food Regulations* require labels to not be misleading. The Singapore Food Agency (SFA) has specific guidelines for plant-based meats; they generally allow terms like "plant-based meat" but restrict direct animal names if they mislead consumers into thinking it is animal-derived.
    *   *Status:* Moderate to High risk. "Beef" is likely too specific; "Plant-based beef alternative" may be acceptable but needs verification.
    *   *Pre-approval:* No pre-approval for standard products, but SFA can request evidence.
    *   *Local Rep:* An importer must be registered with SFA.

**What Flips It, and the Trigger:**
The trigger is the word "Beef." If your label contains specific animal names ("Beef," "Bacon") without clear, prominent qualifiers like "Plant-Based" or "Meat-Free" immediately adjacent, you will face enforcement.
*   *Rule of Thumb:* If a consumer could reasonably believe the product contains animal flesh based on the name alone, it is non-compliant.
*   *Action:* If your current artwork says "Beef-Style," consider a full reprint to "Plant-Based Mince" before any shipping documentation is filed.
