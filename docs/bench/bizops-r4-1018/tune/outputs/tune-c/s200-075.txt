**The Call**
Do not establish a German subsidiary yet; for a single $60k contract, the cost ($40k) destroys unit economics. Proceed with hosting data in the AWS Frankfurt region (`eu-central-1`) and executing a robust Data Processing Agreement (DPA), but treat the "local entity" requirement as a specific legal negotiation point, not an automatic regulatory threshold.

**The Numbers**
*   **Contract Value (Year 1):** $60,000
*   **Subsidiary Setup Cost:** $40,000 (one-time)
*   **Net Impact:** Setting up the entity consumes 67% of the contract's first-year revenue and ~27% of your total cash reserves ($150k).
*   **Break-even Logic:** The subsidiary only makes financial sense if the *lifetime value* (LTV) of this customer (plus immediate follow-on German sales) exceeds the setup cost plus ongoing local administrative burn.
*   **Assumption (UNVERIFIED):** Ongoing German administrative/tax compliance costs ~$10k–$20k/year. If true, you need ~3–4 years of this specific contract just to cover the entity's existence, ignoring the opportunity cost of the depleted runway.

**What Flips It, and the Trigger**
The deciding factor is not data residency (solved by AWS `eu-central-1`), but **permanent establishment (PE) risk** and **contractual liability**.
*   **Trigger:** If the German customer's legal team insists that the contracting entity *must* be domiciled in Germany to enforce local consumer/business protection laws or VAT handling, you face a binary choice: lose the deal or incorporate.
*   **Rule of Thumb:** Data sovereignty laws (GDPR) generally mandate *where data sits*, not *where the company sits*. Corporate law mandates *who signs the contract*.
*   **Step this week:** If the customer demands a German entity, consider a "reseller" model (partnering with an existing German MSP who resells your tool) instead of direct incorporation.

**Routing Note (Tier 3: Binding Determination)**
You must consult a German-qualified attorney specializing in IT and corporate tax. Do not rely on general US counsel. Bring these exact questions:
1.  **Permanent Establishment:** Does signing this specific contract and hosting data in Frankfurt, while managing it from Delaware, create a "permanent establishment" triggering German corporate income tax and VAT registration obligations?
2.  **GDPR Controller/Processor:** As a US processor handling EU data, does our current Delaware entity suffice under GDPR Article 44+ (transfers), or do we need an EU representative (Article 27) which is cheaper than a full subsidiary?
3.  **Contractual Enforceability:** Will the customer accept a Delaware entity bound by German law and jurisdiction in the contract, or is a local entity a hard "go/no-go" clause?
