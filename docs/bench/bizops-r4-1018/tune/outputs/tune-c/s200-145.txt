**The Call:** Treat the £80,000 software improvement and associated cloud simulation costs as highly likely qualifying R&D, while the £150,000 formula development requires a strict split between "qualifying scientific uncertainty" (eligible) and "routine formulation/tasting" (ineligible); do not claim the full £230,000 without isolating the failed experiments and novel bioavailability testing from standard quality control.

**The Numbers:**
Under current UK rules (pre-April 2024 rates for context, though rates have merged/simplified for SMEs), the relief is calculated on qualifying expenditure.
*Formula Development (£150k):*
- **Qualifying (Assumed 40%):** £60,000. This covers the nutritionists' time spent specifically resolving scientific uncertainty (e.g., novel bioavailability interactions where the outcome was unknown).
- **Non-Qualifying (Assumed 60%):** £90,000. Routine iteration, taste testing, and standard quality control are excluded.
*Software & Cloud (£80k + Cloud):*
- **Qualifying (Assumed 90%):** £72,000 + Cloud costs. Developing the algorithm to solve technical incompatibilities or efficiency limits is core R&D. Cloud costs used *directly* for these simulations are now explicitly eligible.
- **Non-Qualifying (Assumed 10%):** £8,000. General maintenance or deployment not tied to experimental runs.

*Illustrative Recoverable Spend:* £132,000 (base) + Cloud costs.
*Illustrative Cash Benefit:* If your company is profitable, you deduct an extra 86% (current additional deduction rate) from taxable profits. If loss-making, you may surrender losses for a payable credit (approx. 18.6% of qualifying spend).
- **Profitable Scenario:** £132k × 86% = £113,520 reduction in taxable profit. At 25% tax, that saves ~£28,380.
- **Loss-Making Scenario:** £132k × 18.6% = ~£24,552 cash payment.
*(Note: Rates and thresholds change; these are illustrative based on standard structures.)*

**What Flips It, and The Trigger:**
The classification of the nutritionists' work flips the claim size. If their testing was merely verifying known nutrient interactions (routine), the eligible portion drops near zero for that bucket.
*Trigger:* If more than 50% of the nutritionists' time was spent on standard QC or taste panels rather than resolving unknown scientific outcomes, you must exclude the formula costs entirely to avoid investigation risk.
*Rule of Thumb:* "Did we know the answer before we started the test?" If yes, it is not R&D.
*This Week:* If you have timesheets showing "failed experiments" or "novel interaction testing," consider isolating those hours immediately to build your technical narrative.

**Routing Note:**
HMRC investigations frequently target the boundary between "product iteration" and "scientific uncertainty." You must file a technical report with your CT600 detailing the specific uncertainties resolved; bring your project logs and timesheets to a qualified UK tax advisor specializing in R&D to finalize the claim percentage and ensure the technical narrative meets HMRC's strict "competent professional" standard.
