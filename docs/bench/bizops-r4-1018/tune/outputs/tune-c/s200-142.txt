**The Call:** Do not spend the $250,000 on the pilot study yet; instead, use a fraction of that budget to secure a formal FDA Pre-Submission (Q-Sub) meeting to define the exact evidence pathway. Spending 28% of your current cash reserves ($250k/$900k) on execution before validating the regulatory strategy creates a fatal runway risk (dropping to ~14 months) without guaranteeing the Series A valuation step-up. Investors are likely hesitating not because you haven't started, but because the *cost and timeline* of the required clearance are undefined; a Q-Sub provides the de-risking data point they need at a significantly lower cost.

**The Numbers:**
*   **Current Runway:** $900,000 Cash / $45,000 Monthly Burn = **20 months**.
*   **Scenario A (Pilot Now):** ($900,000 - $250,000) / $45,000 = **~14.4 months**.
    *   *Assumption:* Burn remains flat. If the pilot reveals complications requiring more spend, runway drops further.
    *   *Risk:* 14 months is often insufficient to complete a full clinical trial, compile a 510(k) or De Novo submission, and close a Series A before cash zero.
*   **Scenario B (Q-Sub Strategy):** Estimated cost for regulatory consultancy and Q-Sub preparation: **$40,000–$60,000** (ASSUMED based on typical early-stage medtech consulting rates).
    *   Remaining Cash: ~$850,000.
    *   New Runway: **~18.8 months**.
    *   *Outcome:* You retain capital while obtaining the FDA's written feedback on your clinical protocol, which serves as the "clearance roadmap" investors require.

**What Flips It, and The Trigger:**
The decision flips if the FDA explicitly indicates in a Q-Sub that your software qualifies for an enforcement discretion policy (no clearance needed) or requires a full randomized controlled trial (RCT) costing >$1M.
*   **Trigger Value:** If the Q-Sub feedback indicates a required clinical trial timeline exceeding **12 months**, you must pause development fundraising and bridge immediately, as your current runway cannot cover the gap between study start and Series A close.
*   **Rule of Thumb:** Never commit >15% of total cash reserves to execution before the regulatory *pathway* is validated by the agency.
*   **This Week's Step:** If you have not yet filed, consider engaging a regulatory consultant specifically to draft the Q-Sub request rather than designing the pilot study itself.

**Routing Note:**
Determining whether your specific algorithm constitutes a "medical device" requiring 510(k), De Novo, or PMA clearance is a binding legal/regulatory determination under the Federal Food, Drug, and Cosmetic Act; bring your specific intended use claims and algorithm logic to an FDA regulatory counsel to confirm the submission type and whether the "Software as a Medical Device" (SaMD) action plan applies. Additionally, the term sheet valuation cap and SAFE/equity structure constitute a securities offering; have securities counsel review the investor hesitation language to ensure no unintended warranties regarding regulatory status are embedded in the final agreement.
