**The Call**
Do not reincorporate or form a subsidiary yet; your current Texas LLC is likely sufficient to *begin* insurance billing, but you must immediately secure specific insurance enrollments and contractor agreements before submitting a single claim. The decision to create a holding company or subsidiary is driven by liability isolation for multi-state expansion and potential future investor requirements, not by the mere act of mixing cash and insurance revenue streams.

**The Numbers**
Your current unit economics show a healthy cash model: 2,000 sessions/month ($180k MRR / $90) with 15 contractors.
Switching to insurance introduces a revenue drop and cost increase:
*   **Revenue Impact:** $90 (cash) → $75 (insurance est.) = **16.7% revenue decrease per session**.
*   **Cost Impact:** Operational costs increase by **20%** (assumed based on your prompt) due to billing admin, denials management, and compliance overhead.
*   **Break-even Trigger:** To maintain current gross profit, you must increase session volume by approximately **45%** to offset the combined effect of lower reimbursement and higher operating costs.
*   **Assumption:** Your "20% cost increase" refers to total operational overhead, not just cost of goods sold. If it applies only to admin staff, the volume required to break even is lower.

**What Flips It, and the Trigger**
The one input that changes the call from "stay single entity" to "form subsidiary" is **multi-state regulatory exposure**.
*   **Trigger:** The moment you contract with providers or bill patients in **one new state** outside Texas.
*   **Rule of Thumb:** Single-state operations often fit within one LLC; multi-state healthcare delivery frequently triggers "foreign qualification" requirements and varying corporate practice of medicine (CPOM) doctrines that may necessitate a Professional LLC (PLLC) or a subsidiary structure in each new jurisdiction.
*   **This Week's Step:** If you plan to expand to three new states next quarter, consider engaging healthcare counsel to map the CPOM rules in those specific states before hiring your first out-of-state therapist.

**Routing Note**
While the entity structure is a business decision, the **binding determination** of whether your specific mix of contractor therapists and billing practices violates Texas or target-state Corporate Practice of Medicine (CPOM) laws is a legal conclusion requiring licensed counsel. Additionally, enrolling as a provider with payers (Medicare/Medicaid/private) requires verifying that your LLC type is an eligible provider type under each payer's specific rules, which varies by state and insurer.
