**The Call**
Do not proceed with the GOTS certification solely to justify a $7 price hike based on current data; the math shows you need an unrealistic 86% purchase rate from your existing list just to break even on the fixed fee in year one. The $2 increase in variable costs should be absorbed or addressed via minor efficiency gains, while the certification decision requires validated willingness-to-pay before spending $15,000.

**The Numbers**
*Formula:* Annual Net Impact = (Units Sold × New Margin) − (Units Sold × Old Margin) − Certification Fixed Costs.
*Current State:* Price $35, Cost $12 (new), Margin $23.
*Proposed State:* Price $42, Cost $12 (new), Margin $30.
*Margin Expansion:* $7 per unit.
*Fixed Cost:* $15,000 (ignoring annual fees for this snapshot).

*Break-Even Analysis (Units needed to cover the $15k fee):*
$15,000 / $7 margin expansion = **2,143 units** must be sold *in addition to* whatever volume you currently sustain, or simply 2,143 total units if the certification is the only change.

*Scenario A (Status Quo Loyalty):* If you sell to your full base of 5,000 customers once this year at the new price:
Revenue Lift: 5,000 × $7 = $35,000.
Net Gain: $35,000 − $15,000 = **+$20,000**.
*Assumption (UNVERIFIED):* 100% of your 5,000 loyal customers will buy at $42 without churn. This is highly unlikely.

*Scenario B (Realistic Elasticity):* If only 60% of your base buys at the higher price (3,000 units):
Revenue Lift: 3,000 × $7 = $21,000.
Net Gain: $21,000 − $15,000 = **+$6,000**.
*Assumption (UNVERIFIED):* 40% churn or non-purchase rate due to price sensitivity.

*Scenario C (The Trap):* If the price hike reduces volume to below 2,143 units, you lose cash relative to not certifying.

**What Flips It, and The Trigger**
The single input that changes this call is **Price Elasticity of Demand** among your specific 5,000 customers.
*Trigger:* If a pre-launch survey or A/B test indicates less than **43% conversion** at the $42 price point (2,143 units / 5,000 customers), the certification fails its year-one cash flow test.
*Rule of Thumb:* In niche apparel, a 20% price increase often triggers >30% volume drop unless the brand has monopoly-like power.
*Step this week:* Run a "fake door" test or email survey to 500 random customers offering the $42 item with the GOTS logo prominent. If click-to-buy intent is below 45%, do not certify.
