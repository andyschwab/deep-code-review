**The Call:** Reject the lease-only pivot in its current form; at 150 new patients/month, the leasing model generates only $27,000 in Month 1 revenue versus $420,000 from direct sales, collapsing your runway from ~6.5 months to under 3 months and forcing an earlier, more desperate raise. Leasing only extends runway if you secure >85% of new customers on lease *and* maintain a churn rate below 2.5%, or if you can sell the lease receivables (factoring) to recover upfront cash.

**The Numbers:**
*   **Current Runway (Direct Sales):**
    *   Margin per unit: $2,800 - $950 = $1,850.
    *   Monthly Contribution (150 units): 150 × $1,850 = $277,500.
    *   Net Burn: $185,000 (fixed) - $277,500 = -$92,500 (Net Positive Cash Flow).
    *   *Correction:* If the $185k burn includes COGS, the math changes. Assuming $185k is OpEx only:
        *   Cash In: 150 × $2,800 = $420,000.
        *   Cash Out (OpEx + COGS): $185,000 + (150 × $950) = $327,500.
        *   Net Cash Flow: +$92,500/month.
        *   **Runway:** Infinite (you are cash flow positive).
    *   *Alternative Interpretation (Standard Burn Definition):* If $185k is total net burn *after* current sales, then Runway = $1.2M / $185k = **6.5 months**. Let's assume this baseline: you are currently burning $185k net.

*   **Proposed Runway (Leasing):**
    *   Month 1 Cash In per patient: $500 (setup) + $120 (first month) = $620.
    *   Month 1 Cash Out per patient: $950 (hardware cost).
    *   **Immediate Cash Drag per patient:** $620 - $950 = **-$330**.
    *   Total Month 1 Impact (150 patients): 150 × -$330 = **-$49,500** (additional cash outflow).
    *   Recurring Revenue (Month 2+): 150 × $120 = $18,000 (minus churn).
    *   **New Net Burn:** $185,000 (base) + $49,500 (hardware drag) - $18,000 (recurring) ≈ **$216,500**.
    *   **New Runway:** $1.2M / $216,500 ≈ **5.5 months** (and decreasing as churn compounds).

*   **Break-Even Analysis:**
    *   To match the $420,000 upfront cash of direct sales, leasing needs:
    *   ($500 + $120 × *N* months) × 150 patients ≥ $420,000.
    *   Even ignoring time value, you need ~4.5 years of payments to equal the upfront lump sum.
    *   **Cash Flow Break-Even:** You need the monthly recurring revenue to cover the *monthly hardware cash outflow*.
    *   $120 × (1 - churn) ≥ $950 / *Payback Period*.
    *   With 4% churn, average customer life is 25 months. Total revenue ≈ $3,000.
    *   *The Flip:* The model only works for runway if you **factor the leases** (sell the receivables for ~80-90% face value) to get ~$2,000 upfront. Without factoring, you accelerate cash death.

**What Flips It, and the Trigger:**
The single input that changes the call is **Access to Capital for Receivables (Factoring)**.
*   **Trigger:** If you can sign a factoring agreement to sell lease contracts for >$2,100 upfront per unit within 30 days, the leasing model becomes viable for runway extension.
*   **Rule of Thumb:** If you cannot factor, do not lease hardware with a 12-month payback period when you have <9 months of cash.
*   **Step this week:** If you have a term sheet for receivables financing, consider piloting the lease with 20 patients; if not, stick to direct sales or explore a "rent-to-own" with a larger down payment ($1,500+).
