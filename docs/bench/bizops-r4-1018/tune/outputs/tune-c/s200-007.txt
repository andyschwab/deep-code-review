**The Call:** Proceed with extreme caution; opening a staffed buying office in Quito creates a **high probability** of triggering a Permanent Establishment (PE) in Ecuador, exposing the portion of your global profit attributable to that office to Ecuadorian corporate income tax. The $120,000 operational cost does not reduce this risk; rather, it establishes the financial scale of the profit attribution if a PE is found.

**The Numbers & Criteria:**
Under Ecuadorian domestic law and the **US-Ecuador Income Tax Treaty (1967)**, a PE is generally triggered by a "fixed place of business" through which business is wholly or partly carried on.
*   **The Trigger:** Employing three local staff in a physical office in Quito to manage relationships and quality control typically satisfies the "fixed place" and "personnel" tests.
*   **The Scope of Taxation:** If a PE is established, Ecuador taxes **only the profit attributable** to that office (Article 7 of the Treaty), not your entire global profit. However, "attribution" can be aggressive; tax authorities may argue that your buying office enables the entire supply chain, attributing a significant portion of the margin to Quito.
*   **The Cost Factor:** The $120,000 annual cost is a deductible expense for the PE, but it does not act as a shield against PE status. In fact, having budgeted staff and an office confirms the "fixed place" existence.
*   **Treaty Protections:** The US-Ecuador treaty limits taxation to profits attributable to the PE but does not exempt the PE itself if the physical presence exists. Note that this treaty is dated (1967); modern interpretations by Ecuadorian authorities (SRI) may lean toward broader definitions than older US precedents.

**What Flips It & The Trigger:**
The specific activity that flips this from "low risk" to "certain PE" is granting your local staff the authority to **conclude contracts** or habitually exercise that authority.
*   **Safe Harbor Activities (General Rule):** Staff limited to "preparatory or auxiliary" activities (e.g., collecting market data, quality inspection *without* final acceptance authority, liaison work) often avoid PE status.
*   **The Risk Line:** If your Quito staff negotiate prices, finalize bean purchases, or manage quality control to the extent that their approval is the final step before shipment, they are likely performing core business functions, creating a PE.
*   **Decision Threshold:** If local staff sign any purchase orders or have binding authority on price/quality, assume PE status is triggered.
*   **This Week's Step:** If you must open the office, consider structuring the Quito team strictly as "liaison" personnel with **zero contract-signing authority**, ensuring all final purchase contracts are signed digitally or physically in Vermont.

**Routing Note:**
You must engage an Ecuadorian tax attorney or a cross-border CPA specializing in Andean tax law to: (1) Confirm the current enforcement stance of the Ecuadorian SRI on the 1967 US-Ecuador Treaty regarding buying offices, and (2) Draft local employment contracts that explicitly limit authority to "preparatory/auxiliary" functions to mitigate PE risk.

*Note: The $120k cost suggests a material operation, making it harder to argue the office is merely "auxiliary."*
