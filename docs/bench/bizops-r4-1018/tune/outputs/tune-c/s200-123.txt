**The Call**
Do not proceed with hiring internal legal staff or marketing a bundled "legal and mental health" product until a California-licensed attorney validates your triage workflow; the risk of Unauthorized Practice of Law (UPL) and corporate practice of law violations is high enough to threaten your existing contracts if triggered.

**The Numbers**
Your projected unit economics rely on a $40 cost saving per employee, but this ignores the potential liability cost of a UPL finding.
*   **Current Margin Contribution:** $250 price - (Therapist Cost + $40 Outsourced Legal) = *Unknown Base Margin*.
*   **Proposed Margin Contribution:** $220 price - (Therapist Cost + Internal Legal Overhead).
*   **Assumption (UNVERIFIED):** You assume internal overhead is <$20/user to net the $40 saving vs. the $40 outsourced fee while dropping price by $30. If internal overhead exceeds $20/user, you are compressing margins to chase volume.
*   **Result:** If internal overhead is $25/user, your new margin is $220 - ($Therapist + $25) vs old $250 - ($Therapist + $40). You lose $5 per unit immediately, requiring the 15 new clients just to break even on the price cut, before accounting for legal defense costs.

**What Flips It, and the Trigger**
The single input that changes this from a margin play to an existential threat is **who performs the initial legal triage**.
*   **The Trigger:** If a non-lawyer care coordinator assesses a specific employee's legal facts to recommend a course of action (e.g., "You should file for restraining order X"), you have likely crossed into UPL.
*   **Stop/Pivot Rule:** If your care coordinators cannot limit their script to "Here is the contact info for our licensed attorney; they will call you within 24 hours," pause the hiring plan.
*   **This Week's Step:** If your current triage script includes any decision-making logic beyond scheduling, consider freezing the internalization project and drafting a specific scope-of-work question for counsel.

**Regulatory Red Flags & Required Routing**
In California, the specific red flags for your model are:
1.  **Unauthorized Practice of Law (Bus. & Prof. Code § 6125):** Non-lawyers (care coordinators) cannot interpret laws, apply laws to specific facts, or recommend specific legal remedies. Triage that involves listening to facts and suggesting a legal path is often deemed UPL.
2.  **Corporate Practice of Law:** California strictly prohibits non-lawyer owned entities (or entities where non-lawyers control legal work) from practicing law. If your company employs the lawyers directly but non-lawyer managers dictate how legal services are delivered or marketed as a bundled product, you may violate ethical rules regarding lawyer independence.
3.  **Bundling/Feeforwarding:** Billing a single "package price" where the legal portion is not separately accounted for can look like a non-lawyer entity selling legal services, which is prohibited.

**Activities Care Coordinators Must Avoid:**
*   Assessing the merits of a legal case based on employee descriptions.
*   Recommending specific legal forms, statutes, or strategies.
*   Representing that the *company* provides legal advice rather than facilitating access to an attorney.
*   Managing the attorney's workload in a way that compromises professional judgment.
