**The Call**
You cannot justify a 10x valuation leap on last year's 2x revenue growth alone; the narrative must shift immediately from historical performance to forward-looking unit economics, specifically proving that your current 4% monthly churn is a temporary artifact of early-stage friction that will drop to <1.5% as you mature, thereby exploding your Lifetime Value (LTV).

**The Numbers**
The investor's skepticism relies on a standard revenue multiple (typically 10x–20x for SaaS). At $2.4M ARR, a $100M valuation implies a ~42x multiple, which is unjustifiable without extreme efficiency or growth projections.
*   **Current Burn:** 15 employees × $180k = $2.7M/year.
*   **Current Runway Status:** Your burn ($2.7M) exceeds your revenue ($2.4M). You are burning cash to grow.
*   **Churn Impact (The Critical Variable):**
    *   *Formula:* LTV ≈ (ARPA × Gross Margin) / Monthly Churn.
    *   *Scenario A (Status Quo):* 4% monthly churn implies an average customer life of ~25 months. This caps LTV and makes the CAC payback period likely unsustainable.
    *   *Scenario B (The Promise):* If you can demonstrate a path to 1.5% monthly churn (industry standard for enterprise API), customer life extends to ~67 months.
    *   *Result:* Reducing churn from 4% to 1.5% nearly triples your LTV without adding a single dollar of new revenue.

**What Flips It, and The Trigger**
The single input that changes the call is **Net Revenue Retention (NRR)**. If your existing customers are expanding usage faster than they churn, your NRR could be >120% despite the 4% logo churn.
*   **Trigger:** If your NRR is below 110%, the valuation is unsupported. If you can prove NRR >130% via pilot expansions or committed upsells in the pipeline, the 42x multiple becomes a bet on compounding expansion rather than new logos.
*   **Actionable Step:** If your NRR is currently unmeasured or <100%, consider halting new hiring to focus entirely on stabilizing the existing base before selling the "growth" story.
