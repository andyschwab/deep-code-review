**The Call**
Stick with the **seat-based model ($49/seat)** for now, but introduce a **hybrid tier** for high-volume instance users. Switching entirely to an instance-based model ($500/app) creates a 154% price jump for your pilot example ($9,800 → $25,000), which poses an unacceptably high churn risk during early adoption. The instance model penalizes the exact behavior you want to encourage (building more internal tools), whereas the seat model scales naturally with team growth.

**The Numbers**
*Current State:* 160 seats × $49 = **$7,840 MRR** (rounded to your $8k).
*Burn:* $35,000/month.
*Runway:* $8,000 / $35,000 ≈ **0.23 months** of operating expense covered by revenue (you are pre-product-market fit on cash flow).

*Scenario A (Seat Model - Base Case):*
Enterprise grows to 200 devs.
Formula: $Seats \times \$49$
Result: $200 \times \$49 = \mathbf{\$9,800}$ MRR.
*Assumption:* Churn remains <5% because price scales linearly with headcount, a standard budget line item.

*Scenario B (Instance Model - Proposed):*
Enterprise runs 50 apps.
Formula: $Apps \times \$500$
Result: $50 \times \$500 = \mathbf{\$25,000}$ MRR.
*Assumption (High Risk):* Customer accepts a 2.5x price increase without reducing app count or churning.

*Scenario C (Hybrid Model - Recommended Test):*
Base: $49/seat + Overage fee after threshold.
Example: First 10 apps included; $250 per additional app.
Result for 50 apps: $(200 \times \$49) + (40 \times \$250) = \$9,800 + \$10,000 = \mathbf{\$19,800}$.
*Assumption:* This captures extra value while keeping the base entry price familiar.

**What Flips It, and The Trigger**
The deciding input is **Price Elasticity of Demand regarding "shadow IT" apps**. If your customers are currently spinning up dozens of low-value test apps, the instance model will force them to delete those apps, reducing your product's stickiness.
*Trigger:* If >30% of deployed apps are inactive after 90 days, the instance model is failing to capture value (customers are hoarding licenses but not using them).
*Rule of Thumb:* Enterprise software pricing should rarely exceed 10-15% of a team's fully loaded cost for the problem being solved. If $25k exceeds this threshold for a 200-person team, churn is imminent.
*Action This Week:* If your pilot customers have >20 apps deployed, consider offering a "Platform Fee" add-on rather than changing the core seat price.

**Data to Gather for a Definitive Decision**
To move from assumption to fact, you must instrument the following before your next pricing review:
1.  **App Activation Rate:** Percentage of deployed instances with active daily users. (If low, instance pricing kills utilization).
2.  **Expansion Revenue Coefficient:** Does adding a new app lead to adding more seats? (If yes, seat pricing captures the upside automatically).
3.  **Budget Owner Persona:** Are you selling to the Head of Engineering (owns headcount budget) or the CTO/CIO (owns infrastructure budget)? Seat models sell easier to Eng Heads; instance models sell to Infrastructure leaders.
