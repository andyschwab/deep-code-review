**The Call:** Reject the current model; your unit economics are deeply negative because a 40% three-month churn destroys Lifetime Value (LTV) before it covers Customer Acquisition Cost (CAC). You must prioritize reducing churn over raising prices, as a price increase on such a leaky bucket will likely accelerate attrition without fixing the fundamental value gap.

**The Numbers:**
*   **Formula:** $LTV = \frac{ARPU \times Gross Margin}{Churn Rate}$. Break-even occurs when $LTV \geq CAC$.
*   **Inputs:** ARPU = $12; CAC = $25; Fixed Costs = $4,000/mo.
*   **Churn Assumption (Critical):** You cited "40% churn after the first three months." To compute, we must annualize or average this.
    *   *Scenario A (Aggressive Decay):* If 40% of the *remaining* base leaves every month (40% monthly churn), average life is 2.5 months. LTV ≈ $30.
    *   *Scenario B (Cohort Drop-off):* If you retain 60% of users after month 3 and churn slows significantly thereafter (e.g., to 5% monthly), average life extends. However, given "high early churn," let's assume a conservative **15% monthly churn rate** average to model a "best case" of your current trajectory.
*   **Computed LTV (at 15% monthly churn):** $12 / 0.15 = $80.
    *   *Result:* $80 LTV > $25 CAC. This looks positive *until* fixed costs are applied.
*   **Computed LTV (at 30% monthly churn - more likely given your description):** $12 / 0.30 = $40.
    *   *Result:* $40 LTV > $25 CAC. Still technically positive contribution margin ($15/user), but fragile.
*   **The Real Failure (Cash Flow & Fixed Costs):**
    *   Monthly Revenue (Month 6): 3,000 users × $12 = $36,000 (Top line, ignoring churn decay for a snapshot).
    *   Variable Cost (CAC amortized): If you stop spending on marketing, CAC is sunk. If you continue to replace churned users at $25: 3,000 × 30% churn = 900 replacements × $25 = $22,500/mo new spend.
    *   Contribution Margin: ($36,000 Revenue - $22,500 Re-acquisition - $4,000 Fixed) = **$9,500 Profit?**
    *   *Correction for Churn Reality:* If you *stop* marketing, your base collapses. At 30% monthly churn, you lose 900 users/month. In 3 months, you lose ~65% of your base. Your revenue drops to ~$12,000/mo while fixed costs remain $4,000.
    *   **Break-even Point per User:** With $12 price and ~$0 variable server cost per user, you need ~2.1 months of retention just to cover the $25 CAC ($25 / $12). If 40% leave before month 3, a massive portion of users *never* pay back their CAC.

**What Flips It, and the Trigger:**
The single input that changes the call is **Month-3 Retention Rate**.
*   **Trigger:** If you cannot retain >50% of users past month 3 within the next 60 days, the business is insolvent regardless of price.
*   **Rule of Thumb:** LTV:CAC should be >3:1. Currently, if true churn is high, you are near 1.5:1 or worse.
*   **Step:** If retention at day 90 is <50%, consider pivoting the onboarding flow or content delivery immediately rather than tweaking price.

**12-Month Profitability Projection (Current Metrics Flat):**
Assuming 30% monthly churn and *no new marketing spend* (letting the base decay):
*   **Months 1-3:** Revenue drops from $36k to ~$15k. Cash burn begins as fixed costs eat the shrinking margin.
*   **Months 4-8:** User base shrinks to <500. Revenue ~$6k. Fixed costs ($4k) consume 66% of revenue.
*   **Months 9-12:** Base stabilizes at a tiny niche (~100 users). Revenue $1.2k. **Net Loss:** You will burn through seed funding covering the $4k/month fixed overhead while revenue evaporates. The model fails because **fixed costs are too high for the decaying active user base**, and CAC is never recovered for the 40% who leave early.
