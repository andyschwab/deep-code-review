**The Call:** Do not pass the full €7.50 increase to the customer; the projected 30% volume drop destroys profitability. Instead, absorb a portion of the tax to keep the retail price near €68–€69, accepting a compressed margin temporarily while you validate the prescription exemption.

**The Numbers:**
*Current Baseline:*
Revenue = 10,000 units × €65 = €650,000.
Net Profit (25% margin) = €162,500.
Implied Total Cost (COGS + OpEx) = €487,500 (€48.75/unit).

*Scenario A: Pass Full Tax (Price €72.50, Volume -30%)*
New Volume = 7,000 units.
New Revenue = 7,000 × €72.50 = €507,500.
New Tax Liability = 7,000 × €7.50 = €52,500.
Remaining Revenue after Tax = €455,000.
Less Total Costs (assumed fixed per unit €48.75 × 7,000) = €341,250.
**Estimated Net Profit:** €113,750.
*Result:* Profit drops ~30% from baseline.

*Scenario B: Absorb Cost to Maintain Margin Target (Hypothetical)*
To maintain €162,500 profit with 7,000 units, you would need a price impossible to sustain given the tax.
Let's test your proposed "Absorb to 10% margin" at current volume (assuming no volume drop for this calculation):
Revenue = €650,000.
Target Net Profit (10%) = €65,000.
Total Costs + Tax = €585,000.
Existing Costs = €487,500.
Tax Bill = €75,000 (10k × €7.50).
Total Outflow = €562,500.
**Estimated Net Profit:** €87,500.
*Result:* Better than Scenario A, but still a 46% profit drop from baseline.

You cannot match current profit if the tax applies to all units and you cap margin at 10%.

**What Flips It, and The Trigger:**
The **prescription exemption** is the single variable that flips the strategy. If >40% of your customers can obtain a prescription, you segment your pricing: tax-free for prescribed users (maintain €65) and taxed for OTC users (raise price moderately to €68).
