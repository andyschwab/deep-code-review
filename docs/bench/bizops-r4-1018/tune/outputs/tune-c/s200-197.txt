The deal flips if the grant requires **real-time data integration** that forces a architectural overhaul of your learning platform, delaying product iterations by more than 3 months.
*   **Trigger Value:** If the reporting mandate requires engineering changes exceeding **80 hours** (approx. 2 weeks of a developer's time) before the first disbursement.
*   **Action:** Review the "data deliverables" section of the agreement this week. If it demands custom API builds or non-standard data schema changes, **consider negotiating a simplified reporting schedule** or declining the funds if the technical debt threatens your core roadmap.

**Routing Note:**
Government grants are legally binding contracts with specific statutory reporting frequencies (often quarterly or annually) and clawback provisions for non-compliance or mission deviation. You must have a qualified grant administrator or attorney review the specific "terms and conditions" and "audit rights" clauses to confirm if your current team structure satisfies the "internal control" requirements and to define the exact threshold for "material change" that would restrict your ability to pivot curriculum.

Most accessibility grants focus on **outcome tracking** (e.g., number of veterans trained, completion rates, satisfaction scores) rather than strict financial audits for amounts under $250,000. The "vague" language you see is likely intentional flexibility, but it requires you to proactively propose a reporting cadence (e.g., "We propose submitting a quarterly impact summary") rather than waiting for them to dictate a burdensome format. Failure to meet reporting usually results in a demand to repay unspent funds or funds associated with unverified outcomes, not necessarily the full amount if partial performance is proven.

The trade-off is capital for constraint: you gain 6 months of life but lose the freedom to silently pivot away from the "veteran accessibility" mission without risking repayment. As long as your product iterations remain within the scope of "expanding accessibility training," your agility remains intact.
