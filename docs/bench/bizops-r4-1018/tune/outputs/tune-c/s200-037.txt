**The Call:** Your quiz-based customization likely keeps you in the dietary supplement category *only if* you avoid disease-treatment claims and stay within established nutrient safety limits; however, the moment your algorithm recommends dosages to treat, cure, or prevent a specific disease (e.g., "fix your anemia"), you cross into unapproved drug territory, which is an existential threat requiring immediate legal validation before any funding round.

**The Regulatory Line & Criteria:**
The FDA distinguishes supplements from drugs based on **intended use**, determined by your labeling, marketing, and recommendation logic, not just the physical act of mixing.
1.  **Disease Claims vs. Structure/Function:** If your quiz output or marketing suggests the pack treats a disease (e.g., "for diabetes management"), it is a drug. If it supports normal structure/function (e.g., "supports healthy blood sugar levels"), it remains a supplement. The red flag is any algorithm logic that maps user symptoms to therapeutic outcomes.
2.  **Compounding vs. Manufacturing:** Mixing ingredients is generally permissible for supplements if you follow Current Good Manufacturing Practices (cGMP). It becomes illegal drug compounding if you are effectively creating a new chemical entity or dosage form specifically prescribed for an individual's medical condition without a licensed pharmacist's oversight.
3.  **Dosage Triggers:** Recommending doses exceeding the Tolerable Upper Intake Levels (ULs) set by the Food and Nutrition Board is a major red flag. If your "personalization" pushes nutrients beyond safe daily limits based on quiz answers, the FDA views this as an unsafe adulterant or an unapproved new drug.

**Illustrative Risk Assessment (UNVERIFIED):**
*Assumption:* Your quiz asks about medical conditions (e.g., "Do you have fatigue?") and recommends high-dose B12 specifically to "treat" it.
*Result:* This is a disease claim. The product is legally an unapproved new drug.
*Assumption:* Your quiz asks about diet gaps (e.g., "Do you eat meat?") and recommends standard multivitamin levels to "support energy."
*Result:* This is likely a structure/function claim. The product remains a supplement.

**What Flips It & The Trigger:**
The single input that changes your status from viable business to shutdown risk is **the specific wording of the quiz output and the dosage logic**.
*Trigger:* If your current recommendation engine outputs any phrase linking a nutrient to a disease state, or calculates a dose >100% of the UL for any nutrient based on user input, you must pause customer shipments involving those packs immediately.
*Rule of Thumb:* "Treat/Cure/Prevent [Disease]" = Drug. "Support/Maintain [Normal Function]" = Supplement.
*This Week's Step:* If your quiz logic maps symptoms to therapeutic doses, consider hard-coding a disclaimer that the output is "not medical advice" and capping all recommendations at 100% of the Daily Value until counsel reviews the algorithm.
