**The Call:** Elect accelerated depreciation (likely 100% bonus depreciation or MACRS 5-year) for your new medical equipment to maximize immediate tax shields and improve current cash flow, given your rapid expansion and high loan service costs. This is the standard treatment for growing capital-intensive businesses to defer taxes, not avoid them.

**The Numbers:**
*Formula:* Tax Savings = Depreciable Basis × Marginal Tax Rate.
*Inputs:* $300,000 (Basis); $8,000/mo loan payment (non-deductible principal portion reduces cash but not taxable income); $45,000/mo revenue.
*Assumptions:* ASSUMED 21% Federal Corporate Tax Rate (C-Corp) or pass-through equivalent; ASSUMED Texas has no state corporate income tax (franchise tax thresholds vary); ASSUMED equipment qualifies as 5-year property under MACRS.

| Scenario | Year 1 Deduction | Est. Tax Savings (Yr 1) | 5-Year Total Deduction | Cash Flow Impact (Yr 1) |
| :--- | :--- | :--- | :--- | :--- |
| **Accelerated** (Bonus/MACRS) | ~$300,000 (if 100% bonus) | ~$63,000 | $300,000 | +$63,000 (tax deferred) |
| **Straight-Line** (5-Year) | $60,000 | ~$12,600 | $300,000 | +$12,600 (tax deferred) |

*Note:* Loan principal repayments ($8,000/mo) are cash outflows but not tax deductions; only interest is deductible. Accelerated depreciation creates a larger "paper loss" now to offset cash spent on loan principal.

**What Flips It, and the Trigger:**
The decision flips if your marginal tax rate is significantly higher in future years than it is today.
*Trigger:* If you project net taxable income to triple within 24 months while current rates are low, consider electing out of bonus depreciation to save deductions for later.
*Rule of Thumb:* Defer taxes when growth is high; recognize income when growth stabilizes.
*Step:* If your projected 2024 taxable income exceeds $100,000, consider accelerating; if below $50,000, model the benefit of carrying forward deductions.

Confirm eligibility for 100% bonus depreciation vs. Section 179 expensing limits with your CPA, as Texas franchise tax calculations and federal taxable income thresholds may alter the optimal election.
