**The Call:** Yes, renting commercial kitchen space in Austin, Denver, and Seattle almost certainly creates a corporate income tax and sales tax filing obligation (nexus) in Texas, Colorado, and Washington, regardless of your revenue level. You must register and file in these states immediately upon starting operations; do not wait for a specific revenue threshold to be met.

**The Numbers & Rules:**
Your fear of cash drain is valid, but the risk of *penalties* for non-compliance is higher. Nexus (the connection required for a state to tax you) is triggered by two main tests:
1.  **Physical Presence Nexus:** This is your primary trigger. Leasing space, even part-time (6 hours/day), constitutes a physical presence.
    *   **Texas:** Leasing real property creates nexus. There is no "minimum time" exemption for commercial leases.
    *   **Colorado:** Maintaining an office or place of business (including a rented kitchen) creates nexus.
    *   **Washington:** Having a physical location, even temporarily or part-time, creates nexus.
2.  **Economic Nexus:** This applies if you exceed revenue thresholds *without* physical presence. Since you have physical presence, these thresholds are secondary but still relevant for sales tax collection.
    *   **Texas:** $500,000 remote seller threshold (irrelevant here as you have physical presence).
    *   **Colorado:** $100,000 or 200 transactions.
    *   **Washington:** $100,000 or 200 transactions.

**Assumptions & Impact:**
*   **Assumption:** The $3,000/month rent grants you exclusive or shared use of the facility under a formal lease/license agreement.
*   **Result:** You are no longer just an Illinois entity. You are a foreign entity qualified to do business in three new states.
*   **Cost Estimate (Illustrative):** Beyond the rent, expect annual state franchise taxes or minimum fees (e.g., WA Business & Occupation tax, TX Franchise Tax, CO Annual Report fees) ranging from $500 to $2,000 per state, plus CPA fees for multi-state returns ($1,500+ per state).

**What Flips It & The Trigger:**
*   **The Flip:** The only scenario where you might avoid this is if the agreement is strictly a "service contract" where the kitchen owner retains full control, provides all labor, and you merely buy a finished service, not space. However, if you bring your own ingredients, use your own equipment, or control the production process, it is a lease.
*   **Trigger:** The day you sign the lease or drop off your first batch of ingredients.
*   **Rule of Thumb:** Physical presence overrides economic thresholds. If you touch the ground, you owe the tax.
