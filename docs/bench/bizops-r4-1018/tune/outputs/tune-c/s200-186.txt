**Forecasting Framework:**
To solve the timing mismatch, structure your forecast in three distinct buckets rather than a single checking account balance:

1.  **The Liability Bucket (Restricted):** Treat upfront tuition as unearned revenue until delivered. Immediately move $22,000 × 12 = $264,000 into a separate high-yield savings account. This money is legally and operationally "spent" already; it exists only to pay future monthly bills.
2.  **The Operating Buffer (Liquid):** Keep the remaining $36,000 in your operating account. This covers the surplus needed for unexpected costs or the slight timing lag between billing and payroll.
3.  **The Expansion Fund (Zero-Balanced):** Currently, this bucket is $0. You cannot fund expansion from the current cohort's tuition because every dollar is allocated to the current year's delivery. Expansion capital must come from the *next* cohort's upfront payments or external financing, not the current reserve.

**What Flips It, and The Trigger:**
The single input that changes this verdict is **Churn/Refund Liability**. If you owe refunds for dropouts, your effective revenue drops while fixed costs (rent/salaries) likely remain static.
*   **Trigger Value:** If student dropout exceeds 6 students (12% of cohort) assuming a pro-rata refund policy, your surplus evaporates, and you risk a cash crunch by Month 10.
*   **Stop/Pivot Trigger:** If enrollment for the *next* year does not reach 45 students by **March 31st** (end of Q3), freeze all hiring for the second location.
