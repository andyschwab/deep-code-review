**The Call:** Do not rely on separating the fuel surcharge to avoid sales tax; in most jurisdictions, ancillary fees tied to a taxable medical service are themselves taxable regardless of how they are itemized on the invoice. Your current structure likely creates a false sense of security while increasing audit risk due to inconsistent application across five states. The safest compliant path is to treat the entire charge (base + surcharge) as a single taxable medical service receipt unless a specific state statute explicitly exempts "transportation" as a distinct non-medical service, which is rare for mobile phlebotomy.

**The Numbers & Rules:**
Sales tax on medical services is highly state-specific. Generally, if the underlying service (phlebotomy) is taxable, any mandatory or customary fees required to complete that service (like travel/fuel) are part of the taxable sales price.
*   **Ohio:** Medical services are generally exempt from sales tax if performed by licensed professionals. If your service qualifies for this exemption, the fuel surcharge is also exempt. If not, the surcharge is taxable as part of the service.
*   **Kentucky:** Medical services are exempt. Ancillary fees follow the exemption status of the primary service.
*   **Indiana:** Medical services are exempt. Separately stated transportation *might* be argued as non-taxable if it is optional, but if it is a condition of the sale, it is likely taxable.
*   **Illinois:** Medical services are exempt. However, Illinois strictly scrutinizes "bundled" transactions. If the service is exempt, the total is exempt. If taxable, the total is taxable.
*   **Pennsylvania:** Medical services are exempt. Transportation charges separately stated on an invoice for an exempt service generally remain exempt.

**Assumption:** I am assuming your nurses are licensed professionals and the blood draws are for diagnostic purposes, which typically triggers the medical service exemption in these states. If your service is deemed "non-medical" (e.g., wellness-only without a doctor's order), the entire amount becomes taxable in states that tax services, and the surcharge follows suit.

**What Flips It:**
The determining factor is not the billing structure (bundled vs. separate) but the **taxability of the underlying phlebotomy service** in each specific state.
*   **Trigger:** If any state determines your specific type of draw is not a "exempt medical service," the fuel surcharge immediately becomes taxable regardless of separation.
*   **Action:** Verify the exemption status of "mobile phlebotomy" specifically with a local tax authority in each of the five states. Do not assume "medical" automatically equals "exempt" without checking state-specific codes for mobile vs. clinic-based distinctions.

**Routing Note:**
Sales tax nexus and the definition of taxable "gross receipts" are binding legal determinations that vary by municipality and state statute. You must engage a CPA or tax attorney licensed in OH, KY, IN, IL, and PA to confirm whether your specific mobile model qualifies for the medical exemption and whether separately stated logistics fees are legally severable in those jurisdictions.
