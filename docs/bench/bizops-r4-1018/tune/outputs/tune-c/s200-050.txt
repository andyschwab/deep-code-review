**The Call:** Do not claim your device "helps allergy sufferers" or treats a condition; this phrasing almost certainly triggers FDA medical device classification, which would legally require you to halt sales until clearance is obtained. Instead, pivot your marketing to strictly environmental performance claims (e.g., "removes 99% of pollen and pet dander from cabin air") without linking the removal to a health outcome or specific patient group.

**The Numbers & Regulatory Thresholds:**
The distinction relies on the intended use defined by your labeling, not just the hardware.
*   **General Wellness/Environmental Claim (Tier 2):** Statements about improving air quality or removing particulates.
    *   *Example:* "Captures 99% of airborne pollen."
    *   *Regulatory Status:* Typically regulated as a consumer good (FTC/CARB), not a medical device.
*   **Medical Claim (Tier 3):** Statements implying diagnosis, cure, mitigation, treatment, or prevention of a disease or condition.
    *   *Example:* "Helps allergy sufferers breathe easier" or "Prevents allergic reactions."
    *   *Regulatory Status:* Classifies the product as a medical device under the Federal Food, Drug, and Cosmetic Act.

**Assumptions:**
*   **ASSUMED:** Your current manufacturing ($45) and sales price ($120) do not account for the $50k–$200k+ in clinical testing and legal fees required for FDA 510(k) clearance.
*   **ASSUMED:** "Helps allergy sufferers" is interpreted by the FDA as a claim to mitigate a condition (allergies), which is a medical indication.

**What Flips It, and The Trigger:**
The trigger is the word "allergy" connected to a benefit for a person rather than a statistic about the air.
*   **Trigger Value:** If your copy includes "for allergies," "relief," "sufferers," or "asthma," you cross into medical device territory.
*   **Stop/Pivot Rule:** If the claim answers "What does this do for my health?" rather than "What does this remove from the air?", stop immediately.
*   **Actionable Step:** This week, rewrite all copy to focus exclusively on the *filtration efficiency* of the media (e.g., "HEPA-grade filtration removes 99% of particulates") and remove all references to human symptoms or conditions.

**Routing Note:**
FDA classification is a binding legal determination (Tier 3). Before launching the new campaign, pay a regulatory counsel specializing in FDA Class I/II devices to review your specific ad copy and packaging labels to confirm they do not constitute "intended use" as a medical device. This is time-sensitive; launching with an unapproved medical claim can trigger warning letters and mandatory recalls.
