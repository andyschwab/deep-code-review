**The Call**
Do not recognize the $250,000 as immediate revenue; under standard accounting principles (US GAAP), you must defer and amortize these upfront fees over the term of the agreement (or the period you provide substantial services), while recognizing the 4% royalty as earned each month.

**The Numbers**
The accounting treatment splits your cash inflow into two distinct revenue streams with different recognition timing:
*   **Upfront Fees:** $250,000 total cash received.
    *   *Immediate Recognition (Incorrect):* $250,000 revenue in Year 1.
    *   *Amortized Recognition (Standard):* Assuming a 10-year straight-line amortization, **$25,000 per year** ($250,000 / 10 years) is recognized as revenue. The remaining $225,000 sits on your balance sheet as "Deferred Revenue" (a liability) until earned over time.
*   **Royalties:** 4% of sub-franchisee gross revenue.
    *   *Recognition:* Recognized fully in the month the sub-franchisee generates the sales. If your 10 operators generate $50,000/month each ($500,000 total), your monthly revenue is **$20,000** ($500,000 × 4%), or **$240,000** annually.

**Impact on Profitability**
Choosing the correct amortization method significantly lowers your reported top-line revenue for the current fiscal year compared to booking it all upfront. Instead of showing $490,000 in total revenue ($250k fees + $240k royalties), your Year 1 income statement would reflect approximately **$265,000** ($25k amortized fees + $240k royalties). This prevents a artificial spike in profitability that reverses in future years, ensuring your financial statements accurately reflect the ongoing nature of the franchise relationship.

**What Flips It, and the Trigger**
The specific amortization period could change if your contractual obligations to provide "substantial initial services" (like site selection, training, or grand opening support) are completed in less than the full 10-year term.
*   *Trigger:* If your initial service obligations are fulfilled within the first 12 months, you may be permitted to recognize the fee faster (e.g., over 1 year instead of 10).
*   *Rule of Thumb:* Revenue is recognized when the performance obligation is satisfied. If the work is done upfront, the revenue follows; if the brand license is the primary value over 10 years, the revenue stretches over 10 years.
*   *Action:* Review your franchise disclosure document (FDD) and agreement this week to list exactly which initial services you are contractually bound to perform and estimate their completion date.

**Routing Note**
While the general rule is deferral, the exact amortization schedule depends on the specific "substantial services" clause in your contract and your entity's tax election (cash vs. accrual basis). Bring your signed franchise agreements and a list of initial services to your CPA to finalize the amortization schedule and ensure compliance with ASC 606 (Revenue from Contracts with Customers).
