**The Call**
Do not book the $50,000 immediately; you must spread (amortize) the revenue evenly over the 12-month service period. Recognizing it all upfront would violate accrual accounting principles, artificially inflate your January performance, and mislead investors regarding your true Monthly Recurring Revenue (MRR) and run-rate.

**The Numbers**
Under the revenue recognition principle (ASC 606 / IFRS 15), revenue is recorded when the performance obligation is satisfied (service delivered), not when cash is received.

*   **Formula:** Monthly Revenue = Total Contract Value / Contract Duration (Months)
*   **Your Figures:** $50,000 / 12 months
*   **Result:** **$4,166.67 per month**

*Accounting Treatment:*
1.  **January (Cash Receipt):** Debit Cash $50,000; Credit Deferred Revenue (Liability) $50,000.
    *   *Note:* This sits on your Balance Sheet as a liability because you owe the service.
2.  **January–December (Monthly Recognition):** Debit Deferred Revenue $4,166.67; Credit Revenue $4,166.67.
    *   *Note:* This moves the amount to your Income Statement as earned revenue.

*Assumption:* The contract terms are fixed, and services are delivered evenly throughout the year. If delivery is front-loaded or back-loaded, the monthly recognition amount would need adjustment based on the actual progress of obligations.

**What Flips It, and the Trigger**
The single input that changes this treatment is the **nature of the performance obligation**. If the contract were for a one-time deliverable (e.g., a single software installation or a one-off report) completed in January, you could recognize 100% immediately. However, since your obligation is "continuous access/services" over time, the trigger to stop upfront recognition is the signing of a time-based service agreement.
*   **Rule of Thumb:** If the customer can cancel and demand a refund for unused months, or if the value is delivered continuously, it is deferred revenue.
*   **Action Step:** If your current ledger shows the full $50,000 as January revenue, consider reclassifying the unearned portion to Deferred Revenue before the audit to prevent material misstatement.

**Routing Note**
While the general rule for time-based services is straight-line amortization, the specific tax treatment of deferred revenue (e.g., whether you can defer it for tax purposes or must recognize it upon receipt under local tax codes) varies by jurisdiction and entity type. Bring the question "Does our local tax code allow tax deferral matching our book deferral for 12-month service contracts?" to your CPA, as cash-basis tax reporting often differs from accrual-basis financial reporting.
