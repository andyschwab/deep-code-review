**The Call**
Do not re-categorize the initial fee as a "marketing fund contribution" to alter tax exposure without a binding opinion from a Texas tax attorney; the Texas Franchise Tax computation relies on federal taxable income as the starting point, where the substance of the revenue (earned from granting franchise rights) generally overrides the label, and misclassification creates audit risk that outweighs potential marginal savings.

**The Numbers**
*Formula:* Texas Franchise Tax Due = (Total Revenue – Cost of Goods Sold) × Applicable Rate (0.375% for retail/wholesale or 0.75% for others, unless under the no-tax-due threshold).
*User Figures:* Initial Fee = $45,000; Royalty = 6% of $320,000 ($19,200/year); Projected Annual Unit Volume = $320,000.
*Assumptions (ASSUMED):*
1. BreathFlow is classified as a service business (0.75% rate) unless specific retail elements dominate.
2. The "marketing fund" is either a pass-through agency relationship or recognized revenue depending on control.
3. The franchise is below the 2024 no-tax-due threshold of $1,230,000 in total annualized revenue (aggregated across all entities if combined for tax purposes).

*Scenario A (Standard Structure):*
Revenue recognized immediately: $45,000 (fee) + $19,200 (royalty).
Total First Year Revenue per Unit: $64,200.
*Scenario B (Re-categorized Marketing Fund):*
If treated as a pass-through agent: $0 recognized on the $45k; only $19,200 royalty recognized.
If treated as earned revenue (substance over form): $64,200 recognized.
*Result:* If you are under the $1.23M threshold (likely with only 3 units initially), the tax due is $0 in both scenarios. If you scale to 20+ units ($1.28M+ revenue), the difference in taxable margin becomes material, but only if the re-categorization survives audit.

**What Flips It, and the Trigger**
The single input that changes the call is **Total Combined Group Revenue** crossing the Texas no-tax-due threshold (approx. $1.23M).
*Trigger:* Once you sign your 20th franchisee (assuming similar volumes), you exceed the threshold. At that point, if the "marketing fund" is re-characterized by the state as taxable revenue, you owe back taxes plus penalties on the difference between the 0.75% rate applied to $45k versus $0.
*Rule of Thumb:* If the franchisor controls the fund or benefits from unspent balances, it is likely taxable revenue regardless of the label.
*Step this week:* If you have fewer than 15 signed LOIs, consider deferring the structural optimization until you approach the threshold, as the cost of counsel now may exceed the tax savings.

**Routing Note**
Engage a Texas tax attorney immediately to determine if the "marketing fund" constitutes a pass-through agency relationship under Texas Tax Code §171.1011; specifically ask whether the proposed wording shifts the "substance over form" determination enough to exclude the $45,000 from the taxable margin calculation, as this is a binding legal determination, not an accounting choice.
