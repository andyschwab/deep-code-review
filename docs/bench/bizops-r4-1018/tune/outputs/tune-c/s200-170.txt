**The call:** Reject the unlimited fixed-fee model as currently structured; it exposes you to catastrophic margin erosion if a single client scales to 1,000 seats. Instead, adopt a "Good-Better-Best" tiered fixed-fee model with a hard cap or overage charges to protect unit economics while offering predictability.

**The numbers:**
To find the break-even point where fixed fee equals per-seat revenue, we compare the fixed annual price against the variable per-seat price.
*Formula:* Break-Even Seats = Fixed Fee / Per-Seat Price
*Your Figures:* €45,000 / €150 = **300 seats**.

At 300 seats, both models generate identical top-line revenue. However, profit behavior diverges immediately after this point because your costs are variable (€40/seat).

*Profit Comparison at Scale (Annual):*
*   **Scenario A: 300 Seats (Break-Even Revenue)**
    *   Per-Seat Model: Revenue €45,000 - Cost (300 × €40) = **€33,000 Profit**.
    *   Fixed-Fee Model: Revenue €45,000 - Cost (300 × €40) = **€33,000 Profit**.
*   **Scenario B: 1,000 Seats (The Risk Case)**
    *   Per-Seat Model: Revenue (1,000 × €150) €150,000 - Cost (1,000 × €40) €40,000 = **€110,000 Profit**.
    *   Fixed-Fee Model: Revenue €45,000 - Cost (1,000 × €40) €40,000 = **€5,000 Profit**.
    *   *Result:* You leave €105,000 on the table and operate at a mere 11% margin compared to 73%.

*Assumption (ASSUMED):* Your €40/seat cost remains constant regardless of volume. If instructor time scales linearly, the risk is real. If digital materials dominate at scale, costs may drop, but the revenue cap remains the primary danger.

**What flips it, and the trigger:**
The single input that changes the call is the **maximum expected headcount per client**.
*Trigger Value:* If any client exceeds **300 employees**, the fixed-fee model destroys value relative to per-seat pricing.
*Rule of Thumb:* Never sell unlimited variable-cost services for a fixed price unless the fixed price covers the 95th percentile of usage, not the average.
*Action Step:* If your average client is 250 seats but growth is possible, consider a fixed fee of €45,000 covering up to 300 seats, with a €150 overage fee for every seat beyond that. This captures the predictability clients want without capping your upside.

**Routing note:**
Since you operate in Germany, ensure your new tiered contracts clearly define "seats" and "overage" under German contract law (BGB) to avoid disputes on measurement. Consult a local attorney to verify that automatic overage charges comply with local consumer/business protection norms before drafting the final terms.
