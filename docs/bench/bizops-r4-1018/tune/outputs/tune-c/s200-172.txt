**The Call:** Your current flow—holding funds in a merchant account before disbursing to tutors—almost certainly triggers money transmitter licensing (MTL) obligations in California, New York, and Texas, regardless of the 24-hour hold time. You must cease this specific fund-flow model immediately or engage specialized fintech counsel to structure a compliant exemption or obtain licenses; operating without them carries severe civil and criminal penalties.

**The Numbers & Mechanics:**
The regulatory trigger is not your commission rate (15%) or transaction volume ($240k/mo), but the legal definition of "receiving money for transmission."
*   **The Rule:** In most US states, if you accept funds from a payer (parent) with the obligation to deliver them to a payee (tutor), you are transmitting money.
*   **The "24-Hour" Myth:** There is no universal federal or state exemption for holding funds for less than 24 hours. While some states have narrow exemptions for agents of payees or specific low-value thresholds, relying on a time-based heuristic without a formal legal opinion is a critical failure point.
*   **Jurisdictional Exposure:**
    *   **New York:** Has the strictest regime (BitLicense/MTL). No *de minimis* exemption for time-held funds exists for this model. Operating here without a license is high-risk.
    *   **California:** Requires a license if you control the funds' destination. The "agent of the payee" exemption is narrow and often requires a specific contractual setup you likely haven't formalized.
    *   **Texas:** Explicitly requires licensing for third-party payment processors unless a specific exemption (like being an agent of the *payee* exclusively) is met and documented.

**What Flips It, and The Trigger:**
The single input that changes your status is **custody**. If you can restructure so that funds move directly from Parent to Tutor via a licensed third-party processor (where you never take legal title or possession of the funds, receiving only a fee notification), you may avoid MTL.
*   **Trigger:** The moment your merchant account settlement logic allows you to "net" funds or delay disbursement at your discretion, you are transmitting.
*   **Immediate Step:** If you cannot prove you are strictly an "agent of the payee" with a written agreement reviewed by counsel, consider integrating a "split-payment" infrastructure (e.g., Stripe Connect, Adyen for Platforms) where the platform never touches the principal.
