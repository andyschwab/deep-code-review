**What Flips It, and the Trigger**
The single input that flips this from a service fee to a security is the **absence of a fixed debt obligation**. Under the *SEC v. W.J. Howey Co.* test, courts look for: (1) an investment of money, (2) in a common enterprise, (3) with an expectation of profits, (4) derived from the efforts of others.
*   **The Risk:** If students view the $12k tuition as an "investment" where their return (the education) is contingent on *your* effort to place them in high-paying jobs, and repayment is purely a share of that success, regulators may argue they are investing in your enterprise's success rather than buying a static product.
*   **The Trigger:** If marketing materials emphasize "invest in yourself" with returns tied to Apex Prep's placement network rather than the curriculum itself, the "expectation of profit from others' efforts" prong is met.
*   **Rule of Thumb:** If the repayment obligation disappears entirely based on your performance (job placement) rather than the student's inability to pay, it looks more like equity/profit-sharing than a loan or service fee.
*   **Next Step:** If you cannot structure this as a fixed principal debt with income-contingent *repayment terms* (rather than income-contingent *liability*), consider pausing launch until counsel reviews the contract language.
