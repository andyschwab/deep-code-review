**The Call**
Adopt the 15% success-fee model but implement a "net revenue" recognition policy with an explicit refund reserve; do not defer all revenue, but do not recognize gross revenue without an offset. This approach satisfies auditors for Series A while capturing upside, provided you formalize the refund probability in your accounting policy. Standard legal templates are insufficient for the accounting treatment; you require a specialized finance advisor or CPA to set the reserve methodology, though general counsel can draft the refund clauses.

**The Numbers**
*Current Baseline:* £3,000 flat fee × 60 placements = £180,000/quarter (£60,000/month average).
*Proposed Model Formula:* `Reported Revenue = (Gross Success Fees) − (Estimated Refund Reserve)`

*   *Low Refund (10%):* Reserve £12,000 → Net £108,000.
*   *High Refund (30%):* Reserve £36,000 → Net £84,000.
*   *Volatility Range:* £24,000 swing in reported monthly revenue purely from reserve adjustments, compared to the stable £60,000 of the flat-fee model.

**What Flips It, and the Trigger**
The critical input is the **historical refund rate**. If your actual refund rate exceeds 25%, the net revenue (£90,000 at 25% refunds) drops near your current flat-fee baseline (£60k–£75k range depending on volume), negating the upside while adding accounting complexity.
*Trigger:* If refunds exceed 25% for two consecutive months, pause the percentage model and revert to a hybrid (lower base + capped success fee) to stabilize cash flow.
