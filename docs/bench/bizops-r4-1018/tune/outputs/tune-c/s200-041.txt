**The Call:** Do not bundle the hardware and subscription into a single, unallocated line item on the invoice if you wish to avoid taxing the service portion; instead, explicitly allocate the price between the tangible mirror and the digital service on the customer receipt. In Texas, failing to separately state the service charge on the invoice typically causes the entire bundled transaction to be treated as the sale of tangible personal property, subjecting the full $1,680 to sales tax rather than just the $1,200 hardware portion.

**What Flips It, and the Trigger:**
The single input that changes the outcome is the **invoice line-item structure**. If the receipt shows one lump sum of $1,680 labeled "Smart Mirror System," the Texas Comptroller generally taxes the whole amount. If the receipt lists "Smart Mirror: $1,200" and "1-Year Content Subscription: $480" as distinct lines, the service portion is typically excluded from the tax base.

*   **Rule of Thumb:** In Texas, "true object" tests often favor taxing the whole bundle if the service is incidental to the hardware, unless the service is separately stated.
