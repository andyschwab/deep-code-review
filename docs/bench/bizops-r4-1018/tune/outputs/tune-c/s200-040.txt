**The Call**
Treat the $350 last-mile logistics cost as **Cost of Goods Sold (COGS)**, not Sales & Marketing. This classification provides the truer picture of scalability because the cost is variable and directly tied to fulfilling each unit sold; burying it in operating expenses will artificially inflate your gross margin and mislead investors about the fundamental profitability of your product model.

**The Numbers**
To assess your unit economics accurately, you must calculate Contribution Margin after all variable fulfillment costs.

*   **Formula:** Contribution Margin = Revenue – (Variable COGS + Variable Fulfillment).
*   **Your Figures:**
    *   Revenue per unit: $2,500
    *   Last-mile logistics (Variable): $350
    *   *Assumption (ASSUMED):* Manufacturing/Material cost per unit is unknown. Let's bracket this at $800 (Low), $1,000 (Base), and $1,200 (High) to illustrate the impact.

*   **Scenario A: Correct Classification (Logistics in COGS)**
    *   Gross Margin = $2,500 – ($1,000 Base Material + $350 Logistics) = $1,150.
    *   **Gross Margin %:** 46%.
    *   *Insight:* This reveals that nearly 14% of your revenue is consumed purely by delivery. If you scale to 10,000 units, this $350/unit drag scales linearly, directly capping your gross profit potential unless negotiated down.

*   **Scenario B: Incorrect Classification (Logistics in S&M)**
    *   Gross Margin = $2,500 – $1,000 Material = $1,500.
    *   **Gross Margin %:** 60%.
    *   *Insight:* This looks healthy (60%) but is deceptive. It hides the structural cost of your "white-glove" promise. Investors analyzing SaaS-like scalability will see a high gross margin and assume operating leverage exists where there is none, leading to painful corrections later when S&M budgets balloon to cover fulfillment.

**What Flips It, and the Trigger**
The single input that changes the strategic view is the **negotiated logistics rate**.
*   **Trigger Value:** If you can reduce last-mile costs from $350 to **$200**, your Gross Margin jumps from 46% to 52% (on base assumptions), fundamentally altering your hiring runway and valuation narrative.
*   **Rule of Thumb:** For heavy goods e-commerce, a gross margin below 50% often signals difficulty in funding growth without dilution.
*   **This Week's Step:** If your current logistics partner cannot commit to a volume-based rate reduction plan within 30 days, consider piloting a regional carrier or adjusting the service tier (e.g., threshold delivery vs. room-of-choice) for a test batch of 50 units.

**Routing Note**
While US GAAP (ASC 606) generally requires fulfillment costs to be recognized as expenses when the related revenue is recognized (often COGS for retailers), the specific presentation on your P&L for investor decks should be reviewed by your CPA to ensure consistency with your audit trail and any specific loan covenants regarding "Adjusted EBITDA" calculations.
