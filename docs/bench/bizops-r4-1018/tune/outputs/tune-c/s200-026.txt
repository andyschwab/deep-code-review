**The Call:** Do not raise the price to $18 yet; keep it at $14 to prioritize volume and clear your $60,000 inventory risk. Your shipping savings ($0.90/unit) are real operational margin, not consumer-perceived value, and raising prices by $4 (a 29% increase) without validated willingness-to-pay data will likely crush the conversion rates needed to hit your 10,000-unit target and cover your $10,000/month burn.

**The Numbers:**
*   **Current Unit Economics:** Price $14.00 - Cost $2.10 = **$11.90 Gross Margin**.
*   **Shipping Savings:** Liquid (1.2 lbs) vs. Bar (0.3 lbs) = 0.9 lbs saved. At an estimated $1.00/lb blended shipping rate, this saves **~$0.90 per unit**.
*   **Proposed Scenario ($18 Price):**
    *   New Margin: $18.00 - $2.10 = $15.90.
    *   Required Volume to Cover Burn ($10k/mo): $10,000 / $15.90 = **629 units/month**.
    *   Volume to Clear Inventory (3 months): 20,000 units needed (you only have 60k total, but need cash flow now).
*   **Current Scenario ($14 Price):**
    *   Margin: $11.90.
    *   Required Volume to Cover Burn: $10,000 / $11.90 = **841 units/month**.
*   **The Trap:** To justify $18, you must prove customers value the "eco/weight" benefit at **$4.00 extra**. In beauty, shipping weight is invisible to the consumer; they value the *result* and the *brand*. Unless your branding screams "luxury," a 29% price hike typically drops conversion by 40-60% in mass markets.
*   **Inventory Risk:** You have $60,000 in stock. At $14, you need to sell ~4,285 units just to recoup inventory cost (ignoring other ops). At $18, you need ~3,333 units. The difference in units sold (952 units) is negligible compared to the risk of slowing sales velocity below your burn rate.

**What Flips It, and The Trigger:**
The single input that changes this call is **Validated Willingness-to-Pay (WTP)**. If you can prove via a Van Westendorp survey or A/B test that your specific Seattle demographic perceives the bar as a *premium luxury* rather than a *mass-market substitute*, then $18 works.
*   **Trigger:** If an A/B test on your site shows conversion rates remain above 2.5% at the $18 price point over 500 visitors, consider the price hike.
*   **Rule of Thumb:** Logistics savings improve *your* bottom line; they rarely justify consumer price hikes unless marketed as "Free Shipping" passed directly to the user.
*   **This Week's Step:** If you cannot run an A/B test immediately, keep the price at $14 and explicitly market "Free Shipping" or "Carbon Neutral Delivery" using your cost savings to drive the volume needed to stop the cash burn.
