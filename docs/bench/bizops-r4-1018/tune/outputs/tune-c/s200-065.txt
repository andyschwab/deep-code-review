**The call:** Do not present a single blended CAC; it will distort your efficiency by masking the 9-month enterprise lag. Instead, report **Segmented CAC** alongside **Blended CAC weighted by sales cycle**, and justify the budget increase by showing the payback period for each segment separately.

**The numbers:**
Your raw data shows a total acquisition spend of $350,000 ($150k marketing + $200k sales) against 92 new customers.
*   **Naive Blended CAC:** $350,000 / 92 = **$3,804**. (This is misleadingly low because it ignores the time value of money and resource intensity of enterprise deals).
*   **Segmented CAC (Recommended View):**
    *   *Assumption:* Without specific attribution data, we must allocate spend. A common heuristic for mixed models is allocating marketing by lead volume and sales salaries by time spent. However, lacking that, let's look at the extremes you noted.
    *   If we assume the $200k sales salary is the primary driver for the 9-month enterprise cycle and marketing drives SMB volume:
    *   **SMB CAC Estimate:** If we attribute 60% of marketing ($90k) and 10% of sales ($20k) to SMBs: $110,000 / 80 = **$1,375**.
        *   *Payback:* $1,375 / ($1,200 ACV) = **1.15 months** (extremely efficient).
    *   **Enterprise CAC Estimate:** If we attribute 40% of marketing ($60k) and 90% of sales ($180k) to Enterprise: $240,000 / 12 = **$20,000**.
        *   *Payback:* $20,000 / ($45,000 ACV) = **5.3 months** (still healthy, despite the long cycle).

**What flips it, and the trigger:**
The decision to increase the budget hinges on **Cash Flow Runway**, not just CAC. The "flip" input is your available cash reserve relative to the 9-month enterprise sales cycle.
*   *Trigger:* If your current runway is less than **12 months**, increasing the sales budget by 50% (which likely extends the sales cycle further before revenue recognition) creates a liquidity crisis, regardless of how good the LTV:CAC ratio looks.
*   *Rule of Thumb:* Only approve the budget increase if `(Current Cash / Monthly Burn) > (Enterprise Sales Cycle in Months + 3)`.
*   *Step this week:* If your runway is >12 months, consider reallocating the proposed 50% increase entirely to the enterprise sales team while holding SMB marketing flat, as SMB payback is already near-instant.
