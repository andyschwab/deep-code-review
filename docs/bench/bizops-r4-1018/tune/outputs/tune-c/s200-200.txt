**The Call:** Choose the nonprofit structure only if you can reliably secure the projected $100,000 in grants/donations; otherwise, the C-corp is the solvent choice. With your thin margins ($20k surplus), the nonprofit's $20k compliance cost eliminates your operating surplus, creating an immediate deficit unless the extra funding materializes.

**The Numbers:**
*   **C-Corp Scenario:**
    *   Revenue: $300,000
    *   Expenses: $280,000
    *   **Net Surplus:** $20,000 (pre-tax).
    *   *Tax Implication:* Federal corporate tax (21%) applies only to taxable income. If you reinvest the $20k into mission growth, taxable income may be near zero.
*   **Nonprofit (501(c)(3)) Scenario:**
    *   Revenue (Operations): $300,000
    *   Expenses (Operations): $280,000
    *   Compliance Overhead (ASSUMED): $20,000
    *   **Operational Deficit:** -$20,000 (before new funding).
    *   **Required Grant/Donation Income (ASSUMED):** $100,000.
    *   **Net Surplus with Funding:** $80,000 ($100k new - $20k deficit).
    *   *Tax Implication:* $0 federal income tax on related mission income (training fees). Donations are tax-deductible for donors.

**What Flips It:**
The **grant acquisition rate**. If you secure less than $20,000 in external funding, the nonprofit structure burns cash faster than the C-corp due to compliance costs.
*   **Trigger:** If you do not have written commitments or a high-confidence pipeline for $20k+ in grants within 6 months of launch, pivot to C-corp to preserve the $20k operational surplus.
*   **Rule of Thumb:** Nonprofits with >50% earned revenue (fees vs. donations) face higher IRS scrutiny regarding "commerciality."
*   **Next Step:** This week, draft a one-page grant prospectus and test it with three potential funders; if interest is low, file as a C-corp.

**Routing Note:**
Determining 501(c)(3) eligibility and filing Form 1023 is a binding legal determination; consult a tax attorney to confirm your "educational" purpose qualifies and to assess "unrelated business income tax" (UBIT) risks if commercial training scales significantly.

*Note: The $20k compliance estimate for nonprofits often understates the initial legal filing costs ($5k–$15k one-time) and ongoing state charity registrations.*
