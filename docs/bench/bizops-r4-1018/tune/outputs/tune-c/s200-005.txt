**The Call:** Register for a Canadian GST/HST account immediately; you likely owe tax from the first sale to Canada because your US-based sales count toward the $30,000 CAD "small supplier" threshold, and you must collect province-specific rates for Ontario, British Columbia, and Quebec at checkout.

**The Numbers & Rules:**
Canada's federal threshold for mandatory registration is $30,000 CAD in worldwide taxable supplies over four consecutive quarters.
*   **Your Projection:** 50 orders × $450 USD ≈ $3,000–$3,400 CAD (depending on exchange rates).
*   **The Trap:** This $30,000 limit includes your **US domestic sales**. If ModularLiving's total global revenue exceeds $30,000 CAD annually, you are not a "small supplier" and must register from the first Canadian sale. Given you are an established US entity, you likely exceed this global cap.
*   **Rates to Collect (Furniture is generally fully taxable):**
    *   **Ontario:** 13% HST (Harmonized Sales Tax).
    *   **British Columbia:** 5% GST + 7% PST (Provincial Sales Tax) = 12% total. You must register separately for BC PST.
    *   **Quebec:** 5% GST + 9.975% QST = 14.975% total. You must register separately for QST.
*   **Product Category:** Flat-pack furniture has no special exemption; it is taxed at the standard rate. The $450 AOV does not trigger exemptions; low-value import relief generally applies to duties, not sales tax collection obligations for registered vendors.

**What Flips It & The Trigger:**
The single input that changes this is your **global annual revenue**.
*   **Trigger:** If your total worldwide revenue (US + others) is under $30,000 CAD, you *might* delay registration until Canadian sales alone push you over a specific provincial threshold (rare for non-residents) or you choose to register voluntarily.
*   **Reality Check:** If you are already selling in Washington State, you almost certainly exceed the $30,000 CAD global limit.
*   **Action:** If your global revenue > $30k CAD, register now. If < $30k CAD, monitor closely, but voluntary registration is often safer to avoid retroactive penalties.

**Steps to Take for 50 Orders Next Month:**
1.  **Register for a GST/HST account** with the Canada Revenue Agency (CRA) as a non-resident.
2.  **Register for BC PST** (via eTaxBC) and **Quebec QST** (via Revenu Québec) separately; these are not covered by the federal GST account.
3.  **Configure Checkout:** Update your e-commerce platform to charge 13% for ON, 12% for BC, and 14.975% for QC based on the shipping address.
4.  **Fiscal Representative:** You generally do not *need* a local fiscal representative to register, but you must secure a security bond or letter of credit in some cases if the CRA deems it necessary for non-residents.
5.  **File Returns:** Even with zero sales in a period, you must file nil returns once registered.

**Routing Note:**
Tax registration thresholds and the requirement for a security bond for non-residents are binding determinations based on your specific corporate structure and exact global revenue; consult a Canadian CPA or cross-border tax attorney to finalize your registration filings and verify if a bond is required for your specific case.
