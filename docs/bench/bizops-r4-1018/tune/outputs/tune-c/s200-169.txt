Do not halt your core feature yet; your current model (referral fees + read-only API data) likely falls outside the definition of "accepting deposits" or "executing payments," but you must immediately commission a formal regulatory perimeter assessment from UK counsel to confirm you are not inadvertently acting as an unregistered Payment Initiation Service Provider (PISP) or Account Information Service Provider (AISP) under the Payment Services Regulations 2017 (PSR 2017).

*   **Risk Case (Deemed PISP/AISP):** If your "transfer money" button initiates the payment rather than redirecting to the bank's own secure environment, you may be executing payments.
    *   *Trigger:* Direct API initiation of funds movement without bank-side authentication handoff.
    *   *Consequence:* Mandatory FCA authorization, $50k+ capital lock-up, audit costs.
    *   *Net Impact:* Positive cash flow turns negative immediately due to capital immobilization and compliance overhead.

**What Flips It, and The Trigger**
The single input that changes the call is the **technical mechanism of the money transfer**.
*   **The Rule:** Under PSR 2017, "Account Information Services" (viewing balances) and "Payment Initiation Services" (starting transfers) are regulated activities requiring FCA registration, even if you don't hold funds. However, mere "introducer" status (sending a user to a bank's landing page) is generally unregulated.
*   **The Trigger:** If your API call *executes* the transfer instruction directly to the bank's core ledger rather than redirecting the user to the bank's own app/website for final authorization, you are likely performing a regulated service.
*   **Actionable Step:** If your engineering team confirms the transfer API call originates from your servers rather than a bank-hosted redirect, consider pausing the transfer feature and switching to a deep-link redirect model until counsel confirms your exemption status.

Engage a UK financial services regulatory lawyer immediately with this specific question: "Does our specific API implementation for balance viewing and fund transfers constitute an Account Information Service (AIS) or Payment Initiation Service (PIS) under PSR 2017, or does it qualify as an unregulated commercial introduction?"
