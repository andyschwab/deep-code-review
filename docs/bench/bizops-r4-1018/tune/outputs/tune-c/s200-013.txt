**The Call**
Amortize the $84,000 slotting fees over the 24-month contract term; immediate expensing distorts your unit economics and misrepresents profitability to investors by front-loading costs unrelated to current production volume.

**The Numbers**
*Formula:* Monthly Amortization = Total Upfront Fees / Contract Months.
*Inputs:* $84,000 total fees (12 stores × $7,000/store) over 24 months.
*Result:* $3,500 monthly non-cash expense.

*Unit Economics Impact (Monthly per Store):*
*   **Gross Profit:** 800 bottles × $4 margin = $3,200.
*   **Amortization Expense:** $3,500 / 12 stores = ~$292 per store.
*   **Net Contribution (Pre-other OpEx):** $3,200 - $292 = $2,908 per store.
*   **Total Monthly Burn Impact:** $3,500 (vs. $84,000 if expensed immediately).

*Assumption (ASSUMED):* The contract guarantees shelf space for 24 months with no refund clause if you delist early. If the contract allows immediate termination with a refund, the asset life shortens, increasing monthly amortization.

**What Flips It, and the Trigger**
The deciding factor is the **contractual renewal and refundability clause**. If the agreement states fees are refundable upon early termination or are strictly "pay-per-period" despite the upfront collection, you must expense immediately or amortize over the expected life (which may be shorter than 24 months).
*Trigger:* Review the "Termination" and "Refund" sections of your signed contracts. If fees are non-refundable and tied to a fixed 24-month term, the amortization model holds. If they are refundable, stop amortization and consult your CPA immediately to restate as a receivable or immediate expense.
*Step:* If the contract confirms non-refundable 24-month terms, instruct your bookkeeper to set up a "Deferred Slotting Fees" asset account and amortize $3,500 monthly starting this month.

**Routing Note**
While US GAAP (ASC 340) generally supports capitalizing and amortizing non-refundable slotting fees over the benefit period, the final classification depends on the specific legal wording of your contracts and your company's materiality thresholds. Have your external auditor or CPA review the capitalization policy and the specific contract language during your next financial review to ensure compliance before presenting to investors.
