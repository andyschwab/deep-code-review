**The Call:** Do not deploy a dynamic pricing algorithm for referral fees based on location, season, or demand until a fair housing attorney has audited the specific variables and weighting logic; the risk of creating an illegal "disparate impact" on protected classes is high enough to threaten your business model, even if you charge homes rather than tenants.

**The Numbers:**
Your current model is transparent:
*   **Revenue:** $450,000/year.
*   **Current Unit Economics:** (150 homes × $200/mo) + (Referral Volume × $50).
*   **Proposed Variance:** $30 to $80 per referral.

While the math of increasing average referral fee from $50 to, say, $65 could theoretically lift annual revenue by ~$30,000 (assuming constant volume), this calculation ignores the **liability cost**. In fair housing law, a single successful discrimination lawsuit can result in damages far exceeding this potential gain, plus injunctive relief that could shut down the algorithm entirely. There is no "safe" formula to calculate compliance risk without legal review; the variable here is binary: either the model is vetted, or it is a liability.

**What Flips It, and The Trigger:**
The trigger for severe legal exposure is the use of **proxy variables**. Even if you do not explicitly input race or family status, an algorithm that adjusts fees based on "high-demand areas" often correlates with neighborhood demographics (e.g., zip codes with specific racial compositions or income levels). If your higher fees ($80) consistently apply to homes in minority-majority neighborhoods, regulators may argue your pricing creates a **disparate impact**, effectively penalizing homes that serve protected classes or discouraging them from using your platform.
*   **Rule of Thumb:** If a variable (like zip code or "neighborhood score") correlates with a protected class at a statistically significant level, using it for pricing is likely unlawful.
*   **Stop Trigger:** Do not write a single line of pricing logic or connect the algorithm to your database until the audit is complete.
*   **This Week's Step:** If you intend to pursue dynamic pricing, immediately pause development and engage counsel to define a "safe list" of neutral variables (e.g., purely time-based factors like "days until lease start" rather than location-based factors).
