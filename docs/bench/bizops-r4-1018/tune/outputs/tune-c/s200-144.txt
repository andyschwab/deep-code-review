**The Call:** Do not amend your terms of service to explicitly permit sidewalk riding; the probable surge in liability exposure, municipal fines, and risk of insurance cancellation vastly outweighs the marginal revenue gain from increased convenience.

**The Numbers:**
*   **Current Annual Fixed Risk:** $12,000 (premium) + potential fines on 3% usage.
    *   *Assumption (Fines):* If 50 units run 8 hours/day, 365 days, at 2 rides/hour, that is ~292,000 rides. 3% violation rate = 8,760 violations. Even if only 1% are cited at $500, fines = **$43,800**.
*   **Projected Risk if Policy Changed:**
    *   *Usage Shift (ASSUMED):* Explicit permission likely increases sidewalk usage from 3% to 60%+ (Base Case).
    *   *Fine Exposure:* 60% of 292,000 rides = 175,200 violations. At 1% citation rate ($500/fine) = **$876,000** in potential annual fines.
    *   *Insurance Impact (ASSUMED):* High-risk reclassification often triggers 50–100% premium increases or non-renewal.
        *   Low Estimate (50% hike): +$6,000 premium.
        *   High Estimate (Cancellation): Cost of replacement policy in "high risk" pool (often 2–3x base) = **$24,000–$36,000**.
    *   *Liability Deductible:* Your $50,000 deductible remains, but the *frequency* of claims hitting this threshold increases with higher accident rates on sidewalks (pedestrian collisions).

**What Flips It, and The Trigger:**
The single input that changes this call is **municipal enforcement intensity**. If Orlando actively cites every reported violation rather than spot-checking, your fine exposure moves from thousands to nearly a million dollars annually.
*   **Trigger:** If local police issue more than 5 citations per month to your users, immediately pause operations and review compliance.
*   **Rule of Thumb:** If the cost of potential fines exceeds 10% of projected gross revenue, the policy is unsustainable.
