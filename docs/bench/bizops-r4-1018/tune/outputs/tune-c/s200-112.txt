**The Call:** Proceed with the sliding-scale model immediately; formalizing income-based discounts is a standard charitable activity that strengthens, rather than jeopardizes, your current 501(c)(3) status, provided the criteria are objective and applied uniformly. The compliance risk lies not in the discount itself, but in the lack of written policies defining eligibility and the absence of board approval documenting the charitable intent.

**The Numbers:**
*   **Current Volume:** 3,500 families × $120/session (blended average).
*   **Current Charity Care:** 30% pay $0. Implied charity care value ≈ $630,000 annually (assuming similar utilization).
*   **Proposed Model:** Expanding subsidized access to 40% of the base.
*   **Formula for Compliance:** (Total Discounted Revenue + Explicit Charity Care) / Total Potential Revenue at Full Price.
*   **Assumption (ASSUMED):** If the new 40% includes the existing 30% zero-pay plus 10% partial-pay, the total community benefit contribution remains consistent with or exceeds current levels.
*   **Result:** As long as the "discounted" portion does not result in a net loss that depletes the $400,000 reserve below operational runway (currently ~2.3 months of expenses based on $2.1M revenue), the financial structure supports the transition. The key metric is not the price point, but the *percentage of revenue* foregone for charitable purposes.

**What Flips It, and the Trigger:**
The trigger that converts a charitable discount into "excessive private benefit" (a revocation risk) is **subjective application**. If discounts are given based on personal relationships, inconsistent criteria, or if the benefit disproportionately flows to a specific insider rather than a charitable class, the IRS may challenge the exemption.
*   **Threshold Rule of Thumb:** If more than 5-10% of total revenue is forgiven without documented charitable criteria, scrutiny increases.
*   **Stop/Pivot Trigger:** If the board cannot approve a written "Financial Assistance Policy" by the next meeting (within 30 days), pause the formal rollout.
*   **Actionable Step:** If the board approves the policy this month, consider launching a pilot with 50 families to test administrative burden before full deployment.

**Routing Note:**
While the *strategy* of sliding-scale pricing is safe, the *implementation* requires specific governance documents to satisfy IRS Community Benefit Standards (Rev. Rul. 69-545). You must engage a nonprofit attorney to draft the **Financial Assistance Policy** and ensure the board minutes explicitly link this pricing to your charitable mission before conversion. This is critical to prevent "intermediate sanctions" under IRC §4958 if the transition to for-profit is later audited.
