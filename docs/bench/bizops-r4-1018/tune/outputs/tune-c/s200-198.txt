**What Flips It, and The Trigger**
The single input that changes this recommendation is the **legal definition of "literary agent"** in your primary operating states. In New York (General Business Law Article 11-A) and California (Labor Code § 1700.4), the distinction often hinges on whether you **solicit or negotiate** employment (book deals) for a fee contingent on that employment.
*   **Educational Consultant:** Charges for instruction, editing, and strategy regardless of outcome.
*   **Literary Agent:** Charges a percentage *only* upon securing a deal, often involving negotiation.
*   **Trigger:** If your workshop staff interacts with publishers to pitch student work or negotiates contract terms as part of the "polishing" process, you cross into agency territory.
*   **Rule of Thumb:** If the fee is contingent on the deal closing, regulators frequently presume an agency relationship.
*   **This Week's Step:** If you intend to pursue this revenue stream, consider engaging an entertainment attorney to draft a strict "educational services only" agreement that explicitly forbids your staff from negotiating deals, and verify if a "manager" exemption applies in your state.
