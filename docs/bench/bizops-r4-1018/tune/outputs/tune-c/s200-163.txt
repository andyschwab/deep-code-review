**The Call**
Treat currency variance as a separate, explicit line item in your reporting rather than embedding it in the base price or relying solely on hedging; this preserves your core unit economics story for investors while transparently isolating the volatility driver. Do not raise the flat fee immediately, as this risks demand elasticity in a price-sensitive student market; instead, model a "Base Case" (current rates) and a "Stress Case" (5% depreciation) side-by-side.

**The Numbers**
Your current unit economics per student are distorted by the unmanaged FX swing.
*   **Revenue:** £8,500 (Fixed)
*   **Original Cost Base (implied):** £6,630 (derived from 22% margin on £8,500)
*   **Stressed Cost Base (+10% FX impact):** £7,293
*   **Current Margin:** £1,207 (14.2% actual, noting your 12% projection likely includes fixed overhead allocation)
*   **Projected Loss Scenario:** If costs rise another 5% (total ~15.5% increase from baseline), cost becomes ~£7,658. On 85 students, total revenue is £722,500. If total costs exceed £737,500, you hit the £15,000 loss.

*Assumption:* The "10% increase" refers strictly to the variable cost of goods sold (tuition/housing) converted to GBP.
*   **Base Case Margin:** £1,870 per student (using your original 22% figure).
*   **Stressed Case Margin:** £857 per student (after 10% cost hike).
*   **Break-even FX Shift:** Your margin disappears entirely if costs rise by roughly 26% from the original baseline. You are currently operating with a thin buffer against further sterling weakness.

**What Flips It, and The Trigger**
The single input that flips your position from profit to loss is the **GBP/EUR and GBP/AUD exchange rate at the time of payment commitment**.
*   **Trigger Value:** A cumulative 15-16% depreciation of GBP against your cost currencies from your baseline budget rate.
*   **Decision Rule:** If the pound weakens by more than 3% in any single month leading up to your payment deadline, you must activate a contingency protocol.
*   **Actionable Step:** If the GBP drops 3% next week, consider implementing a "variable fuel surcharge" clause in your terms of service for future bookings, capped at 5%, rather than absorbing the full cost or raising the headline price.

**Routing Note**
While modeling the variance is internal finance work (Tier 1), executing a **forward contract** or **options hedge** to lock in rates is a regulated financial activity. Consult a corporate treasury specialist or your bank's FX desk to structure instruments that match your payment calendar; do not attempt to self-hedge via spot markets without professional advice on settlement risk.
