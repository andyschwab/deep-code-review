**The Call**
A $200M TAM does not make a $10M post-money valuation impossible, but it caps your narrative; you must justify the round by proving you can capture a dominant share (10%+) of this niche quickly, rather than relying on massive market expansion. Reject the premise that small TAM automatically demands excessive dilution; instead, frame the raise as buying dominance in a profitable, defensible segment where unit economics outperform mass-market competitors.

**The Numbers**
*   **Current Unit Economics:**
    *   Price: $80
    *   Gross Margin: 40% → $32 contribution per unit.
    *   *Assumption (ASSUMED):* To support a seed round, your CAC must be significantly below $32. If CAC is $20, Payback is immediate on the first sale.
*   **Valuation & Dilution Arithmetic (Tier 1):**
    *   Raise Amount: $1.5M
    *   Target Post-Money Valuation: $10M
    *   **Formula:** Equity % = Raise Amount / Post-Money Valuation
    *   **Calculation:** $1.5M / $10M = **15% dilution**.
    *   *Scenario Analysis:*
        *   If investors force a $6M post-money (due to TAM fears): $1.5M / $6M = **25% dilution**.
        *   If you hold at $10M post-money: **15% dilution**.
    *   *TAM Context:* A $200M TAM suggests a revenue ceiling of ~$50M–$80M (assuming 25–40% market share). At a 2x–3x revenue multiple (typical for hardware/niche CPG), this supports a $100M–$240M exit. A $10M entry valuation offers investors a 10x–20x return potential, which is acceptable even with a capped TAM, provided the path to $10M+ ARR is clear.

**What Flips It, and the Trigger**
The single input that changes the call is **Net Revenue Retention (NRR)** or **Repeat Purchase Rate**.
*   **Trigger:** If your NRR is >110% (customers buy multiple mats or accessories) or if Lifetime Value (LTV) exceeds 4x CAC, the small TAM matters less because you are extracting more value per user than a mass-market player.
*   **Rule of Thumb:** In niche hardware, investors accept a lower top-line ceiling if the margin per customer is durable.
*   **This Week's Step:** If your current repeat rate is unknown, run a cohort analysis on customers from 12 months ago; if >15% have repurchased or bought accessories, lead negotiations with "High LTV density offsets TAM cap."

**Routing Note**
Selling equity or SAFEs to raise this $1.5M is a **securities offering**. The specific percentage of equity issued, the terms of the instrument, and the legal validity of your valuation cap are binding legal determinations that require counsel to draft and finalize. Bring the term sheet to a securities attorney before signing.

**Metrics to Highlight Against Small TAM**
To counteract the $200M TAM concern, highlight:
1.  **Share of Wallet Growth:** Evidence that customers buy accessories (blocks, straps) increasing average order value (AOV) beyond the $80 mat.
2.  **CAC Payback Period:** If you recover acquisition costs in <3 months, you can reinvest faster than competitors, dominating the niche before they react.
3.  **Brand Moat:** Qualitative data on brand loyalty that prevents price compression, sustaining your 40% gross margin where mass-market competitors struggle at 20–25%.
