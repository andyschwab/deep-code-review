**What Flips It, and the Trigger:**
The determining factor is whether you can qualify for a specific **exemption** (e.g., the "fractional franchise" exemption if the franchisee has 2+ years of experience in the restaurant industry and the new unit represents <20% of their projected revenue, or a "limited offering" exemption).
*   **Trigger:** If a prospective franchisee does *not* meet the strict criteria for an exemption, you must file the FDD.
*   **Decision Rule:** Do not accept any money or sign any agreement in CA or IL until counsel confirms exemption eligibility or the FDD is registered.
*   **Step this week:** If you have a specific candidate with >2 years of restaurant management experience, gather their financials to test the "fractional franchise" exemption; otherwise, consider delaying the CA/IL launch until the legal budget increases to ~$35,000.

**Routing Note:**
Franchise regulation is a binding legal determination (Tier 3). You must engage a franchise attorney licensed in California and Illinois to: (1) confirm if your "cooking school" model meets the statutory definition of a franchise in those states (usually yes, due to the fee + trademark + system association), (2) determine if your financial statements require an audit for the FDD, and (3) file the FDD with the CA Department of Financial Protection and Innovation and the Illinois Attorney General. Time is critical: FDD review in CA can take 30+ days after submission.
