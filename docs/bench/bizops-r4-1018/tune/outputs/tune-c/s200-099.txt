**The call:** Proceed with the annual shift only if you secure at least $750k in additional runway or financing before launch; the model predicts a severe liquidity crunch starting in Month 13 where cash reserves deplete rapidly due to the "renewal cliff."

**The numbers:**
*   **Initial Cash Injection:** $2,000,000 (Given).
*   **Monthly Burn:** $150,000 (Given).
*   **Monthly Revenue Loss:** You are converting 60% of 1,000 customers (600 users).
    *   Current MRR from these users: Assuming equal distribution, $300k / 1,000 = $300/user. 600 users * $300 = $180,000 MRR removed from the monthly stream.
    *   New Monthly Recognized Revenue from annual contracts: $180,000 * 12 months * 0.90 (10% discount) / 12 months = $162,000 recognized per month.
    *   *Net Monthly Cash Flow Change:* You lose $180k in monthly collections but gain $2M upfront. However, for ongoing operations after Month 1, you only recognize $162k/month from this cohort vs the $180k/month you used to collect.
*   **The Gap Calculation (Months 13–18):**
    *   **Cash In (Month 13+):**
        *   Remaining 40% monthly customers (growing at 5% mo/mo): ~ $126,000 (base) growing.
        *   Annual Renewals (85% of original 600): 510 users * $300 * 0.90 = ~$137,700 collected *only* in renewal month.
        *   *Critical Flaw:* In non-renewal months of Year 2, cash inflow from the annual cohort is **$0** until their specific renewal date.
    *   **Cash Out:** $150,000 fixed + growth costs.
    *   **Runway Depletion:**
        *   Month 1 Cash: $2,000,000 (in) - $150,000 (out) = $1.85M net positive.
        *   Months 2–12: You burn $150k/mo against ~$162k (recognized) + $120k (remaining monthly cohort). You are roughly cash-flow neutral or slightly positive *if* growth covers the discount gap.
        *   Month 13 Scenario: If renewals are staggered or lumped, you face months with only the 40% monthly cohort (~$126k + growth) covering a $150k burn.
        *   **Deficit Estimate:** In a low-inflow month in Year 2, you may collect only ~$130k against $150k burn = -$20k/month deficit, eroding the initial $2M cushion if not managed. *However*, the real risk is the **timing mismatch**. If 85% renew in a specific window, the months *between* renewal waves have near-zero inflow from the annual cohort.

**What flips it, and the trigger:**
The **Renewal Distribution Schedule** flips the call. If renewals are concentrated in one quarter, you will face 6+ months of negative cash flow.
*   **Trigger:** If cash reserves drop below $450,000 (3 months burn) by Month 10, halt new annual sales or secure a line of credit.
*   **Step:** Model a "worst-case" where 0 annual renewals hit in Months 13, 14, and 15. If this breaches your minimum cash covenant, stagger the annual contract start dates artificially to smooth inflows.

**Routing note:**
Consult a CPA to determine the tax liability timing on the $2M upfront collection (deferred revenue vs. recognized income) as this may create an immediate cash tax obligation that worsens the crunch.
