**What Flips It, and the Trigger:**
The **Watt-hour (Wh) rating** of your battery is the single flip input.
*   **Trigger:** If your battery exceeds **100Wh**, you move from "Section II" (lighter regulation) to full Class 9 Dangerous Goods requirements, potentially spiking costs to the feared 3x ($36+/unit) and requiring specialized freight forwarders who may delay small shipments.
*   **Rule of Thumb:** Most airline-approved luggage batteries are <100Wh. Verify your spec sheet immediately.
*   **This Week's Step:** If your battery is <100Wh, contact three freight forwarders specifically asking for "Section II Lithium Ion air freight quotes to DE/JP" to validate the $30 estimate; if >100Wh, consider sea freight for the initial 2,000 units to preserve cash.

**Routing Note:**
Lithium battery transport is governed by the **IATA Dangerous Goods Regulations** (air), **ADR** (EU road), and local civil aviation authorities (Japan). The binding determination of your battery's UN number (likely UN 3480 or UN 3481), the exact packaging group, and the validity of your "Section II" exemption status requires a certified Dangerous Goods Safety Advisor (DGSA) or a licensed freight forwarder's compliance team. Do not self-certify labels; incorrect labeling leads to seized cargo and fines in the EU and Japan.

*Note: Removable batteries often simplify some consumer restrictions but complicate transport classification as "loose" vs. "contained in equipment." Confirm the specific packing instruction (e.g., PI 965 vs. PI 967) with your forwarder.*
