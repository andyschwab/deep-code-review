**The Call**
Keep your current ratable recognition policy; do not accelerate revenue to improve reported financials for your Series A. Under US GAAP, the shift to multi-year contracts and upfront implementation work does not automatically justify recognizing subscription revenue earlier than the service period, and attempting to do so creates significant audit and compliance risks that could derail your fundraising.

**The Numbers**
Your current runway is a straightforward calculation:
*   **Formula:** Cash / Monthly Burn = Runway (months)
*   **Inputs:** $1,200,000 / $80,000
*   **Result:** 15 months of runway.

Regarding the revenue impact of a policy change:
*   **Current Policy (Ratable):** A $135,000 three-year deal recognizes $3,750/month ($45,000/year).
*   **Hypothetical Acceleration (Illustrative Only):** If you attempted to recognize 50% upfront due to implementation, you would book $67,500 in month one.
*   **ASSUMED (Low/Base/High):** The percentage of revenue allocable to implementation is unknown without a standalone selling price analysis. If implementation is valued at 10% of the contract, only $13,500 could potentially be recognized early, not the bulk of the subscription fee.

**What Flips It, and the Trigger**
The single factor that could change the accounting treatment is the existence of a distinct "performance obligation" for the implementation services that has a standalone value to the customer.
*   **Trigger:** If your implementation is generic (standard onboarding) rather than highly customized, it is likely bundled with the subscription, requiring the entire fee to be recognized ratably over three years.
*   **Rule of Thumb:** Revenue recognition follows the transfer of control. For SaaS, control transfers continuously over time, not upfront.
*   **Step this week:** If your implementation involves custom code unique to the client, consider documenting a standalone selling price to separate that specific portion; otherwise, maintain the ratable schedule.

**Routing Note**
Changing revenue recognition policies is a Tier 3 binding determination that requires formal approval from your external auditor before implementation. You must consult a CPA firm immediately to perform a ASC 606 revenue allocation analysis; presenting altered financials without auditor sign-off during due diligence is a red flag that can terminate a Series A round.
