**The Call:** A contract clause alone is insufficient to avoid money transmitter classification; you must technically architect your system to ensure you never have the ability to move, freeze, or alter funds, as regulators prioritize functional control over contractual disclaimers.

**The Numbers:**
*   **Licensing Cost:** $50,000/state × 3 states = **$150,000** (one-time).
*   **Surety Bond Requirement:** $200,000 (capital tied up, not spent, but requires liquidity).
*   **Total Immediate Capital Need:** ~$350,000+ (licensing fees + bond collateral).
*   **Your Current Cash:** $80,000.
*   **Annual Revenue:** $120,000.
*   **Result:** You cannot afford the licensing pathway. Your runway is effectively zero if forced to comply as a transmitter today. The business model relies entirely on successfully maintaining a "data processor" status.

**What Flips It, and The Trigger:**
The deciding factor is **functional control**. If your database schema or API allows *any* operation that could be interpreted as initiating a transfer, freezing an account balance pending investigation, or correcting a balance error via a direct write, you risk classification.
*   **Trigger:** The moment a regulator or auditor sees a database log where `UPDATE user_balances` was executed by your system (even for a bug fix) rather than by the customer's own authorized transaction stream.
*   **Rule of Thumb:** If you can change the number, you hold the money.
*   **This Week's Step:** If your current architecture allows your admin panel to directly edit balance fields, **consider** immediately revoking that write access and migrating to an append-only log structure where balances are calculated views, not mutable fields.

**Routing Note:**
Money transmitter laws in NY (BitLicense/DFS), CA (DFPI), and TX (DFF) are fact-specific and rely on "control" tests, not just contract language. You must engage a fintech regulatory attorney in each jurisdiction to review your specific data flow diagrams and API specifications before signing new customers. Do not rely on general counsel for this determination.

*Note: Regulators in these states aggressively pursue entities that "facilitate" value transfer, even without taking custody. The "data hosting" defense holds only if you are truly blind to the value and incapable of moving it.*
