**The Call**
Do not use the phrase "Certified Compostable" on your packaging or marketing materials without holding the actual certificate; launch immediately using the softer, factual claim "Home Compostable in 6 Months" based on your internal testing data, and pursue third-party certification in parallel to avoid the four-month delay and $60,000 burn.

**The Numbers**
The financial impact of waiting for certification versus launching now is calculated as follows:
*   **Delay Cost:** 4 months × $15,000/month (burn rate) = **$60,000** in lost cash.
*   **Revenue Opportunity Cost:** 5,000 units × $35 price = **$175,000** in delayed gross revenue.
*   **Gross Margin at Risk:** 5,000 units × ($35 - $12) = **$115,000** in delayed contribution margin.
*   **Assumption (ASSUMED):** You have internal lab data supporting the 6-month breakdown claim. If you lack *any* data, the risk of a deceptive trade practice suit rises sharply, potentially costing more than the delay.

**What Flips It, and the Trigger**
The deciding factor is the specific wording of your claim. Under US Federal Trade Commission (FTC) Green Guides, using the word "Certified" implies a formal endorsement by a third party. Making this claim without the seal is a high-probability trigger for regulatory action (false advertising) and competitor lawsuits.
*   **Trigger:** If your packaging says "Certified," you must wait. If it says "Compostable" or "Breaks down in home compost," you may launch provided you have competent and reliable scientific evidence (internal tests) to back it up.
*   **Rule of Thumb:** The penalty for false "certification" claims often exceeds the cost of the certification itself.
*   **Step this week:** If you have internal test results, revise packaging copy to "Home Compostable (6 Months)" and consult a marketing attorney for a 48-hour review of the specific phrasing to ensure it meets the "competent and reliable scientific evidence" standard before printing.

**Routing Note**
While the FTC Green Guides provide the federal framework for environmental marketing claims, specific state laws (like California's strict compostability statutes) may impose additional requirements or penalties; bring your specific packaging copy and internal test data to a consumer protection attorney to confirm compliance before finalizing the print run.
