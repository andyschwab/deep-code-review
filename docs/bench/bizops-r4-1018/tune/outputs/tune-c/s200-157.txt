**The call:** Proceed with the money-back guarantee only if you can convert at least 36% of failing students to a paid retake or upsell; otherwise, the policy destroys your unit economics and burns your cash reserve in under two quarters.

**The numbers:**
*   **Current Unit Economics (No Guarantee):**
    *   Revenue: $499
    *   Variable Cost: $120
    *   Contribution Margin: $379 per student.
    *   Quarterly Profit (500 students): $189,500.

*   **Projected Unit Economics (With Guarantee, 100% Refund Rate):**
    *   *Assumption (Base Case):* All 22% of failing students claim refunds.
    *   Effective Revenue per 100 students: (78 passed × $499) + (22 failed × $0) = $38,922.
    *   Total Cost per 100 students: 100 × $120 = $12,000.
    *   New Contribution Margin: $26,922 / 100 students = **$269.22 per student**.
    *   Margin Drop: 29% reduction from current levels.
    *   Quarterly Profit Impact: $134,610 (down from $189,500).
    *   **Cash Burn Risk:** Refund outflow = 110 students × $499 = **$54,890 per quarter**.
    *   *Result:* Your $40,000 reserve is insufficient to cover one quarter of refund spikes if volume holds or increases.

*   **Sensitivity Analysis (The Retake Variable):**
    *   Let $R$ be the % of failing students who choose a retake (costing you extra instructor time, assumed $50 marginal cost) instead of a refund.
    *   If $R = 0\%$ (All refund): Margin = $269/student. (Loss of $54,890/quarter cash).
    *   If $R = 50\%$ (Half retake, half refund): Margin rises to ~$324/student. Cash outflow drops to ~$27,445.
    *   **Break-even Retake Rate:** You need roughly **36%** of failing students to opt for a retake (or pay a processing fee) to maintain your original $379 contribution margin.

**What flips it, and the trigger:**
The single input that changes the call is the **retake conversion rate**. If fewer than 36% of failing students choose to retake rather than refund, the guarantee erodes your margins below sustainable levels for growth.
*   **Trigger:** Monitor the first 50 failures. If refund requests exceed 32 (64% refund rate), pause the guarantee or introduce a "$99 administrative fee" for refunds to alter the incentive structure.
*   **Action this week:** If your historical data shows students value certification highly, consider structuring the guarantee as "Free Retake + Coaching" rather than "Cash Refund" to keep revenue on the books and limit cash outflow.

**Unknown Factors:**
1.  **Moral Hazard:** Guarantees can lower student effort, potentially dropping your pass rate from 78% to 65%, which compounds the loss.
2.  **Retake Costs:** You assumed variable costs are fixed; remedial teaching for failing students often costs *more* than initial instruction. If retake cost > $99, the retake strategy fails.
