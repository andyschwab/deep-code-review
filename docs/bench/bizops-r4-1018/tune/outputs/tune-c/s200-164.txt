**The Call**
Transmitting insurance codes and storing session notes likely classifies CareConnect as a **Business Associate** under HIPAA, not a healthcare provider, provided you do not make clinical decisions or render care. However, "Business Associate" status still mandates full HIPAA compliance (including encryption and audits); it does not offer a "lighter" regulatory burden regarding data security. You must budget for the $25,000 upgrade regardless of the classification distinction, as both Providers and Business Associates face federal penalties for unsecured Protected Health Information (PHI).

**What Flips It, and the Trigger**
The determining factor is not the *transmission* of codes, but whether you **create, receive, maintain, or transmit** PHI. Since you store session notes, you already meet the definition of a Business Associate.
*   **The Trigger:** The moment a therapist uploads a session note containing patient identifiers. You have already crossed this line.
*   **Decision Threshold:** If you cannot implement encryption before scaling to 200 therapists, the risk of a breach (and subsequent federal penalty) outweighs the cost of the upgrade.
*   **This Week's Step:** If you are storing notes now, consider halting new note uploads until the encryption upgrade is scoped, or execute a Business Associate Agreement (BAA) template with your current 60 therapists to formalize the relationship while you build.
