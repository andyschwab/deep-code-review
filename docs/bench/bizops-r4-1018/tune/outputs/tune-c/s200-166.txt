**The Call:** Do not raise the price to $2,100 immediately; instead, hold the price at $1,800 temporarily to capture market share while competitors are vulnerable, but implement a "Good-Better-Best" packaging strategy to isolate the new cost and protect margins without a headline price hike.

**The Numbers:**
Your current situation shows a margin compression from 45% to 28% due to a $250 per-seat cost increase ($450 - $200).
*   **Current Unit Economics (at $1,800):**
    *   Revenue: $1,800
    *   Implied Total Cost (based on 28% margin): $1,296
    *   Gross Profit per Student: $504
    *   Quarterly Profit (40 students): $20,160
*   **Scenario A: Raise Price to $2,100 (15% enrollment drop):**
    *   New Enrollment: 34 students (40 * 0.85)
    *   Revenue: $71,400
    *   Total Cost (34 * $1,296): $44,064
    *   **Quarterly Profit:** $27,336
*   **Scenario B: Hold Price at $1,800 (Gain Market Share):**
    *   To match Scenario A's profit ($27,336) at $504 profit/student, you need **54 students**.
    *   This requires a **35% increase** in enrollment (from 40 to 54) to break even with the price-hike scenario.

**What Flips It, and The Trigger:**
The deciding input is **price elasticity vs. competitor endurance**. If competitors absorb the cost for more than two quarters, your cash flow at 28% margin may become unsustainable before they raise prices.
*   **Trigger:** If enrollment does not increase by at least 10% (to 44 students) within the next 60 days, or if competitors announce a price increase, immediately pivot to the $2,100 price point or introduce a mandatory "Simulator Technology Fee" of $250 separate from the tuition.
*   **Rule of Thumb:** In commodity-adjacent markets, a 15% volume drop often outweighs a 16% price increase unless the brand has distinct premium value. Since competitors are cheaper ($1,750), a jump to $2,100 makes you the most expensive option by 20%, likely causing a drop steeper than your 15% estimate.

**Strategic Recommendation:**
Adopt a **Good-Better-Best** packaging model (Tier 1 pricing structure).
1.  **Base ($1,750):** Course only, student provides own simulator time (or limited access). Matches competitor entry.
2.  **Standard ($1,950):** Includes the mandatory FAA simulator software. This isolates the $250 cost for those who need it, keeping the headline price competitive.
3.  **Premium ($2,400):** Includes simulator, job placement assistance, and advanced flight hours.

This allows you to test willingness to pay (Van Westendorp approach) without alienating price-sensitive leads. If 60% of students choose the Standard or Premium tiers, your blended margin recovers to ~40% without losing the volume required for survival.
