**The Call:** Operate under the assumption that KicksDrop is the "marketplace facilitator" legally responsible for collecting and remitting sales tax in New York, Florida, and any other state where you exceed economic nexus thresholds, not the individual sellers. Do not rely on sellers to handle this; your platform status likely shifts the primary statutory burden to you.

**The Numbers & Rules:**
Most states with marketplace facilitator laws (including NY and FL) define a facilitator as an entity that contracts with sellers to list items and **processes the payment**. Your 10% fee ($30 avg per sale) and control over the transaction interface typically satisfy this definition, regardless of whether you hold inventory.
*   **General Rule:** When a facilitator exists, the state looks to the facilitator for tax collection on the *entire* sale price, not just the fee. The underlying seller is usually relieved of this obligation for those specific transactions.
*   **Nexus Triggers:**
    *   **New York:** You likely have immediate nexus due to physical presence (sellers' inventory stored in homes/warehouses within the state can sometimes be attributed to the marketplace, though this is fact-specific) or economic nexus ($500k+ in sales and 100+ transactions). Your $1M GMV projection exceeds the economic threshold.
    *   **Florida:** Economic nexus triggers at $100k in taxable sales. Your projected $1M GMV exceeds this.
    *   **Other States:** With $1M GMV spread across buyers, you may hit economic nexus thresholds ($100k–$500k sales or 200 transactions) in multiple additional states depending on buyer concentration.

**What Flips It & The Trigger:**
The single fact that could shift liability back to sellers is if your terms of service and payment flow explicitly state you are *not* processing the payment (e.g., pure peer-to-peer transfer without platform interception) and you do not control the listing. However, if you charge the fee and settle the net amount to sellers, you are almost certainly the facilitator.
*   **Trigger:** The moment you process your 101st transaction or $100,001 in sales into any single state, the legal obligation to register and collect activates.
