**The Call:** Your donation and grant revenue is generally tax-exempt if you hold 501(c)(3) status, but the $12,000 interest is likely taxable "Unrelated Business Income" (UBI) if it exceeds IRS thresholds, requiring a specific filing; you must file Form 990 annually to maintain compliance, and corporate sponsorships are safe only if they are unconditional gifts rather than payments for advertising services.

**The Numbers & Rules:**
Under IRS rules for 501(c)(3) organizations, revenue treatment splits into three buckets:
1.  **Contributions ($180k individual + $50k corporate):** These are generally excluded from taxable income *if* they are true gifts with no substantial return benefit to the donor.
    *   *Assumption:* The corporate sponsors received only logo placement or general acknowledgment (not advertising). If they received advertising, that portion becomes taxable commercial income.
2.  **Investment Income ($12k interest):** Passive income like interest, dividends, and royalties is usually excluded from UBI tax *unless* it is debt-financed. However, it must still be reported.
    *   *Threshold:* If your total gross receipts are under $200,000 and assets under $500,000, you likely file **Form 990-EZ**. If you have *any* UBI over $1,000, you must also file **Form 990-T** to report and potentially pay tax on that specific income.
3.  **Commercial Activity Risk:** Accepting corporate funds does not automatically jeopardize status. The risk arises if the sponsorship is a *quid pro quo* transaction (e.g., "We pay $10k, you run our ad campaign"). That shifts the revenue from "contribution" to "unrelated business income." If this commercial activity becomes a "substantial part" of your operations (usually >15-20% of total revenue/time), it threatens exempt status.

**What Flips It:**
The classification flips if the corporate sponsors demand specific advertising deliverables rather than general acknowledgment.
*   *Trigger:* If any single sponsor contract includes language guaranteeing "advertising," "endorsement," or specific calls to action beyond a static logo list, reclassify that portion as taxable income immediately.
*   *Step:* Review your top three sponsorship agreements this week; if they mention "deliverables" or "ROI," consult a tax professional to separate the gift portion from the commercial portion.

**Routing Note:**
Because determining whether a sponsorship is a "gift" vs. "advertising" depends on the specific contract language and Illinois state charity registration laws, you must engage a CPA or nonprofit attorney to review your sponsorship agreements and prepare Form 990-T if your unrelated income exceeds $1,000.
