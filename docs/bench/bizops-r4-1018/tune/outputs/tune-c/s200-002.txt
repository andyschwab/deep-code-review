**The Call:** Classifying the nutrient pods as a "subscription" versus a "recurring order" generally **does not** change your sales tax collection obligation, rate, or filing frequency in California, Texas, New York, Florida, or Illinois. The taxability is driven by the physical nature of the pods (tangible personal property), not the billing cycle or the digital management of the account. Your advisors are likely conflating sales tax rules with income tax nexus or digital-product-specific statutes, which do not apply to physical goods shipped to these states.

**The Numbers & Rules:**
Since the pods are physical items shipped to customers, they are taxable as tangible personal property in all five states regardless of whether the customer buys one at a time or signs up for auto-ship.
*   **Tax Rate:** Determined by the ship-to address (destination-based sourcing) in all five states. The rate remains identical for a subscription box vs. a one-off purchase.
*   **Filing Frequency:** Determined by your total taxable sales volume in the state, not by the product's billing classification.
*   **Nexus Thresholds (Remote Seller Registration):** You must register if you exceed economic nexus thresholds. The "subscription" label does not lower these thresholds.
    *   **California:** $500,000 in gross receipts.
    *   **Texas:** $500,000 in gross receipts.
    *   **New York:** $500,000 in gross receipts AND 100 separate transactions.
    *   **Florida:** *No economic nexus threshold for remote sellers based solely on sales volume yet (physical presence or specific activities still trigger); however, legislation is pending/evolving, so monitor closely.* (Note: As of late 2023/early 2024, FL relies heavily on physical presence or click-through nexus, unlike the others).
    *   **Illinois:** $100,000 in gross receipts OR 200 separate transactions.

**What Flips It:**
The only factor that changes your obligation is crossing the **gross receipt dollar thresholds** listed above. If you hit $500k in CA, TX, or NY, you must register. The "digital management" argument fails here because the underlying transaction is the sale of physical nutrients, not a digital service. The trigger to act is your rolling 12-month sales data; if you approach 80% of these limits, prepare to register.

**Routing Note:**
While the general rule is consistent, Florida's nexus laws are distinct and currently less standardized for pure remote sellers compared to the others; verify current physical presence interpretations with a CPA before assuming no filing is needed there. Additionally, if your "digital management" includes selling separate software access or data reports, that specific portion may have different taxability rules requiring allocation.
