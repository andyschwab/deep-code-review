**The Call:** Proceed with extreme caution; using "human-grade" on pet food packaging triggers strict federal (FDA) and state (Ohio) definitions that, if unmet, render the product misbranded and subject to seizure. You cannot legally use this term unless **every** ingredient and the entire manufacturing process meet human-edible standards verified by documentation; otherwise, switch to "high-quality" or similar non-regulated marketing terms immediately.

**The Rules and Usual Practice:**
Under the Federal Food, Drug, and Cosmetic Act (FD&C Act) and enforced by the FDA Center for Veterinary Medicine (CVM), the term "human-grade" is not merely a marketing flourish; it is a regulatory claim implying the product is fit for human consumption.
1.  **Federal Standard:** For a pet food to be labeled "human-grade," **every** ingredient must be human-edible, and the product must be manufactured, packaged, and stored in a facility approved for human food under 21 CFR Part 117 (Current Good Manufacturing Practice for Human Food). If any ingredient is feed-grade or the facility is only registered for animal food, the claim is false and misleading.
2.  **Ohio State Oversight:** Ohio operates under a state feed law consistent with the Association of American Feed Control Officials (AAFCO) model regulations. The Ohio Department of Agriculture (ODA) requires product registration before sale. They strictly enforce labeling claims; if you claim "human-grade," ODA inspectors will expect proof that the supply chain and facility meet human-food standards.
3.  **Facility Registration:** If you claim "human-grade," your facility likely must be registered as a **human food facility** with the FDA, not just an animal food facility. This subjects you to more rigorous inspections and Hazard Analysis and Risk-Based Preventive Controls (HARPC) plans applicable to human food.

**Documentation Requirements:**
To substantiate this claim during an inspection, you must maintain on file:
*   **Supplier Certificates:** Written guarantees from every supplier stating each ingredient meets human-edible specifications at the time of delivery.
*   **Chain of Custody:** Documentation proving human-edible ingredients were never commingled with feed-grade materials during transport or storage.
*   **Facility Certification:** Proof that your co-packer or own facility holds a valid human-food processing license and passes human-food GMP audits.

**Routing Note (High-Stakes/Liability):**
Determining whether your specific supply chain and Ohio facility legally qualify for the "human-grade" designation is a binding legal and regulatory determination (Tier 3). You must engage a food-regulatory attorney or consultant **before** printing packaging or producing the 5,000 cans. Ask them: "Does our current ingredient sourcing and manufacturing process in Ohio satisfy the FDA's 'human-grade' definition under 21 CFR 117, and what specific liability do we face if the ODA challenges our claim?" Do not rely on supplier verbal assurances; false labeling can lead to product recalls and significant fines.
