**What Flips It, and the Trigger:**
The triggering input is **control of funds**. In California, under the *Money Transmission Act* (Fin. Code § 2000 et seq.), receiving money for transmission or holding it as an intermediary generally requires a license, regardless of whether you call it "escrow" or "facilitation."
*   **The Threshold:** If funds touch your company's bank account before reaching the teacher, you are likely transmitting money.
*   **The Trigger:** Processing the next $45,000 without a license or a licensed partner.
*   **Rule of Thumb:** If you cannot return the funds to the sender instantly without your own manual intervention or banking relationship, you are holding value.
*   **This Week's Step:** If your engineering team has started building a ledger that tracks balances inside your database rather than relying on a processor's API, stop immediately and pivot to evaluating "platform payment" solutions.

**Routing Note:**
California's Department of Financial Protection and Innovation (DFPI) enforces strict MTL rules; the specific determination of whether your "two-party completion" logic qualifies for an exemption (e.g., as an agent of the payee) is a binding legal conclusion requiring counsel. Engage a fintech attorney to review your specific flow before launch; do not rely on general "payment facilitator" definitions found online, as California interprets "transmission" broadly.

**Risks of Proceeding Without License:**
Operating without an MTL when required is a criminal offense in California and can result in cease-and-desist orders, fines, and permanent bans from operating in the state. There is no "unknown interpretation" needed to see this risk; the statute is explicit that holding funds for transmission requires a license. The risk of being shut down mid-year is high if a user complaint or audit triggers a DFPI investigation.

**Specific Legal Threshold:**
The threshold is not a dollar amount but the act of **taking possession** of funds for the purpose of transmitting them. Even if held temporarily, if the money enters an account controlled by SkillSwap, you are the transmitter. Third-party processors solve this by having the funds settle in *their* licensed accounts, never yours.
