**The Call**
For IRS tax purposes, you generally must capitalize the $12,500 accreditation fee and amortize it over the 12-month benefit period rather than expensing it immediately. While expensing it now would lower your current quarter's taxable income, it violates the "12-month rule" for prepaid expenses that provide benefits beyond the current tax year, creating an audit risk that outweighs the short-term cash flow appearance.

**The Numbers**
The treatment changes your reported net income for this quarter, though it does not change your actual cash runway.

*   **Formula:** Quarterly Expense = (Annual Fee / 12 months) × 3 months OR Full Immediate Expense.
*   **Your Figures:** $12,500 annual fee; Current Quarter = 3 months.
*   **Scenario A (Correct Treatment - Amortization):**
    *   Monthly amortization: $1,041.67
    *   Q1 Expense: **$3,125**
    *   Remaining Asset on Balance Sheet: $9,375
*   **Scenario B (Incorrect Treatment - Immediate Expense):**
    *   Q1 Expense: **$12,500**
    *   Difference in Taxable Income: **$9,375** (lower in Scenario B)

*Assumption:* Your fiscal quarter aligns with the calendar start of the accreditation period. If the fee covers a period starting mid-quarter, the amortized amount would be slightly lower (marked ASSUMED).

**What Flips It, and The Trigger**
The deciding input is the **duration of the benefit**. IRS Regulation §1.263(a)-4 (the "12-month rule") allows immediate deduction only if the benefit does not extend beyond the earlier of 12 months after the first date of benefit or the end of the tax year following the payment year.
*   **The Trigger:** Since your accreditation renews every January and covers the full upcoming year, the benefit extends 12 months. If your accreditation period were shortened to 11 months or less, you could expense it immediately.
*   **Rule of Thumb:** Prepayments creating an asset with a useful life >1 year (or >12 months from payment) must be capitalized.
*   **Step this week:** If your accreditation term is exactly 12 months, consider adjusting your bookkeeping to record $3,125 as an expense and $9,375 as a "Prepaid Accreditation" asset to align with IRS expectations.

**Routing Note**
While the general rule for prepaid services is clear, the specific application to professional licensing fees versus membership dues can vary by entity structure (S-Corp vs. LLC) and state conformity. Bring the exact accreditation certificate dates and your entity type to your CPA to confirm the amortization schedule before filing.
