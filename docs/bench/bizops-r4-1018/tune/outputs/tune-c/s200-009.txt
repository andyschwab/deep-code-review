**The Call:** Assume your current General Liability (GL) policy excludes recall logistics and destruction costs; you face personal liability exposure if the corporate veil is pierced due to undercapitalization or negligence. Immediately secure standalone Product Recall Insurance and verify your GL policy's "products-completed operations" clause. Do not distribute the pilot batch until you have written confirmation of coverage or a specific risk acceptance from counsel, as infant nutrition is a high-severity regulatory domain.

**The Numbers:**
*   **Potential Exposure:** $150,000 (logistics/destruction) + unknown regulatory fines + reputational damage.
*   **Coverage Gap:** Standard GL policies typically cover third-party bodily injury/property damage *caused* by the product but explicitly exclude the cost of *withdrawing* the product (recall costs).
*   **Estimated Cost of Recall Insurance:** For a pre-revenue startup in infant nutrition, premiums are high-risk. Expect **$15,000–$40,000 annually** for a $1M–$2M limit, often with a high deductible ($10k–$25k).
    *   *Assumption (Low):* Limited distribution (clinical only), robust QA docs → ~$15k.
    *   *Assumption (Base):* Broader test market, standard risk profile → ~$25k.
    *   *Assumption (High):* Perceived high risk by underwriters, no clinical data yet → ~$40k+ or declination.
*   **Bond/Escrow:** No universal federal mandate exists for a fixed bond amount *before* a test batch, but the FDA may require a corrective action plan funded by the company. State-level requirements vary.

**What Flips It, and the Trigger:**
The single input that changes the call is **written confirmation of coverage**. If your broker cannot provide a certificate of insurance specifically listing "Product Recall" (including first-party logistics, destruction, and crisis management) within 14 days, you must halt distribution.
*   **Trigger:** If the premium exceeds 10% of your remaining cash runway, consider narrowing the pilot to a controlled clinical setting under an Investigational New Drug (IND) exemption or similar regulatory framework where liability structures differ, rather than commercial distribution.
*   **Step this week:** Contact your current broker and ask: "Does our GL policy cover first-party recall expenses for infant formula? If not, bind a standalone Product Recall policy effective immediately."
