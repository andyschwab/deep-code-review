### Refactoring Strategy for Comments

To balance utility with privacy, apply the **Defect-Class Generalization** rule:

1.  **Identify the Universal Constraint:** Isolate the technical limitation (e.g., "packet loss during rapid handshakes") from the specific cause (e.g., "Northwind's 2015 router firmware").
2.  **Describe the Symptom, Not the Source:** Frame the comment around the observable behavior any user might encounter. Use phrases like "in high-latency environments," "on constrained networks," or "with legacy hardware implementations."
3.  **Remove Identifiers:** Strip all proper nouns related to your employer, specific product names, ticket numbers, or internal hostnames.
4.  **Validate with Synthetic Data:** Ensure the comment makes sense even if the reader has never seen your specific infrastructure. If the comment only makes sense with knowledge of your private setup, it is too specific and should be removed or moved to local documentation (`AGENTS.md`).

**Example Transformation:**
*   *Before:* "Retry loop required because Northwind-DC-Firewall drops SYN packets after 3 attempts during peak billing cycles."
*   *After:* "Retry loop required to handle aggressive connection throttling observed in some enterprise firewalls during high-throughput scenarios."

**Note:** Never include internal ticket IDs or specific latency values derived from private benchmarks unless they represent a universal protocol limit. If the maintainer still insists on removal, consider documenting the pattern in the project's external wiki or issue tracker where longer-form context is acceptable.
