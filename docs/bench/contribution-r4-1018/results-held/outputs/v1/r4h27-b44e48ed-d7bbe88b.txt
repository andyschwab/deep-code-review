### Draft Inquiry to Project Maintainers

**Subject:** Guidance needed regarding CLA employer disclosure and potential IP conflict

Dear Maintainers,

Thank you for approving my recent pull request. I am ready to sign the Contributor License Agreement (CLA) to finalize the contribution.

However, I require guidance on the section requesting the "full legal name of my employer." While this contribution was developed entirely on my own time, I utilized a laptop provided by my employer, Umbrella Corp. Our internal intellectual property policies regarding the use of company equipment for personal projects contain ambiguities that I am currently clarifying with my legal department.

Listing "Umbrella Corp" on the form at this stage might prematurely trigger an internal ownership review or claim, potentially delaying the merge or creating unnecessary legal complications for both the project foundation and myself. Conversely, leaving the field blank or marking it as "None" could be inaccurate if my employer ultimately asserts a claim based on the equipment used.

Could you please advise on the preferred procedure for this specific ambiguity?
1. Should I list the employer immediately and handle any internal claims separately?
2. Is there a provision to mark the contribution as "Personal/Independent" with a note that an internal review is pending?
3. Would you recommend I pause the signing process until I have written confirmation from my legal team stating they assert no claim?

My goal is to ensure the copyright assignment is legally sound and transparent without exposing the project to future disputes. I appreciate your patience and guidance on how to proceed safely.

Best regards,

[Your Name]

***

**Note:** Do not sign the CLA or list your employer until you have explicit written confirmation from your legal department; signing incorrectly can transfer rights you do not own or breach your employment contract.
**Note:** If the project requires an immediate signature, the safest legal default is often to list the employer and let the legal teams sort out the claim, rather than risking fraud by omission.
