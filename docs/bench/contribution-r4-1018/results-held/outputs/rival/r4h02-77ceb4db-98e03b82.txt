**Do not open a pull request.** Submitting a PR that explicitly violates a pinned maintainer issue stating "will be closed immediately" is considered spamming and damages your reputation with the project. It forces maintainers to perform unnecessary work (closing the PR) and signals that you did not read their critical guidance.

Since you have already completed the work, here is the correct workflow to preserve your effort without violating community norms:

1.  **Preserve Your Work Locally or on a Fork**
    Keep your code on a dedicated branch in your personal fork (e.g., `feat/dark-theme-preview`). This ensures your work is saved, version-controlled, and accessible via URL, but it does not generate notifications or require action from the maintainers.

2.  **Engage in the Existing Discussion**
    Navigate to the pinned issue where the maintainer announced the freeze. Post a single, polite comment including:
    *   A brief acknowledgment that you read the freeze notice and are respecting it by *not* opening a PR.
    *   A statement that you have prepared a contribution ready for when the rewrite is complete.
    *   A link to your fork/branch for their reference if they wish to inspect the palette implementation early.
    *   An offer to rebase and submit immediately once the "core rendering engine" milestone is merged.

    *Example Comment:*
    > "Hi, I noticed the freeze on new themes until the rendering engine rewrite. I respect this decision and will not open a PR now to avoid clutter. However, I have already implemented an accessible dark theme palette on my fork [link] in case the team wants to preview the color choices. I am happy to rebase and submit formally once the engine work lands next quarter."

3.  **Wait for the Green Light**
    Monitor the pinned issue or the project's milestone tracker. Once the core rendering engine rewrite is merged and the freeze is lifted, you can then open your PR. At that time, reference your earlier comment to show you have been engaged and ready, which often speeds up the review process.

**Why this approach works:**
*   **Respect:** It proves you read the documentation and respect the maintainer's roadmap.
*   **Visibility:** It puts your work on their radar without creating noise.
*   **Readiness:** When the freeze lifts, you are the first person ready to merge, rather than starting from scratch.

**Note:** Never ignore a "do not submit" directive in open source; it is the fastest way to get blocked from future contributions.
**Note:** If the project has a Discord or Matrix channel, you may also share your fork link there with a similar "no PR, just preview" disclaimer.
