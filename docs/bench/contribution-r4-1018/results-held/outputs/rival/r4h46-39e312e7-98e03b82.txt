Do not mention your company's financial sponsorship or infrastructure contributions in the pull request description. In open-source maintenance, introducing financial leverage ("we pay for your servers") into a technical discussion is widely perceived as coercive and often triggers immediate defensiveness, regardless of your intent. It shifts the frame from "is this code good?" to "are we being held hostage?", which jeopardizes the merge.

Instead, handle the conflict of interest through **technical transparency** and **standard affiliation disclosure**:

1.  **Let the Code Speak:** Your PR description should focus exclusively on the technical problem (main-thread blocking), the solution (asynchronous rotation), and the evidence (benchmarks showing the elimination of the 2-second pause). This is the only language that matters for merging code.
2.  **Disclose Employment, Not Sponsorship:** In the "Related Issues" or a brief "Disclosure" section at the bottom of the PR, state simply: *"I am a developer at Umbrella Corporation, a heavy user of this framework."* This satisfies ethical transparency requirements by revealing your employer without invoking financial pressure.
3.  **Provide Reproducible Benchmarks:** Since performance claims are sensitive, include a link to a standalone benchmark script or detailed logs in the PR that any maintainer can run independently. This proves the fix works without requiring them to trust your word or your company's status.
4.  **Separate Channels for Sponsorship:** If your company's sponsorship entitles you to specific support SLAs or roadmap influence, that conversation must happen privately between your management and the project maintainers, completely decoupled from this specific code contribution.

By separating the financial relationship from the technical contribution, you protect the integrity of the review process and demonstrate respect for the project's community-driven norms.

Note: Ensure your internal legal team has approved the code release before submitting.
Note: If the project has a specific "Corporate Contributor" template, use that instead of a custom disclosure.
