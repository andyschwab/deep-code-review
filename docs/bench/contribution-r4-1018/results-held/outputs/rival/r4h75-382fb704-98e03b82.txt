### Escalation Strategy for Stalled Security PR

**1. Immediate Internal Mitigation (Do this today)**
Since the vulnerability allows Remote Code Execution (RCE) and affects production, do not wait for the upstream merge.
*   **Action:** Apply the patch locally in your `Gemfile` by pointing directly to your fork's specific commit hash:
    ```ruby
    gem 'vulnerable_gem', git: 'https://github.com/your-username/vulnerable_gem.git', ref: 'your-commit-sha'
    ```
*   **Reasoning:** This secures Acme Corp immediately without requiring maintainer approval. It decouples your production safety from the open-source timeline.

**2. The "Security-First" Ping (Send within 24 hours)**
Silence on a security fix is not "rudeness" to address; it is a risk vector. You must reframe the conversation from "please review my PR" to "reporting an active security incident."
*   **Channel:** Do not comment on the PR thread alone. Look for a `SECURITY.md` file or a private security email address in the repo. If none exists, email Jane directly (if public) or post in the project's official discussion channel (Discourse/Slack) tagged as "Security."
*   **Script:**
    > "Hi Jane/Team, I'm following up on PR #<N> regarding the RCE vulnerability in file uploads. Since this is currently exploitable in production environments (including ours at Acme Corp), we have applied the patch via our fork to mitigate immediate risk.
    >
    > Could you confirm if there is a specific blocker preventing the merge, or if you need additional verification from our side? If the project is unable to maintain this fix urgently, please let us know so we can coordinate a public security advisory to warn other users."

**3. Leverage the "Public Advisory" Lever**
Maintainers often ignore PRs but react quickly to the threat of a public security disclosure.
*   **Strategy:** Mentioning that you may need to issue a public advisory (or that you are forced to maintain a long-term fork) signals that the status quo is unsustainable.
*   **Goal:** This usually prompts an immediate triage. Most maintainers prefer to merge your PR than to manage a fragmented ecosystem or a public "unmaintained" label.

**4. Corporate Policy Check**
*   **Verification:** Check Acme Corp's open-source contribution policy. Most companies *encourage* upstreaming security fixes but require Legal/Security team sign-off if you plan to maintain a long-term fork or issue public advisories.
*   **Action:** Inform your internal Security Lead that the upstream is unresponsive. They will likely support the "fork-and-patch" approach and may even have existing contacts with the gem's maintainers.

**5. The "Fork and Maintain" Decision**
If there is no response within 3 business days after the security ping:
*   Assume the project is effectively unmaintained regarding security.
*   Formalize your fork as the "Acme Corp Secure Version."
*   Document the attempt to contribute upstream in your internal compliance logs. This protects you from liability for using an "unapproved" gem version, as you can prove the official version is unsafe and unpatched.

**Note:** Do not apologize for following up; RCE vulnerabilities define the timeline, not social etiquette.
**Note:** Ensure your internal team monitors your fork for future updates, as you are now effectively the maintainer for your organization.
