Do not submit the pull request yet. Porting logic from a competing library without confirmed license compatibility poses a severe legal risk to the open-source project and your company, regardless of whether you rewrite the code from scratch.

Copyright law generally protects the expression of an algorithm, and in many jurisdictions, the specific structure, sequence, and organization of code can also be protected. "Rewriting from scratch" does not automatically cleanse copyright infringement if the new code is considered a "derivative work" based on the original implementation's unique expression. Since your legal team is unavailable, you must treat this as a hard stop until you perform the following checks:

1.  **Identify the Competitor's License:** Locate the `LICENSE` file in the TitanCharts repository.
    *   **Copyleft Licenses (GPL, AGPL, MPL):** If TitanCharts uses these, you generally **cannot** port their logic into a permissively licensed project (like MIT or Apache) without relicensing the entire target project to match the copyleft terms. This is often a non-starter for permissive projects.
    *   **Permissive Licenses (MIT, BSD, Apache 2.0):** These usually allow porting, but they have conditions. Apache 2.0, for instance, includes an explicit patent grant; if you port the code, you may be triggering patent clauses you haven't reviewed.
    *   **Proprietary/No License:** If there is no explicit license file, default copyright laws apply, meaning **all rights are reserved**. You strictly cannot use the code or the logic derived from it.

2.  **Analyze "Clean Room" Requirements:** To safely port logic, you ideally need a "clean room" implementation where the developer writing the new code has **not** directly read or copied the source code of the competing library, but instead worked from a functional specification. Since you have already studied their code to prepare your change, you are contaminated from a legal defense standpoint.

3.  **Frame the Contribution Safely:**
    *   **Do not mention the competitor:** In your PR description, do not state "I ported this from TitanCharts." This admission creates immediate evidence of derivative work.
    *   **Focus on the mathematical problem:** Describe the solution purely in terms of the mathematical or geometric problem being solved (e.g., "adjusted angle calculation to handle precision loss in high-segment counts").
    *   **Wait for Legal:** The only safe path forward without legal counsel is to open an issue in the target repository describing the *bug* (not your fix) and ask if maintainers have interest in a solution for >20 segments. If they express interest, you can then engage your legal team to formally clear the specific approach.

Submitting a PR now could force the maintainers to reject your work immediately to protect their project's licensing integrity, or worse, introduce liability that they are ill-equipped to handle.
