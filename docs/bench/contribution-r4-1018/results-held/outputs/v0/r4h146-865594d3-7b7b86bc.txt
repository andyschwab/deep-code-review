### Strategy to Resolve the Style Deadlock

**1. Immediate Action: Stop the "Diff Format" Proposal**
Do not propose sending a raw diff for manual application. Maintainers of legacy projects typically reject this because it bypasses their version control history, makes code review difficult, and shifts the burden of integration entirely onto them. This approach often signals that the contributor is unwilling to engage with the project's workflow, leading to further friction.

**2. The Negotiation Pivot: "One-Time Style Concession"**
Reframe the conversation with the maintainers. Instead of asking them to modernize or you to adopt a permanent standard, propose a **single, isolated commit** that adheres strictly to their existing (albeit undocumented) style *only for this specific fix*.
*   **Argument:** "I understand the project prioritizes historical consistency over modern conventions. I am willing to manually align this specific contribution with the existing codebase patterns as a one-time exception to get this critical memory leak fixed. I am not asking to change the project's standards, only to match them for this patch."
*   **Why this works:** It respects their authority, removes the threat of "modernization," and isolates the effort to a single transaction rather than a policy change.

**3. Solving the "Undocumented Style" Problem (Without Violating Policy)**
Your company policy prohibits adopting *external standards* formally. However, **reverse-engineering a pattern for a single task** does not constitute adopting a standard.
*   **Action:** Analyze the last 50–100 commits in the specific module you are editing. Extract the *de facto* rules (e.g., "braces on new lines," "variables declared at top of function").
*   **Documentation for Internal Review:** Create a temporary, internal document titled "Observation of Project X's Existing Patterns for Patch Y." Explicitly state: *"This is not an adopted standard for Umbrella Corporation engineering. It is a transient reference used solely to format this specific upstream contribution."*
*   **Submit this transient doc** to your legal/compliance team. Since it is framed as "mimicking existing external code" rather than "adopting a new corporate standard," it usually bypasses the formal review blockade.

**4. The "Minimal Diff" Fallback**
If the style gap is too wide to bridge manually without error:
*   Split your contribution into two commits (if the project allows):
    1.  A pure whitespace/style fix commit (touching only formatting).
    2.  The functional memory leak fix.
*   Offer to let *them* squash or reformat these later. Sometimes maintainers reject a mixed bag but will accept a functional patch if they know they can run their own ancient `indent` tool on it afterward without losing logic changes. Ask: "If I ensure the logic is isolated in a clean commit, are you willing to run your own formatting tools on it post-merge?"

**5. Final Escalation Path**
If they still refuse:
*   Fork the project, apply your fix with modern standards, and maintain it internally.
*   Publish the fork (if license permits) as "Umbrella Corporation's Patched Version of [Project]."
*   Inform the maintainers politely: "We will maintain this security fix internally in our fork. If you ever wish to upstream it, the patch is available here." This often prompts a reconsideration, as maintaining divergent security fixes is a burden for them too.

**Note:** Never mention "Umbrella Corporation" or internal policy constraints in the public issue tracker; frame all arguments around "project consistency" and "respect for historical code."
**Note:** Ensure your final patch contains no internal paths, variable names, or comments referencing your employer before submission.
