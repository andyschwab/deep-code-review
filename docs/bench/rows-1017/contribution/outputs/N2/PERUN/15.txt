Skipping mandatory peer review for security patches violates fundamental software supply chain security principles. In almost all mature governance models, the "emergency" procedure does not mean "no review"; it means "expedited review" by an alternate authorized party. Merging without any review creates an unverified trust boundary and risks introducing the very compromise you aim to prevent.

To address this situation compliantly:
1.  **Activate the Emergency Contact Chain:** Consult your project's `SECURITY.md` or governance charter for the list of *alternate* maintainers or the security team roster. The unavailability of one maintainer rarely voids the requirement for a second set of eyes; it simply shifts the responsibility to the next person on the escalation list.
2.  **Request Expedited Review, Not Waiver:** Open the pull request immediately and tag all available senior contributors, explicitly stating the SLA constraint. The audit trail of the request and the urgency is part of the compliance record.
3.  **Document the Incident, Not the Bypass:** Your incident report should detail the vulnerability, the fix, the timeline of the response, and the fact that standard review was accelerated via the escalation path. It should never document a successful bypass of the review gate, as that itself becomes a compliance violation and a potential security finding.

If no other authorized reviewer is reachable, the compliant action is often to delay the merge until one is available, unless your organization has a pre-delegated "break-glass" account or a specific, pre-approved protocol for single-person emergency merges (which typically requires multi-factor hardware key authorization and immediate post-hoc audit). Without such a pre-existing, structural mechanism, a self-authorized merge is indefensible under standard governance models.
