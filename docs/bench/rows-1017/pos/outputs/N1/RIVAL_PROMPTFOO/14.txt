Given your three-week timeline and resource constraints, you cannot rely on broad automated scanning or external beta testing. Instead, you must implement a high-intensity, manual "human-in-the-loop" validation workflow that treats every generated letter as potentially dangerous until proven safe. Your goal is to create a defensive barrier where no output reaches a landlord or court without specific, documented verification.

**Step 1: Construct a "Golden Set" of Test Cases**
Before generating new letters, assemble a static dataset of 20–30 historical eviction scenarios your team has already handled successfully. These should cover your most common defense types (e.g., improper notice, repair-and-deduct, retaliation).
*   Include the original tenant story and the specific local housing codes applied.
*   Include the final, attorney-approved letter that was actually sent.
*   This dataset serves as your ground truth. You will run your AI against these inputs to see if it reproduces the logic and tone of your proven work.

**Step 2: Define Strict "Hard Stop" Validation Rules**
Establish non-negotiable criteria that automatically flag a draft for human rejection. Do not rely on the AI to self-correct. Your reviewers must check for:
*   **Citation Integrity:** Every case law citation, statute number, and court rule must be manually verified against official sources. If the AI invents a case name or misquotes a statute code, the draft is immediately discarded.
*   **Tone Guardrails:** The output must never admit liability, apologize for the tenant's situation, or use emotional language. It must remain strictly formal and assertive.
*   **Fact Fidelity:** The letter must not invent facts not present in the intake story (e.g., claiming a repair request was made if the user didn't say so).

**Step 3: Execute the "Shadow Run" Comparison**
Run your 20–30 "Golden Set" cases through the new AI system. Have your legal staff compare the AI drafts side-by-side with the original approved letters.
*   **Measure Deviation:** Did the AI miss a critical legal argument? Did it add a hallucinated precedent? Did the tone shift from professional to pleading?
*   **Document Errors:** Create a simple log of every error type found. If the AI hallucinates case law in even one instance, you must disable automatic citation generation and require staff to insert all legal references manually.

**Step 4: Implement a Two-Person Review Protocol for Live Cases**
Since you cannot afford malpractice upgrades, your process must act as your insurance. For the initial launch phase, mandate a "four-eyes" principle for every single letter:
*   **Generator:** One staff member runs the intake data through the AI to produce a draft.
*   **Verifier:** A second qualified staff member (preferably an attorney) reviews the draft line-by-line using a printed checklist. They must physically sign off or digitally approve the document before it is saved as final.
*   **Checklist Items:** The verifier specifically checks: Are all citations real? Is the tone appropriate? Are the facts accurate to the intake? Is the recipient address correct?

**Step 5: Create a "Kill Switch" and Feedback Loop**
Designate one senior team member as the Safety Lead. If they detect a pattern of errors (e.g., two hallucinated cases in one day), they have the authority to pause AI usage immediately until the prompt or system is adjusted.
*   Hold a 15-minute daily stand-up for the first two weeks to review any near-misses or errors caught during verification.
*   Use these findings to refine your prompts iteratively. If the AI consistently struggles with a specific type of defense, restrict its use for that scenario until it is fixed.

**Step 6: Limit Scope for Initial Launch**
Do not attempt to automate every possible eviction defense immediately. Restrict the AI's usage to the top three most common, straightforward case types where your "Golden Set" showed high accuracy. Complex cases involving multiple defendants or unusual factual patterns should remain fully manual until the system proves stable.

By treating the AI as a junior drafter that requires supervision rather than an autonomous agent, you mitigate the risk of hallucinations reaching the court. Your rigorous manual verification process replaces the need for expensive insurance or external testing in this critical launch window.
