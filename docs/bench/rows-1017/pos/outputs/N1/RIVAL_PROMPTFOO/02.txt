Given your hard deadline for the winter storm season and the critical nature of maintenance prioritization, your safety plan must focus on immediate observability and rapid reversibility. Since you are in the final integration stage, you cannot rely on lengthy retraining cycles; instead, you must implement robust monitoring guards and a clear "circuit breaker" protocol.

### Monitoring for Biased Prioritization

To detect bias against specific building types or tenant demographics, you need to instrument your system to capture not just the output (the priority score), but the input features and the decision logic for every request. Do not rely on aggregate metrics alone, as they can hide disparities affecting small groups.

**Key Monitoring Actions:**

*   **Feature-Outcome Correlation:** Log the priority score alongside protected attributes (e.g., building age, neighborhood zip code, tenant tenure) in a secure, anonymized format. Run daily statistical checks to see if specific demographics consistently receive lower urgency scores for identical issue descriptions (e.g., "no heat" in Building A vs. Building B).
*   **Shadow Mode Comparison:** Before full traffic cutover, run the new AI model in parallel with your existing rule-based logic (or a simple heuristic baseline) for at least 48 hours. Flag any instance where the AI deprioritizes a request that the baseline would have marked as "Critical."
*   **Human-in-the-Loop Sampling:** Mandate that maintenance supervisors manually review a random 10% of "Low Priority" tickets and 100% of tickets flagged as "Low Priority" but containing keywords like "freeze," "burst," or "no heat." Their overrides serve as immediate ground-truth data for bias detection.
*   **Drift Alerts:** Set thresholds for the distribution of priority scores. If the percentage of "High Priority" tickets drops suddenly by more than 15% compared to historical averages for this season, trigger an alert. This could indicate the model has become overly conservative for certain inputs.

### Rollback and Error Response Plan

Because the cost of failure involves potential property damage and tenant safety during winter, your rollback strategy must be automatic and decisive. You cannot afford a gradual phase-out if the system fails.

**Step-by-Step Rollback Protocol:**

1.  **Define Trigger Conditions:** Establish hard limits that automatically halt AI decision-making. Examples include:
    *   More than 5 confirmed tenant complaints about delayed critical repairs within 24 hours.
    *   Detection of a >20% discrepancy in prioritization between two similar building clusters.
    *   System latency exceeding 2 seconds, causing backlog accumulation.
2.  **The "Circuit Breaker" Switch:** Implement a feature flag that instantly bypasses the AI prioritizer. When triggered, the system must revert to a deterministic, rule-based fallback. This fallback should prioritize based on simple, auditable keywords (e.g., "no heat" = Critical, "leak" = High) regardless of building type or tenant history.
3.  **Immediate Notification:** Upon activation of the circuit breaker, automatically notify the engineering on-call lead, the product owner, and the maintenance dispatch manager via SMS and email. The notification must state clearly: "AI Prioritizer Disabled – Reverting to Rule-Based Logic."
4.  **Backlog Triage:** Once reverted, the maintenance team must manually review any tickets processed by the AI in the hour preceding the failure. These tickets should be re-evaluated using the fallback rules to ensure no critical issues were missed during the error window.
5.  **Post-Incident Analysis:** Do not re-enable the AI until a root cause analysis is complete. This analysis must determine if the error was due to data drift, a model bug, or an edge case in the input data. Fix the specific failure mode and re-validate using the shadow mode process before attempting another launch.

### Pre-Launch Readiness Checklist

Before the winter storm deadline, ensure the following are confirmed:

*   **Data Pipeline:** Verification that all input features and output scores are being logged to a queryable database with <5 minute latency.
*   **Fallback Logic:** Confirmation that the rule-based fallback system is deployed, tested, and ready to take 100% of traffic instantly.
*   **On-Call Rotation:** A defined schedule for engineers and product managers to monitor dashboards 24/7 for the first seven days.
*   **Tenant Communication:** A drafted template to inform tenants of potential delays should a rollback occur, maintaining transparency without causing panic.

By focusing on these observable metrics and maintaining a strict, automated path to revert to simple rules, you protect both your tenants and your property assets while managing the risks of this new system.
