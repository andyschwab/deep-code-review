Given the high stakes of wildfire evacuation and the two-month deadline, your safety evaluation must prioritize preventing false negatives (failing to warn someone in danger) over false positives (warning someone unnecessarily). With legacy mapping tools creating border-zone uncertainty, your simulation plan should stress-test the system's decision logic at the edges of its precision.

Here are the specific scenarios you should simulate to ensure human safety and clarity:

**1. The "Border Zone" Oscillation Test**
Simulate fire spread models where the fire line hovers exactly on the boundary of a neighborhood for an extended period.
*   **Goal:** Verify the system does not flip-flop between "Evacuate" and "Shelter-in-place" for the same residents as the model updates.
*   **Safety Check:** Ensure the logic defaults to "Evacuate" when confidence is low or data is ambiguous. A confused resident who receives conflicting messages is less likely to act than one who receives a clear, perhaps overly cautious, order.

**2. The Latency and Stale Data Scenario**
Simulate a delay between real-time fire movement and the data ingestion into your legacy mapping tool.
*   **Goal:** Test how the generator handles data that is 5, 10, or 15 minutes old during a rapidly moving fire event.
*   **Safety Check:** Confirm the system includes timestamps and urgency qualifiers in the text (e.g., "Based on data from 10 minutes ago..."). More importantly, verify that if the data gap exceeds a safe threshold, the system triggers a "Precautionary Evacuation" for the entire at-risk sector rather than guessing specific neighborhoods.

**3. The "Conflicting Instruction" Cascade**
Simulate a scenario where adjacent neighborhoods receive different instructions due to slight variations in the model's precision.
*   **Goal:** Observe the human reaction to seeing neighbors evacuate while being told to stay.
*   **Safety Check:** Evaluate if the generated text explicitly explains *why* the distinction exists without undermining the order. If the explanation causes hesitation, the logic should be adjusted to broaden the evacuation zone to include the buffer area, ensuring uniform messaging for clustered communities.

**4. The System Overload and Partial Failure**
Simulate a surge in concurrent requests or a partial failure in the mapping module during peak fire activity.
*   **Goal:** Determine the system's failure mode.
*   **Safety Check:** The system must fail "loudly" and "safely." It should never output a "Shelter-in-place" message if it cannot verify location safety. The default output for any unverified zone must be an immediate evacuation order or a directive to tune into emergency radio for manual instructions.

**5. The Ambiguous Address Resolution**
Test inputs with addresses that the legacy tools struggle to geocode precisely (e.g., rural route boxes, new developments not fully mapped).
*   **Goal:** Ensure these addresses do not fall through the cracks or receive a generic "monitor the situation" message.
*   **Safety Check:** Verify that any address the system cannot confidently map to a safe zone automatically triggers the highest alert level. Uncertainty in location mapping must always resolve to evacuation.

**6. The Human-in-the-Loop Override Simulation**
Since you mentioned the system operates without human verification, simulate a scenario where an emergency manager manually identifies a flaw in the generated alert before it sends.
*   **Goal:** Test the speed and reliability of a manual "kill switch" or override mechanism.
*   **Safety Check:** Even if the goal is automation, you need a guaranteed way to halt or broadcast a correction if the model behaves erratically. Measure the time from human realization of error to the correction reaching residents.

**Evaluation Criteria for Success**
For every simulation, measure success not by technical accuracy, but by behavioral outcome:
*   **Clarity:** Did the message leave any room for interpretation?
*   **Actionability:** Was the required action (leave now vs. stay put) the first thing the resident read?
*   **Consistency:** Did residents in similar risk profiles receive consistent instructions?
*   **Conservatism:** In every instance of doubt, did the system choose the path that preserves life over the path that preserves property or reduces false alarms?

Before launch, conduct a "tabletop exercise" with your seven-person team walking through these scenarios manually. If the legacy tools cannot guarantee precision, your policy must be to widen the evacuation net. It is far better to evacuate a safe neighborhood than to leave a dangerous one uninformed.
