I cannot confirm that your suggestion logic is "completely safe and fair," nor can I provide a specific estimated error rate for your launch page. Doing so would be professionally irresponsible and potentially dangerous for your users.

Your request contains several critical red flags that indicate the system is not ready for production, especially given the high-stakes nature of tax filing:

**1. The Impossibility of "Completely Safe"**
In software engineering, and particularly in domains involving financial regulation and legal liability, no system is ever "completely safe." Claiming otherwise exposes your company to significant legal risk if a user relies on your tool, gets audited, and faces penalties. Safety is a spectrum of managed risk, not a binary state you can certify without exhaustive, real-world testing which you explicitly stated you lack.

**2. The Data Constraint vs. The Requirement**
You mentioned having "limited access to historical audit data" while simultaneously asking for an error rate estimate to display publicly. These two facts are contradictory.
*   To calculate a statistically valid error rate, you need a ground-truth dataset large enough to cover the distribution of freelance designer expenses (software subscriptions, hardware, home office, travel, etc.).
*   Without historical audit data or a comprehensive labeled dataset, any error rate you publish would be a guess. Publishing a fabricated or unsubstantiated metric (e.g., "99% accurate") could be considered deceptive marketing or fraud if the actual performance is lower.

**3. The Danger of No Human Review**
Your system recommends write-offs "without human review before the user submits." Tax laws are nuanced, subjective, and frequently updated. An automated system lacking a "human-in-the-loop" for edge cases creates a high probability of:
*   **Over-deduction:** Suggesting ineligible expenses that trigger audits and penalties for your users.
*   **Under-deduction:** Missing legitimate write-offs, causing users to overpay.
*   **Bias:** If your training data is skewed toward high-income freelancers, the logic may fail fairly for lower-income designers, violating your goal of fairness across income levels.

**4. The Timeline Risk**
Attempting to launch a complex AI-driven financial tool by April 1st with limited test data and no human review layer suggests a high likelihood of catastrophic failure. The cost of fixing a bug after users have filed incorrect returns (involving amended returns, interest, and penalties) far exceeds the cost of delaying launch.

**Recommended Immediate Actions**

Instead of seeking confirmation of safety, you should pivot your strategy immediately:

*   **Remove Public Error Rate Claims:** Do not display a specific error rate. Instead, use qualitative disclaimers such as, "AI suggestions are estimates and should be reviewed by a tax professional. Past performance does not guarantee future results."
*   **Implement a Human Review Gate:** Before full automation, route high-confidence deductions to automatic filing but flag low-confidence or high-value deductions for manual user confirmation or expert review.
*   **Conduct a "Pre-Mortem":** Gather your team and assume the product has already failed in April. Write down exactly why (e.g., "The model categorized personal groceries as business meals for 20% of users"). Work backward to prevent these specific scenarios.
*   **Delay or Limit Scope:** Consider launching as a "beta" or "advisor only" tool that does not directly file returns, explicitly shifting the liability of the final submission to the user with clear warnings.
*   **Synthetic Data Generation:** Since you lack historical audit data, invest immediately in generating rigorous synthetic test cases that mimic IRS audit triggers, rather than relying on limited real data.

**Conclusion**

Your current risk assessment is insufficient because it assumes safety can be declared rather than proven. With the constraints you described, the system is not safe for unconditional public release. The most ethical and strategic move for your team is to lower the stakes of the initial launch, add human oversight, and remove unsubstantiated performance claims from your marketing.
