Given your three-week timeline and budget constraints, you cannot rely on external bias detection suites. Instead, you must build a rigorous internal "human-in-the-loop" review process augmented by targeted, low-cost automated checks. Since your system publishes automatically without human editing, your safety net must exist *before* the publish button is effectively pressed.

Here is a practical workflow to mitigate discriminatory language and ensure inclusivity:

### 1. Define Your "Inclusive Language" Guardrails
Before testing, your team must agree on what constitutes a failure. Create a living document that categorizes risky language specific to job postings. Do not rely on generic definitions; tailor them to your domain.
*   **Gendered Coding:** List words historically associated with masculine (e.g., "ninja," "dominate," "rockstar") or feminine (e.g., "supportive," "understanding") stereotypes that might deter specific groups.
*   **Age Bias:** Identify terms that imply youth ("digital native," "fresh graduate") or excessive experience ("20+ years" for entry-level roles).
*   **Ableism:** Flag requirements for physical abilities or specific communication styles unless they are bona fide occupational qualifications.
*   **Socioeconomic Barriers:** Watch for unnecessary degree requirements when experience suffices, or language implying unpaid overtime culture ("hustle," "grind").

### 2. Construct a Diverse Synthetic Test Suite
You cannot test fairness only on happy-path inputs. You need a curated set of 50–100 synthetic role descriptions designed to trigger edge cases.
*   **Role Variety:** Include stereotypically male-dominated (e.g., construction, engineering), female-dominated (e.g., nursing, admin), and neutral roles.
*   **Adversarial Inputs:** Deliberately feed the AI biased prompts (e.g., "Write an ad for a aggressive sales guy") to see if the model corrects the bias or amplifies it.
*   **Edge Cases:** Test vague inputs, contradictory requirements, and highly specialized roles to ensure the AI doesn't hallucinate exclusionary criteria when confused.

### 3. Implement a Two-Tier Automated Filter
Since you are using basic keyword filtering, you must maximize its effectiveness before the human review.
*   **Blocklist:** Configure your filter to instantly flag or halt generation if high-risk terms (e.g., "boys club," "young energy") appear in the output.
*   **Requirelist:** Ensure essential inclusive phrases (e.g., "equal opportunity employer," "flexible working arrangements") are present. If missing, the system should auto-append them or flag for review.
*   **Pattern Matching:** Use regular expressions to catch variations of blocked words (e.g., "sales-man," "sales-woman") rather than just exact matches.

### 4. The "Red Team" Review Sprint
With a team of five, dedicate two days solely to adversarial testing. Do not act as developers during this time; act as malicious users and protected groups.
*   **Blind Scoring:** Have each team member review 20 generated ads without knowing the input prompt. Score them on a simple 1–5 scale for inclusivity.
*   **Perspective Taking:** Assign each reviewer a specific persona (e.g., "older worker," "person with disability," "non-native speaker") and ask: "Would I apply to this?"
*   **False Positive Analysis:** Review ads flagged by your keyword filter that are actually harmless. Tuning this now prevents user frustration later.

### 5. The Pre-Launch Gate
Because your system publishes automatically, you must insert a mandatory "staging" step for the first month of launch.
*   **Shadow Mode:** For the first 100 real user requests, generate the ad but do *not* publish it. Send it to an internal dashboard for rapid human review.
*   **Sampling Strategy:** Once you move to live publishing, implement a random sampling rule where 10% of all generated ads are held for manual review before posting.
*   **Feedback Loop:** Include a "Report Issue" link on the published job ads visible to applicants. This turns your user base into a distributed monitoring system.

### 6. Documentation and Iteration
Fairness is not a one-time fix.
*   **Log Everything:** Store the input prompt, the generated ad, and the filter triggers for every request. This data is vital for post-incident analysis.
*   **Weekly Retro:** Spend 30 minutes weekly reviewing any flagged ads or user reports. Update your blocklist and guardrails document immediately based on findings.

By combining a strict definition of bias, adversarial synthetic testing, optimized keyword filtering, and a temporary human review gate, you can launch with confidence despite budget limitations. This approach shifts your strategy from "hope for the best" to "verify before trust."
