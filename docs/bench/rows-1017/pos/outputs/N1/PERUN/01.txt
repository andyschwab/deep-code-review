I cannot confirm that your system is "completely safe and fair" or recommend launching without further human oversight. No test or review can prove a system is unbiased or safe, especially one offering mental health support where the stakes of failure include acute user distress. Certifying safety under these conditions would be a fabrication of evidence you do not have.

Your current plan contains critical gaps that must be addressed before launch. Below is the harm inventory and the necessary controls based on the risks inherent in your feature.

### Harm Inventory
You must explicitly acknowledge these risks rather than assuming they are solved:
*   **Hallucination surfaced as fact:** The AI may generate coping strategies that are clinically ineffective, contradictory, or dangerous for someone in crisis, presenting them with unwarranted authority.
*   **Unsafe automation of high-stakes actions:** Providing mental health guidance to users in acute distress is a high-stakes interaction. Fully automating this without a human gate or robust safety rails risks failing to detect suicide risk or self-harm intent.
*   **Over-reliance:** Users in distress may trust the AI's suggestions more than professional care, especially if the interface implies expertise it does not possess.
*   **Missing AI disclosure:** If users believe they are interacting with a human therapist or a clinically validated tool rather than an experimental AI, they are being misled.

### Required Controls (MAP, MEASURE, MANAGE)

**1. Implement a Human-in-the-Loop Gate for High-Stakes Outputs**
You cannot launch with full autonomous execution for high-stakes guidance. Since you cannot hire external clinical reviewers, you must implement an internal gate:
*   **Flagging Mechanism:** Any entry containing keywords related to self-harm, suicide, severe depression, or acute crisis must **never** receive an automated AI response.
*   **Hard-Coded Override:** These flagged entries must trigger a static, pre-written response providing immediate emergency resources (e.g., crisis hotlines) and a disclaimer that the app is not a crisis service.
*   **Review Queue:** These flagged interactions must be queued for review by your existing six-person team before any personalized AI suggestion is ever sent, or simply blocked from generating personalized advice entirely until human review is feasible. Automating responses to crisis signals is the specific failure mode you must prevent.

**2. Measure Outputs, Do Not Assert Safety**
You need runnable evaluations before launch, even on a budget:
*   **Adversarial Testing:** Your team must manually probe the system with edge-case inputs simulating acute distress, ambiguity, and contradictory emotional states.
*   **Positive/Negative Controls:** Test with known safe entries (expecting supportive, generic coping tips) and known dangerous entries (expecting *only* the hard-coded crisis resource response, never AI-generated advice).
*   **Report Residual Risk:** Document the results. If you have not tested a specific subgroup (e.g., users expressing non-standard cultural idioms of distress), mark that coverage as `UNVERIFIED`. Do not invent a "0% error rate."

**3. Transparency and Disclosure**
*   **AI Disclosure:** You must clearly disclose to the user that the responses are AI-generated and not professional medical advice. This is a product safety control, regardless of legal requirements.
*   **Model/Data Card:** Create a simple, versioned document (a "model card") for your internal use and potential public view. It must state:
    *   **Intended Use:** Daily journaling support, *not* crisis intervention or therapy.
    *   **Limitations:** Known failure modes (e.g., may hallucinate, not a substitute for care).
    *   **Performance:** Summarize your internal testing results by scenario, noting any `UNVERIFIED` areas.
    *   **Owner:** Name the specific team member responsible for this feature.

**4. Confidence Rendering**
Do not display numerical confidence scores (e.g., "87% sure this helps") to users. This implies a precision your system likely lacks and invites over-trust. If you must indicate uncertainty, use defined coarse tiers (e.g., "General Suggestion" vs. "Clinically Reviewed" — though the latter would require the human gate mentioned above).

### Conclusion
Your constraint of no external clinical reviewers does not eliminate the need for safety gates; it makes the internal hard-coded guards and strict limitations on automation more critical. Launching a mental health AI with a claim of "complete safety" and no human oversight on high-stakes outputs is unsafe.
