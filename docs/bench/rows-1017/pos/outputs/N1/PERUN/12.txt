**Do not launch this feature with fully automated, high-stakes voice calls.**

Your current design—where an AI agent analyzes financial data, determines negotiation limits, and speaks to customers without human intervention—violates the fundamental safety floor for product outputs. This is not a matter of needing a "more robust evaluation plan"; it is a structural failure of the feature design itself.

Automated debt collection is a **high-stakes, irreversible action**. The potential harms include severe financial damage to users, psychological distress from aggressive or hallucinated accusations, legal liability for your organization, and reputational ruin. No amount of pre-launch testing can guarantee that a generative voice agent will not hallucinate a debt amount, misidentify a debtor, or adopt an illegally aggressive tone in a novel situation.

Here is the structured approach you must take to address these risks:

### 1. Immediate Design Correction: The Human Gate
You must redesign the workflow to include a **human-in-the-loop (HITL) gate** before any external communication occurs.
*   **Current Failure:** The system executes the call autonomously.
*   **Required Fix:** The AI may *draft* the call strategy, tone, and script, but a human collector must review and explicitly approve the specific action before the call is placed.
*   **Why:** You cannot certify the system as "safe" or "unbiased" based on tests. The only way to manage the residual risk of hallucination and bias in this domain is to prevent the AI from acting alone. Full automation of this action is the failure mode this safety protocol exists to prevent.

### 2. Map the Harm Inventory
Before proceeding, document the specific harms your feature could cause. Do not leave this blank.
*   **Hallucination as Fact:** The agent invents a debt amount, interest rate, or legal consequence and states it with authority.
*   **Bias/Unfair Outcomes:** The tone or negotiation limits systematically disadvantage specific demographic groups based on flawed patterns in training data.
*   **Misidentification:** The agent contacts the wrong person or discusses debts with unauthorized parties (privacy violation).
*   **Over-reliance:** The design encourages the business to trust the AI's "negotiation limit" calculation without verifying the underlying logic for edge cases.
*   **Deceptive Patterns:** The voice synthesis mimics human empathy to manipulate vulnerable users into agreements they cannot afford.

### 3. Measure with Adversarial Eval (Not Just Code Tests)
You must test the *outputs*, not just the code logic. Since you cannot run these tests for me, you must build this evaluation harness internally:
*   **Probe Sets:** Create adversarial scenarios (e.g., a user claiming identity theft, a user speaking a different dialect, a user in bankruptcy).
*   **Controls:** Include known "bad" inputs that should trigger a refusal or hand-off, and known "good" inputs that should proceed to human review.
*   **Metrics:** Define pass/fail criteria for hallucination rates and tone safety. **Do not invent a metric.** If you have not measured it, label it `UNVERIFIED`.
*   **Stop Condition:** Define the exact failure rate that blocks launch. For high-stakes financial harm, this threshold should effectively be zero tolerance for autonomous errors.

### 4. Transparency and Disclosure Artifacts
You must produce a **Model Card** and **Data Card** before shipping.
*   **Model Card:** Document the intended use (internal draft assistance only, not autonomous calling), limitations, known failure modes, and performance metrics by subgroup.
*   **Data Card:** Detail the provenance of the payment history and negotiation data used to train the tone analysis.
*   **Disclosure:** The system must disclose to the customer that they are interacting with an AI. Whether this is *legally required* in your jurisdiction is a question for counsel, but as a safety control, it is mandatory.

You mentioned a tight budget preventing legal review. This is a critical risk signal.
*   **Route to Counsel:** Determining if this constitutes a regulated automated decision under laws like the EU AI Act, US Fair Debt Collection Practices Act (FDCPA), or local consumer protection laws is **not** a technical task. It is a legal determination.
