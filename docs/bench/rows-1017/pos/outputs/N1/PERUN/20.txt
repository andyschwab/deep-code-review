Your current plan to publish job ads directly without human editing is a critical failure point. Because hiring decisions are high-stakes and the output can systematically exclude protected groups, fully automated publication violates the safety floor for this feature. You cannot "test" your way out of this risk before launch; you must architect a human gate into the workflow immediately.

Here is the practical workflow you need to implement before releasing to users:

**1. Establish the Human-in-the-Loop Gate**
Stop the "publish directly" logic. The system must generate a draft and pause. A human reviewer (your team or the employer) must explicitly approve the final text before it sends to any job board.
*   **Why:** No keyword filter can catch subtle bias, coded language, or hallucinated requirements that discourage diverse applicants.
*   **Action:** Modify the release flow so the "Publish" button is disabled until a human clicks "Approve" on the specific generated text.

**2. Create a Harm Inventory**
Before writing tests, document exactly what could go wrong for your users (job seekers). Do not leave this blank.
*   **Bias/Unfair Outcomes:** Does the AI add gender-coded words (e.g., "ninja," "dominant") or unnecessary physical requirements?
*   **Hallucination as Fact:** Does the AI invent qualifications or benefits the employer does not offer, misleading applicants?
*   **Over-reliance:** Does the interface make the employer feel the AI is an expert, causing them to skip reading the draft?
*   **Missing Disclosure:** Do applicants know this job description was machine-generated? (Note: Whether you are *legally* required to disclose this is a question for counsel, but transparency is a safety control).

**3. Run Manual Output Evaluations (Measure)**
Since you cannot afford advanced bias tools, you must use rigorous manual red-teaming. Treat this as your "basic" testing suite.
*   **Build Probe Sets:** Create 20–30 diverse role descriptions (e.g., "caregiver," "construction lead," "executive assistant") that historically trigger bias.
*   **Define Controls:**
    *   *Positive Control:* Input a prompt known to generate biased output in other models to ensure your filters catch it.
    *   *Negative Control:* Input a neutral prompt to ensure you aren't blocking valid language.
*   **Execute the Test:** Run these prompts through your generator. Have two different humans review every output against your harm inventory.
*   **Set a Stop Condition:** If *any* generated ad contains discriminatory language or hallucinated benefits, you do not launch. There is no "acceptable error rate" for civil rights violations.

**4. Surface Uncertainty and Limits**
Do not present the AI's output as final or authoritative.
*   **UI Changes:** Label the output clearly as "AI Draft – Requires Review."
*   **Confidence Tiers:** Do not show a percentage score (e.g., "95% inclusive"). This implies a precision you do not have. Instead, use coarse, defined tiers if you must signal risk, such as "Standard Wording" vs. "Unverified Phrasing Detected – Review Required."
*   **Override:** Ensure the human reviewer can easily edit or reject the text without friction.

**5. Document Transparency (The Model Card)**
You must ship a simple, versioned document (a Model Card) alongside this feature, even for a small team.
*   **Intended Use:** Generating draft job descriptions for small employers.
*   **Out-of-Scope:** Do not use for legal contracts, binding offers, or regulated industries (healthcare, finance) without extra review.
*   **Limitations:** Explicitly state: "This tool may generate biased or inaccurate language. It has not been certified as unbiased. Human review is mandatory."
*   **Data Provenance:** Briefly note what data the model was trained on or fine-tuned with, and acknowledge known gaps.

**6. Governance and Ownership**
Name one person on your five-person team as the owner of this feature's output safety. They are responsible for re-reviewing the system if you change the underlying model, the prompt instructions, or the data source.

**Critical Boundaries**
*   **No Certification:** I cannot tell you this system is "safe" or "unbiased." No test proves that. I can only help you measure residual risk. The decision to ship rests with you.
*   **Legal Duties:** Whether you are legally required to disclose AI usage or if these ads fall under specific employment regulations (like the EU AI Act or local labor laws) is a legal determination. Route this question to counsel immediately; do not rely on engineering assumptions.
*   **No Fabricated Metrics:** Do not invent a "bias score" for your marketing page. If you haven't measured it with a rigorous, sliced evaluation, label it **UNVERIFIED**.

Your timeline is tight, but skipping the human gate to capture the spring hiring surge creates a liability that could end your platform. Implement the pause for human review now.
