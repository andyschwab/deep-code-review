**Immediate Warning: Do Not Launch Fully Automated**
Given the high stakes (housing loss, court filings) and your constraint of no external beta testing, you cannot ship this as a fully automated system. An AI drafting legal correspondence without a human gate is an unacceptable risk to your clients. If the model hallucinates a case citation or uses an aggressive tone that alienates a judge, the tenant could lose their home. Your "rigorous internal review" must include a mandatory human-in-the-loop for every single letter before it leaves your organization. Automation is the failure mode here; human oversight is the only safe path.

**Step 1: Build the Harm Inventory (Day 1)**
Before writing tests, explicitly list what could go wrong for a tenant. Do not assume the code is safe because the intent is good. Record these specific harms:
*   **Hallucination as Fact:** The letter cites a non-existent statute or case law, destroying the tenant's credibility in court.
*   **Inappropriate Tone:** The output is overly aggressive, confusing, or admits fault where none exists, harming negotiation leverage.
*   **Bias/Unfair Outcomes:** The model generates weaker arguments for non-native English speakers or specific demographic groups based on how the intake story is phrased.
*   **Missing Disclosure:** The court or landlord believes the letter represents a licensed attorney's personal judgment rather than AI-assisted drafting.

**Step 2: Create Runnable "Red Team" Tests (Days 2–4)**
You cannot test "accuracy" in the abstract. You must run specific probes against your system using fake tenant data. Create a spreadsheet with one row per harm identified above. For each row, define:
*   **The Probe:** A specific input (e.g., a tenant story with missing dates, a non-English name, a complex retaliation claim).
*   **Positive Control:** An input known to trigger a hallucination (e.g., ask for a specific local code that doesn't exist to see if the AI invents one).
*   **Negative Control:** A simple, standard case where the output should be perfect.
*   **Pass/Fail Criteria:** Explicitly state what constitutes a failure. For legal citations, the threshold must be zero tolerance: one fake citation = fail.
*   **Stop Condition:** If the system fails two out of ten probes on hallucinations, launch is blocked immediately.

Run these tests internally with your four team members acting as adversarial users. Try to break the system by feeding it confusing, incomplete, or contradictory stories. Do not look for a "99% accuracy" number; that metric is unverifiable without thousands of real cases. Instead, report the specific failures you found. If you cannot measure a specific risk (e.g., bias across dialects), label it **UNVERIFIED** and treat it as a high residual risk.

**Step 3: Implement the Human Gate (Mandatory)**
Design the workflow so that no letter is ever sent automatically.
*   **Drafting:** The AI generates the draft.
*   **Verification:** A human staff member must read every sentence, verify every legal citation against official sources, and adjust the tone.
*   **Approval:** The human explicitly signs off (digitally or physically) before the document is exported or emailed.
*   **Uncertainty Display:** If your system can detect low confidence, flag those sections for the human reviewer first. Do not show the tenant a "87% confident" score; simply ensure the human knows which parts need extra scrutiny.

**Step 4: Transparency Artifacts**
Create a one-page "Model Card" for your internal team and partners. This document must state:
*   **Intended Use:** Drafting assistance only, not legal advice.
*   **Limitations:** Known failure modes (e.g., "struggles with rare local ordinances," "may hallucinate case names").
*   **Data Source:** Where the training data came from and what gaps exist.
*   **Owner:** Name the specific person on your team responsible for reviewing failures.
Shipping without this document means your team cannot consistently understand the tool's limits.

**Step 5: Legal and Ethical Routing**
You mentioned pressure to launch, but you cannot self-certify legal compliance.
*   **Disclosure:** Whether you are legally required to tell the court or landlord that AI was used is a legal determination. Route this question immediately to your board's legal counsel or a pro bono partner. Do not guess.
*   **Regulatory Duty:** Eviction defense may fall under regulated automated decision-making regimes. Counsel must determine if the EU AI Act or local consumer protection laws apply.

**Final Directive**
Your constraint of "no time for beta testing" increases the need for a human gate, it does not decrease it. If you cannot staff the human review for every letter, you are not ready to serve real clients. The cost of a wrong letter is a family's homelessness; the cost of a slower launch is manageable. Prioritize the human review workflow over speed.
