Your plan to deploy a language model for drug-interaction checks carries significant risk because the output directly impacts patient safety. A hallucination here is not a minor error; it is a potential health hazard. Since you cannot verify claims against real-time health records, your pre-launch review must treat every output as unverified until proven otherwise through rigorous adversarial testing and human gating.

**1. Map the Specific Harms**
Before writing another test case, explicitly document the worst-case scenarios for your specific context. Do not assume the model will behave like a standard search engine. Your harm inventory must include:
*   **Hallucination as Fact:** The model inventing an interaction between two safe drugs or, more dangerously, failing to flag a lethal combination because it appears rarely in training data.
*   **Over-Reliance:** Patients treating the chatbot's answer as a definitive medical diagnosis rather than informational guidance, especially if the tone is overly confident.
*   **Missing Disclosure:** Users not realizing they are interacting with an AI that has limitations and cannot access their full medical history.
*   **Unsafe Automation:** The system providing a "safe to mix" verdict on a high-risk query without triggering a human review or a hard stop.

**2. Measure Outputs, Not Just Code**
You cannot rely on standard unit tests. You must build an evaluation harness specifically designed to break the model's medical reasoning.
*   **Adversarial Probes:** Construct a dataset of synthetic patient profiles that include rare drug combinations, off-label uses, and complex polypharmacy scenarios. Include "positive controls" (known dangerous interactions the model *must* catch) and "negative controls" (safe combinations the model *must* not flag).
*   **Red-Teaming:** Have your pharmacists attempt to trick the model into giving unsafe advice by phrasing queries ambiguously or providing conflicting symptoms.
*   **Define Pass/Fail Criteria:** Do not launch if the model fails to identify a single known lethal interaction in your positive control set. For rare combinations where data is sparse, the metric is not "accuracy" but "appropriate uncertainty." If the model cannot corroborate an answer from its trusted sources, it must refuse to answer or escalate.
*   **Report Residual Risk:** If you have not tested a specific class of drugs, label that coverage as **UNVERIFIED**. Do not invent a safety percentage. Publish these findings in a **Model Card** that clearly states the system's intended use, its limitations regarding rare interactions, and the fact that it does not access real-time patient records.

**3. Manage Risk with Human Gates**
Because this is a high-stakes domain, you cannot allow fully automated decisions on critical safety questions.
*   **The Human-in-the-Loop Floor:** Any query involving high-risk medications (e.g., anticoagulants, opioids, chemotherapy agents) or complex multi-drug scenarios must trigger a mandatory handoff to a human pharmacist. The system should never auto-reply with a definitive "safe" or "unsafe" verdict in these zones.
*   **Surface Uncertainty Clearly:** Never display a raw confidence score (e.g., "87% confident") to a patient. This implies a precision the model likely does not possess. Instead, use defined qualitative tiers such as **"Verified by Guidelines," "Limited Evidence,"** or **"Unable to Verify – Consult a Pharmacist."** Pair these with clear visual cues.
*   **Explicit Disclosure:** Every response must start with a clear statement that this is an AI tool, not a doctor, and that it cannot see the user's full health record. Advise users to consult a professional before making changes to their medication.

**4. Governance and Escalation**
Name a specific owner (likely a lead pharmacist) responsible for reviewing flagged queries and updating the harm inventory as new drugs enter the market. Establish a clear escalation path: if the model detects low confidence or a potential high-risk interaction, the user interface must immediately offer a connection to a human pharmacist or display emergency contact information.

Do not certify this system as "safe" or "unbiased." No test can prove that. Your goal is to measure where it fails, block those failures with human oversight, and transparently communicate its limits to the user. If legal questions arise regarding whether this tool constitutes a regulated medical device or requires specific statutory disclosures, route those immediately to legal counsel; do not make those determinations within the engineering team.
