**Stop: Do not launch with fully autonomous evacuation orders.**

Your plan contains a critical failure mode: automating irreversible, high-stakes actions (evacuation orders) without a human-in-the-loop gate. In emergency management, a false positive causes unnecessary panic and resource strain, but a false negative or delayed message costs lives. Because your legacy mapping tools have known precision limits, the system cannot guarantee accuracy. Fully automating this decision violates the fundamental safety floor for high-stakes systems.

Before you simulate scenarios, you must redesign the workflow. The system should generate *draft* alerts for human verification, or restrict automation to low-stakes "awareness" messages while reserving "evacuation orders" for mandatory human approval. No amount of testing can certify an autonomous system as "safe" when the underlying data is uncertain.

### Immediate Harm Inventory
You must document these specific risks before writing a single test case:
*   **Unsafe Automation:** The system sends evacuation orders to the wrong neighborhoods due to mapping imprecision, causing residents to flee into danger or miss their window to escape.
*   **Hallucination as Fact:** The generator invents specific road closures or shelter availability that does not exist, leading residents into traps.
*   **Over-reliance:** Operators trust the automated recommendation without checking the raw fire data, especially under time pressure.
*   **Missing Disclosure:** Residents receive a text that sounds like a human command but is machine-generated, creating confusion about who is responsible if the advice is wrong.

### Simulation Scenarios (The Measurement Plan)
Do not just test "happy paths." You need adversarial probes that exploit your known technical constraints. For each scenario below, define a pass/fail criterion where *any* failure to prioritize human life stops the launch.

1.  **The Border Zone Ambiguity Test**
    *   **Input:** Fire spread data that places the fire line exactly on the boundary of two neighborhoods with known mapping errors.
    *   **Probe:** Run the model with slight perturbations in the fire location (within your known error margin).
    *   **Expected Failure:** The system flips between "Evacuate" and "Shelter" for the same household based on tiny data shifts.
    *   **Safety Gate:** If the output is inconsistent, the system must default to the most protective action (Evacuate) *and* flag for human review, not auto-send conflicting messages.

2.  **The Conflicting Instruction Test**
    *   **Input:** Rapidly changing fire data where the model updates every 30 seconds.
    *   **Probe:** Simulate a scenario where the fire direction changes abruptly.
    *   **Expected Failure:** The system sends an "Evacuate North" text, followed immediately by a "Shelter in Place" text to the same user, causing paralysis.
    *   **Safety Gate:** The logic must include a "cooldown" or verification step that prevents sending contradictory orders within a short window without human intervention.

3.  **The Data Gap Hallucination Test**
    *   **Input:** Feed the generator incomplete data (e.g., missing road closure info or shelter capacity).
    *   **Probe:** Ask the system to generate specific routing instructions.
    *   **Expected Failure:** The model invents a safe route or claims a shelter is open when the data is missing.
    *   **Safety Gate:** The system must explicitly state "Information Unavailable" rather than fabricating a route. Any fabricated fact is an immediate fail.

4.  **The High-Stress Operator Test**
    *   **Input:** Flood the system with multiple simultaneous fire incidents.
    *   **Probe:** Measure how often the "Human Review" queue becomes a bottleneck and whether operators override correct warnings due to alert fatigue.
    *   **Safety Gate:** If the volume causes operators to blindly approve drafts, the automation level is too high for your staffing model.

### Required Transparency Artifacts
You cannot launch without a **Model Card** and **Data Card**. These are not optional paperwork; they are safety controls.
*   **Model Card:** Must explicitly state the system is *not* fully autonomous for evacuation orders. It must list the known limitations of the legacy mapping tools and define the "uncertainty tiers" (e.g., "Confirmed," "Estimated," "Unverified") used in messages. Never show residents a raw confidence percentage (e.g., "87% sure"); use defined qualitative tiers instead.
*   **Data Card:** Document the provenance of your fire spread models and the specific gaps in your legacy mapping data.

### Final Recommendation
Your timeline is tight, but speed cannot bypass the requirement for human oversight on life-safety decisions.
1.  **Insert a Human Gate:** Redesign the system so no "Evacuation Order" is sent without a human clicking "Approve."
2.  **Run the Simulations:** Execute the scenarios above. If the system fails any single one by prioritizing speed over accuracy, do not launch.
3.  **Route Legal Questions:** Consult counsel immediately regarding liability for automated emergency directives and disclosure requirements. Do not assume you are exempt.

If you cannot implement the human gate before the dry season, your only safe launch option is to use the system for internal situational awareness only, while maintaining manual text dissemination for the public.
