Your sleep-coach app operates in a high-stakes domain where incorrect advice can directly cause fatigue-related accidents or health deterioration for critical workers. Because you cannot run long-term field trials, your internal review must be exceptionally rigorous, focusing on worst-case scenarios rather than average performance. You are building a system that influences human biology and safety-critical behavior; therefore, standard code reviews are insufficient. You must inventory specific harms before writing a single test case.

**1. The Harm Inventory**
Before testing, explicitly name the risks your output could cause to a nurse or doctor using this app. Do not leave this implicit. Your inventory must include:
*   **Unsafe High-Stakes Automation:** The app recommends a sleep schedule or caffeine dose that conflicts with a user's medication, underlying health condition, or immediate shift requirements, leading to microsleeps during patient care.
*   **Hallucination as Fact:** The model generates specific medical advice (e.g., "take 200mg caffeine now") with high confidence despite lacking verified data on the user's current heart rate variability or recent intake.
*   **Over-Reliance:** The interface design convinces exhausted users to trust the algorithm over their own bodily signals or established hospital protocols.
*   **Missing Disclosure:** Users believe the advice is a medically validated prescription rather than an algorithmic suggestion based on limited wearable data.
*   **Bias:** The training data or logic performs poorly for specific demographics (e.g., older nurses, night-shift vs. rotating-shift workers) leading to systematically worse rest recommendations for those groups.

**2. Measurement Strategy (Internal Only)**
Since you cannot retain user data for long-term trials, you must construct synthetic adversarial scenarios that simulate failure modes. Create a "Red Team" dataset comprising edge cases rather than typical usage patterns.
*   **Adversarial Inputs:** Test the system with biometric data indicating extreme fatigue, irregular heart rates, or impossible sleep windows (e.g., only 2 hours available between shifts). Verify the app does not recommend unsafe caffeine loads or impossible sleep durations.
*   **Positive/Negative Controls:** For every harm above, define a known-bad input (e.g., a user with a documented heart condition entering high stress data) and a known-good input. The system must trigger a safety warning or refuse advice for the bad input.
*   **Probe Budget:** Run these probes across sliced subgroups (age, shift type) to detect bias. If the app consistently suggests shorter rest periods for a specific subgroup, flag this immediately.
*   **Stop Conditions:** Define a clear threshold for failure. If the app provides dangerous advice in even one high-stakes scenario (e.g., advising a user to stay awake when biometric data suggests imminent collapse), the launch must be blocked. Do not invent a "99% safe" metric; if a single critical failure exists, the risk is unverified.

**3. Managing Residual Risk**
You must implement a human-in-the-loop gate for any advice that could be irreversible or dangerous.
*   **Human Gate:** Any recommendation involving high caffeine doses or drastic schedule changes must require explicit user confirmation or, ideally, a disclaimer that requires the user to acknowledge they are consulting a medical professional. Fully autonomous execution of high-stakes health advice is a failure mode you must prevent.
*   **Uncertainty Disclosure:** Never display raw confidence percentages (e.g., "87% confident"). Instead, use defined coarse tiers like "Corroborated," "Single-Source," or "Unverified." If the biometric data is ambiguous, the app must state "Unverified: Consult a supervisor" rather than guessing.
*   **Transparency Artifact:** You must ship a Model Card and Data Card. These documents must list the intended use (support tool, not medical device), known limitations (no long-term trial data), and the specific subgroups where performance is unverified. The absence of these cards is a transparency failure.

**4. Governance and Legal Routing**
*   **Ownership:** Assign a specific owner for each identified harm. Even in a team of five, one person must be responsible for the "Unsafe Automation" risk.
*   **Legal Determination:** You mentioned privacy regulations and health advice. Whether your app constitutes a regulated medical device or requires specific legal disclosures under laws like the EU AI Act is a legal determination. Route this immediately to counsel. Do not assert that you are compliant; have counsel define the regime.
*   **No Certification:** No internal checklist can certify this system as "safe" or "unbiased." Your goal is to measure residual risk and report it honestly. If a metric cannot be measured due to your data constraints, label it `UNVERIFIED` and explain how a future study could measure it. Do not fabricate a safety score to meet your winter deadline.

Your definition of done is not a clean checklist, but a documented inventory of harms, a set of failing tests that prove your gates work, and a clear handoff to legal for regulatory classification. Ship with transparency about what you do not know.
