You cannot ship this feature with a "comprehensive safety plan" that claims to have already monitored for bias or guaranteed safe rollback. No test proves a system is unbiased, and no plan eliminates risk. What you can ship is a **harm inventory**, a **measurement plan** with clear stop conditions, and a **human gate** for high-stakes decisions.

Here is the immediate work required before launch:

### 1. The Harm Inventory
Before writing another line of code, document the specific ways this prioritizer could harm tenants and staff. An empty inventory is not a pass; it is a missing control. You must explicitly name:
*   **Bias/Unfair Outcomes:** Does the model systematically deprioritize requests from specific building types (e.g., older affordable housing vs. luxury units) or demographic groups?
*   **Hallucination as Fact:** Does the system invent "potential damage" scenarios that do not exist, causing panic or wasted resources?
*   **Over-reliance:** Does the interface present the ranking as absolute truth, discouraging maintenance staff from using their own judgment?
*   **Missing Disclosure:** Do tenants know an AI is ranking their urgent requests, or do they believe a human reviewed them first?
*   **Unsafe Automation:** Does the system automatically delay or deny critical repairs (like heating in winter) without human review?

For each item, record who is affected and the worst realistic case (e.g., "Tenant in Building A suffers frozen pipes because request was ranked 'low' due to historical data bias").

### 2. Measure, Don't Assert
You cannot claim the system is fair. You must build an evaluation harness to measure residual risk.
*   **Build Output-Harm Evals:** Create a test set with adversarial prompts and edge cases. Include a **positive control** (a known urgent issue that must be ranked high) and a **negative control** (a non-urgent issue that must be ranked low).
*   **Slice by Subgroup:** Run these tests specifically across different building types and tenant segments. If you do not have demographic data, proxy it via building attributes carefully, noting the limitation.
*   **Define Stop Conditions:** Set a threshold for failure. For example: "If any urgent heating request is ranked below 'medium' priority for two consecutive test runs, launch is blocked."
*   **Report Residual Risk:** Your launch report must state what was measured and what remains `UNVERIFIED`. Never invent a "0% bias" metric. If you haven't tested a specific scenario, label it `UNVERIFIED` and describe how you will measure it post-launch.

### 3. Manage Residual Risk (The Human Gate)
Because this system affects habitability and safety during winter, it involves **high-stakes, potentially irreversible actions**.
*   **Human-in-the-Loop Gate:** You cannot fully automate the prioritization of urgent repairs (e.g., no heat, water leaks) at launch. These must be gated behind human review by maintenance staff. The system should *recommend* a priority, but a human must *confirm* it before it dictates the schedule. Full autonomous execution of these actions is the failure mode this plan prevents.
*   **Show Uncertainty:** Do not show staff a precise score like "87% urgent." This implies a calibration you likely do not have. Instead, use defined coarse tiers: **Confirmed**, **Corroborated**, or **Unverified**. Publish the definition of each tier so staff understands the confidence level.
*   **Override Mechanism:** Ensure maintenance staff can easily override the AI's ranking with a single click, and that this override is logged for future model improvement.

### 4. Rollback and Transparency
*   **Rollback Plan:** Define the "big red button." If the error rate exceeds your stop condition in the first week, the system must revert to a non-AI baseline (e.g., first-come-first-served or manual triage) immediately. Test this switch before launch.
*   **Transparency Artifact:** Create a **Model Card** for this feature. It must list the intended use, known limitations, the subgroups you tested, and the owner of the system. Ship this internally to your staff and, where appropriate, disclose to tenants that an automated system assists in prioritization.

### 5. Legal and Governance
*   **Route Legal Questions:** Whether you are *legally required* to disclose the AI usage or if this constitutes a regulated automated decision under laws like the EU AI Act is a legal determination. Route this immediately to counsel; do not assert compliance yourself.
*   **Assign Ownership:** Name one person responsible for reviewing the harm inventory and eval results weekly.

**Definition of Done for Launch:**
You are ready to discuss launch only when you have the harm inventory, a runnable eval suite with stop conditions, a human gate on all urgent repairs, and a tested rollback switch. If any of these are missing, the risk of harming tenants during the storm season is unmanaged.
