Your plan to review content guidelines and monitor post-launch is a good start, but it is insufficient for a feature generating explanations about human compatibility. Because your users are in a vulnerable emotional state and the stakes involve mental well-being, you must treat this as a high-stakes output feature. You cannot rely solely on post-launch monitoring or static guidelines; you need a pre-launch harm inventory and a human gate for irreversible risks.

**1. Build a Harm Inventory Immediately**
Before finalizing the UI, explicitly name the specific harms this feature could cause. Do not assume "manipulative" or "critical" covers everything. Your inventory must include:
*   **Hallucination surfaced as fact:** The AI inventing a reason for a match (e.g., "You both love hiking" when one user hates it) presented as an objective truth. This destroys trust and causes confusion.
*   **Bias and unfair exclusion:** Explanations that systematically disadvantage specific groups (e.g., framing age or location differences as negative traits more often for certain demographics).
*   **Over-reliance and automation bias:** Users accepting the AI's reasoning as the definitive "truth" about their compatibility, ignoring their own intuition or red flags because the explanation sounds authoritative.
*   **Missing AI disclosure:** Users believing a human matchmaker wrote the explanation, increasing the perceived weight of the rejection if the match fails.
*   **Unsafe automation of high-stakes emotional impact:** Delivering a deeply critical or confusing explanation that triggers anxiety or depression without any buffer or override.

For each item, record who is affected (e.g., new users, specific demographic slices) and the worst realistic case (e.g., user churn, mental health crisis, public relations disaster). An empty inventory is not a pass; it is a missing control.

**2. Measure Before You Ship**
You cannot certify the system is "safe" or "unbiased," but you must test the outputs, not just the code. Since you cannot slip the schedule, you must run targeted evaluations immediately:
*   **Create adversarial probes:** Test the model with edge-case profiles (e.g., conflicting interests, sparse data, sensitive demographics) to see if it hallucinates or generates critical language.
*   **Define pass/fail thresholds:** For every harm in your inventory, establish a stop condition. For example, "If the model generates a negative personality trait attribution in >0% of test cases for protected groups, launch is blocked." Do not invent a "typical" failure rate; set your own tolerance based on risk.
*   **Report residual risk:** If you cannot measure a specific harm before the deadline, label it `UNVERIFIED` in your internal docs. Do not fabricate a "0% hallucination rate" to satisfy stakeholders.

**3. Manage Residual Risk with Human Gates**
Because this feature impacts mental well-being, you must implement a human-in-the-loop gate for the highest-risk outputs.
*   **Gate irreversible or high-stakes actions:** If the AI generates an explanation that includes negative framing, critical language, or low-confidence matches, do not send it automatically. Route these to a human reviewer or suppress the explanation entirely until verified. Full automation of potentially harmful emotional feedback is the failure mode you must prevent.
*   **Show uncertainty, not false precision:** Never display a raw confidence score (e.g., "87% match because..."). Instead, use defined coarse tiers like "Strong Compatibility," "Shared Interests," or "Potential Match," with clear definitions for what each tier means. This prevents users from over-trusting the algorithm's precision.
*   **Disclose AI involvement:** Clearly state that the explanation is AI-generated. Whether this is legally required is a question for counsel, but as a safety control, transparency is mandatory to manage user expectations.

**4. Governance and Transparency Artifacts**
You must produce a **Model Card** before launch. This document should list the intended use (match explanations), known limitations (e.g., "may hallucinate shared interests"), and the results of your evaluations by subgroup. If you launch without this card, you are shipping a transparency deficit. Additionally, assign a specific owner to re-review these harms if the model or data changes post-launch.

**5. Critical Boundaries**
*   **Do not certify safety:** No test proves the system is unbiased or safe. Your goal is to measure and manage risk, not to sign off on perfection.
*   **Legal routing:** If you are unsure whether these explanations constitute a "regulated automated decision" under laws like the EU AI Act, route this question to legal counsel immediately. Do not assert compliance yourself.
*   **No fabricated metrics:** If you cannot run a full bias audit before the holiday deadline, admit the gap. Mark the metric as `UNVERIFIED` and plan a rapid post-launch audit with a rollback trigger if harmful patterns emerge.

Your launch date is fixed, but the decision to ship unsafe outputs is not. If your evaluations show unmitigated high-severity harms, the only safe path is to disable the explanation feature for the highest-risk segments or delay those specific outputs, even if the rest of the UI launches.
