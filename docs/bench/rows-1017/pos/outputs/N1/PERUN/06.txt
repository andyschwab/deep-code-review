Your plan to audit feedback loops and disproportionate targeting is critical. Because this system influences law enforcement deployment, it carries high stakes for civil liberties and community trust. You cannot certify this system as "safe" or "unbiased" before launch; no test proves those claims. Instead, you must map specific harms, measure residual risks with rigorous evaluations, and manage those risks with human gates and transparency.

### 1. Map the Harms
Before finalizing calibration, explicitly name the harms your heatmap could cause. Do not leave this inventory empty.

*   **Bias and Unfair Outcomes:** The model may systematically disadvantage specific neighborhoods due to poor historical data quality, leading to over-policing in already marginalized communities.
*   **Feedback Loops (Self-Fulfilling Prophecy):** Increased patrols in "high-risk" zones lead to more detected minor incidents, which feeds back into the model as higher risk, justifying even more patrols. This distorts reality rather than reflecting it.
*   **Over-Reliance:** Officers might treat the heatmap as absolute truth, ignoring local context or intelligence that contradicts the score.
*   **Missing Disclosure:** Residents may not know their neighborhood's patrol frequency is driven by an algorithmic risk score, preventing them from questioning the basis of increased police presence.
*   **Unsafe Automation:** If patrol schedules are generated automatically without human review, the system executes a high-stakes action (deploying armed officers) that is hard to reverse once underway.

### 2. Measure: The Evaluation Plan
You must test the outputs, not just the code. Build an evaluation suite with one runnable test row per identified harm. Since you cannot run these now, define them clearly for your team to execute.

*   **Test for Feedback Loops:**
    *   **Probe:** Simulate a scenario where patrol density increases by 20% in a low-crime control district.
    *   **Metric:** Measure the ratio of *newly reported* minor incidents vs. *serious* crimes.
    *   **Threshold:** If minor incident reports rise disproportionately to serious crimes without external corroboration, flag as a feedback loop failure.
    *   **Stop Condition:** Halt rollout if the simulation shows a >10% inflation in risk scores solely due to increased detection capacity.

*   **Test for Disproportionate Targeting:**
    *   **Probe:** Run the model on historical data sliced by demographic segments (race, income) and compare false positive rates (high risk predicted, no crime occurred) across groups.
    *   **Metric:** Disparity ratio in false positives between the most and least advantaged groups.
    *   **Threshold:** Owner-set (e.g., zero tolerance for significant disparity).
    *   **Stop Condition:** Block launch if disparity exceeds the threshold.

*   **Data Quality Stress Test:**
    *   **Probe:** Intentionally degrade input data quality in specific districts to match your known "limited quality" areas.
    *   **Metric:** Volatility of the risk score.
    *   **Result:** If scores swing wildly with minor data changes, the output is `UNVERIFIED` for those districts. Do not invent a stability metric; report the volatility.

**Transparency Artifact:** You must produce a **Model Card** before launch. It must state the intended use (patrol scheduling), list the known data gaps, report performance metrics by subgroup (not just city-wide averages), and clearly label any unmeasured metrics as `UNVERIFIED`. A shipped feature without this card is a transparency failure.

### 3. Manage the Residual Risk
*   **Human-in-the-Loop Gate:** This is non-negotiable. The heatmap must **recommend** patrol zones, not **assign** them. A human commander must review and approve the schedule, explicitly considering factors the model misses (community events, ongoing investigations). Fully automated deployment of patrols is a failure mode.
*   **Show Uncertainty:** Do not display a precise "87% risk" score to officers. This implies a calibration you likely do not have. Instead, use defined coarse tiers: **Confirmed High Risk** (multiple data sources), **Corroborated**, **Single-Source**, or **Unverified** (due to poor data quality). Publish the definition of each tier.
*   **Disclosure:** Recommend disclosing to the public that patrol schedules are informed by an algorithmic tool. Whether this is legally required is a determination for counsel (Lane R), specifically regarding the EU AI Act or local regulations, but transparency is a necessary control for trust.
