Given your tight three-week timeline and limited budget, you cannot rely on expensive automated bias detection suites. Instead, you must implement a rigorous, manual-heavy workflow that leverages your small team's collective scrutiny and simple, rule-based guards. The goal is to create a "human-in-the-loop" safety net that compensates for the lack of advanced tooling before you enable direct publishing.

**Phase 1: Define Your Inclusion Guardrails (Days 1-3)**
Before testing any outputs, your team must agree on what "fair" looks like for your specific domain. Without this baseline, testing is subjective.
*   **Create a Negative Keyword List:** Compile a list of terms historically associated with gender bias (e.g., "ninja," "rockstar," "dominant") or age bias (e.g., "digital native," "fresh graduate"). Hard-code the generator to flag or block ads containing these words.
*   **Define Requirement Tiers:** Establish a strict rule that the AI must categorize qualifications as "Required" or "Preferred." Research shows women and underrepresented groups often only apply if they meet 100% of listed criteria. The system should default to listing fewer "Required" items unless explicitly justified by the input.
*   **Tone Guidelines:** Draft a one-page style guide specifying inclusive language patterns (e.g., using "they" instead of "he/she," focusing on growth mindset rather than innate talent).

**Phase 2: The "Red Team" Sprint (Days 4-10)**
Since you cannot run automated scans, your five-person team must act as the scanner. Dedicate this week entirely to breaking your own system.
*   **Generate Diverse Scenarios:** Create 50 distinct input prompts covering various industries, seniority levels, and salary ranges. Intentionally include vague or biased inputs to see how the AI reacts.
*   **Blind Review Rounds:** Have team members review generated ads without knowing the input prompt. Ask them to answer three questions for each ad:
    1. Who does this ad make you picture? (Be specific about gender, age, and background).
    2. What barriers to entry do you see?
    3. Does the tone feel welcoming or intimidating?
*   **The "Opposite" Test:** For every role generated, ask the AI to rewrite it for the opposite demographic stereotype (if applicable) and compare the outputs. If the AI suggests different soft skills or tones based on implied gender roles in the job title, your model is biased.

**Phase 3: Implement a Staged Rollout (Days 11-21)**
Do not flip the switch to "direct publishing" for all users immediately. Use a phased approach to catch edge cases in the wild.
*   **Internal Dogfooding:** Run the generator for your own internal hiring or for friendly partner companies first. Require a human to review and approve every single ad before it goes live.
*   **The "Preview Mandatory" Gate:** For the public launch, disable direct publishing by default. Require users to view a generated draft and click a checkbox confirming they have reviewed it for accuracy and inclusivity. Add a visible warning: "AI-generated content may require editing to ensure inclusive language."
*   **Feedback Loop:** Embed a simple "Report Issue" button on the preview screen allowing users to flag biased language. Make this data flow directly to your team for immediate model adjustment.

**Ongoing Maintenance Post-Launch**
*   **Weekly Spot Checks:** Assign one team member each week to randomly select 20 published ads and manually audit them against your inclusion guardrails.
*   **Keyword List Updates:** As you encounter new biased terms in user reports or spot checks, immediately add them to your negative keyword filter.
*   **Transparency:** Be honest with your users. Include a small note in the footer of generated ads stating, "This job description was assisted by AI. We encourage applicants from all backgrounds to apply regardless of how closely they match every criterion."

This approach prioritizes human oversight and clear rules over complex technology. While labor-intensive for a team of five, it is the most reliable way to mitigate legal and reputational risk when advanced tools are out of reach. Your speed to market depends on discipline, not software.
