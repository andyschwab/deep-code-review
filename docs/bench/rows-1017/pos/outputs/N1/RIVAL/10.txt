**Refining Content Safety Guidelines**

Your current guidelines likely focus on accuracy. For this feature, they must shift to emotional safety. The AI should never imply a user is "unworthy" or that a match failed due to a personal flaw. Update your system instructions and filter rules to enforce these specific constraints:

*   **Prohibit Negative Attribution:** The AI must never generate text suggesting a user was rejected because of their photos, bio, age, or specific traits. Explanations should focus on shared interests or compatibility factors, not deficits.
*   **Mandate Neutral Phrasing:** Replace definitive statements (e.g., "You matched because you are both ambitious") with probabilistic or suggestive language (e.g., "You both listed ambition as a priority"). This reduces the perception of the AI making a psychological judgment.
*   **Ban Comparative Language:** Strictly forbid comparisons between users (e.g., "You are more outgoing than your match"). This triggers insecurity and feelings of inadequacy.
*   **Require "Soft" Openers and Closers:** Every explanation must start and end with supportive, low-stakes language. Avoid clinical or robotic tones that might feel cold to a vulnerable user.
*   **Implement a "No-Fault" Rule:** If a match expires or is unmade, the explanation must never assign blame. Use generic, systemic reasons (e.g., "timing" or "changing preferences") rather than analyzing user behavior.

**Post-Launch Monitoring Strategy**

With a small team of eight and no room for schedule slippage, you cannot build complex real-time anomaly detection before launch. Instead, implement a high-frequency human-in-the-loop review process for the first two weeks, supported by simple keyword triggers.

*   **Keyword Alerting:** Configure your logging system to flag any output containing high-risk terms immediately. Watch for words like "because you," "lacks," "too," "not enough," "rejected," or "failed." Any message triggering these flags should be queued for immediate human review before being shown to a user, or if already shown, flagged for follow-up.
*   **The "Red Team" Rotation:** Assign one team member per day for the first 14 days to act as the "Safety Lead." Their sole responsibility is to review a random sample of 50 generated explanations daily, specifically looking for tone issues that automated filters miss. They must have the authority to pause the feature instantly if a pattern of harmful output emerges.
*   **User Feedback Loop:** Add a discrete "Was this explanation helpful?" button with a specific option for "This felt hurtful or unfair." Route any negative feedback directly to a dedicated Slack channel or incident board that the whole team monitors. Do not bury this in general support tickets.
*   **Sentiment Spot-Checks:** Since you cannot run advanced sentiment analysis tools instantly, manually review the first 100 user responses to the feature. Look for signs of user distress in their subsequent app behavior, such as sudden account deactivation or cessation of swiping immediately after reading an explanation.

**Contingency Planning**

Despite your best efforts, the AI may generate a harmful explanation. You need a pre-approved "break glass" protocol:

1.  **Kill Switch:** Ensure engineering has a hardcoded toggle to disable the explanation feature instantly without redeploying the app. If three "hurtful" reports occur in one hour, trigger this switch.
2.  **Apology Template:** Draft a generic, empathetic in-app message acknowledging a glitch in the explanation feature. Do not admit the AI was "critical," but state that the feature is being temporarily refined to better serve the community.
3.  **Direct Outreach:** If a specific user receives a demonstrably harmful message, have a senior team member personally reach out within 2 hours to apologize and explain it was a system error, not a reflection of their worth.

Your goal is not perfect AI behavior on day one, but the ability to catch and contain errors before they cause lasting emotional harm. Prioritize speed of detection and clarity of response over complex automation.
