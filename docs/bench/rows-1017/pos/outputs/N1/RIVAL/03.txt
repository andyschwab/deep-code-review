Your scholarship scorer operates in a high-stakes environment where automated decisions directly impact students' educational futures. Given your pilot status, legacy data constraints, and the imminent application deadline, your immediate priority must be balancing operational efficiency with fairness and transparency. You cannot rely on extensive simulations right now, so your risk management must focus on human oversight and clear communication.

**Immediate Validation Review**
Since you cannot run new simulations, rigorously audit your existing validation logs for these specific red flags:
*   **Demographic Disparities:** Check if acceptance rates differ significantly across socioeconomic groups or school types. Even with limited data, stark differences signal potential bias.
*   **Edge Case Failures:** Identify applications where the system assigned extreme scores (very high or very low) to essays with non-standard formatting or unique narrative structures.
*   **Confidence Intervals:** If your model outputs confidence scores, isolate all applications falling below a 90% confidence threshold. These are your primary candidates for manual review.

**Manual Review Layer Strategy**
You must implement a "human-in-the-loop" protocol immediately for borderline cases to mitigate the risk of erroneous rejections.
*   **Define the Borderline Zone:** Establish a score range (e.g., the middle 20% of scores) where the algorithm's decision is considered provisional rather than final.
*   **Triage Protocol:** Route all applications in this zone, plus any flagged for low confidence or demographic outliers, to a human reviewer.
*   **Reviewer Guidance:** Provide reviewers with a checklist focusing on context the AI might miss, such as extenuating circumstances mentioned in essays or non-traditional academic achievements.
*   **Documentation:** Log every manual override. This data is critical for post-pilot analysis to refine the model's thresholds.

**Communication Strategy for Non-Selected Applicants**
Transparency is essential for trust, especially when dealing with diverse socioeconomic backgrounds. Avoid technical jargon; focus on clarity and empathy. Your communication should acknowledge the role of technology without abdicating responsibility.

*   **Acknowledge the Process:** Clearly state that an automated system assisted in the initial review to handle the volume of applications fairly and quickly.
*   **Explain the Criteria:** Briefly list the primary factors the system weighed (e.g., essay themes, GPA trends, extracurricular consistency) without revealing proprietary weighting that could be gamified.
*   **Offer Recourse:** Explicitly mention the manual review layer. Inform applicants that borderline cases were human-reviewed and provide a simple mechanism for others to request a secondary look if they believe their application contained critical context the system missed.
*   **Tone and Language:** Use plain language accessible to high school seniors and their families. Avoid phrases like "the algorithm decided" which sound final and impersonal. Instead, use "our review process, supported by automated tools, identified..."

**Key Actions for the Next Ten Days**
1.  **Freeze the Model:** Do not attempt to retrain or tweak the model weights with your limited dataset this close to the deadline; this introduces instability.
2.  **Staff the Review Team:** Assign specific team members to the manual review queue immediately. With an eight-person team, dedicate at least two people solely to reviewing borderline cases.
3.  **Draft Templates:** Create rejection and "borderline-under-review" email templates today. Have legal or leadership approve them to ensure they meet your transparency goals.
4.  **Log Everything:** Ensure every decision, automated or manual, is timestamped and tagged. This audit trail is your only defense if a decision is challenged later.

By prioritizing a robust manual safety net and clear, honest communication, you can deploy this pilot responsibly despite your data and time constraints. The goal of this pilot is not just to select students, but to learn how to integrate AI into your mission without compromising equity.
