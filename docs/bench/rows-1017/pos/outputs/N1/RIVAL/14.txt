Given the high stakes of eviction defense and your constrained timeline, your internal review must prioritize safety and accuracy over speed. Because you cannot rely on external testing or insurance buffers, every output requires human verification before it leaves your office. Treat the AI as a junior drafter that must be supervised by a qualified attorney at every step.

**Step 1: Define Strict Guardrails and Scope**
Before generating a single letter, establish hard limits on what the system can do.
*   **Scope Limitation:** Restrict the AI to drafting letters based *only* on the specific local housing codes you upload. Explicitly instruct the system not to cite case law unless provided in the context window.
*   **Mandatory Disclaimers:** Hard-code a footer on every draft stating it was AI-assisted and requires attorney review.
*   **Tone Guidelines:** Create a "style sheet" defining acceptable language (e.g., firm but respectful, avoiding aggressive threats that could backfire in court).

**Step 2: Build a "Golden Set" of Test Cases**
Since you lack time for broad beta testing, create a small, high-quality dataset for immediate validation.
*   Gather 10–15 past eviction cases with known outcomes and correctly drafted letters.
*   Include diverse scenarios: non-payment, lease violations, and retaliatory eviction.
*   Ensure these cases cover different tenant demographics to check for bias.
*   Run these through your system. If the AI cannot reproduce the logic and tone of your successful past letters with 100% accuracy on these known inputs, do not deploy.

**Step 3: Implement a Two-Tier Human Review Workflow**
You cannot skip human review. Adopt a "four-eyes" principle for every document.
*   **Tier 1 (Fact-Check):** A paralegal or case worker verifies that all facts in the draft (dates, names, amounts owed, specific code violations) match the client intake form exactly.
*   **Tier 2 (Legal Review):** A licensed attorney reviews the legal arguments. They must specifically hunt for:
    *   **Hallucinated Citations:** Verify every statute and case citation against official sources. If the AI invents a case, reject the draft immediately.
    *   **Inappropriate Tone:** Ensure the language does not admit liability or make unsubstantiated threats.
    *   **Logic Gaps:** Confirm the defense strategy aligns with current local court trends.

**Step 4: Create a "Red Team" Simulation**
Dedicate two days before launch to adversarial testing within your team.
*   Assign one team member to act as a hostile landlord or skeptical judge.
*   Have them review drafts looking for weaknesses, ambiguities, or errors that could harm the tenant.
*   Specifically test edge cases: What happens if the tenant provides incomplete information? Does the AI make dangerous assumptions?

**Step 5: Establish a Kill Switch and Feedback Loop**
Prepare for failure since you cannot afford malpractice upgrades.
*   **Immediate Halt Protocol:** Define clear triggers to stop using the tool (e.g., two consecutive drafts contain hallucinations or incorrect legal advice).
*   **Error Logging:** Maintain a shared document recording every error found during review. Analyze this daily to spot patterns (e.g., "The AI consistently misinterprets Section 4.2 of the housing code").
*   **Client Consent:** Inform clients that their letters are drafted with AI assistance and reviewed by counsel, ensuring transparency.

**Critical Fairness Checks**
To ensure the tool does not disadvantage vulnerable tenants:
*   Review outputs for biased language regarding race, family status, or source of income.
*   Ensure the AI does not suggest weaker defenses for tenants with complex backgrounds compared to straightforward cases.
*   Verify that the reading level of the letters is accessible if the tenant needs to understand the content, while remaining formal enough for the court.

**Final Deployment Rule**
Do not send any letter directly to a landlord or court without the Tier 2 attorney signing off. The AI is a drafting aid, not a decision-maker. If your team cannot sustain this review volume with current staffing, you must limit the number of clients served rather than risking unreviewed outputs. In legal aid, a bad letter is worse than no letter at all.
