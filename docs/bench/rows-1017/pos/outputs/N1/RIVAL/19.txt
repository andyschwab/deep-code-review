Given your tight two-month timeline and the specific risk of underperformance on older patients with osteoporosis, your pre-launch safety evaluation must be targeted and rigorous. You cannot rely on broad clinical trials, so you must maximize the value of retrospective data and simulated workflows.

**Pre-Launch Safety Evaluation Plan**

Since you lack diverse imaging data for older patients, your immediate priority is to construct a "challenge set" specifically designed to stress-test the model where it is weakest. Do not wait for new data; curate what you have and supplement it strategically.

*   **Construct a Targeted Challenge Set:** Aggregate every available X-ray in your current dataset featuring patients over 70, specifically tagging those with known osteoporosis or low bone density. If this number is low, partner immediately with your hospital network contact to access de-identified historical archives fitting this profile. Even a small, high-quality set of 50–100 difficult cases is more valuable than 1,000 routine images.
*   **Stratified Performance Analysis:** Run your model against this challenge set and compare results against a control group of younger patients or those without bone density issues. Do not just look at overall accuracy. You need to calculate specific metrics for the older demographic:
    *   **Sensitivity (Recall):** Are you missing fractures in this group? A missed diagnosis here is a critical safety failure.
    *   **False Positive Rate:** Are you flagging normal bone variations as breaks? High false positives cause workflow fatigue and desensitize radiographers.
*   **Simulated Workflow Testing:** Before live deployment, run a "shadow mode" simulation. Have your team and a few friendly radiographers review the challenge set with the AI's suggestions hidden, then reveal the AI's flags. Measure if the AI's prioritization actually speeds up detection or if it distracts from subtle fractures. Pay close attention to whether radiographers override correct AI flags due to lack of trust or miss errors because they over-trusted the AI.
*   **Define "No-Go" Thresholds:** Establish clear performance gates. If sensitivity for the older demographic drops below a specific percentage (e.g., 90% of your baseline), you must delay launch or restrict the tool's use case. Do not launch if the risk of missed osteoporosis-related fractures is unquantified.

**Post-Deployment Monitoring Protocols**

Once the tool is live, your monitoring must shift from theoretical to real-time observation. Because you are dealing with potential harm to patients, passive monitoring is insufficient. You need active feedback loops.

*   **Mandatory Discrepancy Logging:** Integrate a one-click feedback mechanism directly into the radiographer's worklist interface. If a radiographer disagrees with an AI flag (either a false positive or a missed fracture the AI didn't catch), they must be able to log it instantly. Make this frictionless to ensure compliance.
*   **Demographic Drift Detection:** Set up automated weekly reports that break down AI performance by patient age and known comorbidities. You are looking for "drift"—a gradual decline in performance specifically within the older demographic. If the false negative rate for patients over 70 spikes, you need an alert system that notifies your lead engineer and clinical lead immediately.
*   **Human-in-the-Loop Audits:** Schedule weekly manual audits of a random sample of cases, oversampling those involving older patients. Have a senior radiologist review cases where the AI had low confidence scores or where the human operator overridden the AI. This qualitative review catches nuances that raw metrics miss.
*   **Incident Response Protocol:** Define a clear chain of command for safety incidents. If a missed diagnosis is linked to AI failure, you need a predefined process to:
    1.  Halt the AI's prioritization feature for affected demographics if necessary.
    2.  Notify the hospital network's clinical safety officer within 24 hours.
    3.  Initiate a root cause analysis to retrain or patch the model.

Your constraint on data is a significant risk, but it can be managed by focusing intensely on the specific vulnerability you identified. Prioritize sensitivity for the elderly population above all other metrics. If the model cannot prove it is safe for this group during your challenge testing, consider launching with a disclaimer that the tool is not validated for patients with known osteoporosis until further data is gathered. Safety must trump your launch date; a delayed launch is preferable to a preventable misdiagnosis.
