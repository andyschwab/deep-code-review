Given the high stakes of binding financial decisions, the six-week deadline, and the lack of realistic stress data, your pre-launch review must prioritize safety and fairness over speed. Deploying an autonomous system without human oversight that lacks testing for economic downturns poses a significant risk of erratic behavior and regulatory violation. The following plan outlines a rigorous validation process to address bias and stability before any real accounts are exposed.

**1. Immediate Data Synthesis and Stress Scenario Construction**
Since your current environment lacks data for economic downturns, you must synthesize it immediately. Real-world testing during an actual downturn is not an option; you must simulate one.
*   **Create Synthetic Stress Profiles:** Generate synthetic customer profiles representing various demographic groups (age, income, geography, ethnicity proxies) and apply historical downturn parameters (e.g., 2008 recession, 2020 pandemic shock) to their spending patterns.
*   **Define "Erratic" Thresholds:** Explicitly define what constitutes an unacceptable limit change. For example, a credit limit reduction of more than 20% within 24 hours or a fluctuation of ±15% week-over-week without a corresponding change in user behavior should be flagged as erratic.
*   **Adversarial Data Injection:** Introduce noise and edge cases into the test data, such as sudden spikes in spending due to fraud versus legitimate emergency purchases, to ensure the model distinguishes between risk signals correctly.

**2. Bias Auditing and Demographic Parity Testing**
To identify potential biases against specific demographic groups, you must isolate the model's decision logic from protected characteristics.
*   **Disaggregated Performance Analysis:** Run the model against your synthetic stress profiles and segment the results by demographic group. Compare the average credit limit changes, the frequency of reductions, and the volatility of decisions across these groups.
*   **Threshold Sensitivity Testing:** Vary the decision thresholds slightly to see if specific groups disproportionately cross into "limit reduction" zones. If a small adjustment in the model's confidence score causes a massive disparity in outcomes for a specific demographic, the threshold is likely unstable or biased.
*   **Proxy Variable Review:** Analyze the "external economic indicators" and "spending patterns" used as inputs. Ensure these do not act as proxies for protected classes (e.g., zip code correlating with race, or merchant categories correlating with gender).

**3. Stability Validation and Decision Threshold Calibration**
Before validating accuracy, you must validate stability. An unstable model that oscillates limits erodes customer trust instantly.
*   **Hysteresis Implementation:** Implement and test hysteresis loops (dead bands) in your decision logic. Ensure that once a limit is changed, the opposing economic indicators must move significantly further to trigger a reversal. This prevents "flipping" where a customer's limit goes up and down repeatedly due to minor data fluctuations.
*   **Rate-of-Change Limits:** Hard-code absolute caps on how much a limit can change in a single event or over a rolling 7-day period, regardless of what the AI suggests. The AI should recommend; the hard code should enforce the safety guardrails.
*   **Shadow Mode Deployment:** Do not activate the system for real users yet. Run the model in "shadow mode" against live transaction streams for two weeks. The model will make decisions, but these decisions will not be executed. Compare the shadow decisions against your defined "erratic" thresholds and bias metrics.

**4. Governance and Go/No-Go Criteria**
Given the lack of human oversight, your governance gates must be stricter than usual.
*   **Establish a "Kill Switch":** Ensure there is an immediate, manual override capable of freezing all automated limit changes across the entire portfolio if the system begins behaving erratically in shadow mode or post-launch.
*   **Define Failure Conditions:** Set clear metrics for failure. If the synthetic stress test shows a bias disparity greater than 5% between groups, or if shadow mode reveals more than 1% of accounts experiencing erratic fluctuations, the launch must be delayed.
*   **Documentation for Regulators:** Document every test case, synthetic data parameter, and bias audit result. Since this system makes binding decisions, you will need to demonstrate to auditors exactly how you validated fairness and stability.

**Recommendation on Timeline**
A six-week timeline is extremely aggressive for a system of this risk profile, especially given the current gaps in stress testing. If the shadow mode deployment (Step 3) reveals any instability or bias, you must be prepared to delay the holiday launch. Releasing a biased or erratic credit limit system could cause immediate reputational damage, customer harm, and regulatory enforcement actions that far outweigh the benefits of a holiday season deployment. Prioritize a stable, fair launch over a timely one.
