Given your constraints—specifically the lack of historical data and the high stakes of academic grading—your pre-launch safety checklist must prioritize human oversight and rigorous limitation management over automated confidence. Because you are relying on synthetic data, the system's ability to generalize to real student arguments is unproven, making the "human-in-the-loop" requirement your primary safety mechanism rather than a secondary feature.

**Bias Mitigation in Language Interpretation**
Without diverse historical data, your model is highly susceptible to learning biases present in your synthetic examples or the underlying foundation model. You must address how the system interprets non-standard English, cultural idioms, or emotional distress signals which often appear in appeal letters.

*   **Adversarial Testing with Dialects:** Manually construct test cases using various dialects, non-native English phrasing, and informal tones to ensure the system does not penalize students for linguistic differences rather than argument merit.
*   **Sentiment vs. Substance Check:** Verify that the model evaluates the logical strength of an appeal rather than the politeness or aggression of the tone. A rude but valid appeal should not be downgraded; a polite but baseless one should not be upgraded.
*   **Synthetic Data Audit:** Review your synthetic dataset for homogeneity. If all synthetic "successful" appeals follow a specific structure or vocabulary, the model will bias against students who write differently. Diversify these examples manually before final testing.
*   **Contextual Blind Spots:** Ensure the system cannot access or infer protected characteristics (race, gender, disability status) from the text. If the model attempts to guess these traits, it must be flagged immediately as a privacy and bias violation.

**Transparency in Decision Suggestions**
Students and faculty must understand *why* a specific adjustment was suggested. Since you cannot provide perfect accuracy guarantees due to data limitations, explainability is your currency of trust.

*   **Evidence Highlighting:** The interface must highlight the specific phrases in the student's argument and the faculty's comments that triggered the suggestion. Do not present a score without showing the textual evidence.
*   **Confidence Scoring:** Every suggestion must include a visible confidence metric. Given your reliance on synthetic data, any suggestion with low confidence should automatically default to a "requires full human review" status without displaying a specific outcome recommendation to the user.
*   **Limitation Disclosure:** Clearly state on the dashboard that this is an assistive tool trained on limited examples. Avoid language that implies the AI is "deciding" the grade; use phrasing like "preliminary analysis for reviewer consideration."
*   **Logic Trace:** Provide a brief, plain-language summary of the logic used (e.g., "Suggested adjustment based on identified discrepancy between rubric criteria and submitted evidence") rather than technical model weights.

**Mandatory Human-in-the-Loop (HITL) Protocols**
You specified mandatory human review for failing grades and disciplinary actions. This protocol must be architecturally enforced, not just policy-based.

*   **Hard Stop Triggers:** Configure the system to physically prevent the submission of any decision involving a failing grade (F, NF, etc.) or disciplinary notation. The workflow must route these exclusively to the Academic Review Board with the AI output marked as "Draft Input Only."
*   **Dual-Review for Edge Cases:** Implement a rule where any appeal where the AI confidence score is below a defined threshold (e.g., 70%) requires review by two independent faculty members, regardless of the grade involved.
*   **Override Documentation:** Create a mandatory field for human reviewers to justify why they accepted or rejected the AI's suggestion. This creates an audit trail and helps you collect the real-world data you currently lack for future training.
*   **Deactivation Switch:** Establish a clear procedure for the review board to pause the AI system immediately if they detect a pattern of erroneous suggestions during the initial rollout.

**Final Validation Steps**
Before the semester ends, conduct a "red team" exercise where your six team members attempt to fool the system using edge cases derived from real (anonymized) past appeals if available, or highly creative synthetic ones. If the system cannot consistently identify these tricks, delay the launch. Remember, in the absence of robust data, the safest deployment is one that assumes the AI is often wrong and requires constant human verification.
