**The Call**
Do not bundle the hardware and subscription into a single, unallocated line item on the invoice if your goal is to avoid taxing the service portion; in Texas, commingling tangible personal property (the mirror) with a taxable data processing or information service without a reasonable allocation likely subjects the **entire** transaction price to the 6.25% state sales tax (plus local rates). To preserve your current margin structure, you must explicitly allocate the $1,680 bundle price between the hardware and the subscription on the customer's invoice using a reasonable method (such as relative fair market value), ensuring the service portion is identified separately.

**The Numbers**
*Formula:* `Tax Liability = (Taxable Amount) × (Combined Tax Rate)`
*Assumptions:*
- **Combined Tax Rate:** 8.25% (Austin base 6.25% + local caps, marked **ASSUMED**; verify exact zip code rate).
- **Service Taxability:** Texas taxes "data processing services" and information services. If your streaming is classified as such, it is taxable. If it were purely a non-taxable service, allocation would save you 100% of tax on that portion. We assume the worst-case (taxable service) to test the bundling risk.
- **Scenario A (Current/Separate):** Hardware taxed; Service taxed separately (or exempt if ruled non-taxable, but we assume taxable for comparison).
- **Scenario B (Bundled & Unallocated):** Entire $1,680 taxed.
- **Scenario C (Bundled & Allocated):** Hardware portion taxed; Service portion taxed (if taxable) or exempt (if exempt).

*Computed Figures (per unit, first year):*

2.  **Allocated Bundle (Fair Market Value Method):**
    - Hardware FMV: $1,200.
    - Service FMV: $480 ($40 × 12).
    - Total FMV: $1,680.
    - Allocation is 100% to respective parts in this specific math case since sum equals price.
    - If the service is deemed taxable in TX: Tax is still $138.60.
    - *Critical Nuance:* If the service qualifies for any exemption or lower rate (rare for digital content in TX, but possible if structured as distinct from "data processing"), allocation is the **only** way to claim it. If the Comptroller views the bundle as a "true object" sale of the mirror with incidental service, the whole thing is taxed anyway. However, if they view it as a service with incidental hardware, the whole thing is taxed.
    - **The Real Risk:** Without allocation, the Comptroller defaults to taxing the **entire** amount. With allocation, you at least have the argument to tax only the hardware if you can successfully argue the service is non-taxable (e.g., if it's considered a non-taxable electronic transmission rather than data processing).

**What Flips It, and the Trigger**
The determining factor is the **invoice presentation**. If the invoice shows a single line item "Smart Mirror System - $1,680," the Texas Comptroller will almost certainly tax the full amount. If the invoice shows "Mirror: $1,200; 1-Year Subscription: $480," you maintain the ability to argue the tax treatment of each component individually.
