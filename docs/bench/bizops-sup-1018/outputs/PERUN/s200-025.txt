**The Call**
Recognize the $120 hardware revenue immediately upon delivery and the $35 subscription revenue ratably over the service period; the 20% bundle discount requires you to allocate the reduced total price between the two components based on their relative standalone selling prices, not simply defer everything or recognize it all at once.

**The Numbers**
*Revenue Recognition Logic (ASC 606 Framework):*
1.  **Standalone Prices:** Hardware ($120) + Subscription Month 1 ($35) = $155 Total Value.
2.  **Bundle Price:** $155 × 0.80 (20% discount) = $124 Actual Charge.
3.  **Allocation Ratio:**
    *   Hardware Share: $120 / $155 = 77.4%
    *   Subscription Share: $35 / $155 = 22.6%
4.  **Recognized Revenue per Bundled Unit:**
    *   Hardware (Immediate): $124 × 77.4% = **$96.00**
    *   Subscription (Deferred): $124 × 22.6% = **$28.00** (recognized as treats are delivered).

*Sales Tax Implication (Colorado/Denver General Rule):*
Tangible personal property (collar, treats) is generally taxable; services are often exempt.
*   **Unbundled:** Tax applies to $120 + $35.
*   **Bundled:** If the transaction is seen as a single sale of tangible goods with a "free" service element, tax may apply to the full $124. If distinct, tax applies only to the allocated tangible portion.
*   **Assumption:** Colorado treats the treat box as tangible property (taxable). The collar is tangible (taxable). The "service" element is minimal here since the core value is physical goods.
*   **Result:** Likely collect sales tax on the full bundled amount ($124) in Denver, as both primary components are tangible goods.

**What Flips It, and the Trigger**
The allocation method flips if your contract legally defines the subscription as a distinct "service" (e.g., access to a premium GPS tracking platform) rather than just a vehicle for delivering physical treats.
*   **Trigger:** If the GPS data access is sold separately or has a defined standalone value >$0, you must formally document that Standalone Selling Price (SSP) to justify allocating more revenue to the service portion (which might be non-taxable or deferred differently).
*   **Rule of Thumb:** If >20% of your marketing pitch focuses on the *software/app* rather than the *treats/collar*, re-evaluate the SSP.
*   **Step this week:** If your contract lumps "tracking + treats" into one line item, consider separating them in your checkout flow to clarify the tangible vs. service split for tax engines.
