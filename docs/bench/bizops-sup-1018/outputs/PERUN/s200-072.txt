**The Call:** Retain the per-seat model ($49) for now to maximize adoption and minimize churn risk; the instance-based model ($500/app) creates a 154% price jump for your pilot profile that acts as a adoption tax, likely suppressing the expansion needed to offset the higher unit price. Switch only after validating that customers derive discrete, measurable revenue per additional app instance that exceeds the $500 cost.

**The Numbers:**
*Current State (Seat Model):*
$49 \times 160 \text{ seats} = \$7,840$ MRR (matches your ~$8k).
*Hypothetical Pilot (Instance Model):*
$500 \times 50 \text{ apps} = \$25,000$ MRR.
*The Gap:* The instance model increases revenue by **218%** for this specific customer profile.
*Runway Check:*
Current Burn: $35,000.
Current Runway: $\$8,000 / \$35,000 \approx 0.23$ months of coverage (you are burning $27k net monthly).
*Break-even Analysis:*
To cover $35k burn on seats: $35,000 / 49 \approx 715$ seats needed.
To cover $35k burn on instances: $35,000 / 500 = 70$ apps needed.
*Assumption (ASSUMED):* You can sell 70 app instances faster than you can sell 715 seats without stalling initial deployment. Given enterprise sales cycles, selling 70 distinct "app deployments" often requires more proof-of-value than selling 715 seats within an existing enterprise agreement.

**What Flips It, and The Trigger:**
The deciding input is **Expansion Velocity vs. Price Resistance**.
*Trigger:* If pilot customers add >5 new internal apps per quarter *despite* the seat model, the instance model captures that value. If they hesitate to spin up test environments or micro-tools under the current model, the instance model will kill adoption entirely.
*Rule of Thumb:* If the price per app exceeds 2% of the estimated annual value that app generates for the client, adoption will stall.
*Action This Week:* Interview your 2 Fortune 500 pilots. Ask: "If we charged $500/month for every new micro-frontend you deploy, would you reduce the number of experiments you run?" If the answer is "Yes," the instance model destroys your product's utility value.

**Data to Gather for a Definitive Decision:**
1.  **Apps-per-Developer Ratio:** Calculate the average number of apps per seat across your 12 pilots. If it's <0.25 (as in your example: 50 apps / 200 devs = 0.25), the instance model is exponentially more expensive for them.

**Pros/Cons Summary:**
*   *Seat Model:* Pros: Predictable for customers, encourages widespread adoption, faster sales cycle. Cons: Revenue caps if headcount is frozen; doesn't capture value from high-automation/low-headcount teams.
*   *Instance Model:* Pros: Aligns with infrastructure value, scales with customer success, higher revenue ceiling. Cons: Penalizes experimentation (killing innovation), complex to track/audit, high friction for new internal tools.
