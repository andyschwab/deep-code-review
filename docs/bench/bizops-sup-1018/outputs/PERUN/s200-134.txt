**The Call:** Reject an exclusive pivot to government contracts under current terms; the transition creates an immediate cash deficit that exceeds your six-month runway, requiring approximately **$145,000** in additional working capital just to survive the first quarter. Do not switch unless you secure bridge financing or negotiate partial upfront payment.

**The Numbers:**
*   **Current Runway:** $35,000 (burn) × 6 months = **$210,000** cash on hand.
*   **Transition Gap Calculation:**
    *   Monthly Burn (ASSUMED constant): $35,000.
    *   Financing Period: 3 months (no revenue inflow).
    *   Operating Cost during gap: $35,000 × 3 = **$105,000**.
    *   *Correction for Revenue Timing:* Even if revenue starts Month 4, you must cover the full burn for 3 months before the first net-90 payment clears (which effectively pushes cash inflow to Month 4+3 = Month 7 if billing starts Month 4, or Month 4 if billing is immediate upon delivery).
    *   *Conservative Scenario (Billing upon delivery, payment Day 90):* You need to fund 3 months of burn ($105k) plus the gap between your burn and the delayed inflow.
    *   *Critical Deficit:* Your current cash ($210k) covers 6 months of burn. The new model requires funding operations for 3 months with **zero** inflow, then waiting 90 days for payment.
    *   Total cash needed to reach first payment receipt (assuming delivery Month 1, payment Month 4): $35,000 × 4 = **$140,000**.
    *   However, you must also sustain operations *after* the first payment arrives until the cycle stabilizes.
    *   **Required Additional Capital:** To safely cover the 3-month zero-revenue period plus a buffer for the 20% delay risk:
        *   Base Need (3 months burn): $105,000.
        *   Risk Buffer (20% chance of +30 days delay): $35,000 × 0.20 × 1 month ≈ $7,000 (expected value), but prudent modeling requires the *maximum* exposure: +$35,000.
        *   Total Additional Capital Needed: **$140,000 - $145,000**.
    *   *Result:* Your current $210k cash covers ~6 months. The transition consumes 3-4 months of cash before the first dollar returns. If the first payment is delayed, you hit zero cash by Month 5 or 6. You cannot absorb a second delay.

**What Flips It, and the Trigger:**
The single input that changes this verdict is **Payment Terms**. If you can negotiate **Net-30** or a **30% upfront deposit**, the required working capital drops below $50,000, making the pivot viable with existing reserves.
*   **Trigger:** If the government contract strictly enforces Net-90 with no deposit, **do not sign**.
*   **Rule of Thumb:** Never accept a B2G contract where the payment term exceeds 50% of your current cash runway.
*   **Step this week:** If the client insists on Net-90, consider factoring the invoice (selling the receivable at a discount) to get immediate cash, or reject the exclusive pivot and pursue a hybrid model.

**Routing Note:**
Government procurement contracts in Ontario often have specific statutory payment timelines that may override "Net-90" clauses; have a commercial lawyer review the *Prompt Payment Act* implications. Additionally, before approaching lenders for bridge financing, a CPA should validate your cash flow forecast, as banks will require audited projections for government-backed receivables.
