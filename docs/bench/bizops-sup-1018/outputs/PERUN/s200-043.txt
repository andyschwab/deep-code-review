**The call:** Construct a defensible TAM using a bottom-up proxy model anchored to the global sports nutrition market, applying a conservative "early-adopter penetration" factor derived from plant-based protein adoption curves, rather than seeking nonexistent insect-specific data.

**The numbers:**
To estimate your potential revenue, we apply the following logic:
1.  **Global Sports Nutrition Market (Base):** Industry consensus places this between $50B–$60B USD annually. We will use **$55B** as the base figure.
2.  **Target Segment Filter:** Insect protein appeals primarily to the "sustainable/ethical" sub-segment. Plant-based proteins currently represent ~15–20% of this market. We assume insect protein is at a much earlier stage.
3.  **Penetration Rate (Your Variable):** You proposed capturing **0.5%** of the *total* global market.
    *   Calculation: $55,000,000,000 × 0.005 = **$275,000,000** (Global Revenue Opportunity).
4.  **Unit Economics Check (Your Data):**
    *   Price: £35 (~$44 USD).
    *   COGS: £12 (~$15 USD).
    *   Gross Margin: £23 (~$29 USD) per unit (65.7%).
    *   To hit $275M revenue, you need to sell ~6.25 million units annually.

*Assumption Note:* The 0.5% figure is aggressive for a nascent category without specific adoption data. A more defensible "Serviceable Obtainable Market" (SOM) for a pitch deck often starts with a fraction of the *sustainable* niche, not the total market.

**What flips it, and the trigger:**
The single input that changes the call is the **comparative adoption rate of plant-based vs. insect-based protein**. If insect protein adoption lags plant-based by a factor of 10x (due to the "yuck factor"), your realistic near-term capture might be 0.05%, not 0.5%.
*   **Trigger:** If primary research (surveys of 500 target customers) shows willingness to try insect protein is below 5%, revise the penetration rate to 0.05% for your base case.
*   **Step this week:** Run a Van Westendorp price sensitivity survey and a concept test with 200 US-based gym-goers to validate if the £35 ($44) price point holds in a market with cheaper whey/plant options. If <10% express intent to buy at that price, consider a "good-better-best" packaging strategy to lower the entry price.
