**The Call**
Do not rely on invoice structure alone to exclude shipping from California's economic nexus threshold; treat the $5 fee as part of your gross receipts for nexus determination until a licensed professional confirms otherwise. While separating shipping *might* exempt that specific portion from being *taxed*, California's nexus threshold (gross receipts from sales *into* the state) is broad and often captures the total transaction value if the shipping is mandatory or integral to the sale. Given you are already at $576,000 in base revenue, adding $192,000 (3,200 users × $5 × 12 months) pushes you to $768,000, creating a high-risk zone where an aggressive exclusion could trigger penalties if audited.

**What Flips It, and the Trigger**
The deciding factor is not the invoice line item, but whether California considers your shipping charge a "pass-through" cost (where you act purely as an agent for the carrier) or part of the sales price. However, the critical trigger here is your **base revenue of $576,000**. Since this alone exceeds the $500,000 threshold, the shipping fee structure is irrelevant for *establishing* nexus—you already have it. The shipping structure only matters for determining the *taxable base* (i.e., whether you collect tax on the $5 or just the $15).
*   **Trigger Rule:** If gross receipts from sales into CA > $500,000 in the current or preceding calendar year, you must register.
*   **Action Step:** If your base revenue remains above $500,000 next month, consider registering for a California seller's permit immediately rather than waiting to optimize the shipping line item.
