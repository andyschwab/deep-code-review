**The Call**
Amortize the $84,000 upfront slotting fees over the 24-month contract term to match expenses with the revenue they generate; expense the $2,000 annual marketing fees immediately as incurred. This approach prevents distorting your unit economics and presents a clearer picture of operational profitability to investors.

**The Numbers**
*Formula:* Monthly Amortization = Total Upfront Fees / Contract Months.
*Inputs:* $84,000 total fees / 24 months.
*Result:* $3,500 monthly amortization expense.

*Impact on Unit Economics:*
*   **Gross Profit:** 800 bottles/store × 12 stores × $4 margin = $38,400 monthly gross profit.
*   **Scenario A (Immediate Expense):** Month 1 shows a $45,600 loss ($38,400 profit - $84,000 fee). Months 2–24 show full profit. This creates a false "unprofitable" signal in Month 1.
*   **Scenario B (Amortization - Recommended):** Monthly profit is $34,900 ($38,400 profit - $3,500 amortization). This reflects the true recurring cost of shelf access.
*   **Burn Rate Impact:** Amortization is a non-cash accounting entry. It reduces *reported* net income but does **not** increase your actual cash burn rate. Your cash burn occurred entirely when you paid the $84,000 upfront.

**What Flips It, and the Trigger**
The decision flips if the contract allows for immediate refundability or if the relationship terminates within the first few months.
*   **Trigger:** If a chain terminates the contract before 6 months, you must impair (write off) the remaining unamortized asset immediately.
*   **Rule of Thumb:** If the fee secures access for a defined period >1 year, capitalize and amortize. If it is a one-time promotional spike with no lasting benefit, expense it.
*   **Next Step:** If your contract includes a "clawback" clause for early termination, consider setting aside a cash reserve equal to one month's amortization ($3,500) rather than changing the accounting method.

**Routing Note**
While the general rule (GAAP/IFRS matching principle) favors amortization for multi-year access fees, the final classification of these specific fees as "deferred assets" versus "prepaid expenses" and the treatment of the marketing fees should be confirmed by your external accountant or auditor. They must verify that the contract terms (specifically renewal options and refundability) support capitalization under current tax and reporting standards.
