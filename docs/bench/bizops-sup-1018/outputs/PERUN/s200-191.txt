**The Call:** Adopt a 10% early-bird discount targeting 40% of enrollment; this generates ~$26,400 in April cash, covering 73% of your upfront staffing bills while preserving sufficient margin to remain profitable at realistic enrollment levels. A 20% discount risks eroding total profit unless you can guarantee a massive volume spike, which is unproven.

**The Numbers:**
*   **Fixed Costs:** $35,000 (Ops) + $72,000 (Upfront Staffing: 60% of $120k) = **$107,000** cash needed by April 1.
*   **Baseline Revenue:** 450 campers × $650 = $292,500.
*   **Variable Cost Assumption:** To model profit, we must estimate variable costs per camper. *ASSUMED:* Variable costs (materials, food, remaining staffing) are $350/camper (leaving $300 contribution margin at full price). If your actual variable cost is higher, margins shrink.

**What Flips It & The Trigger:**
The decision hinges on **conversion elasticity**: how many *extra* campers the discount brings in.
*   **Trigger:** If a 20% discount does not increase total enrollment by at least **7%** (from 450 to ~482 campers), Option A (10% discount) yields higher total profit.
*   **Rule of Thumb:** Do not discount deeper than 15% unless your historical data shows price is the primary barrier to entry.
*   **Step for this week:** If your current registration pace is <10% of last year's by March 1, consider launching the 10% tier immediately; if pace is strong, stick to 5% or skip early-bird to protect margin.
