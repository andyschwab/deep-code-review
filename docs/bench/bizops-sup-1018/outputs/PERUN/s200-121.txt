**The Call**
Do not re-categorize the initial fee as a "marketing fund contribution" to alter tax exposure without a binding opinion from a Texas tax attorney; the Texas Franchise Tax computation relies on federal taxable income as the starting point, where the substance of the revenue (earned vs. deferred) and the deductibility of expenses dictate the margin, not the label applied in the contract. Proceed with the standard $45,000 initial fee and 6% royalty structure for your financial modeling, but treat any structural deviation intended to minimize tax as a Tier 3 (routing) item requiring immediate professional review before signing the three pending LOIs.

**What Flips It, and the Trigger**
The single input that changes the outcome is the **legal characterization of dominion and control** over the "marketing fund."
*   **Trigger Value:** If the franchise agreement grants BreathFlow discretion to use the funds for general brand building rather than specific, auditable pass-through expenses on behalf of the franchisee, the "marketing" label fails.
*   **Stop/Pivot Trigger:** Before executing the third LOI (target date: within 14 days), if the draft agreement defines the marketing contribution as non-refundable and commingled with operating accounts, **stop** and pivot to counsel.
*   **Rule of Thumb:** In Texas, labels in a contract do not override economic substance. If you spend the money, it is likely your revenue.
*   **This Week's Step:** If the proposed "marketing fund" structure involves commingling funds or discretionary spending by the franchisor, consider engaging a Texas franchise tax specialist to draft a specific "agency" clause or abandon the re-categorization strategy entirely.
