**What Flips It, and the Trigger**
The single input that changes the call is the **legal characterization of the contract** under the *Howey Test* (the standard used by the SEC and US courts).
*   **The Test:** Does the student provide money (or value) with an expectation of profit solely from the efforts of others?
*   **The Risk:** While you are selling education, an IBR where you profit *only* if they succeed financially can be argued as the student "investing" their future labor in your school, expecting returns generated by your curriculum and placement efforts.
*   **Trigger:** The moment you market this plan emphasizing the *financial return* to the student or the *investment nature* of the agreement, rather than purely as a tuition financing mechanism.
*   **Rule of Thumb:** If the repayment terms look more like an equity stake in the student's future income than a standard loan, you are in Tier 3 territory.
