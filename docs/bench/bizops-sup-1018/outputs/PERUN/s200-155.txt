**The Call**
Reject the 2% flat conversion assumption for your investor deck; it likely overstates demand by ignoring intent segmentation and cannibalization of existing paid users. Instead, present a segmented TAM model that isolates "tutoring-ready" users from general power users, using a sensitivity analysis on conversion rates (0.5%–2.0%) to show investors you understand the risk of over-projecting.

**The Numbers**
To size the Specific Addressable Market (SAM) for tutoring without double-counting, use this funnel:
1.  **Total Registered:** 200,000
2.  **Free Tier Pool:** 170,000 (85% of total)
3.  **Daily Active Users (DAU):** 17,000 (10% of Free Tier)
4.  **Target Segment (Tutoring Interested):** *Unknown Variable A* (% of DAU seeking live help vs. self-study)
5.  **Conversion Rate:** *Variable B* (Your proposed 2% vs. conservative 0.5%)

*Formula:* `SAM = Free DAU × Target Segment % × Conversion Rate`

*Scenario Analysis (based on 17,000 Free DAU):*
*   **Optimistic (Your Assumption):** 100% of DAU want tutoring × 2% conversion = **340 users**.
*   **Base Case (Segmented):** 40% of DAU want tutoring × 1.5% conversion = **102 users**.
*   **Conservative (Intent-Low):** 20% of DAU want tutoring × 0.5% conversion = **17 users**.

*Revenue Impact (at $30/hr, assuming 4 sessions/month/user):*

*   Conservative: ~$2,040/month

**What Flips It, and The Trigger**
The single input that changes the call is **Target Segment %** (users specifically wanting live tutoring vs. self-study). If you cannot prove that at least 30% of your DAU actively seek human interaction, the 2% conversion rate is mathematically possible but strategically dangerous.
*   **Trigger:** If a survey of 500 DAU shows <15% interest in live tutoring, pivot the narrative from "mass conversion" to "high-value niche."
*   **Rule of Thumb:** Live services typically convert at 1/3 to 1/5 the rate of self-serve SaaS features due to friction and price.
*   **Step this week:** If <20% of surveyed users express intent, consider a waitlist launch to validate demand before building full supply-side capacity.

**Unknown Variables & Overlap Risks**
1.  **Cannibalization:** You did not provide the size of your *existing* paid base. If 5% of your *total* users (10,000) are already paying for self-study, some may downgrade or switch to tutoring, inflating your "new" revenue while shrinking recurring self-study MRR. You must net out expected churn from existing plans.
2.  **Intent vs. Action:** "Engaging daily" with self-study tools does not correlate perfectly with wanting a tutor. Many power users prefer autonomy. Without isolating this intent, your 17,000 DAU denominator is too large.
3.  **Supply Constraints:** TAM is theoretical; your Serviceable Obtainable Market (SOM) is capped by tutor availability. At 340 users taking 4 hours/month, you need 1,360 tutor-hours monthly immediately.
