**The Call**
Choose the **nonprofit structure** only if you can reliably secure the additional $100,000 in grants/donations annually; otherwise, the C-corp is the solvent choice. Based on your current projections, the nonprofit's $20,000 compliance burden wipes out your entire $20,000 projected surplus, creating an immediate deficit unless the extra funding materializes. The tax advantage is not in income tax (you likely owe little either way given thin margins) but in the ability to raise unrestricted capital via tax-deductible contributions.

**The Numbers**
*Formula:* Annual Surplus = (Base Revenue + Grant/Donation Uplift) – (Operating Expenses + Compliance Costs).

*Scenario A: C-Corp*
*   Revenue: $300,000
*   Expenses: $280,000
*   Compliance (estimated): $5,000 (ASSUMED lower regulatory burden)
*   **Result:** +$15,000 surplus (before corporate income tax).
*   *Note:* Corporate tax rate ~21% applies only to taxable income. With reinvestment, liability may be near zero.

*Scenario B: Nonprofit (501(c)(3) equivalent)*
*   Revenue: $300,000
*   **Grant/Donation Uplift:** $0 (Low) to $100,000 (High) [ASSUMED variable based on fundraising success]
*   Expenses: $280,000
*   Compliance: $20,000 (User provided)
*   **Result (Low Uplift):** -$20,000 deficit (Insolvent in Year 1).
*   **Result (High Uplift):** +$80,000 surplus (Fully tax-exempt).

**What Flips It, and the Trigger**
The single input that changes the call is the **Grant/Donation Uplift**.
*   **Trigger Value:** You must secure at least **$20,001** in additional tax-deductible funding annually just to break even with the C-corp's baseline surplus.
*   **Decision Rule:** If you do not have signed letters of intent or a proven donor pipeline for >$25k within the first 6 months, the nonprofit structure creates unnecessary financial risk.
*   **Step this week:** If you cannot list three specific grant opportunities or major donors ready to give immediately, consider starting as a C-corp (or LLC) and filing for nonprofit status later once revenue validates the mission.

**Routing Note (Tier 3)**
The determination of whether your "paid training programs" constitute "unrelated business income" (UBI) for a nonprofit is a binding legal/tax question. If commercial activities become substantial, the nonprofit could lose its tax-exempt status or owe taxes on that portion. You must ask a tax attorney: "Does our specific curriculum model risk generating significant UBI that jeopardizes 501(c)(3) status?" Additionally, the formation documents for a nonprofit strictly prohibit profit distribution; ensure your personal compensation plan is reasonable and documented to avoid IRS scrutiny.
