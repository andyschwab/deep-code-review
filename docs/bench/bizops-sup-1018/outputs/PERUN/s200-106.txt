**The Call:** Do not book the $50,000 immediately; you must recognize it ratably over the 12-month service period to comply with accrual accounting principles and avoid misleading stakeholders.

**The Numbers:**
Under the revenue recognition principle (accrual basis), revenue is recorded when earned (service delivered), not when cash is received.
*   **Formula:** Monthly Revenue = Total Contract Value / Service Period Months
*   **Your Figures:** $50,000 / 12 months
*   **Result:** **$4,166.67 per month** recognized as revenue.

The remaining balance sits on your Balance Sheet as a liability called **Deferred Revenue** (or Unearned Revenue) until the service is performed.
*   **January Entry:** Cash +$50,000; Deferred Revenue +$50,000. Revenue Recognized: $4,166.67. Ending Deferred Revenue: $45,833.33.
*   **February Entry:** Cash $0; Deferred Revenue -$4,166.67; Revenue Recognized +$4,166.67.

**What Flips It, and The Trigger:**
The only factor changing this treatment is the contract term or delivery schedule. If you delivered all workshops on Day 1, you could recognize 100% immediately. Since you deliver continuously, the trigger is the passage of time or completion of milestones.
*   **Risk Threshold:** Booking 100% upfront inflates January profit by ~$45,800 and creates a "revenue cliff" in subsequent months where you show sales but zero income from that client. This distorts your Monthly Recurring Revenue (MRR) and churn metrics, making the business look volatile to investors.
*   **Action:** If your current ledger shows the full $50,000 as January revenue, consider reclassifying the unearned portion to Deferred Revenue before the audit to align with GAAP/IFRS standards.

**Routing Note:**
While the principle of ratable recognition is standard for service contracts, the specific journal entries, tax treatment of deferred income in your jurisdiction, and audit adjustments must be finalized by your CPA. Bring them the exact contract start/end dates and delivery schedule to confirm the monthly amortization schedule.
