**The Call**
Accept the term sheet only if your growth targets require capital you cannot raise later at better terms; the deal implies **20% dilution**, which sits at the high end of standard Series A ranges (typically 15–25%), leaving you with 80% of the post-money entity.

**The Numbers**
Dilution is calculated as the investment amount divided by the post-money valuation.
*Formula:* `Dilution % = Investment / (Pre-money Valuation + Investment)`
*Your Figures:* €4M Investment / (€16M Pre-money + €4M Investment) = €4M / €20M.
*Result:* **20% dilution**. You retain **80%** ownership.

Your current monthly revenue is €373,800 (4,200 students × €89). With a 3.5% churn, you lose ~147 students monthly. To justify an aggressive expansion into Southeast Asia and Latin America, you must overcome the "J-curve" where marketing spend initially widens your burn before new LTV matures.

**What Flips It, and the Trigger**
The single input that changes the verdict is your **projected net revenue retention (NRR)** in the new markets.
*Trigger:* If your pilot campaigns in Q3 show an NRR below 95% (churn + expansion revenue) in target regions, **stop** the aggressive spend and renegotiate the valuation or tranche the investment.
*Rule of Thumb:* In ed-tech, Series A dilution above 25% signals distress or a weak negotiating position; below 15% suggests you left money on the table. At 20%, you are in the "standard but expensive" zone.
