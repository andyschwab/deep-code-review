**The Call**
Treat the $350 last-mile logistics cost as a **Cost of Goods Sold (COGS)** component, not Sales & Marketing. This classification provides the truer picture of scalability because your "white-glove delivery" is a bundled fulfillment obligation required to transfer the product to the customer, not a discretionary customer acquisition cost. Classifying it as S&M would artificially inflate your gross margin, misleading investors about the fundamental profitability of each unit sold and obscuring the operational leverage needed to justify your next hire.

**The Numbers**
To determine your unit economics, we must isolate the contribution margin using the correct cost structure.

*   **Formula:** Contribution Margin per Unit = Revenue per Unit – Variable COGS per Unit.
*   **User Figures:**
    *   Revenue: $2,500
    *   Last-Mile Logistics: $350 (Variable)
    *   Industry Standard Freight (Benchmark): $150
*   **Assumptions (ASSUMED):** Since you did not provide the manufacturing cost of the sofa itself, I will bracket this variable to show the impact of the logistics classification. Let $M$ be the manufacturing cost.

| Scenario | Classification of $350 Logistics | Formula Application | Result (in terms of $M$) |
| :--- | :--- | :--- | :--- |
| **Correct (COGS)** | Included in Variable COGS | $2,500 - (M + 350)$ | **$2,150 - M** |
| **Incorrect (S&M)** | Excluded from COGS | $2,500 - M$ | **$2,500 - M** |

*Analysis:* By moving the $350 to S&M, you artificially increase your reported Gross Margin by $350 per unit ($420,000 total over 1,200 units). However, this cost is incurred on *every* sale. If your manufacturing cost ($M$) is $1,000:
*   **True Gross Margin:** ($2,150 - $1,000) / $2,500 = **46%**
*   **Distorted Gross Margin:** ($2,500 - $1,000) / $2,500 = **60%**

The 14-point difference misrepresents the capital efficiency of your business model.

**What Flips It, and the Trigger**
The input that changes the call is the **contractual nature of the delivery**. If delivery were an optional, third-party upsell chosen by only a fraction of customers (e.g., "Standard Shipping: Free, White-Glove: +$350"), you could separate the revenue and cost.
*   **Trigger Rule:** If >95% of customers receive the service as part of the base price (which is your current model), it is a COGS component.
*   **Actionable Step:** If your logistics cost per unit drops below $200 through volume negotiations, consider unbundling the service to test price sensitivity; until then, keep it bundled in COGS to maintain pricing transparency.
