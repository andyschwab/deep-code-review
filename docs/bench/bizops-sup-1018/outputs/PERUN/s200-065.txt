**The call:** Do not present a single blended CAC; it will distort your efficiency by masking the 9-month enterprise sales cycle. **The numbers:**
Your current blended math ($350k spend / 92 customers = ~$3,800) is misleading because it treats an SMB and an Enterprise customer as equal units of growth. You must separate the pools.

*Formula:* `Segment CAC = (Attributed Sales + Marketing Spend) / New Customers in Segment`

Since you did not provide a split of the $350k spend between SMB and Enterprise efforts, we must assume an allocation. A common baseline for mixed models is to attribute spend by sales resource usage or lead volume.

**What flips it, and the trigger:**
The decision to increase the budget hinges on **Payback Period**, not raw CAC. In the table above, despite the high $17,500 CAC, the Enterprise segment pays back in under 5 months (assuming revenue is recognized upfront or monthly without heavy churn), whereas SMB takes over 17 months.
*Trigger:* If your actual Enterprise sales cycle is truly 9 months *to close*, but revenue is recognized over a 12-month contract, your cash flow gap is the risk, not the CAC.
*Rule of Thumb:* If Payback Period < 12 months, the CAC is generally sustainable for SaaS.
