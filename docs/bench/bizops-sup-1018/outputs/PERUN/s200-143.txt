**The Regulatory Landscape & Licensing Requirements:**
Insurance licensing in the US is state-specific, and the "line of authority" determines what you can sell.
*   **The Bundle Problem:** While your P&C license covers travel cancellation (often classified as limited lines travel or property/casualty), adding "health accident insurance" introduces a life and health line of authority. Regulators generally view bundled products containing multiple lines of insurance as requiring the agent to hold licenses for *all* contained lines. You cannot "hide" the health component inside a P&C product.
*   **Duration Irrelevance:** The five-day duration of the retreat does not exempt you from health licensing requirements. Short-term accident and health policies are still classified as health insurance. There is no standard regulatory exemption that reclassifies health coverage as P&C simply because the term is less than seven days.
*   **State Specifics (General Rule):**
    *   **California:** The Department of Insurance strictly separates lines. Selling accident and health requires a specific Life Agent license. Penalties for unlicensed activity can include administrative fines up to $50,000 per violation and criminal misdemeanor charges.
    *   **Texas:** The Department of Insurance requires a separate Life, Accident, and Health license. Non-compliance can result in fines up to $25,000 per violation and license suspension.
    *   **Florida:** The Office of Insurance Regulation mandates distinct licensure. Unauthorized representation can lead to fines up to $50,000 and permanent license revocation.
