**The Call:** Do not sign the "guaranteed zero breaches" clause as written; it creates an uninsurable, existential risk that likely voids standard cyber liability coverage and exceeds your capacity to pay. You must negotiate this down to a "commercially reasonable efforts" standard with a liability cap tied to your insurance limits or contract value, not unlimited damages.

**The Numbers:**
*   **Current Exposure:** $400k revenue vs. potential "all damages" from a financial services breach (often multimillion-pound).
*   **Insurance Cap:** $2M (General Liability). Cyber policies often exclude guarantees of specific security outcomes.
*   **Deal Size:** ~$160k (40% of $400k).
*   **Risk/Reward Ratio:** You are risking >$2M (and company survival) for $160k revenue.
*   **Assumption (UNVERIFIED):** Standard cyber policies in the UK exclude liability for "assumed liabilities" where you guarantee a result (zero breaches) rather than a service level (uptime/scan frequency).

**What Flips It, and the Trigger:**
The trigger is the **liability cap**. If the client refuses to cap liability at your insurance limit ($2M) or a multiple of the contract value (e.g., 12x monthly fees), you must walk away.
*   **Rule of Thumb:** Never accept unlimited liability for software defects; the industry standard is a cap between 12–24 months of fees or the available insurance proceeds, whichever is lower.
*   **This Week's Step:** If the client insists on "zero breaches," consider offering a service credit (refund of fees) instead of cash damages for missed vulnerabilities, while keeping the liability cap for gross negligence/willful misconduct only.
