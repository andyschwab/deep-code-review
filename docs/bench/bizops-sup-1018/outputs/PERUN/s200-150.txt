**The Call:** Do not pivot to an insurance-heavy model yet; the math shows a 60% revenue collapse if you simply swap rates while losing half your volume. Instead, adopt a hybrid "good-better-best" packaging strategy immediately: retain a premium cash tier for high-touch/long-duration care while launching a streamlined, lower-cost insurance track to capture volume without cannibalizing your core margin.

**The Numbers:**
*Current Monthly Revenue:* 400 patients × $150 = **$60,000**.
*Scenario A (50% Churn to Insurance):*
Assumption: 50% of patients switch to insurance providers; remaining 50% stay cash.
Formula: `(Remaining Cash Patients × $150) + (Switched Patients × $60)`
Calculation: `(200 × $150) + (200 × $60)` = $30,000 + $12,000 = **$42,000**.
*Result:* A **30% total revenue drop** despite maintaining patient count, driven by the rate differential.

*Scenario B (Hybrid Volume Play - ASSUMED):*
Assumption: You create a specific insurance product line that attracts *new* volume to fill the gap, while retaining 80% of original cash patients (20% churn).
Formula: `(Retained Cash × $150) + (New Insurance Volume × $60)`
To match current $60k revenue with 320 cash patients ($48k), you need $12k from insurance.
Required Insurance Volume: $12,000 / $60 = **200 additional insurance patients**.
*Result:* You must nearly double your total patient load (400 → 520) just to break even against a modest 20% cash churn.

**What Flips It, and The Trigger:**
The deciding input is **Cash Price Elasticity**: the percentage of patients willing to pay a premium (e.g., $200+) for extended sessions versus accepting standard insurance limits.
*Trigger:* If a Van Westendorp price-sensitivity survey (run this week) shows less than 30% of your current base is "very likely" to pay >$175 for extended care, the luxury niche is too small to sustain operations alone.
*Rule of Thumb:* If Customer Acquisition Cost (CAC) for new insurance patients exceeds $300, the volume play destroys value.
*This Week's Step:* If <30% express high willingness-to-pay, consider launching a pilot "insurance-access" track with limited session counts to test acquisition costs before hiring billing staff.
