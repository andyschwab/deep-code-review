**The Call**
Do not launch the $50/month for 14 months program until counsel confirms the structure avoids "true lease" vs. "secured transaction" reclassification; your current draft likely triggers lending regulations because the total payments ($700) exceed the cash price ($600) and title transfers automatically, creating a de facto loan with implied interest.

**The Numbers**
*   **Cash Price:** $600
*   **Lease Structure:** $50/month × 14 months = $700 Total Cost
*   **Implied Finance Charge:** $700 - $600 = $100
*   **Effective Interest Rate (Approx.):** ~24% APR (assuming simple interest over 14 months on declining balance)
*   **Inventory Exposure:** 1,000 units × $600 cost basis = $600,000 tied up
*   **Burn Rate Impact:** At $40,000/month burn, you have 15 months of runway if sales stop; converting inventory to receivables via a potentially unlicensed lending product risks freezing cash flow if regulators halt the program.

**What Flips It, and the Trigger**
The single input that changes the classification is the **purchase option price** at the end of the term.
*   **Trigger:** If the customer *must* pay a nominal fee (e.g., $1) or if the total payments equal/exceed the fair market value such that ownership is effectively guaranteed, the Uniform Commercial Code (UCC) and state consumer credit laws typically reclassify the "lease" as a **secured installment loan**.
*   **Rule of Thumb:** In California and most states, a "true lease" requires a meaningful residual value risk for the lessor and a genuine option to return the goods. If the economics compel the buyer to own, it is a loan.
*   **Action This Week:** If your contract includes an automatic title transfer or a "$1 buyout" clause, consider pausing the draft and engaging counsel to restructure the end-of-term option as a genuine fair-market-value purchase choice, or prepare for lender licensing.
