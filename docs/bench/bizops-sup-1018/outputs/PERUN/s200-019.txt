**The call:** Reject the price cut; keep the $32 price point and invest in the premium packaging upgrade. Lowering the price to $24 anchors LuxGlow as a mid-market commodity, likely increasing return rates among luxury buyers who equate low price with low quality. The packaging upgrade directly addresses the "subtle scent" complaint by elevating perceived value, which is critical for luxury goods where price signals quality.

**The numbers:**
*Current State:*
Revenue: $32 | COGS: $9 | Shipping: $6 | Return Rate: 22%
Effective Units Sold (net of returns): 1,170 (1,500 * 0.78)
Net Revenue: $37,440 | Total Variable Cost (on all units): $22,500
**Net Contribution:** $14,940/month

*Scenario A (Price Cut to $24, no packaging change):*
Assumption: Return rate drops slightly to 18% due to lowered expectations, but conversion dips as luxury appeal fades.
Revenue: $24 | COGS: $9 | Shipping: $6
Effective Units Sold: 1,230 (1,500 * 0.82)
Net Revenue: $29,520 | Total Variable Cost: $22,500
**Net Contribution:** $7,020/month (**-$7,920 vs. Current**)

*Scenario B (Keep $32, +$4 Packaging Cost):*
Assumption: Return rate drops to 10% due to higher perceived quality; shipping cost remains flat (see trigger below).
Revenue: $32 | COGS: $13 ($9 + $4) | Shipping: $6
Effective Units Sold: 1,350 (1,500 * 0.90)
Net Revenue: $43,200 | Total Variable Cost: $28,500 (1,500 * $19)
**Net Contribution:** $14,700/month (Comparable to current, but with 15% higher gross volume and brand equity preservation).
*Note: If the packaging upgrade reduces returns to 8%, Net Contribution rises to $16,320.*

**What flips it, and the trigger:**
The decision hinges on whether the heavier packaging pushes shipments into a higher carrier tier.
*Trigger:* If the new package weight/size increases shipping costs by more than $1.50 per unit, the margin benefit of Scenario B evaporates.
*Action:* Weigh one packaged unit this week. If the carrier surcharge is <$1.50, proceed with Scenario B immediately. If >$1.50, renegotiate carrier rates or optimize box dimensions before rolling out.

**Routing note:**
While rotating packaging for an A/B test generally does not require specific consumer protection disclosures if the product contents remain identical, ensuring that marketing claims on the new packaging (e.g., "premium," "enhanced") do not constitute deceptive trade practices is a jurisdiction-specific legal determination.
