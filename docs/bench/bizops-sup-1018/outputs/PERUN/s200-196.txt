**The Call:** You cannot launch as currently structured; your $60,000 cash reserve is insufficient to cover the $90,000 upfront server cost, creating an immediate $30,000 funding gap before acquiring a single user. Even if funded, the high fixed costs delay profitability significantly compared to a standard SaaS model, requiring roughly 18–24 months to break even at your projected adoption rate, making the $4,000 monthly burn a critical risk factor.

**The Numbers:**
*Formula:* Break-Even Month = (Upfront Fixed Costs + Monthly Burn × Months) / (Monthly Revenue per User × Cumulative Users).
*Inputs:* Upfront Cost = $90,000 (ASSUMED fully payable now); Monthly Burn = $4,000; Price = $200 one-time + $20/month.
*Scenario A (Base Case: 500 users over 6 months, linear growth):*
- Month 1–6 Revenue: Users grow 0 to 500. Avg users ~250.
- One-time revenue: 500 × $200 = $100,000.
- Recurring revenue (avg): 250 × $20 × 6 months = $30,000.
- Total Cost (6 months): $90,000 + ($4,000 × 6) = $114,000.
- Net at Month 6: $130,000 (rev) - $114,000 (cost) = +$16,000.
*Result:* You technically cross cash-flow positive in Month 6 *if* you have the initial $90k. However, your cash runway is only $60k. You run out of money in Month 15 ($60k / $4k) if no users join, or earlier if the $90k bill hits immediately.

*Scenario B (Slow Adoption: 250 users over 6 months):*
- One-time: $50,000. Recurring: $15,000. Total Rev: $65,000.
- Total Cost: $114,000.
- Net: -$49,000 deficit. Break-even pushed to Month 14+.

*Unknown Variable (Server Scaling):* The $4,000/month is a fixed assumption. If user growth accelerates (e.g., video storage for certifications), variable costs may rise. If cost-per-user exceeds $5/month at scale, the contribution margin ($20 - variable cost) shrinks, extending the break-even date by 3–6 months.

**What Flips It, and the Trigger:**
The single input that changes the call is **Upfront Capital Availability**. Without an additional $30,000 minimum (to cover the $90k infra bill), the project halts immediately.
*Trigger:* If you cannot secure $30k–$50k in bridge funding by [Current Date + 30 days], consider pivoting to a usage-based server architecture (e.g., AWS pay-as-you-go) to convert the $90k CAPEX into OPEX, aligning costs with revenue.
*Rule of Thumb:* In high-CAPEX models, ensure cash reserves cover 1.5× fixed costs before launch.
*Step this week:* If your server vendor demands 100% upfront, consider negotiating a 50% deposit or migrating to a cloud provider with monthly billing to preserve cash flow.
