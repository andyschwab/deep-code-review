**The Numbers & Structure:**
Your model swaps a fixed price ($8,000) for a variable contingent payment (15% of salary > $50k for 3 years).
*   **Implied Cost to Student:** If a graduate earns $60,000, they pay $9,000/year × 3 = $27,000. If they earn $100,000, they pay $45,000.
*   **The Risk Variable:** The regulatory trigger is not the percentage, but whether the obligation is deemed a "debt" with a fixed repayment term regardless of income sufficiency, or a true equity-like share of income.
*   **Assumption:** You are treating these as commercial contracts, not registered securities or licensed loans.
*   **Result:** In many jurisdictions, if the payment obligation looks like a loan (fixed term, fixed percentage, mandatory payment once threshold is met) without a usury cap or lending license, it is an **unlicensed loan**.

**What Flips It, and The Trigger:**
The single input that changes this from a commercial contract to an illegal loan is **state-specific statutory language regarding "educational institutions" vs. "lenders."**
*   **Texas:** Texas has specific statutes governing ISAs. If your contract terms do not strictly align with the Texas Education Code exemptions for proprietary schools, you are operating as an unlicensed lender.
*   **California:** California is aggressively litigating ISA providers. The DFPI (Department of Financial Protection and Innovation) has previously taken action against ISA providers for operating without a financing law license.
*   **Trigger:** The moment you sign a student residing in California under this current template, you likely trigger a violation of the California Financing Law.
*   **Rule of Thumb:** If the state regulator views the transaction as "advancing money (education) for a guaranteed return (future income)" rather than a true risk-sharing partnership, it is a loan.
