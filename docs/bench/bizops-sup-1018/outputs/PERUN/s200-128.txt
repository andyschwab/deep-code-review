**The Call**
Pass the 8% tax to the consumer in New York immediately; absorbing it destroys unit economics and creates an unsustainable cross-subsidy from rural to urban bookings. Do not absorb the cost unless urban cancellation rates exceed 15% in a controlled test.

**The Numbers**
*Current Monthly Gross Booking Value (GBV):* 400 bookings × $1,200 = $480,000.
*Current Monthly Revenue (12% commission):* $57,600.

*Urban vs. Rural Split:*
*   Urban (60%): 240 bookings × $1,200 = $288,000 GBV → $34,560 Revenue.
*   Rural (40%): 160 bookings × $1,200 = $192,000 GBV → $23,040 Revenue.

*Scenario A: Absorb the 8% Tax in Urban Market (Tier 1 Compute)*
If you absorb the tax, your effective commission margin on urban bookings drops by the tax amount relative to the transaction value.
*   Tax Cost per Booking: $1,200 × 8% = $96.
*   Current Commission per Booking: $1,200 × 12% = $144.
*   New Net Commission per Booking: $144 - $96 = $48.
*   **Monthly Revenue Loss:** 240 urban bookings × $96 = **$23,040**.
*   **New Total Monthly Revenue:** $57,600 - $23,040 = $34,560 (a 40% total revenue drop).

*Scenario B: Pass Tax to Consumer (Tier 1 Compute)*
*   New Consumer Price: $1,200 + ($1,200 × 8%) = $1,296.
*   Your Commission (12% of $1,296): $155.52.
*   *Assumption (UNVERIFIED):* Demand elasticity is low for niche retreats. If volume holds at 240 bookings, revenue increases slightly to $37,324.
*   *Trigger:* If passing the tax causes urban booking volume to drop by more than 15% (falling below ~204 bookings), the revenue benefit erodes.

**What Flips It, and the Trigger**
The deciding input is **price elasticity of demand** in the urban segment.
*   **Trigger Value:** If urban bookings drop below 204 per month after implementing the price hike, reconsider absorbing a *portion* of the tax or running targeted promotions rather than a full absorption.
*   **Rule of Thumb:** Never subsidize a government tax obligation with operating margin unless the Lifetime Value (LTV) of retaining the customer strictly exceeds the immediate cash burn.
