**The Call**
Reject the standard recurring-revenue LTV model; instead, present a "Single-Cycle Contribution Margin" model to investors, as your business is fundamentally a high-volume acquisition engine, not a retention play. Do not artificially inflate LTV with speculative referral or merchandise revenue unless those streams are already instrumented and generating verified cash flow; doing so will erode credibility during due diligence.

**The Numbers**
Your current unit economics show a negative contribution on a standalone basis if overhead is significant, but the math for the core transaction is clear:
*   **Revenue per Student (ARPU):** $2,500
*   **Customer Acquisition Cost (CAC):** $450
*   **Gross Margin per Student:** $2,050 (before variable delivery costs like instructor pay, materials, and venue).

*Formula:* `Contribution = ARPU - CAC - Variable Costs`
*Assumption:* You have not provided variable delivery costs. I will assume a conservative 50% variable cost ratio ($1,250/student) to illustrate the break-even.
*   **Scenario A (Base Case - 50% Variable Cost):** $2,500 - $450 - $1,250 = **$800 profit per student.**
*   **Scenario B (High Cost - 70% Variable Cost):** $2,500 - $450 - $1,750 = **$300 profit per student.**
*   **Scenario C (Low Cost - 30% Variable Cost):** $2,500 - $450 - $750 = **$1,300 profit per student.**

Standard LTV formulas (`ARPU / Churn`) fail here because churn is ~100% annually. Your effective LTV is simply the Gross Margin of that single six-week engagement. Your LTV:CAC ratio is not a multi-year projection; it is `(ARPU - Variable Costs) / CAC`. Using the Base Case: $2,050 / $450 = **4.5x**. This is a healthy ratio for a single-touch business, provided the "Variable Costs" are accurately accounted for.

**What Flips It, and the Trigger**
The one input that changes the call is **Variable Delivery Cost**. If your cost to deliver the camp (instructors, rent, software, insurance) exceeds $2,050 per student, you are losing money on every enrollment regardless of how many students you sign up.
*   **Trigger Threshold:** If variable costs hit $2,050/student, your contribution margin hits zero.
*   **Rule of Thumb:** In seasonal education, aim for a contribution margin of at least 3x CAC ($1,350 in your case) to cover fixed overhead (marketing salaries, admin, website).
*   **Step this week:** Calculate your exact variable cost per head for last summer. If it is below $1,600, double down on ad spend immediately. If it is above $2,000, pause ads and renegotiate vendor/instructor rates before selling another seat.

**Regarding Referrals and Merchandise**
Only factor these in if you have historical data. If you estimate a 10% referral rate where a referred student costs $0 in ads, your *blended* CAC drops:
*   *New Blended CAC:* $450 / 1.1 (approx) = ~$409.
*   Do not project merchandise sales into LTV unless you sold merch to >20% of students last year. Investors view unproven cross-sell assumptions in a one-time model as "hope-based" accounting.
