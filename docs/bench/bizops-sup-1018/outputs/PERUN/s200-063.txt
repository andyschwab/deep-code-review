**The Analysis:**
Under US federal law (HIPAA), data derived from Protected Health Information (PHI) remains subject to regulation unless it is successfully "de-identified." There are two safe harbors for de-identification:
1.  **Safe Harbor Method:** Removal of 18 specific identifiers (names, dates, geographies, etc.). This is often difficult for longitudinal patient data where dates and sequences are clinically vital.
2.  **Expert Determination Method:** A qualified statistician certifies that the risk of re-identification is "very small."

Your current model relies on the *logic* of Expert Determination (statistical distributions, no 1:1 mapping), but HIPAA compliance is not automatic based on algorithm design. The regulation requires a formal, documented certification by a qualified expert who applies generally accepted statistical and scientific principles. The fact that a potential client's audit identified re-identification risks via dataset combination suggests your current "very small" risk threshold has not been met or documented to the satisfaction of a HIPAA-covered entity.

In the US healthcare market, "legal cover" is not just about the math; it is about the *attestation*. Without a formal Expert Determination letter, your data is technically still PHI in the eyes of a regulator or litigator if a breach occurs. The pause by the hospital chain's legal team is a standard Tier 3 (binding determination) trigger; they cannot accept liability for your unverified de-identification status.
