**The Numbers:**
*Formula:* LTV = (Monthly Price × Gross Margin) / Monthly Churn Rate.
*Inputs:* Price = $12; CAC = $25; Fixed Costs = $4,000/mo.
*Assumptions:*
1.  **Churn Rate:** You cited 40% churn *after three months*. To model this, we assume a simplified average monthly churn rate of ~16% (derived from retaining 60% over 90 days). Marked **ASSUMED** for modeling; verify with cohort data.
2.  **Gross Margin:** Server/content costs are $4,000. At 3,000 users × $12 = $36,000 revenue, variable costs are negligible relative to fixed costs, so we approximate Gross Margin per user as ~$11.50. Marked **ASSUMED**.

*Results:*
*   **LTV:** $11.50 / 0.16 = **$71.88**.
*   **LTV:CAC Ratio:** $71.88 / $25 = **2.87**.
*   **Payback Period:** $25 / $11.50 = **2.2 months**.

*Correction for Early Churn:* The standard LTV formula above assumes constant churn. Your "40% drop after three months" indicates a "leaky bucket" where early cohorts die faster. If 40% leave at month 3, the *actual* average lifespan is roughly 5–6 months, not the 6.25 months implied by the constant rate.
*   **Adjusted LTV (Realistic):** ~$12 × 5.5 months = **$66**.
*   **Adjusted Profit per User:** $66 (LTV) - $25 (CAC) = **$41**.

*Where the model actually fails:* The "40% churn after 3 months" is catastrophic for *valuation* and *scalability*, even if currently cash-flow positive.
