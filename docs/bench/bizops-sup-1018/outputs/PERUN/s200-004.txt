**The Call:** Do not print "human-grade" on your packaging or website until you have engaged legal counsel to verify your supply chain and labeling against specific FDA and Ohio state definitions; using this term without strict adherence triggers significantly higher regulatory scrutiny and potential liability compared to standard pet food claims.

**The Rules and Risks:**
In the United States, the term "human-grade" is not merely a marketing flourish; it is a legally defined claim regulated by the FDA's Center for Veterinary Medicine (CVM) and enforced at the state level by agencies like the Ohio Department of Agriculture. Unlike standard pet food claims (e.g., "natural" or "premium"), "human-grade" asserts that the **entire product**—every ingredient and the final processed food—is fit for human consumption and has been manufactured, packed, and held in strict compliance with FDA regulations for human foods (21 CFR Part 110/117).

If you use this label, you are effectively claiming your facility operates as a human food plant. This triggers the following distinctions:
1.  **Facility Registration:** Your manufacturing facility must be registered with the FDA as a human food facility, not just an animal food facility. This subjects you to more rigorous Current Good Manufacturing Practice (cGMP) inspections.
2.  **Ingredient Sourcing:** Every single ingredient must be sourced from suppliers certified for human consumption. You cannot mix human-grade meat with standard pet-grade vitamins or minerals without voiding the claim for the whole product.
3.  **Pre-Market Approval:** While pet food generally does not require pre-market approval, misbranding a product as "human-grade" when it fails to meet the strict definition is a violation of the Federal Food, Drug, and Cosmetic Act. The FDA can seize products or issue warning letters immediately upon finding non-compliance.

**Documentation Requirements:**
To substantiate this claim during an inspection, you must maintain a complete paper trail proving the human-edible status of every component. This includes:
*   **Certificates of Analysis (COA):** From every supplier, explicitly stating the ingredients meet human-edible specifications.
*   **Supply Chain Audits:** Proof that ingredients were transported and stored in facilities compliant with human food safety standards to prevent cross-contamination with animal-byproducts.
*   **Processing Records:** Documentation showing your manufacturing line is either dedicated to human-grade products or underwent a validated sanitation process acceptable for human food production before your run.

**Ohio vs. Federal Oversight:**
Ohio adopts the model pet food regulations distributed by the Association of American Feed Control Officials (AAFCO). While the FDA sets the federal baseline, Ohio state inspectors perform the actual facility checks. If your label says "human-grade," Ohio inspectors will evaluate your operation against human food safety standards. A discrepancy between your claim and your operational reality (e.g., using a shared line without proper validation) constitutes misbranding under both state and federal law.

**Routing Note:**
This is a high-stakes regulatory determination. You must consult with an attorney specializing in FDA food law and an Ohio-licensed regulatory consultant before finalizing your label. Specifically, ask them: "Does our current supply chain and co-packer arrangement legally satisfy the FDA's definition of 'human-grade' for the finished product, and what specific indemnity clauses do we need in our supplier contracts?" Do not proceed with the 5,000-can batch until this verification is complete.
