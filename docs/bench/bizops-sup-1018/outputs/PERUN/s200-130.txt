**Specific Data Points to Avoid:**
To minimize risk, your pricing logic must strictly exclude any variable that acts as a proxy for protected classes. Do not use:
1.  **Geographic identifiers:** Zip codes, census tracts, or specific neighborhood names.
2.  **Demographic data:** Racial composition, income levels, or family size statistics of the area surrounding the home.
3.  **Tenant characteristics:** Any data regarding the specific protected class status of current or prospective tenants (e.g., source of income like Section 8, disability type).
4.  **Historical bias:** Past referral success rates if those rates were influenced by discriminatory market forces.

Safe variables might include purely operational metrics like "speed of placement" or "verification completeness," provided these are not themselves correlated with protected geography.

**How Algorithms Create Disparate Impact:**
Algorithms inadvertently discriminate when they optimize for "efficiency" or "demand" using data that reflects historical segregation. For example, if "high demand" is historically higher in wealthier, predominantly white neighborhoods, an algorithm maximizing fees in "high demand" areas effectively penalizes homes serving minority populations if those areas are deemed "low demand" or priced differently. Even if you charge the *home*, if the fee structure discourages homes from operating in or serving protected communities, the effect is discriminatory.
