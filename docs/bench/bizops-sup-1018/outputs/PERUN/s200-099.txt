**The call:** Proceed with the annual shift only if you secure at least $750k in additional runway or financing before launch; the model predicts a severe liquidity crunch starting in Month 13 where cash reserves deplete rapidly due to the "renewal cliff."

**The numbers:**
*   **Initial Cash Injection:** $2,000,000 (as stated).
*   **Monthly Burn:** $150,000 (ASSUMED constant for this model).
*   **Runway from Injection:** $2,000,000 / $150,000 = **13.3 months** of operating coverage from the lump sum alone.
*   **The Gap Mechanism:**
    *   *Current Monthly Stream (Lost):* 600 customers (60% of 1,000) paying monthly would generate recurring cash. By moving them to annual, you lose this monthly inflow.
    *   *Month 13 Inflow:* Only 85% of the annual cohort renews. If the renewal is also annual upfront, you get a lump sum then. If the renewal drops or delays, the inflow stops.
    *   *Critical Deficit:* Your $2M covers exactly 13.3 months of burn. If renewals in Month 13 are delayed by even 30 days, or if the 15% churn is higher than expected, you hit zero cash before new money arrives.
    *   *Growth Offset:* The remaining 400 monthly customers growing at 5% MoM provide some inflow, but starting from a reduced base ($300k MRR * 40% = $120k initial base), this growth is insufficient to cover the $150k burn immediately without the annual cash.

**What flips it, and the trigger:**
The single input that changes the call is the **timing of the Month 13 renewal collection**.
*   **Trigger Value:** If renewal collections slip beyond Day 15 of Month 13, you face a negative cash balance.
*   **Stop/Pivot Trigger:** If you cannot confirm contract language that mandates payment *on the anniversary date* (not net-30 or net-60), do not execute the full 60% conversion.
*   **Rule of Thumb:** Never rely on a lump sum to cover more than 12 months of burn without a confirmed bridge.
*   **Step this week:** Model a "worst-case" scenario where Month 13 renewals are collected over 60 days instead of instantly; if this breaks your cash balance, consider reducing the discount to 5% to incentivize earlier renewal or stagger the conversion (e.g., convert 30% now, 30% in 6 months).

**Routing note:**
While the math highlights the risk, the enforceability of "payment on anniversary" clauses and the accounting treatment of deferred revenue (ASC 606) for the $2M influx require review by a CPA and legal counsel to ensure you aren't recognizing revenue you haven't earned or violating covenant thresholds with existing lenders.
