**The Call**
Do not raise the price to $14 yet; the math suggests this will destroy value unless you can prove the new price significantly extends user lifetime. With an 8% monthly churn, your current customers stay only ~12.5 months, generating ~$112 in lifetime revenue against a $45 acquisition cost. A price hike that cuts trial conversions by 30% and potentially accelerates churn will likely shrink your total addressable pool faster than the extra $5/month can compensate. Focus immediately on lowering the $45 acquisition cost and reducing the 8% churn before attempting a price increase.

**The Numbers**
*Formula:* LTV = (Price × Gross Margin) / Monthly Churn. (Assuming 100% gross margin for software).
*Current State:* $9 / 0.08 = **$112.50 LTV**.
*Unit Economics:* LTV:CAC = $112.50 / $45 = **2.5x**. Payback period = $45 / $9 = **5 months**.
*Proposed State (Base Assumption):* If price rises to $14 but churn remains 8%, LTV = $14 / 0.08 = **$175**.
*The Volume Hit:* You project a 30% drop in conversion. If you acquire 100 users today, you get 70 tomorrow. Even with higher LTV, your total net revenue growth slows if the volume drop outpaces the LTV gain.
*The Churn Risk (ASSUMED):* If the price hike increases churn from 8% to 10% (a common reaction to >50% price hikes), the new LTV drops to $14 / 0.10 = **$140**. The gain shrinks from $62.50 to only $27.50 per user, which may not offset losing 30% of your new pipeline.

| Scenario | Price | Churn | LTV | LTV:CAC | Verdict |
| :--- | :--- | :--- | :--- | :--- | :--- |
| **Current** | $9 | 8% | $112.50 | 2.5x | Viable but thin |
| **Price Hike (Optimistic)** | $14 | 8% | $175.00 | 3.9x | Strong, *if* churn holds |
| **Price Hike (Realistic)** | $14 | 10% | $140.00 | 3.1x | Marginal gain |
| **Price Hike (Pessimistic)** | $14 | 12% | $116.60 | 2.6x | Failure |

**What Flips It, and the Trigger**
The single input that changes the call is **churn elasticity**. If raising the price increases monthly churn above **9.3%**, your LTV falls below $150, making the risk of a 30% conversion drop unjustifiable.
*Trigger:* Run a split test on 10% of new traffic next week. If the $14 cohort shows a trial-to-paid conversion drop >20% OR a month-2 retention rate lower than the current 92%, abort the price hike.
*Action:* If your current churn is driven by lack of engagement rather than price, consider Y: launching a "annual plan" at $90 (2 months free) to lock in duration before testing a monthly price increase.

**Data Points Needed**
To finalize this decision, you must gather:
1.  **Price Sensitivity Data:** Run a Van Westendorp survey with current trial users to find the "too expensive" threshold before changing live pricing.
2.  **Cohort Retention Curves:** Determine if the 8% churn is constant or if users leave specifically after price renewal reminders.
3.  **Competitor Anchor:** Verify the exact pricing of top 3 K-12 math competitors; if they are at $10, a jump to $14 breaks the category anchor.
