**The Call**
Your shift to leasing likely reclassifies your revenue from a one-time taxable sale of tangible personal property to a recurring taxable lease of tangible personal property in most US states, potentially creating immediate sales tax filing obligations (nexus) in every state where a simulator is physically located. Do not assume you are exempt; treat the presence of hardware as creating a physical nexus trigger and prepare to register in those jurisdictions.

**The Numbers & Mechanism**
*   **Current State:** $10,000/month recurring revenue from 25 units.
*   **Unit Cost:** $40,000 (irrelevant for sales tax classification, relevant for income tax depreciation).
*   **Tax Regime Shift:**
    *   *Sales Model:* Tax collected once at the point of sale on the full transaction value.
    *   *Lease Model:* Tax is typically due on each monthly lease payment. In many states, the lessor (you) owes the tax on the purchase of the equipment (if not exempted via resale certificate) and must collect tax from the lessee on every rental payment.
*   **Illustrative Impact (Assumption: 7% avg blended rate):**
    *   *Old:* Tax collected once on $1M total value (hypothetical).
    *   *New:* ~$700/month in ongoing collection/remittance obligations ($10,000 * 7%), compounding annually, plus potential use-tax liability on the $1M of equipment already deployed if you didn't pay sales tax when manufacturing/buying the parts.

**What Flips It & The Trigger**
*   **The Flip:** The definition of "nexus." Physical presence of your equipment in a state almost universally creates a filing obligation, whereas previous sales might have been shipped from a single warehouse without triggering nexus in the destination state (depending on economic nexus thresholds which you may not have hit with one-off sales).
*   **The Trigger:** The date the first unit was installed in a new state.
*   **Rule of Thumb:** If the hardware sits there, you likely owe filings there.
*   **Next Step:** If you have units in more than one state, consider engaging a sales tax specialist immediately to map unit locations against state-specific "lessor" statutes before your next filing cycle.

**Routing Note (Tier 3 - Binding Determination)**
This is a high-stakes compliance issue involving multi-state nexus and potential retroactive liability. You must consult a CPA or tax attorney specializing in multi-state sales/use tax.
*   **Exact Question:** "Does our fleet of 25 leased simulators create physical nexus in [State X, Y, Z], and do we need to file voluntary disclosure agreements to mitigate penalties for past months where units were deployed but no tax was remitted?"
*   **Time-Sensitive:** Many states have strict deadlines for voluntary disclosure; delay increases penalty exposure.

*Note on Income Tax:* While sales tax treats this as a lease, federal income tax generally still views this as business income, but the *character* (ordinary income vs. capital gain) and depreciation schedules (Section 179 vs. MACRS) will change significantly. A CPA must determine if you qualify for "true lease" tax treatment versus a "conditional sale."
