**The Call**
Do not recognize the $675,000 implementation fees immediately. Because you have stated the implementation services are **not distinct** from the software license, GAAP (ASC 606) requires you to combine them into a single performance obligation and amortize the total contract value ratably over the three-year term. Attempting to decouple them to boost Series B revenue creates a high risk of audit qualification or restatement, which would likely derail the fundraising round entirely.

**The Numbers**
*Formula:* Annual Recognized Revenue = (Total Contract Value) / (Contract Term in Years).
*Inputs:* License ($250k/year × 3 years = $750k) + Implementation ($75k one-time) = $825k Total Contract Value per deal.
*Assumption (Base Case):* All three deals are identical 3-year contracts with non-distinct implementation.

| Scenario | Total Contract Value (3 Deals) | Recognition Period | Annual Revenue Impact |
| :--- | :--- | :--- | :--- |
| **Your Proposal (Immediate)** | $675,000 (Impl only) | Year 1 | **$675,000** (Plus $750k license amortization) |
| **Compliant (Amortized)** | $2,475,000 (Total) | 3 Years | **$825,000** (Total annualized) |

*Analysis:* While immediate recognition spikes Year 1 cash-basis-looking revenue by $675k, the compliant method recognizes $275k per year per deal ($825k / 3). Over three years, the total revenue is identical; only the timing differs. Your current cash runway is **3.75 months** ($150k cash / $40k monthly burn), unrelated to the revenue recognition method which is a non-cash accounting entry.

**What Flips It, and the Trigger**
The single input that changes this call is the **distinctness** of the implementation service.
*Trigger:* If the customer can benefit from the implementation service on its own (or with readily available resources) AND the service is separately identifiable from the license (not highly customized or integrated), you may separate the obligations.
*Rule of Thumb:* If the implementation involves significant customization, modification, or integration specific to your software such that the license is useless without it, they are combined.
*Action:* If your Statement of Work (SOW) describes "generic setup" rather than "custom integration," review the contract language with your auditor this week. If the work is truly distinct, you can allocate a portion of the transaction price to the completed service. However, based on your admission that they are "not distinct," the trigger is not met.
