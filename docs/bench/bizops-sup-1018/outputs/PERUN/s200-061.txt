**The Call:** Present a three-tiered TAM in your pitch deck: a core "Strict Serverless" number (event-driven only), an expanded "Cloud-Native Compute" number (including containers), and a "Serviceable Obtainable Market" (SOM) based on your current trajectory. This approach satisfies conservative due diligence while demonstrating awareness of the adjacent opportunity to aggressive VCs, turning the definition ambiguity into a strategic narrative rather than a data flaw.

**The Numbers:**
You cannot calculate a single definitive TAM without fixing the boundary, so you must compute three distinct figures using your internal data as the anchor.
*   **Formula:** $TAM = (\text{Global Daily Function Invocations in Segment}) \times (\text{Average Price Per Million}) \times 365$.
*   **Your Data:** 4M daily functions, $0.02/1M requests.
    *   *Current Annual Recurring Revenue (ARR) Proxy:* $4,000,000 \times 365 \times \$0.02 / 1,000,000 = \$29,200$ (This is your baseline, not TAM).
*   **Scenario A (Strict Definition):** Use analyst reports limiting scope to event-driven, stateless compute.
    *   *Input:* Global volume estimate for strict serverless (e.g., 100B daily invocations).
    *   *Result:* $100B \times 365 \times \$0.02 / 1M = \$730M$ (Illustrative low bound).
*   **Scenario B (Expanded Definition):** Include containerized microservices and FaaS hybrids.
    *   *Input:* Global volume estimate including containers (e.g., 600B daily invocations).
    *   *Result:* $600B \times 365 \times \$0.02 / 1M = \$4.38B$ (Illustrative high bound).
*   **Assumption:** The global invocation volumes above are **ASSUMED** based on typical market ratios; you must replace these with specific data points from the competitor sheets you possess, labeling the source for each tier.

**What Flips It, and The Trigger:**
The credibility of your Series A hinges on the **conversion rate of container users to serverless patterns**. If your data shows that >20% of your target enterprise customers are actively migrating container workloads to event-driven architectures, the "Expanded" TAM becomes your primary narrative.
*   **Trigger:** If your internal logs or competitor analysis show a year-over-year decline in standalone container growth vs. serverless growth by Q3 2024, pivot the deck to lead with the "Strict" TAM as the immediate prize and the "Expanded" TAM as the long-term optionality.
*   **Rule of Thumb:** Investors discount TAMs that rely on capturing 100% of a broad definition; they prefer a smaller TAM where you can realistically capture 10-20%.
*   **Step this week:** Segment your 300 active developers by workload type (pure event-driven vs. containerized). If >30% are hybrid, build the "Cloud-Native" narrative; if <10%, stick to "Strict Serverless" to avoid appearing unfocused.

**Routing Note:**
While market sizing is strategic, ensure your specific revenue recognition and growth metrics used to derive the SOM align with US GAAP before sharing with auditors or lead investors; consult your CPA if your billing model for hybrid workloads creates ambiguity in recurring revenue classification.
