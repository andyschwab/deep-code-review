**The Call**
Classifying the nutrient pods as a "subscription" versus a "recurring order" generally **does not change** your sales tax collection obligation, rate, or filing frequency in California, Texas, New York, Florida, or Illinois. In all five states, tangible personal property (the pods) remains taxable regardless of billing cadence. The "digital management" of the subscription does not trigger different nexus rules for the physical goods; nexus is driven by your total sales volume or transaction count into the state, not the billing mechanism.

**The Numbers & Rules**
Since you provided no sales volume figures, I cannot compute your specific nexus status. Instead, here are the **economic nexus thresholds** (the dollar amounts that force registration) for remote sellers in your target states. These apply to your total taxable sales (hardware + pods), not the subscription label.

| State | Economic Nexus Threshold (Remote Seller) | Taxability of Pods | Effect of "Subscription" Label |
| :--- | :--- | :--- | :--- |
| **California** | $500,000 in sales (no transaction count) | Taxable (Tangible Property) | **None.** Rate and filing frequency depend on total liability, not billing type. |
| **Texas** | $500,000 in sales (no transaction count) | Taxable (Tangible Property) | **None.** Recurring charges are treated as continuous sales of tangible property. |
| **New York** | $500,000 in sales **AND** 100 transactions | Taxable (Tangible Property) | **None.** However, hitting 100 transactions is easier with monthly pods; this may trigger nexus *sooner* than hardware-only sales, but the *rate* remains unchanged. |
| **Florida** | $100,000 in sales (no transaction count) | Taxable (Tangible Property) | **None.** No distinction for subscription vs. recurring orders for tangible goods. |
| **Illinois** | $100,000 in sales **OR** 200 transactions | Taxable (Tangible Property) | **None.** Like NY, the transaction count (200) could be hit faster with monthly pods, triggering registration earlier, but tax rates remain static. |

**What Flips It, and the Trigger**
The one input that changes your compliance burden is **total annual revenue** and **transaction count** into each specific state.
*   **Trigger:** If your cumulative sales into New York exceed $500k AND you process 100 separate charges (e.g., 100 customers x 1 month, or fewer customers over multiple months), you must register.
*   **Pivot:** If you label the pods a "subscription" but fail to count each monthly charge as a separate "transaction" for nexus thresholds in NY or IL, you may under-register.
*   **Action Step:** If your current monthly pod revenue in any of these states exceeds $10k, consider running a nexus exposure analysis this week to see if you are close to the thresholds.

**Routing Note**
While the general rule is that tangible goods are taxable regardless of billing frequency, the definition of a "transaction" for nexus purposes (especially in NY and IL) and the specific sourcing rules (origin-based vs. destination-based) for your specific warehouse locations require a binding determination. Bring your exact sales logs and warehouse addresses to a **CPA or sales tax attorney** to confirm your registration deadlines and filing frequencies (monthly vs. quarterly), as these depend on your total tax liability dollar amount, which varies by jurisdiction.
