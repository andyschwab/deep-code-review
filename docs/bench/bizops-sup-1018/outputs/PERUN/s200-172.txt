**The Call:** Your current flow—holding funds in a merchant account before disbursing to tutors—almost certainly triggers money transmitter licensing (MTL) obligations in California, New York, and Texas, regardless of the 24-hour hold time. You must cease this specific fund-flow model immediately or engage specialized fintech counsel to structure a compliant exemption or partnership.

**The Numbers & Mechanics:**
The regulatory trigger is not your commission rate (15%) or volume ($240k/mo), but the legal definition of "receiving money for transmission."
*   **Your Model:** Parent pays Platform → Platform holds funds → Platform pays Tutor.
*   **The Risk Factor:** By taking custody, even briefly, you become the principal obligated to pay the tutor, rather than a pure agent.
*   **The "24-Hour" Myth:** There is no universal federal or state exemption based solely on a <24-hour hold period. While some states have narrow exemptions for "agent of the payee" models, these require specific contractual language and operational setups that standard merchant accounts often do not satisfy by default.
*   **Jurisdiction Exposure:**
    *   **New York:** Strictest regime. Operating without a BitLicense or MTL while transmitting value is a criminal offense. No de minimis exemption exists for your volume.
    *   **California:** Requires a license if you receive money for transmission. The "agent of the payee" exemption is narrow and fact-specific; relying on it without counsel is high-risk.
    *   **Texas:** Requires registration if you receive money for transmission. The "monetary value" definition is broad.
    *   **Delaware:** As your home state, you likely need a license here too if the transmission originates or is controlled from within the state.

**What Flips It, and The Trigger:**
The single input that changes the call is **custody**. If you can restructure so funds move directly from Parent to Tutor (with your commission sliced via a separate mechanism or a compliant payment facilitator model), you may avoid MTL.
*   **Trigger:** The next transaction where a parent pays *you* rather than the tutor directly.
*   **Rule of Thumb:** If your balance sheet ever reflects a liability to pay a tutor (even for 1 hour), you are transmitting.
*   **Immediate Step:** If you cannot verify a written "agent of the payee" opinion from counsel for your specific merchant agreement, consider pausing direct disbursements and switching to a "PayFac" (Payment Facilitator) model via a provider like Stripe Connect or PayPal for Platforms, which absorbs the MTL burden under their master license.
