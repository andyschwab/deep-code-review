**The Call**
Adopt the 15% variable fee model but implement a "net revenue" recognition policy with an explicit refund reserve; do not defer all revenue, but do not recognize gross fees without an offset. This approach satisfies auditors for Series A while capturing upside, provided you formalize the reserve calculation methodology immediately.

**The Numbers**
*Current Baseline:* £3,000 flat × 60 placements = £180,000/quarter.
*Proposed Model (Base Case):* £6,000 avg fee × 60 placements = £360,000/quarter (gross).

*Volatility Analysis (20% Refund Scenario):*
Assumption: 20% of placements trigger a partial refund. We assume a "partial refund" implies returning 50% of the fee if a candidate leaves within the guarantee period (a standard industry clause).
*   **Gross Revenue Recognized:** £360,000.
*   **Refund Liability (Reserve):** 20% (incidence) × 50% (refund depth) × £360,000 = £36,000.
*   **Net Revenue Reported:** £360,000 - £36,000 = **£324,000**.

*Volatility Range:*
If refund incidence spikes to 30% (high risk): Reserve = £54,000 → Net Revenue = £306,000.
If refund incidence drops to 10% (low risk): Reserve = £18,000 → Net Revenue = £342,000.
*Result:* Monthly reported revenue fluctuates between £102k and £114k (based on quarterly run rates), rather than swinging wildly between £0 and full fee.

*Reserve Requirement:*
Yes, you must establish a **Refund Liability Reserve** (often grouped with provisions for doubtful accounts or specific contract liabilities). Under UK GAAP (FRS 102) and IFRS 15 (likely relevant for Series A investors), you cannot recognize revenue you expect to return. You must estimate this variable consideration at the contract start and reduce recognized revenue immediately, rather than waiting for the refund to occur.

**What Flips It, and The Trigger**
The single input that changes the accounting treatment is the **predictability of your historical refund data**.
*   *Trigger:* If your historical data shows refund rates varying by more than ±10% quarter-over-quarter, auditors may force you to defer 100% of the revenue until the 3-month guarantee period expires, destroying your current-quarter top-line growth.
*   *Rule of Thumb:* If (Standard Deviation of Refund Rate) > 5%, move to full deferral.
*   *Action:* If your last 3 quarters of refund data are stable, consider implementing the reserve model immediately; if volatile, consider extending the guarantee period to 6 months to smooth the data before switching pricing.
