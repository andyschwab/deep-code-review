**The Call:** Yes, renting commercial kitchen space in Austin, Denver, and Seattle almost certainly creates a corporate income tax and sales tax filing obligation (nexus) in Texas, Colorado, and Washington, regardless of your revenue level or the number of hours rented. You must register and file in these states immediately; do not wait for a threshold to be met.

**The Numbers & Rules:**
*   **Physical Nexus Rule:** In state taxation, "physical presence" is generally binary, not proportional. Leasing commercial real estate, even for limited hours (6 hours/day), constitutes a physical footprint.
    *   **Texas:** Leasing tangible property (kitchen equipment/space) creates nexus. Texas has no corporate income tax but imposes a **Franchise Tax**. With $200k revenue, you are likely below the "no tax due" threshold (approx. $1.23M for 2024), but you **must still file** a "No Tax Due" report annually to remain compliant.
    *   **Colorado:** Physical presence creates income tax nexus. Colorado has a corporate income tax rate of 4.55%. You will likely owe tax on the apportioned income generated from Colorado sales.
    *   **Washington:** No corporate income tax, but a **Business & Occupation (B&O) Tax** applies to gross receipts. Physical presence triggers this. With $200k revenue, you may owe tax depending on the apportionment of sales to WA, and you must register.
*   **Economic Nexus:** This is a secondary trigger based on sales volume (e.g., $100k or 200 transactions). Since you already have physical nexus via the leases, economic nexus thresholds are irrelevant for establishing the *obligation* to file, though they may impact sales tax collection rules specifically.
*   **Assumption:** We assume the rental agreement is a standard commercial lease or license to use space. Even a "shared kitchen" agreement typically counts as physical presence if you have exclusive access to the space during your rented window.

**What Flips It & The Trigger:**
*   **The Flip:** The only factor that would negate this obligation is if your legal counsel determines your agreement is strictly a "service contract" where you have no possessory interest in the space (i.e., you are merely hiring a baker in their kitchen, not renting the kitchen yourself). This is rare for commercial kitchen rentals.
*   **Trigger:** The date you signed the lease or moved equipment into the spaces. Penalties and interest accrue from the date nexus was established, not from the date you realized it.
*   **Rule of Thumb:** Physical presence > 0 days = Filing obligation. Revenue thresholds only determine *how much* tax is owed, not *if* you must file.
*   **This Week's Step:** If you have signed leases, consider halting shipments from those new hubs until a tax professional confirms your registration status, or immediately gather the lease agreements to quantify potential back-filing liabilities.
