**The Call**
Do not recognize the $250,000 as immediate revenue; under standard accounting principles (US GAAP), you must amortize these upfront fees over the term of the agreement (or the period you provide substantial services), while recognizing the 4% royalty as earned each month. Recognizing the full amount upfront creates a high risk of material misstatement and audit adjustment.

**The Numbers**
*Formula:* Annual Amortized Revenue = Total Upfront Fees / Contract Term (Years).
*Your Figures:* $250,000 total fees; 10-year term.
*Assumption (ASSUMED):* The contract term is exactly 10 years with no early termination clauses that shorten the service period, and you provide no significant initial services that would alter the recognition period.

| Scenario | Method | Year 1 Revenue Impact | Remaining Liability (Deferred Revenue) |
| :--- | :--- | :--- | :--- |
| **Correct Approach** | Amortization (Straight-line) | **$25,000** | $225,000 |
| **Risk Scenario** | Immediate Recognition | $250,000 | $0 |

*Note:* The 4% royalty on gross revenue is recognized separately in the month it is earned, regardless of the upfront fee treatment.

**What Flips It, and the Trigger**
The single input that changes the amortization period is the **duration of substantial services** you provide. If your agreement requires you to perform significant initial setup, training, or site selection services that conclude before the 10-year mark, you may need to recognize the revenue over that shorter service period rather than the full contract term.
*Trigger:* If your initial service obligations end within 2 years, consult your accountant to accelerate recognition to that shorter window.
*Rule of Thumb:* Upfront fees are generally deferred until the franchisor has substantially performed all material services.
*Step this week:* If your contract lists specific "initial services" with completion dates, consider drafting a memo to your accountant detailing those milestones to verify the correct amortization schedule.
