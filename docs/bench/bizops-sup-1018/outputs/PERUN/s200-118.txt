**What Flips It, and the Trigger**
The deciding factor is not the 25% (or 43%) revenue share, but whether the IRS views "charging employers for access to candidates" as *substantially related* to your charitable purpose of aiding people with disabilities.
*   **The Rule of Thumb:** If the activity contributes importantly to the exempt purpose (beyond just raising funds), it is Related Business Income (RBI) and tax-free. If it is merely a fundraising mechanism using exempt assets, it is Unrelated Business Income (UBI).
*   **The Trigger:** If the IRS determines the service competes with commercial job boards without a distinct charitable methodology (e.g., specialized advocacy, accommodation consulting included in the fee), the entire $100,000 becomes UBI.
*   **Threshold Reality:** There is no fixed percentage (e.g., "under 20% is safe"). However, if UBI exceeds **$1,000**, you must file Form 990-T and pay corporate tax rates on that portion. The risk to *exempt status* arises only if UBI becomes "substantial" relative to overall activities (often interpreted as >50% of time/resources or revenue, but fact-specific).
