**The Call**
Do not rely on your current "independent influencer" classification to avoid payroll liabilities; the high degree of control inherent in a managed coaching platform (scheduling, method standards, and integration into your core product) creates a significant risk that the IRS and state agencies will reclassify these coaches as employees, triggering back taxes and penalties that could exhaust your $50,000 runway.

**The Numbers**
*Formula:* Potential Liability = (Gross Coach Payments × Employer Tax Rate) + Penalties + Interest.
*User Figures:* $80,000 monthly revenue implies ~$114,000 in monthly gross coach payouts (since you retain 30%, $80k / 0.30 = $266k total GMV; wait, correction: if revenue is your 30% cut, total GMV is $266,666. Coach share is $186,666/month. If revenue is total GMV, coach share is $56,000. Let's assume your $80k is *your* net revenue based on "we pay them 70% of the session fee... our monthly revenue is $80k" usually implying net, but often founders mean GMV. Let's bracket both).

*Assumption A (Revenue = Net Take):* Total GMV = $266,666. Coach Payout = $186,666/mo.
*Assumption B (Revenue = GMV):* Total GMV = $80,000. Coach Payout = $56,000/mo.
*Employer Tax Rate (ASSUMED):* ~7.65% (FICA) + FUTA/SUTA estimates (~2-5% depending on state). Total estimated burden: 10-13%.

*Results (Monthly Exposure):*
*   Scenario A (Net Rev): $186,666 × 12% ≈ **$22,400/month** in unremitted taxes.
*   Scenario B (GMV): $56,000 × 12% ≈ **$6,720/month** in unremitted taxes.

*Runway Impact:* With $50,000 cash, a single adverse determination covering just 3-6 months of Scenario A liabilities plus penalties (which can equal the tax due) would insolvent the company immediately. Doubling coach count next quarter doubles this exposure.

**What Flips It, and the Trigger**
The single input that flips this from "low risk" to "certain reclassification" is **behavioral control**. If you set specific coaching hours, mandate specific teaching methods, provide the training materials, or discipline coaches for how they coach, you fail the "Independent Contractor" test regardless of what your contract says.
*Trigger:* The moment you enforce a standardized curriculum or fixed shift schedule for coaches.
*Rule of Thumb:* If the coach looks like an employee to the gamer (wearing your brand, following your script, booked via your rigid slots), the IRS sees an employee.
*Step this week:* If you currently dictate *how* or *when* coaches work, consider pausing new coach onboarding until a professional reviews your operating agreement.

**Routing Note**
Worker classification is a binding legal determination (Tier 3) dependent on specific facts under IRS Common Law rules (20-factor test) and stricter state tests (like California's ABC Test). You must engage an employment tax attorney to audit your actual operational practices against your contracts and file Form SS-8 if necessary to get a formal determination before expanding.
