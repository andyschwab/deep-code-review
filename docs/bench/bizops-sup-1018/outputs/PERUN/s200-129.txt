**What Flips It, and the Trigger:**
The single input that changes the call is the **specific phrasing of the "lifestyle recommendations."**
*   **Trigger:** If any output suggests diagnosis, treatment, prevention, or mitigation of a specific disease (e.g., "Take supplement X to lower cancer risk" vs. "General wellness tip"), regulators will likely classify it as medical advice.
*   **Rule of Thumb:** The FDA and state medical boards distinguish "wellness" from "medical advice" based on **intended use** and **claim specificity**. General population statistics are often wellness; personalized actionable directives are often medical advice.
*   **Step this week:** If your current reports contain any verb implying a clinical outcome (prevent, treat, cure, diagnose), consider pausing sales and engaging counsel immediately before processing user #2,001.

**Regulatory Criteria & Penalties (Tier 2 Explanation):**
Regulators (FDA, FTC, State Medical Boards) typically apply a "totality of the circumstances" test. Key criteria include:
1.  **Personalization:** Does the report analyze individual genetic markers to give specific advice?
2.  **Actionability:** Does it recommend a specific action (drug, diet, test) tied to a health outcome?
3.  **Context:** Does the marketing imply clinical utility?
*Potential Penalties:* If deemed unlicensed practice of medicine or an unapproved medical device, penalties can include cease-and-desist orders, forced recall of all reports, civil fines (often tens of thousands per violation), and in severe cases, criminal charges against founders. The FTC may also pursue actions for deceptive trade practices if disclaimers do not match the product's actual function.
