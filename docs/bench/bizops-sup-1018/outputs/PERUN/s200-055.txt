**The Call:** Your unit economics have broken; at $7.50 variable cost against $15 revenue, your contribution margin has collapsed from 73% to 50%, slashing monthly profit from $6,000 to a precarious $1,500. You must immediately introduce a tiered pricing model based on storage volume (e.g., a "Standard" tier up to 60MB and a "Pro" tier for larger repos) rather than waiting for a specific break-even size, as your current flat rate subsidizes heavy users at the expense of runway.

**The Numbers:**
*Formula:* Monthly Profit = (Price - Variable Cost) × Repositories - Fixed OpEx.
*Current Reality (Base Case):* ($15.00 - $7.50) × 2,000 - $18,000 = **$1,500 profit**.
*Previous Reality:* ($15.00 - $4.00) × 2,000 - $18,000 = $4,000 profit.
*Assumption (High Risk):* If average repo size grows another 33% (to ~133MB) and costs scale linearly to $10/repo, profit turns negative: ($15 - $10) × 2,000 - $18,000 = **-$8,000 loss**.
*Runway:* At the current $1,500 profit, you are safe. However, if costs rise to $8/repo next month, you burn $2,000/month. With $110,000 cash, your runway drops from "indefinite" to **55 months** under a burn scenario, but this ignores the risk of churn during price changes.

**What Flips It, and The Trigger:**
The single input that flips your call from "manageable" to "critical" is the **variable cost per repository**.
*Trigger Value:* **$8.00 per repo**. At this cost, your contribution margin ($7) barely covers fixed costs if volume dips slightly.
*Computed Trigger Date:* If repository sizes continue doubling every 6 months (as implied by your recent data), you hit the $8.00 cost threshold in approximately **45 days**.
*Rule of Thumb:* Never let variable costs exceed 40% of your price point in SaaS; you are currently at 50%.
*Action This Week:* If your average repo size exceeds 60MB in your next data pull, consider grandfathering current users for 90 days while launching a "Pro" tier at $25/month for repos over 75MB.
