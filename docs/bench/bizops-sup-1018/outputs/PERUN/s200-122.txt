**The Call**
Reject the lease-only pivot as structured; it collapses your immediate cash inflow by ~82% per unit, shortening your runway from ~6.5 months to under 3 months unless you secure upfront debt financing. To delay Series A, you must retain a high-upfront component (e.g., a hybrid model) or the leasing model will force an earlier raise, not a later one.

**The Numbers**
*Current State (Outright Sales):*
- Contribution Margin per Unit: $2,800 (Price) - $950 (Cost) = **$1,850**.
- Monthly Cash Inflow (150 units): 150 × $1,850 = **$277,500**.
- Net Monthly Burn: $185,000 (OpEx) - $277,500 (Inflow) = **-$92,500** (Net Cash Gain).
- Runway: $1.2M / -$92.5k = **Infinity** (You are currently cash-flow positive on an operating basis).

*Proposed State (Leasing Only):*
- Month 1 Cash Inflow per Unit: $500 (Setup) - $950 (Cost) = **-$450** (Immediate cash loss per acquisition).
- Recurring Margin per Unit: $120 (Revenue) - $0 (variable cost assumed) = **$120**.
- Month 1 Total Cash Flow: (150 × -$450) + (150 × $120) - $185,000 OpEx = -$67,500 + $18,000 - $185,000 = **-$234,500**.
- Runway: $1.2M / $234,500 = **~5.1 months**.
- *Note:* This ignores the 4% churn which reduces recurring revenue monthly, worsening the burn over time.

*Break-Even Analysis:*
Leasing only becomes superior to outright sales for *runway extension* if the Net Present Value (NPV) of the lease exceeds the outright margin *and* the immediate cash drag is funded. However, for immediate runway (cash in bank), leasing never beats outright sales unless the setup fee covers the hardware cost ($950+) or you raise debt.
To match the current $277,500 monthly cash surplus:
Required Setup Fee ≈ $950 (Cost) + ($1,850 Margin / 150 units) → You need ~$1,233 upfront per unit to match current cash generation, assuming no recurring value.
If relying on recurring revenue to justify the dip, you need a financing facility to cover the -$450/unit initial hole. Without debt, the break-even point for *cash flow positivity* requires reducing customer acquisition to ~38 units/month (to stop the bleeding), which contradicts your growth goal.

**What Flips It, and the Trigger**
The single input that changes this call is **access to non-dilutive debt financing** (equipment leasing lines).
- **Trigger:** If you can secure a credit facility covering 100% of hardware costs ($950/unit) within 30 days, the model flips to viable.
- **Rule of Thumb:** If debt service + churn > $120/mo, the unit economics fail.
- **Step:** If you cannot term-sheet a debt line by next Friday, consider a "Rent-to-Own" hybrid with a $1,500 upfront payment to preserve cash flow.

**Routing Note**
Presenting a lease model without a committed debt facility to fund the hardware inventory is a securities and banking compliance risk; consult a CFO to structure the Special Purpose Vehicle (SPV) or debt line required to separate hardware capex from your operating cash, and verify if this pivot triggers new revenue recognition rules (ASC 606) that could alter your audited financials for Series A.
