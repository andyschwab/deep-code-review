**The Call:** Retain the per-session model (£12) as your primary track for insurance reimbursement; a flat unlimited subscription (£45) will likely be rejected by UK and German private insurers as a non-billable "wellness" bundle rather than a medically necessary treatment.

**The Numbers:**
*   **Per-Session Revenue:** £12/session.
*   **Variable Cost:** £0.50/session.
*   **Contribution Margin:** £11.50/session (95.8%).
*   **Break-even Usage for Subscription:** At £45/month, a user must consume **4 sessions** to match the revenue of the per-session model (£45 / £12 = 3.75).
*   **Risk Assumption (ASSUMED):** If the average subscribed user consumes >4 sessions/month, your margin compresses rapidly against the fixed £45 cap while server costs scale linearly.
*   **Reimbursement Sensitivity:** Insurers typically reimburse discrete, coded events. A bundled fee lacks the granularity to map to specific diagnostic codes (ICD-10/11) required for medical necessity validation.

**What Flips It, and the Trigger:**
The deciding factor is the **average session frequency per user**.
*   **Trigger:** If your data shows the average active user completes **fewer than 4 sessions per month**, the subscription model generates higher top-line revenue *if* you can secure a self-pay market. However, for *insurance* purposes, the trigger is binary: if the product is bundled, it is generally categorized as wellness.
*   **Rule of Thumb:** In the UK (private health) and Germany (private Zusatzversicherung), reimbursement requires itemized billing tied to a specific therapeutic intervention. Bundling dilutes this link.
*   **Action This Week:** If >60% of your current users average <4 sessions/month, consider a hybrid: keep per-session for insurance claims and offer a separate, self-pay "wellness" subscription for unlimited access, clearly firewalled from reimbursement claims.

**Routing Note (High-Stakes/Regulatory):**
Determining the optimal structure for insurance approval **requires a licensed healthcare compliance expert** specializing in UK (CQC/Private Medical Insurance standards) and German (SGB V/Private Insurance Association) regulations. General market data cannot safely predict admissibility because:
1.  **Medical Necessity Documentation:** For per-session, you need clinical notes, therapist credentials, diagnosis codes, and session duration logs per event. For subscriptions, you would need to prove every interaction within the bundle was medically necessary, which is administratively nearly impossible under current frameworks.
2.  **Jurisdiction Specifics:** Germany's strict separation of "Heilmittel" (remedies) vs. wellness services and the UK's private insurer coding requirements are binding legal determinations, not arithmetic ones. Engage counsel to define the billing codes before finalizing the pricing model.
