**The Call**
Recognize the full $4,500 per bike as revenue at the point of sale (delivery), provided the sale meets standard criteria for transfer of control. The fact that you are subsidizing the interest via a third-party lender does not defer the revenue recognition of the bike itself; instead, the cost of that subsidy is recorded as an expense (likely marketing or interest expense) in the same period.

**The Numbers**
*Formula:* Total Q1 Revenue = (Units Sold × Sale Price) + (Deferred Revenue, if any).
*Your Figures:* 200 units × $4,500 = $900,000.
*Assumption (ASSUMED):* Control of the bicycles transfers to the customer upon delivery or pickup in Q1, and collection is probable.
*Result:* $900,000 recognized in Q1. $0 deferred.
*Expense Impact:* The interest subsidy paid to the lender is an expense recognized in Q1, reducing net income but not top-line revenue. If the subsidy rate were, for example, 5% annually on the financed amount, that cost is booked immediately as the cost of acquiring the sale, not amortized against revenue.

**What Flips It, and the Trigger**
The single input that changes this call is **transfer of control**. If your terms state that title or risk of loss does not pass to the customer until the final financing payment is made (unlikely for a standard retail bike sale), you must defer revenue.
*Trigger:* Review your sales contract terms. If "title passes on delivery," book the $900,000 now. If "title passes on final payment," defer the entire amount until month 12.
*Rule of Thumb:* In retail manufacturing, revenue is recognized when the product is delivered and the customer has the ability to direct the use of the asset, regardless of when cash is fully collected or financing costs are settled.
*Step:* If your contracts confirm delivery equals transfer of control, consider updating your Q1 forecast to show $900,000 in revenue and a separate line item for "Financing Subsidy Expense" to reflect the true cost of the promotion.

**Routing Note**
While the general rule under ASC 606 (US GAAP) and IFRS 15 is clear on transfer of control, the specific classification of the subsidy (contra-revenue vs. marketing expense) and the accounting for the financial guarantee with the lender requires a definitive review by your CPA. Bring your draft financing agreement and sales terms to your accountant to confirm the expense classification before finalizing the board deck.
