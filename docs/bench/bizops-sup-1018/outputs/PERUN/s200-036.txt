**The Call:** Do not proceed with the $15,000 certification investment based solely on the current numbers; the projected margin expansion covers the fixed cost but leaves an unacceptably thin buffer for risk, and the assumption that 100% of customers will accept a 20% price hike purely for a label is unverified.

**The Numbers:**
*   **Current Unit Contribution:** $35 (Price) - $12 (New Cost) = **$23**.
*   **Proposed Unit Contribution:** $42 (New Price) - $12 (New Cost) = **$30**.
*   **Margin Lift per Unit:** $7.
*   **Break-Even Volume for Certification:** $15,000 (Fixed Cost) / $7 (Lift) = **2,143 units**.
*   **Annual Revenue Impact (Base Case):** If all 5,000 customers buy one unit/year at the new price, extra gross profit = 5,000 * $7 = **$35,000**.
*   **Net Gain after Fixed Cost:** $35,000 - $15,000 = **$20,000**.

*Assumptions (Marked ASSUMED):*
1.  **Retention Rate:** ASSUMED 100% of 5,000 customers remain at $42. (If retention drops to 85%, volume = 4,250; Gain = $29,750 - $15,000 = $14,750).
2.  **Purchase Frequency:** ASSUMED 1 unit/customer/year.
3.  **Certification Timing:** ASSUMED immediate price realization within the 12-month runway.

**What Flips It, and the Trigger:**
The single input that flips this from "risky" to "go" is **Price Elasticity of Demand**. If more than **43%** of your customers (2,143 units) refuse to buy at $42, you fail to recover the certification fee.
*   **Trigger Rule:** If a pre-launch survey or A/B test indicates willingness to pay below $40 for >30% of your list, **stop** the certification spend.
*   **Actionable Step:** This week, email 500 loyal customers with a "concept test" (not a sale) offering the certified product at $42 vs. non-certified at $35. If conversion on the $42 option is below 60%, do not file for certification yet.
