**The Call**
Do not sign binding agreements or submit formal bids in these five states until you complete a jurisdiction-specific compliance review; while general vendor registration is a manageable administrative task, public school procurement rules and minor data privacy statutes are binding legal constraints that vary significantly by state and district, making professional counsel mandatory before market entry.

**The Numbers**
There are no unit economics to compute here, as the risk is binary: a contract is either valid and payable, or void and uncollectible.
*   **Potential Revenue:** 200 schools × $2,500 = $500,000 (Gross Pipeline).
*   **Risk Exposure:** 100% of the pipeline if contracts are invalidated due to procurement non-compliance.
*   **Assumption (ASSUMED):** You assume standard commercial terms apply; this is the critical error. Public entities cannot waive statutory bidding thresholds or privacy mandates.
*   **Result:** Without legal validation, the realized revenue is $0. With proper structuring, the net revenue is $500,000 minus legal setup costs.

**What Flips It, and The Trigger**
The single input that changes the outcome is the **procurement threshold** in each specific district.
*   **The Trigger:** If a district's informal bid threshold is below $2,500 (common in large districts like NYC or Chicago-adjacent areas), your direct sale attempt without a formal bid process is voidable.
*   **The Rule of Thumb:** Never assume a price point is "small enough" to bypass bidding; thresholds range from $5,000 to $50,000 depending on the state and district size.
*   **Actionable Step:** If you have a signed Letter of Intent from a school, consider pausing execution to verify that school's specific "small purchase" limit before invoicing.

**Routing Note: Mandatory vs. Researchable**
You must split your next steps into two tracks:

1.  **Do It Yourself (General Research):**
    *   **Vendor Registration:** Most states (NY, IL, OH, GA, OR) have a central vendor portal (e.g., NY State Vendor Portal, Georgia Gateway). Registering here is administrative and can be done internally.
    *   **General Privacy Baselines:** Reviewing FERPA (federal) requirements is safe for internal review.

2.  **Hire a Specialist (Non-Negotiable):**
    *   **State-Specific Minor Data Laws:** You need counsel to interpret **New York's Ed Law 2-d**, **Illinois' SOPPA**, and **Oregon's ORS 336.635**. These laws dictate specific data residency, deletion, and breach notification terms that, if missing from your contract, can block adoption or trigger liability. A generic "commercial" privacy policy will likely fail these tests.
    *   **Procurement Validity:** An education law specialist must confirm whether your $2,500 price point triggers a formal bid in the specific districts you are targeting. In New York City, for example, rules differ from upstate districts. Signing without this confirmation risks non-payment even after service delivery.
    *   **Contract Terms:** Public entities often require specific indemnification, insurance limits, and termination clauses that differ from university contracts.

**Summary of State-Specific Flags**
*   **New York:** High risk. Strict data privacy (Ed Law 2-d) and complex NYC-specific procurement rules.
*   **Illinois:** High risk. SOPPA requires specific operator agreements; non-compliance bars contracts.
*   **Ohio/GA/OR:** Moderate risk. State vendor registration is key, but district-level bidding thresholds vary wildly.

Engage a local education law firm in one representative state (preferably NY or IL due to complexity) to create a template that can be adapted for the others, rather than attempting to navigate five distinct legal regimes alone.
