**The Call:** Proceed with extreme caution; opening a staffed buying office in Quito creates a high probability of establishing a "Permanent Establishment" (PE) in Ecuador, which would subject the *profits attributed to that office* (not your entire global profit) to Ecuadorian corporate income tax. You must engage a local Ecuadorian tax counsel before signing a lease or hiring staff to structure the office's mandate narrowly.

**The Numbers & Risk Profile:**
The $120,000 annual operating cost does not directly trigger PE status (PE is based on *activity* and *presence*, not spend), but it confirms you have a significant, fixed physical presence.
*   **Tax Exposure Formula:** Ecuadorian Tax Liability = (Attributable Profit to Quito Office) × (Ecuadorian Corporate Tax Rate).
*   **Attributable Profit:** Typically calculated as the revenue generated by the office's activities minus its direct expenses (the $120k). If the office only performs "preparatory or auxiliary" activities, attributable profit may be zero. If it negotiates contracts or concludes sales, profit attribution becomes complex and significant.
*   **Assumption (UNVERIFIED):** Ecuador's standard corporate income tax rate is approximately 25% (confirm current rate with counsel).
*   **Illustrative Figure:** If the office is deemed to generate $500,000 in attributable profit, the potential tax exposure is ~$125,000 annually, effectively doubling your operational cost.

**What Flips It, and The Trigger:**
The single input that flips this from "low risk" to "certain PE" is **contract conclusion authority**.
*   **Trigger:** If your three local staff members have the authority to *negotiate and conclude* contracts for the sale of beans or the purchase of services on behalf of CocoaCraft US, you have a PE.
*   **Safe Harbor (General Rule):** Staff limited to "preparatory or auxiliary" activities—such as quality control inspection, market research, information gathering, or maintaining a stock of goods for delivery *by the foreign enterprise*—often do not create a PE. However, "buying" offices often cross the line if they actively negotiate purchase terms that bind the US entity.
*   **Treaty Status:** **Critical Gap:** As of current general knowledge, there is **no comprehensive Double Taxation Treaty (DTT)** in force between the United States and Ecuador. This means you cannot rely on treaty definitions to limit PE status or prevent double taxation; you are subject to Ecuador's domestic tax code entirely. This significantly increases your risk compared to jurisdictions with a US treaty.

**Routing Note (High-Stakes/Jurisdiction-Specific):**
You must consult a qualified Ecuadorian tax attorney immediately. The specific question to bring is: "Under Ecuador's Domestic Tax Law (Ley de Régimen Tributario Interno), does a fixed place of business used solely for quality control and bean sourcing negotiation constitute a Permanent Establishment for a US entity, given the absence of a US-Ecuador tax treaty?" Do not hire staff or sign a lease until this is answered in writing.
