**The Call:** Do not form three separate LLCs yet; the administrative cost and complexity will likely outweigh the marginal liability protection for a $350k revenue business. Instead, operate as a single LLC with robust insurance and strengthened contractual disclaimers, while treating the "outdated content" risk through a rigorous content-update protocol rather than corporate structuring.

**The Numbers:**
*   **Current Structure Cost (1 LLC):** ~$500–$800/year (state filing + registered agent + basic tax prep).
*   **Proposed Structure Cost (3 LLCs):** ~$1,500–$2,400/year (tripled filing fees, registered agents, and potentially tripled tax preparation fees).
*   **Revenue Impact:** $350,000 annual revenue.
*   **Cost-Benefit Analysis:** Adding ~$1,600 in annual fixed costs represents a 0.46% drag on current revenue.
*   **Assumption (ASSUMED):** Litigation defense costs for a single entity vs. multiple entities are similar in the early stages of a suit; the primary benefit of separation is isolating assets *after* a judgment.
*   **Result:** You are spending nearly 0.5% of revenue to potentially isolate assets that are not yet large enough to justify the fragmentation. If a lawsuit exceeds your insurance limits, plaintiffs may still attempt to "pierce the corporate veil" if they can argue the entities are alter-egos or undercapitalized, rendering the structural split less effective than intended.

**What Flips It, and the Trigger:**
The decision flips if your annual revenue exceeds **$2 million** or if you hold significant tangible assets (real estate, large cash reserves) within the company that exceed your liability insurance coverage limits.
*   **Trigger Value:** $2M annual revenue or >$500k in uninsured corporate assets.
*   **Stop/Pivot Date:** Re-evaluate this structure at your next fiscal year-end if revenue grows by 40% year-over-year.
*   **Rule of Thumb:** Corporate structuring for liability isolation is usually justified when the cost of maintenance is <0.1% of revenue *and* the assets at risk exceed insurance caps. Currently, your risk is better mitigated by insurance than by entity fragmentation.
*   **This Week's Step:** If your current Errors & Omissions (E&O) insurance limit is under $1M, consider increasing coverage before filing any new entity paperwork; if you have no specific "educational content" exclusion review, consult a broker immediately.
