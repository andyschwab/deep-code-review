**The Call**
Proceed with the Chicago expansion but model it as a "pilot cluster" rather than a scale market; the 500-scooter cap destroys unit economics in that specific city due to fixed cost dilution, but it does not materially alter your overall company valuation if framed correctly to investors as a regulatory testing ground rather than a primary growth engine.

**The Numbers**

*Chicago Unit Economics (500-Scooter Cap):*
Revenue potential scales linearly with fleet size if demand exists: 500 units × $547 = $273,500 monthly top-line.
**Critical Assumption:** OpEx does not scale linearly. Permitting, legal compliance, local management, and charging logistics often have a high fixed-cost floor.
If Chicago requires a dedicated local manager and compliance overhead of $25,000/month (**ASSUMED LOW**), plus variable charging/maintenance at 60% of Miami's per-unit efficiency due to scale loss:
Estimated Chicago OpEx ≈ $25,000 (fixed) + ($180k/5000 * 500 * 1.2 efficiency penalty) ≈ $46,600.
Projected Chicago Profit ≈ $226,900.
*Impact:* While profitable, the *margin percentage* and *absolute cash flow* are too small to move the needle on a Series A valuation model expecting metro-scale returns. The "scalability" narrative breaks if every major city caps you at 10% of your Miami footprint.

**What Flips It, and The Trigger**
The single input that changes the call is the **permit renewal expansion clause**. Many cities cap initial fleets but allow increases based on performance metrics (utilization, safety, equity zones).
*Trigger:* If the Chicago permit allows a fleet increase after 6 months of >85% utilization, the risk profile flips from "capped growth" to "phased entry."
*Rule of Thumb:* If a market cannot support at least 2,000 units within 12 months, it is a lifestyle business, not a venture-scale market.
*Action Step:* If the permit application lacks a performance-based expansion clause, consider lobbying for a pilot program amendment before committing capital, or deprioritize Chicago for a city with clearer scaling pathways.
