**The Call:** Changing your pricing from per-transaction to a flat-rate subscription does **not** alter your legal classification as a Consumer Reporting Agency (CRA) under the Fair Credit Reporting Act (FCRA) or New York state law, nor does it automatically reclassify you as a "data broker" solely due to the billing model. Your obligations are driven by the *nature of the data* (background checks for employment/caregiving) and the *purpose* (eligibility decisions), not the payment frequency. However, an "unlimited" model introduces significant compliance risk regarding "permissible purpose" verification for every single check, which is a stricter operational burden than your current model.

**The Numbers & Operational Impact:**
*   **Current Volume:** 2,000 checks/month × $40 = $80,000 monthly revenue.
*   **Subscription Break-Even:** To match current revenue at $300/month, an agency must run **267 checks/month** ($80,000 / $300).
*   **Risk Assumption:** If an agency subscribes but only runs 50 checks, your revenue drops 83%. If they run 1,000 checks, your margin compresses unless your marginal cost per check is near zero.
*   **Compliance Cost Variable:** The critical input is not the price, but the **cost of verifying permissible purpose** for every request. Under FCRA, you must have a certified permissible purpose for *each* report. An unlimited model may incentivize agencies to run "fishing expedition" checks. If you fail to audit each request individually because the fee is sunk, you violate the FCRA regardless of your billing cycle.

**What Flips It & The Trigger:**
The trigger for regulatory action is not the subscription contract itself, but a failure to document permissible purpose for individual reports generated under that contract.
*   **Trigger Value:** If an agency runs >50 checks/month on a single subscription without distinct, documented consent/need for each subject, you are at high risk of an FTC or NY AG enforcement action for lax controls.
*   **Rule of Thumb:** Billing frequency is irrelevant; audit frequency must remain 1:1 with report generation.
*   **This Week's Step:** If you proceed with the subscription pilot, consider implementing an automated "purpose certification" checkbox that must be clicked for *every* individual search, even within the unlimited plan, to maintain your FCRA defense.

**Routing Note (Tier 3 - Binding Determination):**
While the pricing model doesn't change your CRA status, the specific wording of your "unlimited" terms could inadvertently create liabilities under New York's data breach notification laws or specific NY consumer protection statutes if "unlimited" is interpreted as waiving reasonable access controls. You must have NY counsel review the specific subscription agreement language to ensure it explicitly preserves your right to deny any specific check lacking permissible purpose, preventing the contract from being argued as a waiver of FCRA duties. Additionally, confirm with counsel whether NY's specific "data broker" registration requirements (if applicable to your specific data aggregation methods) are triggered by the *volume* of data accessed under a subscription, distinct from the transactional model.
