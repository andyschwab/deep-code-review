**The Call**
Offer exactly **20%** equity to the investor; this matches the $1M investment on a $4M pre-money valuation. Do not alter this percentage to "retain control" via math alone; control is preserved through board composition and voting rights, not just ownership percentage. Your current debt does not change the equity calculation but must be disclosed during due diligence.

**The Numbers**
*Formula:* Investor % = Investment / (Pre-Money Valuation + Investment).
*Your Figures:* $1,000,000 / ($4,000,000 + $1,000,000) = **20%**.

*Post-Money Cap Table (Illustrative):*
*   **Total Post-Money Value:** $5,000,000.
*   **Investor:** 20% ownership ($1M value).
*   **Founders Pool (Remaining 80%):** $4M value.
    *   Founder A (60% of founders): 48% of total company (0.60 × 0.80).
    *   Founder B (20% of founders): 16% of total company (0.20 × 0.80).
    *   Founder C (20% of founders): 16% of total company (0.20 × 0.80).

*Assumptions & Notes:*
*   **Debt:** The $50k supplier debt is a liability on the balance sheet, not an equity line item. It reduces the net asset value but does not dilute the percentage ownership calculated above unless the debt converts to equity (which you have not stated).
*   **Scaling Math:** To scale from 15k to 50k units, you need ~3.3x production capacity. Ensure the $1M covers the working capital required for this jump without triggering cash-flow insolvency before revenue arrives.

**What Flips It, and the Trigger**
The deal flips from "standard" to "dangerous" if the investor requests **>25% ownership** for this $1M check.
*   **Trigger Value:** If the implied post-money valuation drops below $4M (e.g., they want 25% for $1M, implying a $3M post-money), reject the term sheet or renegotiate the pre-money valuation.
*   **Rule of Thumb:** Seed rounds typically dilute founders 15–25%. Going above 25% creates signaling problems for Series A investors who fear the founders are insufficiently motivated.
*   **This Week's Step:** If the investor pushes for >20%, consider countering with a **valuation cap** on a SAFE note instead of priced equity, deferring the exact valuation discussion until you hit the 50k unit milestone.

**Routing Note (Securities & Legal)**
Selling equity is a securities offering; the binding terms cannot be self-calculated. Before signing, consult a securities lawyer to define:
1.  **Liquidation Preferences:** Ensure the term sheet specifies "1x non-participating" preferences. Avoid "participating" preferences, which effectively double-dip and drastically reduce your payout in an exit.
2.  **Board Control:** Verify that the 20% equity stake does not automatically grant the investor a board seat or veto rights that cede operational control of BuildBot Toys.
3.  **Debt Covenants:** Have counsel review if the $50k supplier debt contains change-of-control clauses triggered by this investment.
