**The Call:** Elect accelerated depreciation (likely Section 179 or Bonus Depreciation) to maximize immediate tax shields and improve current cash flow, provided your business has sufficient taxable income to absorb the deduction this year. This strategy front-loads benefits when capital outlay is highest, aligning tax savings with loan repayment pressure.

**The Numbers:**
*   **Asset Basis:** $300,000 (Medical Equipment).
*   **Revenue:** $45,000/month ($540,000 annualized).
*   **Loan Payment:** $8,000/month ($96,000 annualized).
*   **Method A (Accelerated):** Deduct ~100% of $300,000 in Year 1 (subject to current IRS limits and taxable income caps).
    *   *Year 1 Taxable Income Impact:* Reduces by $300,000.
    *   *Years 2–5 Depreciation:* $0.
*   **Method B (Straight-Line):** Deduct evenly over 5 years (assuming 5-year MACRS class life for simplicity).
    *   *Annual Deduction:* $60,000/year.
    *   *Year 1 Taxable Income Impact:* Reduces by $60,000.
*   **Comparison (Illustrative at 21% effective tax rate):**
    *   *Accelerated Year 1 Tax Savings:* ~$63,000 cash retained.
    *   *Straight-Line Year 1 Tax Savings:* ~$12,600 cash retained.
    *   *Cash Flow Difference:* Accelerated puts ~$50,400 more cash in hand immediately to service the $96k loan obligation.

**What Flips It, and the Trigger:**
The decision flips if your **net taxable income before depreciation is less than $300,000**. Accelerated deductions cannot create a net operating loss for certain entities or may be limited if you don't have enough profit to offset.
*   *Trigger:* If your annualized net profit (Revenue minus Operating Expenses minus Interest, *before* depreciation) is under $300,000, you cannot utilize the full accelerated deduction this year.
*   *Rule of Thumb:* If Profit < Asset Cost, accelerate only up to the profit level to zero out taxes, then carry forward the rest.
*   *Future Impact:* Deducting more now reduces future depreciation deductions, potentially increasing taxable income in Years 2–5 when expansion slows. This is generally acceptable because $1 today is worth more than $1 in five years, and you can manage future tax liability through new equipment purchases or operational scaling.
*   *This Week:* Calculate your Year-To-Date net profit before depreciation; if it exceeds $300,000, prepare to elect full expensing. If lower, calculate the exact amount needed to zero out current tax liability.
