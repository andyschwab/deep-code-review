**The Call:** You must register for and collect Canadian GST/HST immediately upon making your first taxable sale to Canada; there is no revenue threshold for non-resident sellers of physical goods. For Ontario and British Columbia, you will collect the harmonized sales tax (HST) at checkout; for Quebec, you must register separately for the QST. Your $450 average order value does not create an exemption, as Canada eliminated the low-value import exemption for commercial goods in 2021. Furniture is generally fully taxable in all three provinces.

**The Numbers & Rules:**
*   **Federal Rule (GST/HST):** Non-resident sellers must register for a GST/HST account if they make taxable supplies in Canada. Unlike the US or EU, there is **no small-supplier threshold** for non-residents; registration is required from the first dollar of sales.
    *   **Ontario:** HST rate is 13% (5% GST + 8% provincial).
    *   **British Columbia:** HST rate is 12% (5% GST + 7% provincial).
    *   **Quebec:** Requires separate registration for QST. Rate is 9.975% QST + 5% GST (total ~14.975%).
*   **Product Specifics:** Furniture (chairs, tables) is a standard-rated supply. There are no special exemptions for flat-pack furniture.
*   **Order Value Impact:** The $450 AOV is fully taxable. Previously, imports under CAD $20 were exempt, but this threshold was removed for commercial imports. Duties may also apply depending on the origin of manufacture (e.g., USMCA/CUSMA rules), but sales tax applies regardless of duty status.

**What Flips It & Triggers:**
*   **Trigger:** The moment you process a sale to a Canadian consumer.
*   **Flip Input:** If you decide *not* to sell to Canada, you avoid registration. If you proceed with the planned 50 orders next month, you are legally obligated to have these accounts active before charging the first customer.
*   **Actionable Step:** If you plan to ship next month, **register for a non-resident GST/HST account with the CRA immediately** (processing can take weeks) and initiate the QST registration for Quebec concurrently. Do not wait for revenue to accumulate.

**Routing Note (High-Stakes/Binding):**
While the general rule mandates registration, the specific mechanics of filing returns, claiming input tax credits for foreign vendors, and determining if you require a **fiscal representative** (often required for non-residents to account for GST/HST securely) are binding legal determinations. You must engage a Canadian cross-border tax specialist or CPA to handle the actual registration forms, verify if a security deposit or fiscal representative is mandated for your specific corporate structure, and set up the correct tax codes in your checkout system for Ontario, BC, and Quebec.
