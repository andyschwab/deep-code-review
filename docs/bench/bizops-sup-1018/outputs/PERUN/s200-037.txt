**The Call:** Your quiz-based customization likely keeps you in the dietary supplement category *only if* you avoid disease-treatment claims and stay within established nutrient safety limits; however, the moment your algorithm recommends dosages to treat, cure, or prevent a specific disease, or exceeds Upper Tolerable Intake Levels (ULs), you cross into unapproved drug manufacturing or illegal compounding, which requires immediate legal validation before any funding round.

**The Numbers & Regulatory Thresholds:**
The FDA distinction relies on two primary vectors: **Intent (Claims)** and **Composition (Dosage)**.
1.  **Claims (The "Drug" Trigger):** Under the FD&C Act, a product is a drug if intended to diagnose, cure, mitigate, treat, or prevent disease.
    *   *Safe Zone:* "Supports immune health," "Helps maintain energy levels."
    *   *Red Flag (Drug Classification):* "Treats Vitamin D deficiency," "Prevents osteoporosis," "Lowers high blood pressure." If your quiz outputs a recommendation based on a medical condition (e.g., "You have symptoms of anemia, take this iron dose"), that is a medical diagnosis and drug claim.
2.  **Dosage (The "Compounding" Trigger):** Supplements must use ingredients with a history of safe use.
    *   *Safe Zone:* Dosages at or below the FDA's Upper Tolerable Intake Levels (ULs) for the general population.
    *   *Red Flag:* Exceeding ULs (e.g., >40mg Zinc/day long-term) or creating novel combinations without safety data. If you are mixing raw powders based on individual prescriptions (even self-generated ones) to treat a specific patient's imbalance, you may be acting as an unlicensed compounding pharmacy, which is strictly regulated under Section 503A/503B of the FD&C Act.

**What Flips It & The Trigger:**
The single input that changes your status from "supplement" to "unapproved drug" is **the language of the quiz output and the marketing copy**.
*   *Trigger Value:* Any instance where the system maps a user input (symptom/lab result) to a specific disease state and recommends a formulation to address it.
*   *Stop/Pivot Rule:* If your quiz asks "Do you have joint pain?" and recommends "Glucosamine for arthritis treatment," you are manufacturing a drug. If it asks "Do you want to support joint mobility?" and recommends "Glucosamine for joint health," you are likely a supplement.
*   *Actionable Step:* This week, audit your quiz logic and marketing materials. If any output contains disease names (e.g., diabetes, hypertension, depression) or implies treatment, strip that language immediately and replace it with structure/function claims authorized for supplements.
