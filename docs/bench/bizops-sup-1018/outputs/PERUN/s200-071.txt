**The Call:** Do not launch the beta displaying code snippets without immediate counsel review; the risk of copyright infringement is significant despite fair use arguments, particularly given your global user base and the scraping of 2 million repositories. While US fair use may offer a defense for short snippets, it is a costly litigation argument, not a pre-launch shield, and EU exceptions are far narrower. Implementing an opt-out mechanism is a prudent risk-mitigation step for community relations but must be framed carefully to avoid appearing as an admission of liability; this framing requires legal drafting.

**The Numbers & Risk Context:**
You have $150,000 in runway. A single copyright infringement lawsuit in the US can easily cost $50,000–$100,000 in initial defense fees before any ruling on merit, potentially consuming 33–66% of your remaining runway. In the EU, statutory damages and injunctions can halt operations entirely.
*   **Assumption (Low Risk):** Courts rule that 3-line snippets are *de minimis* or transformative fair use immediately. (Unlikely to be decided without litigation).
*   **Assumption (Base Risk):** You face a cease-and-desist or suit requiring a settled defense. Cost: >$75k. Result: Runway critically compromised.
*   **Assumption (High Risk):** An injunction stops your service pending trial. Result: Business failure.

**What Flips It & The Trigger:**
The deciding factor is **jurisdiction and the nature of the display**. If your service is deemed to substitute the original work (even partially) or if EU "text and data mining" exceptions do not explicitly cover your commercial search display, the risk flips from "manageable" to "existential."
*   **Trigger:** If you launch next month without a legal opinion on EU Directive (EU) 2019/790 (Copyright in the Digital Single Market) and US fair use applicability to search engines, you risk an injunction within 90 days of traction.
*   **Rule of Thumb:** If your display allows a user to solve a problem *without* visiting the source repo, it leans against fair use.
*   **This Week's Step:** If you cannot pause the snippet display, consider implementing a "click-through" to the source repo as the default view, showing snippets only after user interaction, and draft the opt-out policy language for review.

**Routing Note (Specific Questions for Counsel):**
You must engage an IP attorney specializing in software and international data immediately. Bring these exact questions:
1.  **US Fair Use:** Does displaying 3-line contextual snippets for a commercial search engine qualify as fair use under *Google v. Oracle* precedents, or does the "scraping" of 2M repos alter the "amount and substantiality" factor?
2.  **EU Compliance:** Does our activity fall under the Text and Data Mining (TDM) exceptions in Article 3 or 4 of the EU Copyright Directive, and do we need to honor "opt-out" reservations explicitly stated in repository metadata?
3.  **Opt-Out Strategy:** How do we structure an opt-out mechanism (e.g., `robots.txt` extension or specific file flag) so it functions as a compliance tool rather than an implicit admission that our baseline activity is infringing?
4.  **DMCA Safe Harbor:** Does our indexing and snippet generation disqualify us from DMCA safe harbor protections as a "service provider"?
