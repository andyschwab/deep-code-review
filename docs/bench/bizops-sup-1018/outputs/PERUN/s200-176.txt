**The Call:** Your donation and grant revenue is generally tax-exempt if you hold 501(c)(3) status, but the $12,000 interest is likely taxable "Unrelated Business Income" (UBI) if it exceeds specific thresholds, and corporate sponsorships require careful structuring to avoid being classified as taxable advertising. You must file an annual information return (Form 990 series) to maintain compliance; failure to file for three consecutive years automatically revokes exempt status.

**The Numbers & Rules:**
*   **Donations ($180k) & Corporate Sponsorship ($50k):** Under IRS rules, contributions made with no expectation of a substantial return benefit are tax-exempt gifts.
    *   *Assumption:* The $50k corporate sponsorship is structured as a "qualified sponsorship payment" (acknowledgment only, no advertising or contingent benefits). If true, this is **$0 taxable income**.
    *   *Risk:* If the sponsorship includes advertising (e.g., "Buy from Sponsor X"), it may be treated as taxable commercial income.
*   **Interest Income ($12k):** Passive investment income (interest, dividends) is generally excluded from UBI *unless* it is debt-financed. However, if your organization is a private foundation rather than a public charity, this income is subject to a 1.39% excise tax on net investment income.
    *   *Illustrative Figure:* If you are a private foundation, tax ≈ $12,000 × 1.39% = **$166.80**. If you are a public charity, this is likely **$0 taxable**, provided it doesn't trigger UBI rules via debt financing.
*   **Total Potential Taxable Income:** $0 (if public charity + qualified sponsorships) to ~$167 (if private foundation) + any portion of sponsorship deemed advertising.

**What Flips It & The Trigger:**
The classification flips if the corporate sponsorship provides a "substantial return benefit" (e.g., exclusive marketing rights, discounts to sponsors) or if the interest is derived from debt-financed property.
*   **Trigger:** If the value of benefits provided to corporate sponsors exceeds 2% of the payment (or $106 for 2024, adjusted annually), the excess becomes taxable advertising income.
*   **Rule of Thumb:** Keep sponsorship acknowledgments passive (logo + name only). If you add "Call to Action" language, consider it taxable.
