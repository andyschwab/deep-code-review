**The Call:** Reject the flat fee increase to $35 as the primary strategy; it carries a high risk of spiking churn among price-sensitive members without validating willingness to pay. Instead, pilot the tiered model ($15/$35/$75) immediately, but treat the 20% "Elite" conversion assumption as dangerously optimistic. The tiered model beats the flat hike if you can convert just **8.5%** of users to the Elite tier (assuming the rest split evenly between Basic and Premium), provided churn on the lower tiers remains stable.

**The Numbers:**
*Current Baseline:*
Revenue = 8,000 members × $25 = **$200,000/mo**.
Variable Costs (Server/Content) = $12,000 (treated as fixed for this volume step).
*Contribution Margin* = $188,000 (before team salaries).

*Option A: Flat Fee Hike ($35)*
Revenue = 8,000 × $35 = **$280,000/mo** (assuming 0% churn impact, which is unlikely).
*Risk:* A $10 (40%) price increase typically triggers significant churn in community models. If you lose just 15% of members (1,200 users) due to the hike, revenue drops to $238,000, eroding the gain.

*Option B: Tiered Model ($15 / $35 / $75)*
Let $x$ be the % upgrading to Elite ($75).
Assumption (Base Case): 20% Elite, 40% Premium ($35), 40% Basic ($15).
Weighted Average Revenue Per User (ARPU) = (0.20 × 75) + (0.40 × 35) + (0.40 × 15) = $15 + $14 + $6 = **$35**.
Result: Matches the flat hike revenue ($280,000) but retains price-sensitive users in the $15 tier.

*The Break-Even Conversion Rate:*
To beat the *risky* flat hike (assuming a conservative 10% churn on the flat hike, leaving 7,200 users × $35 = $252,000), the tiered model needs an ARPU of $31.50.
If we assume users not in Elite split 50/50 between Basic ($15) and Premium ($35) (avg $25), the formula is:
$75x + 25(1-x) = 31.50$
$50x = 6.50$
$x = 13\%$
**You only need 13% of users to upgrade to Elite to outperform a flat hike that causes modest churn.** Your suspected 20% is well above this threshold, making the tiered model mathematically superior *if* the segmentation holds.

**What Flips It, and The Trigger:**
The single input that flips this decision is **churn elasticity on the $15 tier**. If moving the bottom 40% of users to a paid $15 tier causes them to cancel entirely rather than downgrade, your total addressable base shrinks.
*Trigger:* If pilot data shows >25% cancellation rate among users offered the $15 tier (vs. current 5%), abandon the tiered model and reconsider a modest flat increase to $29.
*Rule of Thumb:* In community businesses, a 3x price spread (Basic to Elite) is sustainable only if the top tier offers distinct, high-touch value (e.g., 1:1 coaching), not just content access.
*Step this week:* Run an A/B test on 10% of your traffic. Offer the tiered structure to this segment only. Measure the actual Elite conversion rate and the cancellation rate of those offered the $15 option. If Elite conversion >13% and downgrades <10%, roll out globally.
