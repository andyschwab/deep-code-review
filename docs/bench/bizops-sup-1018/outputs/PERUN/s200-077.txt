**The Call**
Proceed with the 20% savings model but decouple your fee calculation from the client's financial audit by contractually defining "Verified Savings" as a distinct, pre-agreed operational metric rather than a GAAP/IFRS accounting adjustment. Do not attempt to draft specific legal clauses yourself; instead, structure the commercial term so your revenue is a service fee based on a mutually signed monthly report, leaving the client's internal accounting treatment of the underlying savings entirely to them.

**The Numbers**
Your unit economics rely on the formula: `Revenue = Verified Savings × 20%`.
Using your example: `$20,000 (Savings) × 0.20 = $4,000 Revenue`.
To reach profitability with 5 enterprise deals, we must reverse-engineer the required deal size.
*Assumption (ASSUMED):* Your monthly burn rate is unknown. Let $B$ be your monthly burn.
*Formula:* `Required Avg Revenue per Deal = B / 5`.
*Illustrative Scenario:* If your burn is $50,000/month, each of the 5 deals must generate $10,000/month.
*Implied Savings Needed:* `$10,000 / 0.20 = $50,000 monthly savings per client`.
*Feasibility Check:* A client spending $100k/month yielding $50k savings implies a 50% reduction. This is likely unsustainable for mature enterprises (typical waste is 15–30%).
*Trigger:* If your actual burn exceeds $30,000/month, this model requires clients with >$300k monthly spend to hit profitability with only 5 deals. Verify your burn immediately; if high, you may need more than 5 deals or a hybrid base fee.

**What Flips It, and The Trigger**
The deal-breaker is the **definition of "Savings."** If the contract allows you to unilaterally determine the savings amount used for billing, auditors will flag it as a conflict of interest.
*The Fix:* The contract must state that fees are based on a "Jointly Approved Savings Report" signed by both parties by the 5th of each month. Once signed, this report becomes the binding invoice basis, regardless of how the client later adjusts those numbers for their own financial statements.
*Trigger Rule of Thumb:* If a client refuses to sign a monthly operational report defining the savings baseline, do not sign the deal.
*Action This Week:* Draft a "Savings Validation Protocol" appendix for your pilot contracts that mandates a 3-day window for client dispute of calculated savings before they are deemed "Verified."
