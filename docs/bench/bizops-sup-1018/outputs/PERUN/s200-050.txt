**The Call:** Do not launch the "99% pollen/dander removal for allergy sufferers" claim yet; it creates a high risk of your product being classified as a regulated medical device by the FDA, which would legally require you to halt sales until clearance is obtained. Instead, pivot your messaging to "general wellness" and "air quality" (e.g., "reduces airborne particulates") while immediately engaging regulatory counsel to validate a specific, non-medical wording strategy.

**The Numbers & Risk Assessment:**
*   **Current Unit Economics:** Price $120, COGS $45, Gross Margin $75/unit.
*   **Regulatory Cost Variable:** FDA 510(k) clearance for a Class II medical device typically costs $50,000–$150,000+ in consulting/testing fees and takes 6–12 months, plus potential clinical data costs.
*   **The Trigger:** The phrase "helps allergy sufferers" combined with "removes 99% of pollen/dander" explicitly claims to diagnose, cure, mitigate, treat, or prevent a disease (allergic rhinitis). Under the Federal Food, Drug, and Cosmetic Act, this defines a medical device.
*   **Consequence:** If the FDA determines you are selling an uncleared medical device, they can issue a Warning Letter and demand an immediate recall. Your $75,000 gross profit (1,000 units × $75) is at risk of being wiped out by recall logistics and legal fines.

**What Flips It, and The Trigger:**
*   **The Flip Input:** The specific wording of the claim.
    *   *High Risk:* "Treats allergies," "Helps allergy sufferers," "Prevents allergic reactions." (Medical Device)
    *   *Lower Risk:* "Captures dust," "Reduces airborne particles," "Improves cabin air quality." (General Consumer Product)
*   **The Rule of Thumb:** If the claim references a specific disease, condition, or symptom (sneezing, itchy eyes, allergic reaction), it is medical. If it references the environment (dust, smoke, particles), it is likely general wellness.
*   **Stop/Pivot Trigger:** If your marketing draft contains the word "allergy" or "asthma," stop publication.
*   **This Week's Step:** If you want to target this segment, consider A/B testing copy that says "Designed for cleaner air" and tracking conversion, while paying a regulatory attorney $500–$1,000 for a one-hour opinion letter on your specific proposed phrasing before printing new packaging.
