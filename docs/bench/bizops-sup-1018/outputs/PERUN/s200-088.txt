**The Call**
Keep your current ratable recognition policy; do not accelerate revenue to improve reported financials for your Series A. Under US GAAP (ASC 606), revenue for SaaS subscriptions must be recognized over the period the service is provided, regardless of when cash is received or when implementation occurs. Accelerating revenue based on upfront effort or cash collection would likely constitute a material misstatement, creating severe compliance risks and potential fraud liability that would derail your fundraising rather than help it.

**The Numbers**
Your current runway and recognition math are as follows:
*   **Runway:** $1,200,000 Cash / $80,000 Monthly Burn = **15 months** of runway.
*   **Current Recognition (Base Case):**
    *   Annual Deal: $15,000 / 12 months = $1,250/month recognized.
    *   Multi-year Deal: $135,000 / 36 months = $3,750/month recognized.
*   **Hypothetical Acceleration (Illustrative Only):**
    *   If you attempted to recognize 50% of the multi-year deal upfront due to implementation: $67,500 in Month 1.
    *   *Result:* This creates a $63,750 variance from the GAAP requirement ($3,750) in the first month alone.
    *   *Assumption:* This scenario assumes implementation represents 50% of total value, which is rarely defensible for ongoing SaaS access.

**What Flips It, and the Trigger**
The only factor that changes the recognition timing is the identification of **distinct performance obligations**. If your contract explicitly separates "Implementation Services" from "Software Access" and the implementation is not integral to the software's functionality (i.e., the customer could use the software without it, or a third party could perform it), you *might* recognize the implementation fee upfront upon completion.
*   **Trigger:** If your implementation is customized, complex, and essential for the customer to benefit from the license, it is bundled with the subscription. In this case, the entire contract value must be recognized ratably over the 3-year term.
*   **Rule of Thumb:** If the customer cannot cancel the implementation separately without cancelling the whole deal, it is one single performance obligation recognized over time.
*   **Next Step:** This week, review your Master Services Agreements (MSAs). If implementation is listed as a separate line item with a standalone price, flag this for your auditor; if it is bundled into the "subscription fee," maintain ratable recognition.

**Routing Note**
Revenue recognition under ASC 606 is a binding accounting determination. Before filing your Series A data room or changing any policy, you must consult your external auditor or a CPA specializing in SaaS. Specifically, ask them: "Does our specific implementation workflow constitute a distinct performance obligation separable from the ongoing subscription access under ASC 606-10?"
