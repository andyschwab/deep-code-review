**The Call**
Do not allocate the $100k based on global remote worker counts; that top-down approach will overstate your market and obscure the real constraint. Instead, build a bottom-up model using "HR Training Budget per Employee" as your primary filter, then apply a "Remote-First Adoption Rate" to distinguish between video (high volume, low touch) and live (low volume, high touch) opportunities. Your immediate growth likely lies in scaling the asynchronous video segment via self-serve expansion, while the live segment requires a sales-led motion that your current $100k may not support efficiently without proof of US demand.

**The Numbers**
To size the market accurately, use this formula for each segment:
`TAM = (Target US Companies) × (Avg Employees per Company) × (HR Training Budget % of Revenue) × (Segment Allocation %)`

Since you lack specific US data points, we must bracket the inputs with assumptions based on your current Toronto metrics:

*   **Target US Companies:** Unknown. *Assumption:* Focus on companies with 50–500 employees (your current sweet spot). Let's assume 50,000 such remote-first companies in the US.
*   **HR Training Budget:** Industry standard is 1–3% of revenue. *Assumption:* $1,000 per employee annually (blended average).
*   **Segment Allocation (The Split):** This is where video and live diverge.
    *   *Video:* Typically captures 60–70% of L&D budgets for scale. *Assumption:* 65%.
    *   *Live:* Typically captures 10–20% due to cost/facilitator limits. *Assumption:* 15%.

**Computed Market Size (Illustrative Only):**
*   **Asynchronous Video TAM:** 50,000 companies × 200 employees × $1,000 × 65% = **$6.5 Billion**
*   **Live Facilitated TAM:** 50,000 companies × 200 employees × $1,000 × 15% = **$1.5 Billion**

*Note: These figures are illustrative based on assumed penetration. Your actual Serviceable Obtainable Market (SOM) depends on your sales capacity.*

**What Flips It, and the Trigger**
The one input that changes the call is the **Customer Acquisition Cost (CAC) payback period** for the US market. If your CAC to acquire a US video client exceeds $600 (30% of first-year value), the "larger" video market becomes unprofitable compared to the smaller, higher-margin live segment.
*   **Trigger:** If your pilot US campaign shows a CAC >$600 for video or >$4,500 for live by **Month 6**, pivot the $100k from product development to sales enablement or channel partnerships.
*   **Rule of Thumb:** Allocate 70% of budget to the channel with a payback period <12 months.
*   **This Week's Step:** If you have no US data, consider running a $5k smoke test (landing page + ads) to measure click-to-signup rates for both formats before committing the full $100k.
