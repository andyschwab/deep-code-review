**The Call:** Do not create separate SKUs solely for tax reporting; manage this through retailer-specific price files and point-of-sale (POS) tax configurations. The $10,000 annual logistics cost of new SKUs likely exceeds the administrative burden of managing variable pricing, provided your distributors can handle region-specific price lists.

**The Numbers:**
*   **Current Monthly Revenue (Tax Cities):** 50,000 cans × $2.00 = $100,000.
*   **Current Monthly Margin:** $100,000 × 40% = $40,000.
*   **Scenario A (Absorb Tax):** Margin drops to 32%. New Monthly Margin = $32,000. **Loss = $8,000/month ($96,000/year).**
*   **Scenario B (Pass Tax):** Price $2.15. Revenue = $107,500.
    *   *Assumption (Base):* Volume remains flat at 50,000 units.
    *   *Result:* Gross revenue increases by $7,500/month. Even with a slight volume dip (up to ~7%), you outperform the "absorb" scenario financially.
    *   *Assumption (High Risk):* If volume drops >15% due to price sensitivity, absorbing the tax may be safer short-term.
*   **SKU Complexity Cost:** $10,000/year fixed cost (~$833/month) to avoid price management.

**Comparison:** Absorbing the tax costs ~$96k/year. Creating new SKUs costs ~$10k/year. Passing the tax costs $0 in hard costs but risks volume. Financially, absorbing the tax is the most expensive option.

**What Flips It, and the Trigger:**
The deciding factor is **retailer POS capability**. If major retailers in Philadelphia/Boulder cannot map a single UPC to multiple tax rules based on store location, you are forced into separate SKUs.
*   **Trigger:** Confirm with your primary distributor whether their system supports "geofenced pricing" or automatic tax adders for specific municipalities.
*   **Rule of Thumb:** If >20% of your volume in these cities comes from small retailers with rigid POS systems, the operational friction may justify the $10k SKU cost.
*   **Step this week:** If your distributor confirms they can handle region-specific price files, instruct them to implement a price override for Philadelphia/Boulder zip codes rather than cutting new SKUs.

**Operational & Compliance Considerations:**
1.  **Tax Remittance Liability:** In most sugar-tax jurisdictions (like Philadelphia), the *distributor* or *retailer* is responsible for collecting and remitting the tax, not the manufacturer. You generally do not need to file tax returns for these cities unless you are self-distributing. Verify this immediately; if the distributor handles it, your compliance burden is near zero.
2.  **Price Files vs. SKUs:** CPG brands routinely manage regional pricing without new barcodes. You issue a specific price file to the distributor for the affected region. The barcode remains the same; the system applies the tax at the register or adds the cost to the invoice.
3.  **Retailer Acceptance:** Large chains accept variable pricing by region daily. Small independents may struggle if their manual entry processes are rigid, but this is a training issue, not a compliance one.
