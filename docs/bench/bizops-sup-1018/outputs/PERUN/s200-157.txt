**The call:** Proceed with the money-back guarantee only if you can convert at least 36% of failing students to a paid retake or upsell; otherwise, the policy destroys your unit economics and burns your cash reserve in under two quarters.

**The numbers:**
*   **Current Unit Economics (No Guarantee):**
    *   Revenue: $499
    *   Variable Cost: $120
    *   Contribution Margin: $379 per student.
    *   Quarterly Profit (500 students): $189,500.

*   **Projected Unit Economics (With Guarantee, Worst Case):**
    *   *Assumption (Base):* 22% fail rate; 100% of failures request full refunds; 0% retake paid.
    *   Effective Revenue per 100 students: (78 × $499) + (22 × $0) = $38,922.
    *   Total Cost per 100 students: 100 × $120 = $12,000 (costs are sunk regardless of pass/fail).
    *   New Contribution Margin: $26,922 / 100 = **$269.22 per student**.
    *   Margin Drop: $109.78 per student (29% reduction).
    *   Quarterly Profit (500 students): $134,610.
    *   *Cash Impact:* Refund outflow = 110 students × $499 = **$54,890 per quarter**.

*   **Cash Reserve Stress Test:**
    *   Reserve: $40,000.
    *   Quarterly Refund Liability (Base Case): $54,890.
    *   **Result:** Your reserve is insufficient to cover one quarter of refunds. You would face a cash flow crisis immediately upon implementation.

*   **Sensitivity Analysis (The Retake Variable):**
    *   To break even on the *margin loss* (maintain $379 avg margin), failing students must generate ~$499 in new revenue (a full price retake).
    *   To cover the *cash flow gap* using your $40k reserve, you must limit net refund payouts to $40k/quarter. This requires converting ~27 failing students (12% of total cohort) to a paid option or negotiating partial refunds.

**What flips it, and the trigger:**
The single input that changes the call is the **retake conversion rate**. If fewer than 36% of failing students purchase a remedial module or retake course at a price point that offsets the refund, the guarantee is unsustainable.
*   **Trigger:** If your pilot shows a refund request rate >15% of total enrollment (rather than 22% of failures) or a retake conversion <36%, halt the full guarantee.
*   **Rule of Thumb:** Treat the "cost of failure" as a marketing expense. If CAC rises by >$110 per student due to refunds, the model breaks.
*   **Step this week:** Run a survey with recent failures: "If we offered a 50% refund plus a free retake, would you take it?" If >40% say yes, structure the guarantee as "Pass or Free Retake" instead of "Money Back" to protect cash flow.
