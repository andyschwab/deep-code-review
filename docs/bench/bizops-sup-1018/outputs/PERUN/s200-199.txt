**The Call:** Reject the raw $12.5M top-line figure; your viable immediate market is likely 60–70% of your total count unless you validate offline hubs, and you are currently leaking ~$600k in wasted support costs annually on failed enrollments.

**The Numbers:**
*   **Raw TAM:** 5,000 farms × $2,500 = **$12,500,000**.
*   **Connectivity Adjustment:** You lack the specific percentage of farms with sufficient bandwidth.
    *   *Formula:* `Viable Farms = Total Farms × (1 - Connectivity Failure Rate)`
    *   *Assumption (Base):* If 40% dropout is purely connectivity-driven (a high-risk assumption), viable rate is 60%.
    *   *Base Case:* 5,000 × 60% = 3,000 viable farms.
    *   *Adjusted TAM:* 3,000 × $2,500 = **$7,500,000**.
*   **Revenue Loss (Churn Cost):**
    *   *Formula:* `Lost Support Cost = Total Enrollments × Dropout Rate × Cost Per Failure`
    *   *Calculation:* 5,000 × 40% × $300 = **$600,000** in sunk support costs for zero revenue.
*   **Offline Hub Expansion (The Unknown Factor):**
    *   *Variable:* `Hub Lift %` (Percentage of "failed" farms near a co-op/community center willing to travel).
    *   *Scenario:* If hubs recover 50% of the 40% dropout (2,000 farms × 50% = 1,000 recovered).
    *   *Recovered Revenue:* 1,000 × $2,500 = **$2,500,000** additional TAM.

**What Flips It, and The Trigger:**
The single input that changes the call is the **actual cause of dropout**. If the 40% failure rate is due to content difficulty rather than bandwidth, your connectivity adjustment is wrong, and you are shrinking your market unnecessarily.
*   **Trigger:** Conduct a "exit survey" on the next 50 dropouts. If >70% cite "connection error" vs. "content too hard," proceed with the offline hub strategy.
*   **Rule of Thumb:** If the cost to establish one offline hub (hardware + partnership) is less than the LTV of 20 students ($50,000), the hub model is mathematically superior to pure online delivery.
*   **Step this week:** If >30% of dropouts are in zip codes with known low broadband speeds, consider piloting a "download-and-go" USB drive or local co-op viewing party model for one county.
