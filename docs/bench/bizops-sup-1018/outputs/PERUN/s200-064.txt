**The Call:** Do not lower prices; the 8% churn signals a product-market fit gap in integration capabilities, not a pricing failure. Pull the product lever first by building the top two requested integrations, as discounting a deficient product accelerates cash burn without fixing retention.

**The Numbers:**
Your current unit economics are fragile. With $200k ARR (~$16.7k MRR) and $3k monthly server costs, your gross margin is ~82%.
*   **Current State:** 8% monthly churn implies a customer lifetime of ~12.5 months.
*   **Discount Scenario (Tier 1 Assumption):** If you cut prices by 20%, MRR drops to ~$13.4k immediately.
    *   *Assumption (Labelled):* You assume churn improves from 8% to 4% (industry standard). Even with this optimistic **ASSUMED** improvement, you lose $3.3k in top-line revenue monthly.
    *   *Result:* To maintain the same $13.7k gross profit ($16.7k - $3k) on lower revenue, your margin must stay high. However, if churn only improves to 6% (a more realistic **ASSUMED** partial fix), you face a "double death": lower revenue per user and insufficient lifetime value to recover acquisition costs.
*   **Break-even Sensitivity:** At 8% churn, you need a Net Revenue Retention (NRR) >100% from upsells to grow. Discounting reduces the base upon which upsells occur.

**What Flips It, and The Trigger:**
The single input that changes this call is **integration adoption rate**.
*   *Trigger:* If building the top two integrations does not reduce churn to <5% within 90 days, then the issue is likely core utility, and a pivot is required.
*   *Rule of Thumb:* Price sensitivity usually manifests as "sticker shock" at signup, not "missing features" at cancellation. Contradictory survey feedback ("too expensive" vs. "missing integrations") typically masks the real issue: customers say "price" when they mean "value isn't high enough." Since 60% of revenue comes from Pro users (who presumably need integrations), their departure confirms a feature gap.
