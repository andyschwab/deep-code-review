**The call:** Do not switch to a pure usage-only model; it will likely extend your four-month sales cycle and cause procurement stalls in the US financial sector due to budget unpredictability. Instead, adopt a "committed usage" tier structure (good-better-best packaging) that guarantees a baseline rate in exchange for an annual minimum spend, preserving the flexibility of usage billing while offering the CFOs the predictable line item they require.

**The numbers:**
*   **Current Baseline:** $48,000/year average deal size.
*   **Pipeline Value:** $1,000,000 total / 20 prospects = $50,000 average potential deal.
*   **Risk Factor:** Pure variable cost introduces budget variance. If a prospect fears a 20% overspend due to test suite bloat, the perceived risk is $10,000/year per deal.
*   **Proposed Structure (Illustrative):**
    *   *Tier 1 (Commit):* Pre-purchase $48,000 of credits/year. Rate: $X/minute. Overage: Standard rate.
    *   *Tier 2 (Flex):* No commit. Rate: $X * 1.25/minute.
    *   *Assumption (ASSUMED):* Enterprise finance teams reject models where the final invoice cannot be estimated within ±10% of the budget.
    *   *Result:* A committed tier locks the $48k–$50k revenue upfront (or via installment), removing the "unknown variable" objection while keeping the unit economics of usage.

**What flips it, and the trigger:**
The single input that changes this recommendation is **procurement policy strictness**. If your prospects operate under "fixed-budget only" mandates (common in banking/insurance), pure usage is an automatic disqualifier.
*   **Trigger:** If more than 3 of your next 10 discovery calls with financial sector prospects mention "unpredictable OpEx" or "variable cost caps" as a blocker, immediately pause the pure usage rollout and finalize the committed-tier contracts.
*   **Rule of Thumb:** In enterprise B2B, flexibility for the vendor often equals risk for the buyer. Shift the risk back to yourself via caps or commits to close the deal.
*   **Step this week:** If you have a friendly prospect in the pipeline, propose a "hybrid pilot": a fixed $12,000 quarterly commit with true-up/down reconciliation, and measure their procurement response time against your current four-month baseline.

**Routing note:**
While pricing strategy is operational, specific contract terms regarding "true-up" clauses, annual minimums, and liability caps for US financial institutions often trigger legal review regarding service level agreements (SLAs) and data sovereignty. Have counsel review any new master service agreement (MSA) templates before presenting them to regulated entities.
