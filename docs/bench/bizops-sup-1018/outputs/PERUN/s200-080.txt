**The Call:** Do not assume your early contracts are sufficient; standard early-stage agreements often lack the specific "present assignment" language and contractor chain-of-title documentation required for a $12M acquisition, so you must audit before entering exclusive negotiations. Prioritize a targeted review of contractor files and moral rights waivers over renegotiating all eight employee contracts immediately to preserve cash.

**The Analysis & Common Gaps:**
In rigorous due diligence, acquirers do not just look for a signature; they look for an unbroken chain of title. Two specific gaps frequently derail deals or force escrow holdbacks:
1.  **Contractor vs. Employee Distinction:** In California, if a contractor's agreement lacks explicit "work made for hire" language *plus* a present assignment of future rights, the contractor may legally retain ownership of the code they wrote. A simple invoice or NDA is insufficient. If you cannot produce a signed IP assignment for every line of code written by non-employees, the acquirer will view the core asset as unowned.
2.  **Moral Rights & Prior Inventions:** Standard contracts sometimes fail to explicitly waive "moral rights" (the right to be attributed or object to changes), which can technically block code modification post-acquisition. Additionally, if your engineers' "Prior Inventions" disclosures were left blank or vague, they could later claim core schema logic was pre-existing personal IP, not company property.

**What Flips the Deal & The Trigger:**
The deal flips from "proceed" to "halt/renegotiate" if you cannot produce a signed IP assignment agreement for **100% of contributors** (employees and contractors) covering the specific repository history.
*   **Trigger:** If your audit reveals even one contractor without a signed assignment or one employee with an ambiguous prior inventions list, you must pause exclusive negotiations.
*   **Rule of Thumb:** Acquirers typically demand 100% clean title; 95% is often treated as 0% regarding the missing 5% if it touches core IP.
*   **Step this week:** If you find missing contractor docs, consider offering a small one-time bonus ($500–$1,000) conditioned on signing a retroactive IP confirmation and waiver, rather than full contract renegotiation.
