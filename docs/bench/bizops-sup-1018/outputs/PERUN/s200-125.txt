**The Call:** Do not proceed with the "franchisee" reclassification as described; requiring an upfront fee ($15k) and ongoing royalty (5%) while exerting control over methods creates a high-probability franchise relationship under federal and state law, triggering immediate registration requirements in Florida and Georgia that you likely cannot meet without a costly Franchise Disclosure Document (FDD). Attempting this without registration exposes SleepNest to rescission rights (coaches demanding their money back), fines, and personal liability for the founders.

**The Numbers & Liability Shift:**
*   **Current Model (Affiliate/IC):** You pay 20% commission ($60/coach). Liability is generally limited to your own direct negligence; coaches carry their own insurance. Tax reporting is Form 1099-NEC.
*   **Proposed Model (Franchise):** You collect $15,000 upfront + 5% royalty ($15/coach).
    *   **Cash Impact (Year 1, 30 converts):** +$450,000 upfront cash – $135,000 lost commission volume (assuming same sales) + $13,500 royalty revenue. Net short-term cash boost is significant, but legally precarious if unregistered.
    *   **Liability Exposure:** Franchisors often face "vicarious liability" if they exercise too much control over daily operations (e.g., mandating specific sleep training scripts, hours, or strict disciplinary methods). If a child is harmed, plaintiffs will sue both the coach and SleepNest, arguing the coach was an agent of the brand.
    *   **Tax/Reporting:** Shifts from simple 1099 issuance to complex franchise tax filings, potential state registration fees, and strict adherence to FTC Franchise Rule disclosure timelines.

**What Flips It (The Trigger):**
The "Three-Prong Test" defines a franchise federally (and in FL/GA): (1) Payment of a fee, (2) Grant of a trademark/license, and (3) **Significant Control** or a prescribed marketing plan.
*   **The Trigger:** The moment you mandate *how* the sleep consulting is delivered (scripts, mandatory training, strict operational manuals) *and* charge the $15,000 fee, you cross the threshold.
*   **Stop/Pivot Rule:** If you cannot file a registered FDD with the Florida Department of Agriculture and Consumer Services and the Georgia Secretary of State before collecting the first $15,000, you must pivot.
*   **This Week's Step:** If you require strict method standardization, consider a "licensed distributor" or "agency" model where coaches remain 1099 contractors paid a higher commission for adherence to protocols, *without* an upfront buy-in fee, until counsel confirms the structure avoids the "fee" prong.

**Routing Note (Mandatory):**
You must engage a franchise attorney licensed in Florida and Georgia immediately. The specific documents requiring review before any solicitation are: the **Franchise Disclosure Document (FDD)** (all 23 items), the **Franchise Agreement**, and the **state-specific registration applications**. Do not collect any fees or sign any agreements until the FDD is filed and effective in both states; violating the FTC Franchise Rule carries severe civil penalties and allows coaches to void contracts retroactively.
