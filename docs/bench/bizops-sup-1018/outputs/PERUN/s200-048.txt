**The Call:** Do not lower the price; invest the $5,000 immediately in better photography and sizing tools. A 22% return rate driven by "size mismatches" is a product-information failure, not a pricing problem. Lowering the price to $150 will likely degrade your luxury brand perception without fixing the root cause of the returns, while simultaneously compressing your already threatened margins.

**The Numbers:**
Your current unit economics are being destroyed by the return rate, not the sticker price.
*   **Formula:** Net Revenue per Unit = (Price × (1 - Return Rate)) - COGS - (Marketing Spend / Units Sold).
*   **Current State ($180 Price):**
    *   Units Sold: $90,000 / $180 = 500 units.
    *   Effective Sales (after 22% returns): 500 × 0.78 = 390 units.
    *   Net Revenue: $90,000 × 0.78 = $70,200.
    *   Total COGS (on all 500 units shipped): 500 × $60 = $30,000.
    *   Gross Profit (before marketing): $70,200 - $30,000 = $40,200.
    *   Marketing Cost per *Acquired* Customer: $15,000 / 390 = ~$38.46.
    *   **Contribution Margin per Kept Unit:** ($180 - $60 - $38.46) = **$81.54**.
    *   *Note: This assumes returned units have zero salvage value and full shipping loss.*

*   **Scenario A: Lower Price to $150 (Assumption: Returns drop slightly to 18% due to lowered expectations):**
    *   New Units Needed for $90k revenue: 600 units.
    *   Effective Sales: 600 × 0.82 = 492 units.
    *   Net Revenue: $90,000 × 0.82 = $73,800.
    *   Total COGS: 600 × $60 = $36,000.
    *   Gross Profit: $37,800.
    *   **Result:** Your gross profit dollars *decrease* despite selling more units, and you dilute the "luxury" positioning.

*   **Scenario B: Invest $5,000 in Content (Assumption: Returns drop to 8% industry standard):**
    *   Units Sold: 500 (Revenue holds at $90k).
    *   Effective Sales: 500 × 0.92 = 460 units.
    *   Net Revenue: $90,000 × 0.92 = $82,800.
    *   Total COGS: $30,000.
    *   Gross Profit: $52,800.
    *   New Marketing Budget: $20,000 ($15k base + $5k investment).
    *   **Contribution Margin per Kept Unit:** ($180 - $60 - ($20,000/460)) = **$76.52**.
    *   **Total Monthly Profit Impact:** Scenario B yields ~$12,600 more in gross profit than the current state, easily covering the one-time $5k investment in the first month.

**What Flips It, and The Trigger:**
The deciding input is the **elasticity of returns to information clarity**. If better sizing guides do not reduce returns below 15% within 60 days, only then should you suspect a product-quality mismatch.
*   **Trigger:** If returns remain >15% by [Date 60 days from now], pause marketing spend and conduct customer interviews to verify if the foam quality itself is the issue.
*   **Step:** This week, audit your current size guide against competitor luxury brands; if it lacks visual volume references (e.g., "fits a 40lb Golden Retriever"), replace it immediately.
