**The Call**
Your made-to-order model likely exempts you from Texas finished-goods inventory tax, but your raw material stock ($8,000 now, projected $25,000) remains taxable business personal property; scaling materials increases your tax bill but does not change your fundamental filing status.

**The Numbers**
Texas Business Personal Property (BPP) tax applies to tangible property used to produce income, including raw materials and supplies, regardless of whether finished goods exist.
*   **Formula:** `Taxable Value × Local Tax Rate = Estimated Liability`
*   **Current Scenario:** $8,000 (Raw Materials) × ~2.0% (Avg. TX Combined Rate, ASSUMED) = **~$160/year**
*   **Scaled Scenario:** $25,000 (Raw Materials) × ~2.0% (Avg. TX Combined Rate, ASSUMED) = **~$500/year**
*   **Finished Goods:** $0 (Made-to-order, no liability here).
*   **Assumption:** Local tax rates vary by county/appraisal district (0.5%–3.0% range); the 2.0% figure is an illustrative average.

**What Flips It, and the Trigger**
The single input that changes your compliance burden is crossing the **$2,500 exemption threshold** (a common rule of thumb for many TX appraisal districts, though some vary).
*   **Trigger:** You are already above this with $8,000 in stock.
*   **Impact:** Because you exceed the de minimis threshold, you must file a rendition form annually. Scaling to $25,000 simply increases the assessed value; it does not trigger a new *type* of tax, only a higher payment on the existing BPP category.
*   **Rule of Thumb:** If raw materials exceed $2,500, file the rendition; if under, you might claim exemption (verify locally).
*   **This Week's Step:** If your material costs are rising, consider Y: contact your specific county appraisal district to confirm their exact "free port" or low-value exemption threshold before the April 1 filing deadline.

**Documentation & Filing Route**
To prove made-to-order status and distinguish raw materials during an audit:
1.  **Maintain:** Sales invoices showing production dates *after* payment dates; bills of lading for filament arriving before specific customer orders; a ledger separating "Raw Stock" from "Work in Progress" (which should be near zero).
2.  **Filing Method:** You can likely handle the initial rendition (Form 50-144) through the standard online portal of your county appraisal district. However, **routing note**: Because you are scaling operations and hiring staff, consult a local Texas CPA or property tax consultant to verify if your specific "work-in-progress" valuation methods qualify for any additional manufacturing exemptions and to ensure the rendition categorizes materials correctly to avoid double taxation.
