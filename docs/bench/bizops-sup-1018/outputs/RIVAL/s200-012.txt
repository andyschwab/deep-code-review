Bundling your $12 app fee with the $49 hardware into a single $61 charge significantly alters your sales tax risk profile and likely accelerates your obligation to register in states like California and New York.

Currently, your hardware-only revenue is approximately $7,200 per month per state (150 units × $49), totaling roughly $86,400 annually per state. This keeps you safely under the common $100,000 economic nexus threshold. However, bundling the app fee raises your average transaction value to $61. At 150 monthly sales, your new annual revenue per state becomes approximately $109,800. This single change pushes you over the $100,000 threshold in any state where you maintain that sales volume, immediately triggering economic nexus obligations regardless of your physical inventory location.

The compliance risks are compounded by how states tax bundled transactions. Many jurisdictions apply the "true object" test or specific bundling rules. If a state deems the primary purpose of the transaction to be the taxable hardware, they may tax the entire $61 bundle at the hardware rate. Conversely, some states might unbundle the transaction, taxing the hardware portion while exempting the software, requiring you to track and remit tax on split components. More critically, treating the app as a digital service or SaaS subscription introduces complexity because digital goods taxation varies wildly; some states tax SaaS heavily, while others exempt it. By combining them, you risk having the entire bundle taxed at the highest applicable rate or facing audits for misclassification.

Furthermore, the recurring nature of the app fee does not disappear just because it is bundled upfront. If you later offer renewals or if the state views the $12 as a prepayment for a service, you may create a continuous taxable presence. The primary danger is that by crossing the $100,000 threshold via the bundle, you establish nexus. Once nexus is established, you are generally required to collect tax on *all* taxable sales into that state, not just those exceeding the threshold. This could retroactively complicate your filings if you cross the threshold mid-year.

You must consult a **state and local tax (SALT) attorney** or a **CPA specializing in SALT and digital goods** before launching. A general business CPA or standard tax preparer is insufficient for this specific issue because the intersection of physical tangible personal property (hardware), digital services (app), bundling rules, and multi-state economic nexus thresholds requires specialized legal interpretation. Do not rely on generic advice; the penalties for uncollected sales tax after establishing nexus can include back taxes, interest, and significant fines.
