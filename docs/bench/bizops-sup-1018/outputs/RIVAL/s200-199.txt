**Methodology for Quantifying Viable Market Size**

1.  **Segment by Connectivity Tier:** Do not treat the 5,000 farms as a monolith. You must overlay your customer list with broadband availability maps (e.g., FCC National Broadband Map) to categorize farms into three buckets:
    *   **High Viability:** Reliable broadband (>25 Mbps). These users can complete online training without intervention.
    *   **Marginal Viability:** Intermittent connectivity. These users face high friction and contribute to your 40% dropout rate.
    *   **Non-Viable:** No reliable internet. These users cannot access the product in its current form.

2.  **Calculate Realizable Revenue (Current Model):**
    Apply your known dropout metrics to the "Marginal" segment. If 40% of students in poor bandwidth areas drop out, your effective conversion in that segment is only 60%.
    *   *Formula:* `(High Viability Count × $2,500) + (Marginal Count × 0.60 × $2,500)`
    *   *Hidden Cost Deduction:* Subtract the support cost for failures: `(Marginal Count × 0.40 × $300)`.
    *   *Result:* This yields your true net revenue potential under the status quo. The difference between this number and the theoretical $12.5M is your **Revenue Loss due to Infrastructure Mismatch**.

3.  **The "Unknown Factor": Community Hubs as Force Multipliers**
    You correctly identified local co-ops and community centers as a variable. In rural Midwest agriculture, these are not just possibilities; they are critical infrastructure. By partnering with local Farm Bureaus, cooperatives, or extension offices, you can convert "Non-Viable" and "Marginal" farms into "High Viability" accounts.
    *   **Hybrid Model Adjustment:** Instead of selling individual online seats, sell "Hub Licenses." A local co-op hosts a weekly download/upload session or a local server cache.
    *   **Market Expansion:** If 30% of your "Non-Viable" farms are within 15 miles of a potential hub partner, you can immediately reclaim that portion of the market. This shifts the cost structure from per-student support ($300 loss) to a B2B partnership model (e.g., $5,000/year per co-op for unlimited local access), drastically improving unit economics.
