### Blended CAC Structure for TestCloud

To present an accurate picture to your board, you must stop calculating a single "blended" CAC across disparate segments. Combining a 14-day SMB cycle with a 9-month enterprise cycle into one average creates a misleading metric that obscures both your efficient self-serve motion and your capital-intensive sales motion. Instead, structure your board deck around **Segment-Specific CAC** and a **Weighted Blended CAC** that accounts for sales cycle timing.

**1. Calculate Segment-Specific CAC (The Primary Metric)**
Your board needs to see the efficiency of each engine separately. Based on your data:
*   **Total Spend:** $350,000 ($150k Marketing + $200k Sales).
*   **Allocation Assumption:** Since enterprise deals require heavy sales involvement and SMBs are likely marketing-led, you must allocate costs proportionally. A reasonable split for your model: 70% of sales salaries to Enterprise, 30% to SMB; Marketing split 50/50.
    *   *Enterprise Cost:* $140k (Sales) + $75k (Mkt) = $215,000.
    *   *SMB Cost:* $60k (Sales) + $75k (Mkt) = $135,000.
*   **Resulting CAC:**
    *   **Enterprise CAC:** $215,000 / 12 clients = **$17,916** (Not $25k; your previous calc likely over-allocated costs).
    *   **SMB CAC:** $135,000 / 80 customers = **$1,687**.

**2. Address the Timing Mismatch (The "Cohort" Adjustment)**
The distortion you fear comes from recognizing revenue too early against costs incurred today. For the board deck, introduce a **Payback Period** metric alongside CAC.
*   **SMB Payback:** $1,687 CAC / ($1,200 ACV / 12 months) ≈ **17 months**.
*   **Enterprise Payback:** $17,916 CAC / ($45,000 ACV / 12 months) ≈ **4.8 months**.
*   *Insight:* Despite the high absolute CAC, your enterprise segment is actually *more* efficient in cash-flow terms because the contract value is so large. The long sales cycle is a cash-flow challenge, not necessarily an efficiency failure.

**3. The "Single Number" for the Board (Weighted by Revenue)**
If finance demands a single blended number, do not weight by *customer count*. Weight by *revenue contribution* to reflect where your business value lies.
*   Total New Revenue: (80 × $1,200) + (12 × $45,000) = $96,000 + $540,000 = $636,000.
*   Enterprise Revenue Share: 85%.
*   SMB Revenue Share: 15%.
*   **Revenue-Weighted Blended CAC:** ($17,916 × 0.85) + ($1,687 × 0.15) = **$15,481**.

**Recommendation for the 50% Budget Increase**
Present the argument that the "high" enterprise CAC is an investment in high-LTV accounts with a sub-5-month payback. The requested 50% increase should be explicitly tagged to **Enterprise Sales capacity** to shorten that 9-month cycle. If you can reduce the cycle to 6 months, your cash efficiency improves dramatically without changing the CAC.

**Summary Table for Deck:**
| Metric | SMB | Enterprise | Blended (Revenue-Wtd) |
| :--- | :--- | :--- | :--- |
| **CAC** | $1,687 | $17,916 | $15,481 |
| **Payback Period** | 17 Months | 4.8 Months | ~6 Months |
| **Strategy** | Optimize Marketing | Add Sales Headcount | Hybrid Growth |

Use the segment-specific data to prove that the enterprise engine is profitable despite the long cycle, justifying the budget increase to accelerate those deals.

*Note: Ensure your cost allocation logic (Step 1) is documented in an appendix, as shifting the sales/marketing split ratio will materially change the absolute CAC numbers.*
