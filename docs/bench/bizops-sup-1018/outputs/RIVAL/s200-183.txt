Based on the facts provided, here is an outline of the disclosure obligations and strategic considerations for ScholarLink regarding your Article Processing Charge (APC) model under European Union guidelines.

**Regulatory Landscape and Disclosure Obligations**

Currently, there is no specific EU statutory law that explicitly bans the APC model or mandates a unique "conflict of interest" disclaimer solely because an author pays a fee. The European Commission and bodies like COPE (Committee on Publication Ethics) recognize APCs as a legitimate funding mechanism for Open Access, provided strict separation exists between payment and editorial decision-making.

However, you are subject to general consumer protection laws (Unfair Commercial Practices Directive) and emerging research integrity frameworks. The primary obligation is **transparency**, not prohibition. You must clearly disclose that the fee covers publication services (editing, hosting, indexing) and is charged *only after* acceptance, not as a condition for review.

**Specific Disclosure Requirements**

To mitigate perceived conflicts and comply with best practices (such as those from the OASPA and COPE), your submission portal and editorial policies must explicitly state:

1.  **Separation of Duties:** A clear statement that editorial decisions are made independently of financial considerations. Editors must not know the payment status of a manuscript during the peer-review process.
2.  **Fee Timing:** Explicit confirmation that the €1,200 APC is invoiced only *after* a paper has been accepted through peer review, never before.
3.  **Waiver Policies:** A publicly accessible policy detailing discounts or waivers for authors from low-income countries or those without funding, which is increasingly expected under Plan S principles.
4.  **Service Breakdown:** A itemized explanation of what the fee covers (e.g., platform maintenance, DOI registration, long-term archiving) to distinguish the transaction from "buying" acceptance.

**Risk Assessment of Current Structure**

Your current volume (50 submissions/month) and cost structure (€25,000/month operating cost vs. ~€60,000/month potential revenue at 100% acceptance) suggest a healthy margin, but the *perception* of a "pay-to-publish" scheme is your highest risk. If authors believe payment guarantees acceptance, your reputation and legal standing could be damaged by accusations of predatory publishing.

While self-regulatory codes (COPE, OASPA) are currently sufficient, they are strict. Failure to adhere to their transparency guidelines can lead to delisting from major directories (like DOAJ), which effectively kills visibility. There is no immediate statutory requirement to alter your fee amount, but you must alter your **communication** of that fee.

**Recommended Actions**

1.  **Update Submission Portal:** Add a mandatory checkbox for authors to acknowledge that "Payment of the APC is not a condition for acceptance and occurs only post-review."
2.  **Editorial Policy Revision:** Publish a formal "Ethics and Integrity" page detailing the firewall between the editorial board and the finance department.
3.  **Audit Peer Review:** Ensure your 12 editors are trained to reject papers on merit alone, regardless of the author's ability to pay.

You do not need to change your €1,200 price point, but you must rigorously document and display the firewall between money and acceptance to avoid regulatory scrutiny and reputational harm.
