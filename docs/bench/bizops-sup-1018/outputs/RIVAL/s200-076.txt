### Immediate Action Plan: The Unit Economics Audit

Your current model is mathematically broken. You are generating $3,500 in revenue against $30,000 in costs, resulting in a **negative unit economics ratio of -8.5x**. With $200k in seed funding, you have approximately 6–7 months of runway left if costs remain static, assuming zero other operational expenses. You must treat this as a crisis, not an optimization problem.

Stop analyzing "lifetime value" in the abstract; for infrastructure-heavy products, LTV is irrelevant if the Cost to Serve (CTS) exceeds revenue on day one. Instead, execute this three-step audit immediately:

**1. Segment Free Users by Infrastructure Cost (The "Whale" Hunt)**
Do not look at total free users (5,000). Isolate the top 5% of free accounts responsible for 80% of your $30k bill. These are likely developers running load tests, not evaluating your product for future purchase.
*   **Action:** Filter free users by bandwidth/CPU consumption. Identify accounts exceeding a specific threshold (e.g., >$50/mo in infra cost).
*   **Decision Rule:** If a free user costs you >$10/month in infrastructure and has not converted within 14 days, they are a liability, not a lead.

**2. Calculate the "Paid Conversion Efficiency" Ratio**
Analyze the conversion path of your 120 paid users. Did they come from the heavy-usage segment or the light-usage segment?
*   **Hypothesis:** Heavy users (load testers) rarely convert to paid plans because they need massive scale for free; light users (functional testers) convert because they value reliability over volume.
*   **Metric:** Compare conversion rates of users under 100MB/day traffic vs. users over 1GB/day. If the heavy users have a conversion rate <1%, they are subsidizing nothing.

**3. Implement Hard Usage Caps Immediately**
You cannot afford a "soft" freemium model. You must decouple "testing functionality" from "testing scale."
*   **Restriction:** Cap free tiers at a level that allows functional testing (e.g., 1,000 requests/day or 500MB bandwidth) but makes load testing impossible.
*   **Pivot:** Move heavy usage to a "Pro Trial" (time-boxed, e.g., 7 days of high limits) or a paid "Load Testing" add-on.
*   **Communication:** Frame this as "ensuring reliability for all users" rather than a price hike.

**Recommendation on Model Pivot**
Given your burn rate, **restrict the free tier immediately**. Your data suggests you are subsidizing non-paying users who have no intent to buy.
*   **New Model:** Free Tier (Functional testing only, strict low limits) + 14-Day Pro Trial (High limits for evaluation) + Paid Plans (Scale).
*   **Goal:** Reduce infrastructure costs by 60–70% within 30 days by cutting off the load-testing freeloaders.

Your goal is not to maximize free signups; it is to maximize *profitable* signups. A smaller free tier with higher conversion intent is infinitely more valuable than 5,000 users draining your seed capital.

**Note:** Do not wait for perfect data; your current burn rate demands immediate caps on high-cost free accounts.
**Note:** Communicate changes to existing heavy free users with a grace period to avoid immediate backlash, but enforce limits strictly for new signups today.
