In the food industry, **liability rarely transfers automatically** to the co-packer, even if they are at fault. As the brand owner (FrostBite Pro), you are generally considered the "manufacturer of record" by regulators (FDA) and consumers. If a listeria outbreak occurs, your brand name is on the label, making you the primary target for lawsuits, recalls, and regulatory fines. A vague contract does not protect you; it exposes you.

**Standard Liability Dynamics:**
1.  **Regulatory Liability:** The FDA holds the brand owner responsible for the safety of the product sold under their name. You cannot contract away your regulatory obligations.
2.  **Consumer Lawsuits:** Plaintiffs will sue the brand because you are the visible entity with assets ($200k cash) and insurance. They may also sue the co-packer, but you will almost certainly be named first.
3.  **Indemnification:** This is your only contractual shield. If the contract is vague, you have no guaranteed recourse to recover costs from the co-packer after you pay a settlement. You need a **broad-form indemnification clause** specifically covering food safety breaches, negligence, and regulatory non-compliance caused by the co-packer's operations.

**Immediate Red Flags in Your Arrangement:**
*   **Vague Indemnification:** If the contract does not explicitly state that the co-packer must indemnify (reimburse) you for all costs arising from their negligence (including legal fees, recall costs, and settlements), you are personally liable.
*   **Lack of Insurance Requirements:** Paying a per-unit fee does not guarantee they carry sufficient insurance. If they cause a $2M recall and only have $500k in coverage, you are on the hook for the remaining $1.5M.
*   **"Flat Fee" Ambiguity:** Ensure the $0.90 fee structure doesn't inadvertently limit their liability cap to the value of the production run. Liability caps should never be lower than potential catastrophic loss.

**Actionable Steps for Your Contract Negotiation:**
1.  **Demand Specific Insurance Coverage:** Require the co-packer to maintain **Product Liability Insurance** and **General Liability Insurance** with limits sufficient to cover a major outbreak. For a brand scaling to 10,000 gallons/month, do not accept less than **$2M–$5M per occurrence** and **$5M aggregate**. Require them to list FrostBite Pro as an **"Additional Insured"** on their policy. This ensures their insurer defends *you* directly if a claim arises from their facility.
2.  **Strengthen Indemnification Clauses:** The contract must explicitly state that the co-packer assumes full financial responsibility for any contamination, recall, or safety violation originating from their facility, equipment, or processes. 3.  **Define "Cause" Clearly:** Specify that liability transfers to the co-packer if the breach results from their sanitation failures, equipment malfunction, or employee negligence.
4.  **Audit Rights:** Include a clause allowing you to conduct unannounced food safety audits of their facility.
5.  **Recall Protocol:** Define exactly who pays for what during a recall (logistics, destruction, public relations, customer refunds). Do not leave this open to interpretation.
