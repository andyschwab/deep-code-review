### Market Sizing Methodology for LinguaFlow Tutoring Service

To calculate the Total Addressable Market (TAM) for your new $30/hour tutoring feature without double-counting, you must move from your total registered base down to a "Serviceable Obtainable Market" using a funnel that isolates intent and excludes existing premium subscribers.

**1. The Segmentation Funnel**

Do not apply a single conversion rate to your total user base. Instead, segment your 200,000 users into three distinct buckets before applying conversion logic:

*   **Bucket A: Existing Paid Subscribers (Exclude or Upsell Only)**
    *   *Action:* Remove these users from your "new revenue" TAM calculation unless you are specifically modeling an upsell path. If they already pay for self-study tools, they are not "new" market size; they are expansion revenue.
    *   *Estimate:* If 15% of your 200k are paid (based on 85% free), that is 30,000 users. Set these aside for a separate "Expansion TAM."

*   **Bucket B: Free Users – Low Intent (Exclude)**
    *   *Action:* Exclude the 90% of free users who do not engage daily. Live tutoring at $30/hour is a high-commitment purchase; non-daily users rarely convert to high-ticket services immediately.
    *   *Calculation:* 200,000 total - 30,000 paid = 170,000 free.
    *   170,000 × 10% (Daily Active) = **17,000 High-Intent Free Users.**

*   **Bucket C: The Tutoring TAM (The Target)**
    *   *Action:* Apply your tutoring-specific conversion rate only to Bucket B.
    *   *Calculation:* 17,000 Daily Active Free Users × 2% (Tutoring Conversion) = **340 Users.**

**2. The Realistic Market Size Calculation**

Using your provided 2% assumption on the daily active pool yields a near-term market size of **340 users**.

*   **Monthly Revenue Potential:** 340 users × (Assumed 2 hours/month) × $30 = ~$20,400 MRR.
*   **Annual Revenue Potential:** ~$244,800.

*Note:* If you apply the 2% to the *total* registered base (200,000 × 2% = 4,000 users), you are likely overestimating by 10x because you are ignoring the intent filter (daily activity) and potentially double-counting existing paid users who may not need tutoring.

**3. Critical Unknown Variables Skewing Projections**

Your current model assumes "interest in the app" equals "interest in live tutoring." This is the highest risk area for investor scrutiny. You must address these unknowns:

*   **Self-Study vs. Human Interaction Intent:** Many language learners specifically choose apps to *avoid* the anxiety or cost of live conversation. Your 17,000 daily users might be highly engaged with gamified lessons but have zero willingness to pay for live human interaction. The 2% conversion rate for a *feature* (like offline mode) rarely applies to a *service* (live tutoring).
*   **Price Sensitivity at $30/Hour:** You have not validated willingness to pay (WTP) for this specific price point. A user willing to pay $10/month for an app may balk at $30 for a single hour. If the real conversion rate is 0.5% rather than 2%, your market size drops from 340 to 85 users.
*   **Cannibalization:** Will users cancel their existing self-study subscriptions to spend that budget entirely on tutoring? If so, your net new revenue is lower than gross tutoring revenue.

**Recommendation for Investor Deck:**
Present a "Conservative," "Base," and "Optimistic" scenario.
*   **Conservative:** 0.5% conversion of daily active free users (85 users).
*   **Base:** 2% conversion of daily active free users (340 users).
*   **Optimistic:** 2% conversion of *all* free users (3,400 users) — *only* if you have data proving non-daily users are seeking tutoring.

Label the 2% of daily users as your "Serviceable Obtainable Market (SOM)" for Year 1, rather than your total TAM, to show investors you understand the difference between user base size and actual purchasable intent.

*Ensure you explicitly state that existing paid subscribers are treated as a separate upsell opportunity to avoid double-counting revenue in your projections.*
*Consider running a "fake door" test on the 17,000 daily users to validate the 2% assumption before finalizing the deck.*
