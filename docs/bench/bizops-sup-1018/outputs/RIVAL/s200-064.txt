**Diagnosis: This is a Product-Market Fit (PMF) and Packaging issue, not a pricing level issue.**

Do not lower your prices. A 20% discount on the Starter plan ($49 → ~$39) will not fix 8% monthly churn, but it will destroy your unit economics and signal desperation to investors. Your churn rate suggests customers are not deriving enough recurring value to justify staying, regardless of cost.

**Analysis of Conflicting Signals**
The contradictory feedback from your 40 departing customers is actually a clear pattern when viewed through a segmentation lens:
*   **"Too expensive"** usually comes from users on the **Starter plan** who are not activating core value. If a startup isn't integrating your flags deeply into their deployment pipeline within the first 14 days, $49 feels like waste. They aren't saying "$49 is too high for this value"; they are saying "The value I'm getting is near zero."
*   **"Lacks essential integrations"** comes from users attempting to derive value but hitting a hard ceiling. These are likely customers who *would* pay more but cannot because the product doesn't fit their stack. They are churning due to utility gaps, not price sensitivity.

**The Real Problem: Misaligned Value Metric and Onboarding**
Your 8% churn indicates that 60% of your customers likely never reach the "aha" moment where feature flags become indispensable. In the feature flag market, stickiness comes from deep integration (CI/CD pipelines, specific frameworks). If your Starter plan lacks the specific integrations your UK startup cohort needs, they sign up, fail to implement, and leave.

Lowering the price attracts *more* of the wrong customers—those with even lower willingness to pay and higher support needs—while doing nothing to solve the integration gap driving the "Pro" prospects away.

**Action Plan: Pull the Product Lever First**

1.  **Freeze Pricing Immediately:** Maintain the $49/$299 structure. Changing this now introduces a variable you cannot control during your seed raise. Investors want to see that you can retain customers at current prices through product improvements.
2.  **Audit the "Starter" Integration Gap:** Analyze the 40 churned accounts. Did they attempt to use an integration you don't support? If 50% of churn cites missing integrations, your roadmap must prioritize the top 3 requested connectors immediately. This is a packaging failure, not a price failure.
3.  **Fix Onboarding, Not Price:** For new signups, implement a "Time-to-First-Flag" metric. If a user hasn't shipped a flag within 24 hours, trigger high-touch onboarding (a personal call from you). The goal is to force value realization before the first billing cycle.
4.  **Re-segment the Feedback:** Separate the "price" complainers by usage. If they used the product <2 times, the issue is onboarding. If they used it heavily but still called it expensive, *then* you have a pricing problem. Given your metrics, it is almost certainly the former.

**Investor Narrative Strategy**
When approaching VCs, do not present a price cut. Present a hypothesis: "We identified that churn was driven by missing integrations for the SMB segment. We are deploying X and Y integrations to capture this value, projecting churn reduction to 4% within two quarters." This shows command of your business mechanics. Discounting suggests you don't believe in your product's value; fixing the product gap shows you understand your market.

*Note: Your server costs ($3k/mo) against $16k/mo revenue leaves healthy margins; the break-even fear is unfounded unless churn accelerates further.*
*Focus entirely on reducing time-to-value for the Starter tier to stop the bleeding.*
