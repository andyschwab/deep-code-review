### Dilution Analysis for Canvas Global Series A

**1. The Math: How Much Equity You Are Selling**
To find the percentage of the company you are giving up, you divide the investment amount by the post-money valuation.

*   **Investment Amount:** €4,000,000
*   **Pre-Money Valuation:** €16,000,000
*   **Post-Money Valuation:** €16,000,000 + €4,000,000 = **€20,000,000**

**Dilution Calculation:**
€4,000,000 (Investment) ÷ €20,000,000 (Post-Money) = **0.20 or 20%**

In this specific deal, you will give up **20%** of your company. Your existing ownership stake (yours and your co-founders') will be diluted by 20%. If you currently own 100% collectively, you will own 80% after the round.

**2. Is This Reasonable? (Benchmarking)**
Yes, this falls squarely within standard ranges for a Series A round.

*   **Standard Range:** Typically, founders sell between **15% and 25%** of their company in a Series A.
*   **The "Sweet Spot":** Many venture capitalists target owning **20%** post-investment to ensure they have enough equity to remain motivated through future rounds while leaving sufficient ownership for the founding team.
*   **Verdict:** Selling exactly 20% is a market-standard outcome. It is not excessively dilutive, nor is it unusually low.

**3. Strategic Context for Your Discussion**
While the dilution percentage is standard, your specific situation presents unique leverage points and risks to discuss with your co-founders:

*   **Runway vs. Growth:** You have 8 months of runway. With a burn of €120k/month, you are burning roughly €960k over that period. The €4M raise gives you significant capital, but your plan involves an "aggressive marketing campaign" in new regions (SE Asia, Latin America). Customer Acquisition Costs (CAC) in new markets can be unpredictable. Ensure the €4M is sufficient to reach the *next* milestone (Series B) without needing a "bridge" round, which would cause further dilution.
*   **Unit Economics Check:** Your current churn is 3.5% monthly (~35% annually). For a subscription business, this is on the higher side. Investors may accept this for now given the growth phase, but expanding into new international markets could temporarily worsen churn if product-market fit isn't perfectly localized. Be prepared to explain how the new capital will specifically address retention, not just acquisition.
*   **Valuation Logic:** At a €16M pre-money valuation, you are valued at roughly **3.8x** your current Annual Recurring Revenue (ARR).
    *   Current ARR: 4,200 students × €89 × 12 months ≈ **€4.48M**.
    *   Multiple: €16M / €4.48M ≈ **3.57x**.
    *   This is a reasonable, perhaps slightly conservative, multiple for an education tech company, suggesting the term sheet is fair but not overly generous.

**Summary for Co-Founders:**
The deal requires selling **20%** of the company. This is **standard market practice** for Series A. The critical discussion should not be about the percentage lost, but whether the €4M capital is enough to execute the international expansion and lower that 3.5% churn rate before the money runs out.
