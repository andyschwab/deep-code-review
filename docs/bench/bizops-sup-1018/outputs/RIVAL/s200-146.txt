### Strategic Evaluation: The Nonprofit Freemium Model

Your concern is valid but solvable. Giving 40% of your user base away for free does not inherently signal poor product-market fit (PMF) to Venture Capitalists (VCs); however, presenting it as a standard "freemium" growth hack will. If you frame this as a customer acquisition cost (CAC) strategy, investors will see distorted unit economics. If you frame it as a **strategic distribution and validation channel**, it becomes a competitive moat.

**How Investors Will View This**
Canadian and global impact-focused VCs prioritize **sustainable unit economics** and **clear paths to scale**. They do not expect every user to pay, but they do expect every user to contribute to the business value.
*   **The Risk:** If you present 40% zero-revenue users alongside low overall conversion rates, investors will assume you cannot monetize the market. They will fear your Total Addressable Market (TAM) is effectively 60% of what you claim.
*   **The Opportunity:** If nonprofits are your *distribution* mechanism to reach the actual end-users (bereaved individuals) who might pay, or if the nonprofits validate the efficacy of the platform for B2B/Government contracts later, the free tier is an asset, not a liability.

**Reframing the Narrative for Your Pitch Deck**
Do not label this "Freemium." Label it **"Verified Partner Access"** or **"Community Infrastructure."**

1.  **Separate the Metrics:** In your deck, create two distinct funnels.
    *   *Funnel A (Commercial):* Individuals/For-profit clinics. Measure strict ARPU, LTV, and CAC. This proves you can make money.
    *   *Funnel B (Impact/Channel):* Nonprofits. Measure *engagement*, *retention*, and *referral volume*. Do not blend these conversion rates with commercial metrics.
2.  **Define the Value Exchange:** Explicitly state what you get from the 40% free users. Is it data? Is it network effects? Is it that a bereaved individual joins via a nonprofit and then converts to a paid "premium support" tier? If the free users never convert and generate no referrals, they are a cost center. You must articulate the indirect revenue path.
3.  **The "Land and Expand" Logic:** Position the nonprofit tier as the "Land." The "Expand" must be clear. Are you selling enterprise analytics to large health organizations? Are you licensing the tech to government bodies based on nonprofit adoption? Investors need to see that the free tier lowers the barrier to entry for a much larger paid contract later.

**Answering Your Specific Questions**
*   **Will investors see this as lack of PMF?** Only if you cannot prove that the free users drive value for the paid users or future contracts. If the free users are dead weight, yes, it looks like failed PMF. If they are active, referring paid users, or validating the model for government grants, it looks like a smart go-to-market strategy.
*   **Active Users vs. Conversion Rates:** For a marketplace or network-effect platform, **Active Users** (specifically *engaged* users) often matter more initially than conversion rates, *provided* you have a credible hypothesis for monetization later. However, since 40% are contractually free, investors will scrutinize the conversion rate of the *remaining* 60% heavily. That segment must show strong willingness to pay to prove the underlying value metric works.

**Actionable Advice for the Deck**
*   **Metric to Highlight:** "Cost of Distribution." Show that acquiring these nonprofit partners costs significantly less than buying ads to reach individuals, making your blended CAC efficient.
*   **The Ask:** When raising your Series A, explicitly state that the $4M will be used to monetize the *infrastructure* built by the nonprofit network (e.g., selling to insurers, governments, or corporate wellness programs), not just to convert nonprofits themselves.
*   **Burn Rate Context:** With $30k/month burn and $600k left, you have 20 months of runway. This is healthy. Use this time to prove that the nonprofit segment drives *paid* segment growth. If you reach Series A with high nonprofit usage but zero spillover into paid tiers or B2B contracts, the narrative will fail.

**Note:** Ensure your "verified nonprofit" process is rigorous; loose verification invites abuse and dilutes the exclusivity of the partner program.
**Note:** Consider a "freemium" cap (e.g., limited group sizes) even for nonprofits to preserve a potential upsell path for larger organizations.
