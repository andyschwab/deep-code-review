To size your market accurately for SoftSkill Sync, you must abandon "global remote worker" counts as a primary metric. That number is too broad and includes individuals and companies with zero training budgets. Instead, build a **Bottom-Up TAM model** focused on **HR Training Budgets** and **Ideal Customer Profiles (ICP)**.

Here is the step-by-step framework to distinguish between your asynchronous video and live facilitated segments:

### 1. Define Your Unit of Value (The Denominator)
Do not count "workers." Count **paying entities**.
*   **For Asynchronous Video:** Your unit is the **SMB/Mid-Market Company** (likely 50–500 employees) that buys self-serve tools. Your current $2k price point suggests this is your volume driver.
*   **For Live Facilitation:** Your unit is the **Enterprise/Large Mid-Market Company** (500+ employees) with dedicated L&D (Learning & Development) budgets and HR staff to manage vendors. Your $15k price point confirms this is a high-touch sale.

### 2. The Calculation Formula
Calculate TAM for each segment using this specific chain:

**Segment A: Asynchronous Video TAM**
1.  **Total US Companies** in your target size range (e.g., 50–500 employees). *Source: US Census Bureau or Crunchbase.*
2.  **Filter for Remote/Hybrid:** Apply a percentage estimate of those with significant remote workforces (e.g., 40%).
3.  **Filter for Budget Intent:** Estimate the % of those with a dedicated L&D budget >$5k/year.
4.  **Multiply by Price:** (Resulting Company Count) × $2,000.

**Segment B: Live Facilitation TAM**
1.  **Total US Companies** in your target size range (e.g., 500+ employees).
2.  **Filter for Remote/Hybrid:** Apply the remote workforce percentage.
3.  **Filter for Budget Intent:** Estimate the % with an L&D budget >$20k/year (since your price is $15k, they need room in the budget).
4.  **Multiply by Price:** (Resulting Company Count) × $15,000.

### 3. Data Sources & Reliability
*   **Most Reliable:** **Number of companies by employee count** (US Census/SBA data) and **average L&D spend per employee** (ATD or LinkedIn Learning reports). These are hard numbers.
*   **Least Reliable:** "Global remote worker" stats. These fluctuate wildly by definition and include freelancers who will never buy your corporate product.
*   **The Critical Filter:** Look for data on **"Self-serve SaaS adoption" vs. "Services procurement cycles."** The video market is limited by how many companies buy software without a sales rep. The live market is limited by how many companies have a procurement process for external trainers.

### 4. Strategic Recommendation for Your $100k Budget
Based on your current traction (800 video clients vs. 40 live clients), your **Asynchronous Video** segment likely has a larger *volume* TAM, but the **Live Facilitation** segment may have a higher *value* TAM per customer.

However, for US expansion:
*   **Video Scale:** Requires marketing spend to reach thousands of SMBs. The market is wider, but customer acquisition costs (CAC) must remain very low.
*   **Live Scale:** Requires sales headcount to close fewer, larger deals. The market is narrower, but one client equals 7.5 video clients.

**Actionable Next Step:**
Do not guess the filters. Take your existing 800 video clients and 40 live clients. Analyze their employee counts and industries. If 90% of your video clients are under 200 employees and live clients are over 1,000, apply those exact employee-count filters to the US company database. This creates a "Serviceable Addressable Market" (SAM) that is realistic, rather than a theoretical TAM.

Allocate the $100k to the segment where your **current win rate** multiplied by the **US SAM count** yields the highest projected revenue, not just the largest total number of companies.

*Note: Prioritize US-specific data over global stats to avoid inflating your model with irrelevant markets. Ensure you distinguish between "training budget" (general) and "soft skills budget" (specific) for higher accuracy.*
