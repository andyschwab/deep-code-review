### Financial Impact Analysis: Service Burden on Unit Economics

Your current model is indeed deteriorating. Here is the breakdown of your unit economics before and after the service cost increase, followed by the impact of your three proposed options.

**Current State (Per Customer):**
*   **Annual Revenue:** $30,000 ($2,500 × 12)
*   **Old Service Cost:** $750 (5 hrs × $150)
*   **New Service Cost:** $3,000 (20 hrs × $150)
*   **Gross Margin Shift:** Your service cost has jumped from 2.5% of revenue to 10%. While your software margin is 72%, the *blended* gross margin including services drops significantly when factoring in this new burden.
*   **Customer Lifetime (L):** At 3% monthly churn, average lifespan is ~33 months.
*   **Current LTV (Software Only):** ~$72,000.
*   **Current LTV (Net of Services):** ~$63,000 (assuming old 5hr cost).
*   **New LTV (Net of Services):** ~$54,000 (assuming new 20hr cost).
*   **Impact:** You have lost ~$9,000 in lifetime value per customer solely due to service inefficiency.

**Option 1: Increase Base Subscription Price**
*   **Strategy:** Raise price to $3,000/mo (+20%) to absorb the $2,250 annual cost increase.
*   **LTV Impact:** Restores LTV to ~$66,000.
*   **Risk:** High. A 20% hike may spike churn beyond 3%, especially if competitors offer self-serve alternatives. If churn rises to 4%, lifespan drops to 25 months, negating the price increase.
*   **Payback Period:** Extends initially due to potential sales friction, but recovers if churn holds.

**Option 2: Unbundle Services (Recommended)**
*   **Strategy:** Keep subscription at $2,500. Charge $200/hr for professional services (cost $150, margin 25%). Include only 5 hours in the base tier; bill the remaining 15 hours.
*   **Revenue Impact:** Subscription remains $30k. Services revenue adds $3,000 (15 hrs × $200). Total ACV: $33,000.
*   **Cost Impact:** You still incur $3,000 in costs, but $2,250 is now covered by direct billing.
*   **LTV Impact:** Restores LTV to ~$65,000+ while maintaining price integrity.
*   **Benefit:** Forces customers to value the service. Often, when billed directly, clients reduce unnecessary requests, naturally lowering the 20-hour average.

**Option 3: Reduce Included Support Hours**
*   **Strategy:** Cap included onboarding at 5 hours. Anything extra is a change order or requires the customer to do it themselves.
*   **LTV Impact:** If customers adapt and stay at 5 hours, LTV returns to ~$63,000. If they churn because they can't onboard, LTV collapses.
*   **Risk:** High churn risk if the product genuinely requires 20 hours to derive value. This suggests a product-market fit or usability issue, not just a pricing issue.

**Recommendation:**
Adopt **Option 2 (Unbundling)** immediately. It aligns price with value consumption, protects your software margin, and creates a feedback loop where clients self-optimize their service usage. Simultaneously, investigate *why* onboarding time quadrupled; this is likely a product usability debt that needs engineering attention.

**Weekly Metrics to Track:**
1.  **Services Attach Rate:** % of new customers purchasing additional hours.
2.  **Average Onboarding Duration:** Trend line of hours per client (target: reduction).
3.  **Blended Gross Margin:** (Total Revenue - COGS - Services Cost) / Total Revenue.
