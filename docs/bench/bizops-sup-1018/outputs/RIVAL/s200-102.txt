**1. The Federal "Economic Reality" Test (FLSA)**
The Department of Labor focuses on whether the worker is economically dependent on you or in business for themselves. Key factors include:
*   **Opportunity for Profit/Loss:** Can caregivers increase earnings by working more efficiently, marketing themselves, or managing expenses? If their income is strictly tied to hours booked through you with no ability to impact profit beyond working more hours, this leans toward employee status.
*   **Investment:** Do they invest in their own equipment or marketing? Using their own transportation is a factor, but if you provide all leads and set the rates, their investment is minimal.
*   **Permanence:** Is the relationship indefinite? Exclusive or near-exclusive work for one platform suggests employment.
*   **Control:** This is often the deciding factor. Do you set the schedule, mandate specific care methods, or require uniforms? If families set schedules and caregivers determine how to provide care, this supports contractor status. If CareConnect dictates shifts and procedures, it suggests employment.

**2. Florida Common Law Test**
Florida generally looks at the degree of control the hiring entity exercises.
*   **Supervision:** Do you supervise the daily details of the care?
*   **Integration:** Is the caregiving service integral to your business? Since matching caregivers is your core business model, courts often view these workers as integral, which can lean toward employee status unless strict independence is proven.
*   **Skill:** Does the work require specialized skills? While caregiving requires training, if the skills are not unique to an independent trade, this may not protect contractor status.

**Potential Risks of Misclassification**
If a government agency or court reclassifies your caregivers as employees, potential liabilities include:
*   **Back Payroll Taxes:** You could be liable for the employer portion of Social Security and Medicare taxes (7.65%) for all past wages, plus unpaid unemployment insurance taxes.
*   **Penalties and Interest:** The IRS and state agencies impose significant penalties for failure to file returns and pay taxes, plus interest accruing from the due date.
*   **Overtime and Minimum Wage:** You may owe back pay for any hours worked over 40 in a week (if not properly exempt) or below minimum wage after expenses.
*   **Benefits:** Potential liability for unpaid benefits if you offered them to other employees.
