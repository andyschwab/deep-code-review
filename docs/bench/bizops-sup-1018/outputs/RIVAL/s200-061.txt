### The "Three-Circle" TAM Framework for CloudNimbus

To satisfy both conservative accountants and aggressive VCs without compromising credibility, abandon the binary choice of "containers in" or "containers out." Instead, present a **Three-Circle TAM** in your pitch deck. This approach acknowledges the shifting definition of serverless as a market reality rather than a confusion point, turning your dilemma into a strategic narrative.

**Circle 1: Core TAM (The "Honest" Number – $2B)**
Define this strictly as **Event-Driven, Ephemeral Compute**. This includes only workloads that fit CloudNimbus's current architecture: true serverless functions where the user manages zero infrastructure and billing is purely per-request.
*   **Narrative:** "This is our beachhead. It is where we currently win, where our unit economics are proven, and where our product-market fit is undeniable."
*   **Audience:** Satisfies accountants and due diligence teams by grounding your valuation in current, verifiable revenue streams.

**Circle 2: Serviceable Expandable Market (The "Bridge" – $7B)**
Define this as **Cloud-Native Microservices**, including containerized workloads that *could* migrate to serverless but currently sit on managed Kubernetes or ECS. This is not "all containers," but specifically those running in cloud environments suitable for refactoring.
*   **Narrative:** "This is our 18–24 month roadmap. As we introduce container support or sidecar architectures, these are the specific workloads we will capture. We are not guessing; we are tracking our own users who are hitting limits on pure functions and asking for container support."
*   **Audience:** Shows VCs you understand the technical convergence of the market without claiming you can instantly service the entire container universe.

**Circle 3: Total Addressable Market (The "Vision" – $12B)**
Define this as the **Broad Cloud Compute Frontier**, encompassing all serverless and containerized microservices globally.
*   **Narrative:** "This is the ceiling if we become the default compute layer for the cloud-native era. While we start with Circle 1, our architecture is designed to expand into Circle 2, eventually competing for the full Circle 3."
*   **Audience:** Gives aggressive VCs the massive number they need to justify a Series A check size, while clearly labeling it as a long-term vision rather than immediate opportunity.

**How to Present This in the Deck**
Visualize these as concentric circles. Explicitly state your methodology: *"We define our market not by static analyst categories, but by workload migratability."*

Address the "shifting definition" head-on with this script:
*"The line between serverless and containers is blurring. Rather than picking a side, we segment by workload intent. Our Core TAM is where we dominate today. Our Expandable market is where our current customers are asking us to go next. This prevents us from overpromising on immediate revenue while proving we are not ignoring the $10B adjacent opportunity."*

**Data Validation Strategy**
Use your internal logs to validate the "Bridge." Calculate what percentage of your 300 developers are trying to run container-like workloads on functions (e.g., long-running processes, large deployment packages). If 20% of your users are friction-heavy because they need containers, that data point proves the $7B Expandable market is real and accessible to *you*, not just a theoretical number.

This framework builds credibility by showing you understand the nuance of the market evolution. It tells investors you are disciplined enough to focus on the $2B you can win now, but visionary enough to capture the $12B over time.

*Note: Ensure your financial model ties Series A milestones specifically to capturing share in Circle 1 and building the bridge to Circle 2.*
*Do not let analysts define your market; define it by your customer's migration path.*
