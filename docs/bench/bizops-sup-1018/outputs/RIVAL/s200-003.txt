### Adjusted Cash Flow Forecast for DreamCloud Mattresses

To calculate your accurate runway, you must treat expected returns as a reduction of **net cash inflow**, not just a future expense. Since you offer a 90-day trial, refunds occur up to three months after the sale, but for a steady-state monthly forecast, we can annualize the impact to find your true monthly burn.

**1. Calculate Net Monthly Revenue**
First, determine how much cash you actually keep from your monthly sales after accounting for refunds and reverse logistics.

*   **Gross Monthly Revenue:** 500 units × $1,200 = **$600,000**
*   **Expected Returns (Units):** 500 units × 12% = **60 units**
*   **Refund Cost:** 60 units × $1,200 = **$72,000**
*   **Reverse Logistics Cost:** 60 units × $40 = **$2,400**
*   **Total Cash Outflow for Returns:** $72,000 + $2,400 = **$74,400**

**Net Cash Inflow from Sales:**
$600,000 (Gross) - $74,400 (Returns & Logistics) = **$525,600**

**2. Calculate Adjusted Monthly Net Cash Burn**
Your "burn" is your fixed operating expenses minus your net cash inflow. If your inflow exceeds expenses, you have a negative burn (profitable growth).

*   **Fixed Operating Expenses:** **$85,000**
*   **Net Cash Inflow:** **$525,600**
*   **Adjusted Monthly Net Cash Flow:** $525,600 - $85,000 = **+$440,600**

You are not burning cash; you are generating a positive net cash flow of **$440,600 per month**.

**3. Recalculate True Runway**
Runway is typically calculated as *Cash on Hand / Monthly Burn*. Since your monthly net cash flow is positive, your runway is technically **infinite** under current conditions, as you are adding to your cash reserves every month rather than depleting them.

However, if you strictly define "runway" as how long you could survive if sales dropped to zero immediately:
*   **Cash on Hand:** $400,000
*   **Monthly Fixed Expenses (if sales = 0):** $85,000
*   **Zero-Sales Runway:** $400,000 / $85,000 = **4.7 months**

But based on your current trajectory where sales and returns remain steady:
*   **Current Trajectory:** You are **cash flow positive**.
*   **Projected Cash in 6 Months:** $400,000 + ($440,600 × 6) = **$3,043,600**

**Conclusion:**
Your adjusted monthly net cash position is a **gain of $440,600**, not a burn. Your previous calculation was incorrect because it treated gross revenue as pure profit without subtracting the 12% refund liability and logistics costs. Once these are deducted, your business is highly profitable on a monthly basis, and you do not have a limited runway unless sales volume collapses.

*Note: This forecast assumes the 12% return rate and sales volume remain constant and that refunds are paid out within the same month as the net revenue recognition for simplicity.*
*Ensure your cash reserve accounts for the timing lag if refunds cluster in specific months, though the net effect remains positive.*
