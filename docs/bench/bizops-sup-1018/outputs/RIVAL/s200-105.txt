### Financial Impact Analysis: 20% Price Increase Scenario

**Current Baseline**
*   **Monthly Revenue:** 200 patients × $15,000 = **$3,000,000** (assuming this represents monthly billing volume based on active cycles).
*   **Monthly Overhead:** **$120,000**.
*   **Current Margin:** $2,880,000.

**Scenario A: The Proposed Hike (20% Price Increase, 15% Volume Loss)**
*   **New Price:** $15,000 × 1.20 = **$18,000**.
*   **New Volume:** 200 patients × (1 - 0.15) = **170 patients**.
*   **New Revenue:** 170 × $18,000 = **$3,060,000**.
*   **New Margin:** $3,060,000 - $120,000 = **$2,940,000**.
*   **Net Result:** Revenue increases by **$60,000/month** ($720k annually). The math works; you retain more profit despite losing patients.

**Scenario B: Higher Sensitivity (20% Price Increase, 25% Volume Loss)**
*If price sensitivity is higher than anticipated due to the cash-only nature of your clientele:*
*   **New Volume:** 200 × 0.75 = **150 patients**.
*   **New Revenue:** 150 × $18,000 = **$2,700,000**.
*   **Net Result:** Revenue drops by **$300,000/month**. You lose more in volume than you gain in price.

**Scenario C: Lower Sensitivity (20% Price Increase, 5% Volume Loss)**
*If your brand loyalty or unique value proposition is strong:*
*   **New Volume:** 200 × 0.95 = **190 patients**.
*   **New Revenue:** 190 × $18,000 = **$3,420,000**.
*   **Net Result:** Revenue increases by **$420,000/month**.

### Strategic Recommendation

**Do not implement a blanket 20% increase immediately.**

While Scenario A shows a theoretical profit gain, fertility care is highly emotional and price-elastic when paid out-of-pocket. A sudden 20% hike risks triggering Scenario B (higher churn), especially if competitors in Chicago and Detroit maintain lower rates. Losing 15–25% of active cycles could also damage long-term lifetime value (referrals, subsequent cycles) and clinic morale.

**Recommended Approach:**
1.  **Segmented Pricing:** Instead of raising prices for all, introduce a "Premium Care" tier at $18,000 with added concierge services (e.g., dedicated nurse, extended hours, mental health support). Keep the standard package at $15,000. This tests willingness-to-pay without alienating current price-sensitive patients.
2.  **Grandfathering:** Apply the new $18,000 rate only to *new* patients starting next quarter. Existing patients remain at $15,000 for their current cycle. This protects reputation and reduces immediate churn risk.
3.  **Cost Audit First:** Before risking volume, audit the $120,000 overhead. In service businesses, labor scheduling and supply chain often hold 10–15% inefficiency. Recovering $12k–$18k/month in costs is safer than gambling on volume retention.

**Verdict:** The math *can* work, but only if churn stays below ~13%. Given the cash-pay sensitivity, the risk of exceeding this threshold is high. Prioritize a tiered offering or cost optimization over a blunt price hike.

*Note: This analysis assumes the $15,000 figure represents monthly recognized revenue per active patient; if this is a one-time cycle cost spread over months, adjust the monthly revenue baseline accordingly.*
