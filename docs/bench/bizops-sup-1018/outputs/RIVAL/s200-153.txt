### Unit Economics Analysis: $9 vs. $14 Pricing

Based on the figures provided, **raising the price to $14/month is mathematically risky and likely to worsen your unit economics** unless you simultaneously fix retention.

**Current State ($9/mo):**
*   **Monthly Churn:** 8% implies an average customer lifespan of ~12.5 months ($1 \div 0.08$).
*   **Lifetime Value (LTV):** $9 \times 12.5 = \$112.50$.
*   **LTV:CAC Ratio:** $112.50 \div \$45 = \mathbf{2.5x}$.
*   *Verdict:* This is a healthy, sustainable ratio for a subscription business.

**Proposed State ($14/mo) with Projected Impacts:**
You anticipate a 30% drop in conversion and fear higher churn. Let's model a conservative scenario where churn rises slightly to 10% (common when price increases without added value) and acquisition volume drops by 30%.
*   **New Lifespan:** At 10% churn, lifespan drops to 10 months.
*   **New LTV:** $14 \times 10 = \$140$.
*   **New CAC Efficiency:** While LTV increases nominally, your *volume* of new users drops 30%. More critically, if the price hike causes existing users to churn faster (e.g., churn spikes to 12% due to price sensitivity), your lifespan drops to ~8.3 months.
    *   *Worst Case LTV:* $14 \times 8.3 = \$116.20$.
    *   *Worst Case Ratio:* $116.20 \div \$45 = \mathbf{2.58x}$.

**The Verdict:**
The math shows **negligible gain** in efficiency (moving from 2.5x to ~2.6x) while introducing massive risk. A 30% drop in conversion means you must spend significantly more on ads just to maintain the same number of new subscribers. If churn exceeds 10.5%, your LTV:CAC ratio actually **worsens**, and you lose revenue velocity.

**Recommendation:** Do not raise prices yet. Your primary leak is the 8% monthly churn. Reducing churn from 8% to 6% at the current $9 price point would increase your LTV to $150 (a 33% gain) without risking conversion rates or alienating current users. Focus on retention first; price increases work best when churn is <5%.

**Critical Data Points Needed Before Deciding:**
1.  **Price Elasticity by Segment:** Do parents of high-schoolers churn less than elementary parents? You may be able to raise prices only for specific grades.
2.  **Feature Usage Correlation:** Identify the top 3 features used by users who stay >6 months. If you bundle these into a "Pro" tier at $14, you justify the hike with value rather than just inflation.
3.  **Competitor Price Sensitivity:** Survey churned users specifically asking if price was the sole factor or if content depth was the issue.

*Note: A price increase without a corresponding increase in perceived value (new content, AI tutoring, progress reports) will almost certainly accelerate churn in the K-12 market.*
*Focus your immediate roadmap on reducing the 8% churn; every 1% reduction adds ~$13 to your LTV.*
