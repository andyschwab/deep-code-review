However, I can outline the general business considerations typically weighed when evaluating multi-entity structures versus single-entity operations with robust risk management:

**General Considerations for Separate Entities:**
*   **Liability Isolation:** The primary theoretical benefit of separate LLCs is that a legal judgment against one entity (e.g., the Florida LLC) generally does not expose the assets of the others (California or Illinois LLCs) or the parent company, provided corporate formalities are strictly maintained.
*   **Operational Complexity:** Maintaining three distinct entities requires separate bank accounts, tax filings, annual reports, and registered agents in each state. This significantly increases administrative costs and accounting complexity.
*   **Piercing the Corporate Veil:** Courts may ignore the separation between entities if they are not operated independently (e.g., commingling funds, shared staff without proper allocation, identical branding without clear distinction). If the veil is pierced, the liability protection fails.

**General Considerations for Single Entity with Risk Management:**
*   **Cost Efficiency:** Operating as a single LLC is far less expensive and administratively burdensome than maintaining three.
*   **Insurance:** Many businesses address liability concerns through professional liability (errors and omissions) insurance rather than corporate structuring alone. This is often the first line of defense for content providers.
*   **Contractual Protections:** Strong terms of service, including clear disclaimers that materials are for study purposes only and do not guarantee exam passage, are standard. However, their enforceability varies by state and specific circumstances, particularly regarding consumer protection laws.
