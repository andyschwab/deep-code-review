### Liability Exposure Differences

**Affiliate Model (Current):**
As independent affiliates, your coaches likely operate as separate business entities. Your liability is generally limited to your own direct negligence (e.g., negligent hiring or training). If a coach gives bad advice harming a child, the coach is primarily liable. However, if you exert too much control over their daily operations, courts may pierce this veil, treating them as employees or agents, thereby exposing SleepNest to vicarious liability for their actions.

**Franchisee Model (Proposed):**
Franchising does not automatically eliminate liability; in some contexts, it can increase scrutiny. While franchisees are independent owners, franchisors often face "apparent agency" claims if the brand integration is tight. If a parent believes the coach *is* SleepNest due to uniform branding and mandated methods you enforce, you could be held liable. However, a well-structured franchise agreement explicitly defines the franchisee as an independent contractor responsible for their own torts, which can provide a stronger contractual defense than a loose affiliate agreement, provided you maintain operational separation in practice.

### Tax Reporting Obligations

**Affiliate Model:**
- **Reporting:** You issue IRS Form 1099-NEC to each coach earning over $600 annually.
- **Payroll Taxes:** You do not withhold income tax, Social Security, or Medicare. Coaches pay self-employment tax.
- **State Filings:** No franchise tax filings specific to the relationship; standard business taxes apply.

**Franchisee Model:**
- **Reporting:** Franchise fees and royalties are reported as income on your corporate return. You do not issue 1099s for royalty payments made *to* you; you issue 1099s only if you pay *them* for services (unlikely in a pure franchise model).
- **Payroll Taxes:** Still no W-2 obligations for the franchisees themselves, as they remain independent business owners.
- **State Filings:**
    - **Florida:** No state income tax, but you must register the franchise offering.
    - **Georgia:** Requires registration of the franchise offering with the Secretary of State before selling any franchises. You may also face specific franchise tax nuances depending on your corporate structure, though Georgia largely repealed its net worth tax; standard corporate income tax applies.

### The Control Threshold and Franchise Registration

The critical legal risk is not just "reclassifying" but inadvertently creating a franchise relationship without complying with laws. Under the FTC Franchise Rule and both Florida and Georgia state laws, a business relationship is legally a **franchise** if three elements exist, regardless of what you call it:
1.  **Trademark License:** The coach uses your brand (SleepNest).
2.  **Fee:** The coach pays a fee (the proposed $15,000).
3.  **Control/Assistance:** You exert **significant control** over their operations or provide **significant assistance** in marketing/operations.

**The Threshold:** If you mandate specific sleep methods, require strict adherence to scripts, control pricing, or dictate hours, you have crossed the "significant control" threshold. At this point, you **are** a franchisor by law. Operating an unregistered franchise in Florida or Georgia is illegal and can result in rescission rights (coaches demanding their money back), fines, and cease-and-desist orders. You cannot avoid franchise registration simply by calling them "affiliates" if the economic reality fits the definition.

### Required Documents and Filings for Attorney Review

Before converting even one coach, a franchise attorney must review and prepare:

1.  **Franchise Disclosure Document (FDD):** A federally mandated document with 23 specific items detailing fees, litigation history, and financial performance. This must be filed with the state.
2.  **Franchise Agreement:** The binding contract replacing your current affiliate terms.
3.  **State Registration Applications:** Specific filings for Florida (Department of Agriculture and Consumer Services) and Georgia (Secretary of State Securities Division).
4.  **Operations Manual:** While internal, its content determines the "control" element and must be vetted to ensure it doesn't inadvertently create employment liability.
5.  **Financial Statements:** Audited or reviewed financials are often required for the FDD.

Do not solicit the $15,000 fee or sign new agreements until the FDD is registered in both states.
