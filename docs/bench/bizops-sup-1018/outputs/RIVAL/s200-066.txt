**Investor Narrative: "Distribution Leverage, Not Dependency"**

Do not frame your marketplace revenue as a lack of sales capability. Instead, position it as **capital-efficient distribution leverage**. Investors prefer founders who recognize where their Ideal Customer Profile (ICP) already buys and exploit that channel rather than burning cash on inefficient direct sales.

Your current data supports a "Smart Leverage" narrative, not a "Weak Sales" one. Last year, your direct team generated $30k while marketplaces generated $160k. This does not prove you cannot sell; it proves that **your customers prefer buying through marketplaces**. In the enterprise security sector (secrets management), procurement teams often mandate using established cloud marketplaces to simplify billing, utilize committed spend (EDP), and reduce vendor risk. Fighting this friction with a nascent direct team was likely the inefficient move, not the marketplace reliance itself.

**The Strategic Pivot for Series A**

To secure your Series A, you must shift the story from "We are dependent" to "We are optimizing our mix." Investors worry about concentration risk (one partner delisting you) and margin erosion (the 20% rev share). Address these specifically:

1.  **Reframe the 20% Cost:** Acknowledge the 20% fee explicitly but contextualize it as a **Customer Acquisition Cost (CAC) substitute**. If your direct CAC would be $50k per customer and your payback period 18 months, paying 20% lifetime revenue to the marketplace for instant access and zero sales overhead is a superior unit economic decision. You are trading margin for velocity and capital efficiency.
2.  **The "Hybrid Motion" Roadmap:** Do not aggressively shift revenue *away* from marketplaces if it kills growth. Instead, propose a **hybrid motion** where marketplaces remain the primary acquisition engine for SMB/Mid-Market, while you build a specialized direct team solely for **Enterprise upsells and multi-cloud deals** that marketplaces cannot easily handle.
    *   *Narrative:* "We use marketplaces for low-touch acquisition (80% of revenue) to keep burn low ($45k/mo). We are now hiring two senior AEs to capture the top 5% of accounts that require complex negotiation, aiming to increase direct ARPU without sacrificing total growth."
3.  **Mitigating Concentration Risk:** Investors fear a single point of failure. Your mitigation strategy is **multi-cloud presence**. If you are only on AWS, that is a risk. If you are on AWS, Azure, and GCP, you have diversified your distribution risk. Highlight that your product is cloud-agnostic, making you portable if terms change.

**Actionable Advice**

Do not slow growth to force direct revenue. Slowing growth to prove a point about sales capability is a classic Series A mistake. Growth is your primary asset. Instead:
*   **Keep the marketplace engine running** to maintain your $800k ARR trajectory.
*   **Hire selectively for direct sales** only for accounts too large or complex for the marketplace flow.
*   **Present a model** showing that even if you lost one marketplace tomorrow, your remaining diversified channels and improved direct motion would sustain the business, but emphasize that this is a "black swan" scenario, not a baseline expectation.

**Bottom Line:** Your marketplace success signals product-market fit and smart go-to-market execution. Your lack of direct success signals that you haven't yet found the right *type* of direct sale, not that you can't sell. Sell the efficiency, not the dependency.
