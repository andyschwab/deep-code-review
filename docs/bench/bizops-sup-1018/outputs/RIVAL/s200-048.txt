**Diagnosis: This is a product information failure, not a pricing problem.**

Lowering your price to $150 is the wrong move. It will not reduce returns; it will likely increase them while destroying your brand positioning and margin.

**The Logic**
Your return rate of 22% is driven almost entirely by "size mismatches." This is a functional error in the buying process, not an emotional reaction to value.
*   **If it were a value problem:** Customers would return the beds because the foam felt cheap, the fabric tore, or the bed looked nothing like the photos. They would say, "This isn't worth $180."
*   **The reality:** Customers like the product but bought the wrong size. This means they *want* the product but lack the data to make the correct selection.

**Why Lowering Price Fails**
1.  **Irrelevant to Root Cause:** A customer who buys a Medium bed when they need a Large will still return it if it doesn't fit, regardless of whether they paid $180 or $150. Price does not fix sizing confusion.
2.  **Margin Destruction:** Your current unit economics are healthy ($120 gross profit per unit). Dropping to $150 reduces your margin to $90. With a 22% return rate, you are already absorbing significant reverse logistics costs. Cutting price deepens the hole without plugging the leak.
3.  **Brand Signal:** PawsLux positions itself as "luxury." In the luxury segment, price is a proxy for quality. Dropping your price signals lower quality, potentially increasing skepticism about the memory foam claims and hurting conversion rates among high-intent buyers.

**The Solution: Invest in Information, Not Discounts**
You should immediately allocate the $5,000 from your marketing budget to overhaul your product pages. The goal is to eliminate sizing ambiguity before the purchase occurs.

**Action Plan**
1.  **Visual Sizing Guides:** Replace static charts with lifestyle photography showing dogs of specific breeds and weights in each bed size. A customer owning a 60lb Golden Retriever needs to see *that* dog in the bed, not just a number.
2.  **Interactive Tools:** Implement a "Find My Fit" quiz or tool. Ask for breed, weight, and sleeping style (curled vs. stretched). Output a specific recommendation with a confidence guarantee.
3.  **Enhanced Descriptions:** Explicitly state dimensions in relatable terms (e.g., "Fits dogs up to 75lbs who sleep stretched out").
4.  **Video Content:** Add short videos demonstrating the memory foam rebound and the actual scale of the bed in a living room setting.

**Projected Impact**
By fixing the information gap, you target the 22% return rate directly. Even reducing returns by half (to 11%) saves you roughly $11,000–$15,000 monthly in lost revenue and logistics costs, instantly paying for your $5,000 investment. This preserves your $180 price point, maintains your luxury status, and restores accurate unit economics.

Do not discount your way out of a data problem. Fix the guide, keep the price.

*Note: Monitor returns for 30 days post-update; if "size mismatch" drops but "quality" returns rise, only then re-evaluate product construction.*
