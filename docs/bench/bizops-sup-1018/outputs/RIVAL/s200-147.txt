**Scenario B: Pass Full Cost to Customer (Price €72.50)**

*   **COGS:** €20.
*   **Gross Profit per Unit:** €72.50 - €20 - €7.50 = €45.
*   **Fixed Costs:** ~€9.75/unit (derived from baseline).

*   **Total Monthly Revenue:** €507,500.
*   **Profit Comparison:** This scenario yields **€159,250 more profit** than Scenario A, despite the volume drop.

**Break-Even Analysis**
To match the current profit of €162,500 under Scenario B (Price Hike):
*   Required Units = Total Fixed Costs + Target Profit / Contribution Margin per Unit.
*   Assuming Fixed Costs = €97,500 (based on 10k units × €9.75).
*   Contribution Margin (Price - COGS - Tax) = €45.
*   Units Needed = (€97,500 + €162,500) / €45 = **5,778 units**.
*   **Conclusion:** You can afford to lose up to 42% of your volume (dropping to 5,778 units) and still match your *current* absolute profit dollars if you raise prices. Since you only expect a 30% drop, **raising prices is financially superior.**

**Subscription Bundle & Exemption Variables**
1.  **Exemptions:** If doctor-prescribed units are exempt, segmenting your sales funnel to require a prescription upload could save the 15% tax entirely for that cohort, allowing you to keep prices at €65 with full margins.
2.  **Subscription Loophole:** If the tax applies to "retail price per pack," bundling 3 packs for €180 (€60/pack) might technically keep the per-unit display below €50 if structured as a single SKU "Monthly Supply" rather than "3 Packs." However, if the tax authority calculates the per-unit equivalent of any transaction over €50, this strategy will fail. If the tax triggers strictly on the *single pack* SKU price, selling only via subscription bundles where the line item is "Monthly Supply" (not individual packs) may bypass the threshold definition.

**Recommendation:** Pass the cost to consumers. The math shows that even with a severe 30% volume drop, your total net profit nearly doubles compared to absorbing the tax. Immediately investigate the "prescription exemption" and "bundle definition" loopholes to potentially mitigate the tax entirely.
