**Immediate Recommendation: Do Not Sign the Current Clause**

Accepting a "guaranteed zero breaches" clause with unlimited liability for all damages is commercially suicidal for a company with $400k in revenue. No software vendor can guarantee perfection, and no standard insurance policy will cover an uncapped, absolute guarantee of security outcomes. Signing this as written exposes you to existential risk far beyond your $2M general liability cap.

**Insurance Implications**
Yes, accepting an absolute guarantee of "zero breaches" typically voids or severely compromises standard cyber liability and errors & omissions (E&O) policies in the UK and globally. Insurers underwrite based on "reasonable care" and "best efforts," not absolute outcomes. By contractually guaranteeing a result (no breaches) rather than a service level (scanning within X hours), you fundamentally alter the risk profile, likely triggering exclusions for "assumed liability" or "contractual liability" that exceeds standard norms. Your insurer may deny coverage entirely for any claim arising from this specific contractual breach.

**Language to Avoid**
You must strike any language implying absolute certainty or uncapped liability. Specifically avoid:
*   "Guarantee," "Warrant," or "Ensure" zero breaches/vulnerabilities.
*   "Indemnify for all damages, losses, or consequential costs" without a cap.
*   "Liability shall be unlimited" or references to "full actual loss."
*   Any clause waiving your right to limit liability for negligence.

**Standard Industry Alternatives**
Negotiate for these standard structures instead:
1.  **Service Level, Not Outcome:** Change the guarantee from "zero breaches" to "scanning frequency and reporting SLAs" (e.g., "New critical vulnerabilities scanned within 4 hours of disclosure"). You guarantee the *process*, not the *result*.
2.  **Liability Cap:** Set a hard cap on total liability. Standard is 12 months of fees paid or a fixed amount (e.g., $500k–$1M). Given your size, fight hard for a cap equal to your insurance limit ($2M) or 12x monthly contract value, whichever is lower. Never accept uncapped liability.
3.  **Carve-outs:** Limit indemnity to direct damages only. Explicitly exclude indirect, consequential, lost profit, or reputation damages.
4.  **Mutual Indemnity:** Ensure indemnity is mutual, not one-sided.
