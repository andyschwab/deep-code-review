### Analysis: Complexity vs. Transparency for EdgeCompute

Your hypothesis is correct: the drop in conversion (8% to 4.5%) amidst rising traffic strongly indicates that pricing complexity is creating friction. In the gaming sector, where latency and predictability are paramount, a pricing model with 15+ add-on variables creates "calculation anxiety." Prospects cannot instantly determine their total cost of ownership, leading to cart abandonment.

**Trade-off Evaluation**

1.  **Current Complex Model:**
    *   **Pros:** Theoretically captures exact value from every user; protects margins against heavy users.
    *   **Cons:** High cognitive load stalls decision-making. Your $1,200 CAC suggests you are targeting serious businesses, yet the pricing page feels like a utility bill estimator rather than a growth enabler. The 4.5% conversion rate implies that for every 100 qualified leads, you lose 3.5 deals purely due to confusion.
    *   **Risk:** At your current burn, losing $90k/month in efficiency threatens your 10-month runway if not corrected within 60 days.

2.  **Proposed Usage-Band Model (Simplified):**
    *   **Structure:** Bundle the 15 variables into three clear usage bands (e.g., "Up to 5TB + 1M Compute Seconds").
    *   **Pros:** Drastically reduces time-to-value perception. Gamers prefer predictable scaling. This aligns price with *outcome* (serving players) rather than *inputs* (seconds/bandwidth).
    *   **Cons:** Risk of undercharging heavy users or overcharging light ones.
    *   **Mitigation:** Set the "Pro" band slightly below your current average user consumption to ensure most feel they are getting a deal, while using soft caps with clear overage rates only *after* the band is exceeded, rather than upfront complexity.

**Impact on ARPU:**
Simplification often increases ARPU initially because friction removal converts more mid-tier users who previously abandoned. While you may leave some money on the table from power users, the volume increase from 4.5% back to 6-7% conversion will likely outweigh the marginal loss, especially given your high CAC. Recovering even 1% conversion saves you ~$25k/month in wasted spend.

### Testing Methodology: The "Band" Pilot

Do not roll this out to all 5,000 trial users immediately. Use a staggered A/B test over 3 weeks.

**Phase 1: Segmentation (Days 1-2)**
Split incoming trial signups into two groups:
*   **Control Group (50%):** Sees current complex pricing.
*   **Variant Group (50%):** Sees new "Usage Band" pricing (e.g., Starter: $500 flat up to X limit; Pro: $2,000 flat up to Y limit). Remove all 15 add-ons from the initial view; hide them behind a "Detailed Specs" accordion.

**Phase 2: Metric Tracking (Days 3-21)**
Track three specific metrics:
1.  **Trial-to-Paid Conversion Rate:** Primary success metric. Target: >6%.
2.  **Time-to-Checkout:** Measure time from pricing page view to payment entry. Expect a 30-50% reduction.
3.  **Support Tickets:** Count inquiries asking "How much will this cost?" A drop here confirms reduced friction.

**Phase 3: Revenue Validation**
Calculate the blended ARPU for the Variant group. If conversion rises by >20% and ARPU drops by <10%, the net revenue impact is positive. If ARPU holds steady while conversion jumps, the new model is an immediate win.

**Rollout Decision:**
If the Variant group shows a statistically significant lift in conversion without a catastrophic drop in ARPU after 50 paid conversions, roll out the simplified model to 100% of new traffic. Then, migrate existing trial users via an email campaign highlighting "New Simpler Pricing."

*Note: Ensure your "Enterprise" tier remains "Contact Sales" to allow for custom negotiation on massive gaming launches.*
*Monitor churn closely post-migration; simplified pricing sometimes attracts users who scale faster than anticipated.*
