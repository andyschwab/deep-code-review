To estimate your niche market size and validate your marketing budget, shift from top-down employment data to bottom-up technical signal detection. Your current $1.2M projection assumes 100% of companies with 50+ engineers have the specific pain point (monorepos), which is a dangerous overestimation.

**Step 1: Estimate Monorepo Prevalence via Proxy Data**
Since private repository data is inaccessible, use public proxies to establish a baseline adoption rate:
*   **GitHub Public Data:** Analyze the top 5,000 organizations by contributor count on GitHub. Calculate the percentage using monorepo structures (look for single repos with multiple distinct service directories or tools like Nx, Turborepo, Bazel in their tech stack). While public repos differ from private enterprise repos, the *trend line* among large engineering orgs is a strong leading indicator. Industry heuristics suggest monorepo adoption scales with team size; for teams >50 engineers, adoption often ranges between 15–30%, not 100%.
*   **Job Description Analysis:** Scrape job postings for "monorepo," "Nx," "Turborepo," "Bazel," or "single repository" within companies of 50+ engineers. This signals active maintenance of such architectures.
*   **Tech Survey Cross-Reference:** Utilize data from the Stack Overflow Developer Survey or State of JS reports, filtering for enterprise respondents, to find self-reported monorepo usage rates.

**Step 2: Refine the Market Calculation**
Apply a conservative adoption rate to your TAM. If research suggests 20% of your 5,000 target companies use monorepos:
*   **Realistic TAM:** 1,000 companies.
*   **Serviceable Obtainable Market (SOM):** At 2% capture of this *refined* list, you are targeting 20 customers.
*   **Revenue Reality Check:** 20 customers × $12,000 = $240,000 ARR.
*   **Budget Implication:** A $200,000 marketing budget against a $240,000 potential return in year one is unsustainable (CAC > LTV). You must either expand your definition of "environment drift" to include polyrepos with complex sync needs, lower your CAC through product-led growth, or increase your ACV significantly.

**Step 3: Critical Assumptions to Validate Before Spending**
Before finalizing your go-to-market strategy, conduct 15–20 discovery calls with engineering VPs at target companies to validate these three specific assumptions:
1.  **The "Prerequisite" Assumption:** Confirm that *only* monorepo users face your specific version of environment drift. Many polyrepo teams suffer severe drift due to microservices complexity; excluding them may artificially shrink your market.
2.  **The Budget Priority:** Ask prospects, "Is solving local environment sync a top-3 engineering productivity blocker this quarter?" If it's a "nice to have," your sales cycle will exceed your cash runway.
3.  **The Willingness to Pay:** Validate if $12,000/year is perceived as a bargain for this specific problem. If they view it as a CI/CD feature, they will expect it to be included in existing infrastructure costs, not a standalone line item.

**Recommendation:** Do not commit the full $200,000 budget yet. Allocate $20,000 to a targeted outbound pilot aimed at validating the monorepo prevalence rate and ACV. Use the learnings to adjust the SOM before scaling spend.

*Note: Your current math relies on a 100% problem-fit assumption which is rarely true; validating the actual percentage of monorepo users is your highest priority task.*
*Consider expanding your value proposition to "complex multi-repo environments" if monorepo-only data proves too thin to support your revenue goals.*
