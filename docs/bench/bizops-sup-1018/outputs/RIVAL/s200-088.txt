### Factors Determining Revenue Recognition Timing

Under ASC 606 (the current GAAP standard for revenue recognition), the timing of revenue recognition is driven by when **control** of the promised goods or services transfers to the customer, not by cash collection or contract length. For your multi-year SaaS contracts, the following factors are critical:

1.  **Distinct Performance Obligations:** You must identify if your contract contains multiple distinct obligations.
    *   **Subscription Access:** Typically, SaaS access is a "series of distinct services" provided over time. If the customer receives and consumes the benefit simultaneously as you perform (which is true for most API testing tools), revenue must be recognized **ratably** over the access period (e.g., 36 months), regardless of upfront payment.
    *   **Implementation Services:** You mentioned significant implementation work in the first month. If this service is **distinct** (the customer could benefit from it on its own or with other readily available resources, and it is separately identifiable from the subscription), you may recognize revenue for this portion **upon completion** of the implementation. If the implementation is highly customized and integral to the subscription (not distinct), it must be bundled and recognized ratably with the subscription.

2.  **Transfer of Control:**
    *   **Over Time:** If the customer controls the asset as it is created (typical for SaaS), revenue is recognized over time.
    *   **Point in Time:** If control transfers only at a specific moment (e.g., delivery of a perpetual license or a one-time distinct service), revenue is recognized at that point.

3.  **Variable Consideration & Constraints:** Ensure there are no refunds, penalties, or usage caps that would make the transaction price variable, which could delay recognition until the uncertainty is resolved.
