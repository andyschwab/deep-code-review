**StrideCustom GDPR Compliance Roadmap for Biometric Foot Scanning**

**1. Data Classification: Special Category Data**
Under Article 9 of the GDPR, data processed to uniquely identify a natural person is classified as **biometric data**. While a simple photograph is not automatically biometric, your app processes images to generate a **3D model** for bespoke manufacturing. Because this model derives unique physical characteristics to create a product specifically for that individual, regulators will likely classify this as **special category data**. This classification triggers a higher standard of protection than standard personal data (like names or emails).

**2. Consent Requirements: Explicit vs. Standard**
Your standard Terms of Service (ToS) are **insufficient** for this data type.
*   **Explicit Consent Required:** You must obtain "explicit consent" via a separate, unbundled action (e.g., a distinct checkbox not pre-ticked).
*   **Granularity:** Users must be able to consent to the foot scan specifically without being forced to agree to unrelated marketing or general terms.
*   **Withdrawal:** You must provide an easy mechanism for users to withdraw this specific consent, which should trigger data deletion unless retention is required for contract fulfillment (see below).

**3. Data Residency and Architecture**
*   **Local Storage Not Strictly Mandatory, but Transfer Restrictions Apply:** The GDPR does not explicitly mandate that data *must* be stored physically in the EU. However, it strictly prohibits transferring special category data to countries without an "adequacy decision" (like the US) unless specific safeguards are in place.
*   **Recommendation:** Given your £50k budget and risk aversion, **host your data within the EU** (e.g., AWS Frankfurt, Google Cloud London). This eliminates the complexity of Standard Contractual Clauses (SCCs) and Transfer Impact Assessments for biometric data, saving legal costs and reducing architectural risk.

**4. Mandatory Disclosures (Privacy Notice)**
Your privacy policy must clearly state:
*   **Purpose Limitation:** The scan is used *solely* for shoe manufacturing and cannot be repurposed (e.g., for health analytics or selling to third parties) without new consent.
*   **Retention Period:** You must define a specific deletion timeline.
*   **Security Measures:** Briefly describe encryption standards (in-transit and at-rest).

**5. Retention Limits and "Contract Necessity"**
You may process this data without indefinite consent if it is **strictly necessary** to fulfill the sales contract (making the shoes).
*   **Strategy:** Once the shoes are delivered and the return window closes (e.g., 30 days post-delivery), the legal basis for holding the biometric model weakens.
*   **Action:** Implement an automated deletion policy. Delete the raw scan and 3D model **35 days after delivery** unless the user explicitly opts in to "Save my fit for future orders." Do not store this data indefinitely by default.

**6. Immediate Compliance Steps**
1.  **Data Protection Impact Assessment (DPIA):** Mandatory for systematic processing of biometric data. Document the risks and your mitigation strategies before launch.
2.  **Update Consent Flow:** Implement a distinct "I consent to biometric processing for shoe fitting" checkbox in the app scan flow.
3.  **EU Hosting:** Configure your database region to an EU location immediately.
4.  **Automated Deletion Script:** Build the logic to purge scan data automatically after the defined retention period.
