There is a fundamental misunderstanding in your premise regarding HIPAA. **HIPAA applies only to "Covered Entities" (healthcare providers, health plans, clearinghouses) and their "Business Associates."** As a direct-to-consumer (DTC) company selling at-home kits, GeneInsight is generally **not** a Covered Entity unless you have specific contracts with healthcare providers. Therefore, HIPAA regulations do not directly govern your data sales to pharma.

However, this does not mean you are unregulated. You are governed by:
1.  **FTC Act:** Prohibits unfair or deceptive practices (e.g., selling data if your privacy policy promised anonymity you cannot guarantee).
2.  **State Laws:** California's **CCPA/CPRA** (you are in CA) and other state biometric privacy laws (like Illinois BIPA) strictly regulate the sale of "sensitive personal information" and biometric data.
3.  **FDA Regulations:** If your kits make health claims, you are under FDA scrutiny regarding data usage.

**The "Anonymization" Trap**
Under federal law and modern re-identification standards, **true anonymization of DNA data is nearly impossible.** DNA is inherently identifiable. Even if you strip names, genetic markers can be cross-referenced with public genealogy databases to re-identify individuals. If re-identification is possible, the data is considered "personal information" under CCPA and "de-identified" (not anonymous) under HIPAA standards. Selling "de-identified" data without strict contractual safeguards creates massive liability.
