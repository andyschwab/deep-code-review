### Unit Economics Analysis: MindfulPath

**Current Metrics:**
*   **Price (ARPU):** $12/month
*   **Acquisition Cost (CAC):** $25
*   **Churn Rate:** 40% (after 3 months)
*   **Fixed Costs:** $4,000/month

**1. Lifetime Value (LTV) & Break-Even Calculation**
Your current model is mathematically unsustainable. Here is the breakdown based on a typical user journey:

*   **Months 1–3:** Assuming the 40% churn happens *after* month 3, the average user stays for roughly 3 months before the bulk of attrition hits. Even if we assume a slightly more gradual decay, a 40% monthly churn rate implies an average lifespan of only ~2.5 months.
*   **Conservative LTV:** $12/month × 2.5 months = **$30**.
*   **Aggressive LTV (if they stay exactly 3 months):** $12 × 3 = **$36**.

**The Verdict:**
*   **LTV ($30) < CAC ($25):** You are barely breaking even on variable costs per user, leaving **$5–$10** to contribute toward your $4,000 fixed costs.
*   **Break-Even Point:** At $12/month, a user must stay **2.1 months** just to pay back the $25 CAC. With a 40% churn rate, your average user lifespan is hovering right at this danger zone. Once you factor in payment processing fees (~3%) and taxes, you are likely losing money on every new signup.

**2. 12-Month Profitability Projection (Current Metrics Flat)**
Assuming you continue acquiring users at a rate that spent your previous budget (approx. 125 users/month to get 3,000 in 6 months implies a specific burn rate, but let's assume you maintain the *volume* of 500 new users/month to show scale):

*   **Revenue:** With 40% monthly churn, your active user base will plateau or shrink unless acquisition scales exponentially. If you add 500 users/month but lose 40% of the base, your net growth stalls quickly.
*   **Contribution Margin:** (~$12 revenue - $0 variable cost) × Users.
*   **Net Profit:** (Total Contribution) - $4,000 Fixed Costs - (500 users × $25 CAC).
*   **Result:** You will burn cash indefinitely. For every 100 users acquired, you spend $2,500. They generate ~$3,000 lifetime revenue. That leaves $500 gross profit. If you have 4,000 fixed costs, you need 800 new users *just to cover fixed costs*, ignoring the fact that the LTV:CAC ratio is effectively 1.2:1 (healthy SaaS requires 3:1).

**Where the Model Fails:**
The model fails because **high early churn destroys LTV**. You are buying users for $25 who only pay you $36 total. There is no margin to cover server costs, salaries, or profit. You are effectively paying customers to leave.

**Strategic Recommendation: Focus on Churn First**
Do **not** raise prices yet. Increasing price on a product with 40% churn will likely accelerate attrition, as users clearly do not perceive enough value to stay at $12.

1.  **Fix Retention Immediately:** Investigate *why* 40% leave after three months. Is the content exhausted? Is the onboarding weak? Your goal is to extend average lifespan from 2.5 months to 6+ months.
2.  **Target LTV:CAC of 3:1:** To justify a $25 CAC, your LTV needs to be $75. At $12/month, users must stay for **6.25 months** on average.
3.  **Pivot Pricing Only After Retention:** Once you stabilize churn, introduce an annual plan ($120/year) to capture upfront cash and lock in retention, then consider a price increase to $15–$18.

**Immediate Action:** Halt aggressive paid acquisition until you reduce churn to <10% monthly. You are currently scaling losses.

*Note: This analysis assumes the 40% churn is a monthly rate; if it is a one-time drop after month 3, the LTV improves slightly but remains dangerously low relative to CAC.*
*Seed funding runway will deplete rapidly if acquisition continues at current efficiency.*
