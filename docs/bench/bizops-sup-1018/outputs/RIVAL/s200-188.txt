### Go-to-Market Recommendation: Self-Serve Only

**Do not hire a sales team.** The unit economics of a dedicated three-person sales team are mathematically impossible to sustain given your Total Addressable Market (TAM) and pricing. A self-serve model is the only viable path for BarMaster.

#### The Math: Why a Sales Team Fails

**1. Maximum Revenue Potential**
*   **TAM:** 1,200 candidates/year
*   **Price:** $1,500
*   **Max Theoretical Revenue (100% share):** $1,800,000
*   **Realistic Revenue (at 5% conversion):** $90,000/year ($7,500/month)

**2. The Sales Team Deficit**
*   **Sales Team Cost:** $180,000/year ($15,000/month)
*   **Current Operating Costs:** $10,000/month
*   **Total Monthly Burn with Sales Team:** $25,000
*   **Projected Monthly Revenue:** $7,500
*   **Monthly Net Loss:** -$17,500

**Result:** With a sales team, you would burn through your $100,000 funding in approximately **5.7 months**. Even if the sales team miraculously increased conversion to 20% (capturing 240 students, or 20% of the entire US market for this niche), revenue would only reach $30,000/month. While this covers costs, capturing 20% of a hyper-niche market via cold outreach is statistically improbable without brand dominance, which takes time you do not have.

Furthermore, the Customer Acquisition Cost (CAC) logic breaks down. To justify a $15,000/month sales payroll, the team must generate at least $15,000 in *new* monthly recurring revenue or one-time sales constantly. At $1,500 per sale, they need to close **10 new students every single month**. Given only 100 students exist per month total (1,200 / 12), your sales team would need to capture **10% of the entire national market every month** just to pay their own salaries, leaving zero margin for product development or marketing spend.

#### The Self-Serve Path (Recommended)

**1. Unit Economics**
*   **Revenue (5% conversion):** $7,500/month
*   **Operating Costs:** $10,000/month
*   **Monthly Net Loss:** -$2,500
*   **Runway:** 40 months (3+ years)

**2. Strategic Advantage**
While currently operating at a slight loss, the self-serve model extends your runway from 6 months to over 3 years. This gives you time to:
*   Optimize the funnel to increase conversion from 5% to 10-15%.
*   Expand into adjacent niches (e.g., general maritime law certification, international bar prep).
*   Build organic brand authority, which is critical in legal education.

**3. Growth Levers**
Instead of fixed sales costs, reinvest any surplus into performance marketing or content partnerships with law schools. If conversion rises to 10%, you become profitable ($15k revenue - $10k cost = $5k profit/month) without the risk of a heavy fixed payroll.

#### Conclusion
The market size of 1,200 users is too small to support high-touch sales overhead. The "high-ticket" nature of the product ($1,500) might suggest a sales-led motion in larger markets, but here the volume ceiling caps revenue before it can cover the cost of humans selling it.

**Action Plan:**
1.  Commit 100% to a self-serve, digital-first funnel.
2.  Focus aggressively on increasing conversion rates through social proof and targeted content.
3.  Re-evaluate hiring only if you expand the TAM (e.g., adding more exam sections) or raise prices significantly without losing volume.

*Note: Your current 5% conversion assumption yields a loss even in the self-serve model; immediate focus must be on funnel optimization to reach breakeven.*
*Do not attempt a "hybrid" model; even one salesperson costs too much relative to the total available revenue pool.*
