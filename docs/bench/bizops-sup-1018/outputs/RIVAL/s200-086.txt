**The Legal Reality of PHI and HIPAA**
Handling PHI shifts the risk profile from standard software failure to regulatory catastrophe. Under HIPAA, fines can reach $1.5 million per violation category per year, not including civil lawsuits or state attorney general actions. A breach involving 2 terabytes of data could affect millions of patients, triggering class-action litigation where damages easily exceed tens of millions.

Your current cap of $100,000 creates a massive gap between your potential liability and your ability to pay. If a court finds your cap "unconscionable" or against public policy—which is common when statutory fines vastly exceed contractual limits—the cap may be thrown out entirely, leaving your startup's assets fully exposed. Furthermore, most General Liability and Errors & Omissions policies exclude intentional regulatory violations or have sub-limits for privacy breaches that may not cover HIPAA penalties.

**Industry Standards for Healthcare SaaS**
In the healthcare sector, liability caps are rarely fixed at low flat fees like $100,000. Standard market practice for vendors handling PHI typically involves:
1.  **Higher Fixed Caps:** $1 million to $5 million is common for mid-market deals.
2.  **Multiple of Fees:** Caps often range from 12 to 24 months of recurring revenue. For your $25k ARR clients, this would be $25k–$50k, which explains your current low cap, but this formula fails for enterprise deals where risk scales with data volume, not just contract value.
3.  **Carve-outs:** Crucially, liability caps almost always have "carve-outs" for specific high-risk areas. Indemnification for data breaches, HIPAA violations, gross negligence, and willful misconduct is frequently **uncapped** or capped at a much higher specific limit (e.g., $5M–$10M).
