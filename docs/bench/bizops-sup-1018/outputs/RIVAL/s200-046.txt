**Immediate Recommendation: Do Not Launch with a "25-Year" Claim Without Third-Party Validation**

You cannot legally or safely rely solely on internal formulation data to support a specific "25-year shelf life" claim on packaging for a food product intended for human consumption. In the United States, the FDA requires that shelf-life claims be substantiated by valid scientific evidence. While the FDA does not prescribe a single specific test method, asserting a precise duration like "25 years" without accelerated aging data or real-time validation exposes TrailBite to severe regulatory action, retailer rejection, and catastrophic liability if the food spoils.

**The Regulatory and Liability Reality**
Making an unverified long-term claim classifies your product labeling as potentially "misbranded." If a consumer gets sick after 10 years because your internal theory was wrong, your company will be held liable for negligence. Retailers (REI, Backcountry, etc.) and insurance carriers will almost certainly demand third-party verification (such as data from a lab like Mérieux NutriSciences or Eurofins) before stocking a product with such an extreme claim. Internal data is insufficient for external validation requirements.

**The Financial and Timeline Impact**
Your fear of a six-week delay burning $40,000 is mathematically plausible given your burn rate (~$13,300/week based on $200k seed/5 team/3 months runway), but the cost of *not* testing is higher.
1.  **Accelerated Aging Tests:** These typically take 4–8 weeks. If you start immediately, you might miss your 3-month launch window by 2–4 weeks.
2.  **The Cost of Delay:** Burning an extra $40,000 is painful, but launching a product that gets recalled, rejected by retailers, or causes illness will burn your entire $200,000 seed fund and end the company.

**Strategic Pivot to Preserve Cash and Launch Date**
You do not need to delay your launch or burn the extra cash if you adjust your packaging claims immediately. You have three viable options:

1.  **Change the Claim to "Best By: [Date 2-3 Years Out]"**: Launch on time with a conservative, standard shelf-life claim (e.g., 2–3 years) based on standard industry practices for freeze-dried meals. This requires no exotic testing to validate initially. You can continue running accelerated aging tests in the background. Once you have the 6-week data proving 25-year viability, you can update future packaging runs.
2.  **Use Qualitative Language**: Instead of "25 Years," use language like "Long-Term Storage Ready" or "Extended Shelf Life" without specifying the number. This reduces regulatory risk while marketing the benefit, though it is less powerful than a specific number.
3.  **Phased Verification**: Launch with a conservative date, conduct the accelerated testing immediately post-launch, and market the "Verified 25-Year" status as a major milestone update once the data arrives.

**Conclusion**
Do not print "25 Years" on your packaging yet. The risk of regulatory enforcement and product liability outweighs the marketing benefit. Adjust your initial packaging to a conservative, defensible shelf life (e.g., 3 years) to hit your launch date. Use the next two months to conduct the necessary accelerated aging tests. This allows you to launch on time, preserve your cash reserves, and legally upgrade your claim to "25 Years" as soon as the third-party data confirms your formulation works.
