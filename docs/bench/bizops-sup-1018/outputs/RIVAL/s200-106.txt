You must **spread the revenue out over the twelve-month service period**. You cannot book the full $50,000 immediately when the cash hits your account.

Under standard accounting principles (such as GAAP and IFRS), revenue is recognized when it is **earned**, not when cash is received. Since WellCorp delivers workshops and coaching continuously over a year, you earn the revenue ratably as those services are provided. Booking the entire amount upfront would violate the **matching principle**, which requires expenses and revenues to be recorded in the same period they occur.

**How to record this transaction:**

1.  **Upon receiving the $50,000 payment in January:**
    *   **Debit Cash:** $50,000 (Asset increases)
    *   **Credit Deferred Revenue:** $50,000 (Liability increases)
    *   *Note:* "Deferred Revenue" (or Unearned Revenue) represents your obligation to deliver services in the future. It sits on your Balance Sheet as a liability, not on your Income Statement as revenue.

2.  **At the end of each month (e.g., January 31):**
    *   You have now earned one-twelfth of the contract value ($50,000 / 12 = ~$4,166.67).
    *   **Debit Deferred Revenue:** $4,166.67 (Liability decreases)
    *   **Credit Revenue:** $4,166.67 (Income increases)
    *   *Result:* Your Income Statement shows ~$4,167 in revenue for January, accurately reflecting the work performed that month.

**Risks of recognizing revenue too early:**

*   **Misleading Investors:** If you book $50,000 in January, your Month 1 performance looks artificially massive, while Months 2–12 show zero revenue from this major client despite you actively delivering services. This distorts your Monthly Recurring Revenue (MRR) and growth trends, making the business look volatile or stagnant after the initial spike.
*   **Tax Issues:** Recognizing revenue before it is earned can accelerate your tax liability. You might owe taxes on income you haven't technically "earned" yet, creating unnecessary cash flow strain. While tax rules vary by jurisdiction, most align with accrual accounting for businesses of your scale to prevent this distortion.
*   **Audit Failure:** An auditor will flag immediate recognition of annual contracts as a material error. They will require you to restate your financials to move the unearned portion into a deferred revenue liability.

By spreading the revenue, your financial statements will accurately match your operational reality: steady service delivery resulting in steady revenue recognition.

*Ensure your accounting software is set to "Accrual Basis" rather than "Cash Basis" to automate these adjustments.*
*Consult a CPA to confirm specific local tax treatments for deferred income.*
