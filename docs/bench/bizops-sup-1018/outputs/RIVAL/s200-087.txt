**Strategic Recommendation: Pursue the Seed Extension**

Given your specific constraints, bootstrapping the $450,000 development cost presents an existential risk that outweighs the dilution concerns of a seed extension.

**The Bootstrap Trap**
Your current annual revenue is $600,000. Cutting marketing spend by 50% will almost certainly stall your 25% growth rate, likely causing revenue to flatten or decline as churn outpaces new acquisitions. Simultaneously, freezing hiring while building a "major next version" creates a delivery bottleneck. You are effectively betting that you can build a complex, market-defining product with reduced resources while your competitors accelerate. In the database migration sector, speed and trust are paramount; slowing down to preserve equity often results in losing the market entirely, rendering your 100% ownership of a stagnant company worthless.

**The Case for Capital**
Raising $750,000 allows you to maintain your growth engine while funding development. While a "funding winter" suggests lower valuations, raising at a modest valuation now is preferable to raising at a "down round" later because you ran out of cash or market share. Dilution is the cost of de-risking your execution. With $750,000, you secure an 18–24 month runway (assuming a modest burn increase), giving you time to launch the new version and prove its value before needing Series A.

**Trade-off Analysis**
*   **Bootstrapping:** Preserves equity but sacrifices speed and market position. High probability of product delay and revenue stagnation.
*   **Seed Extension:** Incurs 10–15% dilution (estimated) but preserves growth velocity and ensures product competitiveness. Maximizes the probability of reaching Series A viability.

**Required Milestones for a Viable Seed Extension**
To justify a $750,000 raise at a reasonable valuation in the current climate, you must demonstrate more than just historical growth. Investors need proof that the new version will unlock step-change value. You need to hit these specific milestones before or during the raise:

1.  **Design Partner Commitments:** Secure 3–5 signed Letters of Intent (LOIs) from existing customers committing to migrate to the new version upon launch. This validates demand and reduces technical risk.
2.  **Unit Economics Validation:** Demonstrate that your current $600k revenue is efficient. If your churn is below 5% monthly and CAC payback is under 12 months, you are investable. If not, fix this before raising.
3.  **Technical De-risking:** Complete a functional prototype or beta of the core migration engine of the new version. Show, don't just tell, that the $450k budget is sufficient to finish the job.
4.  **Clear Path to $2M ARR:** Present a credible model showing how the new version accelerates you from $600k to $2M+ ARR within 18 months post-launch. This is the threshold for Series A interest.

**Conclusion**
Do not starve your growth to fund development. The market penalty for being slow in infrastructure software is severe. Raise the capital, accept the dilution, and execute aggressively to dominate the niche.

*Note: Ensure your financial model explicitly details how the $750k extends your runway relative to your current burn rate before approaching investors.*
