### PopGourmet JFK Terminal 4 Unit Economics Framework

**1. Effective Margin Calculation**
Your concession fee acts as a variable cost, not a fixed overhead. You must deduct it from your contribution margin before calculating profit per unit.

*   **Selling Price:** $8.50
*   **COGS (Labor + Packaging):** $2.20
*   **Concession Fee (18% of $8.50):** $1.53
*   **Total Variable Cost:** $3.73 ($2.20 + $1.53)
*   **Effective Contribution Margin per Unit:** **$4.77**
*   **Effective Margin %:** **56.1%** (Down from 74.1% in standard retail)

*Formula:* `Effective Margin = Price - (COGS + (Price × Concession %))`

**2. Break-Even & Profitability Modeling**
Since the fee scales with revenue, your break-even point depends entirely on your **fixed operating costs** (e.g., manager salary, insurance, equipment depreciation, marketing) which are not included in your COGS.

*   **Monthly Fixed Costs (Estimate):** Let's assume $15,000 for staff salaries beyond bag-level labor, utilities, and admin.
*   **Break-Even Units:** $15,000 / $4.77 = **3,145 bags/month**.
*   **Projected Profit (at $40k sales):**
    *   Revenue: $40,000
    *   Total Variable Costs (47.9%): $19,160
    *   Contribution Margin: $20,840
    *   Less Fixed Costs: ($15,000)
    *   **Net Operating Profit:** **$5,840/month**

**3. Cash Flow Implications vs. Fixed Rent**
*   **Fixed Rent Model:** High risk at low volume (must pay rent even if sales are zero), but higher margin at high volume.
*   **Percentage Rent Model (Your Case):** Lower risk at low volume (fees drop if sales drop), protecting cash flow during slow months. However, it caps your upside; as you scale, you permanently surrender 18% of top-line revenue.
*   **Planning Tip:** Your cash flow is more predictable relative to revenue, but you have less operating leverage. A 10% sales dip reduces your fee burden automatically, acting as a natural hedge.

**4. Critical Contract Clauses & Hidden Costs**
Do not assume the 18% is the only cost. Scrutinize the agreement for:
*   **Minimum Annual Guarantee (MAG):** Most airport contracts include a MAG. If 18% of sales falls short of the MAG, you must pay the difference. *Calculation:* If MAG is $100k/year and you only generate $80k in fees, you owe $20k extra. This effectively creates a fixed cost floor.
*   **Common Area Maintenance (CAM) Charges:** Often billed separately per square foot, adding a fixed cost layer that distorts the "pure variable" model.
*   **Marketing/Advertising Fees:** Airports often charge an additional 1–3% of gross sales for terminal-wide marketing.
*   **POS/Technology Fees:** Mandatory use of airport-specific POS systems with monthly fees or transaction % cuts.

**5. Seasonal Fluctuation Strategy**
Airport traffic is highly seasonal. Model three scenarios:
*   **Peak (Summer/Holidays):** Assume 150% of baseline ($60k sales). Check if you hit MAG early; surplus revenue is pure profit driver.
*   **Shoulder (Spring/Fall):** Baseline ($40k sales).
*   **Trough (Jan/Feb):** Assume 60% of baseline ($24k sales). Verify if 18% of $24k covers your MAG obligation. If not, calculate the cash top-up required from your $120k working capital.

**Recommendation:** With $120k working capital, you are well-capitalized for 3 months of operations even if sales lag. Focus negotiation on capping CAM charges and clarifying the MAG calculation frequency (monthly vs. annual true-up) to avoid surprise cash outflows.

*Note: Ensure "COGS" strictly includes only variable labor; fixed staff salaries must be moved to the fixed cost line for this model to hold.*
*Verify if the 18% fee applies to gross sales including tax; if so, your effective fee rate on net revenue is actually higher.*
