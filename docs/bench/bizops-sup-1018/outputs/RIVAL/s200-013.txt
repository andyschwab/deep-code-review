**Recommendation: Amortize Over the Contract Term**

You should **amortize** the $84,000 in slotting and marketing fees over the 24-month contract term rather than expensing them immediately. Under standard accounting principles (GAAP), costs incurred to secure a specific revenue stream over a defined period should be matched to the revenue they generate. Since these fees grant you shelf access for two years, recognizing the entire cost in month one violates the **matching principle**, artificially crushing your net income now while inflating it later. This distortion validates your concern: immediate expensing would make SpicyRoute appear unprofitable to investors despite healthy operational performance.

**Financial Impact Analysis**

*   **Immediate Expensing (Accountant's Suggestion):**
    *   Month 1 Impact: **-$84,000** expense.
    *   Result: Your P&L shows a massive loss, obscuring the fact that your product is profitable on a per-unit basis. Investors seeing this may question your burn rate efficiency.

*   **Amortization (Recommended):**
    *   Monthly Expense: $84,000 / 24 months = **$3,500 per month**.
    *   Monthly Gross Profit: 12 stores × 800 bottles × $4 margin = **$38,400**.
    *   Reported Monthly Net (before other opex): $38,400 (Gross Profit) - $3,500 (Amortization) = **$34,900**.

By amortizing, your reported monthly burn rate related to these fees is only **$3,500**, accurately reflecting the ongoing cost of maintaining shelf space against the revenue it produces. This presents a stable, growing business to investors rather than one suffering a one-time cash shock.

**Authority and Sign-off Requirements**

Whether your internal bookkeeper can make this call depends on your company's size and audit status:

1.  **Internal Bookkeeper:** If SpicyRoute is a private company not currently undergoing an external audit, your bookkeeper can record this as an amortizable "Deferred Charging Asset" (or similar prepaid asset account) based on the contract language. The key criteria they must verify in the contract are:
    *   **Defined Term:** The agreement explicitly covers 24 months.
    *   **Non-Refundable:** The fees are sunk costs, not deposits.
    *   **Renewal Terms:** The contract does not automatically renew without new fees (which would reset the amortization clock).

2.  **External Auditor:** If you are preparing for an investment round where due diligence will include an audit review, or if you already have an external auditor, you **must** get their sign-off on this policy. While amortization is the standard treatment, auditors often scrutinize "slotting fees" to ensure they aren't actually bribes or indefinite marketing costs disguised as assets. An auditor's confirmation protects you during investor due diligence.
