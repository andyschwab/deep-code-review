### Recommended Pricing Structure: Hybrid Commitment Tiers

To satisfy enterprise CFOs in the financial sector while maintaining usage-based flexibility, do not offer pure pay-as-you-go. Instead, implement a **Commitment-Based Usage Model**. This provides the budget certainty finance teams require while retaining the fairness of usage billing.

**1. The "Pre-Purchased Credit" Model (Recommended)**
Sell annual commitments in dollar amounts rather than fixed features. The customer pre-purchases a block of execution minutes at a discounted rate.
*   **Structure:** Customer commits to $50,000/year upfront (or quarterly).
*   **Mechanism:** This buys them a specific pool of minutes (e.g., 500,000 mins) at a preferred rate (e.g., $0.10/min vs. $0.15/min on-demand).
*   **Benefit:** The CFO sees a fixed line item ($50k) on the budget. The engineering team gets flexibility to run tests as needed without overage surprises.
*   **Overage Protection:** Include a "soft cap" with automatic throttling or a pre-negotiated, capped overage rate so bills never exceed a defined multiple of the commitment (e.g., 110%).

**2. Tiered Volume Bundles**
Create three distinct commitment tiers that align with your average deal size ($48k) to anchor expectations.
*   **Standard:** $30k/year commitment (includes X minutes).
*   **Professional:** $50k/year commitment (includes Y minutes + priority support).
*   **Enterprise:** $100k/year commitment (includes Z minutes + dedicated infrastructure).
*   **Rollover Policy:** Allow 20–30% of unused minutes to roll over to the next period. This reduces the fear of "wasting" pre-purchased budget, a common objection in financial services.

**3. The "True-Up" Reconciliation**
For your largest prospects, offer a quarterly true-up model.
*   They pay a fixed minimum fee quarterly based on estimated usage.
*   At the end of the quarter, you reconcile actual usage. If they under-consumed, they get a credit; if they over-consumed, they pay the difference.
*   This mimics utility billing familiar to large organizations but keeps the variance contained within a predictable range.

### Anticipated Finance Stakeholder Objections

**1. "We cannot approve variable OpEx without a hard cap."**
*   *Response:* Your contract must explicitly state a **Maximum Annual Liability**. Even if billing is usage-based, the legal agreement should cap the total spend at the commitment level plus a small, pre-approved buffer (e.g., 10%).

**2. "Forecasting next year's budget is impossible with fluctuating rates."**
*   *Response:* Emphasize the **Pre-Purchased Credit** model. Since they are buying a fixed dollar amount of credits upfront, the cost is static. The variable element is only how efficiently they utilize those credits, which is an operational metric, not a financial risk.

**3. "Procurement will stall if we can't compare this to a flat seat license."**
*   *Response:* Provide a **Cost Equivalency Calculator**. Show them that at their current testing volume, your usage model equals a flat $48k fee, but offers the downside protection of paying less if their testing needs decrease. Frame it as "insurance against under-utilization" rather than "risk of over-spending."

**4. "What happens if we have a critical release and spike usage unexpectedly?"**
*   *Response:* Highlight the **Overage Protection** clause. Guarantee that emergency spikes will not result in shock bills; instead, they will be billed at a standard, pre-agreed rate or drawn from a contingency buffer included in the contract.

Adopting a hybrid model signals that you understand enterprise procurement constraints. Pure usage-based pricing often stalls deals in regulated industries; hybrid commitments remove the friction while keeping your value metric aligned with consumption.

*Note: Ensure your sales team is trained to sell "budget certainty" first and "usage flexibility" second when speaking to CFOs.*
