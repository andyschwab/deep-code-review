**Direct-to-Consumer (DTC) vs. Wholesale: The Critical Difference**

Yes, selling directly to end-users counts differently toward economic nexus thresholds than your wholesale sales.

*   **Direct-to-Consumer Sales:** Every dollar sold to a final consumer in a state counts fully toward that state's economic nexus revenue threshold (typically $100,000 or $500,000 depending on the state). Every individual shipment counts as a transaction toward the transaction threshold (often 200 transactions).
*   **Wholesale Sales to Galleries:** In most states, sales made to a gallery for resale **do not count** toward your economic nexus thresholds, provided you collect a valid resale certificate from the gallery. Since the gallery is the one responsible for collecting sales tax from the final consumer, your transaction is considered a business-to-business (B2B) sale and is generally exempt from nexus calculations.

**Impact of Shifting to 80% DTC Revenue**

If you shift your strategy to generate 80% of your revenue ($800,000 total projected revenue = $640,000 DTC) from direct online sales, you significantly increase your risk of triggering economic nexus obligations in more states compared to relying on galleries.

*   **Current Exposure:** Your $400,000 DTC revenue is spread across various states. Unless you have a high concentration of sales in a single state, you may currently be under the $100,000 threshold in most jurisdictions. Your $600,000 wholesale revenue likely contributes $0 to these thresholds if proper resale documentation is maintained.
*   **Future Exposure:** Increasing DTC sales to $640,000 means you are much more likely to exceed the $100,000 revenue threshold in multiple states. For example, if you sell $110,000 worth of vases to customers in New York, $120,000 in California, and $105,000 in Texas, you would trigger nexus obligations in all three states, requiring you to register, collect, and remit sales tax in each. Additionally, shipping individual items increases your transaction count, potentially triggering the 200-transaction threshold even if revenue remains lower.

**How to Calculate Your Exposure Accurately**

To calculate your exposure accurately, you must track sales data by **destination state**:

1.  **Segregate Revenue:** Separate your DTC sales from wholesale sales. Exclude wholesale sales from nexus calculations if you have valid resale certificates on file.
2.  **Map by State:** Sum your total DTC revenue and count the number of separate shipments (transactions) delivered to addresses in each specific state over the last 12 months.
3.  **Compare to Thresholds:** Check the current economic nexus thresholds for each state where you have sales. Note that thresholds vary; while many are $100,000/200 transactions, some states have higher revenue limits (e.g., $500,000) or no transaction threshold at all.
4.  **Monitor Continuously:** Economic nexus is usually calculated on a rolling 12-month basis. You must monitor your sales monthly to see if you cross a threshold, which typically triggers an obligation to register and collect tax starting the following month or quarter.

By focusing on DTC, you are effectively taking on the tax collection liability that galleries previously handled for those units. You will need robust sales tax software or a dedicated accountant to manage compliance across multiple jurisdictions once you expand.

*Note: Sales tax laws vary by state and change frequently; this information is for strategic planning and not legal advice.*
