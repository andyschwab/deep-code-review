**You must amortize the $250,000 in master franchise fees over the life of the agreement (or the period services are provided), not recognize it immediately.**

Under standard accounting principles (specifically ASC 952 for franchisors in the US), revenue from initial franchise fees can only be recognized when the franchisor has substantially performed all material services or conditions required by the agreement. In a master franchise arrangement, your obligation typically includes ongoing support, brand protection, territory management, and training throughout the contract term. Because these duties extend over the ten-year life of the agreement, the earnings process is not complete at the signing moment.

Recognizing the full $250,000 immediately would be non-compliant and creates significant audit risk. It would artificially inflate your current year's profitability, misrepresenting the economic reality that this cash represents an advance payment for a decade of future obligations. If audited, you would likely be forced to restate your financials, damaging credibility with lenders and investors.

**Correct Accounting Treatment:**
1.  **Cash Receipt:** Record the $250,000 as a liability on your balance sheet labeled "Deferred Revenue" or "Unearned Franchise Fees."
2.  **Monthly Recognition:** Amortize this liability into revenue systematically over the agreement term. Assuming a straight-line method over 10 years, you would recognize approximately $2,083 per month ($250,000 / 120 months) as revenue.
3.  **Ongoing Royalties:** Continue to recognize the 4% gross revenue share as revenue in the month it is earned from the sub-franchisees, as this relates to ongoing services already performed.

**Impact on Reported Profitability:**
*   **Immediate Recognition (Incorrect):** Shows a massive $250,000 revenue spike this year, followed by artificially low revenue in subsequent years. This creates a "cliff" effect that makes the business look volatile and unsustainable.
*   **Amortization (Correct):** Smooths revenue recognition, adding ~$25,000 annually to your top line for ten years. This accurately matches revenue with the expenses you will incur to support these franchises over time, providing a stable and realistic view of profitability.

While the cash flow impact is immediate (you have the money in the bank), the accounting revenue must be delayed to match the delivery of value.

*Note: While this reflects standard GAAP principles for franchising, you should confirm the specific "substantial performance" milestones in your contract with your CPA to finalize the amortization schedule.*
