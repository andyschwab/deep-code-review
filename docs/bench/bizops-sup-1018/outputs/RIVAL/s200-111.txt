### Defensible TAM Framework for RecoveryPath

To address Ravi's concern about double-counting and present a defensible Total Addressable Market (TAM), you must shift from a **top-down population count** to a **bottom-up revenue opportunity** model. In addiction recovery, a single individual often traverses multiple levels of care (detox, residential, outpatient) simultaneously or sequentially. Counting "people" inflates the market; counting "treatment episodes" or "active management needs" aligns with your value metric.

Here is the logical framework to segment the population and calculate a non-inflated TAM:

#### 1. Define the Unit of Analysis: "Active Recovery Management"
Do not size the market by "number of people with addiction." Instead, size it by **"number of individuals actively seeking or receiving structured support at any given time."**
*   **Logic:** A user in residential care still needs outpatient-style digital support for aftercare planning or family connection. They are not two separate customers; they are one customer with a continuous need.
*   **Action:** Define your TAM user as any individual currently engaged in *any* formal treatment modality who could benefit from continuous digital navigation.

#### 2. The Segmentation Matrix (The "Overlap" Fix)
Create a three-tier segmentation based on **intensity of care**, treating them as mutually exclusive states for the *primary* payer, but acknowledging the digital layer spans all three.

*   **Segment A: High-Intensity (Residential/Inpatient)**
    *   *Characteristics:* 24/7 care, short duration (30–90 days).
    *   *Overlap Risk:* High. These users are often counted in outpatient stats prematurely.
    *   *RecoveryPath Value:* Discharge planning, family updates, early aftercare.
*   **Segment B: Medium-Intensity (Outpatient/IOP)**
    *   *Characteristics:* Scheduled visits, living at home.
    *   *Overlap Risk:* Medium. Often overlaps with Segment A during step-down.
    *   *RecoveryPath Value:* Daily tracking, community support, relapse prevention.
*   **Segment C: Low-Intensity (Self-Directed/Aftercare)**
    *   *Characteristics:* No active clinical visits, long-term maintenance.
    *   *Overlap Risk:* Low. This is your "long tail."
    *   *RecoveryPath Value:* Sustained recovery tools, peer connection.

**The Calculation Adjustment:**
Instead of summing $A + B + C$, use a **weighted prevalence rate**.
$$TAM_{Users} = (Population_{US} \times Prevalence_{Addiction}) \times \%_{Actively\_Seeking\_Help}$$
*Crucially*, apply a **"Concurrency Discount Factor"** (estimated at 15–20% based on industry step-down norms) to remove those counted in both residential and outpatient datasets simultaneously.

#### 3. Bottom-Up Revenue TAM (The Investor Favorite)
Since you have real unit economics, lead with this. It bypasses the demographic overlap debate entirely.

*   **Current Signal:** 12,000 MAU, $45 ARPU, 8% churn.
*   **Assumption:** Your reachable market is defined by the number of treatment slots available annually, not the total addicted population.
*   **Formula:**
    $$TAM_{Revenue} = (\text{Total Annual Treatment Admissions}_{US}) \times (\text{Digital Adoption Rate}) \times (\text{Your ARPU} \times 12)$$

*   **Data Sources for "Total Annual Treatment Admissions":** Use SAMHSA's N-SSATS data (distinct admissions, not unique people). This inherently de-duplicates because an "admission" is a discrete event.
*   **Defensibility:** Tell Ravi: *"We are not sizing the total addicted population; we are sizing the addressable market of active treatment episodes where digital intervention is clinically relevant."*

#### 4. Presenting to Ravi
Structure your slide as follows:
1.  **The Problem:** "Top-down models double-count users moving between care levels."
2.  **The Solution:** "We size based on **Active Treatment Episodes** (SAMHSA data) rather than unique prevalence."
3.  **The Number:** Present the Bottom-Up Revenue TAM.
4.  **The Sensitivity Analysis:** Show a range using a 10%, 15%, and 20% concurrency discount to prove you have stress-tested the overlap risk.

This approach turns the "overlap" from a liability into a demonstration of your nuanced understanding of the patient journey.
