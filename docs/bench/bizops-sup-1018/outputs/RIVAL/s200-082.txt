**High-Risk Classification Breakdown for Northwind AI**

Under the EU AI Act, your automated hiring platform is almost certainly classified as **High-Risk**. The regulation explicitly categorizes AI systems intended for recruitment, selection, and promotion (Annex III, Section 5) as high-risk because they significantly impact access to employment and livelihoods.

The specific criteria triggering this status for you are:
1.  **Use Case:** You are using AI to make or substantially influence decisions regarding job applicants.
2.  **Automation Level:** Your system filters out 60% of applicants before human review. This constitutes "substantial influence" on the outcome, regardless of the final human step.
3.  **Data Sensitivity:** Video interviews often involve biometric data processing, which carries additional scrutiny under the Act.

**Does Human Override Lower the Risk Profile?**
**No.** The presence of a "human-in-the-loop" who can override the decision does **not** remove the High-Risk classification. The EU AI Act acknowledges that human oversight is a *mitigation requirement* for high-risk systems, not an exemption from the category. Because your algorithm performs the initial heavy lifting (filtering 60%), it shapes the pool of candidates the human ever sees. If the AI biases the input, the human cannot fairly evaluate what they never receive. Therefore, the system remains high-risk, and you must implement formal human oversight protocols as part of your compliance, rather than using oversight as a defense against the classification.

**Immediate Steps for Verification and Filing**

1.  **Conduct a Fundamental Rights Impact Assessment (FRIA):** Before engaging an auditor, you must internally document how your system impacts fundamental rights (non-discrimination, privacy). This is a mandatory internal step for all high-risk providers.
2.  **Verify Exemptions (Unlikely but Necessary):** Review if you qualify as a "micro-enterprise" providing a component to a larger system, but since you are the primary provider of the hiring decision logic, this likely does not apply.
3.  **Prepare Technical Documentation:** Compile details on your training data, logic, and accuracy metrics. The Act requires proof that your data is representative and free from bias.
4.  **Engage a Specialized External Auditor:** **Do not rely on an internal review for the final conformity assessment.** While you can perform the initial gap analysis internally, the EU AI Act requires high-risk AI systems to undergo a **conformity assessment**. Given your core product is the decision-making logic itself (not just a feature within a larger tool), you will likely need a third-party notified body to validate your compliance before CE marking and deployment, especially given the biometric/video component.

**Recommendation:** Initiate the internal FRIA immediately to understand your gaps, but budget for and engage a specialized external auditor now. Waiting for the internal review to "suffice" is a strategic error; the complexity of HR algorithms and video analysis typically mandates external validation to ensure your conformity assessment holds up to regulatory scrutiny. Delaying this engagement risks the 18-month profitability delay you fear, as remediation after a failed audit is far costlier than proactive preparation.

*Note: This analysis is based on the current text of the EU AI Act; final implementation depends on specific harmonized standards yet to be fully published.*
