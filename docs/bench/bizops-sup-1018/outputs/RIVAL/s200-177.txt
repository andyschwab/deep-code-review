**General Concepts for Your Discussion with a Tax Professional:**

When shifting from sales to leasing, the following general distinctions often arise in tax discussions, though rules vary by location:

*   **Sales Tax vs. Use Tax:** In many jurisdictions, selling a unit triggers sales tax on the full purchase price at the time of sale. Leasing may instead trigger sales tax on each monthly lease payment, or require the lessee to pay use tax. Some states treat leases as "continued sales," while others view them as service contracts.
*   **Nexus Creation:** Placing physical equipment (your simulators) in a state for lease can create "physical presence nexus," potentially obligating your company to register and collect taxes in that state, even if you have no office or employees there. This is a critical risk area for your expansion.
*   **Income Classification:** For income tax purposes, lease revenue is generally treated as ordinary income, similar to sales revenue, but the timing of recognition and deductibility of the hardware costs (depreciation vs. cost of goods sold) will differ. It typically does not reclassify your business from selling goods to providing services for federal income tax purposes, but state gross receipts taxes may differ.
*   **Filing Requirements:** If nexus is established, you may need to file monthly or quarterly sales tax returns in those specific states, rather than just in your home state.
