### PrimeCare Concierge: Break-Even & Strategy Analysis

**Current Financial Snapshot**
*   **Revenue:** $960,000 (400 members × $2,400)
*   **Fixed Costs (Ops):** $600,000
*   **Insurance (New):** $84,000 (Increased from $60,000)
*   **Total Fixed Costs:** $684,000
*   **Variable Costs:** $80,000 (400 members × $200)
*   **Total Costs:** $764,000
*   **Current Profit:** $196,000 (Down from $220,000 previously)

**Scenario A: Acquire New Members at Current Price ($2,400)**
To absorb the additional $24,000 insurance cost solely through volume:
*   **Contribution Margin per Member:** $2,400 (Price) - $200 (Variable Cost) = $2,200.
*   **Additional Members Needed:** $24,000 / $2,200 = **10.9 members**.
*   **Action:** You must acquire **11 new members** to fully restore previous profit levels. This represents a 2.75% growth in your member base.

**Scenario B: Raise Fees with Current Members (400)**
To absorb the $24,000 cost solely through price increases:
*   **Increase per Member:** $24,000 / 400 members = $60.
*   **New Annual Fee:** $2,460.
*   **Percentage Increase:** 2.5%.
*   **Action:** A **2.5% price increase** maintains your exact previous profit margin assuming zero churn.

**Scenario C: Maintaining Margin with Potential Churn**
You asked specifically about the risk of a >10% price hike.
*   **If you raise fees by 10% ($240 increase):** New price is $2,640.
*   **Revenue Gain (if 0 churn):** $96,000. This would not only cover the $24,000 insurance hike but add $72,000 to profit.
*   **Break-Even Churn Rate:** To merely cover the $24,000 insurance cost with a 10% price hike, you can afford to lose **9.1% of your members** (approx. 36 members).
    *   *Math:* You need $24,000 net gain. Each remaining member pays $240 extra. If you lose $X$ members, you have $(400-X)$ payers. $(400-X) \times 240 = 24,000$. Solving for $X$ yields ~36 members.

**Strategic Recommendation**
1.  **Immediate Fix:** Implement the **2.5% price increase** ($60/year or $5/month). In concierge medicine, this is often within the "noise" of inflation and is unlikely to trigger significant churn compared to a 10% hike.
2.  **Growth Target:** Simultaneously aim to acquire **11 new members**. This is a highly achievable target (less than 1 new member per month) and restores your full previous profit buffer.
3.  **Avoid Deep Cuts:** Reducing staff or cutting core services risks devaluing the concierge proposition, which could trigger the very churn you fear. The math shows you do not need drastic measures; a minor price adjustment combined with modest growth solves the deficit.

*Note: The 10% hike scenario provides a massive safety buffer, allowing for nearly 10% churn before you are worse off than today. However, starting with the minimal 2.5% hike is the safest path to preserve trust.*
