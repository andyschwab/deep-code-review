### Seed Round Equity & Dilution Analysis for BuildBot Toys

**1. The Core Math: Pre-Money vs. Post-Money**
The investor's offer is based on a **$4 million pre-money valuation** with a **$1 million investment**.
*   **Pre-Money Valuation:** $4,000,000 (Value before cash)
*   **Investment Amount:** $1,000,000
*   **Post-Money Valuation:** $5,000,000 ($4M + $1M)

**Equity Percentage for Investor:**
The investor receives ownership based on the *post-money* valuation.
$$ \text{Investor Equity} = \frac{\text{Investment}}{\text{Post-Money Valuation}} = \frac{1,000,000}{5,000,000} = \mathbf{20\%} $$

You should offer exactly **20% equity** for this $1M check. Offering more would be giving away value unnecessarily; offering less would reject their stated valuation terms.

**2. Founder Dilution Breakdown**
The remaining **80%** of the company is shared among the existing shareholders (you and your co-founders). The $50,000 in supplier debt is a liability on the balance sheet; it reduces the company's net asset value but **does not** change the percentage calculation of the equity round unless the debt is converted to equity (which is not stated here). The debt must simply be paid down using operating cash or the new capital.

*Current Cap Table (Pre-Money):*
*   You: 60%
*   Co-Founder A: 20%
*   Co-Founder B: 20%

*New Cap Table (Post-Money) — Everyone is diluted by 20%:*
*   **Investor:** 20.0%
*   **You:** $60\% \times 0.80 = \mathbf{48.0\%}$
*   **Co-Founder A:** $20\% \times 0.80 = \mathbf{16.0\%}$
*   **Co-Founder B:** $20\% \times 0.80 = \mathbf{16.0\%}$
*   **Total:** 100%

**3. Control Assessment**
You retain **48% ownership**. While this is not a majority (>50%), you and your two co-founders combined retain **80%**, ensuring you maintain voting control as a founding team. However, individually, you no longer have unilateral control. Ensure your shareholder agreement protects founder voting rights if one co-founder exits.

**4. Critical Legal Questions for Your Lawyer**
Before signing, clarify these terms which can drastically alter the *effective* valuation and your control:

*   **Valuation Cap:** Is this $4M cap a hard ceiling for this round, or is it a cap for a convertible note/SAFE that converts later? If this is a priced equity round, the math above holds. If it is a SAFE/Note with a $4M cap, the final percentage depends on the valuation of the *next* round.
*   **Liquidation Preference:** Is the preference **1x non-participating** (standard) or **participating** (double-dipping)? A participating preference could mean the investor gets their $1M back *plus* their 20% share of remaining proceeds, severely reducing your payout in an exit.
*   **Option Pool Shuffle:** Is the employee option pool (typically 10-15%) created *before* (diluting founders only) or *after* (diluting everyone) the investment? Investors often demand it be created pre-money, which effectively lowers your valuation further.
*   **Board Composition:** With 20% equity, the investor may demand a board seat. Will the board be 2 founders + 1 investor, or 2 founders + 1 independent + 1 investor? This determines actual operational control more than share percentage.

**Note:** The $50k debt should be paid immediately upon funding to clean up the balance sheet before scaling production.
**Note:** Retaining 48% is strong for a seed round; focus now on hitting the 50k unit target to justify a higher Series A valuation.
