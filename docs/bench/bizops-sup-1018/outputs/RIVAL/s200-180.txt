However, from a **pricing and offer construction perspective**, I can highlight how your current structure creates misaligned incentives and suggest how to reframe the "guarantee" to protect your business model without needing immediate legal drafting:

**1. The Risk of Absolute Guarantees in Pricing**
An unconditional "job placement" promise functions as a binary trigger for liability. In pricing theory, this is a "fixed outcome" promise with variable costs. If the market softens, your cost to serve (finding jobs) becomes infinite, while your revenue is capped at $12,000. This asymmetry threatens solvency. Successful education providers rarely guarantee *employment*; they guarantee *employability* or *outcomes contingent on student effort*.

**2. Reframing the Value Metric**
Instead of selling "Job Placement," consider shifting your value metric to "Career Launch Support" or "Certification + Placement Assistance."
*   **Current Model:** Pay $12k → Get Job (Binary risk).
*   **Resilient Model:** Pay $12k → Get Training + Guaranteed Support until Hired (Process guarantee).

**3. Structuring the Offer for Safety**
To maintain the marketing power of a guarantee while reducing exposure, successful programs typically attach conditions that shift some responsibility to the buyer (the student). Common non-legal structural elements include:
*   **Eligibility Criteria:** The guarantee only activates if the student completes 100% of coursework, attends career coaching sessions, and applies to a minimum number of roles per week.
*   **Definition of "Placement":** Marketing materials often clarify that "placement" includes relevant contract work, internships, or roles within a broader salary band, rather than a specific full-time threshold.
*   **Remedy Definition:** Instead of an open-ended liability, define the remedy clearly upfront (e.g., "100% tuition refund" or "free continued coaching") rather than leaving it open to interpretation or damages.
