### Capital Efficiency Analysis: DTC vs. Medicare Advantage (18-Month Horizon)

**Current State Constraints**
With a $90,000 monthly burn and 4 sales staff, you have approximately 18 months of runway if revenue remains flat. However, your current DTC model is likely cash-flow negative on a per-unit basis when factoring in COGS (Cost of Goods Sold), which is not provided but critical.

**Scenario A: Direct-to-Consumer (DTC) Status Quo**
*   **Unit Economics:** Revenue $199 + ($15 × 18 mo) = $469 LTV (gross). CAC is $85.
*   **Volume Potential:** At 2.5% conversion, acquiring 1,000 customers requires 40,000 ad impressions. Assuming a reasonable CPM, you can scale this immediately.
*   **18-Month Cash Flow:** This model generates immediate cash inflow. If your hardware COGS is under $114 per unit, you are contribution margin positive. The primary risk is scaling ad spend without exhausting your audience or driving up CAC.
*   **Capital Deployment:** Highly efficient. Every dollar spent on ads yields a return within 30 days. No upfront sunk costs other than ad spend.

**Scenario B: Medicare Advantage (MA) Pivot**
*   **Unit Economics:** Revenue $140 + ($10 × 18 mo) = $320 LTV (gross). No CAC, but high fixed entry cost.
*   **The Hurdle:** You must spend $75,000 immediately on compliance. With a 9-month sales cycle, you will incur $810,000 in operating burn (9 months × $90k) before signing a single deal or recognizing a dollar of revenue.
*   **Volume Requirement:** To recoup the $75k compliance cost alone at $140/device, you need to sell ~536 units just to break even on the entry fee.
*   **18-Month Cash Flow:** Months 1–9: Negative $885,000 (Burn + Compliance). Months 10–18: Revenue begins trickling in. Unless you close massive deals immediately in Month 10, you will run out of cash before the channel becomes profitable.
*   **Capital Deployment:** Highly inefficient for your current runway. The "J-curve" is too deep; you will likely insolvency before the first check clears.

**Verdict**
Do not pivot 100% to Medicare Advantage. The 9-month sales cycle combined with your $90k monthly burn creates a fatal cash gap. The lower CAC is irrelevant because you cannot survive the "zero revenue" period required to access it.

**Recommended Hybrid Approach:**
Continue DTC to fund operations and prove clinical utility. Dedicate *one* salesperson (not all four) to pilot the MA channel part-time while seeking non-dilutive grants or specific healthcare venture capital to cover the $75k compliance and extended burn. Do not commit the full team or runway to this channel until a Letter of Intent (LOI) is signed.

**Regulatory & Contractual Unknowns (AI Cannot Resolve)**
1.  **Formulary Inclusion Criteria:** Specific clinical endpoints required by *each* MA plan to list your device on their formulary; these vary by payer and are not public.
2.  **Risk Adjustment Codes:** Whether your device qualifies for specific HCC (Hierarchical Condition Category) coding that drives payer reimbursement value.
3.  **Contractual "Most Favored Nation" Clauses:** Terms that might prevent you from selling DTC at a lower effective price than the negotiated MA rate.
4.  **Data Interoperability Mandates:** Specific HL7/FHIR integration requirements mandated by individual payers that go beyond general HIPAA compliance.

**Expert Consultation Required**
You need a **Healthcare Reimbursement Strategist** with specific experience in **Medicare Advantage Part C benefits design**. Do not hire a general regulatory consultant; you need someone who has successfully negotiated vendor contracts with MA plans and understands the "supplemental benefits" budget allocation process.
