### Viral-Pricing Elasticity Framework for DevNote

To model the trade-off between constraining the free tier (to drive conversion) and preserving the viral loop (to drive sign-ups), use the **Net Growth Impact Equation**. This framework calculates whether a specific friction change yields a net positive or negative impact on your total paid seat growth.

#### 1. The Core Equation
The goal is to ensure that the **Gain from Conversion** outweighs the **Loss from Viral Decay**.

$$ \Delta P_{net} = (U_{free} \times \Delta C_{rate} \times S_{avg}) - (U_{lost} \times K_{current} \times C_{base} \times S_{avg}) $$

Where:
*   $\Delta P_{net}$: Net change in new paid seats per month.
*   $U_{free}$: Current active free users (12,000).
*   $\Delta C_{rate}$: The *increase* in conversion rate required (e.g., moving from 1.2% to 1.5% is a 0.003 increase).
*   $S_{avg}$: Average seats per paying account (assume 1 for now unless team features drive multi-seat).
*   $U_{lost}$: The estimated number of free users lost due to the 20% compute reduction.
*   $K_{current}$: Current viral coefficient (1.8).
*   $C_{base}$: Current baseline conversion rate (1.2% or 0.012).

#### 2. Modeling the 20% Compute Reduction Scenario
You need to estimate two unknown variables based on small-scale A/B tests or historical churn data:
1.  **Friction Churn ($F_c$):** What % of free users leave immediately if compute is cut by 20%?
2.  **Conversion Lift ($L_c$):** What % of remaining free users upgrade to avoid the limit?

**The Break-Even Formula:**
To find the required conversion lift to offset a specific drop in sign-ups, rearrange the logic to solve for the minimum $\Delta C_{rate}$:

$$ \text{Required } \Delta C_{rate} = \frac{U_{lost} \times 1.8 \times 0.012}{12,000 - U_{lost}} $$

*Example Calculation:*
If reducing compute causes 5% of free users to leave immediately ($U_{lost} = 600$):
*   **Viral Loss:** $600 \text{ users} \times 1.8 \text{ invites} \times 1.2\% \text{ conv} = \mathbf{13 \text{ lost paid seats/month}}$.
*   **Required Gain:** You need to generate at least 13 *additional* paid seats from the remaining 11,400 users.
*   **Required Lift:** $13 / 11,400 = \mathbf{0.11\%}$.
*   **Result:** Your conversion rate only needs to rise from 1.20% to **1.31%** to break even. If the friction drives conversion to 1.5%, you have a net positive growth engine.

#### 3. Implementation Strategy
Do not apply this globally yet. Use a **Cohort-Based Rollout**:
1.  **Segment:** Isolate 10% of new free sign-ups (1,200 users).
2.  **Apply Friction:** Implement the 20% compute limit for this group only.
3.  **Measure:** Track three metrics over 30 days:
    *   Immediate churn rate (Day 1–3).
    *   Viral coefficient of the cohort (invites sent per user).
    *   Conversion rate to paid (Day 30).
4.  **Calculate:** Plug these real numbers into the equation above.

#### 4. Strategic Recommendation
Given your low burn ($8k) and high runway, avoid blunt "hard limits" that kill the viral coefficient entirely. Instead, consider **Soft Friction**:
*   **Speed Throttling:** Reduce compute speed rather than hard caps for free users. This annoys power users into upgrading but doesn't prevent casual users from inviting others.
*   **Team Feature Gating:** Since you have 450 paid seats, your best lever may not be compute limits but **collaboration limits**. Allow unlimited compute for individuals but restrict shared notebooks to 2 users on the free plan. This targets the "team" value metric without hurting the individual viral spread.

Your current conversion (1.2%) is low for a specialized tool, suggesting the value gap between "free" and "paid" is unclear. A 20% compute cut might be invisible to casual users but fatal to power users. Test specifically for the point where frustration converts to payment rather than abandonment.

*Note: With $95k remaining and only $8k burn, you have ~12 months of runway. Prioritize learning the elasticity curve over immediate revenue maximization.*
