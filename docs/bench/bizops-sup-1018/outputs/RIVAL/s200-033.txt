**Immediate Assessment: Yes, you have likely created a corporate tax filing obligation.**

By renting commercial kitchen space in Austin, Denver, and Seattle, you have established "physical nexus" in Texas, Colorado, and Washington. In state tax law, physical presence is the strongest trigger for nexus. It generally does not matter if the rental is for six hours a day or if the agreement is a license rather than a lease; the fact that you are paying for and utilizing specific physical space to conduct business operations (baking) creates a taxable connection to those states.

**Specific Thresholds and Distinctions**

You asked about thresholds regarding physical presence versus economic nexus. It is critical to distinguish between the two, as they operate independently:

1.  **Physical Nexus:** This is triggered by having a tangible presence in a state. Your rented kitchens qualify immediately. There is usually no revenue threshold for physical nexus; the mere act of renting space and operating there creates the filing obligation. The type of rental agreement (lease vs. license) rarely negates this if you have exclusive use of the space during your operating hours.
2.  **Economic Nexus:** This is triggered solely by sales volume or transaction count into a state, regardless of physical presence. While you now have physical nexus, you should also know that most states (including WA and CO) have economic nexus thresholds (often $100,000 in sales or 200 transactions). Since you generate $200,000 *total* nationally, you may not hit economic nexus thresholds in each individual state yet, but your physical presence makes those revenue thresholds irrelevant for establishing the *requirement to file*.

**State-Specific Implications**

*   **Texas:** Renting space creates nexus for the Texas Franchise Tax. Even if your revenue apportioned to Texas is below the "no tax due" threshold, you likely still must file a "No Tax Due" report annually to remain compliant.
*   **Colorado:** Physical presence triggers income tax filing requirements. You will likely need to file a Colorado corporate income tax return and apportion your income based on sales, property, and payroll factors in the state.
*   **Washington:** Washington does not have a corporate income tax but imposes a Business and Occupation (B&O) tax. Physical presence mandates registration and filing for B&O tax on gross receipts derived from Washington activities.

**Action Plan and Professional Criteria**

To avoid penalties and protect your cash reserves, you must take the following steps immediately:

1.  **Register to Do Business:** You likely need to register as a foreign entity in TX, CO, and WA before filing taxes.
2.  **Apportionment Analysis:** You do not pay tax on your entire $200,000 in every state. You must calculate what percentage of your income is taxable in each state based on apportionment formulas (typically involving sales, property, and payroll). Since your property (kitchen rental) and potentially payroll (if you hire local bakers) are now in these states, your apportionment factors will shift.
3.  **Sales Tax Collection:** Physical nexus almost certainly requires you to collect and remit sales tax on orders shipped to customers in these states, regardless of the corporate income tax impact.

**Required Professional Evaluation**

Do not attempt to calculate apportionment yourself. You need to engage a **state and local tax (SALT) specialist** or a CPA firm with specific expertise in multi-state bakery/food manufacturing. When interviewing professionals, ask for:
*   Experience with **nexus studies** for businesses expanding physical footprints.
*   Specific knowledge of **apportionment rules** in TX, CO, and WA.
*   Ability to perform a **voluntary disclosure agreement (VDA)** analysis if you have already been operating in these states without registering, which can often reduce penalties.

*Note: Operating without registration despite having physical nexus can lead to back taxes, interest, and significant penalties. Address this before your next quarterly estimated tax payment.*
