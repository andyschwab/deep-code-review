**Conceptual Distinctions in Regulation**

Regulators typically look at **substance over form**. This means that while contract language (disclaimers stating you have no custody) is necessary, it is rarely sufficient on its own if the technical reality suggests otherwise. The regulatory line is generally drawn based on **control** and **access**.

1.  **Custody vs. Record Keeping:**
    *   **Record Keeping (Data Hosting):** You store information *about* transactions that occurred elsewhere. You are a passive repository. The data is a historical log or a snapshot provided by the customer.
    *   **Custody/Transmission (Financial Facilitation):** You have the ability to initiate, authorize, or alter the state of funds. If your system allows you (or your code) to write directly to a "balance" field in a way that changes the actual available funds for the end-user without an external trigger from the licensed entity, regulators may view this as exercising control over the funds.

2.  **The Role of Technical Architecture:**
    *   **Write-Access:** The concern about write-access is valid. If your platform allows arbitrary updates to balance fields that are treated as the "source of truth" for spendable funds, you may be seen as maintaining the ledger of record in a capacity that resembles a bank or transmitter.
    *   **Immutability and Audit Trails:** Fintech architectures often use append-only logs for transactions. Balances are usually calculated views derived from the log, not fields that are directly overwritten. If your architecture allows direct overwriting of balance snapshots without a corresponding immutable transaction record, it increases regulatory risk.
    *   **Segregation of Duties:** Regulators look for clear separation between the entity moving the money (the licensed fintech) and the entity storing the data (you). Your system should technically enforce that it cannot initiate value transfer.

3.  **Contractual Limitations:**
    *   A clause stating "no custody" is a declaration of intent, not a technical safeguard. Regulators will test whether your system *actually* prevents you from taking custody. If a bug or a feature allows you to modify balances, the contract clause will likely not protect you from enforcement actions.
    *   Contracts should explicitly define the customer (the fintech startup) as the sole entity responsible for compliance, licensing, and the accuracy of the data they upload.
