### Sales Tax Treatment: Post-Sale Rebates vs. Instant Discounts

For sales tax purposes, the timing and structure of your promotion fundamentally change your tax liability.

**1. Post-Sale Mail-In Rebates (Your Current Plan)**
In California and most U.S. jurisdictions, a **mail-in rebate** issued *after* the sale is generally **not** deductible from the sales price for sales tax calculations.
*   **Taxable Amount:** You must collect and remit sales tax on the full **$110** selling price.
*   **Reasoning:** The transaction is complete at the register for $110. The subsequent $25 check you mail to the customer is treated as a separate financial transaction (a refund or incentive payment) occurring after the tax point. The state views the full $110 as the gross receipts subject to tax.
*   **Result:** You collect ~$9.63 in sales tax per unit (assuming 8.75% avg CA rate) on the full $110. Your taxable revenue base remains $110 per unit.

**2. Instant Discounts (Point-of-Sale Reduction)**
If you restructure the promotion as an **instant discount** applied directly at the register:
*   **Taxable Amount:** You collect and remit sales tax only on the net price of **$85** ($110 - $25).
*   **Reasoning:** The customer never pays the $25 portion; the seller absorbs it immediately as a price reduction. Sales tax is calculated on the actual money changing hands at the moment of sale.
*   **Result:** You collect ~$7.44 in sales tax per unit. Your taxable revenue base drops to $85 per unit.

### Compliance & Documentation Requirements

To legally claim the reduced tax base, you must shift from a rebate model to an instant discount model.

**Documentation for Instant Discounts:**
*   **Receipts:** Customer receipts must clearly show the $25 deduction as a "Promotional Discount" or "Instant Rebate" line item *before* the sales tax calculation.
*   **POS Configuration:** Your point-of-sale system must be configured to apply the discount prior to tax computation, not as a post-tender refund.
*   **Audit Trail:** Maintain records of the promotion terms showing that the discount was available to all customers at the point of sale without requiring post-purchase registration.

**If You Stick with Mail-In Rebates:**
*   You must remit tax on the full $110.
*   The $25 payout is recorded as a marketing expense or contra-revenue on your income tax return, but it does not lower your *sales tax* liability.
*   Ensure your rebate forms clearly state that the rebate is a post-purchase incentive and does not alter the original sales price paid.
