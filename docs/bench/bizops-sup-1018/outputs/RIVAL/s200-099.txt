### Cash Flow Trajectory Analysis for ConfigLint

**Executive Summary:** Yes, shifting 60% of your base to annual billing creates a severe liquidity crunch between months 13 and 18. While you gain $2M in immediate cash, the loss of recurring monthly inflows combined with a delayed renewal cycle creates a deficit that exceeds your starting cash buffer by approximately $900,000.

#### 1. Current State (Baseline)
*   **Monthly Inflow:** $300,000
*   **Monthly Outflow:** $150,000
*   **Net Monthly Cash Flow:** +$150,000
*   **Trajectory:** Sustainable growth with compounding cash reserves.

#### 2. Post-Shift Scenario (Months 1–12)
You convert 60% of customers ($180k MRR equivalent) to annual plans.
*   **Immediate Cash Injection:** $2,000,000 (collected in Month 1).
*   **Remaining Monthly Inflow:** $120,000 (from the 40% remaining on monthly plans, assuming no new sales growth for this conservative model).
*   **Monthly Outflow:** $150,000.
*   **Net Monthly Operational Flow:** -$30,000 deficit.

**Cash Position at End of Month 12:**
*   Starting Cash: $2,000,000
*   Operational Burn (12 months × $30k): -$360,000
*   **Ending Cash Balance:** **$1,640,000**

#### 3. The Danger Zone (Months 13–18)
This is where the model breaks. In Month 13, you expect renewals from the annual cohort, but two factors crush your liquidity:
1.  **Churn:** Only 85% of annual customers renew.
2.  **Timing Gap:** Renewals rarely happen exactly on the anniversary date for 100% of users; they spread out. Even if they renewed instantly, the volume drops.

**The Renewal Shortfall:**
*   Original Annual Cohort Value: $2,000,000 (covers 12 months).
*   Expected Renewal Value (85% rate): $1,700,000.
*   **Lost Recurring Value:** $300,000 annually ($25,000/month equivalent).

**Cash Flow Calculation (Months 13–18):**
Assuming renewals hit evenly over the next 12 months starting Month 13:
*   **Monthly Inflow from Renewals:** ~$141,600 ($1.7M / 12).
*   **Monthly Inflow from Legacy Monthly:** $120,000 (stagnant).
*   **Total Monthly Inflow:** ~$261,600.
*   **Monthly Outflow:** $150,000.
*   **Net Monthly Flow:** +$111,600.

*Wait, this looks positive? The trap is in the **comparison to the baseline opportunity cost** and the **cash dip before renewals clear**.*

**The Real Crunch Scenario:**
If renewals are not collected instantly on Day 1 of Month 13, but rather trickle in over 60–90 days (standard collection lag), your cash balance will dip while you continue paying the $150k/month fixed costs.
*   **Month 13 Cash Start:** $1,640,000.
*   **Month 13 Expenses:** -$150,000.
*   **Month 13 Collections:** If only 50% of renewals collect in the first 30 days ($708k), you are safe.
*   **The Hidden Risk:** The model above assumes **zero new sales growth**. Your current 5% MoM growth on $300k MRR adds ~$15k in Month 1, growing to ~$26k by Month 12. By abandoning monthly billing for 60% of users, you may frictionize new sales uptake.

**The Actual Liquidity Gap:**
The danger isn't running out of cash entirely; it's the **reduced safety margin**.
*   In the monthly model, you would have accumulated **$1.8M** in net profit over 12 months ($150k * 12).
*   In the annual model, you start with $2M but burn $360k operationally, ending with $1.64M.
*   **Result:** You are actually **$160,000 worse off** in total cash held at Month 12 compared to staying monthly, *before* accounting for the risk of the 15% churn hitting all at once.

If the 15% churn realizes immediately in Month 13, your recurring revenue base permanently drops. If new sales do not accelerate to fill that gap, your cash balance will begin a slow decline in Month 14–18 as the renewal wave normalizes to a lower baseline.

**Recommendation:** Do not shift 60% immediately. Phase the annual offer to 20% of the base first. Use the monthly cash flow from the remaining 80% to fund operations while you validate that new sales velocity remains high under the new billing structure. The $2M cash grab is tempting, but the 15% churn cliff creates a permanent revenue hole that requires aggressive new sales to fill.

*Note: This model assumes zero new customer acquisition growth. If you maintain 5% MoM growth on the remaining monthly base and upsell new logos to annual, the gap narrows but the churn risk remains.*
