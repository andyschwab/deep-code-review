**Assessment of Revenue Recognition and Financial Volatility**

Changing from a flat fee to a 15% success fee based on first-year salary introduces significant accounting complexity regarding revenue recognition, specifically concerning variable consideration and refund liabilities under UK GAAP (FRS 102) or IFRS 15.

**Revenue Deferral and Recognition Requirements**
Charging a percentage-based fee contingent on a candidate's tenure does not automatically trigger complex deferral requirements, but it does mandate the estimation of **variable consideration**. Under current accounting standards, you cannot recognize the full 15% fee immediately upon placement if a refund is probable. Instead, you must estimate the amount of revenue you expect to be entitled to (the "most likely amount" or "expected value") and recognize only that portion. The portion subject to potential refund must be recorded as a **refund liability** (a contract liability), not revenue. If a candidate leaves within the three-month guarantee period, you reduce the refund liability and cash, rather than recording a new expense or revenue reversal, provided the initial estimate was accurate. This is a standard adjustment, not a complex deferral scheme, but it requires robust monthly estimation processes.

**Revenue Volatility Calculation**
Your current model yields stable revenue: £180,000/quarter implies £60,000/month based on 20 placements/month at £3,000 each.
Under the new model:
*   **Average Fee:** £6,000 per placement.
*   **Base Monthly Revenue (Gross):** 20 placements × £6,000 = £120,000.
*   **Refund Scenario:** If 20% of placements result in partial refunds (assuming a full refund for simplicity in this volatility check), you face a potential liability of 4 placements × £6,000 = £24,000.
*   **Net Recognized Revenue:** If you conservatively estimate a 20% failure rate upfront, you would recognize only £96,000 (£120k - £24k) immediately.
*   **Volatility Impact:** If actual refunds deviate from your estimate (e.g., only 10% fail one month, 30% the next), your recognized revenue will fluctuate based on **adjustments to prior estimates**. A month with unexpectedly low churn allows you to release previously held reserves, spiking revenue. A month with high churn forces you to increase reserves, depressing revenue. This creates higher reported volatility compared to the flat-fee model, where revenue is fixed upon the triggering event (hire) with no variable component.

**Reserve for Doubtful Accounts**
This model does not typically require a "reserve for doubtful accounts" (which is for customer non-payment/credit risk). Instead, it requires a **provision for refunds** (contract liability). The risk is not that the employer won't pay, but that you must return the money. This is a liability against revenue, not an allowance against receivables.

**Advisor vs. Legal Templates**
Standard legal templates are **insufficient** to mitigate this accounting risk. While lawyers can draft the refund clauses, determining *how* to estimate the variable consideration, calculate the refund liability, and document the methodology for auditors is a specialized finance function. For a Series A round, auditors will scrutinize your revenue recognition policy heavily. You require a **specialized finance advisor or fractional CFO** with SaaS/marketplace experience to design the revenue recognition policy and estimation model. Relying solely on legal templates without an accounting framework for variable consideration will likely result in audit qualifications or restatements, jeopardizing your fundraising.
