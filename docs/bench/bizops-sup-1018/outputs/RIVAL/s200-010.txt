**Revenue Recognition Conclusion**

For Veloce Bicycles, you generally recognize the **full $4,500 as revenue at the point of sale** (when the bike is delivered to the customer), not deferred over the 12-month financing period. The fact that you are subsidizing the interest cost via a third-party lender does not change the timing of revenue recognition for the bicycle itself, provided specific criteria are met.

**Specific Criteria for Immediate Recognition**
Under current accounting standards (ASC 606), you can book the full revenue immediately if:
1.  **Performance Obligation Met:** You have transferred control of the bike to the customer (delivery has occurred).
2.  **Collectibility is Probable:** The third-party lender has approved the customer's credit, and you have a guaranteed right to receive the cash from the lender, regardless of whether the customer defaults later. Since the lender assumes the credit risk, this criterion is usually satisfied upon lender approval.
3.  **Fixed Price:** The transaction price is fixed at $4,500.

**Impact of Subsidizing the Interest**
The method of funding the 0% offer (you absorbing the cost vs. the lender waiving it) affects **expense classification**, not revenue timing.

*   **Your Scenario (You Absorb Cost):** You pay a fee to the lender to buy down the interest rate to 0% for the customer. This fee is recorded as a **sales incentive, marketing expense, or cost of sales** in the period the sale occurs. It is an immediate expense that reduces your net income for the quarter, but it does not reduce the top-line revenue figure or defer it.
*   **Alternative Scenario (Lender Waives Interest):** If the lender absorbed the cost without reimbursement from you, the accounting result for *your* revenue would be identical: full recognition at sale. The difference is purely who bears the cost of capital.

**Financial Forecasting Impact**
For your board meeting forecast regarding the 200 bikes in Q1:
*   **Revenue:** Record $900,000 (200 bikes × $4,500) in Q1 revenue.
*   **Expense:** Record the total cost of the interest subsidy paid to the lender as an expense in Q1. Do not amortize this expense over 12 months; it is incurred to generate the immediate sale.
*   **Cash Flow:** While revenue is recognized now, your cash inflow from the lender may be immediate (if they buy the loan) or monthly (if they service it and remit payments to you). Ensure your cash flow forecast distinguishes between *accrual revenue* (recognized now) and *cash receipts* (timing depends on your agreement with the lender).

**Summary**
The financing arrangement is a separate transaction from the sale of the bike. As long as the bike is delivered and payment from the lender is assured, the revenue is earned immediately. The subsidy is a cost of acquiring that sale, recognized concurrently.
