Entering the K-12 public school market in New York, Illinois, Ohio, Georgia, and Oregon fundamentally shifts your business from a commercial transaction to a government procurement process. Unlike universities, which often have decentralized purchasing authority, public school districts in these states are bound by strict statutory requirements. You cannot safely navigate this expansion with general research alone; specific legal mandates in each jurisdiction require specialist intervention before you sign agreements.

**Mandatory Legal Specialist Requirements**
You must hire a local education law specialist or a government contracts consultant for the following hurdles. General research is insufficient here because misinterpretation leads to contract invalidation or non-payment:

1.  **Vendor Registration and Bidding Thresholds:** In all five states, contracts exceeding specific dollar amounts (often as low as $1,000–$5,000 for goods/services) trigger mandatory competitive bidding or formal Request for Proposal (RFP) processes.
    *   **New York & Illinois:** Have particularly rigid "prevailing wage" and bidding statutes. If your module installation or training services are misclassified, you could face penalties.
    *   **Ohio & Georgia:** Require specific vendor registrations in state-wide portals (OhioBuys, Georgia SWAM) before a district can legally issue a purchase order. Missing these steps renders the contract void.
    *   **Oregon:** Public contracting codes require specific certification for small businesses if you wish to bypass certain competitive hurdles, which requires legal verification of your eligibility.

2.  **Student Data Privacy Statutes:** This is your highest risk area. Each state has unique laws governing minor data that supersede federal FERPA guidelines.
    *   **New York (Ed Law 2-d):** Requires a specific "Parent Bill of Rights" attachment and a data security plan filed with the state. Non-compliance stops payments immediately.
    *   **Illinois (SOPIPA):** Strictly prohibits using student data for targeted advertising or building personal profiles. Your VR telemetry data collection must be legally vetted against this statute.
    *   **California-style laws in OR/GA:** Oregon and Georgia have enacted similar strict prohibitions on data mining. A standard commercial Terms of Service will likely violate these statutes, exposing you to liability and contract termination.

3.  **Appropriation and Payment Clauses:** Public schools operate on fiscal years and appropriated funds. Contracts often contain "non-appropriation" clauses allowing them to cancel if funding lapses. Commercial "net-30" terms are often unenforceable; many districts legally cannot pay until 45–90 days post-invoice receipt. A specialist must rewrite your payment terms to align with state prompt-payment acts to avoid cash flow crises.

**Navigable via General Research**
You can handle the following internally with diligent research into state Department of Education websites:
*   **Identifying Decision Makers:** Determining whether purchasing is centralized at the district level or individual school level (usually district-level for $2,500 contracts).
*   **Budget Cycles:** Learning when each state's school fiscal year begins (e.g., July 1 vs. September 1) to time your sales outreach.
*   **Basic Vendor Forms:** Downloading standard W-9 and vendor registration forms from district websites, though filling them out correctly often requires the specialist noted above.

**Critical Pitfalls to Avoid**
*   **Starting Work Before a Signed PO:** In all five states, verbal agreements or "letters of intent" are legally non-binding for public funds. If you deploy modules before a formal Purchase Order number is issued, you will likely never be paid.
*   **Assuming Uniformity:** Do not assume a contract signed in Ohio works in New York. The data privacy attachments alone differ radically.
*   **Ignoring "Buy American" Provisions:** Some states have preferences or mandates for domestically produced hardware/software components which could disqualify your VR modules if your supply chain isn't vetted.

**Conclusion**
Do not attempt to sign your first K-12 contract in these states without legal review. The cost of a specialist is negligible compared to the risk of providing services to 200 schools with unenforceable contracts or violating minor data privacy laws. Your commercial template is legally incompatible with public sector procurement in these jurisdictions.

*Note: This analysis is based on general regulatory frameworks and does not constitute legal advice. Regulations change frequently; verify current statutes with counsel.*
*Prioritize data privacy compliance in New York and Illinois first, as their enforcement mechanisms are the most aggressive.*
