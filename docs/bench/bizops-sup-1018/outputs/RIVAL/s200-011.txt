### Bottom-Up Market Sizing Model for FloraBag

To validate if your 300-unit goal is achievable without generic reports, build a **Funnel-Based Addressable Market Model** using these specific public data sources and calculations:

**1. Demand Signal (Search Volume)**
*   **Data Source:** Use Google Trends (set to France/Germany, past 12 months) and Ubersuggest (free tier) for specific long-tail keywords: "vegan leather handbag luxury," "sustainable designer bag," and competitor brand names + "alternative."
*   **Calculation:** Sum monthly search volumes for these terms in FR/DE. Apply a conservative **0.5% click-through rate** to a hypothetical landing page, then a **2% conversion rate** (standard for luxury niche e-commerce).
*   **Result:** This yields the *Digital Addressable Market*. If this number is below 1,000 units/year, your SEO/SEM strategy alone cannot hit 300 units; you must rely on offline channels.

**2. Event-Based Concentration (Pop-up Attendance)**
*   **Data Source:** Scrape event listings from *Meetup*, *Eventbrite*, and Instagram location tags for "sustainable fashion Berlin" and "mode éthique Paris." Look for specific past events (e.g., *Neonyt*, *Green Showroom*).
*   **Method:** Since exact attendance is rarely public, use the "Social Proof Proxy": Count unique geotagged posts/stories during the event dates. Assume 1 post represents 15–20 actual attendees.
*   **Calculation:** Estimate total unique attendees across 5 major annual events. Apply a **1% purchase intent filter** for luxury price points (€250+).
*   **Result:** This yields the *Event Addressable Market*.

**3. Competitor Social Saturation**
*   **Data Source:** Analyze the 12 identified competitors' Instagram/Facebook accounts.
*   **Method:** Track engagement rates on posts featuring specific bag launches (likes + comments / followers). Estimate their follower overlap using manual sampling of commenters.
*   **Calculation:** If the total engaged audience across 12 competitors is 50,000, and assuming a 0.5% annual purchase rate for luxury accessories, the *Competitor-Derived Market* is ~250 units. This suggests the market is tight; you must steal share, not find new buyers.

**Feasibility Check: 300 Units vs. €15k Budget**
*   **Revenue Target:** 300 units × €250 (avg price) = €75,000.
*   **Unit Economics:** Price (€250) - COGS (€85) = €165 Gross Margin.
*   **Break-even CAC:** With €15,000 budget, you can afford a Customer Acquisition Cost of €50 per unit (€15,000 / 300).
*   **Verdict:** A €50 CAC for a €250 luxury item is aggressive but feasible *if* your conversion rate exceeds 2%. If your digital demand signal (Step 1) is low, you will burn the budget before hitting 300 units. The market slice is likely large enough *only* if you successfully convert the specific "sustainable luxury" niche identified in Steps 2 and 3, rather than broad fashion shoppers.

**Execution Plan: Internal vs. External**
*   **Do Internally (Team of 2):** Execute the Google Trends analysis, manual social media counting, and event hashtag scraping. Build the Excel model linking these inputs to conversion assumptions. This costs €0 and provides 80% of the directional truth.
*   **Hire a Firm Only If:** You need verified attendance numbers for specific trade shows to secure retail partnerships, or if the internal data shows a "maybe" (e.g., projected sales between 200–400 units) and you need high-confidence validation before committing to the full production run. Do not hire a firm for general market sizing; their top-down data will be too broad for your niche.

*Note: Focus your €15k budget on high-intent channels (influencer gifting to micro-influencers in the sustainable niche) rather than broad ads, as your addressable market is small and specific.*
*Re-evaluate the 300-unit goal if the "Competitor Social Saturation" calculation yields fewer than 20,000 highly engaged potential customers.*
