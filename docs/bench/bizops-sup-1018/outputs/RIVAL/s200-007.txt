### 2. Criteria for Permanent Establishment in Ecuador
Under Ecuadorian domestic law (Ley de Régimen Tributario Interno), a non-resident company is generally deemed to have a Permanent Establishment if it has a **fixed place of business** in the country through which its business is wholly or partly carried out.

*   **Physical Presence:** Leasing an office, buying facility, or even a dedicated workspace in Quito.
*   **Personnel:** Having employees or dependent agents in Ecuador who have the authority to conclude contracts or habitually exercise activities core to your business (such as quality control and supply chain management).
*   **Duration:** While some jurisdictions have a time threshold (e.g., 6 months), Ecuador's domestic rules can be strict regarding any fixed place of business used for core operations.

### 3. Scope of Taxation: Global vs. Attributed Profit
If you are deemed to have a PE in Ecuador, **your entire global profit is NOT subject to Ecuadorian tax.**

International tax norms and Ecuadorian law generally follow the "attribution principle." Ecuador will only tax the profits **attributable** to the permanent establishment. This means you would be taxed only on the income generated by the activities performed by the Quito office (e.g., the margin associated with the sourcing and quality control functions), not on the profits from manufacturing in Vermont or sales in the US. You would need to maintain separate accounting or use an allocation method to determine the specific profit attributable to the Ecuadorian operations.
