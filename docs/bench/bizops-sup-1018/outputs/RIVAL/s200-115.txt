### Financial Impact Analysis: Flat Rate Increase vs. Tiered Model

**Current Baseline**
*   **Revenue:** 8,000 members × $25 = **$200,000/mo**
*   **Fixed Costs:** $12,000 (server/content) + Team salaries (excluded from variable modeling as fixed).
*   **Current Contribution Margin:** $188,000 (before team salaries).

#### Option A: Flat Fee Increase ($25 → $35)
This strategy assumes a uniform price hike. The risk here is total churn exceeding your current 5% baseline due to price sensitivity.

*   **Scenario 1: Zero Additional Churn (Ideal)**
    *   Revenue: 8,000 × $35 = **$280,000**
    *   Gain vs. Baseline: **+$80,000/mo**
*   **Scenario 2: Break-Even Revenue Point**
    *   To match current revenue ($200k) at $35/user, you need **5,715 members**.
    *   **Risk Threshold:** You can afford to lose up to **2,285 members (28.5% total churn)** before this option becomes worse than doing nothing.
*   **Impact:** This is a high-reward, high-risk play. If churn spikes even to 15% (losing 1,200 members), revenue drops to $238,000, still yielding a **+$38,000** gain over baseline.

#### Option B: Tiered Model ($15 Basic, $35 Premium, $75 Elite)
This strategy segments users. We assume the "Premium" tier matches your current offering ($35).
*   **Assumption:** 20% of users convert to Elite ($75).
*   **Variable:** Conversion to Basic ($15) vs. retention in Premium ($35).
*   **Goal:** Determine the minimum Elite conversion required to beat Option A (assuming Option A retains 90% of users, a realistic stress test).

**Comparative Model:**
Let's assume a realistic scenario for Option A where you lose 10% of price-sensitive members (7,200 retained).
*   **Option A Revenue (Stress Test):** 7,200 × $35 = **$252,000**.

Now, model Option B to beat $252,000:
*   **Elite Users:** 20% of 8,000 = 1,600 users × $75 = **$120,000**.
*   **Remaining Revenue Needed:** $252,000 - $120,000 = **$132,000**.
*   **Remaining Users:** 6,400 (split between Basic $15 and Premium $35).

If *all* remaining 6,400 stayed at Premium ($35): Revenue = $224,000. Total = $344,000. (Beats Option A easily).
If *all* remaining 6,400 dropped to Basic ($15): Revenue = $96,000. Total = $216,000. (Loses to Option A).

**The Break-Even Calculation:**
Let $x$ be the number of users in Premium ($35). The rest ($6,400 - x$) are in Basic ($15).
$$35x + 15(6,400 - x) + 120,000 > 252,000$$
$$35x + 96,000 - 15x > 132,000$$
$$20x > 36,000$$
$$x > 1,800$$

**Result:** You need at least **1,800 users (22.5% of total base)** to remain in the Premium tier.
*   **Required Mix to Beat Option A:** 20% Elite, 22.5% Premium, 57.5% Basic.
*   **Risk:** If more than 57.5% of your users downgrade to the $15 tier, the Flat Fee Hike (Option A) generates more revenue, even with moderate churn.

### Recommendation
The **Tiered Model** offers a higher ceiling ($344k+ if segmentation holds) but requires careful feature gating to prevent >57% of users from downgrading to $15. The **Flat Fee Hike** is simpler and guarantees higher revenue unless churn exceeds 28%. Given your community focus, the Tiered Model is safer for retention (keeping price-sensitive users at $15) *if* you can successfully upsell the 20% to Elite.

*Note: Ensure the $15 tier has sufficient value to retain users but clear limitations to drive upgrades. Monitor churn closely in the first 30 days of any change.*
