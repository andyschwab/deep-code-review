**Immediate Compliance Assessment & Action Plan**

**Risk Level: High.** Do not assume your existing DPA covers this. Under GDPR (applicable due to your Irish incorporation and German client), changing data transfer paths—especially if they alter the geographic route or the specific sub-processors involved—typically triggers notification obligations and potentially requires contract updates. The fact that a sophisticated entity (a German bank) has already flagged this confirms the risk is material.

**1. Does this trigger a mandatory contract update?**
Likely **yes**, or at minimum a mandatory notification. Most modern DPAs distinguish between "incidental technical changes" and changes to "sub-processors" or "transfer mechanisms."
*   If your new servers are in a different jurisdiction (e.g., moving from EU-only routing to a path touching the US without adequate safeguards), your current Standard Contractual Clauses (SCCs) may no longer match the reality of the transfer.
*   If the DPA lists specific sub-processors or data center locations, and you changed them without prior consent or a general authorization clause with notice rights, you are technically in breach until rectified.
*   **Verdict:** You cannot rely on "incidental infrastructure" arguments for a 40% traffic shift that alters cross-border paths. This is a material change in processing activity.

**2. Do you need amendments from all 300 clients?**
**No.** Executing individual amendments for 300 clients is cost-prohibitive and operationally impossible with your $20k budget.
*   **Strategy:** Leverage the "General Authorization" clause found in most standard DPAs. These usually allow the processor (you) to engage new sub-processors provided you: (a) maintain a public list of sub-processors, (b) notify customers of changes, and (c) allow them a window (e.g., 30 days) to object or terminate.
*   **Action:** If your DPA has this clause, publish an updated sub-processor list immediately and send a formal notification to all clients detailing the change, the new safeguards, and their right to object. This satisfies the requirement for the majority.
*   **Exception:** Your German bank client and other enterprise customers likely have bespoke terms requiring explicit written consent for such changes. You must negotiate a specific amendment or addendum with them individually.

**3. Is a public policy update sufficient?**
**No.** A privacy policy update is for public transparency; it does not amend a binding Data Processing Agreement. Relying solely on a website update leaves you exposed to breach of contract claims. You must follow the notification mechanism defined in the DPA itself.

**Recommended Next Steps (Budget-Conscious):**
1.  **Audit the DPA:** Immediately review the "Sub-processors" and "International Transfers" sections of your standard DPA. Look for the notification vs. consent requirement.
2.  **Draft a Notification:** Prepare a clear technical explanation of the new routing, confirming that data protection levels (e.g., SCCs, encryption) remain intact or have been enhanced.
3.  **Prioritize the Bank:** Engage directly with the German bank's DPO. Offer a specific addendum confirming the new routes and safeguards. Their approval is your biggest hurdle; once they sign off, you have a precedent for others.
4.  **Execute Notification:** Send the formal notice to the remaining 299 clients per your DPA terms.
