**The Verdict**
Switching to bi-weekly delivery results in a **net decrease in monthly profit of $2,691** ($76,910 current profit vs. $74,219 projected profit). While you save $9,000 in logistics, you lose $11,691 in revenue. Since you are currently profitable, you do not have a "runway" issue to solve; this change would slightly reduce your monthly cash flow generation.

**Compliance and Labor Law Considerations (Chicago/Illinois)**
Changing driver schedules from five days to three days a week triggers several potential legal issues in Illinois:
1.  **Predictive Scheduling:** Chicago's Fair Workweek Ordinance applies to certain industries (mainly retail, food service, and healthcare). While juice delivery might not strictly fall under "food service" (which usually implies prepared food for immediate consumption), if your drivers are classified as retail workers or if the ordinance is interpreted broadly, you may be required to provide 14 days' advance notice of schedule changes. Failure to do so can result in "predictability pay" penalties.
2.  **Hourly vs. Salary:** If drivers are hourly, reducing hours is generally permissible but must not violate minimum wage laws for hours worked. If they are salaried exempt employees, reducing their workweek while maintaining salary could jeopardize their exempt status under the FLSA if their duties no longer align with full-time expectations.
3.  **Constructive Discharge:** Reducing hours by 40% could be viewed as a significant change in employment terms. In Illinois, this could potentially allow affected employees to claim unemployment benefits if they quit due to the reduction, arguing "good cause" attributable to the employer.

**Legal Review Recommendation**
**Do not implement this change without an employment lawyer's review.** Your operations manager is not qualified to assess the risks regarding the Fair Workweek Ordinance, potential constructive discharge claims, or the reclassification of employee status. A lawyer can draft the necessary notices and ensure the schedule reduction does not trigger unintended liabilities or penalties that would outweigh your $9,000 logistics savings.
