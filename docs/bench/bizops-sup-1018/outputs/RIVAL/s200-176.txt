1.  **Individual Donations ($180,000):** These are generally considered **tax-exempt contributions**. They are not taxable income to your organization. Donors may deduct these contributions on their personal tax returns if they itemize deductions, provided you issue them proper acknowledgment receipts.
2.  **Corporate Sponsorships ($50,000):** The tax treatment depends on what the sponsor receives in return.
    *   **Qualified Sponsorship Payments:** If the corporation receives only acknowledgment (e.g., logo on your website, mention in a newsletter) without substantial return benefits (like advertising, exclusive rights, or goods/services), the payment is a **tax-exempt contribution**.
    *   **Unrelated Business Income (UBI):** If you provide substantial benefits (e.g., commercial advertising, booth space at an event, exclusive marketing rights), that portion of the payment may be classified as Unrelated Business Taxable Income (UBTI) and could be subject to tax.

It remains tax-exempt for a 501(c)(3) organization. It does not change your classification, provided it remains a small portion of your total support and is not derived from debt-financed investments.

### Commercial Activity Restrictions

Accepting corporate sponsorships does not automatically jeopardize your status, but you must avoid **private inurement** (benefiting insiders) and ensure that commercial activities do not become your **primary purpose**. If unrelated business activities become substantial compared to your exempt activities (grant distribution), the IRS could revoke your exempt status. Keeping detailed records distinguishing between "gifts" and "payments for services/advertising" is critical.
