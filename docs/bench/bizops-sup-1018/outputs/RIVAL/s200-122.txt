### Financial Runway Analysis: Direct Sales vs. Leasing Model

**Current State (Direct Sales)**
*   **Monthly Revenue:** 150 units × $2,800 = $420,000
*   **Monthly COGS:** 150 units × $950 = $142,500
*   **Gross Margin:** $277,500
*   **Net Monthly Cash Flow:** $277,500 (Margin) - $185,000 (Burn) = **+$92,500**
*   **Runway:** You are currently cash-flow positive. With $1.2M cash, you are not burning capital; you are accumulating it. You do not need to delay Series A for cash flow reasons; you likely don't need Series A for survival at all at this volume.

**Proposed State (Leasing)**
*   **Upfront Cash Inflow:** 150 units × $500 = $75,000
*   **Recurring Revenue:** 150 units × $120 = $18,000 (Month 1)
*   **Total Month 1 Revenue:** $93,000
*   **Monthly COGS:** $142,500 (You still pay this upfront to build/buy units)
*   **Net Monthly Cash Flow (Month 1):** $93,000 - $142,500 (COGS) - $185,000 (OpEx) = **-$234,500**
*   **Result:** You switch from generating +$92.5k/month to burning -$234.5k/month immediately.

**Break-Even Analysis**
There is **no break-even point** where leasing improves your *immediate* runway compared to direct sales under these specific numbers.
*   **Direct Sales** generates cash immediately to fund operations.
*   **Leasing** requires you to fund the entire $950 manufacturing cost per unit upfront while collecting only $500 initially. You lose $450 in cash per unit on day one, plus you must cover operating expenses.
*   To recover the $450 cash deficit per unit via the $120/month margin (assuming negligible variable costs), it takes ~4 months per unit *before* accounting for churn.
*   With a 4% monthly churn, your cumulative recurring revenue will struggle to ever cover the initial cash outlay of the hardware within a reasonable timeframe. You would run out of your $1.2M cash reserve in approximately **5 months** under the leasing model, whereas the current model extends your cash indefinitely.

**Critical Assumptions Requiring CFO/Advisor Review**
An AI cannot validate the following strategic risks; they require human financial expertise before pitching the board:

1.  **Cost of Capital & Working Capital Facility:** The leasing model assumes you can afford to front $142,500/month in hardware costs with delayed returns. A CFO must determine if you need a debt facility to fund this working capital gap, which introduces interest costs and covenants not modeled here.
2.  **Churn Sensitivity & LTV:CAC:** The 4% churn estimate is critical. At 4% monthly churn, the average customer lifetime is only 25 months. If churn increases to 5-6%, the Lifetime Value (LTV) drops below the Cost of Goods Sold, making every new lease a loss-making event.
3.  **Valuation Impact:** Shifting from high-margin upfront revenue to low-margin recurring revenue may lower your valuation multiple in a Series A round, even if it stabilizes long-term revenue. Investors may view the increased cash burn and debt requirement negatively.

**Conclusion:** Do not switch to leasing if the goal is to extend immediate cash runway. This model accelerates your cash burn by over $300,000 per month compared to your current state.

*Note: This analysis assumes no additional financing or debt is available to bridge the hardware cost gap. If you secure a dedicated equipment financing line, the math changes, but the operational complexity increases.*
