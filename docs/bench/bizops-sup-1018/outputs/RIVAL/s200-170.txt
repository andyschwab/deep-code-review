### Break-Even Analysis: Per-Seat vs. Fixed-Fee Pricing

To determine when the fixed-fee model becomes less profitable than your current per-seat model, we must compare the revenue generated under both structures against your variable costs.

**Current Model (Per-Seat):**
*   **Price:** €150 per employee
*   **Variable Cost:** €40 per employee
*   **Contribution Margin:** €110 per employee (€150 - €40)

**Proposed Model (Fixed-Fee):**
*   **Price:** €45,000 per client (flat)
*   **Variable Cost:** €40 per employee (scales with usage)
*   **Contribution Margin:** €45,000 - (€40 × Number of Employees)

**The Break-Even Calculation**
The fixed-fee model yields lower margins than the per-seat model once the cost to serve exceeds the difference in revenue structures. We solve for the number of employees ($N$) where the profit from both models is equal:

$$110 \times N = 45,000 - (40 \times N)$$
$$150 \times N = 45,000$$
$$N = 300$$

**The Verdict:**
The break-even point is **300 employees**.
*   **Below 300 employees:** The **Fixed-Fee (€45k)** model is more profitable. You capture more value than the per-seat equivalent.
*   **Above 300 employees:** The **Per-Seat (€150)** model is more profitable. The fixed fee fails to cover the marginal costs of additional users, causing margins to collapse.

**Risk Assessment Based on Your Data**
Your historical data shows an average of **250 seats** per client. At this volume:
*   **Per-Seat Revenue:** €37,500 (250 × €150)
*   **Fixed-Fee Revenue:** €45,000
*   **Gain:** Switching to fixed fees would have increased revenue by **€7,500 per client** (a 20% uplift) while maintaining healthy margins.

However, the risk is asymmetric. If a single client grows to **1,000 employees**:
*   **Per-Seat Revenue:** €150,000
*   **Fixed-Fee Revenue:** €45,000
*   **Loss:** You would forfeit **€105,000** in potential revenue on that single account, while your delivery costs quadruple to €40,000, leaving a thin €5,000 margin.

**Strategic Recommendation**
Given your 85% retention rate and the volatility of enterprise headcount, a pure fixed-fee model exposes you to significant downside risk if clients scale rapidly. The predictability of fixed fees only outweighs the risk if you can confidently cap the scope or if client growth remains stagnant below 300 seats.

A hybrid approach is safer: Offer the **€45,000 annual fixed fee** as a "Standard Plan" capped at **300 seats**. Any usage beyond 300 employees is billed at a discounted overage rate (e.g., €100/seat). This secures the upsell on your typical 250-seat client while protecting your margins against enterprise-scale expansion.

*Note: This analysis assumes variable costs remain constant at €40/seat; economies of scale in delivery could slightly raise the break-even threshold.*
*Consider implementing a "true-up" clause in annual contracts to reconcile headcount changes mid-term.*
