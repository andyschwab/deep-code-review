### Depreciation Strategy Comparison for Haven Hospice

**Context:** You have $300,000 in new medical equipment. Medical equipment generally falls under a 7-year recovery period (MACRS), but current tax laws (Section 179 and Bonus Depreciation) often allow for immediate or accelerated expensing.

#### Option A: Accelerated Depreciation (Front-Loaded)
*Method:* Utilizing Section 179 or Bonus Depreciation to deduct a large portion (up to 100% in many recent tax years) of the $300,000 cost in Year 1.

*   **Year 1 Impact:** You deduct ~$300,000 immediately. This creates a massive paper loss or significantly reduces taxable income. Given your $45k/month revenue ($540k/year), this deduction could shield a substantial portion of your operating profit from taxes this year.
*   **Cash Flow:** Maximizes immediate cash flow by minimizing the Year 1 tax bill. This frees up capital to service the $8,000 loan payment and fund further operations.
*   **Years 2–5 Impact:** Your depreciation deductions drop to near zero (or very small amounts). Consequently, your taxable income will be higher in these years because you have no large depreciation shield left to use.
*   **Future Risk:** When expansion slows, if your revenue remains steady but deductions vanish, your tax bill will spike. You are essentially "borrowing" deductions from the future to use today.

#### Option B: Straight-Line Depreciation (Spread Evenly)
*Method:* Ignoring immediate expensing options (if eligible) or electing out of bonus depreciation to spread the $300,000 cost evenly over the 7-year life of the equipment (~$42,850/year).

*   **Year 1 Impact:** You only deduct ~$43,000. Your taxable income remains higher now, resulting in a larger tax bill this year compared to Option A.
*   **Cash Flow:** Reduces immediate cash flow due to higher current taxes.
*   **Years 2–5 Impact:** You continue deducting ~$43,000 annually. This provides a consistent tax shield, smoothing out your taxable income and tax liability over time.
*   **Future Benefit:** When expansion slows, you still have these deductions available to offset revenue, preventing a sharp spike in your tax rate later.

#### Financial Implications & Recommendation

**Does deducting more now hurt you later?**
Yes, in terms of *timing*. Depreciation is a fixed total amount ($300,000). If you take it all now, you have none left for later. This does not change the *total* tax paid over the life of the asset, but it changes *when* you pay it.

**The Core Decision:**
1.  **Choose Accelerated If:** You need cash flow *now* to manage the $8,000 monthly loan payment and believe your business will grow significantly in Years 2–5. Higher future revenue will absorb the higher future tax bills easily. This bets on your continued success.
2.  **Choose Straight-Line If:** You expect revenue to plateau or drop after this expansion phase. Spreading the deduction protects you from a high tax bill when income is lower. It acts as an insurance policy against future slowdowns.

**Specific to Medical Rentals:**
Since your equipment is rented, ensure the "predominant use" tests are met for Section 179. If the equipment sits idle, accelerated methods might be restricted.

**Next Steps for Your Accountant:**
Ask your accountant to model two scenarios using your specific projected net income for Years 1–5:
1.  Full Section 179/Bonus expensing in Year 1.
2.  Standard MACRS 7-year schedule.
Compare the Net Present Value (NPV) of the tax savings. Usually, taking the deduction early (Accelerated) is mathematically superior due to the time value of money, unless you anticipate a drastic drop in profitability soon.

*Note: Tax laws regarding bonus depreciation percentages change annually; verify the current percentage for this fiscal year with your CPA.*
*This analysis assumes the equipment qualifies as tangible personal property under IRS guidelines.*
