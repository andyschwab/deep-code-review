### Strategic Recommendation: Upgrade Packaging (Option B)

For a luxury brand like LuxGlow, **lowering the price is the wrong strategic move.** In the luxury home goods sector, price acts as a primary signal of quality. Dropping your price from $32 to $24 confirms the customer's suspicion that the product is "cheap" or low-quality, likely increasing return rates rather than solving the "subtle scent" perception.

The superior approach is **keeping the $32 price point and upgrading the packaging.** Heavier, premium packaging creates a "halo effect," psychologically priming the customer to perceive the scent as richer and more valuable before they even light the candle. This aligns the physical experience with the luxury price anchor.

#### Financial Impact Analysis (Monthly @ 1,500 units)

**Current Baseline:**
*   Revenue: $48,000 ($32 × 1,500)
*   Variable Costs: $22,500 (($9 COGS + $6 Shipping) × 1,500)
*   **Contribution Margin:** $25,500 ($17/unit)
*   *Note: High returns (22%) are eroding this further via reverse logistics and lost inventory, which is not fully captured in simple unit economics.*

**Scenario A: Lower Price to $24**
*   Revenue: $36,000 ($24 × 1,500)
*   Variable Costs: $22,500 (Unchanged)
*   **Contribution Margin:** $13,500 ($9/unit)
*   **Result:** You sacrifice **$12,000/month** in margin immediately. Unless returns drop to near zero (unlikely, as the product hasn't changed), you destroy profitability. You are training customers to expect a bargain, not luxury.

**Scenario B: Keep $32, Add $4 Packaging Cost**
*   Revenue: $48,000 ($32 × 1,500)
*   Variable Costs: $28,500 (($9 COGS + $4 Upgrade + $6 Shipping) × 1,500)
*   **Contribution Margin:** $19,500 ($13/unit)
*   **Result:** You sacrifice **$6,000/month** in margin initially. However, if the premium packaging reduces returns by even 10–15% by managing expectations and enhancing perceived value, you recoup this cost. Furthermore, you preserve the brand's luxury positioning, allowing for future price increases.

#### Psychological Principles & Shipping Concerns

1.  **Price-Quality Heuristic:** In luxury goods, consumers use price as a shortcut for quality. A lower price signals lower quality, validating their complaint that the scent is "weak."
2.  **Sensory Transference:** Heavy, textured packaging subconsciously transfers feelings of weight and substance to the scent itself. A light box suggests a faint scent; a substantial box suggests a potent one.
3.  **Shipping Tiers:** Increasing packaging weight/size *will* affect shipping tiers. You must weigh a prototype of the new package immediately. If the upgrade pushes you into the next weight bracket (e.g., from 1 lb to 2 lbs), your $6 shipping cost could jump significantly, destroying the margin math above.

#### A/B Testing & Legal Disclosures

You **can** rotate packaging for half your inventory without specific consumer protection disclosures or legal review, provided:
*   The core product (the candle) remains safe and matches its description (scent notes, burn time).
*   You are not making false claims about the packaging material itself.
*   You are simply testing two versions of fulfillment for the same SKU.

No special disclaimer is needed for operational A/B tests where the customer receives the product they ordered, just in different packaging. However, ensure your customer service team knows both versions exist to avoid confusion if customers compare notes online.

**Final Verdict:** Choose Scenario B, but validate the new shipping costs immediately. Do not devalue your brand to solve a perception problem.
