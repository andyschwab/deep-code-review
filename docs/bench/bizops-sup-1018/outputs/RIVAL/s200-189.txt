### Regulatory Landscape Analysis

**1. Does Your Model Classify as a Franchise?**
Yes. Under both Federal Trade Commission (FTC) rules and the specific statutes of California and Illinois, your operational structure almost certainly classifies as a franchise. A business relationship is typically defined as a franchise if it meets three criteria, all of which you satisfy:
*   **Trademark License:** You grant the right to operate under the "ChefAcademy" name.

Because you meet this definition, you are subject to franchise registration laws, not general business licensing.

**2. The Securities Law Misconception**
You asked if franchising triggers "securities laws." While franchise investments are sometimes analyzed under securities principles, they are primarily regulated under specific **Franchise Investment Laws**.
*   **California:** Regulated by the Department of Financial Protection and Innovation (DFPI) under the California Franchise Investment Law.
*   **Illinois:** Regulated by the Attorney General's office under the Illinois Franchise Disclosure Act.
*   **Federal Level:** Regulated by the FTC Rule.

In these states, the sale of a franchise is treated as the sale of an investment contract. Therefore, you cannot simply sign a contract; you must register the offering or qualify for a very narrow exemption before any sale or even substantive discussion about specific terms.

**3. Mandatory Registration Steps**
To legally sell franchises in California and Illinois, you must generally follow this process:
*   **Create a Franchise Disclosure Document (FDD):** This is a rigorous, 23-item legal document detailing your litigation history, bankruptcy status, fees, obligations, and financial performance representations.
*   **State Registration:** You must file the FDD with the California DFPI and the Illinois Attorney General.
*   **Review Period:** Both states employ examiners who will review your FDD. They will issue comment letters requiring changes. This process often takes 4–8 months per state.
*   **Effective Date:** You cannot sell a single unit in these states until the registration is declared "effective" by the state regulator.

**4. Exemptions and Thresholds**
Exemptions in California and Illinois are extremely limited and rarely apply to standard franchising models like yours:
*   **Sophisticated Investor Exemptions:** Often require the franchisee to have a net worth exceeding $1–2 million and significant business experience, plus a waiver signed by legal counsel. This is difficult to apply broadly.
*   **Small Number of Sales:** Some states allow exemptions for selling fewer than a specific number of units (e.g., 1-3) in a 12-month period, but California's rules are particularly strict regarding even single sales.
*   **No "Test Market" Loophole:** You cannot test the market in these states without registration.
