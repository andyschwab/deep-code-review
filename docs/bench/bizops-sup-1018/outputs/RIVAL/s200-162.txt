**Risk Assessment: TuneMatch Employment Classification Exposure**

Based on the operational model you described, TuneMatch currently exhibits **high risk** for employee reclassification under emerging Canadian labor guidelines, particularly in Ontario and the upcoming expansion into Quebec. While your 18% commission model and lack of fixed hours are positive indicators of independent contractor status, your involvement in **dispute resolution** and **quality standards** creates significant liability if those mechanisms involve controlling *how* the work is performed rather than just verifying the outcome.

**The Critical Line: Control vs. Facilitation**
The distinction between a platform facilitator and an employer hinges on "control."
*   **Facilitator (Low Risk):** You set broad community guidelines (e.g., "no harassment"), process payments, and offer a neutral venue for disputes without dictating the lesson content, teaching methodology, or specific scheduling terms between parties.
*   **Employer (High Risk):** You mandate specific teaching curricula, require instructors to accept lessons at times you dictate, enforce rigid cancellation policies that you profit from, or intervene in disputes by making unilateral decisions on refunds that effectively penalize the instructor's income without their consent.

If your "quality standards" require instructors to follow a specific TuneMatch teaching script or if your "dispute resolution" allows you to unilaterally withhold pay based on subjective performance metrics, regulators will likely view this as an employment relationship. The fact that you processed $180,000 in payments last year provides a concrete base for back-tax calculations should reclassification occur.

**Jurisdictional Specifics**
*   **Ontario:** Recent legal precedents and guidelines (such as those affecting gig-economy platforms) increasingly scrutinize platforms that exert algorithmic control or impose penalties that mimic disciplinary action. If your dispute resolution acts as a performance review mechanism, you are vulnerable.
*   **Quebec:** Quebec possesses distinct and often stricter labor laws (Civil Code vs. Common Law). The province is highly protective of worker status. Expanding next month without auditing your Terms of Service for Quebec-specific compliance is dangerous. Quebec regulators may view standard platform quality controls as evidence of subordination more readily than Ontario counterparts.

**Immediate Risk Exposure**
Your current exposure is not just the $180,000 in back payroll taxes (CPP, EI, and income tax remittances) but also potential fines for failing to provide statutory benefits (vacation pay, public holidays) and the administrative burden of retroactive compliance. The risk is compounded by the fact that you have 140 instructors; a single successful complaint could trigger a class-action review or a government audit of the entire cohort.

**Strategic Recommendations to Mitigate Risk**
1.  **Decouple Quality from Control:** Reframe quality standards as "community expectations" rather than mandatory operational procedures. Allow instructors to define their own teaching methods and curricula.
2.  **Neutral Dispute Resolution:** Restructure dispute resolution to be purely mediation-based. Avoid unilateral decisions where TuneMatch acts as judge and jury on service quality. Let the student and instructor negotiate refunds, with TuneMatch only facilitating the transfer once mutual agreement is reached.
3.  **Pricing Autonomy:** Ensure instructors set their own rates. If you suggest prices, they must be clearly non-binding recommendations. Your current average of $55 suggests market alignment, but if you enforce this as a cap or floor, it increases employment risk.
4.  **Quebec Pause:** Delay the Quebec launch until a local labor lawyer reviews your specific dispute resolution logs and instructor agreements. Quebec's language and labor laws require specific contractual wording that differs from Ontario.

**Conclusion**
Your current level of involvement in dispute resolution and quality standards likely crosses the line into employment territory if those processes dictate *how* instructors work or penalize them financially for performance issues. To survive expansion, you must pivot from "managing" instructors to "servicing" a marketplace of autonomous businesses.
