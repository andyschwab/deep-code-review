**1. Income Tax Nexus (The Immediate Problem)**
Having employees physically present in a state is the strongest trigger for income tax nexus.
*   **The Rule:** If your agents live and work from these five states, SalesFlow is likely considered "doing business" there.
*   **The Consequence:** You are likely required to register as a foreign entity in each of these states, apportion your income, and file state corporate income tax returns. Furthermore, you likely have **payroll tax obligations** (withholding state income tax, unemployment insurance) for those specific employees. Failure to do so can result in back taxes, penalties, and interest.
*   **State Specifics:**

    *   **TX:** Has a franchise tax (margin tax) triggered by nexus.
    *   **FL:** No state corporate income tax, but you may still have registration and payroll obligations.

**2. Sales Tax Nexus (The Complex Layer)**
Whether you need to collect *sales tax* on your SaaS subscriptions depends on two factors:
*   **SaaS Taxability:** Not all states tax SaaS.
    *   **Taxable:** California (sometimes, depending on specific delivery), Texas (taxable as data processing), Illinois (taxable as personal property/license).
    *   **Not Taxable (Generally):** New York (SaaS is generally exempt unless bundled with taxable services), Florida (generally exempt). *Note: These laws change frequently.*
*   **Economic Nexus Thresholds:** Even if SaaS is taxable, you must meet revenue/transaction thresholds to be required to collect.
