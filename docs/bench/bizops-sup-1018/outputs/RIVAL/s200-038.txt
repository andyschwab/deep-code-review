For your Series A financial model, you should calculate your warranty liability using a **blended approach that leans heavily on your specific historical data**, while explicitly stress-testing against the industry average in a sensitivity analysis.

Investors in hardware startups prioritize **credibility and unit economics** over generic conservatism. Using the 5% industry average immediately implies your product quality is inferior to the market norm, despite your "HeavyDuty" positioning and actual performance data. Conversely, ignoring the industry benchmark entirely may signal naivety regarding long-term scaling risks.

**Recommended Methodology for the Investor Deck:**

1.  **Base Case (The Credible Narrative):** Use your **historical 2% failure rate**.
    *   *Calculation:* 10,000 units × 2% × $100 = **$20,000 reserve**.
    *   *Rationale:* This demonstrates product-market fit and superior engineering. It validates your $450 price point against a $200 cost of goods sold (COGS) by showing healthy margins aren't being eroded by defects. Investors want to see that you have solved the reliability problem that plagues competitors.

2.  **Risk Case (The Conservative Stress Test):** Model a scenario where failure rates creep toward **3–4%** as you scale production.
    *   Do not jump straight to 5% unless you have evidence your quality control will degrade.
    *   Present this in a sidebar or footnote: "Even if failure rates double to 4%, our reserve requirement increases by only $20,000, which is negligible relative to our gross margin."

3.  **Addressing Sample Size Significance:**
    *   Your sample of 10,000 units is **statistically significant** for early-stage modeling. With 200 observed failures (2% of 10,000), the margin of error is relatively low. It is not "small data."
    *   The argument that "industry averages are safer" is weak because industry data often includes low-cost, disposable gear, whereas your product is premium and designed for extreme weather. Comparing your specialized tent to generic camping gear is an apples-to-oranges comparison that sophisticated investors will spot immediately.

**Guiding Accounting Principles:**

*   **ASC 460 (Guarantees) / IAS 37 (Provisions):** These standards require you to record a liability when it is **probable** a loss will occur and the amount can be **reasonably estimated**.
*   **Best Estimate vs. Prudence:** Accounting standards dictate using the "best estimate" of the expenditure required. Since your own data is the most predictive indicator of your specific product's future performance, it is the compliant "best estimate."
*   **Matching Principle:** You must match the warranty expense to the revenue generated in the same period. Using your actual historical rate achieves this most accurately.

**Strategic Advice for the Deck:**
Frame the 2% rate as a **competitive moat**. Explicitly state: "While industry failure rates hover at 5%, our rigorous testing and Colorado-based extreme weather validation have driven failures down to 2%. We model reserves at 2.5% to provide a buffer for scaling, still significantly outperforming the market."

This approach shows you are data-driven, understand your unit economics, and are conservative without admitting to hypothetical flaws. It turns a liability calculation into a proof point for product quality.

*Note: Always have your CPA review the final percentage selection to ensure compliance with your specific jurisdiction's GAAP/IFRS interpretations.*
