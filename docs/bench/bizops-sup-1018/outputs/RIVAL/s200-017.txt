### Resale Certificate Verification Protocol & Liability Overview

**1. Verification Frequency: One-Time vs. Per-Transaction**
Generally, you are **not** legally required to verify a resale certificate for every single transaction if you have a valid, blanket resale certificate on file for that specific customer. Once a designer provides a valid certificate (often a multi-state form like the MTC Uniform Sales & Use Tax Certificate or a specific state form), you can apply it to future purchases until:
*   The certificate expires (some states have expiration dates).
*   You have reason to believe the customer is no longer eligible (e.g., their business license is revoked).
*   A significant period has passed (best practice suggests reviewing every 3–5 years).

However, you **must** verify the validity of the document *at the time of collection*. Accepting an obviously fake or incomplete form does not protect you.

**2. Liability for Invalid Certificates**
Yes, if you accept an invalid certificate (or fail to collect one) and do not charge sales tax, **you are personally liable** for the uncollected tax, plus interest and penalties.
*   The state views the seller as the trustee of the tax. If the buyer fails to pay use tax later, the state will come after you for the missing revenue.
*   Penalties can range from 10% to 25% of the unpaid tax, plus statutory interest.
*   "Good faith" acceptance of a fraudulent document may offer some protection in certain jurisdictions, but only if you performed a reasonable check of the document's completeness and the buyer's identity.

**3. Cross-State Transactions (NY Warehouse vs. Out-of-State Designers)**
The requirement depends on **nexus**, not just warehouse location.
*   **Ship-to State Rules:** Generally, the sales tax rules of the *destination state* (where the goods are shipped) apply if you have nexus there.
*   **Nexus Triggers:** Since you are shipping into 5 different states, you likely have nexus in those states due to economic thresholds (often $100k in sales or 200 transactions, though some states have lower thresholds for marketplace facilitators or specific goods).
*   **Verification Standard:** You must hold a resale certificate valid for the *state where the tax would have been due*. A New York resale certificate usually does not exempt sales shipped to California unless specific reciprocity rules apply (which are rare). You typically need a certificate that covers the destination state's tax obligations.
