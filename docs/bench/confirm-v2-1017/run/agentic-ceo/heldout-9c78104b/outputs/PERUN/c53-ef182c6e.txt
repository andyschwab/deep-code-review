**Decision:** Proceed with a **Human-Gated Pilot** workflow; do not connect to the automatic alert system until false positive rates are proven below 0.1% in live trials. The irreversible integration is halted until this threshold is met.

*   **Timeline:** Today to June 15 (Dry Season Start).
*   **Data Gap:** 20 years of fragmented history vs. 3,000 sensors requiring clean training data.
*   **Latency Target:** <10 minutes (current: 120 minutes).

**1. Precedent Data Cleaning Task**
**Priority:** Temporal alignment and gap-filling of the 20-year historical fire records against known weather events.
Before ingesting the 3,000 sensor streams, you must reconstruct a "ground truth" dataset. The fragmented fire history is the limiting factor; without verified past fire locations and times, the model cannot learn the correlation between sensor readings and actual fire spread.
*   **Action:** Cross-reference the fragmented fire logs with independent satellite heat signatures and local news archives from the same dates to validate timestamps. Discard any historical fire records that cannot be corroborated by a second source. Train the model only on this verified subset first.

**2. Ethical Boundaries for Automated Evacuation**
Define these three non-negotiable rules before writing integration code:
*   **The "Human-in-the-Loop" Gate:** No evacuation order affecting populated zones (hospitals, schools, residential) may be executed solely by the model. The system must output a "Recommendation Level" (1–5); only Level 5 (certainty >99.9%) triggers an automatic alert, and even then, it must require a dual-signature from two field operatives or a command center human within a 2-minute window.
*   **Fail-Safe Direction:** In the event of sensor data corruption or model uncertainty, the system must default to "Investigate" (alerting field operatives) rather than "Evacuate." False negatives (missing a fire) are tragic; false positives (mass panic) are systemically fatal to public trust.
*   **Explainability Requirement:** Every alert generated must include the top three contributing sensor anomalies. If the model cannot articulate *why* it predicts a spread (e.g., "Wind speed + Humidity drop + Thermal spike"), the alert is suppressed.

**3. First Concrete Experiment: The "Shadow Mode" Integrity Test**
Do not train the prediction model yet. Run a data integrity stress test on the live sensor feed.
*   **Setup:** Connect the 3,000 sensors to a isolated logging server (no model attached).
*   **Procedure:** Inject known synthetic anomalies (simulated fire signatures) into the stream alongside real-time data.
*   **Verification:** Measure the percentage of sensors that report data within the 10-minute window consistently over a 48-hour period.
*   **Success Criteria:** 95% of sensors must deliver complete, timestamped packets within the latency window. If the infrastructure cannot reliably move the data in <10 minutes now, the prediction model will never meet its SLA. Fix the pipeline before training the AI.

**Halt Trigger:** If the Shadow Mode test reveals >5% data loss or latency >10 minutes after 48 hours, stop all model development and focus exclusively on infrastructure repair.

**Fallback Trigger:** If verified historical data covers less than 30% of the required training volume by May 1st, revert to a rule-based heuristic system (simple threshold alerts) for the dry season rather than deploying an untrained neural network.
