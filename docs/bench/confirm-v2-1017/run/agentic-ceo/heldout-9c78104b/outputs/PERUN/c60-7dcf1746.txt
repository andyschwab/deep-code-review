**Decision:** Execute a staged "Canary-to-Wave" deployment over 12 days, prioritizing a 20-bike pilot in a low-traffic zone before scaling to 200, then 1,800 units, with a strict "stop-on-error" threshold of 2% failure rate.

**Figures:** 2,000 bikes total; 12-day window; 5 engineers available. Pilot = 20 bikes (1%); Wave 1 = 200 bikes (10%); Wave 2 = 1,780 bikes (89%). If 2% of 200 fail in Wave 1, that is 4 bricked bikes requiring retrieval.

**Workflow Design:**
1.  **Days 1–2 (Pilot):** Remotely update 20 bikes in a controlled, low-density sector. Monitor connectivity and lock engagement every 15 minutes. No user-facing changes yet.
2.  **Days 3–5 (Wave 1):** If pilot success is 100%, update 200 bikes across varied terrain. Activate physical repair crews for immediate retrieval of any failures.
3.  **Days 6–10 (Mass Deployment):** Roll out to the remaining 1,780 bikes in batches of 400 per day.
4.  **Days 11–12 (Verification & Buffer):** Final system-wide integrity check and buffer time for unexpected repairs before the Day 22 launch.

**Engineer Oversight:** Assign your **most senior embedded engineer** to own the Failure Recovery Plan. This individual must define the exact telemetry signatures of a "brick" versus a "temporary glitch" and authorize the halt trigger. The other four engineers will manage the deployment waves and real-time monitoring.

**Resource Allocation Decisions:**
You must immediately authorize **two dedicated physical repair crews** (2 vans, 4 technicians) to stand by from Day 3 through Day 10. Do not wait for failures to hire them; their availability determines your maximum acceptable risk. If a bike bricks, it must be retrieved within 4 hours to minimize downtime. Calculate the cost: if 2% of 2,000 bikes brick (40 units), and each retrieval takes 4 hours, you need 160 labor hours. Two crews working 10-hour days for 8 days provide 160 hours, matching the worst-case scenario.

**First Immediate Action:**
**Freeze all remote update capabilities** until the "Kill Switch" protocol is verified. Before any pilot begins, confirm that you can instantly halt the deployment globally if the first 5 bikes show anomalous behavior. This secures the fleet against a cascading failure that could brick hundreds of units before human intervention is possible.

**Halt Trigger:** If >2 bikes fail in the 20-bike pilot, or >4 bikes fail in the first 200-bike wave. Action: Stop deployment, revert firmware on affected units, and initiate root-cause analysis.
**Fallback Trigger:** If >10 bikes are bricked by Day 8. Action: Pause expansion timeline, notify city council of potential delay, and switch to manual, bike-by-bike updates for the remaining fleet.

Note: This plan assumes the new firmware has passed basic lab testing; if not, add 2 days for lab validation before the pilot.
