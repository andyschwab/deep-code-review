**Bike-Share Firmware Migration Workflow**

**Phase 1: Immediate Fleet Security (Days 1–2)**
*   **Action:** Implement a "Soft Lock" via the existing network protocol. This disables new ride initiations in the upcoming 50-square-mile expansion zone without affecting current operations in the existing zone. This prevents users from renting bikes in the new area before the firmware is ready, eliminating the risk of stranded assets.
*   **Owner:** Lead Embedded Engineer.

**Phase 2: Pilot Update & Failure Analysis (Days 3–5)**
*   **Selection:** Select a diverse pilot group of 50 bikes (varying battery levels, hardware revisions, and signal strengths).
*   **Execution:** Perform remote flashing on the pilot group during low-traffic windows (2:00 AM – 4:00 AM).
*   **Verification:** Confirm successful boot, zone recognition, and locking mechanism function.
*   **Contingency:** If >2% failure rate occurs, halt mass deployment. Analyze bricked units to refine the flashing packet or timeout thresholds.

**Phase 3: Staged Mass Deployment (Days 6–11)**
*   **Strategy:** Deploy in batches of 300 bikes per night, grouped by geographic clustering to optimize potential physical retrieval.
*   **Process:**
    1.  Push update to Batch N.
    2.  Wait for heartbeat confirmation (success/fail).
    3.  Only proceed to Batch N+1 if Batch N success rate >98%.
*   **Engineer Allocation:** Assign two engineers to monitor real-time telemetry and abort sequences. Assign two engineers to prepare recovery tools. The fifth engineer acts as the float/lead.

**Phase 4: Final Verification & Launch Readiness (Day 12)**
*   **Action:** Run a diagnostic sweep on all 2,000 units. Generate a "Green List" of fully compatible bikes.
*   **Launch:** Lift the "Soft Lock" on the new zones immediately upon council approval.

**Failure Recovery Plan Oversight**
*   **Assigned Engineer:** The most senior embedded engineer with prior hardware debugging experience must oversee the failure recovery plan. This individual is responsible for defining the "brick" criteria, managing the physical retrieval logistics, and executing the manual JTAG/UART re-flashing process for failed units.
*   **Rationale:** Remote flashing is irreversible; the recovery lead must understand the low-level hardware constraints to minimize downtime per unit.

**Resource Allocation Decisions for Repair Crews**
*   **Decision Required:** You must pre-position two mobile repair crews in the geographic center of the deployment clusters before Phase 3 begins.
*   **Capacity Planning:** Assume a conservative 5% failure rate (100 bikes). With a repair time of 4 hours per bike (travel + fix), one crew can handle ~12 bikes per week. Two crews can handle ~24.
*   **Critical Gap:** The math indicates a severe bottleneck. You must decide to either:
    1.  Contract third-party repair technicians immediately to scale capacity to 5 crews.
    2.  Accept that 60–70 bikes will remain offline at launch and plan a "Phase 5" catch-up repair schedule post-launch.
    *Recommendation:* Secure third-party support now to aim for 100% readiness.

**First Immediate Action**
Execute the **Soft Lock** on the 50 new square miles immediately. This secures the fleet by preventing user interaction in the unready zone, buying the team the full 12-day window to execute the migration without the pressure of active customer complaints or lost bikes in the expansion area.

*Note: This workflow assumes the existing network infrastructure can handle the batched update traffic without congestion.*
*Ensure all engineers have access to the specific hardware revision schematics before Day 3.*
