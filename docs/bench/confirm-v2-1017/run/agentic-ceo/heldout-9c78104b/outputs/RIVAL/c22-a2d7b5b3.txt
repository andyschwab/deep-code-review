### QuickMart POS Migration Workflow

**1. Immediate Critical Task**
Deploy a "Canary Team" of two technicians to one representative store immediately to execute the full hardware swap and firmware update cycle. Do not proceed with the wider rollout until this single unit is verified as transaction-ready and stable for 30 minutes. This validates the firmware image and the physical logistics chain before risking the bricking of hundreds of terminals.

**2. Regional Prioritization Strategy**
Do not prioritize by geography; prioritize by **risk containment**.
*   **Phase 1 (Low Volume/Remote):** Target the 20% of stores with the lowest weekend transaction volumes first. If a firmware failure bricks these devices, the revenue impact is minimized, and they are far enough from high-traffic hubs to avoid customer visibility issues.
*   **Phase 2 (Standard Urban):** Once the success rate is confirmed at 99% across the low-volume sites, move to standard urban locations.
*   **Phase 3 (High Volume/Flagship):** Leave the top 10% highest-revenue stores for the final 6 hours of the window. This ensures that if a catastrophic firmware bug is discovered late, your most critical revenue generators have the highest probability of remaining on the old, stable system or being the last to fail, allowing for a rollback decision before Saturday morning.

**3. Deployment Sequence & Pacing**
With 40 technicians capable of 10 stores/hour, your maximum theoretical throughput is 480 stores in 48 hours. Since you have 800 stores, **you cannot complete this migration with the current crew size within the time limit.** You must immediately adopt a **Staggered Parallel Wave** approach:
*   **Wave A (Hours 0–12):** 120 stores (Low Risk). Requires running technicians in shifts to exceed the 10 stores/hour average or accepting that only 120 can be done. *Correction based on constraints:* If the limit is strictly 10 stores/hour total fleet capacity, you will only finish 480 stores. You must triage: Migrate only the 480 stores with the oldest hardware failing most frequently, and leave the remaining 320 on the old system for this weekend.
*   **Execution Step:** Technicians work in pairs. One handles physical swap; the other monitors the firmware flash. No technician moves to the next store until the previous terminal successfully processes a test transaction.

**4. Contingency Thresholds**
Monitor these three metrics hourly. If any threshold is breached, **halt all operations immediately**:
*   **Brick Rate > 0%:** If even one device is bricked due to firmware, stop. The image is corrupt or the procedure is flawed. Investigate the Canary result again.
*   **Success Time Variance > 15%:** If the average install time exceeds the planned duration by 15%, you will miss the Saturday deadline. Re-evaluate the scope (reduce number of stores).
*   **Test Transaction Failure > 2%:** If more than 2% of installed units fail to process a test payment, the configuration is flawed. Do not deploy further.

**Note:** Based on the math (40 techs / 4 stores per tech per hour = 10 stores/hr total), you can only service 480 stores in 48 hours. You must explicitly decide which 320 stores stay on the old system, as completing all 800 is mathematically impossible with current resources.
The "Canary" step is non-negotiable; skipping it to save time risks bricking the entire fleet.
