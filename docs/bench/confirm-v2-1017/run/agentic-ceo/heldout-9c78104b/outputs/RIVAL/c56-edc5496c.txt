### Aquaculture Automation Deployment Plan (14-Day Critical Path)

**1. Immediate First Task**
Conduct a **Design Validation Freeze and Drainage Simulation Review** immediately. Before any physical work or final risk assessment, the Data Analyst and Lead Technician must jointly verify that the current automation design accounts for the exact water volume, drainage flow rates, and larvae evacuation timelines of the specific tanks targeted. This prevents the irreversible error of draining tanks while larvae are present due to design-model mismatches.

**2. Risk Assessment Lead**
The **Data Analyst** must initiate the risk assessment. While technicians understand physical installation, the critical failure point here is temporal and biological (timing the drainage against larvae lifecycle stages). The Data Analyst possesses the growth cycle data required to model the "point of no return" for larvae survival. They must quantify the risk window before the technicians can safely plan the physical drainage steps.

**3. Required Approvals for Tank Drainage**
You must personally sign off on the **"Biological Zero-Point Certification"** alongside the **Lead Technician**. This approval confirms that:
*   The Data Analyst has verified the larvae have either matured past the critical vulnerability stage or have been successfully relocated/harvested from the specific tanks scheduled for draining.
*   The drainage schedule aligns precisely with the automation installation sequence, ensuring no tank sits empty longer than necessary (to prevent structural stress or contamination) before the new feeders are active.

**4. Structured Order of Operations (Days 1–14)**

*   **Days 1–2: Validation & Modeling (Data Analyst Lead)**
    *   Finalize design validation against current tank conditions.
    *   Model the exact drainage timeline to ensure zero larvae loss.
    *   Generate the "Biological Zero-Point Certification" for your approval.

*   **Days 3–4: Risk Assessment & Resource Allocation (Data Analyst + Lead Tech)**
    *   Complete the risk assessment focused on timing failures.
    *   Assign specific technicians to specific tanks based on the drainage sequence.
    *   Prepare contingency plans for pump failures or delays.

*   **Days 5–9: Phased Drainage and Installation (Technicians Lead)**
    *   Execute drainage strictly according to the certified schedule.
    *   Install automation hardware immediately post-drainage.
    *   Perform dry-run calibration of feeders before refilling.

*   **Days 10–12: System Integration & Testing (All Hands)**
    *   Refill tanks and integrate sensors with the central control system.
    *   Run simulated feeding cycles to verify consistency.
    *   Data Analyst monitors initial sensor feedback for anomalies.

*   **Days 13–14: Final Validation & Handover**
    *   Execute a full live test with minimal feed.
    *   Confirm consistent distribution patterns.
    *   Official handover to operations team prior to the 21-day harvest countdown.

**Note:** Do not allow physical drainage to commence until the Data Analyst signs off on the larvae status; this is the single point of failure for the $500k batch. The 14-day window allows no rework; every step must be right the first time.
