**Optimal Preparation Sequence**

1.  **Final Review of Rollback Contingencies (Immediate Priority):** Before any testing begins, you must validate the "break-glass" procedure. Since the old configuration is permanently overwritten, your primary risk is not just failure, but the inability to revert. Define the exact manual override protocols and communication chains required to halt trains safely if the digital link fails. This is the foundation upon which all other testing rests.
2.  **Sector-by-Sector Validation Plan:** Once rollback is secured, map the 1,200 miles into logical, isolated sectors based on traffic density and interdependency. Do not treat the network as a monolith. Define the specific success criteria and hand-off procedures for each sector. This plan dictates how the four-hour window is sliced and allocated.
3.  **Dry Run in Isolated Lab:** Execute the full deployment sequence in the lab only after the plan and contingencies are fixed. The lab run should simulate the *process* and *timing* of the sector rollout, not just the code functionality. Use this to identify bottlenecks in the engineers' workflow, not just software bugs.

**Authorization vs. Independent Preparation**

**Requires Your Direct Authorization:**
*   **The Rollback Protocol:** You must personally sign off on the manual fallback procedures. If the software fails, you are the one authorizing the shift to manual signaling.
*   **Sector Sequencing:** You must approve the order in which sectors are updated. This involves strategic risk assessment (e.g., updating low-traffic rural lines before major hubs like London Waterloo or Manchester Piccadilly).
*   **Go/No-Go Checkpoints:** Define the specific timestamps within the four-hour window where you will make the final call to proceed to the next sector or abort the entire operation.

**Engineers Can Prepare Independently:**
*   **Lab Environment Setup:** The eight signal engineers should immediately configure the isolated lab to mirror the production topology.
*   **Scripting and Automation:** Engineers can write and unit-test the deployment scripts for individual sectors.
*   **Documentation Drafting:** Safety inspectors can draft the updated safety case documents and checklists required for the post-deployment sign-off, pending your final review.

**First Concrete Actions (Take Immediately)**

1.  **Convene the "Red Team" Session:** Gather the four safety inspectors and the lead engineer for a 60-minute session dedicated solely to identifying every possible point of failure in the rollback process. Do not discuss the new code; discuss only how to survive its failure.
2.  **Map the Critical Path:** Create a minute-by-minute timeline of the four-hour window. Work backward from the moment trains must start moving. Identify the latest possible moment you can abort a sector update and still recover.
3.  **Isolate the First Sector:** Select the lowest-risk sector for the initial deployment pilot. Instruct the engineering team to prepare the deployment package specifically for this single sector for the upcoming lab dry run.

Note: The permanence of the overwrite makes the rollback strategy more critical than the deployment itself.
Do not proceed to lab testing until the manual fallback procedure is proven viable on paper.
