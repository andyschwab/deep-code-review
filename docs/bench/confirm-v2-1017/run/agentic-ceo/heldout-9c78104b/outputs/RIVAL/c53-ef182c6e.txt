### Wildfire Prediction Model Planning Session Workflow

**1. Priority Data Cleaning Task: Temporal Alignment and Gap Filling**
The highest precedence must be given to reconciling the temporal fragmentation in the twenty-year historical fire data. Since the model relies on correlating past fire spread patterns with real-time sensor inputs, any misalignment in timestamps or significant gaps in the historical record will render the training data useless for predicting future spread within a ten-minute window. Before addressing missing values or outlier removal, the team must synchronize all historical fire incident logs with available weather records from that era to create a continuous, time-aligned baseline. Without this chronological integrity, the machine learning algorithms cannot learn the causal relationships between weather conditions and fire behavior.

**2. Ethical Boundaries for Automated Evacuation Orders**
You must define a "Human-in-the-Loop" constraint for any evacuation order affecting critical infrastructure, specifically hospitals and care facilities. The ethical boundary should state that the automated system acts solely as a high-fidelity recommendation engine, not a final decision-maker, when the predicted impact zone includes vulnerable populations. The system may automatically trigger alerts for general residential zones if the confidence score exceeds a specific threshold (e.g., 95%), but any prediction involving hospitals requires explicit verification by a human field operative or command center supervisor before the alert is broadcast. This prevents the irreversible erosion of public trust caused by false positives while maintaining the speed necessary for general public safety.

**3. First Concrete Experiment: Sensor Cross-Validation Drill**
The team should immediately execute a "Static Cross-Validation Drill" using a controlled subset of the network. Select fifty weather sensors located in a non-fire, stable environment with known, consistent conditions. Compare their real-time readings against each other and against the nearest satellite feed over a twenty-four-hour period. The objective is to quantify the variance and latency between the ground sensors and the satellite data without the noise of an active fire event. If the sensors cannot agree with each other or the satellite baseline within a tight margin of error during stable conditions, they will produce unreliable data during a dynamic fire event. This experiment verifies the fundamental integrity of the input stream before any predictive modeling begins.
