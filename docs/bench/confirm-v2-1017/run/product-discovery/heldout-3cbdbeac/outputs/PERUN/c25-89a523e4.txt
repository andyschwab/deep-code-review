**Verdict: Do not pivot the entire monetization strategy yet. The current evidence explicitly disconfirms the core assumption required for the subscription model, but the sample size is insufficient to declare the model dead. Proceeding with a full launch based on three pilots is a high-risk fabrication of product-market fit.**

**1. The Riskiest Assumption Gate**
*   **Assumption:** "Jakarta small retailers prefer predictable recurring costs over large upfront capital expenditure due to cash flow volatility."
*   **Current Evidence:** 20 interviews in Glodok indicate the opposite: distrust of recurring charges due to *actual* cash flow volatility. 92% of historical revenue validates the one-time fee model.
*   **Status:** **Disconfirmed.** The qualitative data directly contradicts the hypothesis.
*   **Decision Rule:** You cannot build the full March launch on this foundation. The "cheap test" (interviews) has already signaled a failure. To proceed, you must isolate *which* specific segment might accept subscriptions, rather than forcing it on the general market.

**2. Evaluation of Current Pilot Data**
*   **Sample Size:** 3 pilots.
*   **Assessment:** Statistically insignificant. With a burn rate of $15k/month, converting 3 users provides no evidence of scale, retention, or willingness to pay at a level that sustains the business.
*   **Risk:** Treating these 3 users as validation is a "false positive" trap. They may be outliers or friends being polite. Relying on them to secure funding is dangerous; investors will view a 3-user pilot against 20 negative interviews as ignoring market signals.

**3. Recommended Path: The "Concierge" Bridge**
Do not choose between "all subscription" or "all one-time." Instead, run a behavioral test to find the boundary condition before writing more code.
*   **The Test:** Offer the 20 interviewed shop owners (and 50 more) a **hybrid concierge offer**: A low upfront fee + a very small monthly "support" fee, framed as insurance/updates rather than a "subscription."
*   **Metric:** Conversion rate on the *monthly* component.
*   **Kill Threshold:** If fewer than 10% of interested leads accept *any* recurring charge component after hearing the Glodok feedback, **drop the pure subscription roadmap**. Pivot the narrative to "Lifetime License + Optional Annual Renewal" which matches the cultural preference for ownership while generating recurring revenue.

**4. Investor Narrative Correction**
Your investors want a path to recurring revenue, not necessarily a SaaS subscription model.
*   **The Pitch:** "We tested the standard SaaS assumption against 20 local retailers and found a cultural mismatch regarding recurring debt. We are now pivoting our *mechanism* (not the goal) to a hybrid model that respects local cash flow while securing recurring touchpoints. We are pausing the full March build to validate a hybrid pricing structure with a kill-criterion of X% conversion."
*   **Why this works:** It shows you are data-driven (you listened to the 20 interviews) rather than dogmatic. It protects the $15k burn from being wasted on a feature the market rejected in interviews.

**Immediate Action:** Stop engineering the full subscription tier. Design the hybrid offer and re-interview the 20 shop owners with a concrete price anchor, not a hypothetical question.
