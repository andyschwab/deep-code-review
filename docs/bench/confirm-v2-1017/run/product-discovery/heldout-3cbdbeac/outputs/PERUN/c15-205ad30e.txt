### Decision Framework: Build vs. Negotiate

You are facing a classic **viability vs. feasibility** tension with high concentration risk. Do not commit the entire team yet. Instead, run the **Riskiest-Assumption Gate** immediately to validate your core assumption: *"No other clients will need this functionality in the near future."*

If this assumption is false, building a custom, one-off solution creates the exact technical debt you fear, complicating your European expansion. If it is true, the revenue risk (40% of ARR) outweighs the debt risk, provided the implementation is isolated.

#### Step 1: Validate the Assumption (Days 1–2)
Before writing code, verify if this is a unique request or a market signal.
*   **Action:** Contact 5–10 other active clients or prospects.
*   **Question:** "We are updating our invoicing module. Apart from AED/USD, which specific currencies would be critical for your operations in the next 6 months?"
*   **Decision Rule:**
    *   **If ≥2 others name specific currencies:** This is a market requirement. Build a scalable, multi-currency architecture now (6 weeks) to serve Europe later. The debt is an investment.
    *   **If 0 others care:** This is a custom request. Proceed to Step 2.

#### Step 2: The "Cheap Test" Negotiation (Days 3–4)
Since the client represents 40% of revenue, you have leverage to negotiate scope, not just timeline. Do not accept "all 5 currencies in 6 weeks" as the only option.
*   **Proposal A (The Concierge Test):** Offer to manually handle the extra 5 currencies via credit notes or external conversion for *this* fiscal year-end only, while hardcoding only the top 2 most-used new currencies.
    *   *Goal:* Reduce dev time from 6 weeks to 2 weeks.
*   **Proposal B (The Paid Expedite):** Offer the full 6-week build but require a **contract amendment** locking in a 12-month renewal at current rates + a 15% surcharge for "custom development."
    *   *Goal:* Monetize the technical debt and offset the opportunity cost of delayed improvements.

#### Step 3: Technical Debt Mitigation Strategy
If you must build all 5 currencies:
*   **Non-Goal for Q1:** Do not refactor the entire ledger or build a dynamic currency exchange rate engine for *all* users.
*   **Scope Defense:** Limit the scope strictly to the invoicing module's display and calculation logic for these 5 specific ISO codes. Hardcode the list; do not build an admin UI for adding currencies yet.
*   **Acceptance Criteria:** The system must allow the client to generate compliant invoices by March 31. It does *not* need to support automated recurring billing in those currencies yet.

#### Recommendation
**Negotiate first, then build.**
1.  **Do not** commit the full team today.
2.  **Run the 5-client check** tomorrow.
3.  **Present Proposal B** to the client: "We can hit March 31, but given the custom nature, we require a signed renewal and surcharge to prioritize this over our roadmap."
    *   If they sign: Build it. The revenue secures your runway for Europe.
    *   If they refuse: Pivot to Proposal A (partial build + manual workarounds).

**Metric for Success:** Secure a signed amendment covering the development cost *before* sprint planning begins. If you cannot secure this, the concentration risk is too high to justify halting all other improvements.

---
*Note: This approach treats the request as a business validation test, not just a feature ticket. It forces the client to share the risk of your roadmap delay.*
