# Critical Evidence Gaps: Biometric Login Decision

**Status: UNVERIFIED / NO-GO FOR BUILD**
Current evidence is insufficient to proceed. The riskiest assumption (compliance via on-device processing) and the primary value hypothesis (net DAU gain vs. churn) remain untested. Building now risks regulatory violation and reputational damage.

### 1. The Compliance Assumption (Riskiest)
**Assumption:** "On-device processing satisfies upcoming January 1st regional regulations."
**Gap:** This is a legal conclusion, not a validated fact. An incorrect assumption here renders the entire feature illegal, regardless of user demand.
**Required Action:** Route immediately to legal counsel or a certified privacy expert for a formal opinion on the specific January 1st text. Do not rely on internal assumptions.
**Decision Rule:** If legal cannot confirm in writing that the proposed architecture is compliant before Jan 1st, the project is a **KILL**.

### 2. The Value Hypothesis (Usability vs. Churn)
**Assumption:** "Friction reduction will increase DAU without triggering a churn spike due to privacy fears."
**Gap:** Survey intent (68% interest) contradicts stated behavior (42% privacy concern). Stated interest in a survey rarely predicts actual adoption when biometric data is involved. We do not know if the 42% will churn or simply refuse to enable the feature.
**Required Test:** A "Fake Door" or low-fidelity prototype test with a subset of users (e.g., 500 users) *before* full development.
*   **Metric:** Conversion rate to enable biometric login vs. opt-out/churn rate in the test group.
*   **Kill Threshold:** If churn in the test group exceeds **1.5%** (approx. 180 users of the 12k base, representing a reputational tipping point) or if enablement is <15%, **DROP** the feature.
*   **Sample Size:** Minimum 500 active users for statistical significance on churn signals.
*   **Deadline:** Must be completed 3 weeks before the executive decision to allow analysis time.

### 3. Reputational Damage Quantification
**Gap:** "Reputational damage" is currently qualitative. The executive committee needs a quantitative bound.
**Required Input:** Define the maximum acceptable loss of trust.
*   **Metric:** Number of support tickets regarding privacy, social media sentiment score, or direct cancellation reasons.
*   **Threshold:** If >5% of negative feedback in the pilot cites "biometric fear" as the reason, the reputational cost outweighs the usability gain.

### 4. Financial Viability (ICE Input)
**Gap:** We have a cost (€40,000) but no validated impact score.
**Required Calculation:** To justify €40k, how many additional daily active users (or retained subscribers) are needed?
*   **Formula:** (€40,000 / Average Revenue Per User) = Minimum users needed to break even.
*   **Missing Data:** You must supply the ARPU to calculate the specific numeric target for the "Impact" column in our prioritization matrix. Without this, we cannot score the initiative against other backlog items.

### Immediate Next Steps
1.  **Stop Development:** Do not write code for biometric storage or processing until the legal assumption is externally validated.
2.  **Design the Pilot:** Create a non-functional mockup or limited release to test the *behavioral* response (enablement vs. churn) rather than relying on the conflicting survey data.
3.  **Define the Kill Criteria:** Explicitly agree on the churn percentage that triggers a project cancellation before spending any of the €40,000.

**Note:** The survey data (68% interest vs. 42% concern) indicates a polarized user base; this increases the risk of a backlash if the feature is forced or poorly explained.
**Note:** "On-device" claims must be technically audited; marketing this incorrectly is a primary source of reputational damage in health tech.
