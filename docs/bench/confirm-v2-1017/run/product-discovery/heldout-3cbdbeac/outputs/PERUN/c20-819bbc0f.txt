### Evidence Evaluation: Referral Program vs. Supply Density

**Current Evidence Strength: Weak / Unverified**
Your hypothesis relies on a single variable (financial incentive) while explicitly acknowledging a potential blocker (trust) that money may not fix. A viral coefficient of 0.4 indicates that for every 5 users, you only gain 2 more organically; this is a structural leak, not just a motivation gap. Launching a referral program now risks paying for referrals that never convert or churn immediately because the underlying value proposition (supply density/trust) isn't there yet. You are attempting to scale a leaky bucket.

**The Riskiest Assumption**
*Assumption:* "Financial incentives (10% fee waiver) are sufficient to overcome local trust barriers and drive high-quality referrals."
*Status:* Untested. If false, you burn runway on fees for users who do not stick.

**Required Validation Plan (Before Building the Feature)**

Do not build the automated referral system yet. Run these two cheap tests to gather the missing evidence:

#### 1. The "Trust Barrier" Interview (Qualitative)
**Goal:** Determine if trust, not price, is the blocking force.
**Method:** Conduct 5–7 "Mom Test" interviews with current sellers who have *not* referred anyone yet.
**Ask about past behavior, not future intent:**
*   "Tell me about the last time you recommended a marketplace to another seller in Cairo. What happened?"
*   "When was the last time you hesitated to recommend a business tool to a peer? What specifically made you pause?"
*   "Walk me through the last time you tried to find new suppliers/buyers locally. How did you verify they were legitimate?"
**Success Signal:** If users cite "fear of looking bad if the platform fails" or "don't know enough serious sellers" as the primary blocker, a fee waiver will not work. If they say "I would have, but the fees are too high," the incentive might work.

#### 2. The "Concierge" Referral Test (Quantitative)
**Goal:** Test the incentive mechanism without coding.
**Method:** Manually recruit 20 active sellers. Offer them the 10% waiver *manually* if they bring in one verified seller who lists 5+ items within 48 hours. Do not build a dashboard; track this via spreadsheet and manual verification.
**Metric:** Conversion rate of invited sellers.
**Kill Threshold:** If fewer than **3 out of 20** (15%) successfully refer a *qualified* seller who stays active after 2 weeks, **do not build the automated feature**. This indicates that either the incentive is too weak or the supply density/trust issue is the real bottleneck.

**Decision Rule for Tuesday's Sprint**
*   **GO:** If the concierge test hits >15% conversion *and* interviews confirm trust is not the primary blocker.
*   **NO-GO (Pivot to Supply Density):** If the concierge test fails OR interviews reveal that users are afraid to vouch for the platform due to low liquidity/trust. In this case, your sprint must focus on manually seeding supply (e.g., onboarding key anchor sellers) until the marketplace feels "dense" enough to be safe to recommend.

**Immediate Non-Goal**
Building an automated referral dashboard or integrating fee-waiver logic into the billing system. This is a waste of engineering cycles until the manual test proves the unit economics work.

---
*Note: Do not estimate market size or total addressable revenue for this decision; those are unknowable without the behavioral data above.*
*Focus strictly on the "very disappointed" signal: Ask your 5,000 sellers how they would feel if they could no longer use the platform tomorrow to gauge true retention vs. vanity metrics.*
