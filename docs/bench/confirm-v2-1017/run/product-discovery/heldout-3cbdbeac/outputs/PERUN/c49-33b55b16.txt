### Strategic Risk & Resource Implication Breakdown: Build vs. Buy

**Context for Decision Makers:** The core tension is between **immediate revenue recovery** (stopping the loss of contracts due to 4-day onboarding) and **long-term operational liability** (maintenance overhead of a custom solution). The following analysis structures the unknowns that must be resolved before Q3 roadmap approval.

#### Option A: Build Internal Distribution Tools
*Hypothesis: Reduces onboarding from 4 days to <24 hours within two release cycles.*

| Dimension | Strategic Risk | Resource Implication |
| :--- | :--- | :--- |
| **Time-to-Value** | **High.** If the "two release cycles" assumption is incorrect, the team continues losing contracts during development. The 4-day bottleneck remains until launch. | **Immediate.** Requires diverting ~12 engineers (or a dedicated squad) from other roadmap items for the build duration. |
| **Financial** | **Sunk Cost.** The $150k budget is spent regardless of success. If adoption stalls, this capital yields no recurring ROI compared to the variable cost of Option B. | **Upfront CAPEX.** $150k initial outlay. Future costs are internal (salaries for maintenance, bug fixes, infrastructure). |
| **Control/Flexibility** | **Low Risk (Advantage).** Full ownership of the workflow allows customization to specific retailer needs that third-parties may ignore. | **Ongoing OPEX.** Permanent headcount required for maintenance, security patches, and feature updates. |
| **Unknown Variable** | **Maintenance Overhead.** You explicitly noted uncertainty here. Without data, the "long-term control" benefit may be negated by a "forever tax" on engineering time. | **Opportunity Cost.** Every hour spent maintaining this tool is an hour not spent on core inventory software differentiation. |

#### Option B: Integrate Third-Party Marketplace
*Cost: $20k/year; Development time: Negligible.*

| Dimension | Strategic Risk | Resource Implication |
| :--- | :--- | :--- |
| **Time-to-Value** | **Low.** Immediate resolution to the 4-day onboarding bottleneck. Stops the bleed of lost contracts in Q3. | **Minimal.** Integration effort only (likely days/weeks vs. months). Engineers return to core product work quickly. |
| **Financial** | **Recurring Liability.** $20k/year forever. Risk of vendor price hikes or tier changes as you scale. | **Predictable OPEX.** Easy to model in P&L. No large upfront CAPEX hit. |
| **Control/Flexibility** | **High.** You are bound by the vendor's roadmap, API limits, and uptime. If they change a critical feature, your onboarding breaks. | **Integration Debt.** Future migrations away from this vendor will be costly if the partnership sours or limits growth. |
| **Unknown Variable** | **Fit Gap.** Does the third-party tool actually support the specific nuances of your local retailers? If not, manual workarounds may persist despite integration. | **Vendor Management.** Requires ongoing relationship management and contract negotiation. |

#### The Riskiest Assumption Gate
Your current plan relies on a single unproven assumption: *"Building internally will reduce onboarding to under twenty-four hours within two release cycles."*

**The Test Before Commitment:**
Before approving the $150k build, you must validate the **feasibility** and **impact** of this assumption with a cheap test, not a full build.
*   **Proposed Test:** Map the current 4-day manual process into a "Concierge" or "Wizard-of-Oz" prototype using existing low-code tools or a hardened script *without* building the full platform infrastructure.
*   **Kill Threshold:** If this lightweight test cannot achieve <24 hours with <20% of the proposed $150k effort, the assumption that "building is the answer" is false. The delay may be process-related, not tool-related.
*   **Decision Rule:** If the test fails to hit the time target, **do not build**. Pivot to optimizing the manual process or selecting the third-party option immediately.

#### Critical Data Gaps (Required for Final Verdict)
To move from "debate" to "decision," the following evidence is missing and must be sourced before Friday:
1.  **Maintenance Baseline:** Do we have data on similar internal tools built previously? What was the actual engineer-hours/year spent maintaining them vs. the initial build time? (Do not guess; retrieve historical logs).
2.  **Vendor Fit Audit:** Has a technical lead performed a gap analysis of the third-party API against our top 5 retailer requirements? (Yes/No list, not a feeling).
3.  **Churn Quantification:** Exactly how much revenue did the "three lost contracts" represent? If the value is low, the $150k build may never pay back.

**Status:** **UNVERIFIED.** The choice cannot be made on the current assumption alone. The "Build" path requires a passed feasibility test; the "Buy" path requires a confirmed fit audit.
