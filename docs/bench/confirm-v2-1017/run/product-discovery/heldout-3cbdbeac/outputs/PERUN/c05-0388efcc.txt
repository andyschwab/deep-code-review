### Decision Framework: TokyoMarket Hub Marketplace Feature

**Current Status:** **DO NOT BUILD YET.**
You are currently attempting to validate a high-cost infrastructure build (marketplace) using a single data point (one vendor's promise). This violates the riskiest-assumption gate. The vendor's enthusiasm is a *hypothesis*, not evidence of broad demand or network effects.

#### 1. The Riskiest Assumption Gate
**Assumption:** "One major vendor's enthusiasm signals broad demand among existing users and will trigger network effects without cannibalizing direct sales."
**Impact:** High (Architectural shift, potential cannibalization).
**Uncertainty:** Critical (Based on one relationship, not market behavior).

**Required Cheap Test (Before June 30 Code Freeze):**
Do not write code. Run a **Concierge Test** or **Fake-Door Test** with your existing user base *excluding* the champion vendor.
*   **Action:** Create a landing page or email campaign announcing "Coming Soon: Multi-Vendor Marketplace" to 500 existing buyers/sellers.
*   **Metric:** Click-through rate to "Join Waitlist" or "Pre-register as Seller."
*   **Kill Threshold:** If fewer than **5%** of contacted users express interest (or if <3 *other* sellers commit to onboarding without the feature being built), **drop the feature** or pivot. Do not proceed based on the single vendor's promise alone.

#### 2. Critical Questions for the Strategy Workshop
Use these questions to structure your discussion. Do not answer them with opinions; answer them with data you must gather this week.

**A. Validating Broad Demand (Desirability)**
*   "Aside from our champion vendor, have any other sellers *unsolicited* asked for multi-vendor capabilities in the last 6 months?"
*   "If we launched this tomorrow, can we name 5 other sellers who would onboard *without* being personally called by the head of sales?"
*   "What specific problem does the *buyer* have that our current direct sales model fails to solve, which a marketplace fixes?"

**B. Assessing Cannibalization & Viability**
*   "If the champion vendor moves 50 suppliers to the marketplace, what happens to their current direct transaction volume? Does it shift, grow, or disappear?"
*   "Does the $200k GMV estimate include sales that would have happened anyway on our current platform (cannibalization), or is this truly incremental revenue?"
*   "What is the cost of supporting 50 new suppliers (support tickets, disputes, onboarding time) versus the projected margin on $200k GMV?"

**C. Testing the 'Promise' (Feasibility/Risk)**
*   "Can we get the champion vendor to sign a non-binding Letter of Intent (LOI) stating they will onboard 50 suppliers *only if* the feature launches by June 30, with a clause that the deal voids if fewer than 10 other sellers join in month one?"
*   "What happens to our roadmap if this vendor churns three months after launch? Is the feature useless without them?"

#### 3. Immediate Next Steps (Evidence Gathering)
1.  **Interview 10 Other Sellers:** Ask about their past attempts to sell via marketplaces. Do not pitch your idea. Ask: "Tell me about the last time you tried to expand your distribution channels. What happened?" (The Mom Test).
2.  **Survey Buyers:** Send a "Very Disappointed" survey variant. "How would you feel if you could no longer buy directly from single vendors but had to navigate a multi-vendor catalog?"
3.  **Define the Non-Goal:** Explicitly state: "We are NOT building scalable marketplace infrastructure (auto-onboarding, complex split payments) until the Concierge Test proves 5+ independent sellers are ready to transact."

**Workshop Output Required:** A signed decision to either (A) Run the Concierge Test next week, or (B) Kill the feature based on lack of secondary evidence. No code commits allowed until (A) passes the kill threshold.

*Note: The $200k figure is a vendor estimate, not validated data. Treat it as a maximum upside scenario, not a baseline.*
*Do not let the June 30 deadline force a build; a failed test by June 15 saves more money than a wasted build.*
