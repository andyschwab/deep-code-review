### Situation Summary
DataSafe Corp has received four enterprise support tickets in the last month regarding accidental bulk data deletions. These incidents required 15 hours of manual restoration by the support team and caused significant customer distress. The proposed solution is a 30-day version history tool to enable user self-service rollback. The decision to include this in the November 5th platform update must be made immediately.

### Critical Gap: Evidence vs. Assumption
The current evidence confirms **pain** (4 incidents, 15 hours labor, distressed clients) but does not confirm **frequency** or **solution fit**.
*   **Unknown Frequency:** We lack telemetry on "near-miss" deletions or how often users attempt to revert changes beyond a simple "undo."
*   **Unverified Assumption:** The belief that version history will "eliminate these tickets entirely" is untested. Users may delete intentionally and regret it later, or the UI may be causing the errors rather than the lack of a rollback feature.
*   **Risk:** Building a complex versioning engine based on four anecdotes risks over-engineering a niche problem or solving the wrong problem entirely.

### Recommendation: Do Not Commit to Full Build Yet
**Verdict:** **Refuse to build the full feature for the November 5th release until the riskiest assumption is tested.**

The riskiest assumption is: *"Users frequently need to revert bulk changes beyond the immediate session, and a 30-day history is the cheapest way to prevent support tickets."*

**Immediate Action Plan (Before Nov 5th):**
1.  **Run the "Cheapest Test" (Concierge/Manual):** Instead of building the tool, interview the four affected customers. Ask specifically about the *moment* before deletion: What were they trying to achieve? Did they realize the mistake immediately or days later?
2.  **Instrument "Near-Miss" Telemetry:** If engineering time allows before Nov 5th, ship *only* the logging for bulk delete actions and subsequent support contacts. Do not build the UI yet.
3.  **Define the Kill Threshold:**
    *   *Hypothesis:* If fewer than X% of enterprise tenants perform a bulk delete that requires restoration >1 hour after the event, the 30-day history feature is not viable for this cycle.
    *   *Decision Rule:* If the interviews reveal that 3 of the 4 incidents were due to UI confusion (not lack of rollback), the solution is a UI warning, not version history.

**Sprint Planning Decision:**
*   **Option A (Recommended):** Dedicate the sprint to **Discovery & Instrumentation**. Ship the telemetry hooks and conduct the interviews. Delay the feature build to the following cycle.
*   **Option B (High Risk):** Proceed with build only if the CTO confirms the engineering cost is negligible (<2 days) and the Customer Success Head confirms these 4 clients represent >20% of enterprise revenue at risk. Otherwise, the cost of building the wrong thing exceeds the cost of 15 hours of manual support.

**Non-Goal for This Sprint:**
Building the full 30-day rollback UI/UX. This is a scope defense until we validate that "30 days" is the correct window and that "rollback" is the correct mechanism.

---
*Note: This recommendation follows the "Riskiest-Assumption Gate"; building now assumes the solution without validating the frequency or root cause.*
