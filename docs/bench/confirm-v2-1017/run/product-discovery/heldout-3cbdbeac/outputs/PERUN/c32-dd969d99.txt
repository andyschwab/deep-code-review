**Recommendation: Do not build the full integration yet. Run a 3-week Concierge Test instead.**

**The Decision Logic**
Your situation presents a classic "High-Value Signal vs. Broad Uncertainty" conflict. Five C-level requests are significant, but building a deep, automated integration based solely on stated intent ("this will save us time") violates the principle of testing the riskiest assumption first. The riskiest assumption is not whether the executives *want* this, but whether the underlying workflow actually functions as described for mid-level managers who will ultimately use it. If you build the automation now and the workflow proves flawed, you waste three weeks of engineering capacity on a feature that may not deliver the promised 10-hour savings.

**The Plan: Concierge Test (April 1 – April 21)**
Instead of writing code, manually perform the "sync" for these five executives for three weeks.
1.  **Setup:** Ask the five executives to forward their scheduling conflicts or draft plans to a dedicated email address.
2.  **Execution:** Your team (or a designated operator) manually checks the calendars of relevant team members and sends back optimized scheduling proposals within 2 hours.
3.  **Measurement:** Track the *actual* time saved per week for the teams (verified by the users, not estimated) and the error rate of your manual proposals.

**Success Criteria (Kill Thresholds)**
Before April 1st, agree on these numbers with Sales and Engineering:
*   **Adoption:** At least 4 of the 5 executives must use the service at least 3 times per week.
*   **Value Validation:** Users must confirm *actual* time savings of at least 6 hours/week (60% of the claimed 10).
*   **Renewal Link:** The Sales Director must secure a written commitment that successful completion of this test directly influences the renewal decision.

If these thresholds are not met by April 21st, **do not build the automated integration**. Pivot to investigating why the manual process failed (e.g., mid-level manager resistance, data accuracy issues) before writing code.

**Why This Wins**
*   **For Sales:** It gives an immediate "yes" to the C-level executives ("We are starting your dedicated service immediately"), showing responsiveness without delaying the release cycle.
*   **For Engineering:** It protects the 3-week window. If the manual test fails, you saved weeks of dev time. If it succeeds, you build with confidence, knowing exactly what logic to automate.
*   **For Product:** It validates the *behavior* and *value* rather than just the *request*. It answers whether mid-level managers will actually engage with the synced data before you hard-code the dependency.

**Non-Goals for This Stage**
*   **Automated Sync Logic:** Do not write code to parse calendar APIs yet.
*   **Broad Rollout:** Do not market this to the wider 2,000 seats until the pilot proves the value metric.
*   **Perfect UI:** The interface for this test is email; do not spend time building a dashboard.

**Note:** This approach turns a "build vs. wait" debate into a "validate then build" certainty.
**Note:** Ensure the Sales Director communicates this as a "premium white-glove onboarding" rather than a "delayed feature" to maintain momentum.
