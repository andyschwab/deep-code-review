Your current data (2% engagement) indicates the *current implementation* failed, but it does not prove the *concept* is invalid. You are conflating "badges don't work" with "these specific badges, presented this way, to this audience, didn't work." Before investing in a new strategy or redesign, you must test your riskiest assumption: **that the low engagement is due to design irrelevance rather than a fundamental lack of desire for gamification.**

**1. The Riskiest Assumption & Cheap Test**
*   **Assumption:** Users ignore badges because the designs are unappealing or the rewards feel meaningless, not because they oppose gamification itself.
*   **Cheapest Test (Concierge/Fake-Door):** Do not build new badge mechanics. Instead, manually award a "high-value" badge to a small cohort (e.g., 50 users) who just completed a significant workout. Send a personalized push notification: "You've unlocked the 'Stockholm Strider' elite status. Click here to see your profile."
*   **Metric:** Click-through rate on the profile link and subsequent session length.
*   **Kill Threshold:** If fewer than **15%** of the manually awarded users click the link or extend their session, drop the gamification concept entirely. If >15%, the issue is likely visibility or design, warranting iteration.
*   **Deadline:** Run this within 48 hours.

**2. Required Evidence (Mom-Test Interviews)**
You cannot decide based on flat metrics alone. You need to interview 5 users who ignored the beta badges and 5 who engaged.
*   **Do not ask:** "Would you use better badges?" (Hypothetical/Lying).
*   **Ask:** "Tell me about the last time you checked your progress in the app. What specifically were you looking for?"
*   **Ask:** "When you saw the beta badges, what did you think they meant for your training?"
*   **Goal:** Determine if they *noticed* the badges and *understood* them, or if they simply don't care about external validation.
