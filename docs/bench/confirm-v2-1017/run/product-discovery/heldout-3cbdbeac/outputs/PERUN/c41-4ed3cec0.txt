**Recommendation: Prioritize Refining the Single-Entry Interface**

Based on the evidence provided, refining the single-entry interface is the lower-risk, higher-certainty initiative for this quarter. While bulk upload addresses the speed complaint for half your clients, it introduces unvalidated assumptions about data formatting that could fail to reduce the 30% error rate or even increase support load.

**Impact Comparison & Time Saved Calculation**

*Current Baseline:* 150 error incidents/month; 4 minutes per item entry time.

**Option A: Bulk CSV Upload (High Uncertainty)**
*   **Hypothesized Impact:** Eliminates manual typing, theoretically reducing entry time to near zero for bulk actions.
*   **Time Saved Calculation:** *Cannot be calculated yet.* The savings depend entirely on the volume of items users attempt to upload in bulk versus those still entered individually. Without data on average batch sizes, the total minutes saved is unknown.
*   **Risk:** If users format CSVs incorrectly (see unknowns below), error rates may not drop from 30%, and "upload failed" tickets could replace "entry error" tickets.

**Option B: Refined Single-Entry Interface (High Certainty)**
*   **Hypothesized Impact:** Directly targets the root cause of the 30% error rate (typos, wrong formats) and the 4-minute latency.
*   **Time Saved Calculation:** If refinements (e.g., validation, auto-complete) reduce entry time by 50% (from 4 mins to 2 mins):
    *   *Assumption:* If a typical user enters 10 items/day.
    *   *Current:* 40 mins/day. *New:* 20 mins/day.
    *   *Savings:* 20 mins/user/day.
    *   *Error Reduction:* If validation prevents 80% of the 150 monthly incidents, you save ~120 support tickets/month immediately.
*   **Verdict:** This option guarantees a reduction in the known 150 incidents and provides calculable speed gains based on current usage patterns.

**Unknown Variables: Bulk Upload Data Formatting Risks**

Before building bulk upload, you must validate how users actually hold their data. The following unknowns could cause the feature to fail or generate new errors:

1.  **Source Heterogeneity:** Do clients have their data in a consistent digital format (e.g., exported from another ERP), or are they manually typing data into Excel just to upload it? If the latter, error rates will not improve.
2.  **Column Mapping Ambiguity:** How will users handle column headers that don't match your schema exactly? (e.g., "Item_Name" vs. "ProductName"). Will this cause silent failures or rejected rows?
3.  **Data Cleaning Burden:** Who is responsible for fixing formatting errors (dates, special characters, whitespace) before the upload? If the tool rejects the whole file on one error, users may revert to single entry.
4.  **Partial Success Handling:** If a 1,000-row CSV has 5 errors, does the system upload 995 and fail 5, or reject all 1,000? Users' expectations here are currently unknown.

**Next Step:** Do not build the bulk uploader yet. Run a "concierge test": ask three enterprise clients to send you their actual inventory spreadsheets. Attempt to import them manually. The friction you encounter defines the real requirements and validates whether the "30% error" problem exists in their source files or only during manual entry.
