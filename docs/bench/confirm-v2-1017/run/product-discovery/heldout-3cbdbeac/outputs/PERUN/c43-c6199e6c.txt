### Recommendation: Proceed with a "Riskiest-Assumption Gate" Before Full Build

**Verdict:** Do not commit to a full Role-Based Access Control (RBAC) refactor yet. The current evidence indicates **high desirability** but **unverified viability** regarding the engineering timeline. You must validate the "15% conversion lift" assumption with a cheap test before risking the Q4 release date.

#### 1. The Evidence State
*   **Strong Signal (Desirability):** Three formal security questionnaires and specific questions from two Toronto prospects confirm that the current "admin-vs-user" toggle is a blocking issue for enterprise sales. This is not hypothetical; it is a documented barrier in active deals.
*   **Weak Signal (Viability/Impact):** Your assumption that RBAC will increase conversion by **15%** is untested. If the engineering effort delays the release by more than 3 weeks, the opportunity cost may exceed the gain from those three deals.
*   **Missing Data:** We do not know the *minimum* viable version of RBAC required to pass the security questionnaires, nor do we have a confirmed engineering estimate for the refactor.

#### 2. The Riskiest Assumption & Test
**Assumption:** "A full RBAC refactor is the *only* way to satisfy these security questionnaires and achieve the 15% conversion lift, and it can be done without missing the Oct 15 deadline."

**Required Action: The Cheap Test (Run this immediately)**
Before writing production code, execute this disconfirming test:
*   **The Test:** Present the three security questionnaires to your lead backend engineer. Ask: "What is the absolute minimum change to our current logic (e.g., adding 2 specific roles vs. a full system) that satisfies these specific questions?"
*   **Metric:** Estimated days to implement the *minimum* solution.
*   **Kill Threshold:** If the minimum solution requires **>10 engineering days** (pushing release past Oct 1), **do not build**. Instead, negotiate a temporary exception with the prospects or delay the release formally.
*   **Decision Rule:**
    *   **Go:** If the minimum fix is <10 days AND the engineer confirms no critical path blockage.
    *   **Pivot:** If the fix is >10 days, propose a "Concierge RBAC" (manual role management by your team for these specific clients) to close the deals while deferring the full build to Q1.
    *   **Drop:** If the prospects refuse any workaround and the build kills the Q4 release entirely, the cost of delay outweighs the 15% gain.

#### 3. Immediate Next Steps for Tomorrow's Meeting
1.  **Ask Engineering:** "What is the smallest scope that answers 'Yes' to the specific questions in these three questionnaires?" (Do not ask "How long to build RBAC?").
2.  **Ask Sales:** "If we implement a manual workaround for 90 days, will you still close these three deals?"
3.  **Hold Scope:** Declare "Full generalized RBAC" as a **Non-Goal** for this sprint. The goal is "Pass these three questionnaires."
