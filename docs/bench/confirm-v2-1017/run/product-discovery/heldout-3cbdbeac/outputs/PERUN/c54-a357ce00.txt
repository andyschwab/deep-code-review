### Strategic Evaluation: Custom Domain Request

**1. Financial Necessity vs. Strategic Precedent**
The immediate financial imperative is clear: the $120,000 renewal represents 18% of total revenue. Losing this client creates an immediate hole that the projected $200,000 from three pipeline deals cannot guarantee, especially since those deals are currently contingent on unproven assumptions. However, treating this as a standard feature request ignores the structural risk. If this concession is framed as a "custom build" rather than a "platform capability," it sets a precedent where future enterprise clients expect bespoke engineering in exchange for contract signatures. This shifts your business model from scalable SaaS toward high-touch services, directly validating your fear of a "slippery slope" in maintenance costs.

**2. Architectural Implications: The "Slippery Slope" Test**
Your concern about bloating maintenance costs is the **riskiest assumption** in this scenario. Before committing engineering resources, you must distinguish between:
*   **Configuration:** A single toggle or DNS record mapping a custom domain to existing infrastructure (low maintenance, scalable).
*   **Customization:** Unique code paths, isolated databases, or distinct logic for this client (high maintenance, unscalable).

If the requirement is purely configuration, the "slippery slope" is a false positive; every SaaS eventually needs custom domains. If the client demands unique *behavior* tied to that domain, you are building a fork of your product. The architectural decision must be binary: either build a multi-tenant feature that *any* customer can use (turning this client into a beta tester for a new tier), or explicitly reject any logic that diverges from the core codebase.

**3. Validation of Growth Assumptions**
You are assuming this feature unlocks $200,000 in ARR from three other enterprises. This is an untested hypothesis.
*   **Evidence Gap:** Do you have written confirmation from the other three prospects that "custom domain" is the *only* blocker?
*   **Test:** Before re-architecting, verify if the other three deals would close with a standard subdomain if priced differently, or if they genuinely require this specific technical capability. Building based on the assumption that "Enterprise = Custom Domain" without verifying the specific needs of the pipeline risks over-engineering for a market signal that may not exist.

**4. Resource Allocation and Opportunity Cost**
Your team is at capacity. Diverting resources to this feature halts the core roadmap.
*   **Non-Goal Declaration:** For this specific sprint, any feature not related to enabling this renewal or validating the custom domain architecture is a non-goal.
*   **Opportunity Cost:** Calculate the cost of delaying the core roadmap. If the core product loses competitiveness because the team is building a one-off solution, the $120,000 renewal may not offset the churn of smaller clients or the loss of future market fit.

**5. Recommendation for Board & Sales Strategy**
Do not frame this as "building a custom feature." Frame it as "accelerating the Enterprise Tier roadmap."
*   **Strategic Pivot:** If you build this, it must be architected as a general-purpose capability available to all future customers, not a bespoke script for one. This prevents the "slippery slope" by ensuring every subsequent request utilizes the same standardized mechanism.
*   **Decision Rule:** If the engineering effort required exceeds a specific threshold (e.g., 3 weeks of full-team time) or requires unique logic for this client, the cost of delivery outweighs the renewal value when factoring in technical debt.
*   **Unknowns to Resolve:** You must determine if the other three pipeline deals have signed LOIs contingent on this feature. Without that evidence, you are spending 100% of your capacity on a 18% revenue retention play with speculative upside.

**Summary:** The renewal is financially critical, but the execution method determines your future scalability. Build a platform feature, not a client patch. Verify the pipeline demand before assuming the $200k upside is real.
