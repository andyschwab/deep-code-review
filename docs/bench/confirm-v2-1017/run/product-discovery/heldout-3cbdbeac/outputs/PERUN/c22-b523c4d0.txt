### Evidence Interpretation & Discovery Plan for Customer Success Portal

**Current Evidence Status:**
Your data confirms a *symptom* (NPS drop, 200 detractors citing "lack of self-service") but does not yet validate the *solution* (a dedicated portal). The assumption that users prefer self-service over your established high-touch model is the **riskiest assumption**. Building the portal before testing this preference risks a €200,000 investment in a feature customers may ignore, failing to reduce support volume.

**Required Qualitative Evidence (The "Mom Test" Approach):**
To justify the investment to the VP and Finance, you must replace the assumption with behavioral evidence. Do not ask users "Would you use a portal?" (hypotheticals yield false positives). Instead, gather evidence on **past behavior**:

1.  **Search Behavior Audit:** Analyze support logs for the 200 detractors. Did they attempt to find answers on the website *before* calling? If they never searched, a portal won't stop the call.
    *   *Metric to find:* % of detractors who visited help pages prior to ticket creation.
2.  **"Switch" Interviews (5–10 Detractors):** Conduct interviews focusing on the last time they faced an issue.
    *   *Ask:* "Walk me through the exact steps you took when you last needed help."
    *   *Listen for:* Moments of friction where they *wanted* to self-serve but couldn't, vs. moments where they simply preferred human reassurance.
3.  **Competitor/Alternative Analysis:** What are they currently using to self-serve? (e.g., Google searches, third-party forums, asking colleagues). If they aren't using existing self-service options elsewhere, adoption of a new portal is unlikely.

**Proposed Validation Test (Before December 5th):**
Run a **fake-door test** or **concierge prototype** to validate demand without building the full portal.
*   **Test:** Add a "Track Ticket" or "Search Knowledge Base" button to your current email signature or support landing page.
*   **Metric:** Click-through rate (CTR) and subsequent engagement.
*   **Kill Threshold:** If fewer than **10%** of exposed users click the link, or if <**5%** of those clickers return a second time, the assumption that users *want* self-service is likely false. **Do not build the portal.** Proceed only if behavioral data exceeds this threshold.

**Decision Framework for Finance:**
Present the €200k request not as a certainty, but as a conditional next step pending the result of this cheap test.
*   **If Test Passes:** Evidence shows users actively seek self-service; portal investment is justified to capture the 15% volume reduction.
*   **If Test Fails:** The NPS drop is likely due to other factors (e.g., resolution quality, not channel preference). Investing in the portal would be waste; redirect funds to agent training or process improvement.

**Immediate Next Steps:**
1.  Extract search/click data for the 200 detractors (IT/Analytics team).
2.  Schedule 5 interviews with recent detractors using the script above.
3.  Launch the fake-door test immediately to gather behavioral data before the Dec 5th meeting.

*Note: Do not cite market size or generic industry trends; your VP needs evidence specific to Iberia's high-touch customer base. A "plausible" persona is insufficient for a €200k decision.*
