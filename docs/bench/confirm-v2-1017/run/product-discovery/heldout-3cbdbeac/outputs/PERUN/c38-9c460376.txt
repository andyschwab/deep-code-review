**To:** CTO, SeoulStream
**From:** Product Manager
**Subject:** Risk Assessment: Public Template Gallery Investment vs. Validated Demand

**Executive Summary**
Proceeding with the $8,000 infrastructure build for a public template gallery carries a high risk of resource waste. Our current data indicates a critical disconnect: while pre-built templates receive 600 clicks/day (150/hr), zero custom templates have been created or shared in six weeks among 4,000 daily active users.

**The Core Risk**
The engineering assumption—that users need a *browsing* interface for templates—relies on the existence of user-generated content (UGC). However, we have no evidence that users want to *create* templates.
*   **The Empty Shelf Problem:** Building a gallery without creator activity results in an empty product. Users browsing an empty gallery will not suddenly start creating; they will simply leave.
*   **Misdiagnosed Friction:** The lack of creation (0 saves) suggests the barrier is not "discovery" (needing a gallery), but likely "capability" (the tool is too hard) or "desire" (users prefer our pre-built library and do not want to design their own).
*   **Opportunity Cost:** Allocating $8,000 and engineering cycles to a gallery assumes the creation problem is solved. If the root cause is that users *don't want* to create, this spend yields zero ROI.

**Recommendation**
Pause the gallery infrastructure build. We must first validate *why* creation is zero. If users cannot or will not create templates, a gallery is useless. We need to determine if we should:
1.  Improve the creator tool (if desire exists but friction is high).
2.  Double down on pre-built libraries (if desire to create is non-existent).
3.  Pivot the template strategy entirely.

**Discovery Plan: Power User Interviews**
Before writing code, we must interview our top 10 power users (those with highest engagement/pre-built usage) to uncover the truth. We will ask about past behaviors, not hypothetical futures.

**Interview Guide (The "Mom Test" Approach):**
1.  **Past Behavior:** "Walk me through the last time you tried to customize a layout on SeoulStream. What specifically were you trying to achieve that the pre-built options didn't cover?"
2.  **Workarounds:** "When you couldn't find the exact layout you wanted, what did you do instead? Did you use a different tool, settle for something close, or abandon the task?"
3.  **Pain Point Specifics:** "You've used our pre-built library frequently. Tell me about a time a pre-built template *almost* worked but failed. What was missing?"
4.  **Creation Friction:** "We noticed the beta creator tool hasn't been used much. When you opened it, what was the very first thing that made you hesitate or close it?"
5.  **Value Validation:** "Have you ever paid for or spent significant time building a template system on another platform? What made that worth the effort?"
6.  **The "Gallery" Hypothesis Check:** "If we launched a gallery of user templates tomorrow, what specific problem in your current workflow would that solve? Can you describe the last time you needed a template *style* you couldn't find?"

**Decision Criteria**
*   **Build Gallery:** Only if interviews reveal users are *already* creating templates externally or begging for a way to share what they *would* create if the tool were easier.
*   **Pivot/Stop:** If users express satisfaction with pre-built options or indicate they have no desire to design layouts, we cancel the gallery and reallocate the $8,000 to expanding the pre-built library or fixing the creator tool's usability.

***

*Note: Do not ask users "Would you use a gallery?" as this invites false positives. Focus entirely on their past actions and current workarounds.*
*Next step: Schedule these 10 interviews within 48 hours; no code deployment until findings are synthesized.*
