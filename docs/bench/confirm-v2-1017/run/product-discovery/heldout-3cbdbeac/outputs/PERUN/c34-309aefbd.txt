**Assessment: UI Redesign Alone Is Insufficient; Root Cause Unverified**

Proceeding with only the pricing page UI update carries high risk. Your current evidence confirms *symptoms* (confusion, billing inquiries) but does not distinguish between **presentation failure** (users can't understand the value) and **value failure** (users understand the price but cannot afford it or don't believe it's worth it). In an economically volatile region, "confusing pricing" is often a polite proxy for "too expensive" or "unpredictable costs."

**Critical Data Gaps Before Committing Engineering Resources**

To validate whether a UI fix will work or if a deeper strategy pivot is needed, you must gather the following evidence immediately:

1.  **Segmented Churn by Plan Tier & Tenure**
    *   *Why:* If churn is concentrated in the lowest tier or among customers <6 months old, it suggests price sensitivity (affordability). If churn is high across all tiers or among long-term users, it suggests a value mismatch or specific feature gaps, not just confusion.
    *   *Action:* Break down the 5.5% churn rate by plan level and customer age.

2.  **Win/Loss Analysis on "Price" vs. "Features"**
    *   *Why:* Exit surveys are lagging indicators. You need to know what *new* prospects are saying. Are lost deals citing "competitors are cheaper" (market price issue) or "couldn't figure out which plan fits" (presentation issue)?
    *   *Action:* Interview 5–10 sales losses from the last quarter who cited price. Ask specifically: "Did you understand what you were getting, or did you just think the number was too high?"

3.  **Support Ticket Sentiment & Resolution Analysis**
    *   *Why:* A spike in billing inquiries could mean users are confused (UI fix helps) or that they are shocked by overage fees/hidden costs (strategy fix needed).
    *   *Action:* Sample 20 recent billing tickets. Categorize them: "Where is this charge?" (transparency issue) vs. "I didn't expect to pay this much" (pricing model issue). If the latter dominates, a UI tweak will not stop churn.

4.  **The "Very Disappointed" Baseline**
    *   *Why:* If users wouldn't be "very disappointed" without VendaFacil, no amount of pricing clarity will retain them. The product may simply not be solving a critical job.
    *   *Action:* Run a quick PMF survey with current active users. If <40% say they would be "very disappointed" to lose the product, the root cause is product-market fit, not the pricing page.

**Recommendation: The Riskiest Assumption Gate**

Your assumption is: *"Simplifying descriptions will reduce churn to baseline."*
*   **Risk:** High. If the real driver is economic inability to pay, a clearer page will only make the "too expensive" realization happen faster, potentially *increasing* churn.
*   **Cheapest Test (Run before May 10):** Conduct a "Concierge Pricing" test with 10 at-risk customers. Manually walk them through the *current* pricing page via screen share. Ask them to calculate their expected bill.
    *   *Kill Threshold:* If >5 of 10 can calculate the cost correctly but still say "it's too much," **stop the UI redesign**. The issue is price point/strategy, not UI.
    *   *Go Signal:* If >7 of 10 cannot calculate the cost or express shock at hidden fees, proceed with the UI redesign.

**Verdict:** Do not approve the full engineering redesign yet. Run the concierge test and segment the churn data first. If the data points to affordability, a UI update is a waste of resources; you need a pricing strategy review (e.g., localized pricing, simplified tiers, or usage caps).
