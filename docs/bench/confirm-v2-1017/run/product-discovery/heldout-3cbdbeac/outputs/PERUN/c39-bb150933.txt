### Assessment: Video Hosting Investment & Unit Economics

**Verdict: Do not build the adaptive bitrate streaming tool yet.**
Current evidence indicates that video resolution is not the primary driver of engagement or completion. Investing $15,000 to optimize a feature that yielded only a 4% engagement lift despite a 210% cost spike violates basic unit economics. The riskiest assumption—that "higher resolution causes better learning outcomes"—remains untested and currently looks false based on your March data.

#### 1. Financial Implications

| Scenario | Immediate Cost | Monthly Run Rate | Projected Annual Impact | Value Signal |
| :--- | :--- | :--- | :--- :--- |
| **Status Quo (4K Direct Upload)** | $0 | $4,500 (+$3,150 vs baseline) | +$37,800/year burn | **Negative:** 4% engagement gain does not justify 210% cost increase. Churn risk if prices rise to cover costs. |
| **Build Adaptive Bitrate Tool** | $15,000 (Dev) | Unknown (Likely lower bandwidth, higher maintenance) | +$15k upfront + ongoing dev debt | **Unvalidated:** Solves a bandwidth problem, not a value problem. If students don't watch 4K, they likely won't notice adaptive streaming. |
| **Recommended Pivot (Cap & Test)** | ~$0 (Policy change) | Drop to baseline (~$1,350 est.) | Save ~$37k/year | **Positive:** Forces validation of whether quality actually matters before optimizing delivery. |

**Analysis:**
Your current Cost-to-Value ratio is broken. You are paying a premium for a signal (4K) that users are ignoring. Building the engineering lead's tool optimizes the *delivery* of a value proposition that hasn't been proven. Before spending $15,000, you must prove that video quality correlates with completion. If capping uploads to 1080p reduces costs by 60% but leaves engagement flat (or drops it only slightly), the 4K feature was waste, not an asset.

#### 2. The Riskiest Assumption Gate
*   **Assumption:** "Instructors/Students believe 4K resolution significantly improves learning outcomes/completion rates."
*   **Current Evidence:** Contradictory. Massive cost increase yielded negligible engagement lift.
*   **Cheapest Test (Do this before coding):**
    1.  **Action:** Immediately cap new uploads to 1080p. Notify existing users that "to improve platform stability, we are optimizing video formats."
    2.  **Metric:** Track **Course Completion Rate** and **Support Tickets** regarding video quality over 30 days.
    3.  **Kill Threshold:** If completion rates drop by >2% OR support tickets regarding "blurry video" exceed 5% of active users, *then* reconsider high-res investment. If metrics stay flat, the 4K feature is a non-goal.

#### 3. Recommended Metric for Validation
Stop tracking "Bandwidth Consumed" or "Resolution Uploaded." Start tracking:
**"Completion Rate Delta by Device/Connection Speed"**
*   **Why:** If 4K matters, it should matter most on large screens/high-speed connections. If completion rates are identical across 1080p and 4K viewers, resolution is irrelevant to learning outcomes.
*   **How to obtain:** Segment your existing March data. Compare completion rates of users who watched >50% of a lecture in 4K vs. those who watched in lower resolutions (if available) or compare cohorts before/after the March 1st launch.

**Investor Narrative:**
"We identified a unit economics leak where premium video features drove costs up 210% without moving completion needles. Instead of sinking $15k into optimization, we are running a lean validation test to isolate the true driver of learning outcomes. We expect to reduce monthly burn by ~$3k immediately while validating if 'quality' is a real retention lever or a vanity metric."
