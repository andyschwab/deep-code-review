### Executive Summary: Evidence Gaps for Board Advisors

The recent accessibility audit provides a **valid problem signal** (12/15 participants struggled with navigation) but **insufficient solution validation** to justify a full pivot to voice infrastructure. The audit confirms *that* motor-impaired users face barriers; it does not prove that *voice* is the correct, desired, or viable solution for your specific office-worker demographic. Committing engineering resources now risks building a "compliance feature" that fails in real-world office environments due to noise, privacy concerns, or workflow mismatch.

**Critical Evidence Gaps to Close Before Sprint Commitment:**

1.  **Solution Fit vs. Problem Fit:**
    *   **Gap:** The audit identified the friction (complex forms), not the preferred resolution. Motor impairments vary widely; some users may prefer keyboard shortcuts, switch devices, or simplified UI over voice.
    *   **Required Data:** Conduct 5–7 "switch interviews" with the 12 struggling participants. Ask specifically about their current workarounds and what tools they already pay for or use. Do not pitch voice; ask what would make the task possible *today*.
    *   **Decision Rule:** If fewer than 4 of 7 participants explicitly cite voice as their primary missing tool (vs. other aids), voice is not the default answer.

2.  **Environmental Viability (The "Office" Context):**
    *   **Gap:** Your core user base works in offices. Voice interfaces often fail in open-plan environments due to background noise and privacy constraints (users may not want to speak sensitive data aloud).
    *   **Required Data:** A "fake-door" or concierge test. Instead of building infrastructure, simulate voice commands manually for 3 users in their actual office setting. Measure success rate and user comfort.
    *   **Decision Rule:** If users hesitate to use voice due to privacy or noise in >50% of tasks, the solution is viable only for niche use cases, not a roadmap pivot.

3.  **Market Expansion Validation:**
    *   **Gap:** The assumption that voice expands Total Addressable Market (TAM) is unverified. You need to know if the *new* market segment (motor-impaired office workers) is large enough to sustain the infrastructure cost.
    *   **Required Data:** Do not estimate TAM internally. Route this to external market research or disability advocacy groups to size the specific segment of office workers with motor impairments who *currently* cannot use your product but *would* adopt it with voice.
    *   **Decision Rule:** Without a sourced market size figure, treat TAM expansion as a hypothesis, not a business fact.

**Immediate Next Steps for the Design Sprint:**
Do not start building voice infrastructure. Dedicate the sprint to **designing the cheapest disconfirming test**. Define a numeric kill threshold: e.g., "If we cannot get 5 users to successfully complete a form via a manual 'Wizard-of-Oz' voice simulation within 2 minutes, we pause infrastructure investment."

**Note:** The audit is a starting point for inquiry, not a green light for build.
**Note:** Prioritize behavioral evidence (what users do in their office) over stated intent (what they say in a survey).
