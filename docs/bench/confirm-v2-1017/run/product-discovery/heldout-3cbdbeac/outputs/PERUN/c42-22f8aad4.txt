**Advisory Board Summary: NordShop Recommendation Engine Pivot**

**Current State & Verdict**
The personalized recommendation engine is currently destroying value. Despite a target of 3.5% click-through rate (CTR), performance stagnates at 0.8%. More critically, the feature correlates with a 15% drop in session duration, indicating active user friction rather than passive ignoring. The hypothesis that "users lack discovery options" is contradicted by behavioral data; users are present but disengaging faster when recommendations appear.

**Recommendation:** Do not retrain the model. Doubling down on algorithmic logic assumes the problem is accuracy, but the evidence suggests the problem is **intrusiveness** or **irrelevance of placement**. Retraining on historical data without fixing the user experience will likely optimize for a metric users are already rejecting. The immediate action is to treat the current placement as a failed experiment and either relocate the feature or remove it to restore baseline session duration.

**Opportunity Cost Analysis**
Continuing to optimize the current logic incurs three specific costs against your $20,000 experimentation budget:
1.  **Retention Erosion:** Every day the feature remains on the home screen, you risk normalizing the 15% session drop, potentially causing permanent churn that no algorithm update can fix.
2.  **Misallocated Capital:** Spending the remaining budget on data science hours to retrain a model addresses a symptom (low CTR) while ignoring the disease (user annoyance). This delays investment in high-impact areas like checkout flow or search relevance.
3.  **Signal Contamination:** Keeping a friction-causing feature live pollutes all other A/B tests running on the home screen, making it impossible to accurately measure the impact of other initiatives.

**Discovery Plan: Who to Interview**
To validate whether this is a placement issue or a fundamental lack of trust in suggestions, conduct "Mom Test" style interviews focusing on past behavior, not future opinions. Do not ask "Do you like recommendations?" Instead, investigate specific recent sessions.

**Target Segments for Interviews:**
1.  **The "Bouncers" (High Priority):** Users whose session duration dropped most sharply (bottom 20% of time-on-site) immediately after the launch.
    *   * Inquiry Focus:* Walk them through their last visit. Ask what they were looking for specifically when they left. Did they see the recommendations? What did they do immediately after seeing them?
2.  **The "Scroll-Past" Cohort:** Users who visit frequently but have a 0% interaction rate with the recommendation module.
    *   *Inquiry Focus:* Ask about the last time they bought something similar to what was recommended. How did they find it? (Search? Category? External link?) This reveals their actual discovery habit.
3.  **The "Cart Abandoners" Post-Exposure:** Users who added items to the cart but left after scrolling past the recommendation section.
    *   *Inquiry Focus:* Investigate if the recommendations caused decision paralysis or distracted them from their original intent.

**Next Step Decision Rule**
Allocate $2,000 of the budget to run a "removal test" (A/B test where 50% of users see the home page *without* recommendations). If the control group (no recommendations) shows a statistically significant recovery in session duration and conversion, the feature placement is confirmed as the root cause. Only then consider redesigning the module for a less intrusive location (e.g., post-purchase or deep within product details) rather than retraining the core logic.

*Note: Do not proceed with model retraining until the placement hypothesis is falsified via the removal test.*
*All interview findings must be recorded as direct quotes and observed behaviors, not summarized sentiments.*
