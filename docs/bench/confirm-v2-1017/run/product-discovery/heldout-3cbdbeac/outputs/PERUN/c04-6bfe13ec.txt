**BRIEFING: Roadmap Prioritization for US Government Contract Renewal**

**Decision Recommendation**
Allocate 100% of immediate sprint capacity to resolving the twenty-three identified accessibility violations. Defer all development of the new advanced analytics features until the accessibility remediation is verified complete.

**Rationale: Contractual Risk Minimization**
1.  **Compliance is Binary; Features are Discretionary:** The twenty-three accessibility violations represent a confirmed breach of federal compliance standards (likely Section 508). In government contracting, failure to meet mandatory compliance criteria often constitutes a material breach, providing the agency legal grounds to terminate the contract or withhold payment regardless of other delivered value. Conversely, the request for "advanced analytics" is a functional enhancement. Unless explicitly codified as a mandatory deliverable for the November 1 renewal milestone in the current contract language, partial or delayed delivery of new features carries significantly lower legal risk than known non-compliance.
2.  **The "Partial Delivery" Assumption is Unvalidated:** The current plan assumes the agency will accept a "minimal version" of analytics alongside unresolved compliance issues. This is a high-risk assumption. Government evaluators typically score proposals using rigid rubrics where mandatory compliance items are "go/no-go" gates. A proposal with outstanding violations is often disqualified before feature sets are even reviewed. Without explicit written confirmation from the contracting officer that accessibility defects will be tolerated in exchange for early analytics, proceeding on this assumption jeopardizes the $2.5M annual revenue.
3.  **Timeline Fragility:** The four-week estimate for accessibility fixes leaves zero buffer for testing, verification, or unexpected complexity. Diverting any resources to analytics increases the probability of missing the November 1 deadline for the *mandatory* fixes. Missing the compliance deadline guarantees failure; missing the feature deadline creates negotiation leverage but preserves the contract base.

**Required Immediate Actions**
*   **Stop Work on Analytics:** Halt all design and development on the analytics dashboard immediately.
*   **Validate the Assumption:** Before any code is written for analytics, the Product Lead must obtain written clarification from the agency contracting officer regarding the weighting of "strict compliance" vs. "new functionality" for the renewal decision. Do not rely on verbal cues from client contacts; require formal guidance.
*   **Resource Reallocation:** Move all developers assigned to analytics to the accessibility backlog. The definition of "done" for this sprint cycle is the closure of all twenty-three violations.

**Risk Assessment Summary**
*   **Path A (Current Plan - Split Focus):** High Risk. Potential for material breach due to unresolved violations. High probability of renewal denial.
*   **Path B (Recommended - Compliance First):** Low Risk. Ensures mandatory gate criteria are met. Maximizes probability of renewal, even if new features are deferred to a post-renewal modification.

***

*Note: This recommendation treats the accessibility violations as confirmed facts and the analytics request as unvalidated scope based on the input provided. No market research or user interviews were fabricated to support this conclusion.*
