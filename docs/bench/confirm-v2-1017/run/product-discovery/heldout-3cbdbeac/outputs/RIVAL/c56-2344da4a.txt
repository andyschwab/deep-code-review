### Recommendation: Iterate Before Pivoting

Based on the evidence provided, **do not pivot away from gamification yet.** The current data is insufficient to declare a fundamental mismatch between your Scandinavian demographic and digital rewards. Instead, you should **iterate on the badge mechanics and value proposition** before abandoning the strategy.

**Reasoning:**
Your assumption that users care more about personal health metrics than social recognition is likely a false dichotomy. In fitness, these two drivers often work together: health metrics are the *input*, and recognition (badges) is the *validation* of that progress. The flat 2% engagement rate after only one month with "basic" badges suggests a **Usability** or **Value Perception** issue, not necessarily a total rejection of the concept.

The low engagement is more likely caused by:
1.  **Irrelevant Triggers:** The three basic badges may not align with the specific goals your users are already chasing (e.g., consistency vs. intensity).
2.  **Lack of Visibility:** Users may not understand how to earn them or why they matter.
3.  **Design Friction:** "Basic" designs may fail to trigger the dopamine response required for habit formation.

Scandinavian markets often value understated achievement and personal mastery over loud social competition, but this does not mean they are immune to gamification. It means the *type* of gamification must shift from "social showing off" to "personal mastery tracking."

**Immediate Next Steps for Q3 Roadmap:**
1.  **Qualitative Discovery:** Interview 5–10 users who earned a badge and 5–10 who did not. Ask about their reaction to the badge, not if they "like gamification." Determine if they even noticed the feature.
2.  **A/B Test Value Propositions:** Instead of new designs, test different triggers. Compare a "Streak" badge (consistency) against a "Personal Best" badge (metric-focused) to see which resonates with your hypothesis about health metrics.
3.  **Integration Check:** Ensure badges are surfaced immediately after a workout when the emotional high is highest, rather than buried in a profile tab.

**Verdict:**
Invest limited engineering hours in **targeted iterations** (better triggers, metric-aligned rewards) for 4–6 weeks. If engagement remains flat after aligning the rewards with personal health milestones, *then* consider pivoting to a non-gamified retention strategy.

---
*Note: This advice assumes the 2% figure represents users interacting with badges, not just viewing them. Ensure your tracking distinguishes between "earned" and "viewed."*
