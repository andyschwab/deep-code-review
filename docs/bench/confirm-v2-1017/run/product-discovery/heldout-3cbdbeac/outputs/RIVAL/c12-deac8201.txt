# Content Strategy Evaluation: Nairobi GrowTech Pilot Analysis

## Executive Summary
The pilot program launched in January revealed a critical engagement failure: only 12% of 300 enrolled farmers watched beyond the first three minutes of video content, despite mobile data subsidies. While the initial hypothesis attributes this to video length, evidence suggests the root cause may be a misalignment between content relevance and the immediate needs of the current planting season. This evaluation compares the cost-effectiveness of producing new short-form video assets against pivoting to text-based guides or audio messages for the June harvest cycle.

## Problem Diagnosis
The low completion rate (12%) indicates a barrier to value delivery. Two primary hypotheses exist:
1.  **Format Friction:** Videos are too long for the farmers' context (bandwidth constraints, attention spans, or device limitations).
2.  **Relevance Gap:** The agricultural advice provided does not match the specific timing or crop cycles of the farmers, rendering the content useless regardless of format.

Testing the "length" assumption by simply chopping videos into 30-second clips risks investing in high-production assets that still fail to address the core lack of relevance. If the advice is untimely, a 30-second video will be ignored just as quickly as a 10-minute one.

## Comparative Cost-Effectiveness Analysis

### Option A: Short-Form Video Series (30-second clips)
*   **Production Cost:** High. Requires scripting, filming, editing, and potentially reshooting for different crops/regions.
*   **Data Usage:** Moderate. Even short clips consume more data than text/audio, potentially straining users without subsidies.
*   **Agility:** Low. Updating video content for changing weather patterns or pest outbreaks is slow and expensive.
*   **Risk:** High. If relevance is the true barrier, this approach wastes budget on polished but unused content.

### Option B: Text-Based Guides (SMS/USSD/WhatsApp)
*   **Production Cost:** Low. Can be drafted by agronomists and distributed instantly.
*   **Data Usage:** Negligible. Accessible on basic feature phones; works well with low connectivity.
*   **Agility:** High. Content can be updated daily based on local conditions.
*   **Risk:** Moderate. Requires literacy and may lack the visual demonstration needed for complex techniques.

### Option C: Audio Messages (Voice Notes/Radio integration)
*   **Production Cost:** Low-Moderate. Requires recording but minimal editing; can leverage local dialects easily.
*   **Data Usage:** Low. Compressed audio files are significantly smaller than video.
*   **Agility:** High. Rapid recording and distribution possible.
*   **Risk:** Low. Aligns well with oral traditions and allows for hands-free consumption while farming.

## Strategic Recommendation
**Do not proceed with a full-scale video production pivot yet.** The data suggests a "Relevance Gap" is more likely the primary blocker than format length. Investing heavily in new video assets (even short ones) before validating content relevance is financially imprudent for both the startup and impact investors.

**Proposed Roadmap for June Harvest Cycle:**
1.  **Immediate Pivot to Audio/Text:** Reallocate 70% of the video budget to producing localized audio messages and SMS alerts. These formats offer the highest cost-effectiveness, lowest data burden, and fastest iteration speed.
2.  **Validate Relevance First:** Use these low-cost channels to test specific agricultural advice. Track engagement rates. If farmers engage with the *advice* via audio/text, the relevance hypothesis is confirmed.
3.  **Targeted Video Experiments:** Only produce short video clips for specific, high-complexity tasks where visual demonstration is proven necessary (e.g., pruning techniques), and only after the underlying advice has been validated as relevant through audio/text channels.

This approach minimizes sunk costs, maximizes reach across device types, and ensures the June harvest cycle delivers timely, actionable value to farmers.

---
*Note: This strategy prioritizes validated learning over asset production to ensure investor capital drives actual farmer outcomes.*
