### Recommendation: Prioritize Single-Entry Interface Refinement

**Executive Summary**
While bulk CSV upload addresses the speed complaint for half our enterprise clients, refining the single-entry interface offers a more immediate and guaranteed reduction in the 30% error rate driving our support costs. Building bulk upload first introduces high risk of data formatting failures without solving the root cause of entry errors, which may simply shift from manual typos to CSV mapping errors.

**Impact Comparison & Time Savings Calculation**

*Current Baseline:*
- **Volume:** 150 error incidents/month (Feb data).
- **Cost of Error:** 30% of total support tickets.
- **Current Speed:** 4 minutes per item.

*Scenario A: Build Bulk CSV Upload*
- **Speed Impact:** Potentially reduces entry time from 4 minutes/item to seconds per batch. If a manager uploads 50 items, time drops from 200 minutes to ~5 minutes (97% speed gain).
- **Error Impact:** Uncertain. While it eliminates manual typing typos, it introduces parsing errors, header mismatches, and formatting issues. If 20% of bulk uploads fail due to formatting, we may see a spike in "upload failed" tickets rather than a net decrease in the current 150 incidents.
- **Risk:** High dependency on user data hygiene.

*Scenario B: Refine Single-Entry Interface*
- **Speed Impact:** Conservative estimate of 25% improvement via auto-complete, dropdowns, and validation. New time: 3 minutes per item.
  - *Time Saved:* 1 minute per item. For a typical batch of 50 items, this saves 50 minutes per session.
- **Error Impact:** High confidence. Real-time validation and constrained inputs (dropdowns vs. free text) directly target the source of the 30% error rate.
  - *Projected Reduction:* If we cut errors by 50%, we eliminate 75 support tickets/month immediately.
- **Risk:** Low. Changes are contained within the existing, stable workflow.

**Recommendation**
Proceed with **Scenario B (Refine Single-Entry)**. The 150 monthly error incidents represent a known, bleeding cost. Fixing the interface guarantees a reduction in these tickets and provides a moderate speed boost. Bulk upload should be a Phase 2 initiative, undertaken only after we understand the specific data structures our clients use to ensure the tool doesn't generate new categories of support tickets.

**Unknown Variables Regarding Bulk Upload Data Formatting**
Before building bulk upload, we must validate the following unknowns which could cause failure:
1.  **Header Consistency:** Do clients use consistent column headers, or will every client require a custom mapping template?
2.  **Data Cleanliness:** How often do source systems export special characters, leading/trailing spaces, or non-standard date formats that will break our parser?
3.  **Partial Failures:** If a 1,000-row CSV has 5 errors, does the whole batch reject, or do we create partial inventory states that confuse users?
4.  **Validation Feedback:** Can users easily identify which specific row caused an error in a large file, or will they abandon the tool in frustration?
5.  **Version Control:** How do we handle clients uploading outdated CSV templates if our schema changes?

*Note: This recommendation assumes the 150 incidents are purely data-entry related. If "slow entry" is causing users to abandon tasks entirely (lost revenue), the speed argument for bulk upload strengthens.*
