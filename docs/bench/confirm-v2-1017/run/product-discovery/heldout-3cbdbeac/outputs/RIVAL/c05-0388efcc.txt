### Critical Questions for Strategy Workshop

**Challenge the Data Point (The "One Vendor" Trap)**
*   Is this vendor's enthusiasm driven by a unique internal need (e.g., legacy system migration) that other sellers do not share?
*   If this vendor represents 15% of volume, does their behavior correlate with the remaining 85%, or are they an outlier in size and sophistication?
*   What specific friction in their current workflow does a "marketplace" solve that our direct sales model does not?

**Validate Broader Demand (Beyond Opinions)**
*   Have any other sellers explicitly asked for a multi-vendor environment in the last 12 months?
*   Can we identify three other sellers who would onboard *before* June 30 without a finished feature, perhaps via a manual concierge process?
*   What evidence exists that buyers want to compare multiple vendors on our platform, rather than buying specific items from us directly?

**Assess Cannibalization & Economics**
*   If this vendor onboarded 50 suppliers, how much of that $200k GMV is *new* demand versus shifting existing transactions to a lower-margin marketplace model?
*   What is the cost of supporting 50 new supplier accounts (onboarding, disputes, payouts) compared to our current cost of goods sold?
*   Does introducing third-party sellers dilute our brand promise of quality control or delivery speed in Akihabara?

**Test Feasibility & Timing**
*   Can we manually facilitate the connection between this vendor and their 50 suppliers using existing tools (email, spreadsheets) to validate the workflow before coding?
*   If we miss the June 30 deadline, will the vendor withdraw their commitment? If so, is the risk too high to proceed?

### Decision Framework: The "Marketplace Readiness" Matrix

Use this 2x2 matrix to plot the findings from your discovery work. Do not commit to development unless you land in the top-right quadrant.

**Axis X: Validated Seller Demand**
*   *Low:* Only the champion vendor is interested; others are indifferent or confused.
*   *High:* Multiple sellers have expressed pain points; at least 2-3 competitors are willing to pilot a manual version.

**Axis Y: Net Positive Unit Economics**
*   *Low:* High risk of cannibalizing direct sales; support costs outweigh marketplace fees; margin erosion likely.
*   *High:* Clear path to new GMV without sacrificing core margins; operational costs scale efficiently.

**Quadrant Actions:**
1.  **Low Demand / Low Economics:** **Kill the idea.** The vendor is an outlier; building this will drain resources.
2.  **High Demand / Low Economics:** **Pause & Redesign.** The need is real, but the business model is broken. Explore alternative monetization or limited pilots.
3.  **Low Demand / High Economics:** **Wait.** The math works, but the market isn't ready. Focus on education or wait for a regulatory/seasonal trigger.
4.  **High Demand / High Economics:** **Build (with constraints).** Proceed with a Minimum Viable Marketplace (MVM), potentially starting with a manual "concierge" backend to verify operations before automating.

**Immediate Next Step:** Before the workshop, attempt to manually onboard *one* of the vendor's suppliers using current tools. If you cannot make that single transaction happen smoothly without code, the full platform will likely fail.

*Note: Relying on a single vendor's promise is a classic "false positive" in product discovery; their incentives (offloading inventory management) may not align with yours (platform growth).*
