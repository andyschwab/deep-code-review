### Strategic Recommendation: Negotiate a Phased Delivery

**Do not commit your entire engineering team to a six-week custom build immediately.** While the client represents 40% of revenue, halting all other development creates a single point of failure for your product's future (European expansion) and validates a dangerous dependency on one customer's timeline.

Instead, propose a **Phased Delivery Plan** that secures the client's fiscal year deadline without derailing your roadmap:

1.  **Immediate "Concierge" Solution (Weeks 1-2):**
    Offer to manually generate multi-currency invoices for them via a secure portal or PDF export while the engineering team builds the core architecture. This solves their immediate compliance need for March 31 without requiring full platform integration. Frame this as a "white-glove service" exclusive to their enterprise tier.

2.  **Core Architecture Sprint (Weeks 3-6):**
    Dedicate only 50% of engineering capacity to building a scalable, multi-currency *foundation* (database schema updates, exchange rate API integration) rather than a full UI rollout for five specific currencies. This ensures you are building for your European expansion (generalizability) rather than hard-coding a custom fix for one client.

3.  **Full Rollout (Post-March 31):**
    Complete the UI and specific currency configurations in April. Since their fiscal year ends March 31, they likely need the *capability* to report in those currencies by that date, not necessarily a fully automated self-service flow on day one. Verify this distinction; it is often the key to negotiating time.

### Risk Analysis

*   **Concentration Risk:** If you build a custom solution solely for this client, you increase technical debt. If this client churns next year, you are left with complex code no one else uses. By building a generalizable foundation now, you mitigate the risk of "dead code."
*   **Opportunity Cost:** Stopping all other improvements for six weeks delays your European entry. If the market window for Europe is tight, the cost of delay may exceed the revenue risk of upsetting this one client, especially if you offer the manual workaround.
*   **Precedent Setting:** Delivering a rushed, custom feature under pressure sets a precedent that urgent client demands override your product strategy. This encourages future scope creep.

### Action Plan for Co-Founders

1.  **Validate the "Hard" Deadline:** Ask the client specifically: "What happens if the automated feature isn't live by March 31? Do you need the data exported in these currencies, or does the system itself need to process them?" Their answer will dictate if the manual workaround is acceptable.
2.  **Reframe the Conversation:** Present the phased approach as a risk-mitigation strategy for *them*. Explain that a rushed full build increases the chance of billing errors during their critical fiscal close. A controlled, phased rollout ensures accuracy.
3.  **Resource Allocation:** Assign one senior engineer to design the scalable architecture (benefiting future European plans) and one mid-level engineer to handle the manual data exports. Keep the rest of the team on the core roadmap.

By choosing negotiation over immediate full commitment, you protect your long-term viability while still supporting your most important customer.

*Note: Ensure any manual process includes strict data privacy checks to maintain compliance while handling sensitive financial data outside the main platform.*
