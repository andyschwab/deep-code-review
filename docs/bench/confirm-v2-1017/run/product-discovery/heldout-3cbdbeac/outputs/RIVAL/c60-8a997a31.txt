### Recommendation: Implement Advanced Search Filters with Demographic Safeguards

Based on the user testing data from May 5th, the evidence points strongly toward implementing **advanced search filters** rather than enhancing sorting options.

**Analysis of Evidence**
The core problem identified is a **discovery failure**, not a ranking failure. Thirty out of 50 participants (60%) could not locate specific product variants within ten seconds. Sorting algorithms rearrange existing lists based on criteria like price or popularity; they do not reduce the dataset to show only specific variants (e.g., "Size 12," "Red," "Organic"). Since users are struggling to *find* specific items amidst a large catalog, filtering is the only mechanism that directly addresses the root cause by narrowing the result set. Enhancing sorting would likely fail to improve the "time-to-find" metric because users would still have to scan irrelevant items to locate their specific variant.

**Addressing the Demographic Risk**
Your assumption that filters solve the issue faster is correct regarding efficiency, but your concern about the older demographic (40% of revenue) being overwhelmed is a valid **Usability Risk**. If this group abandons the site due to interface complexity, the net conversion gain will be negative.

Therefore, the recommendation is not just to "add filters," but to implement **progressive disclosure filtering**:
1.  **Default State:** Show only the top 3 most critical filters (e.g., Price, Size, Brand) expanded.
2.  **Secondary State:** Place remaining filters behind a "More Filters" button or accordion.
3.  **Visual Clarity:** Use large click targets and clear labels, avoiding dense checkboxes that confuse users with lower digital literacy.

**Strategic Advice for Stakeholders**
*   **For the UX Team:** Do not build a static sidebar with 20 open checkboxes. Design a simplified, progressive filter interface. Conduct a rapid A/B test specifically with users over 60 to validate that the simplified view reduces cognitive load while maintaining findability.
*   **For the CEO:** The data indicates a 60% failure rate in product discovery, which is a direct revenue leak. While sorting improvements are cheaper, they address the wrong problem. Investing in a well-designed filtering system addresses the immediate bottleneck. The risk to the older demographic is manageable through design patterns, whereas ignoring the discovery issue guarantees continued friction for all users.

**Conclusion**
Proceed with **advanced search filters**. Sorting optimization should be deprioritized until the fundamental ability to narrow down product variants is resolved.

*Note: Ensure the "More Filters" interaction is tested on mobile devices, as older demographics increasingly use tablets for shopping.*
