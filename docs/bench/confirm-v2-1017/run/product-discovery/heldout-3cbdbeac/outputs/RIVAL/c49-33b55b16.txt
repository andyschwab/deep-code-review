### Strategic Risk & Resource Implication Analysis: Build vs. Buy

**Option A: Build Internal Distribution Tools**
*Strategic Premise:* Prioritize long-term control and differentiated onboarding speed.

*   **Resource Implications:**
    *   **Upfront Capital:** Consumes the full $150,000 budget immediately.
    *   **Engineering Load:** Requires diverting significant hours from the 12-engineer team for two release cycles. This creates an opportunity cost, delaying other roadmap items.
    *   **Timeline:** Zero immediate impact; onboarding remains at four days until deployment.
*   **Strategic Risks:**
    *   **Maintenance Overhead:** The primary risk is that ongoing maintenance costs (bug fixes, API updates, scaling) exceed the initial speed gains after Year 1, turning a strategic asset into a liability.
    *   **Execution Failure:** The assumption that onboarding will drop to <24 hours may not materialize if edge cases in retailer data require manual intervention anyway.
    *   **Technical Debt:** Rushing to meet the "two release cycle" goal may introduce instability requiring future refactoring.
*   **Potential Upside:** Full ownership of the user experience, no recurring licensing fees, and a defensible competitive moat if the tool becomes superior to market standards.

**Option B: Integrate Third-Party Marketplace**
*Strategic Premise:* Prioritize immediate speed to market and cost efficiency.

*   **Resource Implications:**
    *   **Upfront Capital:** Requires only $20,000 annually, leaving $130,000 of the budget available for other initiatives.
    *   **Engineering Load:** Minimal development time required for integration; team remains focused on core product features.
    *   **Timeline:** Near-immediate deployment; could capture the three lost contracts in the current quarter.
*   **Strategic Risks:**
    *   **Vendor Lock-in:** Dependence on a third party's roadmap, pricing changes, and uptime. If they pivot or raise prices, your margins and features are held hostage.
    *   **Commoditization:** If competitors use the same tool, onboarding speed ceases to be a differentiator.
    *   **Integration Limits:** The tool may never achieve the <24-hour target if the third-party API has inherent latency or rigid workflows.
*   **Potential Upside:** Instant reduction in sales friction, predictable operating expenses, and reduced burden on internal engineering resources.

**Critical Unknowns for Board Consideration**
1.  **Maintenance Ratio:** What is the estimated annual engineering cost to maintain the custom build versus the $20k license fee? At what point does the custom solution become more expensive than the vendor?
2.  **Differentiation Lifespan:** How long will "fast onboarding" remain a key decision factor for retailers before it becomes an industry standard expectation?
3.  **Opportunity Cost:** If the engineering team builds this tool, what high-value features are being delayed, and what is the revenue impact of those delays?

**Next Steps for Approval**
To finalize the Q3 roadmap, the team must validate the maintenance cost assumption for the "Build" scenario and confirm the SLA guarantees for the "Buy" scenario. Both paths address the four-day bottleneck but carry distinct long-term trade-offs regarding control versus operational simplicity.

*Note: This analysis isolates risks and resource impacts without prescribing a solution to allow the Board to weigh risk tolerance against strategic goals.*
*Recommendation: Validate the maintenance cost assumption with a technical spike before final commitment.*
