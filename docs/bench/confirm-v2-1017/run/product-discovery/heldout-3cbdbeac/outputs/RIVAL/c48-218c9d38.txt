### Executive Summary: Data Recovery Feature Proposal

**Situation Analysis**
Over the past month, DataSafe Corp has recorded four distinct enterprise support incidents involving accidental bulk data deletions. These events required fifteen hours of manual engineering intervention for restoration and caused significant customer distress. While these incidents highlight a critical pain point for affected clients, the sample size (four tickets) is statistically small relative to the broader enterprise base. Currently, there is no telemetry data to indicate the frequency of "near-miss" events or minor revert needs, making it impossible to determine if these four cases represent a widespread systemic issue or isolated user errors.

The proposed solution is a comprehensive version history tool enabling 30-day rollbacks. The underlying assumption is that this feature would eliminate such support tickets entirely. However, without data on how often users actually need to revert changes beyond simple "undo" actions, we cannot validate the demand for a full 30-day history versus a simpler, short-term safety net.

**Recommendation**
**Do not commit to building the full 30-day version history tool for the November 5th release at this time.**

Proceeding immediately carries high risk for two reasons:
1.  **Unvalidated Scope:** We are assuming users need 30-day history when they may only need a 24-hour "trash bin" or a confirmed "bulk delete" warning. Building the full feature may be over-engineering based on anecdotal evidence.
2.  **Lack of Baseline:** Without telemetry, we cannot measure the success of the feature post-launch or confirm the frequency of the problem.

**Proposed Action Plan**
Instead of full development, execute the following lightweight discovery and mitigation steps before the November 5th deadline:

*   **Immediate Mitigation (High Priority):** Implement a low-cost UI guardrail immediately. Add a mandatory confirmation modal with a typed verification phrase for any bulk deletion action. This addresses the root cause (unintended action) without complex backend engineering.
*   **Rapid Discovery (1 Week):** Conduct five targeted interviews with the specific customers who filed tickets to understand their workflow leading to the deletion. Ask: "What were you trying to achieve?" and "How would you have preferred to recover?"
*   **Telemetry Implementation:** Instrument tracking for "deleted item count" and "support requests related to data loss" to establish a baseline.
*   **Re-evaluation:** Once qualitative data and initial telemetry are gathered (post-November 5th), reassess whether a full version history tool is justified or if a simpler "Recycle Bin" (7-day retention) suffices.

This approach protects the customer experience immediately via guardrails while preventing significant engineering spend on an unvalidated assumption.

***

*This strategy shifts the focus from building a assumed solution to validating the actual scope of the problem.*
*The immediate UI guardrail can be deployed within the current sprint cycle with minimal engineering effort.*
