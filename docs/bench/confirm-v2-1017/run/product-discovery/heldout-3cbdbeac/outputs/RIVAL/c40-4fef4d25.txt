### Decision Framework: Mobile Editor Investment vs. Churn Risk

**1. The Core Conflict**
*   **Signal A (Qualitative):** 42 tickets, 12 explicit churn threats. Indicates high frustration among a vocal minority.
*   **Signal B (Quantitative):** 2% mobile open rate. Suggests low *recipient* mobile usage, but does not measure *sender* (marketer) mobile usage.
*   **The Gap:** We are conflating "reading on mobile" with "editing on mobile." The 2% metric is irrelevant to the hypothesis that marketers need to edit campaigns while away from their desks.

**2. Risk Assessment Matrix**
| Scenario | Probability | Impact | Decision Path |
| :--- | :--- | :--- | : |
| **Churn is Real & Mobile Editing is the Cause** | Medium | High (Loss of 12+ accounts + reputation) | **Build:** Prioritize the 3-week sprint. |
| **Churn is a Bluff / Issue is Different** | Medium | Low | **Pivot:** Address the root cause (e.g., template bugs, desktop UX) without building mobile editor. |
| **Demand is Niche** | High | Medium | **Defer:** Build a lightweight MVP or wait for more data; 3 weeks may be too costly for <5% of users. |

**3. Critical Evidence Needed**
Before committing the April 10th development window, we must validate:
*   **Sender Behavior:** What percentage of our *users* currently log in via mobile devices? (Current analytics likely track recipient opens, not sender sessions).
*   **Churn Verification:** Are the 12 threatening customers actually high-value accounts? What is their historical churn probability?
*   **Workflow Context:** Do users attempt to edit on mobile and fail (high drop-off rate on mobile login), or do they simply not try?

**4. Proposed Low-Effort Experiment: "The Fake Door" Test**
*   **Objective:** Measure actual intent to use mobile editing without writing code.
*   **Method:**
    1.  Add a "Edit on Mobile" button to the mobile web view of the dashboard for 50% of users.
    2.  When clicked, display a modal: *"Mobile editing is coming soon! Join the waitlist to be notified."*
    3.  Track the click-through rate (CTR) and waitlist sign-ups.
*   **Success Metric:** If >15% of mobile visitors click the button, demand is validated. If <2%, the vocal tickets represent an edge case, and churn threats may be mitigated by better communication rather than a build.
*   **Timeline:** Can be designed and implemented in 2 days, leaving 2.5 weeks for the actual build *only if* metrics justify it.

**Recommendation:** Do not start the full build on April 10th. Run the "Fake Door" experiment immediately. If validation fails, use the developer time to fix the specific desktop issues causing the frustration behind the tickets.
