### Financial Implications & Strategic Recommendation

**Current Status Quo Analysis**
Your current trajectory indicates a severe misalignment between cost and value. Since March 1st, a 210% spike in bandwidth costs ($4,500/month) has yielded only a 4% increase in engagement. This suggests that 4K resolution is not a primary driver of user behavior for your specific market in Mumbai.
*   **Annualized Burn:** At the current run rate, the 4K feature alone will cost **$54,000/year**.
*   **Value Gap:** The marginal 4% engagement lift does not justify a 210% cost increase. If course completion rates (your original hypothesis) have not moved proportionally, the feature is destroying unit economics.
*   **Investor Perspective:** Continuing this without correction signals poor capital efficiency. Investors will view the $4,500 monthly burn as a leak rather than an investment, especially if it doesn't correlate with retention or revenue.

**Proposal: Adaptive Bitrate Streaming (ABS) Tool**
Building the proposed ABS tool requires a **$15,000 upfront development cost**.
*   **The Trade-off:** ABS optimizes video quality based on the user's internet speed, delivering lower resolutions (e.g., 480p or 720p) when bandwidth is constrained, which is common in Mumbai. This would likely reduce your monthly bandwidth bill significantly, potentially returning it to pre-March levels.
*   **ROI Calculation:** If ABS reduces your monthly bandwidth cost from $4,500 back to ~$1,500 (a conservative estimate), you save $3,000/month. The $15,000 development cost would be recouped in **5 months**.
*   **Strategic Verdict:** Do **not** build the tool solely to enable *higher* quality. Build it (or a simpler alternative) to *reduce* costs while maintaining acceptable quality. However, before committing $15,000, verify if a simpler configuration change (e.g., capping uploads at 1080p by default) achieves 80% of the savings with 0% development cost.

**Recommendation**
1.  **Immediate Action:** Cap default upload resolution at 1080p immediately. This requires no engineering time and will likely slash bandwidth costs instantly without impacting the 4% engagement gain.
2.  **Secondary Action:** Re-evaluate the $15,000 ABS build only if 1080p capping causes user complaints or if engagement drops.
3.  **Investor Narrative:** Frame the 4K experiment as a successful "fail fast" discovery that identified a cost leak. Present the capping strategy as an immediate fix to restore unit economics, demonstrating fiscal discipline.

**Primary Metric to Track**
Stop tracking "Video Resolution" or raw "Engagement Time." Instead, track **Completion Rate per Bandwidth Dollar**.
*   *Formula:* (Number of Courses Completed) / (Total Video Hosting Cost).
*   *Why:* This directly ties your core learning outcome (completion) to the specific cost driver (hosting). If completion rates remain stable while hosting costs drop by 60% after capping resolution, you have validated that 4K was unnecessary waste.

***

*Note: The 4% engagement increase may be noise; ensure statistical significance before attributing it to video quality.*
*Consider surveying students on "video buffering issues" before building complex streaming tools; the problem may be connection speed, not resolution.*
