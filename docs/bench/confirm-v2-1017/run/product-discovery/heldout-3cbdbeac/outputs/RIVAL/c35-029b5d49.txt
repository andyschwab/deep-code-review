### Decision Framework: Offline Mode vs. Low-Bandwidth Optimization

**1. Critical Unknowns: Data Conflict Resolution**
Before committing to the eight-week timeline, the following technical and behavioral unknowns must be resolved to prevent data corruption:
*   **Conflict Frequency:** How often do multiple users actually edit the *same* plot record simultaneously while offline? If users are typically assigned unique plots, complex conflict resolution may be unnecessary overhead.
*   **Resolution Logic:** In a collision, which data wins? (e.g., "Last write wins," "Manual merge prompt," or "Field manager override"). The chosen logic must align with field workflows to avoid accidental data loss.
*   **Sync Latency:** What is the maximum acceptable time for data to reconcile once connectivity is restored? Does the current infrastructure support batch syncing hundreds of records without timing out on unstable networks?
*   **Storage Limits:** What is the maximum database size a typical low-end Android device (common in the region) can handle without crashing when storing months of historical data locally?

**2. Validation of Complaints vs. Development Effort**
The 47 formal complaints over six weeks average to roughly 1.1 complaints per day. To justify an eight-week development sprint:
*   **Severity Verification:** Do these 47 complaints represent total data loss, or just frustration with slow loading? If data is merely delayed but not lost, low-bandwidth optimization might solve 80% of the pain for 20% of the effort.
*   **Silent Churn:** Are there users who simply stopped using the app after losing data once without filing a complaint? If silent churn is high, the 47 complaints are just the tip of the iceberg, strongly favoring offline mode.
*   **Opportunity Cost:** If the team spends eight weeks on offline sync, what specific metrics (beyond the projected 20% DAU increase) will suffer? Will critical bug fixes or other high-value features be delayed past the June 30th deadline?

**3. Recommended Next Steps (Before Full Build)**
*   **Run a "Wizard of Oz" Test:** Have field agents manually record data on paper or a simple local notes app for one week in the most affected areas. Measure if their productivity and data accuracy actually improve compared to the current app. This validates the *value* of offline work before building the *sync engine*.
*   **Prototype the Sync Conflict:** Build a minimal technical spike (3–5 days) specifically to simulate two devices editing the same record offline and reconnecting. If this proves too complex for the current stack, the eight-week estimate may be optimistic.
*   **Hybrid Approach:** Consider a "Queue-Only" mode where users can draft entries offline that sync automatically when connection returns, without full read/access to historical data. This reduces storage and conflict risks while solving the primary data-loss complaint.

**Decision Rule:** Proceed with full offline mode only if the "Wizard of Oz" test shows a significant productivity lift AND the technical spike confirms conflict resolution is manageable within six weeks. Otherwise, prioritize low-bandwidth optimization and a "draft-only" offline feature.

*Note: The 20% DAU increase assumption should be treated as a hypothesis to be validated, not a guaranteed outcome. Ensure the field operations manager defines what "success" looks like beyond just activity metrics.*
