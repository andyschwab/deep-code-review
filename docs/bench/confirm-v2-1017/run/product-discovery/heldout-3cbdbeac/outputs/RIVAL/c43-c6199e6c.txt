### Recommendation: Proceed with Immediate Discovery, Delay Full Build Commitment

**Executive Summary**
While the demand signal from three Toronto enterprise prospects is strong, the current evidence is insufficient to commit to a full Role-Based Access Control (RBAC) refactor before the Q4 release. The risk of missing the October 15th deadline outweighs the unvalidated assumption of a 15% conversion lift. We should prioritize a two-week "Spike" to validate the specific granularity required and assess engineering effort before locking the roadmap.

**Evidence Analysis**
*   **Supporting Factors:** Three formal security questionnaires explicitly requesting granular control indicate a hard blocker for enterprise sales. The shift from 500 SMB accounts to enterprise targets validates the need for evolved permissions.
*   **Gaps in Evidence:**
    *   **Specifics Unknown:** "Granular control" is vague. Do they need 5 roles or 50? Do they need attribute-based access or just static roles? Building a generic system may be over-engineering.
    *   **Unvalidated Assumption:** The projected 15% conversion increase is a hypothesis. We do not know if RBAC is the *only* missing piece or if pricing, integration, or other security features are also blockers.
    *   **Effort Uncertainty:** Refactoring permission logic from a binary toggle to RBAC is high-risk. Without a technical spike, estimating the delay to the Oct 15 date is guessing.

**Proposed Action Plan (Next 2 Weeks)**
1.  **Conduct Targeted Interviews (Days 1-3):** Interview the three Toronto prospects. Ask: "Walk me through the last time you had to deny access to a specific team member. What roles existed? What happened when the system didn't support it?" Determine the *minimum* viable role set they actually need.
2.  **Engineering Spike (Days 4-10):** Assign the lead backend engineer to map the current schema against the minimum viable requirements. Output must be a concrete effort estimate and a risk assessment for the Oct 15 deadline.
3.  **Decision Gate (Day 14):**
    *   *If* the effort is low (<2 sprints) and prospects confirm RBAC is the sole blocker: **Greenlight full build**, potentially descaling other Q4 features to meet the date.
    *   *If* the effort is high or requirements are complex: **Propose a phased approach**. Offer a manual workaround or a limited "beta" RBAC for these three pilots in Q4, with full general availability in Q1.

**Risks of Immediate Full Build**
*   **Missed Deadline:** Refactoring core logic often reveals hidden dependencies, likely pushing the release past Oct 15 and damaging credibility with *all* prospects, not just enterprise ones.
*   **Over-building:** Creating a flexible system for hypothetical future needs rather than the specific needs of the three current leads wastes resources.

**Conclusion**
Do not commit to the full build tomorrow. Commit to the validation sprint. This protects the Q4 date while demonstrating to the CTO and engineering lead that we are de-risking the investment before spending it.

*Note: Ensure the engineering spike includes a review of data migration risks for existing 500 accounts.*
*Prepare a fallback "manual provisioning" offer for the Toronto clients in case the full build cannot meet their immediate contracting timelines.*
