### Critical Evaluation: Pricing Page Redesign vs. Strategy Review

Proceeding solely with the UI update is **high-risk and likely insufficient**. While "confusing pricing" is the stated reason for churn, in times of economic volatility, this phrase often acts as a euphemism for "too expensive" or "I can no longer justify this cost." A redesign addresses *usability* (can they understand the cost?) but fails to address *viability* (can they afford the cost?). If the root cause is affordability, a clearer page will only accelerate churn by making the high price more transparent.

You need to distinguish between a **comprehension problem** (they don't get it) and a **value/affordability problem** (they can't pay it) before committing engineering resources.

#### Additional Data Required to Confirm Root Cause

Before finalizing the May 10th launch scope, gather the following specific data points:

**1. Granular Churn Segmentation by Plan & Tenure**
*   **Action:** Break down the 3% → 5.5% churn increase by specific pricing tier and customer tenure.
*   **Why:** If churn is concentrated in the lowest tier or among customers <6 months old, it suggests an affordability or entry-level value mismatch. If churn is uniform across all tiers, the issue may be broader market contraction. If only long-term customers are leaving, the perceived value may have eroded over time regardless of price clarity.

**2. "Price Sensitivity" Analysis in Exit Surveys**
*   **Action:** Re-analyze the qualitative data from the 60% citing "confusing pricing." Look for adjacent comments mentioning "budget cuts," "cost," "ROI," or "competitors."
*   **Why:** Customers often blame "confusion" to avoid an awkward conversation about money. If 40% of those also mentioned "budget" elsewhere in their feedback, the issue is affordability, not UI.

**3. Support Ticket Sentiment & Resolution Analysis**
*   **Action:** Categorize the spike in billing inquiries (Jan–Mar) into "Clarification" (e.g., "What does this feature cost?") vs. "Dispute/Reduction" (e.g., "Can I get a discount?" or "Why was I charged this much?").
*   **Why:** A spike in *clarification* supports the UI redesign hypothesis. A spike in *discounts/disputes* strongly indicates a pricing strategy/affordability crisis requiring a strategic review, not just a visual facelift.

**4. Competitor Pricing & Positioning Audit**
*   **Action:** Map VendaFacil's current effective price point against top 3 local competitors, adjusted for the current Brazilian economic context (inflation impact on small retailers).
*   **Why:** If competitors have lowered prices or introduced "survival tiers" while VendaFacil has held steady, the churn is likely due to market misalignment. A prettier page will not fix a non-competitive price point.

**5. Pre-Churn Usage Correlation**
*   **Action:** Correlate churned accounts with their product usage intensity in the 30 days prior to cancellation.
*   **Why:** If churned users had *low* usage before leaving, they likely didn't see enough value to justify the cost (Value Risk). If they had *high* usage but still left, the price is genuinely unsustainable for their current business reality (Viability Risk).

#### Recommendation
Do not approve the full redesign yet. Run a **rapid, low-fidelity test** (e.g., a landing page test or concierge offer) with a simplified pricing structure *and* a potential price adjustment for a small segment. If churn drops only when the price is adjusted, you need a strategy review. If churn drops with clarity alone, proceed with the UI update.

*Note: Presenting a "clarity-only" solution to the CEO without ruling out affordability could result in wasted engineering spend and continued churn.*
