### Executive Assessment: TagalogSupport AI Chatbot Initiative

**Recommendation: Pause Development. Proceed immediately to low-cost validation.**

Based on the metrics provided, proceeding with a $40,000, four-month development cycle is a high-risk decision driven by unverified assumptions. While 300 tickets represent a clear signal of market interest, they do not confirm willingness to pay or technical feasibility within your specific niche. Your hesitation regarding "complex policy questions requiring human empathy" identifies a critical **Value Risk**: if the bot fails to resolve these specific queries, it will increase frustration and churn rather than reducing volume.

**Critical Unverified Assumptions**
1.  **Value Assumption:** Clients are willing to pay an extra fee for automation. Currently, you only know they *asked* about it, not that they will *buy* it.
2.  **Usability/Feasibility Assumption:** The inquiries are simple enough for current AI to handle without human empathy. If 80% of those 300 queries require nuanced policy interpretation, the bot will fail.
3.  **Viability Assumption:** The cost of building ($40k) and maintaining the bot is lower than the cost of the manual labor it replaces, factoring in potential churn.

**Strategic Risks**
*   **The "Fake Positive" Trap:** Customers often ask for features they think they want but won't use or pay for once deployed. Building now bets $40k on the hope that these 300 requests translate to revenue.
*   **Churn Acceleration:** In the BPO sector, end-customer experience is paramount. A bot that cannot handle complex Tagalog/English code-switching or nuanced policy questions will degrade the service quality your partners sell, directly threatening your core contracts.
*   **Opportunity Cost:** Four months of engineering time diverted from core workflow automation could delay other high-value improvements.

**Proposed Validation Plan (2-3 Weeks, <$2,000)**
Before writing code, execute the following experiments to de-risk the investment:

1.  **Willingness-to-Pay Test (Viability):**
    *   Contact the 10 largest partners who generated the most tickets.
    *   Present a mockup of the bot and a pricing tier.
    *   **Success Metric:** Secure 3 Letters of Intent (LOIs) or pre-commitments to pay the proposed fee. If they hesitate to commit money, they will not adopt the feature post-launch.

2.  **"Wizard of Oz" Resolution Test (Value/Usability):**
    *   Select a small batch of incoming tier-one queries (e.g., 50 tickets).
    *   Have your support team draft responses *as if* they were the bot, strictly adhering to scripted logic without human empathy.
    *   Measure the resolution rate and customer sentiment.
    *   **Success Metric:** If >70% of these "simulated bot" interactions resolve the issue without escalation, the technology is viable. If resolution drops below 50%, your assumption about complexity is correct, and building the bot is dangerous.

3.  **Data Audit:**
    *   Analyze the text of the 300 existing tickets. Categorize them by complexity. If >40% contain unique policy exceptions or emotional distress markers, an automated solution is likely insufficient for your niche.

**Conclusion for the Board**
The request for a chatbot is a symptom of high volume, but the solution is not yet proven. Investing $40k now is speculation. By spending two weeks validating willingness to pay and actual resolution capabilities, you protect the company from building a feature that drives churn. Only proceed to development if you secure prepaid commitments and prove the bot can handle the specific complexity of your clients' policies.

*Note: This assessment relies solely on the data points provided in the request. Actual technical feasibility requires an engineering spike once value is confirmed.*
