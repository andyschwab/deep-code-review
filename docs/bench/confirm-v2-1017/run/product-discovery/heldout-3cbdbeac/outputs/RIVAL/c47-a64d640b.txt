# Decision Memo: Visual Drag-and-Drop Store Builder

**To:** VP of Product, Engineering Director
**From:** Product Manager, Mexico City Makers
**Date:** October 26, 2023
**Subject:** Go/No-Go Recommendation for Q3 No-Code Builder Initiative

### Executive Summary
Based on recent discovery research, we recommend **proceeding with a targeted technical spike** to validate feasibility before committing three engineers to a full build. While user demand is clear, the technical risk regarding our legacy rendering engine on older mobile devices presents a critical blocker that must be resolved before full-scale development.

### Evidence of Value (The "Why")
Recent qualitative data strongly supports the hypothesis that store customization complexity is a primary driver of early churn:
*   **User Pain Point:** In interviews with 20 non-technical merchants, 60% (12 users) explicitly stated they cannot edit storefronts without hiring external developers.
*   **Churn Correlation:** 40% of new sign-ups churn within the first month, with "complexity" cited as the leading cause.
*   **Projected Impact:** Solving this friction point has the potential to reduce early churn by 50% and significantly improve user satisfaction scores, directly impacting MRR retention.

### Critical Technical Uncertainty (The "Risk")
The primary risk is not market demand, but **technical feasibility**. Approximately 30% of our user base relies on older mobile devices. Our current rendering engine has not been stress-tested for real-time preview functionality in a drag-and-drop context on this hardware.
*   **Failure Mode:** If the builder causes latency or crashes on these devices, we risk alienating a significant portion of our existing base while failing to retain new users.
*   **Unknown:** We do not yet know if the engine can support real-time DOM manipulation without degrading performance below acceptable thresholds.

### Recommendation & Next Steps
We cannot yet justify allocating three engineers for a full quarter based on current data. The technical unknown is too high. Instead, we propose the following immediate actions:

1.  **Authorize a 2-Week Technical Spike:** Assign one senior engineer to build a bare-bones prototype of the drag-and-drop interface connected to the current rendering engine.
    *   **Success Metric:** The prototype must maintain <200ms latency on the oldest supported mobile device in our top 30% segment.
2.  **Decision Gate:** Following the spike, we will reconvene to make the final build/no-build decision.
    *   **If Feasible:** Commit three engineers for Q3 to build the MVP.
    *   **If Infeasible:** Pivot to exploring a "template-swapping" model or prioritize engine refactoring before attempting the builder.

### Conclusion
The market signal is strong, but the technical foundation is unproven. Investing three engineers immediately is premature. A short, focused technical validation will ensure we do not waste resources building a feature that fails on the devices our customers actually use.
