### Trade-off Assessment: Simplified Signup Flow

**Executive Summary**
The proposal to remove three verification steps targets a critical friction point where 60% of users currently abandon the process. While the potential uplift in conversion (from 14% to a projected 22%) represents significant growth opportunity, it introduces unquantified exposure to fraud risk that could exceed the 1.5% acceptable threshold.

**Growth Projection vs. Current Baseline**
*   **Current State:** 8,000 applications in Q2 with a 14% conversion rate yields ~1,120 funded accounts.
*   **Projected State:** Achieving a 22% conversion rate on the same volume would yield ~1,760 funded accounts, an increase of 640 new customers per quarter.
*   **Assumption Validity:** The projection relies on competitor benchmarks from last year. Market conditions, user expectations, and competitor flows may have shifted, making this benchmark a high-confidence assumption that requires validation before full rollout.

**Risk Exposure Analysis**
*   **Financial Impact:** With 8,000 applications, a 1.5% fraud rate equals 120 fraudulent accounts. If the simplified flow increases fraud to 2.0%, that rises to 160 accounts.
*   **Cost Implication:** The prompt notes that *any* increase in fraud could cost over $50,000. If the fraud rate spikes significantly due to removed checks, losses could scale rapidly, potentially eroding the margin gained from the additional 640 legitimate customers.
*   **Unknown Variable:** The correlation between specific removed verification steps and fraud detection rates is currently unknown. Without historical data on how these specific steps filter bad actors, the risk magnitude is speculative.

**Strategic Trade-offs**
1.  **Speed to Market vs. Control:** Removing steps accelerates user onboarding but reduces the "time to detect" fraudulent intent.
2.  **Volume vs. Quality:** Higher application volume increases the absolute number of fraud attempts the operations team must manually review or absorb.
3.  **Data Gap:** The decision relies on the assumption that competitor contexts (risk appetite, customer demographics, fraud sophistication) match your Nairobi micro-loan segment.

**Recommended Post-Launch Monitoring Metrics**
If the simplified flow proceeds, the following metrics must be tracked daily for the first 30 days to ensure risk remains within tolerance:

*   **Fraud Rate by Cohort:** Compare the percentage of confirmed fraud in the "Simplified Flow" cohort vs. the "Legacy Flow" cohort (if A/B testing) or vs. the previous 30-day baseline.
*   **Early Delinquency Rate (FD30):** Percentage of new loans defaulting within the first 30 days, serving as a proxy for identity fraud or synthetic identities.
*   **Manual Review Trigger Rate:** If any automated flags remain, track how often they are triggered and the false positive/negative ratio.
*   **Cost Per Acquired Customer (CPAC) Adjusted for Losses:** Calculate CPAC including estimated fraud losses to ensure unit economics remain positive.
*   **Time-to-Fund vs. Time-to-Detect:** Monitor the lag between account funding and fraud detection to ensure operations can react before funds are withdrawn.

**Immediate Next Steps for Decision Makers**
Before the marketing push on the 15th, consider running a limited canary release (e.g., 5% of traffic) to validate the 22% conversion assumption and measure the actual fraud impact on a small scale before committing full volume.

*Note: This assessment highlights data gaps regarding the specific function of the removed verification steps. Validating the "competitor benchmark" assumption via a small-scale test is critical before full deployment.*
