### Executive Summary: Evidence Gaps for Voice Integration Pivot

The recent accessibility audit successfully identifies a **problem** (motor impairment exclusion) but fails to validate your proposed **solution** (voice commands) or the **market demand** within your existing user base. The audit data indicates that 80% of test participants struggled with navigation, confirming a severe usability barrier. However, assuming voice is the immediate fix without further validation risks building infrastructure that users cannot or will not adopt, particularly in open office environments.

To determine if this pivot is justified, you must address the following critical evidence gaps before committing engineering resources:

**1. Solution Fit: Is Voice the Right Mechanism?**
The audit highlights a failure in motor interaction, but it does not prove voice is the preferred or feasible alternative.
*   **Gap:** We do not know if users with motor impairments prefer voice over other inputs (e.g., switch control, eye tracking, keyboard shortcuts, or simplified UI).
*   **Risk:** Voice may introduce new barriers regarding privacy, accuracy in noisy offices, or cognitive load.
*   **Required Data:** Qualitative feedback from the 12 struggling participants specifically asking what *they* would prefer, not what we assume they need.

**2. Contextual Viability: The "Office Worker" Constraint**
Your primary persona is the office worker. Voice interfaces often fail in shared workspaces due to social friction and noise.
*   **Gap:** We lack data on whether your current users are willing to speak commands in their specific work environments (open plans, client calls, quiet zones).
*   **Risk:** High adoption friction if users feel self-conscious speaking to their screens, rendering the feature useless despite its technical accessibility.
*   **Required Data:** Observational studies or surveys of current users regarding their comfort level with voice interaction during typical workflow hours.

**3. Demand vs. Compliance: Is This a Market Expander?**
You assume voice will expand the Total Addressable Market (TAM), but this assumes the excluded demographic is large enough to justify the build cost and that they are currently trying to use your product.
*   **Gap:** We do not know if the users excluded by motor impairments are actively seeking your solution or if they have already churned to competitors with better accessibility.
*   **Risk:** Building for a niche that does not generate sufficient ROI, treating accessibility as a "check-box" rather than a value driver.
*   **Required Data:** Market sizing of the specific segment of office workers with motor impairments who need your specific tool, and analysis of churn reasons for users with disabilities.

**Immediate Next Steps for the Design Sprint**
Do not proceed to full engineering implementation. Instead, use the sprint to:
1.  **Re-interview the 12 participants** who failed the audit to co-design potential solutions (voice vs. alternative inputs).
2.  **Prototype a "Wizard of Oz" voice test** with 5 current office workers to gauge comfort levels and social acceptance in their actual workspace.
3.  **Define success metrics** beyond compliance (e.g., task completion time, user satisfaction) to ensure the feature delivers actual value.

**Conclusion**
The audit justifies *investigating* accessibility improvements, but it does not justify a blind pivot to voice infrastructure. You must validate that voice is the desired solution for both the excluded users and your existing base before writing code.

*Note: Present these gaps to the board as a risk mitigation strategy, not a delay tactic. Validating demand now prevents costly rework later.*
*Focus the sprint on learning, not shipping, to ensure the roadmap solves a real human problem.*
