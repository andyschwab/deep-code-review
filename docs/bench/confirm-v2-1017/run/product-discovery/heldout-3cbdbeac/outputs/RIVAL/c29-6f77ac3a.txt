### Risk Analysis: Legacy Compliance Module Sunset

**Executive Summary**
Sunsetting the legacy compliance module by December 31st presents a **critical, unquantified risk** to relationships with twelve major German banking clients. While usage metrics (5% DAU) and lack of feature requests suggest low engagement, the silence (zero complaints) combined with the high-cost maintenance profile (30% of server costs) indicates a "silent dependency" rather than obsolescence. The assumption that users are merely captive due to contracts is unverified and dangerous; they may be reliant on the module for specific, non-negotiable regulatory reporting that lacks internal workarounds.

**Key Risk Categories**

1.  **Regulatory & Compliance Risk (Critical)**
    *   **Unknown Dependency:** German banking regulations are stringent. If the legacy module handles specific statutory reports required by BaFin (Federal Financial Supervisory Authority), shutting it down without a certified migration path could cause clients to fail audits.
    *   **Liability:** If a client misses a regulatory deadline due to our sunset, RheinBank Solutions could face contractual penalties and reputational damage far exceeding the saved maintenance costs.

2.  **Strategic & Relationship Risk (High)**
    *   **Captive vs. Critical:** The assumption that clients are "captive" is unproven. If they have no workarounds, they are not just captive; they are vulnerable. Forcing a shutdown without confirming their readiness may be viewed as a breach of partnership trust, potentially triggering churn in the *new* cloud-native platform once contracts expire.
    *   **Silence as Noise:** Zero complaints often mean the system works "well enough" in the background. It does not mean the system is unused. In compliance, "no news" is often the desired outcome, masking deep reliance.

3.  **Operational & Feasibility Risk (Medium-High)**
    *   **Migration Gap:** We do not know if the new cloud-native platform supports 100% of the legacy module's specific compliance logic. If gaps exist, clients cannot migrate, making the sunset impossible without custom development.
    *   **Workaround Verification:** The assumption that clients have built internal workarounds is a guess. If false, the shutdown creates an immediate operational crisis for twelve major accounts simultaneously.

**Recommended Immediate Actions (Before Q4 Planning)**

*   **Conduct Targeted Discovery:** Do not rely on usage data. Interview the primary compliance officers at all twelve banks immediately. Ask: "Walk me through the last time you generated a regulatory report using this module. What happened if you didn't use it?"
*   **Audit Regulatory Mapping:** Map every output of the legacy module to current German banking regulations. Identify which outputs are legally mandatory.
*   **Pause Sunset Date:** Do not commit to the December 31st date publicly or internally until the above discovery is complete. A delayed sunset is cheaper than a catastrophic client failure.

**Conclusion**
The evidence currently supports **delaying the decision**. The cost savings are clear, but the risk of catastrophic client impact is unknown and potentially existential for these key accounts. Proceeding based on the assumption of "captive users" without validating regulatory dependencies is a high-stakes gamble that contradicts prudent risk management for a financial services provider.

***

*Note: This analysis relies solely on the provided context; no external data or client interviews were conducted. Immediate qualitative discovery with the 12 banks is required to convert these risks into facts.*
