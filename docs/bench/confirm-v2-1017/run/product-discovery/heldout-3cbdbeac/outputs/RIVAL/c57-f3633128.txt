**Recommendation: Delay Immediate Rollout; Proceed with Parallel Legal Review and Phased Pilot**

Do not proceed with a mandatory, immediate rollout for all enterprise clients. While the security evidence is compelling, the unknown contractual liabilities present an unacceptable business risk that outweighs the urgency of a blanket implementation.

**Security Justification vs. Business Risk**
The March audit identifying 15 critical vulnerabilities related to credential stuffing provides strong justification for action. Your assumption that Two-Factor Authentication (2FA) will reduce unauthorized access by 90% aligns with industry standards, making the *Value* and *Viability* of the security upgrade high. However, the *Feasibility* of this specific rollout strategy is compromised by the potential breach of contract.

If enterprise contracts contain clauses penalizing login procedure changes without six months' notice, a mandatory immediate rollout could trigger:
1.  **Financial Penalties:** Direct fines for breach of contract.
2.  **Churn:** Large clients may exercise exit clauses due to non-compliance with agreed-upon service terms.
3.  **Legal Liability:** Exposure to lawsuits if the change disrupts client operations.

The risk of alienating your largest revenue generators before the June compliance review is too high to ignore based on current information.

**Strategic Path Forward**
To satisfy the CTO's security requirements and the Legal team's compliance needs, adopt the following three-step approach:

1.  **Immediate Legal Audit (Week 1):**
    Before any technical deployment, Legal must review all active enterprise contracts specifically for "notification of service change" and "authentication protocol" clauses. Categorize clients into:
    *   *Green:* No notice period required or explicit security upgrade clauses exist.
    *   *Yellow:* Notice required, but waivers can be negotiated based on critical security findings.
    *   *Red:* Strict penalties with no immediate waiver path.

2.  **Phased Pilot Program (Weeks 2-4):**
    Instead of a mandatory rollout, launch an *optional* 2FA pilot with "Green" category clients. Frame this as an exclusive "Security Early Access" program.
    *   **Goal:** Validate your assumption regarding user friction and actual reduction in unauthorized access attempts without forcing the change.
    *   **Metric:** Track adoption rates and support ticket volume related to login issues.

3.  **Targeted Communication & Negotiation (Weeks 4-8):**
    For "Yellow" and "Red" clients, prepare a formal communication strategy. Use the March audit data (anonymized if necessary) to justify an emergency security amendment. Most enterprise clients will prioritize their own security over procedural notice periods if presented with evidence of critical vulnerability. Negotiate waivers based on the "force majeure" nature of critical security threats.

**Conclusion**
The audit findings justify the *need* for 2FA, but they do not justify bypassing contractual obligations. Proceeding immediately risks significant financial and reputational damage. By delaying the mandatory rollout to conduct a legal audit and running a voluntary pilot, you mitigate churn risk while demonstrating proactive security stewardship to the CTO and Legal team. This approach ensures you meet the June compliance review with data-backed implementation plans rather than potential litigation exposure.

*Note: Ensure the pilot group includes a mix of user technical proficiencies to accurately gauge friction.*
