# Urgent Briefing: Usability Findings & Launch Strategy for Bangkok Bazaar

**To:** Design Lead, CEO
**From:** Product Management
**Date:** [Current Date]
**Subject:** Critical Usability Friction vs. March 10th Launch Risk

### Executive Summary
Recent usability testing with 15 target users revealed a critical failure point: **53% (8/15)** could not locate the checkout button within 10 seconds, and **33% (5/15)** abandoned the task entirely. While the team hypothesizes that the menu structure is the root cause, current data cannot distinguish between **information architecture flaws** (labels/structure) and **visual hierarchy issues** (color, size, placement). Proceeding with a full navigation redesign before the March 10th campaign carries high execution risk, yet launching with current friction threatens significant revenue loss.

### Evidence Analysis
The data indicates a severe breakdown in the "Findability" phase of the checkout journey. However, the assumption that "menu structure" is the sole culprit is unproven.
*   **Scenario A (Visual Hierarchy):** If the button exists in the expected location but lacks contrast or visual weight, a full menu restructuring will fail to solve the problem.
*   **Scenario B (Navigation Labels):** If users are looking in the wrong section entirely due to confusing taxonomy, visual tweaks will be insufficient.

Without isolating these variables, a complete redesign is a high-risk gamble with a tight deadline.

### Risk Assessment

**Option 1: Full Navigation Redesign (Before March 10)**
*   **Pros:** Potential to solve deep structural issues; signals commitment to UX.
*   **Cons:** High probability of missing the launch window; introduces new, untested bugs; risks destabilizing other parts of the app under pressure.
*   **Verdict:** **High Risk.** The timeline is likely insufficient for a robust redesign, testing, and deployment cycle.

**Option 2: Launch As-Is**
*   **Pros:** Meets marketing deadline; no development delay.
*   **Cons:** Projected 33%+ abandonment rate at checkout; wasted marketing spend driving traffic to a broken funnel; potential brand damage in the Bangkok market.
*   **Verdict:** **Unacceptable.** The cost of acquired traffic exceeding conversion capacity is too high.

**Option 3: Targeted "Triage" Iteration (Recommended)**
*   **Action:** Implement high-visibility visual changes immediately (e.g., increase button size, change color to high-contrast, add directional cues) without altering the underlying menu structure.
*   **Rationale:** This addresses the most likely cause of "not seeing" the button (visual hierarchy) with minimal code risk.
*   **Follow-up:** Run a rapid 5-user validation test within 48 hours. If failures persist, investigate label taxonomy.

### Recommendation
Do **not** attempt a full navigation redesign before March 10. The risk of delaying the launch or introducing regression bugs outweighs the potential benefit of solving a potentially non-existent architecture problem.

Instead, authorize an immediate **Visual Hierarchy Sprint**:
1.  Enhance the checkout button's visual prominence (size, color, whitespace).
2.  Add a persistent "Checkout" floating action button if feasible.
3.  Validate these changes with 5 users within 2 days.

If these tweaks fail to resolve the 10-second findability metric, we must consider delaying the campaign start date rather than launching a known broken experience. A partial fix that improves visibility is safer and faster than a structural overhaul.

### Next Steps
*   **Design Team:** Mockup high-contrast visual variations by EOD tomorrow.
*   **Engineering:** Prepare hotfix deployment pipeline for minor UI changes.
*   **Product:** Schedule rapid validation session for [Date + 2 days].
