### Evaluation of Current Evidence

Your current evidence is **weak and high-risk**. Relying on the assumption that "financial incentives drive sharing" ignores the specific constraint you identified: **trust**. In marketplace dynamics, a viral coefficient of 0.4 indicates that for every 10 users, you only gain 4 new ones organically. If trust is the primary barrier, a 10% fee waiver is likely insufficient to overcome the social risk of recommending a platform to friends or family. If a referred transaction fails or feels unsafe, the referrer damages their personal reputation, a cost far exceeding a 10% saving.

Before committing runway to building a referral feature, you must validate whether the bottleneck is **motivation** (users don't care enough to share) or **ability/trust** (users are afraid to share).

### Required Customer Interviews

Conduct 5–7 interviews with active sellers who have *not* referred anyone yet. Do not pitch the referral program. Instead, investigate past behaviors using these prompts:

1.  **Past Behavior Probe:** "Walk me through the last time you recommended a business tool or marketplace to another seller. What was the situation, and what specifically made you feel safe recommending it?"
2.  **Barrier Identification:** "Think about a time you considered recommending our platform but decided against it. What was the specific hesitation or fear holding you back?"
3.  **Incentive Stress Test:** "If we offered you a discount for bringing a friend on board, how would that change your conversation with them? Would you lead with the discount, or would you worry about how they'd perceive the platform if something went wrong?"
4.  **Trust Calibration:** "What is the one thing that would need to happen on Cairo for you to feel 100% confident recommending it to your closest business partner, even without any reward?"

### Critical Metrics to Gather

Before building, analyze existing data to correlate trust signals with sharing behavior:

1.  **Referral Correlation:** Segment your current 0.4 viral users. Do they share common traits (e.g., higher transaction volume, longer tenure, specific category)? If your best sharers are also your most successful/trusted sellers, this confirms that **success breeds sharing**, not incentives.
2.  **Churn vs. Referral:** Compare the churn rate of referred users vs. organic signups. If referred users churn faster, it suggests users are referring low-quality leads just to get the fee waiver, validating the fear that incentives might degrade network quality.
3.  **Support Ticket Analysis:** Quantify the percentage of support tickets related to "trust," "fraud," or "disputes." If this number is high, fixing these core issues is a prerequisite for any referral program to work.

### Recommendation for Tuesday's Sprint

**Do not build the full referral system yet.** The risk of spending engineering time on a feature that fails due to underlying trust issues is too high.

Instead, run a **concierge test**: Manually message 50 high-performing sellers offering the 10% waiver personally if they introduce one specific peer. Track the conversion rate and the quality of the introduced lead. If they hesitate or the leads are poor, the issue is trust/supply density, not the lack of a software feature. Focus your sprint on increasing **supply density** and **trust signals** (e.g., verified badges, dispute resolution success stories) first. Only layer on automation once manual referrals prove that trust is no longer the blocker.

*Note: Prioritize fixing the core trust loop before automating growth; incentives amplify existing behavior, they rarely fix broken foundations.*
