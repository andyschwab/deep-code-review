### Strategic Evaluation: Public API Launch for Sydney Connect

**Executive Summary**
Current evidence suggests strong initial demand but insufficient validation to justify a full public API build at this stage. While three integration requests represent 20% of the active user base, this sample size is too small to confirm broader market need or guarantee that engineering costs will not outweigh support savings.

**Pros (Opportunities & Validated Signals)**
*   **Demonstrated Demand:** Three distinct developer teams have proactively requested access, representing a significant concentration (20%) of the current 150-company user base.
*   **Operational Efficiency:** Automating data exports via an API has the potential to eliminate ten hours of manual support work weekly, freeing up resources for core business activities.
*   **Customer Retention:** Fulfilling these specific requests strengthens relationships with key early adopters and reduces churn risk among high-engagement users.
*   **Product Stickiness:** Integrating routing data directly into customer dashboards increases switching costs and embeds Sydney Connect deeper into client workflows.

**Cons (Risks & Unknowns)**
*   **Engineering Capacity Constraint:** The lead developer is currently at capacity with core feature work. Diverting focus to API stability, security, and documentation risks delaying critical roadmap items.
*   **Unverified Maintenance Cost:** The assumption that support savings (10 hrs/week) will exceed engineering maintenance time is unproven. Poorly documented or unstable APIs often generate *more* support tickets than they resolve.
*   **Sample Size Limitation:** Interest from three companies does not confirm demand across the remaining 80% of the user base. Building a public infrastructure for a niche need may result in low ROI.
*   **Technical Debt Risk:** Rushing an API to meet next Friday's timeline promise without dedicated resources could lead to technical debt, versioning issues, and reliability problems that damage brand reputation.

**Recommendation**
Do not commit to a full public API launch immediately. Instead, propose a **private beta program** limited to the three requesting teams. This approach validates the maintenance cost hypothesis and measures actual support reduction without overcommitting engineering resources or exposing unfinished infrastructure to the wider market.

***

*Note: This analysis relies strictly on the provided metrics (150 users, 3 requests, 10 hrs/week savings estimate) and does not account for external market competitors or long-term monetization strategies.*
