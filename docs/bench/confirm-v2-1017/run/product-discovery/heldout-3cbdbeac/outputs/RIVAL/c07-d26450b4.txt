# Executive Briefing: Invoice Data Entry Automation Proposal

**Objective:** Evaluate the strategic viability of automating manual invoice entry for the Toronto accounting team versus broader departmental rollout.

### 1. The Core Assumption & Immediate ROI
**Hypothesis:** A custom parser will reduce data entry from 45 minutes to <2 minutes per invoice.
**Projected Immediate Gain:**
*   **Current Loss:** 18 hours/week across 5 users (~936 hours/year).
*   **Potential Recovery:** ~900 hours/year if the hypothesis holds.
*   **Financial Context:** At an average fully loaded cost of $50/hour, this represents ~$45,000 in annual productivity recovery for just 2.5% of the department.

**The Risk:** This calculation assumes the "Toronto Process" is the standard. If these five users are outliers using unique workarounds not shared by the other 195 accountants, the solution solves a localized symptom rather than a systemic disease.

### 2. Strategic Risk Analysis
Building a custom internal tool introduces four critical risks that must be weighed against the time savings:

*   **Scalability Risk (The "Outlier" Problem):** If the Toronto team's invoice formats or validation rules differ from the rest of the department, a custom build for them may require significant re-engineering to scale. A solution that works for 5 users might fail for 200 due to edge cases not present in the smaller sample.
*   **Maintenance Burden (Technical Debt):** Unlike SaaS solutions, a custom parser requires permanent engineering ownership. Every change in vendor invoice formats, legacy system updates, or regulatory requirements demands developer time. The "three-month build" often becomes a "three-year maintenance commitment."
*   **Opportunity Cost:** Dedicating engineering resources for three months delays other strategic initiatives. We must ask: Is recovering 18 hours/week the highest value use of our engineering capacity right now?
*   **Adoption Friction:** If the tool is built specifically for the Toronto workflow, will the broader department resist adopting it because it doesn't fit their specific nuances? Low adoption rates across the remaining 195 staff would negate the broader ROI.

### 3. Recommended Validation Steps (Before Commitment)
To de-risk the decision before the three-month development clock starts:

1.  **Process Audit (1 Week):** Map the invoice workflows of 10 random accountants outside Toronto. Determine if the "45-minute manual entry" pain point is universal or isolated.
2.  **Prototype Test (2 Weeks):** Instead of a full build, create a low-fidelity script or use an existing low-code tool to automate one week of Toronto's entries. Measure the *actual* time reduction and error rate.
3.  **Maintenance Modeling:** Estimate the annual engineering hours required to maintain the parser (updates, bug fixes, format changes). Subtract this cost from the projected $45,000 savings to find the *net* value.

### 4. Decision Framework for CFO
*   **Proceed with Custom Build IF:** The process audit confirms the Toronto workflow represents >80% of the department's volume, AND the projected maintenance cost is <20% of the saved labor value.
*   **Pilot Only IF:** The workflow is unique to Toronto, but the productivity gain is critical for a specific business reason (e.g., clearing a backlog). Treat this as a contained experiment, not a platform strategy.
*   **Reject/Defer IF:** The workflows are highly fragmented across the department, suggesting a need for a broader ERP upgrade or commercial OCR solution rather than a custom point-solution.

**Conclusion:** The immediate relief for the Toronto team is tangible, but the long-term value depends entirely on whether their process is the rule or the exception. Validating this distinction is the prerequisite for any investment decision.

---
*Note: This briefing isolates the strategic variables; it does not endorse the build. Final approval requires the results of the recommended 3-week validation phase.*
