### Critical Data Gaps & Risk Analysis for Acme Logistics

**1. The Operating System Blind Spot (Highest Immediate Risk)**
Your hypothesis centers on building a **native iOS app**, yet you lack data on the device split in rural India.
*   **The Gap:** You do not know the percentage of your 140% traffic surge coming from iOS vs. Android.
*   **The Market Reality:** In rural India, Android dominates with >95% market share due to affordable handset pricing. iOS penetration is typically negligible in these demographics.
*   **The Risk:** Allocating budget to an iOS app risks building a solution for <5% of your affected users while ignoring the 95% facing the same connectivity issues. This is a critical **Viability** and **Strategy** risk.

**2. The Root Cause Assumption (Value Risk)**
You assume unstable networks cause timeouts, leading to bounces.
*   **The Gap:** You have no qualitative evidence confirming *why* users bounce. High bounce rates on mobile could also stem from:
    *   UI elements too small for touch on low-end devices.
    *   Data-heavy images/scripts that fail to load even on stable 3G.
    *   Complex navigation flows unsuited for first-time digital users.
*   **The Risk:** If the issue is UI complexity or data weight rather than connection drops, an offline-capable app will not fix the retention problem. You might solve the wrong problem entirely.

**3. The Solution Fit: Native App vs. PWA (Feasibility & Usability Risk)**
You are framing the decision as "Native iOS App" vs. "Optimize PWA."
*   **The Gap:** You have not evaluated whether a **Progressive Web App (PWA)** with offline caching could solve the network issue without the friction of app store downloads, storage constraints on low-end phones, and the need for OS-specific development (iOS + Android).
*   **The Risk:** Building two native apps (to cover both OSs) drastically increases cost and maintenance. If a PWA solves the offline need, the native route is an inefficient use of capital.

**4. User Behavior & Willingness to Install (Behavioral Risk)**
*   **The Gap:** Do rural users in your segment have the storage space or data allowance to download a native app? Have they previously attempted to download your app and failed?
*   **The Risk:** Even if you build the perfect offline app, users on 1GB RAM devices with limited data plans may refuse to install it, rendering the investment useless.

### Recommended Immediate Actions (Before Nov 15)

1.  **Segment Analytics by Device/OS:** Immediately filter your web traffic data for the rural regions by "Operating System" and "Device Model." This will likely confirm Android dominance.
2.  **Analyze Performance Waterfalls:** Use tools like Google Lighthouse or real-user monitoring (RUM) to see *exactly* where the timeout occurs. Is it DNS lookup, TTFB, or resource loading?
3.  **Conduct Rapid Contextual Inquiry:** Interview 5–7 users from the rural segment. Ask: "Walk me through the last time you tried to use our site on your phone. What happened?" Do not mention apps yet. Listen for mentions of "data running out," "phone slowing down," or "couldn't find the icon."
4.  **Prototype a PWA Offline Mode:** Before committing to native development, test if service workers can cache critical flows on the existing web platform.
