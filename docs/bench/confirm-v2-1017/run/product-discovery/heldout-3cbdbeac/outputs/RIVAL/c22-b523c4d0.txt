### Executive Summary: NPS Trend Interpretation & Evidence Gap Analysis

**Current Situation:**
The drop in Net Promoter Score (NPS) from 42 to 34, driven by 200 detractors citing "lack of self-service," indicates a critical friction point in the customer journey. However, interpreting "lack of self-service" as a direct request for a portal is risky. In high-touch service environments, customers often use this phrase to mean "I couldn't find the answer quickly" or "I had to wait too long to speak to someone," not necessarily "I want to do it myself."

**The Core Risk:**
Your primary assumption—that users prefer self-service over agent interaction—contradicts your historical high-touch model. If the portal is built but users continue calling for reassurance or complex issue resolution, you will incur the €200,000 build cost without achieving the targeted 15% support volume reduction. This would result in increased operational costs (maintaining both the portal and the call center) and potentially further NPS degradation if the portal creates a barrier to human help.

**Required Qualitative Evidence:**
To justify the investment to the VP of Product and Finance, you must move beyond the quantitative NPS tag and validate user intent through the following qualitative research before December 5th:

1.  **Behavioral Context of Detractors:**
    Conduct 5–7 rapid interviews with recent detractors who cited "lack of self-service." Do not ask if they *want* a portal. Instead, ask:
    *   "Walk me through the last time you needed help and couldn't find it. What exactly were you trying to do?"
    *   "What steps did you take before contacting support?"
    *   "At what point did you decide you needed to speak to a human?"
    *   *Goal:* Determine if the pain is the *absence of information* (solvable by a KB) or the *difficulty of accessing human help* (which a portal might worsen).

2.  **Preference Validation (Past Behavior):**
    Analyze current support logs for the top 10 inquiry types. Identify which are transactional (e.g., "reset password," "check status") versus relational/complex (e.g., "change flight due to emergency," "complaint handling").
    *   *Hypothesis:* Users may want self-service for low-stakes transactions but demand high-touch for high-stakes issues. A portal only reduces volume if the majority of tickets are low-stakes.

3.  **Solution Testing (Low-Fidelity):**
    Before building the full portal, test a "concierge" version. Send a small segment of users a personalized email with direct links to specific knowledge base articles or a simulated tracking page when they contact support.
    *   *Metric:* Measure if they click the links and resolve the issue without further contact.
    *   *Goal:* Validate actual adoption behavior rather than stated preference.

**Recommendation for Budget Meeting:**
Present the €200,000 request as a phased investment. Propose a €20,000 "Discovery & MVP" phase to execute the interviews and low-fidelity tests above. Commit to the full build only if data confirms that at least 40% of current ticket volume consists of transactional queries that users are willing to resolve digitally. This approach mitigates the risk of building an unused feature while demonstrating fiscal responsibility to Finance.

*Note: Prioritize interviewing the 200 detractors immediately; their specific contexts hold the key to distinguishing between a content gap and a channel preference issue.*
*Delay any vendor contracts until the "concierge" test validates the 15% volume reduction hypothesis.*
