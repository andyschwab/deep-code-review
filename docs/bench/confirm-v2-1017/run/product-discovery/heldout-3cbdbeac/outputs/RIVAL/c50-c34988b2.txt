### Critical Evidence Gaps: Biometric Login Implementation

**1. Compliance Verification Gap**
*   **Assumption:** On-device processing automatically satisfies upcoming January 1st regional regulations.
*   **Evidence Needed:** A formal legal interpretation confirming that your specific on-device architecture meets the new "local storage" definitions. The survey indicates 42% of users prioritize privacy; a regulatory misstep here creates immediate reputational catastrophe, not just a fine.
*   **Risk:** High. If the legal team's flag implies ambiguity, technical implementation alone cannot solve a legal definition problem.

**2. Behavioral Intent vs. Action Gap**
*   **Assumption:** The 68% of users expressing "interest" in faster login will actually use biometric authentication, leading to increased Daily Active Users (DAU).
*   **Evidence Needed:** Qualitative data distinguishing between "nice-to-have" and "need-to-have." Survey interest often overstates actual adoption. You need evidence of current friction points: How many users currently abandon the app during emergency access due to login delays? Without baseline abandonment rates, you cannot project DAU lifts.
*   **Risk:** Medium-High. Building a feature users claim they want but do not use wastes the €40,000 sprint budget and adds unnecessary complexity.

**3. Churn Trigger Specificity Gap**
*   **Assumption:** The mere presence of biometric options will cause a churn spike among the 12,000 subscribers due to perceived risk.
*   **Evidence Needed:** Segmented feedback from the 42% privacy-concerned cohort. Do they fear *any* biometric data usage, or specifically cloud storage? If the solution is strictly on-device, will their concern dissipate, or is the objection ideological?
*   **Risk:** High. Misinterpreting the nature of the privacy fear could lead to a communication strategy that fails to retain users.

**4. Reputational Impact Baseline**
*   **Assumption:** The trade-off between usability and reputational damage can be evaluated without current brand trust metrics.
*   **Evidence Needed:** Current Net Promoter Score (NPS) specifically related to "trust" and "security." If trust is already fragile, even a compliant feature launch could be perceived as aggressive. Conversely, if trust is high, users may give the benefit of the doubt.
*   **Risk:** Medium. Without a baseline, "reputational damage" is a theoretical concept rather than a measurable metric.

**5. Emergency Access Context Gap**
*   **Assumption:** "Emergency access" is a frequent enough use case to justify the development cost and risk.
*   **Evidence Needed:** Usage analytics showing the frequency of emergency access attempts. If this scenario occurs once a year per user, the friction reduction may not justify the €40,000 investment or the potential privacy backlash.
*   **Risk:** Medium. Solving a rare problem with a high-risk solution is poor resource allocation.

**Recommended Immediate Actions:**
*   Conduct 5–7 deep-dive interviews with users from the "privacy concerned" segment to test the "on-device" explanation.
*   Request a written sign-off from external counsel regarding the January 1st regulations, specifically referencing your proposed architecture.
*   Analyze session logs to quantify current drop-off rates during emergency login sequences.

*Decision cannot be made until the legal definition is confirmed and the frequency of the "emergency" use case is quantified.*
