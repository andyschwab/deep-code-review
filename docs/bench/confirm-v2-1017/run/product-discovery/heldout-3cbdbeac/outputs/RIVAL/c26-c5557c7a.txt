# Localization Investment Evaluation: Northwind Logistics

## Executive Summary
While a 40% spike in sign-ups from Poland, Vietnam, and Brazil (800 total users) indicates strong market interest, proceeding with a six-week engineering sprint for full localization based solely on the assumption that "language is the primary friction point" is high-risk. Current data shows a 2% conversion to premium, but we lack evidence linking this to language barriers versus feature mismatch or pricing. Before committing resources, we must validate whether language is the actual blocker or a correlation.

## Risk Assessment & Assumption Analysis

### 1. Value Risk (Critical)
*   **Assumption:** Users are not upgrading because they cannot understand the interface or value proposition in their native language.
*   **Counter-Hypothesis:** Users are utilizing the free tier for specific, limited tasks (e.g., occasional tracking) that do not require premium features, regardless of language. Or, the pricing model does not align with local purchasing power in these regions.
*   **Validation Needed:** Do non-English speakers who *do* understand English (e.g., logistics managers in multinational firms) convert at higher rates than local-only speakers? If English-proficient users in these regions also show 2% conversion, language is not the root cause.

### 2. Usability Risk
*   **Assumption:** The current English-only interface prevents users from discovering or understanding premium features.
*   **Counter-Hypothesis:** The UI is intuitive enough that language is secondary; the friction lies in the complexity of the premium features themselves or the onboarding flow.
*   **Validation Needed:** Analyze heatmaps or session recordings for these 800 users. Do they click on premium features but drop off at the payment gate, or do they never navigate to premium areas at all?

### 3. Viability Risk
*   **Assumption:** Localizing will increase conversion enough to justify the six-week development cost plus ongoing maintenance (translations, support).
*   **Counter-Hypothesis:** Even with localization, the Lifetime Value (LTV) of users in these specific markets may not cover the cost of goods sold (COGS) and support overhead.
*   **Validation Needed:** Estimate the projected revenue lift. If conversion moves from 2% to 5%, does the incremental revenue exceed the fully loaded cost of the engineering sprint and ongoing localization operations?

### 4. Go-to-Market Risk
*   **Assumption:** Once localized, we can effectively reach and support these users.
*   **Counter-Hypothesis:** We lack local support channels or marketing presence to sustain growth post-localization.
*   **Validation Needed:** Assess customer support capacity. Can we handle tickets in Polish, Vietnamese, and Portuguese?

## Recommended Next Steps (Before Sprint Commitment)

Do not start the six-week build yet. Execute the following low-effort validations within one week:

1.  **Targeted Survey (Quantitative):** Deploy an in-app survey (using simple icons or auto-translated prompts) to the 800 users asking: "What prevents you from upgrading?" with options including "Language," "Price," "Missing Features," and "Just testing."
2.  **User Interviews (Qualitative):** Conduct 5–6 interviews with users from each region (use a translator if necessary). Focus on their workflow: "Walk me through the last time you tried to solve a complex logistics problem. Where did you get stuck?"
3.  **Cohort Analysis:** Segment the 800 users by domain type (e.g., corporate vs. small business). Corporate domains often have English-speaking staff; if their conversion is also low, language is likely not the primary barrier.

## Decision Framework
*   **Proceed with Localization** if: >40% of surveyed users cite language as the top blocker AND English-proficient cohorts in these regions show significantly higher conversion rates.
*   **Pivot to Feature/Pricing Experiments** if: Users cite price or missing features, or if English-proficient users in these regions still do not convert.
*   **Defer Investment** if: The projected revenue lift from a conservative conversion increase (e.g., 2% → 4%) does not cover the six-week engineering cost within 12 months.

## Conclusion
The traction is promising, but the assumption that language is the sole bottleneck is unproven. A six-week delay in sprint planning to run these validation steps is preferable to building a localized product that fails to move the conversion needle. Recommend a one-week discovery sprint to gather definitive data before committing to the full localization build.

*Note: This evaluation assumes no existing translation infrastructure is in place. If partial tools exist, the feasibility risk decreases.*
*Immediate action: Draft the survey questions and recruit interview participants today.*
