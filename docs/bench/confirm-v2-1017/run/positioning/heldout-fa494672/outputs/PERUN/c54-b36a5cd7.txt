**Segment Focus:** Privacy-first Nordic parents who view constant video surveillance as intrusive rather than reassuring.

**Positioning Statement (Hypothesis to Validate):**
For Nordic parents who need to monitor their infant's well-being without compromising household privacy, CradleGuard is a smart baby monitor that analyzes crying patterns locally to ensure zero data leaves the home; unlike the Nest Cam or Owlet sock, which rely on cloud streaming and external data tracking, CradleGuard delivers peace of mind through on-device intelligence that eliminates the risk of surveillance creep while providing essential health insights.

**Core Message Headline:**
Smart Care That Stays Home: Protect Your Baby Without Recording Your Life.

**Why This Wins Against Alternatives:**
*   **Vs. Nest Cam (24/7 Video):** Parents give up the ability to visually spy on every movement, but they gain a home free from hackable video feeds and the psychological burden of constant surveillance. The winning line is: "Stop watching your baby and start trusting your home."
*   **Vs. Owlet Sock (Vital Signs):** Parents give up medical-grade vital sign tracking, but they gain a solution free from regulatory uncertainty and the anxiety of false alarms, focusing instead on reliable auditory analysis. The winning line is: "Listen to what matters, without the medical noise."

**Riskiest Assumption & Test:**
The riskiest assumption is that parents will accept the loss of remote video features in exchange for privacy. To test this, run a landing page A/B test comparing "Complete Video Control" vs. "Zero-Data Privacy Guarantee" as the primary headline, measuring conversion rates among Stockholm and Gothenburg traffic.

**Note:** This positioning frames privacy as an active security feature ("on-device intelligence") rather than a missing capability. Ensure all claims regarding "zero data" leaving the home are technically verifiable before public release.
