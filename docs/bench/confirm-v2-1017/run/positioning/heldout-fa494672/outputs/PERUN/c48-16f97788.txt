**Recommendation: Commit exclusively to catastrophic risk prevention.**

Your draft positioning ("Stop floods before they start, and save on water bills today") is trying to do too much. It dilutes your primary value by mixing a high-stakes emotional trigger (flood damage) with a low-stakes rational benefit (bill savings). For property management firms in high-density complexes, the pain of a single flood event—tenant displacement, structural repair, and liability—dwarfs the incremental gain of monthly bill reductions. Leading with cost savings positions you as a utility optimizer; leading with risk prevention positions you as an essential insurance against disaster.

**Revised Positioning Hypothesis**
*For property managers of high-density apartment complexes in Tokyo and Osaka who fear catastrophic flood liability and tenant disruption, MizuGuard is an active leak prevention system that automatically shuts off water sources before damage spreads; unlike acoustic alarms or quarterly manual inspections, it guarantees immediate intervention to prevent major flooding incidents, eliminating the risk of costly claims and tenant displacement.*

**Why this angle wins:**
1.  **The Alternative You Beat:** Your competitors are not just other devices; they are "quarterly manual inspections" and "acoustic alarms." These methods detect leaks only after water has already spread or rely on human reaction time. Your differentiator is **active shut-off**. This directly addresses the "false alarm" fear: a false shut-off is a minor inconvenience compared to the alternative of a flooded hallway. You must reframe the fear: the cost of a false positive is a phone call; the cost of a false negative (missing a leak) is a ruined building.
2.  **The Proof Point:** You have prevented 15 major flooding incidents this year. This is your strongest asset. In the risk-prevention narrative, this statistic proves you stop disasters that alarms would have missed. The "60% reduction in claims" supports the financial argument for insurers and owners, but the *prevention of the incident itself* is the hook that gets the meeting.
3.  **Addressing the Hesitation:** Owners hesitate due to false alarms. By focusing on risk, you change the conversation. Ask them: "Would you prefer one unnecessary shut-off call this year, or one tenant lawsuit and 10 million yen in repairs?" The daily cost savings (10,000 yen/month) should be mentioned only as a secondary "bonus" that lowers the total cost of ownership, not as the primary reason to buy.

**Message House Structure**
*   **Core Message:** MizuGuard prevents catastrophic flood damage in high-density housing through automatic active shut-off, rendering manual inspections and passive alarms obsolete.
*   **Pillar 1 (Risk Elimination):** We have stopped 15 major flooding incidents this year alone. *Proof: Incident logs from Tokyo/Osaka deployments.*
*   **Pillar 2 (Superiority over Alternatives):** Acoustic alarms react too late; manual inspections miss 90% of events between visits. MizuGuard acts in seconds. *Proof: Comparative response time data.*
*   **Pillar 3 (Financial Safety):** Reduces average water damage claims by 60%. *Proof: Insurance claim data from current 2,000-unit deployment.*
*   **Secondary Benefit (Do not lead with this):** Residents save an estimated 10,000 yen/month on bills.

**Next Step to Validate:**
Test this hypothesis by rewriting your sales deck headline to focus solely on "Zero Flood Liability" and removing bill savings from the first slide. Measure if this increases the conversion rate of hesitant building owners who previously cited "false alarm" fears. The 10,000 yen saving is real, but it is the cherry on top, not the cake.

*Note: Ensure your "15 major incidents" and "60% claim reduction" stats are ready to be shown as verified case studies during the sales cycle, as these are your critical proof points.*
*Note: Do not claim trademark clearance or legal insurance implications without consulting a professional; frame benefits as "risk reduction" rather than "guaranteed liability elimination."*
