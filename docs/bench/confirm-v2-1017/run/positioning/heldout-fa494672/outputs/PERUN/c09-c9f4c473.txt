Your draft message, "The fastest way to file taxes without the human error," fails to counter the trust issue; it actively reinforces the fear that ComptaFast is impersonal. By leading with "fastest" and "without human error," you are competing on algorithmic efficiency against a competitor (human accountants) whose primary value proposition is relational trust and advisory nuance. To a freelancer worried about losing personal connection, this message sounds like a cold transaction, confirming their bias that algorithms cannot understand their specific business context.

To compete with established human networks like Cabinet Dupont or the Freelancer Guild, you must reframe your value proposition from "replacing the human" to "augmenting the freelancer's control." Your differentiator is not just speed; it is the liberation from the six-hour monthly burden, allowing them to focus on their craft while retaining accuracy. The trust gap is bridged not by claiming perfection, but by positioning the tool as the reliable foundation that frees them to seek human advice only when strategically necessary, rather than for routine compliance.

**Reframed Positioning Hypothesis:**
"For Lyon and Marseille freelancers who need reliable compliance without sacrificing billable hours, ComptaFast is an automated bookkeeping service that guarantees 90% fewer errors and cuts monthly filing time from six hours to 45 minutes. Unlike relying on local accountants or guild lists for routine data entry, ComptaFast handles the repetitive groundwork instantly, giving you the accuracy to file with confidence and the time to consult humans only for strategic growth."

**Recommended Core Message:**
"Stop trading six billable hours for data entry. Get guild-grade accuracy in 45 minutes, so you can save human advice for growing your business."

**Why this works against the alternatives:**
*   **Against Cabinet Dupont/Independent Clerks:** You are not attacking their expertise; you are re-categorizing their role. You position them as expensive resources for "strategic growth" rather than "data entry," making your service the logical, cost-effective precursor to hiring them.
*   **Against the Freelancer Guild:** You align with their goal (compliance) but expose the inefficiency of their recommended manual processes.
*   **Addressing the Trust Gap:** The message shifts the benefit from "no human error" (which implies a lack of human touch) to "guild-grade accuracy" (which borrows the trust of the institution) and "time for strategy" (which validates the need for human interaction, just at the right time).

**Riskiest Assumption to Test:**
Whether freelancers perceive "routine data entry" as a low-value task they are willing to automate, or if they genuinely believe the *process* of doing it with a human provides necessary security. Test this by running two landing page variants: one highlighting "90% error reduction" and the other highlighting "Reclaim 5.5 hours/month for client work." Measure which drives more sign-ups among your target segment.

Note: This positioning is a hypothesis to validate with your current 850 users before broadening. Ensure any claim of "guild-grade" accuracy is backed by your internal data to avoid overpromising.
