Your draft positioning, "Stop floods before they start, and save on water bills today," is indeed trying to do too much. It dilutes your message by attempting to solve two distinct psychological problems simultaneously: fear of catastrophe and desire for efficiency. For property management firms managing high-density complexes, these are not equal priorities. The fear of a single major flood event (liability, tenant displacement, reputation damage) vastly outweighs the incremental gain of monthly bill savings.

Furthermore, your draft fails to address the specific objection you identified: the fear of false alarms disrupting tenants. By focusing on "saving bills," you sound like a utility optimization tool, which invites scrutiny on accuracy and nuisance. By focusing on "stopping floods," you position yourself as an insurance and risk mitigation partner, where the cost of a false alarm is negligible compared to the cost of a missed leak.

You should focus exclusively on **catastrophic risk prevention**.

Here is the stress-tested Geoffrey Moore-style positioning statement that aligns with your data and overcomes your sales friction:

**For** property managers of high-density apartment complexes in Japan
**that need** to eliminate the liability and operational chaos of major water damage incidents without disrupting tenants with false alarms
**MizuGuard**
**is a** smart active shut-off system
**that** prevents flood damage before it spreads by verifying leaks before cutting water, unlike simple acoustic alarms or manual inspections
**which** have already prevented 15 major flooding incidents this year and reduced damage claims by 60% across 2,000 deployed units in Tokyo and Osaka.

### Why this works better:
1.  **It targets the real buyer motivation:** Property managers are motivated by risk avoidance (lawsuits, angry tenants, insurance hikes) more than minor operational savings. The "10,000 yen savings" is a nice bonus to mention in a sales deck, but it is not the hook that opens the door.
2.  **It directly addresses the objection:** By explicitly stating "without disrupting tenants" and highlighting "active shut-off" vs. "simple alarms," you differentiate based on intelligence and reliability, not just detection.
3.  **It uses proof, not promises:** Instead of saying "save money," it cites the specific stat that matters: "reduced damage claims by 60%." This speaks the language of insurance and asset protection.

**Recommendation:** Drop the "save on water bills" angle from your primary headline and elevator pitch. Use the 60% claim reduction and the "15 incidents prevented" as your primary proof points. Frame the monthly savings as a secondary operational efficiency benefit only after the risk conversation is won. Your competition is not "high water bills"; your competition is "doing nothing and hoping for the best" or "using dumb alarms that cry wolf." Position against those.
