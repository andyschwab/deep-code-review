Your draft positioning, "The only thermostat that respects your home's history while lowering your bills," is indeed too vague. It attempts to serve two masters equally, which often results in resonating with neither. For owners of Grade II listed properties, the primary barrier to purchase is not the potential energy saving; it is the fear of violating preservation laws or damaging architectural integrity. Standard competitors like Nest or Hive are disqualified immediately because they represent a threat to the building's status, regardless of their efficiency claims.

Therefore, you should **focus entirely on the preservation angle**. Your differentiation is not that you save energy *and* preserve history; your differentiation is that you are the *only* solution that allows modern comfort without compromising historical status. Energy savings are the hygiene factor—the expected outcome of any thermostat—but non-invasive installation is the unique value proposition that unlocks the market.

Here is the stress-tested Geoffrey Moore-style positioning statement for HeritageHeat:

**Value Proposition**
*   **For** owners and estate managers of UK Grade II listed Victorian and Edwardian homes
*   **that need** to modernize heating controls without violating preservation constraints or drilling invasive wiring
*   **HeritageHeat**
*   **is a** non-invasive smart thermostat system
*   **that** delivers measurable energy efficiency while guaranteeing zero structural alteration to historic fabric

**Differentiation Statement**
*   **Unlike** standard smart hubs (e.g., Nest, Hive) that require disruptive installation teams and new cabling
*   **HeritageHeat**
*   **provides** a fully wireless, sensor-based architecture proven in 300 listed buildings to achieve 15% energy savings with absolutely no drilling or structural impact.

**Strategic Recommendation on Buyer Motivation**
Do not pivot to cost-conscious landlords as your primary narrative for Grade II owners. While landlords care about costs, the specific constraint of "listed building status" creates a unique psychological profile: the buyer is risk-averse regarding compliance and aesthetics first, and cost-second.

If you lead with "energy savings," you invite comparison to cheap, mass-market thermostats where you will lose on price and feature density. If you lead with "preservation," you create a monopoly category where you have no direct competitors. The argument that will close more deals is: *"You do not have to choose between compliance and comfort. We are the only system approved by the constraints of your building."*

Once the customer accepts that HeritageHeat is the *only* safe option, the 15% energy saving becomes the financial justification that seals the deal, rather than the primary hook. Your 45 five-star reviews praising "non-invasive sensors" confirm that your current users value the lack of damage above all else. Lean into that. Reframe the narrative from "balancing" two motivations to "solving the impossible constraint" of modernization in protected structures.

Note: Positioning for heritage consultants should be a secondary track focused on technical compliance, not the primary consumer message.
Use the "zero structural alteration" claim as your definitive proof point in all sales collateral to neutralize efficiency gap fears.
