### Choice Analysis: Sales Growth vs. Margin Protection

**Option A: The Marketing Director (Sales Growth Angle)**
*   **Focus:** Consumer confidence, conversion rates, and average order value (AOV).
*   **Argument:** Leverages your proven $12 increase in AOV. This frame appeals to top-line growth, framing DenimFit as a revenue engine that turns hesitant browsers into buyers by eliminating size anxiety.
*   **Risk:** In a tight economic climate, marketing budgets are often the first cut. If the brand cannot directly attribute the $12 lift to your tool amidst other variables, this value prop becomes "nice to have" rather than essential.

**Option B: The Operations Lead (Margin Protection Angle)**
*   **Focus:** Reverse logistics, return rates, and net margin.
*   **Argument:** Leverages your 35% reduction in return rates. For denim, where reverse shipping and restocking destroy margins, this is a direct P&L fix. It frames DenimFit not as an upsell tool, but as a cost-avoidance necessity.
*   **Risk:** Operations teams often lack the budget authority of CMOs, and "saving money" is less sexy than "making money" in pitch decks.

**Definitive Recommendation**
**Lead with the Operations Lead (Margin Protection) angle.**

While the $12 AOV lift is attractive, the 35% reduction in returns is your defensible moat. In the current retail landscape, particularly for mid-sized brands competing against giants, margin preservation is existential. Returns in apparel are a logistical nightmare that erodes profitability faster than marketing can generate it. By positioning DenimFit as a "logistics saver," you align with the most urgent pain point: the cost of doing business. Furthermore, a 35% drop in returns *automatically* improves the customer experience (fewer hassles), which indirectly drives the conversion and AOV metrics the Marketing Director cares about. You can sell the revenue upside as a secondary benefit of a healthier operation, but the primary hook must be the hard cost savings.
