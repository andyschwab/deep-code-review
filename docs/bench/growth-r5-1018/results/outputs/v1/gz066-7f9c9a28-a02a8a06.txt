### Retention Framework for RioNews: Aligning Windows with Editorial Rhythms

To align with your editorial director and the news consumption habits of Buenos Aires commuters, do not rely on a single generic window. Instead, adopt a **multi-window retention model** that distinguishes between habit formation (daily) and content loyalty (weekly).

**1. The Core Metric: 24-Hour "Habit" Retention**
*   **Definition:** A user is retained if they open the app within 24 hours of their previous session.
*   **Why:** For a news aggregator targeting morning commuters, the primary value proposition is daily relevance. Your drop from 40% to 28% in this specific window confirms the March 10th push schedule change disrupted the daily habit loop.
*   **Decision Rule:** If 24-hour retention drops, the issue is **timing or frequency** of the trigger (push), not content quality. This is your primary guardrail for push notification experiments.

**2. The Secondary Metric: 7-Day "Loyalty" Retention**
*   **Definition:** A user is retained if they open the app at least 3 times within a rolling 7-day window.
*   **Why:** News consumption is not perfectly linear. A user who reads deeply on weekends but skips weekdays may still be highly valuable. A strict 24-hour window incorrectly labels them as "churned."
*   **Decision Rule:** Use this to evaluate **content strategy**. If 24-hour retention is low but 7-day retention is stable, your push timing is annoying users, but your content remains essential. If both drop, the product value itself is eroding.

**3. Handling the "Weekend-Only" User**
*   **Logic:** Do not classify a weekend-only user as "retained" in the 24-hour sense, but do classify them as **"Active Weekly"** in your cohort analysis.
*   **Implementation:** Create a specific segment called `Weekend_Warriors`. Measure their retention on a **48-hour window** (Saturday/Sunday) rather than 24-hours.
*   **Action:** If this segment is growing while daily commuters shrink, your editorial calendar should shift heavy features to Friday PM, rather than trying to force weekday engagement via aggressive pushing.

**4. Diagnosing the March 10th Drop**
Your current data indicates a **Habit Failure**, not a Loyalty Failure. The 7 AM push likely arrived too early, too late, or too frequently for the current commuter reality, causing users to dismiss the notification and break the 24-hour chain.
*   **Immediate Test:** Revert the push schedule to the pre-March 10th baseline for 50% of users (A/B test).
*   **Success Criteria:** Look for a recovery in the **24-hour return rate** specifically. Do not wait for 7-day data to make this tactical fix.

**Summary for the Editorial Director**
*   **Stickiness Definition:** We measure "Daily Habit" (24h) for push optimization and "Weekly Loyalty" (3 opens/7 days) for content planning.
*   **Current Status:** The 12-point drop is a habit disruption. The 7 AM slot is currently failing to trigger the morning commute action.
*   **Next Step:** Run a split test on push timing immediately using 24-hour retention as the sole success metric. Weekend users are tracked separately and do not influence the morning push strategy.

*Note: All metrics must be calculated against RioNews' own historical baselines; no external industry benchmarks are applied.*
