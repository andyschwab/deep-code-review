### Cohort Segmentation and Comparative Analysis Plan

**1. Grouping Logic (The Segments)**
To isolate the effect of the new cat food line and test your multi-pet hypothesis, construct your dataset with the following hierarchical keys. Do not aggregate before slicing.

*   **Primary Time Buckets:**
    *   **Control Cohort:** Subscribers acquired in December (pre-launch baseline).
    *   **Treatment Cohort:** Subscribers acquired in January (post-launch exposure).
*   **Secondary Pet-Type Split (within each Time Bucket):**
    *   Cat-only owners.
    *   Dog-only owners.
    *   Multi-pet owners (flagged by >1 active subscription profile).
*   **Tertiary Delivery Stage:**
    *   Track status specifically at the **3rd delivery milestone** (the hypothesized churn point).

**2. Comparative Metrics to Calculate**
Run these four specific comparisons to answer your questions. All metrics must be calculated as rates, not raw counts, to account for the 2,500 new January signups.

*   **Comparison A (Product Line Impact):** Compare the **Day-90 Retention Rate** of the *January Cat-Only* segment against the *December Cat-Only* segment.
    *   *Goal:* Isolate if the new food line changes cancellation behavior for cat owners specifically.
*   **Comparison B (Multi-Pet Hypothesis):** Compare the **Churn Rate at Delivery #3** for *Multi-Pet* vs. *Single-Pet* owners across both cohorts.
    *   *Goal:* Validate if multi-pet status correlates with lower churn regardless of acquisition month.
*   **Comparison C (Dog vs. Cat Baseline):** Compare the **Churn Rate at Delivery #3** for *Dog-Only* (Jan & Dec combined) vs. *Cat-Only* (Jan & Dec combined).
    *   *Goal:* Determine inherent species-based retention differences.
*   **Comparison D (Revenue Quality):** Calculate **Average Revenue Per Retained User (ARPRU)** at Month 3 for Dog vs. Cat segments.

**3. Interpreting the "High Spend / High Churn" Dog Scenario**
If your data shows dog owners cancel faster after the third delivery but spend more per order, interpret this as a **Cash-Flow vs. Lifetime Value tension**, not a simple failure.

*   **The Dynamic:** Dog owners likely have higher average order values (larger food volume) but lower loyalty or higher price sensitivity, leading to early exit. Cat owners may have smaller baskets but stickier habits.
*   **Supply Chain Implication:** Do not forecast inventory based on *total* projected subscribers. You must weight your forecast by **expected survival months**.
    *   *Formula:* `Forecasted Volume = (New Dog Subs × 2.5 months avg life × High Unit Count) + (New Cat Subs × 6.0 months avg life × Low Unit Count)`.
    *   *Risk:* If you ignore the churn rate and only look at the high spend, you will overstock dog food for Q3 and Q4, assuming these users remain when they statistically will not. The "high spend" is a short-term revenue spike, not a long-term volume guarantee.

**4. Actionable Output for the Supply Chain Manager**
Present the forecast as two distinct curves:
1.  **The "Burn" Curve (Dog):** High initial inventory demand in Month 1-2, dropping sharply by Month 4.
2.  **The "Base" Curve (Cat/Multi-pet):** Lower initial demand, but stable/recurring inventory needs through Month 6+.

Recommend holding a **safety stock buffer** for dog food only for the first 90 days of the quarter, while committing to long-term recurring orders for the cat/multi-pet segments.

*Note: Ensure your "Multi-Pet" flag relies on a stable user ID, not just email matches, to avoid double-counting households. If the January cohort size (2,500) yields fewer than 30 churn events in any specific slice (e.g., Jan/Dog/Multi-pet), label that specific data point `UNVERIFIED` due to statistical noise.*
