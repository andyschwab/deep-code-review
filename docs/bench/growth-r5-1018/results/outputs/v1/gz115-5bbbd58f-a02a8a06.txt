**Recommended North Star Metric: 30-Day Driver Return Rate (Cohort-Based)**

For TruckGig, the single most important goal metric is the **percentage of drivers who complete at least one load and return to complete another load within 30 days**.

**Why this aligns satisfaction with liquidity:**
Total loads completed is a vanity metric in this context; it can rise temporarily by dumping low-paying, inefficient loads on drivers, which increases volume while destroying satisfaction and future supply. Driver earnings per hour is a vital *input* metric, but optimizing it alone does not guarantee liquidity if load availability is sporadic. The 30-day return rate is the only metric that rises only when **both** conditions are met: the driver feels compensated fairly enough to stay (satisfaction) AND the platform successfully matches them with work again (liquidity). If pay is low or loads are inconsistent, this metric drops immediately, signaling a break in the value loop.

**Tracking progress without perverse incentives:**
To track this honestly without creating gaming behaviors, you must instrument this as a **cohort metric**, not a rolling average.
1.  **Define the Cohort:** Group drivers by their first completed load date (e.g., "January Week 1 Cohort").
2.  **The Measurement:** Calculate what percentage of that specific group completes a second load within 30 days of their first.
3.  **Preventing Gaming:**
    *   *Avoids "Spamming":* Unlike "total loads," this prevents ops from forcing bad matches on drivers just to hit a number, because a bad match ensures the driver never returns, lowering the rate.
    *   *Avoids "Cherry-Picking":* Unlike "earnings per hour," which might encourage ignoring small or difficult loads (hurting liquidity), this metric rewards keeping the driver engaged in the ecosystem, regardless of the specific load type, provided the economic unit works for them.
    *   *Baseline Comparison:* Compare the current cohort's return rate only against TruckGig's prior cohorts (e.g., December vs. January). Do not compare against external industry benchmarks, as those are often fabricated or irrelevant to your specific freight niche.

**Input Metrics to Monitor:**
While the Return Rate is the North Star, monitor these two inputs to diagnose *why* it moves:
*   **Load Acceptance Rate:** (Diagnoses inconsistent availability).
*   **Net Earnings Per Hour (after deadhead):** (Diagnoses pay issues).

**Immediate Action for Leadership:**
Before next week's strategy meeting, segment the January drop-off by **driver tenure** (new vs. veteran) and **region**. If the drop is isolated to new drivers, the issue is likely onboarding or initial load quality. If it is uniform, the issue is systemic (pay or network density). Do not launch a broad pay increase until you confirm via this segmentation that "low pay" is the universal driver of the churn, rather than "no loads available" in specific lanes.

*Note: Ensure your data engineering team uses a stable, pseudonymous driver ID to track users across sessions, distinct from system logs used for uptime monitoring.*
*Note: Any A/B test on pay structures must pre-commit to a sample size and duration; do not stop early if results look significant on day two.*
