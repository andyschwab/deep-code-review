### Candidate Retention Metrics for Maple Meal Prep

**1. Active Subscription Status at Day-14 (Binary)**
*Definition:* A user is "retained" if their subscription status is active on the 14th day after signup or renewal; otherwise, they are "churned."
*   **Pros:** Directly aligns with your observed spike in two-week cancellations. It is binary and unambiguous, making it easy to track the immediate impact of the price increase on the specific cohort leaving early. It clearly distinguishes permanent churn (cancellation) from temporary pauses (if your system allows pausing without canceling).
*   **Cons:** It ignores *value*. A student downgrading to a cheaper plan or skipping a week but remaining subscribed counts the same as a high-spend corporate client. It does not capture the "portion size" hypothesis unless paired with cancellation reason codes.

**2. Rolling 30-Day Revenue Retention (Monetary)**
*Definition:* The ratio of revenue generated from a specific cohort in their second 30-day window compared to their first.
*   **Pros:** Directly addresses the financial health of the business and the "price sensitivity" hypothesis. If users stay but downgrade to a smaller plan, this metric dips slightly rather than crashing, distinguishing between losing a customer entirely versus losing margin. It naturally weights high-frequency, high-spend users more heavily.
*   **Cons:** Can mask behavioral churn. A user might cancel and re-subscribe later (churn-and-return) within the window, inflating the number while hiding the friction caused by portion sizes or price. It is slower to react than a binary status check, which may delay decisions before your 25th review.

**3. Frequency-Weighted Engagement Score (Hybrid)**
*Definition:* A composite score where a user is "retained" only if they complete ≥3 orders in a rolling 28-day window AND maintain ≥80% of their previous month's spend.
*   **Pros:** Best suited to distinguish between "temporary pauses" (low frequency, high intent to return) and "permanent churn" (zero frequency). It directly tests the "portion size" hypothesis: if users cancel because they are still hungry, they likely won't order frequently before quitting. This metric forces a definition of "core value" (regular eating) rather than just "having an account."
*   **Cons:** Complex to calculate and explain to non-technical stakeholders. Setting the thresholds (3 orders, 80% spend) is arbitrary without historical baseline data and may require adjustment after initial tracking.

**Recommendation for the 25th Review:**
Adopt **Metric #1 (Active Subscription Status at Day-14)** as your primary North Star for this specific crisis. Since the problem is a sudden spike in cancellations at a specific time boundary, you need the cleanest signal to validate if the price hike caused the exit. Use **Metric #2 (Revenue Retention)** as a guardrail to ensure that any solution (like a cheaper plan) does not cannibalize total revenue.

**Note:** Do not fabricate a "good" retention rate; compare the current 14% cancellation rate only against your pre-February 1st baseline of 8%. Ensure your analytics tool distinguishes between "canceled subscription" and "paused delivery" to accurately classify temporary pauses.
