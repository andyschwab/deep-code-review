**Subject: Readout on Faroe Islands Price Experiment (May 1–14)**

**Verdict: Do not roll out globally yet. The current data is `UNVERIFIED` for causal impact.**

We cannot determine if the price change drove sales because the observed noise (daily revenue swinging 450%) completely drowns out any potential signal from the price test. With a total population of only 3,000 across two villages, the sample size is too small to separate the effect of price from the effect of external factors like weather or random chance.

**Why the current read is unreliable:**
The massive daily swings you observed are consistent with external volatility (likely weather, given the location and product) rather than pricing sensitivity. When daily results fluctuate this wildly, a "win" seen on any single day or short cluster of days is statistically indistinguishable from luck. Treating these fluctuations as a trend would be a false conclusion. We have no baseline data from these specific villages prior to May 1st to compare against, and we lack the local weather data needed to mathematically remove that variable from the equation.

**How to read the data correctly (Smoothing the Noise):**
To find a real signal, we must stop looking at daily revenue and instead look at the **total cumulative performance** of the entire 14-day period compared to a control.
1.  **Aggregate the whole period:** Sum the total units sold and total revenue for the full two weeks. Do not analyze day-by-day trends.
2.  **Require a control group:** We can only judge the price if we compare it to a similar group that *did not* see the price change during the same dates. Without a concurrent control group in similar villages, we cannot know if sales were down (or up) due to price or simply because it rained for a week.
3.  **Check the magnitude:** Even if we assume the weather was average, the number of people in these villages is so low that a difference of just 10–20 jackets could look like a massive percentage swing. We need a much larger volume of transactions to be confident the price caused the change.

**Decision & Next Steps:**
Rolling out globally based on this test carries a high risk of setting a price that leaves money on the table or kills demand. The variance is too high to support a global decision.

**This Week's Actions:**
1.  **Pause global rollout:** Flag the Faroe Islands test as inconclusive due to high external noise and insufficient sample size.
2.  **Design a valid follow-up:** Select a larger test market (or combine multiple regions) with at least 10x the traffic volume to dilute local weather effects.
3.  **Pre-commit the read:** Define the total number of sales needed *before* starting the next test, and agree to read the result only once at the end of that period, ignoring daily fluctuations.

*Note: Any attempt to calculate a specific "lift percentage" from the current May 1–14 data would be an invented number, not a fact. We must treat the result as neutral until a controlled, larger-scale test is completed.*
