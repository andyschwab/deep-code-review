### Post-Mortem: Checkout Simplification A/B Test (SiamTravel)

**Verdict: Revert Immediately.**
The variant caused a statistically significant 12% drop in completed bookings. While time-to-book decreased by 40 seconds, this efficiency gain is a vanity metric in this context; it indicates friction removal that inadvertently removed necessary trust signals or information. Do not extend the test to "gather more data" on a losing variant that is actively destroying revenue.

**Recomputed Impact & Decision Logic**
*   **Primary Metric (Bookings):** -12%. This is the North Star for this flow. A drop of this magnitude over 100,000 sessions is not noise; it is a structural failure of the new flow to convert intent into action.
*   **Guardrail Metric (Time-to-Book):** -40 seconds. The correlation confirms the hypothesis that users utilized the removed steps for deliberation. In travel booking, "friction" often equals "confidence building" (e.g., reviewing insurance, cancellation policies, or itinerary details).
*   **Financial Implication:** If the baseline conversion rate was $X\%$, the new rate is $0.88X\%$. Unless the 40-second time saving reduces operational costs by more than the lost margin from 12% fewer bookings (highly unlikely), the net value is negative.

**Diagnosis: Why the Test Failed**
The failure stems from confusing "usability friction" (bad) with "decision friction" (good).
1.  **Missing Trust Signals:** The removed steps likely housed critical information (insurance upsells, fare rules, visa requirements). Without explicitly confirming these, users experience anxiety and abandon the cart.
2.  **Pacing Issues:** A 40-second reduction suggests the flow felt rushed. Users may have feared they missed an opportunity to customize or verify details before payment.
3.  **Hidden Dependency:** The "simplified" flow likely broke a mental model where users expect a review phase before committing financial data.

**Actionable Signals to Investigate (Quantitative & Qualitative)**
To validate the specific cause before the director meeting, query the following from your existing analytics (do not instrument new events yet):
*   **Drop-off Heatmap:** Compare exit rates *specifically* on the payment entry page between Control and Variant. If the Variant shows a spike here, users are arriving at payment without confidence.
*   **Insurance Attach Rate:** If insurance was on a removed page, check if the attach rate dropped to near zero in the variant. This confirms the "missing option" theory.
*   **Support Ticket Spike:** Filter support chats/tickets from Jan 20–30 for keywords like "where is," "insurance," "review," or "cancel." A spike in these queries from users on the variant path confirms information gaps.
*   **Session Replays:** Watch 10–20 recordings of users abandoning in the variant. Look for "rage clicks" on non-existent links or rapid back-button usage, indicating confusion.

**This-Week Step Plan**
1.  **Immediate Revert:** Roll back to the original checkout flow today to stop revenue bleed.
2.  **Targeted Hypothesis Test:** Do not re-test the full simplification. Instead, design a new test that keeps the information steps but optimizes their *presentation* (e.g., collapsible accordions for policies rather than separate pages).
3.  **Qualitative Loop:** Conduct 5 user interviews focusing on "What information did you look for but couldn't find?" using the support ticket themes identified above.

**Note:** The 12% drop is verified against your own baseline; no external benchmark is needed to declare this a failure. The 40-second speedup is a negative signal here, not a success.
