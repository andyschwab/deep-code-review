### Experimental Design: Discounted Pilot Acceleration Test

**Objective**
Validate whether a 20% pilot discount increases pilot-to-contract conversion from 30% to 45% without devaluing the product or increasing long-term churn, specifically for the VP of Sales' board presentation on the 15th.

**1. Cohort Structure**
To isolate the impact of price on behavior and perceived value, split incoming qualified leads into three distinct groups at the point of pilot proposal:
*   **Control Group (Status Quo):** Receives the standard pilot pricing. This establishes the baseline 30% conversion rate and standard LTV trajectory.
*   **Treatment Group A (Discounted Pilot):** Receives the 20% discounted pilot offer. This tests the primary hypothesis regarding conversion lift.
*   **Treatment Group B (Value-Add Pilot):** Receives the standard price but with added implementation support or extended pilot duration (no cost reduction). This acts as a guardrail to distinguish if "conversion lift" is driven purely by price sensitivity or by reduced friction/risk. If Group A outperforms Group B significantly, price is the lever; if they perform similarly, the barrier was risk, not cost.

*Stratification:* Ensure each cohort is balanced by **Industry Vertical** and **Company Size** to detect if price sensitivity varies by sector, addressing your specific uncertainty about vertical differences.

**2. Leading Indicators (Pre-Revenue Signals)**
Given the nine-month sales cycle and 60-day pilot, you cannot wait for final revenue to judge success. Monitor these leading indicators weekly:

*   **Pilot Activation Rate:** Percentage of signed pilots that actually kick off within 7 days. A drop here for the Discounted Group suggests the discount attracted low-intent leads.
*   **Core Feature Adoption Depth:** Measure the % of pilot users hitting the "aha" moment (core value action) within the first 30 days. If the Discounted Group converts to contract but shows shallow usage, they may be "cheap" rather than "valued" customers, signaling future churn risk.
*   **Stakeholder Engagement Score:** Frequency of interactions with key decision-makers (CIO/VP level) during the pilot. A decline in executive engagement in the Discounted Group indicates potential devaluation of the solution in the eyes of leadership.
*   **Pilot-to-Proposal Velocity:** Time from pilot start to final contract proposal submission. The hypothesis expects this to accelerate; if it doesn't, the discount isn't removing the intended friction.

**3. Reading the Results for the Board**
Since a full LTV calculation is impossible within the board timeline, present a "Quality-Adjusted Conversion" metric:
*(Pilot-to-Contract Conversion Rate) × (Core Feature Adoption Depth %)*

This prevents celebrating a conversion win that is actually a loss in customer quality. If the Discounted Group shows 45% conversion but 20% lower adoption depth than Control, the net value is negative.

**4. Immediate Next Steps**
1.  **Define the "Core Action":** Explicitly name the single user action in your product that correlates most strongly with renewal, so adoption depth can be tracked immediately.
2.  **Tagging Protocol:** Ensure your CRM tags every deal with `Experiment_Cohort`, `Industry_Vertical`, and `Offer_Type` at the moment of pilot proposal to enable clean segmentation later.

*Note: Do not peek at interim conversion rates to stop the test early; wait for the pre-committed pilot cycle (60 days) to complete for each batch to avoid false positives. Historical Q4 data should be used only as a contextual reference, not a control, due to seasonality differences.*
