### Short-Term Revenue vs. Long-Term Churn Analysis Plan

**1. Recompute the Immediate Financial Impact**
Before assessing sustainability, verify the net revenue lift from the test using your stated figures:
*   **Control Group (Standard Price):** 300 prospects × 18% conversion = 54 deals.
*   **Test Group (Higher Price):** 300 prospects × 16% conversion = 48 deals.
*   **Volume Loss:** 6 fewer deals in the test cohort.
*   **Break-Even Calculation:** The price increase (12%) must generate enough extra revenue per deal to offset the loss of 6 contracts.
    *   *Formula:* If $P$ is the original price and $V$ is the original volume revenue ($54 \times P$), the new revenue is $48 \times 1.12P = 53.76P$.
    *   *Result:* Based strictly on these conversion rates, the 12% price hike results in a **0.44% net revenue decrease** in the short term ($53.76P$ vs $54P$), assuming the "average contract value rose" refers solely to the list price increase and not an upsell in scope. If ACV rose due to customers selecting higher tiers or add-ons, insert that specific delta here to see if it clears the 6-deal loss hurdle.

**2. Diagnose the Conflicting Signals (The "Quality Signal" Hypothesis)**
Your assumption that higher prices signal quality is currently **UNVERIFIED** by the data provided. A drop in conversion (18% → 16%) indicates price sensitivity, not necessarily quality perception.
*   **Action:** Segment the 16% converters. Did larger firms convert at a *higher* rate than smaller firms within the test group? If the conversion drop is uniform across company sizes, the "quality signal" is failing; you are simply pricing out marginal buyers. If the drop is isolated to smaller firms while enterprise conversion held steady or rose, the hypothesis holds.

**3. Address the Long-Term Churn Risk (The Missing Data)**
You cannot analyze six-month renewal rates yet because that data does not exist. Treating this as a known variable is a fabrication risk.
*   **Proxy Metric:** Until six months pass, measure **activation depth** and **time-to-value** for the new cohort vs. the control. If the higher-priced cohort engages less deeply in the first 30 days, churn risk is high regardless of the "quality signal."
*   **Guardrail:** Define a "churn tripwire." If renewal rates for this cohort fall below your historical baseline (e.g., if baseline is 90% and this cohort hits 85% at month 3), the price hike is unsustainable.

**4. Decision Framework for CFO and Sales VP**
To finalize the strategy by month-end, adopt a **conditional rollout** rather than a binary global decision:
*   **Ship Condition:** Proceed globally only if the net revenue gain (after volume loss) exceeds 5% AND activation metrics for the test cohort match the control group within 30 days.
*   **Stop Condition:** If activation lags or net revenue is flat/negative, revert to standard pricing for new prospects while grandfathering the test group.
*   **Extend Condition:** If revenue is up but activation is unclear, extend the test for 90 days specifically to capture the first renewal signal before globalizing.
