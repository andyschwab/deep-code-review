**Evaluation of Upsell Experiment Failure & Diagnostic Plan**

**Executive Summary for Founding Team**
The experiment failed because the measurement approach conflated pricing sensitivity with workflow disruption, and the "success" metric (conversion) was overshadowed by a critical guardrail failure (account deletion). The 12% spike in deletions indicates the intervention was perceived as hostile or breaking the core value proposition, not merely "too expensive." The assumption that "three projects = serious intent" appears invalid for this cohort; for independent film producers, the third project may coincide with a high-stress production phase where interruptions are catastrophic.

**Key Ambiguities in Current Data**
1.  **Intent vs. Timing:** We do not know if users rejected the *price* ($18 NZD) or the *moment*. Did users who saw the modal during active editing sessions delete accounts at higher rates than those who saw it upon project completion?
2.  **The "Serious Intent" Fallacy:** The baseline conversion (2.1%) suggests the "three project" trigger was already weak. We lack data on whether users hitting this trigger were actually "freelancers" (your target) or "hobbyists" managing personal projects, who would naturally churn when asked to pay.
3.  **Causal Link to Deletion:** The 12% deletion increase is correlated but not yet causally proven to be solely the modal. Was there a concurrent platform stability issue or a change in terms of service? (Assuming no other changes, the modal is the prime suspect, but we need event-level proof).

**Variables You Should Have Tracked (Event Taxonomy)**
To diagnose the churn spike, you needed an event taxonomy that answers specific questions, not just aggregate conversion:
*   `modal_viewed` (properties: `session_duration`, `last_action_type`, `project_status`): To determine if the interrupt occurred during deep work vs. idle time.
*   `modal_dismissed` vs. `modal_clicked`: To separate "not interested" from "annoyed."
*   `account_deletion_requested` (properties: `time_since_modal_view`, `feedback_reason_code`): Crucial. You must capture *why* they left immediately after the prompt.
*   `feature_usage_post_modal`: Did users who stayed stop using budget tools entirely?

**Questions to Answer Before Next Test**
1.  **Workflow Mapping:** At what exact step in a film producer's workflow does "project creation" happen? Is it a celebratory moment or the start of a crunch?
2.  **Segmentation Validity:** Can we distinguish "professional freelancers" from "hobbyists" *before* the third project using behavioral signals (e.g., team invites, budget complexity) rather than just project count?
3.  **Price vs. Value Perception:** Is $18 NZD/month aligned with the value of "advanced budget tracking" specifically, or is the feature itself not compelling enough to warrant *any* friction?

**This Week's Steps**
1.  **Query Deletion Cohort:** Run a query on users who deleted accounts between Oct 5–19. Segment by "saw modal" vs. "did not see modal." If the deletion rate is high only for the "saw modal" group within 1 hour, the timing/aggressiveness is the root cause.
2.  **Interview Churned Users:** Contact 5–10 users who deleted their accounts during the test window. Ask specifically: "What was happening in your workflow when you saw the upgrade prompt?"
3.  **Pause Aggressive Triggers:** Immediately disable the "third project" modal. Replace it with a passive banner or delay the ask until a clearer signal of "professional intent" (e.g., inviting a second team member) is observed.

*Note: Do not run another A/B test on price or copy until the "workflow interruption" hypothesis is validated or rejected via the qualitative data above. The guardrail (deletions) has already been breached.*
