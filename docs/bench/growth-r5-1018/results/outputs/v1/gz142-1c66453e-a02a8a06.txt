### Measurement Framework: Business Registration Portal Pilot

**1. North Star Metric: "Verified Registrations Under 4 Hours"**
Reject "Total Signups" or "Pageviews" as vanity metrics. The single metric that matters is the count of users who successfully complete registration *and* pass identity verification within four hours. This rises only when real value (a registered company) is delivered quickly. Pair this with two input metrics: **Activation Rate** (users completing document upload) and **Verification Success Rate** (first-attempt pass).

**2. Diagnostic Funnel (Retention & Activation First)**
Do not pour traffic into a leaky bucket. Diagnose the 60% drop-off at the document upload stage before optimizing acquisition.
*   **Segment the Drop-off:** Split the 60% into **Technical Failures** (system errors, timeouts, file format rejections) vs. **Abandonment** (users leaving the page without error).
    *   *If Technical:* Prioritize engineering fixes on the upload handler.
    *   *If Abandonment:* The issue is confusion. Instrument a "Help Clicked" event on the upload page to confirm users are seeking clarity on file requirements.
*   **Industry Segmentation:** Tag sessions by "Company Type" (e.g., Sole Proprietorship, OÜ, Branch Office) at the start of the flow. Compare the **Activation Rate** per type. If "Sole Proprietorships" have a 90% activation rate but "Branch Offices" have 20%, the friction is specific to complex entity requirements, not the platform generally.

**3. Event Taxonomy (Answer Named Questions)**
Instrument only events that answer specific decision questions. Do not track everything.
*   `upload_started` (Property: `company_type`, `file_count`): *Are users attempting the step?*
*   `upload_error_triggered` (Property: `error_code`, `file_type`): *Is the system rejecting valid files?*
*   `upload_abandoned` (Property: `time_on_page`, `help_docs_viewed`): *Are users confused or giving up?*
*   `verification_passed_first_attempt`: *Did the simplification assumption hold true?*
*   **Identifier Seam:** Use a stable, pseudonymous user ID to track a single citizen across sessions and days. Do not use high-cardinality operational logs for this; ensure PII handling routes to privacy compliance protocols.

**4. Success Definitions & Interpretation**
*   **Speed:** Median time-to-complete for *successful* registrations must trend toward <4 hours.
*   **Quality:** Measure "Data Correction Rate" (edits made post-submission by clerks). High speed with low quality creates downstream government cost.
*   **Support Load:** Track "Tickets per 100 Registrations." If speed increases but ticket volume spikes, the UI is misleading users.

**5. This-Week Action Plan**
1.  **Query the Drop-off:** Run a cohort analysis on the pilot data splitting the 60% drop-off by `upload_error_triggered` vs. `upload_abandoned`. If >50% are errors, halt feature work and fix the uploader. If >50% are abandonment, draft clearer file requirement copy.
2.  **Segment by Industry:** Generate a table of Activation Rates by `company_type`. Identify the lowest-performing sector and schedule three user interviews with founders from that specific industry to observe their upload attempt.
3.  **Define the Guardrail:** Set a hard threshold for the quarterly review: "Funding continues only if 'Verified Registrations Under 4 Hours' increases by 20% month-over-month *without* increasing 'Tickets per 100 Registrations' above the baseline."

*Note: All benchmarks must be calculated against your own January 15 launch baseline; no external "good" rates exist for this specific government context. Ensure all user identifiers remain pseudonymous in analytics tools.*
