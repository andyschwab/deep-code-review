**Subject:** Recommendation on Homepage Layout Experiment – Do Not Roll Out Globally

**Executive Decision:**
**Stop the global rollout.** The observed 40% drop in session duration (from 8 minutes to ~4.8 minutes) represents a critical risk to our North Star metric: **Weekly Active Listeners Completing a Discovery Loop**. While the 12% increase in satisfaction scores is positive, it is currently `UNVERIFIED` as a driver of long-term retention without correlating behavioral data. In a music discovery context, time spent is often a proxy for exploration depth; a collapse of this magnitude suggests users are finding *less* to listen to, not finding it *faster*.

**Analysis of Conflicting Signals:**
The hypothesis that "efficiency equals happiness" fails if the core value proposition is exploration. If users leave 3.2 minutes earlier per session, they are likely consuming fewer tracks. Satisfaction surveys measure immediate friction, not long-term value. A user can be "satisfied" with a fast exit because they gave up, not because they succeeded. Without evidence that the *number of songs played per session* remained stable or increased, we must treat the duration drop as a failure of the core loop, not an optimization.

**Financial & Product Impact:**
If session duration correlates with ad impressions or subscription conversion trials, a 40% reduction directly impacts revenue per user. We cannot trade a verified 40% engagement loss for an unverified satisfaction gain. The break-even point requires proof that the shorter sessions yield equal or higher completion rates of the core action (e.g., "Save Song" or "Create Playlist"). Currently, that proof is missing.

**Required Segmentation & Data Requests (For Data Engineering):**
To determine if this layout works for specific cohorts before considering a restricted rollout to new users, request the following cuts immediately:

1.  **Core Action Rate by Cohort:** Compare "Songs Played per Session" and "Songs Saved per Session" between Control and Variant.
    *   *Decision Rule:* If "Songs Played" dropped proportionally to time (≈40%), the layout hides content. **Stop.** If "Songs Played" remained flat while time dropped, the interface is genuinely more efficient. **Proceed to limited test.**
2.  **Power User vs. Casual Split:** Segment by users with >5 sessions/week vs. <2 sessions/week.
    *   *Hypothesis:* Power users may have specific intent and benefit from efficiency; casual users may need browsing time to find value. If the duration drop is isolated to casual users, a **new-user-only rollout** might be viable. If power users are also leaving faster, the change damages our most valuable segment.
3.  **Return Rate (Retention):** Check Day-1 and Day-7 return rates for the Variant group.
    *   *Decision Rule:* If short-term satisfaction does not translate to higher return rates, the survey metric is vanity.

**Next Steps for Friday Review:**
1.  **Hold Global Rollout:** Maintain current homepage for 100% of existing users.
2.  **Run Targeted Query:** Execute the segmentation above (Core Action, Power User split, Return Rate) using existing event logs.
3.  **Conditional Path:**
    *   If "Songs Played" is stable AND retention is flat/up: Restrict experiment to **New Users Only** for 2 weeks.
    *   If "Songs Played" dropped OR retention dipped: **Kill experiment** and revert to baseline.

Do not rely on the survey score alone. The behavioral data (duration and play counts) is the leading indicator of business health; the survey is a lagging sentiment check. Verify the volume of music consumed before claiming victory.

*Note: All metrics must be compared against our own pre-experiment baseline; no external industry benchmarks for "good" session duration apply here.*
