**Primary Metric Recommendation: Week-4 Retention of Core Value Delivery**

For your six-week pilot launching November 15, your single North Star metric must be **the percentage of subscribers who successfully receive and accept their second delivery within the promised window.**

Do not select immediate conversion rate or raw signups. With a break-even churn threshold of 4.5% monthly, acquisition volume is irrelevant if the logistics reality in the Klang Valley monsoon causes early attrition. A user who converts but churns before the second delivery destroys unit economics faster than a user who never signs up. Since your hypothesis prioritizes "delivery reliability" over price, this metric directly validates whether your value proposition holds under operational stress.

**Key Factors for Board & Operations Alignment**

1.  **Retention Precedes Acquisition:** Pouring the 3,200-person waitlist into a funnel with unverified delivery reliability is capital waste. If the monsoon delays cause the second delivery to fail, no onboarding flow optimization will save the business model. You must diagnose the retention leak before scaling acquisition.
2.  **The "Core Action" Definition:** In subscription commerce, the "aha moment" is not the signup; it is the successful completion of the recurring cycle. By measuring the *second* delivery, you filter out one-time purchasers and confirm the subscription habit. If this rate drops below 95.5% (inverse of 4.5% churn), the business is not viable regardless of onboarding conversion rates.
3.  **Operational Reality vs. Marketing Promise:** This metric forces alignment between the operations team (logistics performance) and growth (onboarding flows). If the metric fails, the board sees immediately whether the issue is the product (food quality), the promise (delivery window), or the segment (monsoon-affected areas), rather than hiding behind vanity metrics like "total boxes shipped."
4.  **Experiment Design for Onboarding Flows:** When testing your three onboarding flows, do not optimize for "signup completed." Optimize for "signup completed AND second delivery accepted." A flow that converts 20% but retains 50% is inferior to a flow that converts 10% but retains 90%. Set your sample size based on the number of *second deliveries* required to detect a statistically significant difference in retention, not just signups.

**This Week's Execution Steps**

1.  **Instrument the "Delivery Success" Event:** Define a specific event `subscription_cycle_completed` with properties: `cycle_number` (must be 2), `delivery_status` (on-time/delayed/failed), and `region` (Klang Valley vs. others). Do not track generic "page views."
2.  **Pre-commit the Experiment Readout:** For the three onboarding flows, declare the primary metric as "Week-4 Retention" and a guardrail metric as "Average Delivery Delay (hours)." Do not peek at results after week 1; read the data only after the second delivery cycle concludes for the cohort to avoid false positives from early noise.
3.  **Set the Stop Tripwire:** If the pilot cohort shows a second-delivery failure rate exceeding 4.5% by November 30, pause the broader waitlist rollout immediately to investigate logistics, regardless of how high the initial signup rate is.
