**Decision: Do not roll out to 100%. Extend the test with a segmented guardrail or revert immediately for the >18-month cohort.**

The aggregate 10% reduction in cancellation is `UNVERIFIED` as a net win because it masks a negative lift in your highest-value segment (tenure >18 months). In subscription economics, long-tenure users typically carry higher Lifetime Value (LTV) and lower acquisition costs than new users. A "win" driven by price-sensitive new users accepting a discounted pause, while simultaneously alienating loyalists who then churn completely, likely destroys net revenue even if the raw cancellation count drops.

**Recomputed Interpretation:**
You are observing a classic **Simpson's Paradox** risk where the aggregate metric improves while a critical sub-segment degrades.
1.  **The Mechanism:** New users (high price elasticity) utilize the pause option, artificially lowering the overall cancellation rate.
2.  **The Leak:** Long-term users (low price elasticity, high loyalty) are experiencing a *higher* cancellation rate. The hypothesis that they feel "insulted" by a discount or "frustrated" by friction is plausible; for loyalists, a discount offer can signal a lack of trust in the product's core value, and added steps increase friction for a decision they rarely make.
3.  **The Financial Reality:** If the revenue lost from one churned long-term subscriber exceeds the revenue retained from multiple new users pausing, the net effect is negative. The aggregate 10% drop is a vanity metric in this specific context because it weights all cancellations equally, ignoring the margin difference between cohorts.

**Required Actions for the Retention Marketing Lead:**

1.  **Immediate Segmentation Guardrail:** Split the analysis immediately. Calculate the **Net Revenue Impact** rather than just cancellation rates.
    *   *Formula:* `(LTV_new * Retained_New) + (LTV_loyal * Retained_Loyal)` for Treatment vs. Control.
    *   If the net revenue for the >18-month cohort is negative, the feature is failing its primary business goal despite the aggregate drop.

2.  **Targeted Rollout (The "Ship" Condition):** Only proceed if you can isolate the experience.
    *   **Action:** Deploy the new flow *only* to users with tenure <12 months.
    *   **Condition:** Revert to the standard flow for users >18 months immediately to stop the bleeding on loyalists.
    *   **Metric to Watch:** Monitor the cancellation rate of the >18-month group specifically for the next 7 days after reverting them. If it stabilizes to control levels, the hypothesis of "friction/insult" is confirmed.

3.  **Qualitative Validation (The "Why"):**
    *   Trigger a micro-survey or exit interview specifically for the >18-month users who cancelled in the treatment group. Ask: "Did the pause offer influence your decision?" and "How did the offer make you feel about the service?"
    *   Do not rely on assumptions about "insult" vs. "friction" without this data.

**Verdict:** The aggregate win is illusory if it trades high-value churn for low-value pauses. Treat the >18-month segment as a **guardrail metric** that has already been breached. Revert the change for this cohort today. Extend the test for new users only, pre-committing to a sample size that detects a 2% change in their specific retention curve, and read the result once at the end of the period. Do not peek at daily fluctuations.
