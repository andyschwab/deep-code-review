### Core Metrics for KiwiCollectibles: Balancing Seller Growth and Market Health

To determine whether to relax or tighten verification before July, stop measuring raw listing counts. That is a vanity metric driving your current confusion. Instead, track this specific set of actionable metrics tied directly to customer value and revenue.

**1. The North Star: Verified GMV per Active Buyer**
Replace "total listings" with **Gross Merchandise Value (GMV) transacted by verified sellers per active buyer**. This rises only when buyers trust the badge enough to purchase higher-value items. If this metric stagnates while listings rise, your hypothesis is correct: you are accumulating low-quality inventory that buyers ignore.

**2. The Input Metrics (The Levers)**
Track these three inputs to diagnose *why* the North Star is stuck:
*   **Verified Seller Activation Rate:** The percentage of sellers who complete verification and successfully list an item that receives *any* buyer engagement (clicks or watches) within 7 days.
    *   *Decision Rule:* If this is low, the process is too cumbersome or the badge carries no weight. If high but GMV is low, the inventory quality is the issue.
*   **Buyer Conversion Lift by Badge:** Compare the conversion rate (view-to-purchase) of verified items vs. unverified items for similar categories (e.g., vintage jerseys vs. pins).
    *   *Decision Rule:* If verified items do not convert significantly higher than unverified ones, buyers do not trust or notice the badge. Tightening requirements will only shrink supply without boosting demand.
*   **Listing Quality Score (LQS):** A composite of `(Views + Watches) / Listing Age` for new verified listings.
    *   *Decision Rule:* If LQS for new verified listings is lower than the baseline for unverified sellers, you are attracting spam or low-value collectors.

**3. The Guardrail: Seller Churn Post-Verification**
Measure the percentage of sellers who verify but fail to make a sale within 30 days and then leave the platform.
*   *Decision Rule:* If this churn spikes after tightening requirements, you are creating a "pay-to-play" barrier that excludes genuine small collectors without improving buyer trust.

### Immediate Analysis Plan (This Week)

Do not wait for July. Run this analysis on last month's data immediately:

1.  **Segment GMV Growth:** Split the 5% GMV growth into "Verified Seller GMV" vs. "Unverified Seller GMV." If verified sellers contributed <20% of that growth despite a 40% listing surge, the badge is failing to drive value.
2.  **Cohort Retention Check:** Compare the 30-day retention of buyers who purchased from verified sellers vs. those who bought from unverified sellers. If retention is identical, the verification adds no long-term value.
3.  **Funnel Drop-off:** Map the verification funnel. Identify exactly where serious collectors drop off. If >40% drop off at the document upload stage, the friction is too high for your niche market.

### Decision Framework for the Founding Team

*   **Tighten Requirements IF:** Verified items show a >15% conversion lift over unverified items, but the Listing Quality Score is dropping due to low-value spam.
*   **Relax Requirements IF:** The Verified Seller Activation Rate is <30% (process is too hard) AND there is no significant conversion lift (buyers don't care yet). Focus on marketing the badge's meaning before restricting access.
*   **Maintain & Iterate IF:** Conversion lift is positive but modest. Keep the current bar but improve badge visibility on search results rather than changing the rules.

Your assumption that "volume leads to transactions" is dangerous in a trust-based market. In vintage collectibles, one fake item can destroy buyer confidence permanently. Prioritize the *quality* of the transaction over the *quantity* of listings.
