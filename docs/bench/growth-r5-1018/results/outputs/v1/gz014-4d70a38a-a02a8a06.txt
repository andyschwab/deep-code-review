### Cohort Analysis Framework for BoxedDelight Onboarding Redesign

**Objective:** Isolate the impact of the January 20th onboarding flow on Month-2 retention (target: 62% vs. baseline 55%) to inform marketing budget allocation.

#### 1. Cohort Definition & Grouping Logic
To compare "apples to apples" amidst seasonal sign-up fluctuations, define cohorts by **Sign-Up Week**, not Month. Weekly granularity smooths seasonal spikes and aligns better with the January 20th launch date.

*   **Control Cohorts:** Users signing up between Dec 1 – Jan 19. Tag these by `acquisition_channel` and `onboarding_version: legacy`.
*   **Treatment Cohorts:** Users signing up Jan 20 – Mar 31. Tag these by `acquisition_channel` and `onboarding_version: new`.
*   **Segmentation Variable:** Create a binary property `preferences_selected` (True/False) captured at sign-up. This allows you to slice every cohort into "Engaged" vs. "Skipped" sub-groups to test your hypothesis that preference selection drives retention.

**Grouping Rule:** A user belongs to the cohort of the week they created their account. Do not mix users from different weeks, as external factors (ad spend, seasonality) vary weekly.

#### 2. Time Windows & Retention Calculation
Define "Month-2 Retention" strictly as a **binary state** within a specific window to avoid ambiguity.

*   **Time Zero (T0):** Date of sign-up.
*   **Measurement Window:** Days 60–89 post-sign-up.
    *   *Why:* "Month 2" implies the second full month of life. Checking exactly day 60 is too noisy; a 30-day window captures users who retained through the critical period.
*   **Retention Event:** The user must complete the **Core Action** (e.g., "Box Customized" or "Subscription Renewed") at least once within the Measurement Window.
*   **Formula:** `(Users in Cohort who performed Core Action in Days 60-89) / (Total Users in Cohort)`.

#### 3. Specific Drop-off Points to Monitor
Instrument these three events to diagnose *where* the new flow succeeds or fails:

1.  **Preference Selection Rate:** (% of users who complete the preference step). If the new flow increases friction here, overall volume may drop even if retention rises.
2.  **Activation Gap:** Time between sign-up and first "Box Customized." Monitor if the new flow accelerates this. Faster activation often correlates with higher Month-2 retention.
3.  **Day-30 Check-in:** A leading indicator. If Month-1 retention (Days 30-59) does not improve by mid-February, Month-2 targets are unlikely to be met. Use this for early course correction.

#### 4. Isolating Impact vs. Organic Trends
To prove the new flow caused the lift rather than organic trends or channel mix shifts:

*   **Channel-Neutral Comparison:** Compare `New Flow / Channel X` directly against `Legacy Flow / Channel X`. Do not aggregate all channels first; a shift toward higher-quality channels could fake a retention lift.
*   **The "Skipper" Control:** Within the Treatment Cohort, users who *skip* preferences act as an internal control. If `New Flow + Skipped` retains better than `Legacy + Skipped`, the flow itself (not just the preference selection) is driving value.
*   **Baseline Projection:** Plot the trailing 8 weeks of legacy retention. Extend this trend line linearly. Any deviation of the new cohorts above this projected line is your attributable lift.

#### 5. Decision Matrix for the Marketing Director
Use this table to translate data into budget actions:

| Result Scenario | Interpretation | Budget Action |
| :--- | :--- | :--- |
| **Total Retention > 62%** driven by **Preference Selectors** | Hypothesis confirmed: Preferences drive LTV. | Shift spend to channels with highest `preferences_selected` rates. Optimize ads to highlight customization. |
| **Total Retention > 62%** driven by **Skippers** | The new flow UX itself is superior, regardless of input. | Scale spend across all channels; de-prioritize forcing preference selection if it lowers conversion. |
| **Retention < 55%** | New flow introduces friction or misaligned expectations. | Halt spend increases. Revert to legacy flow for high-value channels while iterating. |
| **Retention Flat (55-58%)** | Impact is negligible or masked by seasonality. | Extend test duration to 6 weeks; do not reallocate budget yet. |

**This Week's Step:** Define the `preferences_selected` event property in your tracking plan immediately. Ensure the engineering team tags this boolean on the sign-up event before the Jan 20th launch. Without this property, you cannot segment the cohort to validate your core hypothesis.

*Note: All retention figures must be calculated from your internal analytics platform; no external benchmarks are applied. Ensure user IDs are pseudonymous and consistent across sessions to track the 60-89 day window accurately.*
