### North Star Metric: 48-Hour Completed Care Initiation
**Definition:** The percentage of new sign-ups who not only book but **attend** their first therapy session within 48 hours.
**Rationale:** "Booking" is a vanity metric in this context; it rises even if care quality drops (as your data shows). True customer value occurs only when the user receives care. If speed reduces attendance, the business is optimizing for empty calendar slots, not mental health outcomes.

### Input Metrics & Event Taxonomy
Instrument only these events to answer specific questions about the trade-off between speed and commitment:

1.  **Event:** `onboarding_completed`
    *   **Properties:** `duration_seconds`, `questions_answered_count`, `device_type`.
    *   **Question:** Does reducing friction correlate with lower downstream attendance?
2.  **Event:** `session_booked`
    *   **Properties:** `time_to_book_minutes`, `therapist_match_score` (if applicable), `scheduled_time_delta_hours`.
    *   **Question:** Are rushed bookings scheduling sessions too far in the future or with less suitable matches?
3.  **Event:** `session_attended` (The Critical Filter)
    *   **Properties:** `attended_within_48h` (boolean), `cancellation_reason` (user-reported).
    *   **Question:** Is the "leak" happening between booking and attendance?
4.  **Event:** `post_session_wellbeing_check`
    *   **Properties:** `anxiety_delta_score` (pre vs. post), `nps_score`.
    *   **Question:** Does the faster path compromise clinical efficacy? (Use this as a guardrail, not a primary growth lever yet).

### How to Read the Conflicting Signals
Do not compare your 12% baseline to an invented 18% target. Compare your current **Attendance Rate** against your historical **Attendance Rate** for the same cohort profile.

*   **The Diagnosis:** Your data suggests a "False Activation." Users are activating (booking) but not retaining (attending). This indicates the new flow may be creating low-commitment bookings or poor therapist matches.
*   **The Analysis:** Segment `session_attended` by `onboarding_completed.duration_seconds`.
    *   If the <2 minute group has significantly lower attendance than the 2–5 minute group, the "rushed" hypothesis is confirmed.
    *   If attendance is flat but `anxiety_delta_score` drops for the rushed group, you are compromising care quality.

### Recommendation for the Board & Investors
Present the shift from "Booking Conversion" to "Completed Care Initiation" as a maturity milestone, not a failure.
*   **Narrative:** "We discovered that optimizing for speed alone increased bookings by X% but decreased actual care delivery by Y%. We are pivoting our North Star to ensure every acquisition results in a completed session."
*   **Action:** Pause further reduction of onboarding steps. Instead, A/B test **commitment mechanisms** (e.g., a single high-friction question about goals or a confirmation step) within the fast flow to see if it restores attendance without sacrificing speed.
