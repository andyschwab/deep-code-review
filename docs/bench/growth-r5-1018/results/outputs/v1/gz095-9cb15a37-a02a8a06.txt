**Executive Recommendation**
Adopt **"Weekly Active Households Completing a Conservation Action"** as the primary North Star Metric. Do not use "Gallons Saved" or "Percentage Engaging" as the single top-line metric.

"Gallons Saved" is a **vanity metric** in this context; it rises or falls based on external factors like drought severity, seasonal heat, or baseline infrastructure leaks, none of which reflect the app's specific value. A city could see gallons drop due to rain while the app succeeds, or rise due to a heatwave while the app fails. It misaligns incentives by punishing partners during wet seasons.

"Percentage Engaging" measures activity but not **value**. Residents can click buttons without changing behavior.

The recommended metric focuses on the **core action** that drives value: a user logging a specific conservation step (e.g., fixing a leak, installing a low-flow head, completing an audit). This rises only when users derive real value and aligns directly with long-term behavior change.

**Normalization Strategy**
To address the concern about different climate conditions: **Do not normalize the North Star.** The strength of measuring "Actions Completed" is that it is inherently normalized. Whether it is raining in Springfield or dry in Riverton, the act of a household fixing a leak is a consistent unit of value.

Use "Gallons Saved" only as a **contextual input metric** in reports, presented alongside local weather data to explain variance, but never as the scorecard for GreenFlow's performance.
