**Required Transactional Data Slices**

    *   *Slice:* Compare Test Group vs. Control Group.
    *   *Purpose:* Validates the 1.5x frequency assumption. If frequency does not rise significantly, the program fails its primary growth input.

2.  **Gross Basket Size (Excluding the Reward):**
    *   *Metric:* Average transaction value (CLP) excluding the zero-rated free pastry line item.
    *   *Slice:* Pre-reward visit (visit #4) vs. Post-reward visit (visit #5+) for the Test Group; matched dates for Control.
    *   *Purpose:* Determines if users spend less on paid items when they know a reward is imminent or just redeemed.

3.  **Add-On Attachment Rate & Value:**
    *   *Metric:* Percentage of transactions containing paid add-ons (pastries, extras) and the average add-on revenue per transaction.
    *   *Slice:* Test Group (post-redemption) vs. Control Group.
    *   *Purpose:* Directly measures cannibalization. If the current 300 CLP average drops to near zero in the Test Group, the free pastry is simply replacing a paid one.

4.  **Net Margin Per User (The Decision Metric):**
    *   *Formula:* `(Visit Frequency × [Avg Basket - Cost of Goods Sold]) - (Redemption Rate × Cost of Free Pastry)`.
    *   *Slice:* Aggregate by cohort (Test vs. Control).
    *   *Purpose:* This is the only number the CFO needs. It combines increased frequency against the cost of the giveaway and any lost add-on revenue.

**Analysis Logic for the December Go/No-Go**

Do not present "enrollment" or "total points earned" to the director; these are vanity metrics. The analysis must answer one question: **Does the incremental margin from increased frequency exceed the cost of the free pastry plus any lost add-on sales?**

If the data shows frequency increased by only 0.5 visits instead of 1.5, the program is destroying margin.
