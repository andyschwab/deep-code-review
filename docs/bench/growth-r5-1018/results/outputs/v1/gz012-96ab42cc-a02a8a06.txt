**Executive Summary: Social Accountability Feature Retention Analysis**

**Diagnosis: Novelty Effect with Delayed Churn Risk**
The divergence in your retention curves indicates a classic **novelty effect** rather than sustainable habit formation. The 17-point lift (45% vs. 28%) at Day 30 confirms the feature successfully drives **activation** and early engagement. However, the convergence after Month 4 signals that the social mechanic is failing to provide compounding value once the initial curiosity fades. Users are not integrating the social accountability into their long-term identity; they are treating it as a temporary gamification layer. This is a **delayed churn risk**: you have acquired users who expect a social experience they cannot sustain, leading to a sharper drop-off later than the control group, which likely relies on intrinsic motivation from day one.

**How to Read the Curves for the Board**
Do not present this as a "retention problem" in isolation. Frame it as an **Activation vs. Retention mismatch**:
1.  **The Spike (Months 1–3):** The feature works as a marketing and onboarding tool. It lowers the barrier to entry.
2.  **The Convergence (Month 4+):** The "value prop" of the social feature expires. If the curve for the treatment group drops *faster* than the control group after convergence, the feature may actually be creating dependency or social friction that accelerates churn once the novelty wears off.
3.  **The Verdict:** The feature drives short-term metrics but does not currently drive the **North Star** of long-term habit sustainability. Doubling down on the *current* implementation for 2025 is high-risk without addressing the Month 4 cliff.

**Additional Signals to Investigate Immediately**
To distinguish between "feature fatigue" and "successful graduation" (where users no longer need the feature), analyze these specific cohorts and events:
*   **Feature Usage Decay:** Plot the weekly active usage of the social feature itself for the retained users. If usage drops to near-zero by Month 3 while retention stays flat, users have "graduated" (positive). If usage stays high but retention drops, the feature is becoming friction (negative).
*   **Social Graph Density:** Compare retention of users who formed **reciprocal connections** (two-way accountability) versus those with one-way or no connections. Sustainable habits usually require deep ties, not just presence.
*   **Core Action Frequency:** Measure the frequency of the *primary habit* (e.g., logging a workout) independent of the social click. If the social group logs fewer core actions by Month 4 than the control group, the social layer is distracting from the actual value.

**Recommended Next Steps (This Week)**
1.  **Segment the Month 4 Drop:** Isolate the specific week in Month 4 where the curves merge. Correlate this with any product changes, seasonal trends in Seoul, or natural expiration of initial social challenges.
2.  **Define a Guardrail Metric:** For any 2025 roadmap decisions, establish "Month 6 Retention" as the primary success metric, not Day 30. Any new social feature must prove it does not widen the gap at Month 4.
3.  **Qualitative Loop:** Launch a targeted survey to users who churned between Month 3 and 5 asking specifically about their experience with the social accountability aspect. Was it pressure? Boredom? Lack of active partners?

**Conclusion for Investment Decision**
Do not double down on scaling the feature as-is. The data supports investing in **retention mechanics within the social layer** (e.g., evolving challenges, deeper reciprocity) rather than using social features purely for acquisition. The current model buys time but does not build habits.
