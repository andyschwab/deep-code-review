### Three Data Points to Validate the "Real-Time Verification" Assumption

To test whether brewery managers value real-time verification over long-term impact, track these three specific events during your beta sign-up phase. Each metric directly answers a question about user intent and differentiates your industrial algae solution from generic offset providers.

**1. The "Live Data" Click-Through Rate (CTR)**
*   **Event to Instrument:** `verification_view_clicked`
*   **Definition:** The percentage of visiting brewery managers who click specifically on the "Live Sequestration Feed" or "Real-Time CO2 Tracker" demo link versus those who click on "Annual Impact Report" or "Certificate Download" within the same session.
*   **Why this validates the assumption:** If your assumption is correct, the CTR for the live feed will significantly outperform the static report links. Generic providers offer PDFs; if users ignore the live feed, your core differentiation is not the primary driver for conversion.
*   **Actionable Threshold:** If `verification_view_clicked` is <20% of total engagement while report downloads are >50%, the assumption is false. Pivot instrumentation to prioritize automated report generation over low-latency data streams.

**2. The "Specific Brewery" Filter Usage Rate**
*   **Event to Instrument:** `partner_data_filter_applied`
*   **Definition:** The frequency with which a user applies a filter to view data specific to *their* brewery (or a competitor's local brewery) versus viewing aggregate global platform data.
*   **Why this validates the assumption:** Industrial algae sequestration is hyper-local. Managers care about *their* tons, not the global total. High usage of this filter indicates a need for verified, attributable data for their own ESG reporting. Low usage suggests they view carbon offsetting as a commoditized, generic purchase where specific verification matters less.
*   **Actionable Threshold:** If <30% of engaged users attempt to isolate specific brewery data, the "industrial partner" angle may not be resonating. The technical co-founder should deprioritize complex per-partition data queries and focus on simplified bulk-purchase flows.

**3. The "Verification-to-Signup" Conversion Delta**
*   **Event to Instrument:** `signup_completed` tagged with `last_interaction_type`
*   **Definition:** Compare the conversion rate of users whose last interaction was viewing real-time verification data versus users whose last interaction was viewing pricing or long-term projections.
*   **Why this validates the assumption:** This isolates the causal link between seeing the data and committing. It proves whether the "real-time" feature is a nice-to-have or the actual closer.
*   **Actionable Threshold:** If the conversion rate for the "Real-Time" cohort is not at least 1.5x higher than the "Pricing/Report" cohort, the friction of building real-time infrastructure is not justified by sales lift.

### Implementation Note for Technical Co-Founder
Instrument these events now with a stable, pseudonymous user ID to track sessions across devices. Do not build complex dashboards yet; simply log the event names and properties (e.g., `view_type: "live"` vs `view_type: "report"`) to your backend. This allows you to compute the ratios above immediately after the beta month without needing a full analytics suite.

### Founding Team Decision Rule
If none of these three metrics show a strong signal toward real-time data by the end of the beta, stop development on low-latency verification features. Re-allocate engineering resources to automating the long-term compliance reports, as that is likely the actual purchase driver for this market segment.
