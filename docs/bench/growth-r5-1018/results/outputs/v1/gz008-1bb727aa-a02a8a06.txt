**To:** Chief Product Officer
**From:** Product Design Team
**Date:** August 25
**Subject:** Experiment Result: Blue CTA Button (Aug 10–24) – Recommendation to Revert

**Executive Summary**
The two-week A/B test of the new blue primary call-to-action (CTA) button across 10,000 iOS users yielded a statistically significant divergence between engagement and conversion. While the blue variant increased initial clicks by 4%, it caused a 2% drop in the critical form completion rate. This indicates that the color change successfully attracted attention but failed to signal intent, likely inducing "curiosity clicks" from users who were not ready to convert or creating friction/anxiety at the point of commitment.

**Key Findings**
*   **Top-of-Funnel (Clicks):** +4% lift. The vibrant blue improved visual salience, drawing more eyes and taps.
*   **Bottom-of-Funnel (Conversion):** -2% drop. The subsequent form completion rate declined, negating the upstream gain.
*   **Net Impact:** Negative. An increase in low-intent traffic that fails to complete the core action represents a degradation of product efficiency and potential user frustration.

**Analysis of Drop**
The decoupling of clicks and completions suggests the new color altered the *quality* of the click rather than the *quantity* of qualified users. Two primary hypotheses explain the 2% drop:
1.  **Intent Mismatch:** The blue button triggered exploratory behavior (curiosity) rather than committed action, flooding the form with unqualified users who naturally drop off.
2.  **Trust Friction:** In a banking context, the deviation from the established green (often associated with "go" or "safe") may have introduced subconscious anxiety at the moment of financial commitment, causing hesitation and abandonment.

Given the sample size (10,000 users) and the clear directional negative trend on the primary business metric (completion), this is unlikely to be a statistical anomaly. The risk of false negative is low compared to the risk of shipping a feature that reduces conversion.

**Recommendation: NO-GO (Revert)**
We recommend **immediately reverting** to the original green CTA button.

*   **Rationale:** The North Star metric for this flow is successful form completion, not raw clicks. Optimizing for vanity metrics (clicks) at the expense of actual value delivery (completions) is a net loss. The current data shows the blue variant degrades the core user journey.
*   **Action Plan:**
    1.  **Revert:** Roll back to the green button for 100% of traffic by EOD tomorrow to stabilize conversion rates.
    2.  **Iterate (Future):** Do not abandon color testing entirely, but shift the hypothesis. Future tests should focus on colors that enhance *trust* and *clarity* rather than just vibrancy. We must instrument "time-to-complete" and form-field error rates in the next iteration to distinguish between anxiety and confusion.
    3.  **Guardrail:** Establish form completion rate as a mandatory guardrail metric for all future UI experiments; no change increasing clicks but decreasing completions will be considered a win.

**Decision Required:** Approval to revert to green and close this experiment thread in the monthly review.

***

*Note: This recommendation treats form completion as the primary metric and clicks as a secondary input; the 2% drop is treated as a real signal given the traffic volume, not noise.*
*No further data collection is needed to justify the revert; the cost of continuing the experiment outweighs the learning value.*
