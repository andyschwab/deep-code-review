**Recommendation: Do not adopt Total Transaction Volume or Average Order Value (AOV) as your primary North Star metric.** Both are vanity metrics in your current context: Volume ignores your 15% rising logistics costs, and AOV ignores whether those large orders are actually profitable after fulfillment. Instead, adopt **"Contribution Margin per Transaction"** (Revenue minus Cost of Goods Sold minus Logistics Cost) as your single North Star metric for the upcoming quarter.

**Why Volume and AOV Fail You Now**
*   **Total Transaction Volume:** You explicitly stated logistics costs rose 15% while assuming scale would fix unit economics. This assumption is currently false. Driving volume without fixing the cost leak means you are scaling losses. Investors seeing "market dominance" via volume while margins shrink will lose confidence once the burn rate is revealed.
*   **Average Order Value (AOV):** A high AOV does not guarantee profit. If a high-value order requires expensive, heavy shipping or special handling that exceeds the margin, it destroys value. Without granular category profitability data, optimizing for AOV is blind.

**The Case for Contribution Margin per Transaction**
This metric rises *only* when a transaction adds real economic value after variable costs. It directly addresses your logistics crisis and aligns incentives for both operations and sales.
*   **For Logistics Partners:** It forces the team to optimize shipping routes and packaging *before* pushing more volume, as inefficient deliveries directly lower the metric.
*   **For Investors:** It proves sustainable unit economics rather than hollow growth.
*   **For Daily Decisions:** It stops the team from accepting "loss leader" high-volume artisan goods that cost more to ship than they earn.

**Implementation Plan (This Week)**
Since you lack granular category data, you cannot calculate this perfectly yet. You must instrument the specific events to make this metric visible.

1.  **Instrument the "True Cost" Event:** Immediately tag every transaction event with three properties: `gross_value`, `cogs_estimate` (even if rough), and `actual_logistics_cost`. Do not wait for perfect accounting; use the 15% rise factor to estimate current logistics costs per zone if exacts are missing, but label these `UNVERIFIED` until real data flows.
2.  **Define the Formula:** North Star = Sum(`gross_value` - `cogs_estimate` - `actual_logistics_cost`) / Count(`completed_transactions`).
3.  **Run a "Stop-Loss" Cohort Analysis:** Segment last month's 25,000 transactions by artisan region or product weight class. Identify which segments have a negative contribution margin.
    *   *Decision Rule:* If a segment shows negative margin, pause marketing spend for that segment immediately. Do not acquire more users for a leaky bucket.
4.  **Set the Guardrail:** While optimizing for margin, set a guardrail on **Retention Rate of Artisans**. If you become too strict on margins and reject too many artisan listings, supply dries up. If artisan retention drops >5% week-over-week, the margin target is too aggressive.

**Immediate Action for Leadership**
Present this to investors not as a pivot away from growth, but as a "Unit Economics Correction." State clearly: "We are pausing raw volume targets to ensure every additional transaction contributes positively to cash flow given the 15% logistics inflation." This demonstrates fiscal maturity rather than blind expansion.
