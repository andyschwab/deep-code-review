### Presentation Framework: Checkout Speed vs. Accessibility Compliance

**1. The Core Conflict: Efficiency Gain vs. Exclusion Risk**
Frame the narrative not as "speed vs. safety," but as "verified conversion lift vs. unverified exclusion cost."
*   **The Gain:** Highlight the **4% sales lift** as a verified outcome of the two-week test (Nov 3–17). Explicitly state this is a *total population* figure.
*   **The Breach:** Present the contrast ratio drop (4.8 → 2.9) as a definitive failure of the pre-test assumption (minimum threshold > 3.0 for WCAG AA compliance). Note that 2.9 fails legal standards for low-vision users, creating immediate liability.
*   **The Missing Variable:** Emphasize that the current 4% lift is an aggregate. It does not yet isolate whether the gain came from users *without* visual impairments while conversion *dropped* for those relying on high-contrast modes or screen readers. The net gain may be masking a total loss of accessibility-driven revenue.

**2. Required Data Points for the Trade-off Analysis**
To move from "conflicting metrics" to a decision, request the following specific cuts from the raw data before the meeting:
*   **Segmented Conversion Rates:** Break down the 4% lift by user agent or assistive technology flag (if available). Did conversion rise for mouse/keyboard users while falling for screen reader users?
*   **Error Rate by Device/Mode:** Compare checkout abandonment errors during the test period for high-contrast mode users vs. standard users. A spike in errors for the former confirms active exclusion.
*   **Legal Exposure Estimate:** Quantify the "at-risk" user base. If 5% of your 4,200 monthly users rely on accessibility features, calculate the revenue value of that segment. If the new layout reduces their conversion to zero, does the 4% gain from the other 95% mathematically offset the 100% loss of the 5%?
*   **Session Time Context:** Clarify if the 18-second reduction represents genuine efficiency or users abandoning the flow due to unreadable text. Correlate shorter sessions with lower completion rates for the accessibility segment.

**3. Framing the Decision: Rollback vs. Partial Fix**
Present three distinct paths based on the data gaps, leaving the final choice to the CEO and Legal:
*   **Option A: Immediate Rollback.**
    *   *Trigger:* If Legal defines any WCAG AA failure as non-negotiable liability, or if segmented data shows *any* decline in conversion for accessibility users.
    *   *Narrative:* "We prioritize compliance and inclusive revenue over unverified speed gains. We restore the baseline and re-test with a compliant design."
*   **Option B: Targeted Remediation (The "Fix" Path).**
    *   *Trigger:* If segmented data proves the 4% lift is universal (including accessibility users) *and* Legal agrees a rapid patch is feasible within 48 hours.
    *   *Narrative:* "The speed gain is real and inclusive, but the contrast is a bug, not a feature. We deploy a CSS-only contrast fix immediately while keeping the layout, then re-validate."
*   **Option C: Controlled Canary with Guardrails.**
    *   *Trigger:* Only if the business accepts temporary non-compliance risk to quantify the exact revenue loss of exclusion.
    *   *Narrative:* "We limit the new layout to 10% of traffic excluding known assistive-tech users, measuring the specific revenue delta while we engineer a compliant contrast solution."

**4. Recommended Next Step for Tomorrow**
Do not recommend a final verdict. Instead, propose a **24-hour data sprint**: "Before deciding, we must segment the 4% lift by accessibility needs. If the gain excludes our low-vision customers, the 'efficiency' is actually churn. I will have this segmented breakdown by 9:00 AM tomorrow to inform the rollback/fix decision."

*Note: The 4% lift is currently `UNVERIFIED` as a net positive because it lacks segmentation against the excluded cohort. Do not present the aggregate lift as a pure win.*
*Ensure the "contrast ratio" metric is explicitly linked to the specific legal standard (e.g., WCAG 2.1 AA) your company has adopted.*
