### Diagnostic Framework: Funnel Leakage & Hypothesis Testing

**North Star Definition:** Shift focus from "Demo Requests" (vanity) to **"Qualified Pipeline Value Created"** (actionable). This metric rises only when a prospect with realistic potential advances, filtering out volume that does not convert to revenue.

**1. The Data Slices (Isolate the Leakage)**
To determine if the issue is lead quality, sales capacity, or product fit, slice the last two months of data against the prior baseline by these dimensions. Do not aggregate; the average hides the break.

*   **Lead Source Granularity:** Break down the 35% increase by specific content asset and channel (e.g., "Whitepaper A via LinkedIn" vs. "Blog Post B via Organic").
    *   *Reading Rule:* If one specific source drives 80% of the volume but has a <5% close rate, the content is attracting the wrong persona. If all sources show a uniform drop, the issue is systemic (e.g., sales capacity or pricing).
*   **Company Size / ICP Fit:** Segment by employee count or revenue band (your Ideal Customer Profile).
    *   *Reading Rule:* Compare the close rate of "Small" vs. "Enterprise" segments before and after the campaign. If Enterprise close rates collapsed while Small remained stable, the campaign diluted the funnel with non-ICP traffic.
*   **Sales Cycle Velocity:** Measure the days from "Demo Request" to "Close/Won" and "Close/Lost."
    *   *Reading Rule:* If velocity has slowed significantly while volume rose, the sales team is likely overwhelmed (capacity constraint), causing them to drop complex, high-value deals first.
*   **Deal Size Distribution:** Plot the histogram of deal sizes, not just the average.
    *   *Reading Rule:* Determine if the $12k average is due to a few massive losses (outliers) or a structural shift where *no* deals >$15k are closing.

**2. Hypotheses to Test**
Based on the slices above, validate one of these three hypotheses:

*   **H1: The "Wrong Door" Hypothesis (Lead Quality).** The content marketing attracted users who cannot afford or do not need the full platform.
    *   *Validation:* Close rates for ICP-sized companies remain ~22%, but non-ICP close rates are <5%.
    *   *Action:* Marketing adjusts targeting/messaging to filter smaller accounts pre-demo.
*   **H2: The "Capacity Ceiling" Hypothesis (Sales Process).** The 35% volume spike exceeded the sales team's bandwidth, causing them to rush or neglect high-touch enterprise deals.
    *   *Validation:* Response time to demo requests increased >48 hours; cycle time for large deals elongated; close rates drop uniformly across all segments.
    *   *Action:* Implement lead scoring to route only high-probability leads to senior reps; hire SDRs to qualify before demo.
*   **H3: The "Product-Market Mismatch" Hypothesis (Fit).** The new audience has a genuine need for the demo but finds the product lacking for their specific use case during the trial/demo.
    *   *Validation:* High engagement during demo, but specific objection codes (e.g., "missing feature X") dominate lost reasons across all segments.
    *   *Action:* Product team investigates feature gaps; Sales adjusts qualification criteria to exclude use cases requiring missing features.

**3. This-Week Step**
1.  **Query:** Run a cohort analysis comparing "Close Rate by Company Size" for the last 60 days vs. the prior 60 days.
2.  **Threshold:** If Enterprise (>100 employees) close rate is <15%, the leak is lead quality (H1). If it remains >20% but volume handled per rep dropped, the leak is capacity (H2).
3.  **Decision:** Present this single chart to the VPs. If H1, Marketing owns the fix (refine content). If H2, Sales owns the fix (process/capacity). No further discussion until this slice is viewed.

*Note: All metrics must be compared to your internal baseline; no external benchmarks are used. Ensure "Company Size" is captured as a structured property at the demo request stage to enable this slicing.*
