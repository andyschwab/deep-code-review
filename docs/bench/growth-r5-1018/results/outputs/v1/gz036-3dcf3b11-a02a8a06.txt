**Strategic Recommendation for Q3 Budget Allocation**

**Decision:** Optimize paid social ad sets for **predicted Lifetime Value (LTV)** cohorts, not lowest Acquisition Cost (CAC).

**Rationale:** Your current unit economics show an LTV:CAC ratio of 4:1 (1,800 SEK / 450 SEK). While aggressive prospecting lowers the denominator (CAC), it often attracts low-intent browsers who inflate top-line signups but fail to convert to high-value purchases, degrading the numerator (LTV). In a constrained economic climate, cash flow preservation is critical; acquiring customers who do not generate repeat revenue or high-margin initial orders wastes budget faster than paying a premium for high-quality traffic. Optimizing for predicted LTV aligns daily decisions with long-term profitability, ensuring the 450 SEK spend buys genuine value, not just clicks.

**Budget Split Strategy:**
Allocate 70% of the Q3 budget to **retention and reactivation** campaigns targeting existing high-LTV segments. This leverages your installed base for immediate cash flow with lower marginal cost. Allocate 30% to **prospecting**, strictly gated by creative and audience signals that correlate with your highest LTV deciles (e.g., users engaging with "durability" or "investment piece" content rather than "discount" hooks).

**Data Gaps Skewing Attribution:**
1.  **Cross-Device & Offline Conversion Tracking:** Furniture purchases often involve research on mobile but conversion on desktop, or offline showroom visits. If your attribution model is last-click digital-only, you are undercounting the true LTV of social channels that initiate the journey, falsely making them appear inefficient.
2.  **Time-Decay Mismatch:** Standard 7-day or 28-day attribution windows miss the long consideration cycle of furniture. You are likely attributing sales to generic brand search or direct traffic that were actually nurtured by social ads weeks prior.
3.  **Return & Warranty Data Integration:** If "LTV" is calculated on gross sales without netting out returns (common in DTC furniture) or warranty costs, your 1,800 SEK figure is inflated. Marketing optimization based on gross revenue will scale unprofitable acquisition.

**Accounting for Seasonality:**
Furniture demand in Sweden peaks in late summer (pre-autumn) and pre-Christmas. To account for this:
*   **Normalize by Cohort:** Do not compare raw month-over-month CAC. Compare Q3 acquisition cohorts against Q3 cohorts from previous years to isolate seasonal lift from campaign performance.
*   **Forward-Looking LTV:** Adjust your predicted LTV downward for acquisitions made in low-season months (e.g., January) where time-to-first-purchase is naturally longer, preventing the algorithm from prematurely killing viable audiences.
*   **Budget Pacing:** Front-load the prospecting budget in early Q3 to capture the back-to-school/autumn renovation wave, then shift heavily to retention in late Q3 to maximize share-of-wallet before the holiday spending shift to other categories.

**Immediate Next Steps (Before July 1st):**
1.  **Re-calculate LTV** net of returns and shipping costs for the last 12 months to validate the 1,800 SEK baseline.
2.  **Implement a "Value-Based Bidding"** signal in your ad platform using a hashed customer list of your top 20% LTV users as the seed audience, explicitly instructing the algorithm to find lookalikes of *profit*, not just *converters*.

*Note: Ensure your "predicted LTV" model is updated weekly with actual purchase data; static predictions decay rapidly in volatile economic conditions. Without verified return rates, the 1,800 SEK LTV remains an unverified upper bound.*
