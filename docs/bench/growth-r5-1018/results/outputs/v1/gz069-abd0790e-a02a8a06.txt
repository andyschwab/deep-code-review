**Strategy Memo: Campaign Success Criteria for SavannaGive**

**To:** Board of Directors
**From:** Operations Director
**Date:** February 15
**Subject:** Defining Success Beyond Total Revenue

To balance immediate cash flow with long-term sustainability, we must reject "Total Dollars Raised" as our primary success metric. While $50,000 in two weeks validates initial interest, it is a vanity metric that obscures donor behavior. It treats a one-time $1,000 gift and twenty $50 monthly pledges as equivalent, despite their vastly different impacts on future operations.

**The North Star: Net Present Value (NPV) of Donor Cohorts**
Success should be defined by the projected lifetime value of the donors acquired in this campaign, not the immediate sum. We will evaluate the campaign based on the **ratio of recurring donors to one-time donors**, weighted by the **six-month retention rate** of those recurring donors.

**Logical Trade-offs and Evaluation Criteria**

1.  **Recurring Donor Count vs. Average Gift Size**
    *   *Trade-off:* Prioritizing average gift size maximizes immediate cash flow, solving short-term liquidity needs for well construction. However, it risks over-indexing on one-time donors who churn after the initial emotional peak of the campaign.
    *   *Decision Rule:* If the campaign yields high total revenue but a recurring donor count below 15% of the total donor base, we classify it as a "Cash Flow Win, Sustainability Loss." Future themes must shift messaging from "fund a specific well" (one-time) to "join a water club" (recurring), even if it lowers the initial average gift.

2.  **Immediate Revenue vs. Six-Month Retention**
    *   *Trade-off:* Aggressive acquisition tactics (e.g., matching gifts for first-timers) boost Day-1 revenue but often attract low-intent donors who cancel within 90 days. Focusing on retention requires upfront investment in onboarding (welcome sequences, impact updates) which delays visible ROI.
    *   *Decision Rule:* We will measure the "Break-Even Retention Month." If the cost to acquire a recurring donor is not recovered by their cumulative gifts within six months, the acquisition strategy is unsustainable. A campaign with lower initial revenue but a 6-month retention rate above 70% is superior to a high-revenue campaign with 40% retention.

3.  **The Verdict Framework for Next Week**
    To decide on future themes, the Board should apply this hierarchy:
    *   **Primary Metric:** Percentage of total revenue derived from donors committing to monthly giving. Target: >40%.
    *   **Guardrail Metric:** Total cash raised must exceed the operational cost of the campaign by 2x to ensure immediate liquidity.
    *   **Leading Indicator:** Activation rate of first-month repeat gifts (do they give a second time within 45 days?). This predicts the six-month retention rate before the data actually exists.

**Recommendation**
Adopt a "Sustainability-First" scorecard. If the current campaign data shows that recurring donors constitute less than 30% of the donor base, the next campaign theme must pivot to community-building narratives rather than urgent crisis appeals, accepting a potential 20% dip in immediate total revenue in exchange for a projected 50% increase in donor lifetime value.

***

*Note: All retention projections must be calculated against SavannaGive's own historical baseline; no external nonprofit benchmarks should be used to set targets. Exact figures for the current campaign's recurring split are required to finalize the "Ship/Pivot" decision.*
