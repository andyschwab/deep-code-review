### Three Outcome-Focused Definitions of Success for SafeHaiti Connect

**1. The "Verified Handshake" Rate (Activation Integrity)**
*Definition:* The percentage of registered volunteers who complete a verified two-way confirmation loop (Task Assigned → Acknowledgement Received) within a defined operational window, regardless of the number of transmission attempts required.
*Why it matters:* This rejects "sent" messages as a vanity metric. It measures the system's ability to overcome intermittent connectivity to achieve the core value: confirmed human coordination.
*Reading Rule:* Compare the 450 confirmed against the 1,200 registered (37.5% baseline). Success is not a fixed industry percentage but an improvement against this own baseline over subsequent drills. If the rate drops in future drills, the infrastructure or protocol is degrading; if it rises, the retry logic or offline caching is working. Distinguish "never received" from "ignored" by instrumenting a local "outbox retry count" event on the device; high retries followed by success indicates network friction, while zero retries indicates user refusal or device off.

**2. The "Task-to-Action" Latency Cohort (Operational Velocity)**
*Definition:* The median time elapsed between a volunteer's *first successful receipt* of a task and their *first field-reported status update*, segmented by geographic zone (e.g., Southern Peninsula vs. others).
*Why it matters:* In disaster response, speed is the actual product, not app engagement. This metric isolates whether network delays are materially slowing down field operations or if users are acting immediately once connected.
*Reading Rule:* Do not average this globally. Segment by region to see if the Southern Peninsula cohort has a significantly higher latency than others. If latency is high only in the south, the issue is infrastructure; if uniform, the issue may be task clarity or training. This tells funders exactly where the bottleneck lives without needing perfect real-time data.

**3. The "Offline-First" Completion Ratio (Resilience Efficiency)**
*Definition:* The proportion of total completed tasks that were accepted and logged locally on the device during a connectivity blackout and successfully synced only when coverage returned.
*Why it matters:* This validates the tool's specific design hypothesis for rural regions. A high ratio proves the tool is viable *because* it works without constant signal, turning "poor coverage" from a failure mode into a managed constraint.
*Reading Rule:* Instrument a `task_accepted_offline` event and a `sync_success` event. If this ratio is low, users are waiting for signal to even open the app (design flaw). If high, the tool is successfully decoupling human action from network availability. This is the primary metric to demonstrate viability to funders who fear infrastructure gaps.
