**Deliverable: North Star Metric Recommendation for BookSwap PL**

**Recommended North Star:** **Weekly Successful Matches** (defined as a buyer and seller confirming an exchange).

**Why this over "Total Listings":**
While your hypothesis that listings drive liquidity is logical, "Total Listings Created" is a vanity metric in this context. It rises even if the books are irrelevant, priced incorrectly, or never sold. Optimizing for listings risks incentivizing spam, low-quality posts, or users listing books they don't actually own, which degrades buyer trust and wastes their time. "Weekly Successful Matches" rises *only* when real value is delivered to both sides of the marketplace. It inherently balances supply (listings) and demand (searches/buys) without encouraging noise.

**Trade-off Analysis for Stakeholder Presentation:**

| Metric Option | Buyer Behavior Impact | Seller Behavior Impact | Risk of Spam/Low Quality | Verdict |
| :--- | :--- | :--- | :--- :--- |
| **Total Listings Created** | May increase search friction due to noise; lowers trust if many items are unavailable. | Incentivizes volume over quality; encourages dumping irrelevant books. | **High** | Reject. Measures activity, not value. |
| **Searches Performed** | Encourages clicking but not buying; frustrates users if results don't convert. | No direct incentive to list better; ignores supply side. | **Medium** | Reject. Vanity metric. |
| **Weekly Successful Matches** | Encourages finding relevant books quickly; builds trust in platform reliability. | Incentivizes accurate descriptions, fair pricing, and responsiveness to ensure the match completes. | **Low** | **Adopt.** Aligns with actual marketplace health. |

**Ensuring the Metric Doesn't Incentivize Spam:**
To prevent gaming "Successful Matches" (e.g., users fake-matching to boost numbers), pair the North Star with two **Input Metrics** and one **Guardrail**:
1.  **Input Metric A:** *Quality-Adjusted Listing Rate* (Listings that receive at least one inquiry within 48 hours). This ensures sellers focus on desirable books.
2.  **Input Metric B:** *Time-to-Match* (Median days from listing to successful match). This monitors liquidity efficiency.
3.  **Guardrail Metric:** *Dispute/Cancel Rate post-Match*. If this spikes, it indicates users are gaming the "success" definition. If the guardrail breaches a threshold (e.g., >5% cancellation after "match"), the design win is invalidated.

**Immediate Next Steps (Before Feb 10th):**
1.  **Instrumentation:** Define the exact event `match_confirmed` (triggered only when both parties verify the exchange) and ensure it is tracked distinct from `listing_created`.
2.  **Homepage Redesign Rule:** Evaluate every homepage design variant solely on its projected lift to *Weekly Successful Matches*, not clicks or listings. If a design increases listings but flatlines matches, it is a failure.
3.  **Baseline Check:** Calculate last semester's weekly match rate from the 3,000 transactions to set a realistic baseline for the spring semester.

**Note:** Do not fabricate a "good" conversion rate; compare your spring performance strictly against your own winter baseline. Ensure user identifiers used for tracking matches are pseudonymous to comply with privacy standards.
