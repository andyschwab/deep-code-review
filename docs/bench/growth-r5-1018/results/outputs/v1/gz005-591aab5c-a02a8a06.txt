**To:** VP of Engineering, BookFindr
**Subject:** Recommendation: Hold Rollback, Execute Targeted Analysis by 09:00 Tomorrow

**Decision:** Do not roll back yet. An 8% lift in click-through rate (CTR) indicates improved relevance or engagement, but the 15% drop in Average Order Value (AOV) suggests a mix shift toward lower-priced items or a suppression of premium titles. Rolling back now sacrifices the engagement gain before we confirm the revenue net effect. The goal is to determine if the *Revenue Per Visitor* (RPV) has actually declined or if we are simply selling more units at a lower price point.

**Step-by-Step Analysis Plan (Execute Tonight):**

1.  **Calculate the Net Revenue Impact (RPV):**
    Immediately compute Revenue Per Visitor for the post-launch period vs. the prior baseline.
    *   *Formula:* `RPV = CTR × Conversion Rate × AOV`.
    *   *Logic:* If the 8% CTR increase and any conversion lift offset the 15% AOV drop, total revenue may be flat or up. If RPV is down, quantify the exact deficit. This single number dictates the financial urgency.

2.  **Segment by Price Tier (The "Mix Shift" Test):**
    Split search sessions into three cohorts based on the price of the clicked/booked item: Low (<€10), Medium (€10–€25), and High (>€25).
    *   *Query:* Compare the distribution of clicks and conversions across these tiers pre- and post-launch.
    *   *Hypothesis:* If the "High" tier click share dropped significantly while "Low" tier rose, the algorithm is successfully finding cheaper books users prefer, but burying premium ones. This confirms a ranking bias, not a user dissatisfaction issue.

3.  **Analyze "Premium Title" Visibility:**
    Isolate the top 50 highest-revenue generating SKUs from last month.
    *   *Query:* Measure their average rank position and impression share in search results now vs. before.
    *   *Action:* If these specific titles have fallen below the fold (position >10), the algorithm is over-penalizing them despite their rating. This is a fixable tuning issue, not a fundamental failure.

4.  **Check Conversion Rate by Tier:**
    Verify if the drop in AOV is accompanied by a spike in conversion rate for cheaper books.
    *   *Logic:* If users are buying cheaper books at a much higher rate, the "confidence" hypothesis holds true—they are finding affordable books they like quickly. If conversion rates are flat across all tiers while AOV drops, users are being forced into cheaper options they didn't intend to buy, which risks long-term retention.

**Decision Matrix for Tomorrow Morning:**

*   **Scenario A (RPV is Flat/Up):** **Keep the update.** The engagement gain is monetizing effectively via volume. Task search team to gently re-weight price to recover some AOV without losing CTR.
*   **Scenario B (RPV is Down <5%) & Premium Titles Buried:** **Keep the update but patch.** The engagement win is real. Apply an immediate "business boost" rule to force top-revenue SKUs back into the top 10 results while retaining the new rating logic for the rest.
*   **Scenario C (RPV is Down >5%) & Conversion Flat:** **Rollback.** The algorithm is degrading commercial intent without sufficient volume compensation. Revert before the weekend peak.

**Immediate Next Step:** Run the RPV calculation and Price Tier segmentation now. Have the data ready by 08:00 to support the 09:00 decision.
