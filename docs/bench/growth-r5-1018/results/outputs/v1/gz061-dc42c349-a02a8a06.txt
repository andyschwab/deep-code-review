### LTV Validation & Cash Flow Metrics for CFO

To validate your lifetime value (LTV) model against actual cash flow and inform the Q3 budget, calculate these four specific metrics using your existing transactional data:

**1. Cohort-Based Net Revenue Retention (NRR) by Month**
*   **Logic:** Group users by their start month (March cohort). Calculate the revenue generated in Month 1, Month 2, and Month 3 separately.
*   **Formula:** `(Revenue from Cohort in Month X) / (Revenue from Cohort in Month 1)`.
*   **Why:** This isolates whether the "5x revenue" assumption holds. If retention drops sharply after Month 1, the LTV model is overstated. Do not use cumulative signups (1,200); use active paying users per month.

**2. Adjusted Month-1 ARPU (Excluding Referral Bonuses)**
*   **Logic:** Your current tracking likely inflates Month 1 value by counting referral credits as revenue. You must strip this out to see true cash flow.
*   **Formula:** `(Total Month 1 Cash Collected - Value of Referral Credits Issued) / Active Users in Month 1`.
*   **Why:** If referral bonuses are recorded as revenue, your CAC payback period is artificially shortened. The CFO needs the *cash collected* figure, not the accounting gross including future liabilities.

**3. The "Break-Even" Retention Threshold**
*   **Logic:** Determine the exact retention rate required in Month 3 to justify the current acquisition cost.
*   **Formula:** Solve for `R` where `(Month 1 ARPU) + (Month 2 ARPU * R) + (Month 3 ARPU * R^2) >= CAC + Fulfillment Costs`.
*   **Why:** This gives the CFO a binary tripwire. If actual Month 3 retention is below `R`, the Q3 marketing budget must be paused or reduced.

**4. Referral Conversion Lag**
*   **Logic:** Measure the time between a user receiving a referral bonus and the referred friend actually paying.
*   **Why:** If the lag is >30 days, counting the bonus in Month 1 distorts cash flow projections for Q3.

#### Interpreting a 10% Drop in Average Order Value (AOV) in Month 2

If AOV drops 10% in Month 2, do not assume immediate churn. Interpret this via two scenarios before acting:

*   **Scenario A: The "Referral Cliff" (Data Artifact).** If the Month 1 AOV was inflated by one-time referral credits or introductory bundles that do not repeat in Month 2, the drop is expected and not a failure of product value. *Action:* Verify if the drop matches the exact value of the removed bonuses. If yes, ignore the drop for LTV modeling; focus purely on retention rates.
*   **Scenario B: The "Value Mismatch" (Real Risk).** If the drop persists after adjusting for bonuses, it indicates users are downgrading or skipping add-ons. Since your model relies on Month 3+ value, a Month 2 dip suggests the "premium" perception is fading before the critical retention window. *Action:* This invalidates the "5x revenue" assumption. The CFO should treat Q3 marketing spend as high-risk until Month 2 AOV stabilizes.
