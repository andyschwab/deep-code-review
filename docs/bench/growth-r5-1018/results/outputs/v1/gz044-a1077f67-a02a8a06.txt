**Incident Report: Real-Time Collaboration Module (Flag Test Oct 2–9)**

**Executive Summary**
The A/B test for the new real-time collaboration module delivered high user adoption but failed critical performance guardrails, creating an immediate risk to Service Level Agreements (SLAs) and enterprise client retention. While 40% of the exposed cohort engaged daily—validating the feature's value proposition—the infrastructure incurred a 120ms average latency increase (140% over the <50ms assumption) and a 3.5% error rate spike during peak loads. The negative performance impact outweighs the engagement gain for our largest enterprise client, whose complaints signal a breach of trust and potential contract violations.

**Quantified User Experience Degradation**
The trade-off between adoption and stability is currently unsustainable. The 120ms latency addition directly degrades the core "concurrent edit" experience, introducing perceptible lag that undermines the real-time promise. More critically, the 3.5% error rate during peak hours represents a systemic failure for a collaboration tool; in a session involving multiple concurrent edits, this error probability compounds, leading to frequent data loss or sync failures for active users. The high engagement rate (40%) ironically amplifies the blast radius: more users are actively hitting the broken path, generating more errors and complaints per minute than a low-adoption feature would.

**SLA and Business Risk**
The observed metrics likely violate standard enterprise SLAs regarding availability and response time. A 3.5% error rate typically breaches "five nines" or even "four nines" availability guarantees when sustained during peak windows. The specific complaint from the largest enterprise client elevates this from a technical debt issue to a revenue retention threat. Without geographic segmentation data due to incomplete logging, we must assume the degradation is global or affecting key regions indiscriminately, preventing us from isolating the risk to a non-critical subset of users.

**Recommendation**
**Immediate Action:** Kill the feature flag immediately.
**Rationale:** The current state presents an unquantified but high-probability risk of further SLA breaches and client churn. The "high adoption" metric is irrelevant if the underlying service is unreliable; retaining the flag to gather more data on a broken system compounds the reputational damage. The decision to attempt a hotfix can only be made *after* the system is stabilized to the baseline (flag off). Re-enabling or patching live while error rates are elevated violates the principle of protecting the core business function.

**Next Steps for Decision Maker**
1.  **Revert:** Disable the flag to restore baseline latency and error rates immediately.
2.  **Investigate:** Prioritize the logging gap to determine if the latency spike is geographic or architectural before authorizing any future rollout.

*Note: This assessment relies on the provided aggregate data; specific SLA penalty clauses should be reviewed by Legal once the system is stable.*
*Incomplete logging prevents a precise root-cause analysis, necessitating a full rollback before further experimentation.*
