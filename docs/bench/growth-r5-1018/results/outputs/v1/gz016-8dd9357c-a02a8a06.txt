# Q3 Referral Incentive A/B Test: Executive Summary for CFO

**Decision Context:** The $10/$10 incentive (Test) vs. $5/$5 (Control) generated a 25% volume lift in invites but increased Cost Per Acquired Rider (CPAR) from $18 to $24. The hypothesis that organic spillover would lower blended costs is currently **unverified** by the data.

### 1. Immediate Trade-off: Volume vs. Unit Efficiency
Present the direct arithmetic before projecting long-term value.
*   **The Efficiency Gap:** The test increased acquisition cost by **33%** ($18 → $24).
*   **The Break-Even Requirement:** To justify this higher CPAR, the referred users from the test group must generate **33% more gross margin** over their lifetime than the control group.
*   **The Volume Illusion:** A 25% increase in invites does not equal a 25% increase in *profitable* riders. If the conversion rate of invite-to-ride remained flat, you are simply paying more for the same quality of user. If conversion improved, calculate the net cost per *completed* ride, not just per sign-up.

### 2. Forward-Looking Metrics: Validating Lifetime Value (LTV)
Do not present a single "projected LTV" number without cohort segmentation. The CFO needs to know if the *type* of user acquired has changed.
*   **Cohort Retention Curves (Weeks 1–12):** Compare the retention of users acquired via the $10 incentive vs. the $5 incentive.
    *   *Hypothesis A (Gaming):* If the test cohort drops off sharply after claiming the credit (high churn in Week 2–4), the higher CPAR is a permanent loss.
    *   *Hypothesis B (Quality):* If the test cohort retains at the same or higher rate, the higher upfront cost is an investment recoverable over time.
*   **Payback Period Shift:** Calculate how many weeks it takes to recoup the $24 CPAR vs. the $18 CPAR based on current average revenue per user (ARPU). If the payback period extends beyond your cash-flow constraint (e.g., >6 months), the test fails regardless of total LTV.
*   **Organic Spillover Verification:** Isolate "second-degree" referrals (users referred by the new test users). If this chain is weak, the "blended cost" argument collapses.

### 3. Recommended Decision Framework
Structure the final recommendation around three clear scenarios based on the data above:
*   **Ship (Scale the $10 incentive):** Only if the test cohort's Day-30 retention matches the control AND the projected LTV exceeds the new CPAR by >20%.
*   **Iterate (Targeted Rollout):** If retention is high but CPAR is too broad, restrict the $10 incentive to high-value segments (e.g., airport rides, premium tiers) rather than a blanket offer.
*   **Stop (Revert to $5):** If the test cohort shows signs of "bonus hunting" (low retention) or if the payback period exceeds 9 months. The 25% volume lift is vanity if the unit economics are negative.
