**Analysis of Retention Cliff for FitTrack Head of Product**

The divergence between improved Day-1 retention (45% → 55%) and degraded Day-30 retention (25% → 18%) following the February 1 onboarding update indicates a "hollow funnel." The new flow successfully lowers the barrier to entry but fails to sustain value delivery through the first month. The pattern of high activity (4 workouts/week) for three weeks followed by a sudden stop suggests the drop-off is not due to immediate friction or lack of initial motivation, but rather a specific failure in the **sustainment phase** (Week 4).

Based on the engagement patterns provided, the root cause likely lies in one of three areas where the new onboarding may have inadvertently created a mismatch:

1.  **Content Exhaustion vs. Progression:** If the new onboarding front-loads the most engaging content or sets unrealistic expectations for variety, users may consume the available "novelty" within 21 days. The sudden stop implies a "content wall" where the app fails to present the next logical step in the user's journey once the initial novelty wears off.
2.  **Misaligned Expectations:** The engaging start may attract users with different intent profiles than the previous flow. If the new onboarding appeals to "burst" users (short-term motivation) rather than "habit" builders, the 3-week cliff represents the natural expiration of their intent, not a product failure. The higher Day-1 number includes more low-intent users who dilute the Day-30 metric.
3.  **Missing Trigger Mechanism:** Users averaging 4 workouts/week are highly engaged but lack an external or internal trigger to continue past the 21-day mark. The previous flow may have implicitly built a habit loop (e.g., social accountability, progressive difficulty) that the new streamlined flow removed in favor of immediate engagement.
