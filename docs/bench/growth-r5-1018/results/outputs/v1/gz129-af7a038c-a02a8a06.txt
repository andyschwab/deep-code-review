### Behavioral Cohort Breakdown for AncestryIS Retention Strategy

**North Star Metric:** Weekly Active Users completing a "Meaningful Tree Expansion" (defined as adding ≥3 nodes or 1 photo in a session). Vanity metrics like total signups or cumulative tree size are rejected as they do not correlate with immediate value realization or subscription intent.

**Cohort Definitions & Diagnostic Questions**
To distinguish between "stuck" users and "satisfied" small-tree users, segment the 12,000 registered users into these four behavioral cohorts based on event data:

1.  **The Stalled Builders (Hypothesis: Friction/Privacy)**
    *   *Definition:* Users who initiated a tree, reached 2–9 nodes, and have had **zero** activity for 14+ days.
    *   *Diagnostic Question:* Do drop-offs cluster at a specific workflow step (e.g., "Upload Photo" or "Connect Relative")?
    *   *Action:* If >40% drop at "Upload Photo," run a $2,000 experiment simplifying the privacy consent UI or offering a "private-only" toggle. If drop-off is uniform, the issue is likely content scarcity (lack of records).

2.  **The Satisfied Minimalists (Hypothesis: Value Realized)**
    *   *Definition:* Users with ≤10 nodes who return weekly to view or edit existing data but do not expand.
    *   *Diagnostic Question:* Is their session duration and return frequency comparable to premium subscribers with small trees?
    *   *Action:* Do not target for expansion. Target for referral programs ("Share your family story") or low-cost archival features. Misidentifying this group as "churned" wastes budget.

3.  **The Deep Divers (Hypothesis: Premium Ready)**
    *   *Definition:* Users who have crossed the 10-node threshold within their first 30 days.
    *   *Diagnostic Question:* What is the conversion rate to premium for this group vs. those under 10 nodes?
    *   *Action:* This is your primary acquisition target. Allocate $4,000 to an experiment triggering a premium trial offer immediately upon the 11th node addition.

4.  **The Privacy-Hesitant (Hypothesis: Specific Blocker)**
    *   *Definition:* Users who visit the "Privacy Settings" or "Record Matching" page but abort before saving changes or connecting records.
    *   *Diagnostic Question:* Does abandonment here correlate with the "lack of historical records" error message frequency?
    *   *Action:* Allocate $2,000 to content acquisition for specific Icelandic parishes where errors are highest, or clarify data sourcing on the UI.

**Experiment Rigor & Budget Allocation**
Do not peek at results mid-test. Pre-commit to a 4-week duration for any A/B test with a primary metric of "Tree Expansion Rate" and a guardrail of "Support Ticket Volume."
*   **$4,000:** Activation experiment (Trigger premium offer at 11 nodes).
*   **$2,000:** Friction reduction (Privacy UI simplification for Stalled Builders).
*   **$2,000:** Content gap fill (Targeted record acquisition or "Request Record" feature for high-drop-off regions).

**This Week's Steps**
1.  **Query:** Run a cohort analysis comparing "Days Since Last Active" vs. "Node Count" to isolate the "Stalled Builders" (Cohort 1) from "Satisfied Minimalists" (Cohort 2).
2.  **Instrument:** Ensure the `tree_node_added` and `privacy_view_abandoned` events are logged with properties for `error_code` and `record_source` to answer the "privacy vs. records" question.
3.  **Decide:** If the "Stalled" cohort shows a sharp drop at the photo upload step, approve the Privacy UI experiment design by Friday; otherwise, pivot the budget to content acquisition.

*Note: All retention benchmarks must be calculated against AncestryIS's own historical baseline; no external industry averages are applied. If event data for `privacy_view_abandoned` is missing, that metric is UNVERIFIED and must be instrumented before testing.*
