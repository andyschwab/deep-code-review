### North Star Framework for ServiceHub Leadership

**Recommended North Star Metric:** **Weekly Active Users Completing a Verified, High-Rated Job.**

This metric replaces "Total Transaction Volume" because volume is a vanity metric that rises even when quality drops (as your dipping satisfaction scores indicate). A "Verified, High-Rated Job" is defined as a completed transaction where the homeowner rates the service 4 stars or higher and the contractor confirms payment. This single number forces the balance you seek: it cannot grow unless supply is available *and* demand is satisfied with quality. If you push volume by accepting bad matches, this metric falls. If you restrict supply too much, this metric stalls.

**Evaluation of Candidates:**
*   **Total Transaction Volume (Current):** Reject. It incentivizes speed over quality, directly correlating with your recent satisfaction dip. It measures activity, not value.
*   **Net Revenue:** Too lagging for operational alignment. Revenue is an outcome of value delivered, not the value itself. Use as a guardrail, not the North Star.
*   **Frequency of Use:** Weak for local services. Homeowners do not need plumbers weekly; high frequency might indicate unresolved issues (repeat repairs) rather than health.
*   **Successful Matches (Proposed):** Strong, but only if "successful" includes a quality gate (rating/threshold). Without the quality gate, it reverts to the volume trap.

**Validation Method: Correlation to Sustainability**
Do not guess which metric predicts long-term health; prove it using your two years of historical data.
1.  **Cohort Analysis:** Segment users by their first month of activity. Calculate the proposed North Star for each cohort in Month 1.
2.  **Retention Correlation:** Plot Month 1 North Star performance against Month 6 retention (did they book again?). The metric with the highest correlation coefficient to Month 6 retention is your true predictor of sustainability.
3.  **Supply Health Check:** Ensure the chosen metric correlates with contractor retention (do contractors stay if this metric rises?). If the metric grows while contractors churn, it is unsustainable.

**Socialization Plan for Offsite**
To align leadership, shift the conversation from "hitting a number" to "driving a behavior."
*   **Define the Input Metrics:** Break the North Star into three levers owned by different teams:
    *   *Supply Team:* % of job requests matched within 2 hours (Availability).
    *   *Product Team:* % of completed jobs receiving a rating (Feedback loop).
    *   *Operations Team:* % of jobs rated 4+ stars (Quality control).
*   **The "So What" Test:** For every proposed initiative, ask: "Will this increase Weekly Active Users Completing a Verified, High-Rated Job?" If the answer is "It increases volume but might lower ratings," the initiative is rejected.
*   **Visual Dashboard:** Replace the "Volume Up 15%" chart with a trend line of the new North Star alongside a secondary line of "Average Rating." Show how they diverged last quarter to illustrate why the change is necessary.

**Immediate Next Step:**
Before the offsite, run the historical correlation query on your existing data: compare last year's monthly "High-Rated Completed Jobs" against this year's repeat booking rate. Present this scatter plot at the meeting to empirically demonstrate that quality-adjusted volume predicts retention better than raw volume.

*Note: Ensure your event taxonomy explicitly tags "job completed" with a "rating >= 4" property to calculate this without manual review. If this property is missing, instrument it immediately as a critical gap.*
