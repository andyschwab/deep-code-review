**To:** CFO, Revenue Management Team
**From:** Head of Product
**Date:** April 6
**Subject:** A/B Test Result: Baggage Weight Clarity (March 12 – April 5)

**Executive Summary**
The test comparing "price only" vs. "price + weight clarity" yielded a **statistically inconclusive result**. While the variant showed a nominal lift from 22.0% to 23.1% attach rate (+1.1 percentage points), the small sample size (8,400 users) and wide confidence interval mean we cannot distinguish this lift from random noise. **We cannot claim a win.**

**Statistical Reliability**
With 8,400 participants split evenly, the test lacks the power to validate a 1.1% lift with standard confidence (95%). The observed difference falls well within the margin of error expected for this traffic volume. Interpreting this as a "trend" risks implementing engineering changes based on a false positive. The data does not support a causal claim that clarity drives attachment; it merely suggests a possibility that requires more data to verify.

**Recommendation: Do Not Roll Out Yet**
Rolling out to Croatian and Italian routes now is not justified. The engineering effort to modify the booking flow across markets carries a cost that the current projected revenue gain (which is unverified) cannot guarantee. Furthermore, rolling out a feature based on noisy data prevents us from ever knowing if it truly works, as we lose the control group needed for future measurement.

**Action Plan**
1.  **Kill the immediate rollout:** Maintain the current "price only" flow for all markets, including Croatia and Italy, for the next month.
2.  **Extend the test duration:** Re-run the experiment during the peak summer booking window (May–June) to capture sufficient traffic. To detect a 1.1% lift with statistical significance, we require approximately 35,000–40,000 participants per variant. Our current low-season volume makes this impossible to achieve quickly.
3.  **Pre-commit to a decision rule:** Before restarting, define the minimum detectable effect we care about. If engineering costs are high, we should only care about lifts >2%. If we only need a 1% lift to break even, we must run the test until we hit the required sample size, ignoring daily fluctuations.
4.  **Segmentation analysis (Post-hoc):** Once the extended test concludes, slice the data by route type (Leisure vs. Business). It is plausible that weight clarity matters only to leisure travelers packing their own bags, while business travelers (with fixed allowances) ignore it. Acting on this signal now would be data dredging; wait for a robust dataset.

**Bottom Line:** The current result is **actionable noise**. We preserve capital by not building yet and preserve learning potential by waiting for a statistically valid sample size in peak season.

***

*Note: This assessment assumes a standard fixed-horizon test design; if interim peeking occurred, the false-positive risk is even higher. Exact break-even calculations require the specific engineering hour cost, which was not provided.*
