### Proposal: Redefining Activation for NeoVerse

**Recommended New Activation Definition:**
"Value Realized" = **First Transaction** AND **One Retention Signal within 7 Days**.
*Primary Retention Signal:* Funding a Savings Pocket (any amount) OR setting up a recurring rule (auto-save or bill split).

**Rationale & Diagnosis**
Your current 40% activation rate is a vanity metric because it decouples the initial action from long-term value. The 60% dormancy rate proves that a single transaction does not establish a habit for Gen Z users. Engaging with a savings product (a "financial commitment") is a stronger leading indicator of retention than social features alone, as it requires trust and intent. Social features (bill splitting) are valuable for acquisition/virality but are often one-off events; they should be secondary signals unless paired with a financial outcome.

**Validation Plan (Data to Gather)**
Do not wait for long-term data to make a decision. Run a **cohort analysis** on your existing 1,200 early adopters compared to a control group of standard activators from January.

1.  **Define Cohorts:**
    *   **Cohort A (Savings Activated):** Users who transacted + funded a pocket within 7 days.
    *   **Cohort B (Social Activated):** Users who transacted + used a social feature only.
    *   **Cohort C (Control):** Users who only transacted (current definition).
2.  **Measure Retention:** Compare Day-30 active rates across these three cohorts.
    *   *Hypothesis:* Cohort A will show significantly higher Day-30 retention than Cohort C.
    *   *Decision Rule:* If Cohort A's retention is >15% higher than Cohort C, adopt the new definition immediately. If Cohort B matches A, include social actions as a valid secondary path.
3.  **Instrumentation Check:** Ensure you are tracking `savings_pocket_funded` (property: amount) and `recurring_rule_created` as distinct events. If these are not currently logged, instrument them immediately as high priority.

**Immediate Next Steps (By Next Wednesday)**
1.  **Run the Retroactive Cohort Query:** Pull Day-30 retention for the 1,200 early adopters vs. historical averages. Even with a small sample, a stark difference (e.g., 80% retention vs. 40%) will be statistically sufficient for a directional decision.
2.  **Draft the "North Star" Input:** Propose shifting the team's weekly focus from "New First Transactions" to "New Value Realized Users" (the new composite metric).
3.  **Present the Trade-off:** Explicitly state to the CPO that adopting this definition will likely *lower* the reported activation rate (perhaps from 40% to 25%) but will accurately reflect the user base that actually generates revenue and stays. This aligns marketing spend on quality rather than volume.

**Note on Benchmarks:** No external "good" retention rate is cited here; success is defined strictly by the lift in Day-30 retention relative to your own historical baseline.
**Note on Sample Size:** While 1,200 users is small for fine-grained segmentation, it is sufficient to detect large effects in retention behavior needed for this strategic pivot.
