### Executive Summary: SambaBites Geo-Promotion Analysis

**Current Status:** The promotion generated higher spend per transaction (+14% AOV) but attracted fewer total customers (-9% volume) in targeted coastal zones. The net revenue impact is currently **positive but fragile**, estimated at a **~3.8% net lift** (1.14 × 0.91 = 1.0374), assuming no operational capacity constraints were hit. However, the volume drop signals a critical risk: if the decline was caused by service bottlenecks turning customers away, the true potential revenue was capped, and a nationwide rollout could damage brand reputation during peak summer demand.

**Interpretation of Conflicting Metrics**
The divergence between rising Average Order Value (AOV) and falling Transaction Volume suggests one of two scenarios:
1.  **Successful Upsell, Natural Attrition:** The free coconut water successfully incentivized larger orders, but the specific offer did not appeal to the high-frequency, low-spend demographic (e.g., quick snackers), causing them to skip visiting. This is a healthy trade-off if margins hold.
2.  **Operational Friction (The Risk):** The promotion increased complexity per order (adding a drink), slowing down service speed. In a food stand environment, longer wait times directly suppress volume. If customers arrived, saw the line, and left, the 9% drop represents **lost revenue**, not just a shift in timing.

**Required Data Isolation for Decision**
To distinguish between these scenarios before the nationwide rollout, isolate the following data points immediately:

*   **Peak-Hour Throughput vs. Off-Peak Volume:** Compare transaction counts during lunch/dinner rushes (12:00–14:00, 18:00–20:00) against mid-day periods.
    *   *Signal:* If volume dropped significantly only during peaks but held steady off-peak, the cause is **operational bottleneck**. The promotion slowed service, capping capacity when demand was highest.
    *   *Signal:* If volume dropped uniformly across all hours, the cause is **demand shift** (customer mix change), not operations.
*   **Average Service Time per Ticket:** Extract the timestamp difference between "order start" and "order complete" for promoted vs. non-promoted orders.
    *   *Signal:* An increase of >45 seconds per ticket confirms the free item created a fulfillment bottleneck.
*   **Basket Composition Frequency:** Isolate the count of "Savory Only" orders vs. "Savory + Drink" orders.
    *   *Signal:* If "Savory Only" orders vanished entirely, the promotion may have confused the menu or forced a bundle that alienated simple buyers.

**Recommendation for Regional Director**
Do not approve the nationwide rollout yet. The 3.8% net gain is insufficient to justify the risk of operational meltdown across 120 locations if the volume drop is due to speed issues.

**Immediate Next Step:** Run a 48-hour "speed test" in 3 high-volume coastal stands. Measure average service time explicitly. If service time increased by more than 20%, the rollout must be paused to redesign the fulfillment workflow (e.g., pre-batching coconut water) before expanding. If service time remained flat, the volume drop is a customer preference issue, and the rollout can proceed with adjusted marketing expectations.
