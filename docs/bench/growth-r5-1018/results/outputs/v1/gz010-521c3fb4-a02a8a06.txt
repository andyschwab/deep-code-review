**Diagnosis & Action Plan: Pricing Page Conversion Decline**

**Executive Summary for VP of Sales**
The 38% relative drop in upgrade rate (4.5% → 2.8%) coinciding with a 25% traffic surge indicates a likely mix of lower-intent traffic and friction in the new "Pro" highlight. Without session recordings, we must triangulate using aggregate funnel drops and survey sentiment. The immediate risk to Q4 revenue is real; however, pouring more spend into the current campaign will waste budget on a leaking bucket. We must isolate whether the drop is universal (design flaw) or segmented (traffic quality) before scaling.

**Hypothesis-Driven Investigation (Using Existing Data)**

1.  **Hypothesis A: Traffic Quality Degradation (Mix Shift)**
    *   *Premise:* The new campaign drives high volume but low intent, diluting the conversion rate.
    *   *Data Check:* Segment the Oct 20–present conversion rate by **acquisition channel** and **campaign source**. Compare the new campaign's rate against the baseline of organic/direct traffic.
    *   *Reading Rule:* If organic/direct traffic remains near 4.5% while the new campaign sits at <1.5%, the design is not the primary culprit; the audience is. If *all* segments dropped to ~2.8%, the redesign is the blocker.

2.  **Hypothesis B: "Pro" Highlight Creates Comparison Paralysis**
    *   *Premise:* The prominent "Pro" feature table overwhelms users, causing drop-off before the "Upgrade" click.
    *   *Data Check:* Analyze the funnel step: `Pricing Page View` → `Feature Table Scroll/Interaction` (if tracked) → `Plan Selection Click` → `Checkout Start`.
    *   *Reading Rule:* A sharp drop between "Page View" and "Plan Selection" suggests the layout confuses the value proposition. If users select a plan but drop at "Checkout," the issue is price or form friction, not the table.

3.  **Hypothesis C: Misaligned Expectations (Survey Signal)**
    *   *Premise:* Users arriving from the new campaign expect features not clearly delivered in the "Pro" tier.
    *   *Data Check:* Filter survey responses (NPS or exit polls) from Oct 20 onward by traffic source. Look for keywords: "confusing," "missing," "too expensive," or "expected X."
    *   *Reading Rule:* A spike in "confusing" correlates to Hypothesis B; a spike in "too expensive" or mismatched feature expectations correlates to Hypothesis C.

**Immediate Actions (By Wednesday)**

*   **Action 1: Segment the Bleed (Today)**
    Run the channel-segmentation query immediately.
    *   *Decision Trigger:* If organic traffic is stable, pause the underperforming ad sets to stop budget waste. If all traffic is down, proceed to Action 2.
*   **Action 2: Simplify the "Pro" View (Tomorrow)**
    If the drop is universal, deploy a rapid A/B test (or content swap if dev resources are tight) reverting the feature table to the pre-Oct 20 simplified view for 50% of traffic.
    *   *Metric:* Monitor `Plan Selection Click` rate. Do not wait for full revenue impact; look for leading indicator recovery within 48 hours.
*   **Action 3: Guardrail Check (Wednesday)**
    Calculate the **break-even conversion rate** required to hit Q4 targets given the new 25% higher traffic volume.
    *   *Math:* If previous volume was $V$ at 4.5%, and new volume is $1.25V$, the required rate to match revenue is $4.5\% / 1.25 = 3.6\%$.
    *   *Status:* Current 2.8% is below the 3.6% break-even. We are losing revenue despite higher traffic. This quantifies the urgency for the VP.

**Next Week's Step**
Launch the simplified table variant with a pre-committed sample size of 10,000 visitors (approx. 3 days at current traffic) to detect a lift from 2.8% to 3.6%. Define the primary metric as `Plan Selection Click` and the guardrail as `Revenue Per Visitor`. If the variant does not show a statistically valid lift by Friday, revert to the old layout and audit the campaign targeting criteria.
