**Growth Plan: EduMex FY2025 (July 1 Start)**

**1. North Star Metric: Weekly Active Learners Completing Core Modules**
Reject "cumulative enrollments" as your primary target; it is a vanity metric that rises even if students drop out or receive poor support. Your North Star must be **students actively completing vocational modules weekly**. This only rises if you deliver real value and retain users. If enrollments rise but this metric flattens, you are overloading your team and degrading quality.

**2. Growth Ceiling & Retention-First Diagnosis**
Your assumed 20% month-over-month (MoM) growth is likely unsustainable given your current 48-hour support response time. In a bootstrapped model, retention leaks destroy unit economics faster than acquisition fixes them.
*   **The Constraint:** Do not set enrollment targets higher than the volume your current team can onboard without pushing support response times beyond 24 hours.
*   **The Action:** Before acquiring new students, measure your **Week-4 Retention Rate** for the last three cohorts. If retention is below your internal baseline (e.g., if fewer than 60% of students who start are still active in month 2), freeze aggressive acquisition. Fix the onboarding and support bottleneck first. Pouring more students into a leaky bucket with stretched support will increase churn and burn out your staff.

**3. Operational Guardrails & Hiring Triggers**
Set growth targets based on **capacity-constrained cohorts**, not open-ended percentages.
*   **Target Setting:** Calculate the maximum number of new students your 5-person team can handle while maintaining <24h response time. Let this be your monthly cap.
*   **Hiring Trigger:** Define a specific tripwire: "If active learners exceed [Current Capacity + 15%] for two consecutive weeks, we hire one part-time support specialist before opening the next cohort."
*   **Partnership vs. Organic:** Since LTV is unknown, treat the new business partnership as an experiment. Track these students separately. If their Week-4 retention or completion rate is lower than organic signups, their lower acquisition cost does not matter; they are costing you support hours without delivering value.

**4. Immediate Next Steps (This Week)**
1.  **Define the Cap:** Calculate the exact number of new students your team can absorb next month without exceeding a 24-hour support SLA. Set this as your hard enrollment limit for July.
2.  **Segment Retention:** Run a query comparing Week-4 retention between "organic" and "partnership" students. If data is missing, instrument this event immediately; do not guess.
3.  **Communicate to Staff:** Present the advisory board and instructional staff with a "Capacity-Led Growth" model: "We will grow only as fast as our support quality allows. Hiring triggers are tied to active learner counts, not revenue promises."

*Note: All targets above are derived from your operational baseline, not external benchmarks. Do not fabricate a "good" retention rate; use your own historical data to set the bar.*
*If you cannot currently query retention by cohort, your first task is instrumenting that specific event before setting any numerical growth goal.*
