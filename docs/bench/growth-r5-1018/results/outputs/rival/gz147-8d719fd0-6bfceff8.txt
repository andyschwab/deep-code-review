### Recommended Metrics Framework for Pricing Tier Health

To determine the long-term viability of the "Pro Plus" tier, you must move beyond top-line revenue and analyze **net revenue retention** and **cannibalization sustainability**. Focus on these four specific areas:

**1. Cohort-Based Net Revenue Retention (NRR) by Tier**
Do not look at aggregate churn. Instead, track monthly NRR for three specific cohorts:
*   **Legacy Enterprise:** Are they stabilizing at Pro Plus, or continuing to churn entirely?
*   **Upgraded Basic:** Do Pro Plus users (ex-Basic) have higher retention than pure Basic users?
*   **New Pro Plus:** Are new signups choosing Pro Plus over Enterprise at a rate that offsets the downgrade loss?
*   *Decision Rule:* If Legacy Enterprise NRR drops below 90% due to downgrades and does not recover within two billing cycles, the feature gap is too small.

**2. Feature Utilization Delta**
Map usage data against your pricing walls. Specifically, measure the percentage of Pro Plus users hitting the limits of their new plan versus those who previously hit Enterprise limits.
*   *Measurement:* Compare average usage of "Enterprise-only" features (e.g., team seats, advanced export) between current Enterprise users and downgraded Pro Plus users.
*   *Decision Rule:* If downgraded users rarely touch the features they lost, the downgrade was price-driven and likely permanent. If they frequently hit new limits or request removed features, the tier structure is confusing, and you risk high churn when they realize the limitation.

**3. Customer Lifetime Value (LTV) Projection by Migration Path**
Calculate the projected 12-month LTV for three distinct paths:
*   **Path A:** User stays on Enterprise ($50/mo).
*   **Path B:** User downgrades to Pro Plus ($29/mo) but stays >12 months.
*   **Path C:** User downgrades to Pro Plus ($29/mo) and churns within 6 months.
*   *Decision Rule:* The strategy is only successful if the volume of Path B users exceeds the revenue loss from Path A. If Path C is dominant, the tier is attracting lower-quality customers who cannot sustain the product cost.

**4. Upgrade Velocity and "Trap" Rate**
Measure how many Pro Plus users eventually upgrade to Enterprise versus how many remain static or downgrade to Basic.
*   *Measurement:* Track the % of Pro Plus users who upgrade to Enterprise within 6 months compared to the historical Basic-to-Enterprise rate.
*   *Decision Rule:* If Pro Plus acts as a "ceiling" where users stop growing rather than a stepping stone, it may be stifling your highest-value segment growth.

**How to Read the Data for the Board**
Present the data as a **Net Impact Waterfall**:
1.  Start with projected revenue without the test.
2.  Subtract revenue lost from Enterprise downgrades (Cannibalization Cost).
3.  Add revenue gained from Basic upgrades (Expansion Gain).
4.  Add/Subtract projected churn differences (Retention Impact).

If the "Retention Impact" is negative due to Enterprise users leaving entirely, the tier destroys value. If the net result is positive but marginal (<5%), the operational complexity of managing three tiers may outweigh the benefit. Recommend keeping the tier only if the "Expansion Gain" from new market capture exceeds the "Cannibalization Cost" by at least 20% to justify the risk.

*Ensure all data is segmented by signup date (pre- vs. post-Feb 20) to isolate test effects from seasonal trends.*
*Prepare a sensitivity analysis showing break-even points if downgrade rates increase by another 5%.*
