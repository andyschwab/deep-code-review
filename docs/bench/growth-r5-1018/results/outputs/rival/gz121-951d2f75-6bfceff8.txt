### Recommended Primary Metric: **Day-45 Active Subscription Rate**

For your six-week pilot launching November 15, your single primary success metric should be the percentage of users who started a trial or first order between Nov 15–Dec 1 and are still in an active, paying subscription status on December 27 (Day 45).

This metric explicitly bridges the gap between your immediate conversion goals and your critical churn constraints, accounting for the specific operational risks in the Klang Valley.

#### Key Factors for Board & Operations Alignment

**1. Aligning with the Break-Even Constraint (The "Why")**
Your business model fails if monthly churn exceeds 4.5%. Tracking only "Immediate Conversion Rate" is dangerous because it optimizes for sign-ups that may cancel immediately after the first delivery due to logistics failures. By measuring who remains subscribed after the second delivery window (approx. 30–45 days), you directly validate whether your unit economics hold. If this rate is low, no amount of top-of-funnel conversion will save the business.

**2. Mitigating Monsoon Logistics Risk (The Operational Reality)**
Your logistics partner has flagged potential delays in the Klang Valley. In a subscription model, the second delivery is the highest friction point; a late first delivery often causes cancellation before the second box even ships.
*   **If you track Immediate Conversion:** You might celebrate a successful launch while unknowingly accumulating cancellations that will hit in Week 5.
*   **If you track Day-45 Retention:** You force the operations team to prioritize delivery reliability over aggressive sales tactics. It aligns the growth team's incentives with the operational reality that *reliability* is your assumed value prop.

**3. Validating the "Reliability Over Price" Hypothesis**
You are testing three onboarding flows. If your hypothesis is correct (Malaysian pet owners prioritize reliability), the winning flow will not necessarily be the one with the highest click-through rate, but the one that sets accurate delivery expectations and retains users through the first monsoon-related delay. A metric focused on early conversion might falsely favor a flow that over-promises speed, leading to higher churn later.

**4. The Six-Week Timeline Constraint**
A six-week pilot is too short to measure long-term LTV, but long enough to capture the first renewal cycle for early adopters.
*   **Start Date:** Nov 15.
*   **First Delivery:** ~Nov 20–25.
*   **Second Delivery/Renewal Point:** ~Dec 20–25.
*   **Measurement Date:** Dec 27 (End of Pilot).
This window allows you to capture the "survivors" of the first critical month. Users who cancel before Day 45 represent a failure of the core value proposition; users who stay represent product-market fit.

**5. Dashboard Implementation Strategy**
To satisfy both the Board and Operations:
*   **Primary North Star:** Day-45 Active Subscription Rate (Reported weekly as a cohort projection, finalized at Week 6).
*   **Secondary Diagnostic:** "On-Time First Delivery Rate" (Operations focus).
*   **Guardrail Metric:** "Cancellation Reason: Logistics/Delay" (Must stay below a defined threshold).

By selecting **Day-45 Active Subscription Rate**, you tell the Board you are focused on sustainable unit economics, not vanity sign-ups. You tell Operations that their delivery performance is the primary driver of growth success, ensuring organizational alignment on the reliability hypothesis before scaling spend.
