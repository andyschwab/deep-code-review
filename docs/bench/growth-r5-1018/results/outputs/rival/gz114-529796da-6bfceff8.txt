### Recommended Conversion Definition for LuxResale

**Primary Conversion Event:** **Final Shipment Confirmation**

For your quarterly board meeting and user acquisition justification, define a "conversion" strictly as the moment the high-value item (>€10,000) is shipped to the customer.

**Rationale:**
In a high-end consignment model, the transaction is not complete at the deposit stage. Between the deposit and shipment, significant friction points exist: final authentication checks, buyer's remorse (common in luxury), payment clearance failures, and logistics coordination. Counting the deposit as a conversion inflates your success rate and risks justifying acquisition spend on users who never generate actual revenue. By anchoring conversion to **shipment**, you align your metrics with realized cash flow and successful inventory turnover. This ensures your Customer Acquisition Cost (CAC) reflects the true cost to acquire a *paying* customer, not just a *committed* one. This rigor is essential for a board focused on long-term value and capital efficiency.

**Treatment of Other Actions:**
*   **Deposit Payment:** Classify this as a **Micro-Conversion** or "High-Intent Lead." It is a critical funnel step but not the final business outcome.
*   **Wishlist Addition:** Classify this as an **Engagement Metric**. Given your observation that many users wishlist without buying, this indicates interest but poor purchase intent validation. Do not count this toward conversion rates used for ROI calculations.

---

### Secondary Metrics to Monitor

To provide the board with a complete picture of funnel health and long-term value, track these three secondary metrics alongside your primary conversion rate:

1.  **Deposit-to-Ship Completion Rate**
    *   *Definition:* The percentage of users who pay the deposit and successfully reach shipment confirmation.
    *   *Why it matters:* This isolates the efficiency of your operations and authentication process. If this rate is low, your acquisition might be working, but your fulfillment or trust-building process is leaking revenue.

2.  **Wishlist-to-Purchase Ratio**
    *   *Definition:* The percentage of unique users who add an item to their wishlist and eventually complete a purchase (shipped) within 90 days.
    *   *Why it matters:* Since your team believes wishlisting indicates intent, this metric validates or disproves that hypothesis. A low ratio suggests you need better retargeting strategies for wishlisted items or that the "intent" signal is weaker than assumed.

3.  **Average Order Value (AOV) by Acquisition Channel**
    *   *Definition:* The average value of *shipped* orders segmented by where the user came from.
    *   *Why it matters:* With only 45 high-value purchases last season, volume is low. You must ensure the *quality* of traffic justifies the spend. One channel might drive more deposits, but another might drive higher-value final shipments.

**Note:** Present the "Deposit-to-Ship" rate prominently to show the board you understand the operational risks in your consignment model.
**Note:** Exclude wishlist users from your core conversion rate calculations to avoid diluting the perceived value of your actual buyer base.
