### Executive Summary: SambaBites Coconut Water Promotion Analysis

**The Conflict:** The promotion successfully increased value per transaction (+14% AOV) but failed to drive overall traffic, resulting in a net revenue loss. The 9% drop in transaction volume outweighs the 14% gain in average order value, leading to an approximate **3.5% decrease in total revenue** for the targeted zones during the promotion week.

**Interpretation of Conflicting Metrics**
The data suggests the promotion acted as a subsidy for existing customers rather than an acquisition tool. The higher AOV indicates that customers who *did* visit purchased more savory items to qualify for the free coconut water. However, the 9% volume drop implies the offer failed to attract new foot traffic or bring back lapsed customers in sufficient numbers to offset natural fluctuations.

The decline in volume is likely not due to operational bottlenecks alone. If service speed were the primary constraint, you would expect to see high demand with capped sales (lost sales), not a raw decrease in transaction count. A more probable cause is **cannibalization of timing**: loyal customers may have delayed their visits to align with the promotion, but the promotion window (one week) was too short to capture the full displaced demand, or the "free" offer inadvertently signaled lower quality to price-sensitive segments. Alternatively, the coastal demographic may have been less responsive to the specific creative or channel used, regardless of the heat assumption.

**Critical Data Points to Isolate Next**
To determine if this is an operational issue or a marketing failure before a national rollout, isolate these four specific data cuts:

1.  **New vs. Returning Customer Split:** Compare the ratio of new customers in the test group versus the control. If new customer acquisition is flat or down, the promotion is merely subsidizing regulars. A successful rollout requires net new traffic.
2.  **Hourly Transaction Heatmaps:** Overlay transaction counts by hour for test vs. control zones. If the test group shows sharp peaks and deeper valleys than the control, the drop is due to operational bottlenecks during rush hours (turning away customers). If the curve is uniformly lower across all hours, the issue is lack of demand generation.
3.  **Basket Composition Analysis:** Isolate the "Savory Order" threshold. Did the AOV lift come from customers adding extra items to reach the threshold, or did they simply trade up to more expensive savory items? If they only added low-margin items to qualify, the profitability of the 14% AOV lift may be illusory once the cost of coconut water is deducted.
4.  **Redemption Rate by Zip Code:** Analyze if the 9% volume drop was uniform across all five coastal zip codes or concentrated in specific areas. If only 1-2 zones drove the volume decline while others saw growth, the issue is hyper-local (e.g., specific stand management or local competition) rather than a flaw in the promotion concept itself.

**Recommendation for Regional Director**
Do not roll out nationwide yet. The current data indicates a net negative financial impact. The priority is to distinguish between a **demand problem** (the offer doesn't attract new people) and a **capacity problem** (we turned people away). Run a targeted follow-up in just two locations extending the promotion to two weeks to see if displaced demand recovers, while manually tracking turned-away customers during peak hours.

*Note: Ensure coconut water cost is subtracted from the AOV lift to verify actual margin contribution before any further testing.*
