To isolate the net margin impact of the free pastry reward, you must construct a dataset comparing the **Test Group** (loyalty members who redeemed the free pastry) against a **Control Group** (non-members or members who have not yet redeemed) over the period of September 1st to present.

You need to extract the following specific transactional data slices, aggregated by unique customer ID and visit date:

**1. Visit Frequency & Volume**
*   **Metric:** Total number of visits per user per month.
*   **Slice:** Compare average visits/month for Test vs. Control.
*   **Purpose:** Validates the assumption of a 1.5x increase in frequency. Without this lift, the program fails its primary objective regardless of basket size.

**2. Gross Basket Size (Pre-Reward)**
*   **Metric:** Total transaction value *before* any loyalty discounts or free items are applied.
*   **Slice:** Average spend per visit for Test vs. Control.
*   **Purpose:** Determines if members naturally spend more on coffee/food before the reward is even considered. This isolates selection bias (i.e., do heavy spenders just join the program?).

**3. Paid Add-On Revenue**
*   **Metric:** Revenue specifically from non-coffee items (pastries, sandwiches, merchandise) excluding the free pastry SKU.
*   **Slice:** Average add-on revenue per transaction for Test vs. Control.
*   **Purpose:** Directly measures cannibalization. If the Test Group's paid add-on revenue drops significantly below the 300 pesos baseline while frequency rises, the free item is replacing paid sales rather than incrementally driving traffic.

**4. Net Basket Size (Post-Reward)**
*   **Metric:** Final transaction value after deductions.
*   **Slice:** Average net spend per visit.
*   **Purpose:** Captures the immediate revenue loss from the free pastry giveaway.

**5. Cost of Goods Sold (COGS) Allocation**
*   **Metric:** Variable cost of the free pastry vs. the variable cost of typical paid add-ons.
*   **Slice:** Apply standard food cost percentages (e.g., 25-30%) to the volume of free pastries given vs. paid add-ons sold.
*   **Purpose:** Revenue is not margin. You must compare the *margin lost* on free pastries against the *margin gained* from increased visit frequency and any remaining paid add-ons.

**Analysis Logic for the CFO:**
Calculate the **Incremental Margin per User** using this formula:
*(Test Group Visits × Test Group Net Margin per Visit) – (Control Group Visits × Control Group Net Margin per Visit)*.

If the result is positive, the frequency lift outweighs the cannibalization and giveaway costs. If negative, the 1.5x frequency assumption is either incorrect or insufficient to cover the cost of the free pastry and lost add-on sales. Ensure you filter out users who joined but have not yet reached five visits, as they belong in a "pending" cohort, not the active test group.

Note: Ensure your POS system distinguishes between a "free pastry" transaction line (price $0) and a "no pastry" transaction to avoid skewing average item counts.
Do not include data from before September 1st to prevent historical behavior from diluting the pilot results.
