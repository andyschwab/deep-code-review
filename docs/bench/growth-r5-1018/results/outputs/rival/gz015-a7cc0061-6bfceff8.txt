### Diagnostic Approach: ConnectPro 'Industry Insights' Engagement Drop

To pinpoint the root cause before next Tuesday's presentation, execute this four-step diagnostic framework focusing on segmentation, interaction flows, and cannibalization analysis.

**1. Segment Performance Breakdown**
Aggregate data masks specific friction points. Immediately slice the post-launch data (Sept 12–Present) by the following dimensions to isolate where the drop is most severe:
*   **User Cohort:** Compare *New Users* (joined <30 days) vs. *Established Users*. New users may lack the network data required for relevant insights, causing immediate drop-off.
*   **Industry/Role:** Analyze session duration by industry vertical. If "Tech" users stay while "Finance" users drop, the content curation algorithm is likely failing for specific sectors.
*   **Device Type:** Separate Mobile vs. Desktop. A layout issue often manifests strictly on one form factor (e.g., the feed pushing critical navigation off-screen on mobile).
*   **Geography:** Specifically isolate the Paris user base against the global average to determine if this is a localization/content relevance issue or a platform-wide bug.

**2. Interaction Flow & Funnel Analysis**
The 1.2% CTR suggests a visibility or relevance failure. Map the user journey specifically within the feed:
*   **Impression-to-Scroll Depth:** Measure how far users scroll before abandoning the session. If 80% drop before the second item, the *first* impression is the blocker (headline, image, or load time).
*   **Zero-Click Sessions:** Calculate the percentage of sessions where the feed was viewed but *no* item was clicked. High zero-click rates indicate content irrelevance.
*   **Dwell Time per Item:** Differentiate between "clicking" and "reading." If users click but bounce back instantly (high bounce rate on landing), the content quality does not match the headline promise.

**3. Cannibalization Audit**
To test the hypothesis that the feed steals attention from high-value actions (messaging):
*   **Pre/Post Feature Pathing:** Compare the percentage of sessions containing a "Message Sent" event before Sept 12 vs. after.
*   **Sequential Analysis:** For sessions where the feed *was* viewed, what is the likelihood of a subsequent messaging event compared to sessions where the feed was ignored? If feed viewers are 20% less likely to message, cannibalization is occurring.
*   **Navigation Displacement:** Check if the feed placement has obscured or pushed down the "Messages" entry point in the UI hierarchy, particularly on mobile views.

**4. Qualitative Triage (Rapid)**
Quantitative data tells you *what* is happening; qualitative tells you *why*.
*   **Session Replay Sampling:** Watch 20–30 recorded sessions of users who dropped off within 2 minutes of landing on the feed. Look for "rage clicks," confused scrolling, or immediate exits.
*   **Support Ticket Scan:** Filter customer support tags for "feed," "news," or "loading" since Sept 12 to identify reported bugs or confusion.

**Recommended Output for Director**
Present findings in a "Problem-Solution" matrix:
1.  **Primary Driver:** Identify the single biggest segment or flow causing the drop (e.g., "Mobile users in Finance see 40% lower duration due to irrelevant content").
2.  **Cannibalization Verdict:** Clear yes/no on whether messaging volume has statistically declined due to the feed.
3.  **Action Plan:** Propose either a targeted fix (e.g., "Adjust algorithm for Finance vertical") or a rollback if the core architecture is flawed.

*Ensure all metrics compare identical day-of-week patterns (e.g., Monday vs. Monday) to avoid skewing from weekly usage cycles.*
*Prioritize the cannibalization check first, as losing high-value messaging engagement is a critical business risk.*
