**Strategic Recommendation: Hybrid Optimization with Retention Bias**

Do not optimize strictly for lowest Acquisition Cost (CAC) nor purely for predicted Lifetime Value (LTV). Instead, adopt a **Value-Based Bidding strategy targeting a specific LTV:CAC ratio**, while shifting 60% of your Q3 budget toward retention and expansion campaigns.

**Rationale:**
With a current LTV:CAC ratio of 4:1 (1,800 SEK / 450 SEK), your prospecting is technically efficient by standard benchmarks (often 3:1). However, in a tightening economic climate, cash flow immediacy is critical. Aggressive prospecting for *lower* CAC often attracts lower-quality customers who churn faster, artificially depressing your true LTV over time. Conversely, optimizing solely for predicted LTV can inflate CAC to unsustainable levels, straining monthly cash flow before returns materialize.

**Daily Decision Metric:**
Instruct your paid social team to optimize for **ROAS (Return on Ad Spend) at 90 days**, not initial purchase CPA.
*   **Why:** Furniture is a high-consideration, seasonal purchase. A user might click in June but convert in August. Optimizing for immediate CPA penalizes ads that drive high-intent browsing but have longer conversion windows.
*   **Implementation:** Use platform tools (e.g., Meta's Value Optimization) fed with historical customer value data, not just conversion counts. Set a floor CAC of 450 SEK but prioritize audiences with historical repeat purchase rates or higher average order values (AOV).

**Budget Allocation:**
*   **60% Retention/Expansion:** Target past purchasers with complementary goods (e.g., lighting for sofa buyers). This yields immediate cash flow with significantly lower marginal acquisition costs, stabilizing Q3 revenue.
*   **40% Prospecting:** Focus on "lookalike" audiences derived from your top 20% LTV customers, not all converters. Accept a slightly higher CAC (up to 500 SEK) if the predicted LTV exceeds 2,000 SEK.

**Identified Data Gaps Skewing Your Model:**
1.  **Cross-Device Attribution:** Furniture research often happens on mobile, but purchases occur on desktop. If your model is last-click, you may be undervaluing top-funnel social ads that initiate the journey.
2.  **Offline/Showroom Conversions:** If NordikHome has physical showrooms or phone sales, digital attribution likely misses these closures, making CAC appear higher than it truly is.
3.  **True LTV Horizon:** Your 1,800 SEK LTV may be based on insufficient data if the repurchase cycle for furniture is 3+ years. You may be underestimating the long-term value of current acquisitions.

**Accounting for Seasonality:**
Furniture purchases peak in late summer (pre-semester start) and late autumn (pre-holiday).
*   **Adjustment:** Do not compare Q3 CAC directly to Q1/Q2. Establish a **seasonal baseline**. If CAC rises to 500 SEK in August due to competitive bidding, but conversion volume doubles, the efficiency is higher despite the cost.
*   **Action:** Normalize your metrics by dividing spend and conversions by a seasonal index derived from the last three years of sales data before making daily budget shifts.

**Conclusion:**
Prioritize cash flow via retention (60%) to weather economic uncertainty, but maintain prospecting (40%) optimized for 90-day value, not immediate cheapness. This balances short-term liquidity with long-term asset building.
