**Recommended Metric: Contribution Margin per Cultivated Square Meter per Week**

For VerdantSky's Q3 expansion decision, the single most compelling metric is **Contribution Margin per Cultivated Square Meter per Week**. This metric directly bridges your operational scale (yield/m²) with your financial sustainability (unit economics), addressing both investor concerns and your specific energy volatility.

**Formula:**
$$ \frac{(\text{Weekly Revenue} - \text{Variable Costs})}{\text{Total Cultivated m}^2} $$

Where **Variable Costs** explicitly include:
*   Seeds and nutrients
*   Packaging and labor directly tied to harvest
*   **Energy costs** (electricity for HVAC/lighting)
*   **Water costs**

**Why this metric over "Yield per m²":**
Your assumption that yield is the primary driver is partially correct but dangerous in isolation. If yield increases by 10% but energy costs rise by 12% (as seen since March) due to aggressive climate control adjustments to achieve that yield, your *volume* grows while your *profitability* shrinks. Yield per m² ignores the cost of achieving that yield. Contribution Margin per m² captures the net economic value of every square foot of your facility, forcing the team to optimize for profit density, not just biomass density.

**Interpreting Shifts for Executive Decision-Making:**

1.  **Metric Increases:**
    *   **Interpretation:** You are successfully decoupling yield gains from energy spikes. Either your yield improvements are outpacing energy cost inflation, or you have optimized climate settings to reduce energy intensity without sacrificing output.
    *   **Action:** Validate the specific climate setpoints used during this period. If stable, this supports immediate Q3 expansion, as the unit economics are proven resilient against Dubai's energy fluctuations.

2.  **Metric Decreases (while Yield stays flat or rises):**
    *   **Interpretation:** This is the critical warning sign. It indicates "expensive yield." You are likely over-cooling, over-lighting, or running HVAC inefficiently to chase biomass targets, causing energy costs to consume your margins.
    *   **Action:** Halt expansion plans. Immediately audit climate control algorithms. The goal is to find the "economic optimum" yield, not the biological maximum. In Dubai's context, a slightly lower yield with significantly lower energy load may produce a higher contribution margin.

3.  **Metric Decreases (alongside Yield):**
    *   **Interpretation:** Operational inefficiency or biological failure (e.g., crop stress, disease) compounded by fixed high energy loads.
    *   **Action:** Investigate biological performance first. If crops are underperforming while energy spend remains high, your cost-per-unit skyrockets.

**Weekly Tracking Protocol:**
To make this actionable for next Tuesday:
*   **Numerator:** Calculate weekly revenue based on actual harvest sales. Subtract variable costs, ensuring electricity is allocated weekly based on actual kWh consumption multiplied by the current utility rate (capturing the 12% rise).
*   **Denominator:** Use the *active* cultivated area, not total facility footprint, to account for any downtime or turnover periods.
*   **Contextual Note:** Track this alongside your water efficiency (4.5 L/kg). If water usage spikes while this margin drops, you have a dual-efficiency failure. If water stays efficient but margin drops, the culprit is almost certainly energy management.

This metric tells your board exactly how much cash each square meter of your Dubai facility generates after paying for the specific costs required to run it, providing a clear green light or red light for Q3 capital deployment.

*Note: Ensure energy costs are allocated dynamically per week rather than averaged annually to reflect real-time price volatility.*
*Do not expand capacity until this metric shows a positive trend for three consecutive weeks.*
