**Recommendation: Prioritize Contribution Margin per Transaction (derived from Average Order Value) as your primary KPI.**

Do not choose between Total Transaction Volume and Average Order Value (AOV) in isolation. Given your 15% rise in logistics costs and the lack of category-level profitability data, focusing on Volume is dangerous, while focusing solely on AOV is incomplete. Your primary North Star for the upcoming quarter must be **Contribution Margin per Transaction**.

Since you cannot yet calculate this precisely due to data gaps, use **Average Order Value (AOV)** as your immediate proxy metric to drive daily decisions and incentives, with Total Transaction Volume as a secondary guardrail.

### Why Volume is the Wrong Primary Metric Now
Your assumption that "higher volume leads to better unit economics" has been invalidated by your recent cost structure. With logistics costs up 15%, every additional low-value transaction likely deepens your losses rather than spreading fixed costs.
*   **The Risk:** Incentivizing volume will encourage the team to push low-margin, heavy, or distant deliveries that look good on the top line but bleed cash.
*   **Investor Impact:** Regional investors in Southeast Asia have shifted focus from "growth at all costs" to "path to profitability." Reporting record transaction volumes alongside widening losses will damage credibility and jeopardize future funding.
*   **Logistics Impact:** Your partners need to forecast capacity based on *profitable* density, not just raw count. Flooding the network with unprofitable orders strains relationships without building sustainable value.

### Why AOV is the Better Proxy
Until you have granular cost data, AOV is the strongest lever you can pull to improve unit economics immediately.
*   **Margin Buffer:** Higher order values absorb the 15% logistics increase more effectively than low-value orders.
*   **Behavioral Shift:** Incentivizing AOV encourages bundling, cross-selling, and targeting higher-value artisans, which naturally filters out inefficient micro-transactions.
*   **Simplicity:** It is easy to track daily and communicate to the team without complex data infrastructure.

### Implementation Plan
1.  **Primary KPI:** Set a target for **Average Order Value** (e.g., increase by 10% quarter-over-quarter). Tie team bonuses to hitting this target while maintaining a minimum floor for total transaction volume (e.g., no less than 90% of last month's volume) to ensure you don't shrink the business too aggressively.
2.  **Immediate Data Task:** Before the quarter ends, you must implement tracking to calculate **Contribution Margin per Category**. You cannot sustain a strategy based on proxies forever. Identify which high-volume items are loss leaders and either delist them, renegotiate logistics rates for them, or adjust pricing.
3.  **Communication:** Tell investors you are pivoting from "Top-line Growth" to "Quality of Growth." Frame the AOV focus as a deliberate strategy to optimize unit economics in response to rising logistics costs, positioning PasarCepat as a disciplined operator ready for sustainable scaling.

**Note:** Do not incentivize volume until you confirm that your marginal cost per delivery is lower than your marginal revenue per order.
**Note:** If AOV rises but total volume drops below your minimum floor, investigate whether you are accidentally pricing out your core customer base.
