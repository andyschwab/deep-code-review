### Framework for Evaluating Price Elasticity vs. Churn Risk

To present a defensible recommendation to your CFO and Sales VP, you must separate the confirmed short-term financial gain from the unverified long-term retention risk. Do not blend these into a single "net" figure yet; instead, model them as distinct scenarios.

**1. Quantify the Immediate Revenue Lift (The Known)**
Calculate the net revenue impact of the test using the specific data provided.
*   **Baseline Scenario:** 300 prospects × 18% conversion = 54 customers.
*   **Test Scenario:** 300 prospects × 16% conversion = 48 customers.
*   **Volume Impact:** You lost 6 customers (11% volume drop).
*   **Price Impact:** You gained 12% price per unit.
*   **Net Short-Term Effect:** Since the price increase (12%) exceeds the conversion drop (11% relative drop), your immediate revenue from new logos has increased slightly.
*   **Action:** Present this as the "Floor." Even if churn is catastrophic, the first-year revenue from *new* business is higher. This validates the "quality signal" hypothesis for initial acquisition.

**2. Model the Break-Even Churn Rate (The Unknown)**
Since you lack six-month renewal data, you must calculate the "tolerance threshold." Determine how much additional churn the 12% price premium can absorb before the strategy becomes unprofitable.
*   **Formula:** Calculate the Lifetime Value (LTV) of a customer at the old price vs. the new price.
*   **The Question:** "How many months of retained revenue at the higher price does it take to offset the loss of the 6 customers who didn't buy?"
*   **Scenario A (Optimistic):** If the "quality signal" holds, these enterprise clients may actually churn *less* because they are more committed. If true, the strategy is a double-win.
*   **Scenario B (Pessimistic):** If price sensitivity triggers churn later, calculate the maximum allowable churn increase. If the new tier churns >X% more than the standard tier, the lift is erased.
*   **Action:** Present this as a "Risk Boundary." Tell leadership: "This strategy remains profitable even if renewal rates drop by up to [Calculated %]. If we exceed that, we revert."

**3. Address the Data Gap with Leading Indicators**
You cannot wait six months to decide. Identify proxy metrics available now that correlate with future renewal:
*   **Sales Cycle Friction:** Did the 16% who convert take significantly longer to close or require more executive approval? High friction often predicts future churn.
*   **Usage Depth:** Are the 48 new customers utilizing more features than the historical average? Higher engagement usually offsets price sensitivity.
*   **Qualitative Feedback:** Review lost deal notes for the 54 prospects who didn't convert. Was price the explicit blocker, or was it timing/budget cycles?

**4. Strategic Recommendation Structure**
For your presentation, structure the decision as follows:
*   **Recommendation:** Proceed with a **phased global rollout** limited to enterprise tiers in markets similar to Vienna.
*   **Condition:** Implement a "Churn Watch" protocol. If renewal rates in the first two quarters exceed the calculated break-even threshold, pause expansion.
*   **Rationale:** The short-term revenue lift is mathematically proven. The long-term risk is quantifiable and manageable via the break-even model. Waiting for perfect six-month data delays revenue capture unnecessarily.

This approach shifts the conversation from "guessing" about churn to "managing" a calculated risk boundary.

*Note: Ensure your "average contract value" calculation accounts for any discounting given during the negotiation of the 48 closed deals to avoid inflating the perceived price lift.*
*Do not apply this elasticity coefficient to SMB or Mid-Market segments without separate testing, as enterprise "quality signaling" rarely translates down-market.*
