### Core Metrics for KiwiCollectibles Market Health

To determine whether to relax or tighten seller verification before the winter auction season, track these five core metrics segmented by **Verified** vs. **Unverified** sellers. These move beyond raw volume to measure actual market liquidity and trust.

**1. Sell-Through Rate (STR)**
*   **Formula:** `(Items Sold / Items Listed) × 100` over a 30-day rolling window.
*   **Why it matters:** This is your primary health indicator. A 40% rise in listings with flat GMV suggests your overall STR has likely dropped. Compare the STR of verified sellers against unverified ones. If verified sellers have a significantly higher STR, the badge drives quality transactions. If both are low, the issue is buyer demand, not seller quality.

**2. Average Order Value (AOV) by Seller Type**
*   **Formula:** `Total GMV / Total Number of Transactions` segmented by seller verification status.
*   **Why it matters:** This isolates whether the new listings are genuinely low-value junk or if high-value items are just sitting unsold. If unverified sellers have a low AOV, they are diluting your marketplace with cheap inventory. If verified sellers have high AOV but low transaction volume, your verification process might be too cumbersome for serious collectors with rare, high-value items.

**3. Time-to-Sale (Liquidity Speed)**
*   **Formula:** Average days between `Listing Date` and `Sale Date`.
*   **Why it matters:** Inventory that sits stagnant ties up buyer attention without generating revenue. If verified items sell in 5 days while unverified items sit for 45, the badge is working as a trust signal. If both groups sit equally long, buyers may be ignoring the badge entirely, or pricing is misaligned across the board.

**4. Buyer Return/Dispute Rate**
*   **Formula:** `(Returns + Formal Disputes) / Total Transactions` by seller type.
*   **Why it matters:** This measures the "trust" outcome directly. If unverified sellers have a high dispute rate, tightening verification is essential to protect buyer satisfaction. If dispute rates are identical across both groups, the verification badge adds operational friction without improving actual transaction safety, suggesting you should relax requirements to boost volume.

**5. Verified Seller Application Drop-off Rate**
*   **Formula:** `(Started Application / Completed Verification) × 100`.
*   **Why it matters:** This diagnoses the "cumbersome process" risk. If high-volume sellers start the process but abandon it, your requirements are likely too strict for serious collectors. Track *who* drops off: if sellers with historically high GMV abandon the process, you are actively blocking your best inventory.

### Strategic Recommendation Framework

Use these metrics to make your July decision:

*   **Tighten Verification If:** Unverified sellers show low STR, low AOV, and high dispute rates. The current growth is "noise" hurting buyer experience.
*   **Relax Verification If:** Verified and unverified sellers have similar STR and dispute rates, but the drop-off rate for verification is high among sellers with valuable inventory. The badge is creating a bottleneck without adding trust value.
*   **Refine, Don't Change If:** Verified sellers outperform on all metrics but volume is too low. The badge works, but the process needs streamlining (e.g., faster manual review for known collectors) rather than removal.

Do not rely on raw listing counts; a marketplace with 1,000 unsold items is less healthy than one with 100 sold items. Focus on the velocity and value of transactions, not just the supply of inventory.

*Note: Ensure your analytics window matches the 30-day rolling period for all metrics to avoid comparing full-month spend against partial-month results.*
