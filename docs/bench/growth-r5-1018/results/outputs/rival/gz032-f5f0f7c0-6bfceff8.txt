**Recommendation: Do not roll out globally yet. Restrict the change to new users only while requesting immediate segmentation analysis.**

The conflicting signals suggest the new layout successfully optimizes for "search and leave" behavior but fails to facilitate "discovery and dwell" behavior. For a music discovery app, a 40% drop in session duration (from 8 minutes to ~4.8 minutes) is a critical risk indicator, even with higher satisfaction scores. Satisfaction surveys often measure immediate friction reduction ("I found my song quickly"), whereas session duration correlates with exposure to new artists, ad inventory, and deep engagement. If power users are utilizing the efficient layout to bypass discovery features entirely, you may be trading long-term retention and monetization for short-term task completion.

Rolling out globally risks alienating your core user base who value exploration over efficiency. By restricting the rollout to **new users**, you leverage the layout's strength (reducing early friction and improving first-time success) without disrupting the habitual discovery patterns of existing users. New users often need clarity and speed to convert; existing users need depth and serendipity to retain.
