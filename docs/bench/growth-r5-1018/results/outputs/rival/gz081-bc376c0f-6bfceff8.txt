### Recommended Tracking Plan: Neon Squirrels Launch (Month 1)

Given your team size (14) and the specific investor need to validate the "speed-to-purchase" hypothesis, do not track every tap. That volume will obscure insights and strain your infrastructure. Instead, implement a **Funnel-First Strategy** focused on four critical events. This approach validates your core assumption while identifying exactly where the 58% of users drop off after Day 1.

#### 1. Priority Events & Properties

Track only these four events. All must include `user_id`, `session_id`, and `device_type`.

| Event Name | Trigger | Key Properties | Decision Purpose |
| :--- | :--- | :--- | : |
| `tutorial_started` | User clicks "Play" on first launch | `install_source` (if available) | Establishes the top of the funnel baseline. |
| `level_completed` | User finishes a puzzle | `level_number` (1–5+), `duration_seconds` | Identifies specific drop-off points; validates the "5 levels" threshold. |
| `tutorial_milestone_5` | User completes Level 5 | `total_time_seconds` (sum of levels 1–5) | **Critical:** Validates if the user hit the <2-minute target. |
| `iap_attempted` | User clicks any store item | `item_id`, `price_point` | Measures intent. Distinguishes between "don't want to buy" vs. "payment failed." |

*Note: Do not track individual taps or moves within a level yet. Aggregate those into `duration_seconds` and `moves_count` as properties of `level_completed` if storage allows, but prioritize the completion signal first.*

#### 2. How to Interpret Initial Results

Your dashboard should answer three specific questions for your investors:

**A. Is the "Speed Hypothesis" true?**
Create a cohort split: Users who hit `tutorial_milestone_5` in <120 seconds vs. those who took >120 seconds.
*   **Signal:** If the fast group has a significantly higher `iap_attempted` rate (e.g., >15% vs <2%), your hypothesis holds. Double down on optimizing the first 2 minutes.
*   **Pivot:** If speed shows no correlation to purchase, stop optimizing for speed immediately. Investigate *difficulty* or *engagement* instead.

**B. Where is the "Leak"?**
Plot a funnel: `tutorial_started` → `level_completed` (1) → ... → `level_completed` (5).
*   **Signal:** If 40% drop between Level 2 and 3, that specific puzzle is too hard or broken. Fixing this one level is more valuable than changing the whole game.
*   **Context:** Compare this to your 42% Day-1 return. If they finish the tutorial but don't return, the core loop is boring. If they drop *during* the tutorial, the onboarding is broken.

**C. Are we losing money at the register?**
Compare `iap_attempted` vs. actual successful purchases (tracked by your store SDK).
*   **Signal:** High attempts but low sales indicates a pricing or UX friction issue, not a lack of desire.

#### 3. Implementation Note for Small Teams
Use a lightweight tool like PostHog or Firebase (free tiers suffice for this volume). Configure **sampling** if event volume spikes, but with only four event types, you should be able to ingest 100% of data without infrastructure upgrades.

**Deliverable Summary:** Focus exclusively on the completion funnel and the time-to-level-5 metric. Ignore granular interaction data until the core conversion path is stable.

*Investors need to see a validated link between early engagement and revenue, not a heatmap of every tap. This plan proves that link in week one.*
