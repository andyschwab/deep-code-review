# Referral Quality Evaluation Framework for TutorLink

To determine whether to increase the referral bonus or fix the matching logic, you must shift from measuring **acquisition volume** (300 referrals) to **activation quality**. The following metrics will isolate whether the drop-off is caused by inventory mismatch (your hypothesis) or pricing perception.

### 1. Primary Leading Indicator: "First-Session-to-Second-Session Conversion Rate by Subject/Grade"
This is your most critical diagnostic metric. Do not look at the aggregate 20% rate (60/300). Instead, segment this rate by the specific **Grade Level** and **Subject** requested during signup.
*   **The Signal:** If specific grades (e.g., Grade 10 Math) show <5% conversion while others show >30%, your hypothesis is correct: the matching algorithm or tutor inventory is failing specific segments.
*   **The Action:** If low-conversion segments correlate with low tutor supply, invest in **matching logic** and tutor recruitment. If conversion is uniformly low across all subjects, the issue is likely **pricing** or program quality, not matching.

### 2. Secondary Indicator: "Time-to-First-Match" vs. "Session Completion Rate"
Measure the time elapsed between a referred user's signup and their first booked session, then correlate this with whether they book a second session.
*   **The Signal:** If users who wait >48 hours for a match rarely book a second session, the friction is in the **speed of the initial match**, not the price.
*   **The Action:** Optimize the algorithm for speed in high-demand grades before the August term.

### 3. Pricing Sensitivity Proxy: "Credit Redemption Rate vs. Out-of-Pocket Spend"
Track how many referred users book a second session *immediately* after their $5 credit expires versus those who book while the credit is active.
*   **The Signal:** If drop-off spikes exactly when the credit is exhausted, the issue is **price sensitivity**. If they drop off even while the credit is active, the issue is **tutor fit**.
*   **The Action:** If price sensitivity is high, doubling the bonus might work temporarily. If fit is the issue, a larger bonus will only acquire more low-quality referrals, wasting budget.

### 4. Qualitative Leading Indicator: "Search Zero-Result Frequency"
Track how often a referred user searches for a tutor in their grade/subject and receives zero results or skips the booking flow after viewing profiles.
*   **The Signal:** High "zero-result" searches directly confirm inventory gaps. High "view-but-no-book" rates suggest the available tutors do not match expectations (profile quality, price, or availability).

### Recommendation for Maria's Decision
Present the data in a **Segmented Conversion Matrix** (Grade/Subject vs. Conversion Rate).
*   **Scenario A (Inventory Issue):** If conversion varies wildly by subject, **do not double the bonus**. Direct funds to improving the matching algorithm and recruiting tutors for the specific grades showing <10% retention.
*   **Scenario B (Pricing/Value Issue):** If conversion is consistently low (<15%) across all subjects and users drop off post-credit, the product value proposition is weak. In this case, doubling the bonus is a risky stopgap; consider testing a "first session free" model instead of a credit to reduce initial friction.

**Note:** Ensure your tracking captures the `grade_level` and `subject` properties on the `signup_completed` and `session_booked` events to enable this segmentation. Without these properties, the aggregate data will remain misleading.
