# Incident Report: Real-Time Collaboration Module Performance Impact

**Executive Summary**
The feature flag test for the new real-time collaboration module (October 2–9) successfully validated high user demand but failed to meet critical performance thresholds. While 40% of the exposed cohort engaged with the feature daily, the architecture introduced an average latency increase of 120ms—more than double the 50ms tolerance—and triggered a 3.5% error rate spike during peak loads. This performance degradation directly jeopardizes our Service Level Agreements (SLAs) and has already elicited formal complaints from key enterprise stakeholders.

**Quantified User Experience Degradation**
The trade-off between feature adoption and system stability is currently unsustainable. The test exposed 10% of our user base to an experience that significantly deviates from our baseline standards:
*   **Latency Violation:** Users experienced 120ms of added latency against a target of <50ms. In real-time collaboration, this delay manifests as noticeable lag between keystrokes and screen updates, degrading the perception of "real-time" functionality.
*   **Reliability Failure:** The 3.5% error rate during peak hours on October 6 indicates that approximately 1 in every 29 actions failed for affected users during high-load periods. For enterprise clients relying on continuous uptime for collaborative workflows, this failure rate is material and disruptive.
*   **Adoption vs. Satisfaction Paradox:** The 40% daily active usage rate confirms the feature solves a genuine user need. However, high adoption in the face of performance friction suggests users are tolerating a subpar experience due to lack of alternatives, not because the experience is acceptable. This tolerance is likely to evaporate if the issues persist or expand to the full user base.

**SLA and Enterprise Risk Assessment**
The observed metrics pose a direct threat to our contractual obligations and customer trust:
*   **SLA Breach Potential:** Most enterprise SLAs guarantee specific uptime percentages and maximum latency thresholds. A sustained 3.5% error rate and 120ms latency penalty likely constitute a breach of these terms, exposing DataFlow Systems to financial penalties and service credits.
*   **Enterprise Client Impact:** The complaint from our largest enterprise client signals that the issue is affecting high-value accounts. These clients often have stricter performance requirements and lower tolerance for instability than the general user base.
*   **Geographic Uncertainty:** Due to incomplete logging, we cannot yet determine if the latency is globally distributed or isolated to specific regions. If the issue is global, a full rollout would impact 100% of our enterprise base. If regional, it may indicate infrastructure bottlenecks in specific data centers that could worsen under increased load.

**Recommendation Context for Decision Making**
The data presents a clear conflict: the market validation is strong, but the technical execution is currently failing to meet minimum viability standards for performance.

*   **Immediate Flag Disablement:** This path prioritizes SLA compliance and enterprise trust. It eliminates the risk of further degradation or broader exposure but halts the collection of data needed to isolate the geographic scope of the latency issue.
*   **Hotfix Attempt:** This path attempts to preserve the validated user value while addressing the performance deficit. However, it carries the risk of extending the period of degraded service for the 10% cohort and potentially introducing new instabilities if the root cause is architectural rather than superficial.

The decision rests on whether the immediate risk to enterprise relationships and SLA standing outweighs the value of maintaining the test to gather further diagnostic data. Given the confirmed impact on a major client and the magnitude of the latency deviation, the current state represents an unacceptable risk profile for production traffic.

---
*Note: Complete geographic analysis is pending implementation of enhanced logging. Immediate action is required regardless of final root cause identification.*
