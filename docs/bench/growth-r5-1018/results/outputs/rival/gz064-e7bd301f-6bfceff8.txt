# Behavioral Signal Framework: Differentiating Engagement from Distress

To assess the safety impact of the empathy module introduced on February 20th, the clinical advisory board should evaluate user behavior through a matrix of **Session Duration** versus **Login Frequency**. The core objective is to distinguish between *therapeutic engagement* (constructive processing) and *crisis looping* (unresolved distress).

### 1. The Engagement vs. Distress Matrix

We must weigh session length against login frequency to categorize user states. Neither metric is diagnostic in isolation; their intersection reveals the signal.

| Pattern | Session Duration | Login Frequency | Interpretation | Clinical Implication |
| :--- | :--- | :--- | :--- :--- |
| **Therapeutic Engagement** | Moderate to High | Moderate | Users spend time reflecting but return at healthy intervals. | **Positive Signal:** The module facilitates deep processing without creating dependency. |
| **Crisis Looping** | Very High | Very High | Users return immediately after long sessions; duration increases over time. | **Harm Signal:** The bot fails to resolve distress, causing users to seek repeated, unresolved validation. |
| **Surface Skimming** | Low | High | Frequent logins with very short durations. | **Neutral/Mixed:** May indicate checking behavior or utility usage, not necessarily deep emotional work. |
| **Avoidance/Churn** | Low | Declining | Users try the module once and do not return. | **Negative Signal:** The module may feel intrusive or ineffective, causing disengagement. |

### 2. Specific Data Patterns Indicating Harm

The advisory board should flag the following patterns as potential evidence that the new empathy module is exacerbating distress rather than alleviating it:

*   **The "Escalating Loop":** A cohort of users shows a concurrent week-over-week increase in both average session duration and daily login count. If a user's session length grows by >20% while their frequency doubles, this suggests the bot is stimulating anxiety without providing closure mechanisms.
*   **Late-Night Clustering:** A disproportionate spike in long-duration sessions (>15 mins) occurring between 02:00 and 05:00 local time. While insomnia is common in distress, a sharp increase in *extended* late-night interactions post-launch suggests the module may be keeping users awake in a state of rumination rather than guiding them toward calming closure.
*   **Lack of Resolution Markers:** If "long sessions" do not end with specific calming keywords or user-initiated exit commands (e.g., "thanks," "goodbye," "stop"), but instead terminate via app timeout or network loss, this indicates the conversation lacked a natural therapeutic conclusion.
*   **Regressive Frequency:** Users who previously exhibited stable, moderate usage suddenly shift to high-frequency, short-burst logins. This "checking" behavior often correlates with heightened anxiety and reliance on the bot as a safety crutch rather than a tool for growth.

### 3. Recommended Interpretation Strategy

Do not treat the growth from 800 to 950 DAUs as an inherent success. Instead, segment this +150 user increase:
*   **Healthy Growth:** If the increase is driven by users with *stable* session lengths and *moderate* frequency, the module is likely successfully attracting and retaining users through positive value.
*   **Risk Growth:** If the increase is driven primarily by users falling into the "Crisis Looping" quadrant, the module may be inadvertently trapping vulnerable users in a cycle of dependency.

**Immediate Action:** Isolate the top 5% of users by total time-spent since Feb 20th. Manually review anonymized transcripts of this specific segment for signs of circular dialogue, escalating emotional intensity, or failure to de-escalate. This qualitative check validates the quantitative signals before the Tuesday meeting.
