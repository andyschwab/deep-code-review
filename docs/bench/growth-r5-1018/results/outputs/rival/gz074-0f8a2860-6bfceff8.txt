# Experiment Design: Priority Pickup Feature Test

## Hypothesis
Because high-density areas (Andheri) face capacity constraints during rush hour, we believe introducing a 'priority pickup' feature at a 15% adoption cap will increase premium revenue by 10% without increasing standard user wait times by more than 30 seconds. We will know this is true if the primary metric (premium revenue per active hour) increases while the guardrail metric (median wait time for standard users) remains within the 30-second threshold.

## Traffic Allocation Strategy
Given the risk of degrading service for standard users, a standard 50/50 split is too aggressive. We recommend a **ramped, geo-targeted approach**:

1.  **Phase 1 (Risk Mitigation):** Launch in Andheri only during non-peak hours (10 AM – 4 PM) with a **90/10 split** (90% control, 10% treatment). This limits exposure while validating the technical implementation and initial driver response.
2.  **Phase 2 (Core Test):** If Phase 1 shows no negative guardrail signals after 5 days, expand to rush hours (7–10 AM, 5–8 PM) in Andheri with an **80/20 split**. This respects your assumption that <10% adoption is safe, providing a buffer while gathering significant data on the trade-off.
3.  **Phase 3 (Expansion):** Only proceed to city-wide testing if Phase 2 confirms the "negligible impact" assumption.

*Note: Users are bucketed by User ID to ensure consistency; a user sees the same experience throughout the test.*

## Metrics Framework

### Primary Metric
*   **Premium Revenue Per Active Hour:** Measures the direct financial uplift of the feature, normalized by supply availability.

### Secondary Metrics
*   **Priority Adoption Rate:** Percentage of total requests selecting priority. (Critical for validating the <10% threshold assumption).
*   **Driver Utilization Rate:** Confirms the simulated 20% increase in real-world conditions.
*   **Cancellation Rate (Priority vs. Standard):** Indicates if drivers are cherry-picking priority rides and abandoning standard ones.

### Guardrail Metrics (Stop-Loss Triggers)
The test must be paused immediately if any of the following occur in the treatment group compared to control:
1.  **Median Wait Time (Standard Users):** Increases by >30 seconds in Andheri during rush hour.
2.  **Driver Complaint Volume:** Increases by >15% regarding app complexity or unfair dispatching (addressing Raj's specific concern).
3.  **Standard User Cancellation Rate:** Increases by >5% (indicating frustration with delayed pickups).
4.  **Priority SLA Breach:** >20% of priority pickups fail to arrive within 5 minutes (protects brand promise).

## Risk Mitigation for Operations Director
To address Raj's concerns regarding driver complaints and monsoon readiness:
*   **Driver Communication:** Pre-brief driver unions in Andheri about the test, framing it as a tool to increase their earnings potential during idle times, not a penalty system.
*   **Hard Cap Implementation:** Engineer a hard stop on priority requests if adoption hits 12% in any 15-minute window, automatically reverting to standard dispatch to protect the network.
*   **Monsoon Buffer:** Complete Phase 2 analysis by late May. If results are inconclusive or negative, abort the city-wide rollout before the June monsoon begins, as weather-induced supply shocks could skew data and exacerbate service failures.

## Sample Size & Duration
Assuming a baseline conversion to priority of 5% and aiming to detect a 10% lift in revenue with 80% power:
*   **Required Observations:** Approximately 40,000 ride requests per variant.
*   **Estimated Duration:** 14–18 days for Phase 2 (rush hour only), assuming current Andheri volume.

This structure allows you to validate the revenue opportunity while strictly capping the risk to standard users and driver sentiment.
