**Recommendation: Primary Metric for Investor Health**

For the new commercial marketplace, the single primary metric capturing investor health should be **Active Diversified Capital (ADC)**.

This is a composite metric defined as: *The total capital deployed by investors holding two or more distinct commercial assets within the last 12 months.*

While your team debates choosing between asset count (diversification) and capital volume (deployment), selecting either in isolation creates blind spots for a commercial real estate platform. ADC resolves this by enforcing the condition that healthy capital must be both significant in volume and spread across multiple assets.

**Comparison of Candidates**

**1. Asset Count (Number of Properties Held)**
*   **Pros:** Directly aligns with your data showing diversified investors retain 40% longer. It encourages the behavioral pattern of portfolio building, which is critical for stability in commercial real estate where vacancy risks are higher than in residential. It is easy to communicate to the Board as a measure of platform stickiness.
*   **Cons:** It ignores value. An investor holding three $5,000 shares is technically "diversified" but contributes negligible revenue or stability compared to one holding two $250,000 shares. In commercial real estate, deal sizes vary wildly; counting units without weighting by capital distorts the true economic health of the investor base. It risks incentivizing the sale of micro-shares to inflate metrics without moving the revenue needle.

**2. Total Capital Deployed**
*   **Pros:** Directly correlates with referral activity and immediate revenue (fees are typically % of capital). It reflects the sheer scale of assets under management (AUM), a key valuation driver for fintech platforms. It captures the "whale" investors who drive bulk volume.
*   **Cons:** As you noted, it includes "one-and-done" investors who deploy large sums once and churn. In commercial real estate, where sales cycles are long and re-investment windows are infrequent, a high capital number could mask a looming retention cliff. If 80% of your capital comes from non-repeat investors, your growth is unsustainable. This metric fails to distinguish between a loyal partner and a transient transaction.

**Why Active Diversified Capital (ADC) Wins**

ADC forces the intersection of retention and value. By counting only capital from investors with 2+ assets, you automatically filter out the "one-and-done" large deployers who threaten long-term stability. Simultaneously, by summing capital rather than counting units, you ensure that the metric reflects actual economic weight, avoiding the trap of inflating numbers with insignificant micro-transactions.

This metric specifically addresses the uncertainty of the shift to commercial assets. Since commercial hold times (currently 18 months) may lengthen or shorten based on asset class (e.g., industrial vs. office), focusing on *re-investment behavior* (holding 2+ assets) is a more leading indicator of health than the static hold time duration. It tells the Board not just how much money you have, but how much money is *committed* to the platform long-term.

**Board Presentation Strategy**

Present ADC as the "North Star" for Year 1 of the commercial launch. Frame asset count and total capital as secondary diagnostic metrics: use asset count to monitor user engagement frequency and total capital to monitor market share. But make clear that only ADC predicts the sustainable unit economics required for Series B/C growth.

Note: Ensure the "12-month" window is explicitly defined in your data dictionary to avoid counting legacy residential holdings unless cross-asset diversification is a specific goal.
Note: If the Board requires a simpler headline number, report ADC as "Retained AUM" to emphasize the quality of the capital base.
