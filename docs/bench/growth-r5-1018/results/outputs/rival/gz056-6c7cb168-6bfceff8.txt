**Segmentation Analysis Hypotheses**
Without direct user feedback, we must infer causality from usage patterns associated with specific segments. The Day 14 cliff aligns with two potential technical triggers:

1.  **Battery Calibration Cycle:** If the new firmware alters charging logic, users who charge bi-weekly (typical for moderate runners) may experience a "deep discharge" lockout at the 14-day mark if the battery estimation algorithm is inaccurate. This would disproportionately affect **Runners** (consistent, moderate usage) compared to **Cyclists** (often longer, high-GPS sessions requiring more frequent charging, potentially masking the bug).
2.  **Data Sync Threshold:** If the update changes how data is batched, a buffer overflow might occur after exactly two weeks of accumulated data for users who do not open the app daily. This would impact **Casual Users** more than **Power Users**.

**Recommended Immediate Actions**

*   **Isolate by Charging Frequency:** Cross-reference the drop-off users with their last known battery level. If the majority disconnected at <5% battery, the issue is likely a firmware-induced deep-sleep bug preventing wake-up, not a hardware defect requiring recall.
*   **Segment by Activity Type:** Filter the Day 14 drop-offs by primary activity.
    *   *If Runners are hit hardest:* Investigate GPS battery drain calculations in the new power-saving mode.
    *   *If Cyclists are hit hardest:* Investigate high-intensity data logging limits.
    *   *If uniform across segments:* The issue is likely a general synchronization timeout or account-level authentication expiry introduced in the update.
*   **Proxy Feedback Loop:** Since direct feedback is missing, analyze support ticket volume for "device not turning on" or "sync failed" starting Nov 19 (14 days post-launch). A spike here confirms a technical block rather than voluntary churn.

**Strategic Recommendation**
Do not initiate a hardware recall yet. The stability of Day 1–7 metrics suggests the hardware is functional upon setup. The pattern points to a software logic error triggered by a specific time-or-usage threshold. Prioritize a hotfix patch addressing battery wake-up protocols and sync batching. Prepare customer communications focusing on a "software calibration update" to restore confidence, rather than acknowledging a potential hardware defect.
