### Candidate Retention Metrics for Maple Meal Prep

**1. Active Subscription Status (Binary Weekly)**
*Definition:* A customer is "retained" in a given week if their subscription status is active and they have not paused or canceled, regardless of order value.
*   **Pros:** Simplest to calculate and communicate to stakeholders; directly aligns with the current "two-week cancellation" spike you are observing; clearly distinguishes between a paused account and a churned account.
*   **Cons:** Blind to downgrades (e.g., a user switching from 5 meals/week to 2 meals/week counts as fully retained); fails to capture the revenue impact of price sensitivity if users remain subscribed but order less frequently before eventually leaving.

**2. Revenue-Based Retention (Rolling 30-Day Spend)**
*Definition:* A customer is "retained" if their total monetary spend in the last 30 days meets a minimum threshold (e.g., >$40, representing at least two standard meals).
*   **Pros:** Directly measures financial health and accounts for both frequency and plan tier; effectively identifies "soft churn" where users drastically reduce spend before canceling; ideal for modeling the impact of a new, cheaper plan on total revenue.
*   **Cons:** More complex to explain than a simple count; sensitive to the specific threshold chosen (too high, and you miss legitimate low-frequency users; too low, and you mask churn); may lag behind actual behavioral intent since it relies on a trailing window.

**3. Hybrid Engagement Score (Frequency × Value)**
*Definition:* A composite metric where a customer is "retained" only if they maintain both a minimum frequency (e.g., ≥1 order every 14 days) AND a minimum average order value (AOV) over a rolling period.
*   **Pros:** Most robust for distinguishing temporary pauses from permanent churn; specifically targets your hypothesis by isolating users who drop frequency (portion issues) versus those who drop entirely (price issues); allows segmentation of "at-risk" users who meet one criterion but not the other.
*   **Cons:** Requires defining two arbitrary thresholds which can complicate trend analysis; harder to visualize in a single line chart without breaking it into segments; may require custom SQL or analytics setup compared to standard subscription reports.

**Recommendation:** For your immediate decision on a student plan, **Revenue-Based Retention** is likely the strongest leading indicator. It will reveal if users are "stretching" their subscriptions (ordering less often) due to the price hike before fully canceling, which binary status would miss.

*Note: Ensure your data excludes the 48-hour payment failure window to avoid misclassifying temporary billing issues as churn. When presenting on the 25th, visualize the drop-off in average revenue per user (ARPU) alongside the cancellation rate to highlight the hidden revenue loss.*
