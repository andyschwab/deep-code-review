**Executive Summary: Collaboration Feature A/B Test (April 1–14)**

**Recommendation:** Do not proceed with a global rollout next week based solely on the current 25% lift figure. The observed result is statistically confounded by the external market shock, making the true impact of the feature unquantifiable at this time. Rolling out now risks attributing situational growth to product value, potentially leading to inflated expectations and misallocated resources.

**Contextual Analysis**
The test period coincided perfectly with a forced market shift to remote work. This external event acts as a "history bias," invalidating the assumption that the treatment and control groups differed only by the feature. The 25% increase in Daily Active Users (DAU) for the treatment group likely comprises three components:
1.  **Baseline Organic Growth:** Normal traction the feature would see in stable conditions.
2.  **Situational Lift:** Usage spikes driven purely by the necessity of remote work (affecting all users, but potentially amplified in the treatment group if the feature addresses immediate pain points).
3.  **Interaction Effect:** The specific value of the feature *because* of the remote shift (e.g., the feature is only valuable when teams are distributed).

Without isolating these factors, claiming the feature caused a 25% lift is analytically unsound.

**Approach to Estimate Standalone Impact**

To provide the CEO with a realistic assessment, execute the following three-step analysis immediately:

1.  **Analyze the Control Group Trend**
    Examine the DAU trajectory of the control group (users without the feature) during April 1–14 compared to the prior two weeks.
    *   If the control group also saw a massive spike (e.g., +20%), the majority of the treatment group's lift is situational. The feature's incremental value may be only ~5%.
    *   If the control group remained flat while the treatment group spiked, the feature likely captured the market demand effectively, suggesting a higher standalone value.

2.  **Extend the Observation Window (Holdback Analysis)**
    Keep the test running for an additional 14–21 days as market conditions stabilize or the initial shock absorbs.
    *   Monitor if the lift percentage converges (drops) as the "panic adoption" phase ends.
    *   A sustained lift after the initial shock suggests genuine product-market fit. A rapid decay to baseline suggests the April results were almost entirely event-driven.

3.  **Segment by "Remote-Readiness"**
    If data exists, segment users by their pre-test remote maturity.
    *   Compare lift among teams already remote vs. teams newly forced remote.
    *   If the lift is uniform across both, the feature has broad utility. If the lift is concentrated only in the "newly remote" segment, the feature's value is highly context-dependent.

**Strategic Path Forward**

Present the CEO with a range rather than a single number:
*   **Conservative Estimate:** (Treatment Lift) minus (Control Group Lift). This isolates the feature's incremental contribution above the market tide.
*   **Base Case:** The current 25%, explicitly labeled as "Event-Enhanced Performance."
*   **Action Plan:** Delay global rollout by 2 weeks. Continue the test to capture post-shock data. If the extended data shows sustained lift >10% above the control group, proceed with rollout. If lift collapses, re-evaluate the feature's positioning for stable markets.

This approach protects the company from over-promising while validating the feature's potential in a real-world stress test.
