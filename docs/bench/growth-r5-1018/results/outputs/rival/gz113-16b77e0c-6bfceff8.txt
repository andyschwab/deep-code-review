**Evaluation of Norway Donation Button Test**

**Current Status: Inconclusive / Underpowered**
Do not roll out globally. Do not kill the experiment yet. The current data (5,000 visitors, 0.2% absolute lift) is insufficient to make a reliable decision.

**1. How to Read the Current Results**
Your observed lift is ~9.5% (from 2.1% to 2.3%). However, with only ~105 conversions in the control group and ~115 in the variant, the statistical power is critically low.
*   **The Confidence Interval Problem:** The "wide confidence interval" you noted likely spans from a meaningful decrease (e.g., -15%) to a meaningful increase (e.g., +35%). Because this range includes zero and substantial negative values, you cannot claim the green button is better.
*   **False Positive Risk:** Stopping now because the number looks higher ("peeking") creates a high risk of a Type I error (false positive). Random noise frequently creates temporary lifts of this magnitude in small samples.
*   **Guardrail Verification:** You assumed no impact on average donation size (AOV), but this is unverified. In low-power tests, a change in conversion rate often correlates with a change in donor quality. You must calculate the confidence interval for AOV before proceeding; if green attracts lower-value donors, the net revenue impact could be negative despite higher conversion.

**2. Strategic Recommendation: Extend, Do Not Aggregate**
**Option A: Extend Run Time (Recommended)**
Continue the test specifically for the Norway segment until you reach statistical significance or a pre-determined maximum sample size.
*   *Why:* Norway may have unique cultural color associations. A global rollout based on noisy Nordic data risks diluting conversion in other markets where green might perform neutrally or negatively.
*   *Action:* Calculate the required sample size for your Minimum Detectable Effect (MDE). If you need 40,000 visitors per variant to detect a 5% lift reliably, run the test until you hit that volume, even if it takes weeks.

**Option B: Aggregate Nordic Countries (Conditional)**
Only aggregate data from Sweden, Denmark, and Finland if:
1.  **Cultural Homogeneity:** You have qualitative evidence that color perception regarding "trust" and "donation" is identical across these specific markets.
2.  **Baseline Parity:** The control conversion rates across these countries are statistically similar. If Sweden converts at 4% and Norway at 2%, aggregating them without weighting or stratification will skew results.
*   *Risk:* If the green button works in Norway but fails in Sweden, aggregation might show a "neutral" result, causing you to miss a winning opportunity in Norway specifically.

**3. Immediate Next Steps for the Head of Fundraising**
1.  **Pause Decision Making:** State clearly that the current 0.2% lift is within the margin of error. Rolling out now is a gamble, not a data-driven decision.
2.  **Verify Guardrails:** Immediately analyze Average Order Value (AOV) and Revenue Per Visitor (RPV) for the test period. If RPV is flat or down, the test is a failure regardless of conversion rate.
3.  **Commit to a Timeline:** Define the exact sample size needed for 80% power at 95% confidence. Present the date when this volume will be reached if the test continues.
4.  **Global Rollout Strategy:** Recommend running a simultaneous, smaller-scale test in a high-traffic market (e.g., US or UK) to see if the color effect holds across different cultures before a full global deployment.

**Conclusion:** The responsible path is to treat this as "data collection in progress," not a completed test. Extend the Norway test to gather sufficient data, verify revenue metrics, and avoid the costly error of implementing a change that may not actually work.

*Note: Ensure your tracking captures "Revenue Per Visitor" as the primary metric, not just conversion rate, to account for potential shifts in donor behavior.*
*Do not stop the test early based on the current positive trend; this is the most common cause of failed optimization programs.*
