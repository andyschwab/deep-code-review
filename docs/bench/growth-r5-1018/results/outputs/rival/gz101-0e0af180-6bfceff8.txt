**Recommendation: Redefine Activation to "First Value Realized" (FVR)**

Do not use "5 tickets resolved" as your activation metric. It is a lagging indicator that occurs too late to prevent the 30% churn you are seeing by day three. For B2B ticketing software, resolution is an output of usage, not the trigger for it. Smaller teams may take weeks to hit five tickets, by which time they have already disengaged.

**The New Single Metric: "Workflow Configured + First Agent Invite"**
Define activation as the completion of two specific actions within the first 48 hours:
1.  **Configuration:** Setting up at least one custom workflow, status, or automation rule.
2.  **Collaboration:** Inviting at least one additional team member.

*Why this predicts renewal:*
Ticketing platforms derive value from team coordination and standardized processes. An account that only logs tickets but never configures workflows or invites others is likely being used as a personal notepad, not a support system. These accounts have low switching costs and high churn risk. Accounts that configure workflows and invite teammates have embedded your product into their operational DNA, making renewal highly probable.

**Validation Plan (No New Dashboards Required)**
You can validate this hypothesis against historical data using a simple spreadsheet export by next Tuesday:

1.  **Export Data:** Pull a CSV of all accounts onboarded in the last 6–12 months. Include columns for: `Account ID`, `Signup Date`, `Date of First Workflow Config`, `Date of First Agent Invite`, `Date of 5th Ticket Resolved`, and `Renewal Status` (Renewed/Churned).
2.  **Cohort Analysis:** Create three groups in your spreadsheet:
    *   *Group A (Current Def):* Accounts that hit 5 resolved tickets.
    *   *Group B (Proposed Def):* Accounts that configured a workflow AND invited an agent.
    *   *Group C (Early Action):* Accounts that did either action but not both.
3.  **Calculate Correlation:** For each group, calculate the % of accounts that renewed.
    *   If Group B's renewal rate is significantly higher than Group A's, your new definition is superior.
    *   Check the *time-to-activation*: If Group B achieves this state in <48 hours (vs. weeks for Group A), you have identified a critical intervention window.
4.  **Segment by Team Size:** Filter your CSV by "number of seats purchased." If small teams (2–5 seats) rarely hit 5 tickets but frequently hit the "Workflow + Invite" milestone and still renew, you have definitive proof that the 5-ticket threshold is inappropriate for them.

**Immediate Next Steps**
*   **Stop waiting for 5 tickets.** Trigger your Customer Success team's "at-risk" workflow if an account hasn't hit "Workflow + Invite" by Day 3.
*   **Update Reporting.** Label this new composite metric as "True Activation" in your next director meeting. Present the renewal correlation from your spreadsheet analysis as the justification.

This approach shifts your focus from counting outputs (tickets) to measuring adoption behaviors (setup and collaboration) that actually drive contract value.

*Note: Ensure your data export captures "deleted" or "draft" workflows if those count as intent in your system.*
*Note: If "Workflow Config" is too complex for your smallest tier, substitute it with "First SLA Rule Created."*
