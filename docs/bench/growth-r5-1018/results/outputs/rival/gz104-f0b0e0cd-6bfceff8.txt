### Analysis Plan: Isolating Price vs. Logistics Churn Drivers

**1. Cohort Segmentation Strategy**
To isolate the price effect from the logistics change, you must segment users based on their exposure to each variable. Do not analyze aggregate churn.

*   **Price-Only Cohort:** Customers who renewed *after* Jan 15th (paid new price) but were served by the *old* logistics partner (if the switch happened later).
*   **Logistics-Only Cohort:** If any customers were migrated to the new logistics partner *before* the price hike (e.g., a pilot region), analyze their churn trend from Dec 15–Jan 14.
*   **Combined Exposure Cohort:** Customers hitting both the price increase and the new logistics partner simultaneously.
*   **Control Group:** Customers on legacy plans (if any exist) or regions where neither change has fully rolled out.

**2. Specific Segments to Examine**
Break down the 9% churn rate by these dimensions to identify sensitivity patterns:

*   **Tenure Buckets:** Compare churn for customers <6 months vs. 1–3 years vs. >3 years. Loyalists (>3 years) showing high churn suggests a "betrayal" reaction to price or a severe service drop; new users (<6 months) churning suggests price sensitivity relative to market alternatives.
*   **Geographic Zones:** Map churn against the rollout map of the new logistics partner. If Zone A (new partner) has 15% churn while Zone B (old partner) has 5% churn post-hike, logistics is the primary driver. If both are ~9%, price is the dominant factor.
*   **Plan Tier:** Analyze if the % increase was uniform. If lower tiers saw a higher absolute € increase or higher % increase, they may be driving the spike.
*   **Cancellation Reason Codes:** Filter exit surveys for "Price" vs. "Delivery Issues/Late/Missing." A spike in "Delivery" codes coinciding with the partner switch is a definitive signal.

**3. Analytical Methods to Isolate Variables**

*   **Difference-in-Differences (DiD):**
    *   Compare the change in churn rate for the "Treatment Group" (affected by price/logistics) vs. a "Control Group" (unaffected or partially affected) before and after Jan 15th.
    *   *Formula:* (Churn_Treatment_Post - Churn_Treatment_Pre) - (Churn_Control_Post - Churn_Control_Pre). This isolates the impact of the specific intervention.
*   **Survival Analysis (Hazard Ratios):**
    *   Model the "time to churn" using Cox Proportional Hazards. Include "Price Increase" and "Logistics Partner" as time-varying covariates. This will output a hazard ratio for each variable (e.g., "New logistics increases churn risk by 2.5x, holding price constant").
*   **Regression Discontinuity:**
    *   If the price hike applied strictly to renewals after Jan 15th, compare customers renewing Jan 14 vs. Jan 16. A sharp jump in churn exactly at the cutoff indicates price sensitivity. A gradual increase over February suggests a cumulative service failure (logistics).

**4. Immediate Data Requests for Paris Team**
*   Daily churn rates from Dec 1 to Feb 28, tagged by: Renewal Date, Logistics Partner ID, Region, Tenure, and Plan Type.
*   Customer support ticket volume categorized by "Billing/Price" vs. "Delivery/Quality" for the same period.
*   Net Promoter Score (NPS) or CSAT trends split by logistics region.

**5. Recommended Next Steps**
1.  **Visualize the Breakpoint:** Plot daily churn. A vertical spike on Jan 15 indicates price. A sloping upward trend starting when the new partner launched indicates service degradation.
2.  **Qualitative Triage:** Immediately interview 10–15 recently churned customers from the highest-churn segment to validate quantitative hypotheses.
3.  **Board Prep:** Present findings as "X% of churn attributed to Price, Y% to Logistics." If Logistics is the driver, propose a partner review. If Price is the driver, model a retention offer (e.g., "Lock in old rate for 6 months") for the board.

*Ensure all rate calculations use matching observation windows (e.g., do not divide February churn by January's active user base).*
*Prioritize the DiD analysis if you have a region or segment that experienced only one of the two changes.*
