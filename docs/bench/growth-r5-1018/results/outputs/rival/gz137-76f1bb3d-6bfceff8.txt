### Urgent Analysis Plan: Order Confirmation Drop-Off

**1. Isolate the Variable (The "When" and "Who")**
Correlate the exact timestamp of the 15% drop with your release calendar.
*   **Release Check:** Did the UI tweak or fee disclosure change go live exactly when the drop started? If yes, treat this as a regression, not a market behavior shift.
*   **Segment Split:** Separate users into two cohorts: those who saw the *old* interface (if any remain due to caching/rollout) vs. the *new* interface. If you cannot split by version, segment by user tenure. Did long-term power traders (who know the old flow) drop off more sharply than newer users? A sharp divergence suggests UI friction; a uniform drop suggests external market factors.

**2. Diagnose the Friction Point (The "Why")**
Since latency is stable, the block is likely cognitive or trust-based. Analyze these specific event slices:
*   **Dwell Time on Preview Screen:** Compare average time spent on the order preview screen before the drop vs. now.
    *   *Increased dwell time:* Users are reading the new fee disclosure or hesitating due to confusion.
    *   *Decreased dwell time:* Users are bouncing immediately, suggesting the button is hidden, broken, or looks disabled.
*   **Error & Abort Rates:** Filter for specific exit events on the confirmation screen.
    *   Are users clicking "Edit Order" instead of "Confirm"? This indicates sticker shock from the fee disclosure.
    *   Are there increased "Session Timeout" or "App Backgrounded" events? This suggests anxiety or distraction.
*   **Device & OS Segmentation:** Break the drop-off down by iOS vs. Android and specific OS versions. The UI tweak might have broken the swipe gesture on a specific screen size or OS update, making the button physically unreachable for a subset of high-frequency traders.

**3. Contextualize with Market Data (The "External" Factor)**
Validate the volatility hypothesis against the specific assets being traded.
*   **Asset-Class Correlation:** Did the drop occur across all assets or only high-volatility pairs (e.g., crypto vs. forex)? If power traders are avoiding specific volatile assets due to the new fee disclosure making costs visible, you will see a drop in *volume per user* even if login rates are stable.
*   **Fee Sensitivity Analysis:** Calculate the average order size of the dropped-off users. If the new disclosure highlights fees on small, high-frequency trades disproportionately, users may be pausing to recalculate profitability.

**4. Immediate Qualitative Validation**
Data tells you *what*, but not *how it feels*.
*   **Session Replay:** Pull 10–20 session recordings of power traders from the last 48 hours who reached the preview screen but did not confirm. Watch for "rage clicks" on the confirmation button or rapid scrolling to find fee details.
*   **Support Ticket Scan:** Search for keywords like "confirm," "swipe," "fee," or "stuck" in support logs from the last 14 days.

**Recommendation for the Risk Committee:**
Present the correlation between the release date and the drop-off first. If the timing aligns, propose an immediate A/B test reverting the confirmation button to the previous design for 50% of users while keeping the fee disclosure (if legally required) but changing its presentation. If the timing does not align, present the asset-class correlation to show if traders are strategically pausing due to market conditions rather than product friction.

Note: Prioritize session replays immediately; they often reveal UI breakages faster than aggregate metrics. Ensure all data slices exclude bot traffic to prevent skewing the "power trader" definition.
