# TechTalk Forum Guidelines Impact Analysis Plan

**Prepared for:** Sophie Laurent, Community Manager
**Objective:** Distinguish between reduced spam and reduced genuine engagement following the February 20th guidelines update.

### 1. Immediate Data Segmentation (The "What")
Do not rely on aggregate thread counts. Immediately segment the "new threads" metric into three distinct categories using existing moderation logs:
*   **Confirmed Spam:** Threads removed by moderators or auto-filters.
*   **Confirmed Toxic:** Threads removed for guideline violations.
*   **Approved Genuine:** Threads that remained live.

**Hypothesis Validation:**
*   If the 15% drop in total threads is driven entirely by a >15% drop in *Confirmed Spam/Toxic* categories, while *Approved Genuine* threads remain flat or grow, the change is successful (noise reduction only).
*   If *Approved Genuine* threads have dropped by ~15%, legitimate users are being silenced.

### 2. Funnel Conversion Analysis (The "Where")
Analyze the user journey to pinpoint where legitimate users are dropping off. Compare the period **Feb 1–19** (Baseline) vs. **Feb 20–Present** (Post-Update):
*   **Page Views → Click "New Thread":** Has interest in posting declined? (Indicates intimidation at the entry point).
*   **Click "New Thread" → Form Submission:** Are users starting but abandoning the draft? (Indicates the guidelines text itself is causing friction).
*   **Form Submission → Post Live:** Has the rejection rate increased for non-spam content?

### 3. Qualitative "Pulse" Check (The "Why")
Since quantitative data may lag, gather immediate qualitative signals:
*   **Support Tickets:** Filter for keywords like "post rejected," "guidelines," or "can't publish" since Feb 20.
*   **User Feedback:** Scan recent "meta" discussions or feedback threads for mentions of the new visibility.
*   **Moderator Sample:** Manually review the last 50 rejected posts. Were they clear spam, or borderline legitimate posts that users might find confusing?

### 4. Cohort Behavior Tracking
Compare behavior by user type to see who is being silenced:
*   **New Users (<5 posts):** Are they disproportionately affected? A drop here suggests intimidation.
*   **Power Users:** Has their activity changed? (Unlikely to be intimidated, so their stability serves as a control group).

### Recommended Next Steps for Tomorrow's Report
1.  **Present the Segmented Data:** Show the split between Spam vs. Genuine drops. This is the definitive answer to your core question.
2.  **Define the Observation Window:** Explicitly state that Feb 20–Present is a short window. Recommend a "Watch Period" of 30 days before making permanent UI changes, as user behavior often stabilizes after an initial shock.
3.  **Proposal for A/B Test (If Genuine Drop is Confirmed):** If genuine threads are down, propose testing a softer presentation (e.g., collapsible guidelines or a checkbox acknowledgment instead of full-text visibility) for a small traffic segment to see if it restores volume without reintroducing toxicity.

**Note:** Ensure the "30% drop in reported posts" is cross-referenced with actual removal rates; fewer reports could mean less toxicity OR users stopping reporting because they feel the system is too strict.
**Note:** Do not adjust the guidelines display until the segmentation analysis confirms whether the loss is spam or signal.
