### Cohort Analysis Framework for BoxedDelight Onboarding Redesign

**1. Cohort Definition & Grouping Logic**
To isolate the impact of the new flow from seasonal trends, define cohorts by **Sign-Up Week** rather than month. Weekly granularity reduces noise from intra-month seasonality and provides faster statistical significance given the January 20th launch.

*   **Primary Segmentation:** Split every weekly cohort into two mutually exclusive groups based on the first-session event:
    *   **Preference Completers:** Users who triggered `preferences_selected` during sign-up.
    *   **Preference Skippers:** Users who triggered `signup_completed` without the preference event.
*   **Secondary Dimension:** Tag each user with their `acquisition_channel` (e.g., paid_social, organic_search, affiliate) at the time of sign-up. Do not aggregate channels; analyze them separately to inform budget allocation.

**2. Observation Windows & Time Alignment**
Avoid mixing partial data with full periods. Since the new flow launches mid-month (Jan 20), do not compare January's full-month performance against previous months.

*   **Baseline Period:** Use cohorts from **October 1 – December 31** to establish the pre-launch baseline for both "Completers" and "Skippers" across all channels.
*   **Treatment Period:** Start analysis with the **Week of Jan 20**.
*   **Retention Metric Definition:** Define "Month-Two Retention" strictly as a binary state: Did the user receive and keep their *third* box? (Sign-up = Month 0; Box 2 = Month 1; Box 3 = Month 2).
*   **Window Matching:** Only calculate retention rates for cohorts where the observation window has fully closed. For example, do not calculate the Month-2 retention rate for the "Week of Jan 20" cohort until late March. Until then, label these cohorts as "Pending" in reports to prevent artificially depressing rates with incomplete data.

**3. Essential Event Tracking**
Ensure your analytics tool (e.g., GA4, Mixpanel, Segment) captures these specific events with the noted properties to enable this slicing:

| Event Name | Required Properties | Trigger Point |
| :--- | :--- | :--- |
| `signup_completed` | `channel`, `user_id`, `campaign_id` | Final step of registration |
| `preferences_selected` | `category_count`, `time_spent_seconds` | Upon saving preferences |
| `preferences_skipped` | `reason_code` (if offered), `step_duration` | Upon clicking "Skip" |
| `subscription_renewed` | `box_number`, `plan_type`, `revenue` | Successful monthly charge |

**4. Drop-Off Points to Monitor**
Track conversion rates between these specific steps to identify where the new flow succeeds or fails:

1.  **Sign-Up Initiation → Preference Prompt:** Are users seeing the new prompt? (Monitor for technical drop-offs).
2.  **Preference Prompt → Selection vs. Skip:** What is the split between completers and skippers? If skip rates are high (>40%), the UI may be too friction-heavy.
3.  **Skip/Complete → First Box Delivery:** Does selecting preferences impact the success of the first fulfillment cycle?
4.  **Month 1 → Month 2 Renewal:** This is your primary success metric. Compare the renewal delta between Completers and Skippers in the new flow against the historical delta.

**5. Reporting Structure for Budget Allocation**
Present data as a matrix: **Rows** = Acquisition Channels, **Columns** = Cohort Weeks, **Values** = Month-2 Retention % (split by Completer/Skipper).

*   **Success Criteria:** The new flow is successful only if the *weighted average* retention of the post-Jan 20 cohorts exceeds the baseline by 7 percentage points (55% → 62%), driven specifically by an increased lift in the "Completer" segment.
*   **Budget Implication:** If "Paid Social" shows a high completion rate but low retention among completers, reallocate budget to channels where completion correlates strongly with long-term value.

Ensure all rate calculations divide closed-window numerators by closed-window denominators. Never divide full-period spend by partial-period retention results.
