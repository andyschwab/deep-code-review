### Behavioral Cohort Breakdown for AncestryIS

To differentiate between "stuck" and "satisfied" users and identify the highest churn point before June 17th, segment your 12,000 users into four distinct behavioral cohorts based on tree size and activity velocity. This analysis will direct your $8,000 retention budget effectively.

**1. The "Stuck" Cohort (High Intent, High Friction)**
*   **Definition:** Users who initiated a tree, reached 3–9 nodes, and have had **zero activity for >14 days**.
*   **Diagnosis:** These users likely hit a data wall (missing Icelandic records) or a privacy hesitation. They intended to grow but stopped abruptly.
*   **Action:** Target this group with the majority of your budget ($5,000). Deploy a "Record Hint" email campaign showing exactly which ancestor lacks data, or offer a "Privacy Guide" to alleviate concerns.
*   **Metric to Watch:** Reactivation rate after intervention.

**2. The "Satisfied" Cohort (Low Intent, Complete)**
*   **Definition:** Users with ≤10 nodes who have **logged in within the last 30 days** but have not added nodes.
*   **Diagnosis:** These users are not stuck; they are casual users who uploaded a photo or added immediate family and feel their goal is met. Pushing them to build larger trees may cause annoyance.
*   **Action:** Exclude from aggressive tree-building campaigns. Instead, nurture them with content about "Photo Storytelling" to leverage the 35% photo-upload base, aiming for organic premium conversion through engagement rather than tree size.
*   **Metric to Watch:** Photo upload frequency vs. tree growth.

**3. The "Power Builders" (Validation Cohort)**
*   **Definition:** Users with >10 nodes.
*   **Diagnosis:** Use this group to validate your core assumption. Calculate the exact conversion rate to premium for this segment compared to the others.
*   **Action:** Analyze their path. Did they upload photos first? Did they start with a specific ancestor? Replicate this "happy path" in your onboarding for new users.
*   **Metric to Watch:** Premium conversion % and time-to-10-nodes.

**4. The "Drop-off" Point Analysis**
To find the specific step where churn is highest, create a funnel visualization of the tree-building workflow:
*   *Step 1: Start Tree*
*   *Step 2: Add Self*
*   *Step 3: Add Parents*
*   *Step 4: Add Grandparents*
*   *Step 5: Attach First Photo/Document*
*   *Step 6: Reach 10 Nodes*

Calculate the percentage of users who complete each step. The step with the largest percentage decrease is your primary friction point.
*   **If the drop is at Step 3-4:** The issue is likely **lack of historical records**. Users cannot find their parents/grandparents in your database. *Solution:* Invest budget in content acquisition or manual research guides.
*   **If the drop is at Step 5 (Photo/Document):** The issue is likely **privacy concerns** or UI complexity. *Solution:* Invest budget in trust signals, clear privacy policies, or simplifying the upload UI.

**Recommendation for Founder & Content Team:**
Present the "Drop-off" funnel alongside the cohort sizes. If the largest drop coincides with the transition from known family (parents) to historical family (grandparents), the Content Acquisition team must prioritize digitizing specific Icelandic census or church records to bridge that gap. If the drop is earlier, the Product team must address privacy UX. Allocate the remaining $3,000 to A/B test two interventions on the "Stuck" cohort: one offering "Research Help" (addressing data gaps) and one offering "Privacy Control" (addressing fear).

*Note: Ensure all tracking events (e.g., `tree_node_added`, `photo_attached`) are firing correctly before June 1st to capture pre-holiday behavior. Do not merge "satisfied" and "stuck" users in your reporting, as their responses to retention tactics will be opposite.*
