**Interpretation of Current Results**
Your dynamic allocation (likely a Multi-Armed Bandit algorithm) successfully achieved its primary goal: maximizing total clicks during the test window by shifting 60% of traffic to the winner (Creative C). The 8% overall lift confirms the algorithm minimized "regret" effectively. However, this method inherently sacrifices statistical power on underperforming variants. The significant overlap in confidence intervals for Creatives B and D is an expected artifact of the algorithm starving them of impressions once they were deemed inferior early on. You cannot definitively declare them "losers" with statistical rigor, nor can you diagnose *why* they failed (e.g., poor hook vs. weak call-to-action) because the sample size is insufficient for granular segmentation.

Regarding seasonality: Since the test ran entirely within the late December holiday period, the 8% lift is specific to that high-intent context. The absolute performance metrics (CTR baselines) are likely inflated compared to non-holiday periods. However, the *relative* ranking of creatives (C > A/B/D) often holds true unless the holiday messaging specifically favored Creative C's theme. Without a non-holiday control, you cannot isolate the seasonality effect on the magnitude of the lift, only on the baseline volume.

Re-testing it against known underperformers at equal weight would unnecessarily expose 75% of your Q2 traffic to sub-optimal creatives, wasting budget.
