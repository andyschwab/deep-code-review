# Campaign Success Criteria: Balancing Immediate Impact and Long-Term Sustainability

To evaluate the "Clean Water Wells" campaign effectively, SavannaGive must move beyond total dollars raised as the sole metric. While $50,000 in two weeks demonstrates strong initial traction, your hypothesis—that smaller monthly donors yield higher long-term value—requires a multi-dimensional framework. The board should adopt a **Weighted Success Scorecard** that balances three competing priorities: immediate cash flow, donor lifetime value (LTV), and operational efficiency.

### 1. Primary Metric: Recurring Donor Acquisition Rate
Given your strategic assumption, the number of new monthly donors should be the primary success indicator. One-time gifts solve immediate problems, but recurring revenue funds future wells without requiring new acquisition costs every time.
*   **Target Definition:** Set a specific ratio of recurring-to-one-time donors (e.g., 40% of total donors opting for monthly giving).
*   **Trade-off:** Prioritizing this may lower the *average* gift size initially, as monthly donors often start smaller ($10–$25) compared to one-time major donors ($500+). The board must accept lower immediate cash density in exchange for predictable future revenue.

### 2. Secondary Metric: Donor Lifetime Value (LTV) Projection
Total dollars raised today is a lagging indicator; LTV is a leading indicator of sustainability. You must calculate the projected 12-month value of the cohort acquired in February.
*   **Calculation:** (Average Monthly Gift × Expected Retention Months) vs. One-Time Gift.
*   **Trade-off:** Focusing on LTV requires patience. A campaign yielding fewer total dollars in Week 2 but higher projected LTV is strategically superior for long-term growth, even if it strains short-term liquidity.

### 3. Guardrail Metric: Six-Month Retention Rate
Acquiring monthly donors is futile if they churn quickly. Retention acts as a quality control guardrail.
*   **Target Definition:** Establish a minimum retention benchmark (e.g., 70% of new monthly donors still giving in Month 6).
*   **Trade-off:** Aggressive acquisition tactics (e.g., low-barrier entry points) might boost sign-ups but attract lower-quality donors who churn faster. The campaign must balance ease of signup with donor education to ensure commitment.

### Balancing Cash Flow vs. Sustainability
The tension between immediate needs and long-term goals is resolved by setting **thresholds** rather than choosing one over the other:
1.  **Liquidity Floor:** Determine the minimum one-time revenue required to fund the wells promised in the campaign marketing. If the $50,000 raised covers the immediate well construction costs, the campaign has met its liquidity obligation. Any optimization beyond this point should favor recurring donors.
2.  **Sustainability Ceiling:** If recurring sign-ups exceed targets but one-time revenue falls below the liquidity floor, the campaign risks failing its immediate promise. In this scenario, pivot messaging to encourage larger one-time "bridge" gifts while maintaining the monthly option.

### Recommendation for the Board
Approve a success definition where the campaign is deemed a "Strategic Win" if:
*   Immediate funds cover 100% of promised well construction costs (Cash Flow secured).
*   Recurring donor acquisition exceeds [X]% of total donor count (Sustainability secured).
*   Projected LTV of the new cohort exceeds the cost of acquisition by 3x within 12 months.

This approach validates your hypothesis without jeopardizing current operational obligations. It shifts the narrative from "how much did we raise?" to "how sustainable is our growth?"

*Note: Ensure your analytics tracking distinguishes between one-time and recurring transaction events to measure these metrics accurately.*
*Data for the six-month retention guardrail will not be available until August; use early churn signals (e.g., failed second payments) as a leading proxy.*
