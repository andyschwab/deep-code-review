### Cohort Segmentation and Comparative Analysis Plan

To isolate the behavioral differences driven by the new cat food line and validate your multi-pet hypothesis, structure your data using the following grouping logic and comparative metrics.

#### 1. Data Grouping Logic
Do not analyze the January cohort in isolation. You must construct a parallel control group to distinguish seasonality from product impact.

*   **Primary Segments (Within Each Cohort):**
    *   **Pet Type:** Dog-only, Cat-only, Multi-pet (mixed or same species).
    *   **Acquisition Source:** Organic, Paid Social, Referral (to control for marketing quality differences between Dec and Jan).
*   **Cohort Definitions:**
    *   **Treatment Cohort:** Subscribers acquired Jan 5 – Jan 31 (exposed to new cat line).
    *   **Control Cohort:** Subscribers acquired Dec 1 – Dec 31 (pre-launch baseline).
*   **Time Horizon:**
    *   Align both cohorts by "Subscription Age" (e.g., Month 1, Month 2, Month 3) rather than calendar dates. This ensures you are comparing the third delivery experience for both groups simultaneously relative to their start date.

#### 2. Comparative Metrics
Calculate these specific rates for each segment. Ensure numerators and denominators cover the exact same observation window (e.g., only include subscribers whose third delivery date has passed).

*   **Churn Rate at Delivery 3:**
    *   *Formula:* (Cancellations occurring immediately after Delivery 3) / (Total subscribers who received Delivery 3).
    *   *Comparison:* Compare Jan Cat-only vs. Dec Cat-only. A statistically significant increase in Jan indicates the new product line may be failing retention expectations.
*   **Multi-Pet Retention Premium:**
    *   *Formula:* (Retention Rate of Multi-pet) – (Retention Rate of Single-pet).
    *   *Comparison:* Calculate this delta for both December and January. If the delta widens in January, the new line successfully attracts higher-value, stickier customers.
*   **Revenue Per Active Subscriber (RPAS) at Month 3:**
    *   *Formula:* Total Revenue from Month 3 deliveries / Active subscribers in Month 3.
    *   *Purpose:* Measures the value of the surviving base, accounting for both order size and attrition.

#### 3. Interpreting the "High Spend, Fast Churn" Scenario
If your data shows Dog Owners cancel faster than Cat Owners but generate higher revenue per order, interpret this through the lens of **inventory velocity vs. customer lifetime value (LTV)** for your supply chain manager:

*   **The Dynamic:** Dog owners likely purchase larger volume orders (higher immediate revenue) but exhibit lower loyalty or higher price sensitivity, leading to earlier churn. Cat owners purchase smaller amounts but remain subscribed longer.
*   **Supply Chain Implication:**
    *   **Inventory Risk:** High churn among high-spenders creates "lumpy" demand. You cannot rely on a stable recurring base for dog food; inventory planning must be more agile to avoid overstocking if acquisition slows.
    *   **Cash Flow vs. Stability:** Dog food drives short-term cash flow (large upfront orders), while cat food provides predictable baseline volume.
    *   **Forecasting Strategy:** Do not forecast dog food inventory based on average subscriber lifespan. Instead, model inventory needs based on *acquisition pipelines* (new inflow) rather than *retention bases* (existing stock). For cat food, forecast based on the stable, recurring cohort.

#### 4. Strategic Recommendation for Inventory Forecasting
For the upcoming quarter, segment your forecast into two distinct models:
1.  **Dog Food (Acquisition-Dependent):** Tie inventory purchases directly to forward-looking marketing spend and conversion rates, not historical retention. Assume a shorter lifecycle.
2.  **Cat/Multi-pet Food (Retention-Dependent):** Tie inventory to the existing active subscriber count multiplied by the stable retention rate.

This approach prevents over-ordering dog food based on false retention assumptions and under-ordering cat food by overlooking its compounding stability.
