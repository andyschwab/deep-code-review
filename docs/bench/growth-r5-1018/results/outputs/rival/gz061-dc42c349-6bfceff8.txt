### Metrics to Validate LTV Model Against Cash Flow

To validate your assumption that Month 3+ retention drives 5x revenue, you must calculate the following metrics using row-level transaction data. Do not rely on dashboard averages; compute these directly from your database or export.

**1. Cohort-Specific Cash Flow Curve**
Group users by their signup month (March cohort). For each subsequent month (Month 1, 2, 3...), calculate:
*   **Gross Cash In:** Total subscription fees collected.
*   **Direct Cash Out:** Cost of goods sold (COGS) + shipping + *actual* referral bonus payouts redeemed in that specific month.
*   **Net Cash Flow:** Gross In minus Direct Out.

*Crucial Logic:* Your current model likely treats the referral bonus as a fixed acquisition cost. However, if the bonus is included in the first box, it impacts Month 1 cash flow immediately. You must separate the *liability* of the bonus (when offered) from the *cash event* (when redeemed/fulfilled). If the bonus is a discount on the first box, reduce Month 1 Gross Cash In. If it is a credit for future use, track it as a liability until redemption.

**2. Actual vs. Modeled LTV Ratio**
Calculate the cumulative Net Cash Flow per user for two distinct groups:
*   **Group A (Churned < Month 3):** Sum net cash flow from Month 1 to churn date.
*   **Group B (Retained > Month 3):** Sum net cash flow from Month 1 to current date (or churn).

Divide the average cumulative cash flow of Group B by Group A. If the result is not ≥ 5.0, your assumption is invalid.

**3. Referral Bonus Redemption Rate**
Track the percentage of users who actually utilize the referral bonus within the first 90 days.
*   *Formula:* (Users who redeemed bonus / Users who received bonus offer).
*   If this rate is low, your cash flow impact is lower than assumed, potentially inflating the perceived cost of acquisition.

### Interpreting a 10% Drop in Average Order Value (AOV) in Month 2

If AOV drops 10% in Month 2, do not simply average this across the whole cohort. You must segment the data to determine the cause before presenting to the CFO.

**Scenario A: Downgrade Behavior (Volume Constant, Revenue Down)**
If the number of active subscribers remains stable but revenue drops, customers are likely switching to lower-tier plans or removing add-ons.
*   *Implication:* The "Premium" value proposition is weak after the initial novelty.
*   *LTV Impact:* Recalculate LTV using the *new* lower monthly recurring revenue (MRR). If the 5x ratio disappears under this new MRR, the premium tier is unsustainable without intervention.

**Scenario B: Selective Churn (High-Value Users Leave)**
If the drop in AOV is caused by your highest-spending users churning after Month 1, leaving only lower-spenders.
*   *Implication:* Your product satisfies casual users but fails power users.
*   *LTV Impact:* This is critical. Your "Month 3+" group now consists entirely of lower-value users. The 5x revenue assumption is likely false because the high-value cohort already exited.

**Scenario C: Promo/Discount Dilution**
If Month 1 included a "first box" premium item that inflated AOV artificially, and Month 2 reflects the true steady-state price.
*   *Implication:* Your Month 1 cash flow was anomalous.
*   *LTV Impact:* Restate the model using Month 2 AOV as the baseline. The "5x" claim must be re-tested against this lower baseline. If the ratio still holds, the model is robust. If not, the initial assumption relied on unsustainable Month 1 pricing.

**Recommendation for CFO:**
Present the **Net Cash Flow per Cohort** rather than projected LTV. Show the actual cash burn or profit for the March cohort at Month 1, 2, and 3. If the 10% AOV drop eliminates the path to positive cash flow by Month 4, recommend pausing Q3 acquisition spend until retention or pricing is stabilized.
