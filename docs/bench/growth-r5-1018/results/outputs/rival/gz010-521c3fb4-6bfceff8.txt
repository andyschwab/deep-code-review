# Diagnosis Plan: CloudScale Pricing Page Conversion Drop

**Objective:** Isolate whether the conversion drop (4.5% → 2.8%) is driven by **traffic quality dilution** or **page design friction** using existing aggregate data and survey responses.

### Step 1: Segment Traffic Quality (The "Who" Analysis)
Before blaming the redesign, determine if the 25% traffic increase consists of lower-intent users.
*   **Action:** Break down conversion rates by `utm_source` and `utm_medium` for the periods before (Oct 1–19) and after (Oct 20–Present) the launch.
*   **Hypothesis A (Traffic Dilution):** If conversion rates *within* specific channels (e.g., `google/cpc`, `linkedin/organic`) remain stable near 4.5%, but the *mix* shifted toward lower-converting channels (e.g., broad display ads, social awareness campaigns), the issue is traffic quality, not the page.
*   **Hypothesis B (Universal Drop):** If conversion rates dropped across *all* major channels proportionally, the issue is likely the page design affecting all users equally.

### Step 2: Analyze Funnel Drop-off Points (The "Where" Analysis)
Since session recordings are unavailable, use event logs to pinpoint where users abandon the flow.
*   **Action:** Compare the drop-off rates between these specific steps pre- and post-launch:
    1.  `pricing_page_view` → `feature_table_expand` (or click on comparison details)
    2.  `pricing_page_view` → `select_plan_clicked`
    3.  `select_plan_clicked` → `checkout_started`
*   **Hypothesis C (Confusion):** If `select_plan_clicked` dropped significantly but `checkout_started` (once clicked) remains stable, users are confused by the new layout/feature table and failing to pick a plan.
*   **Hypothesis D (Friction):** If `select_plan_clicked` is stable but `checkout_started` dropped, the issue lies in the post-selection flow (e.g., login prompts, payment form), not the pricing display.

### Step 3: Mine Survey Responses (The "Why" Analysis)
Qualitative data must be quantified to support the VP's decision.
*   **Action:** Filter survey responses containing keywords: "confusing," "compare," "features," "Pro," and "pricing" from Oct 20 onward.
*   **Metric:** Calculate the % of negative sentiment surveys mentioning the "comparison table" or "Pro plan" specifically.
*   **Validation:** If >15% of drop-off surveys cite confusion about features or difficulty comparing plans, this validates the design friction hypothesis.

### Step 4: Synthesize Findings & Recommend Action
Map your findings to one of these three scenarios for the Wednesday presentation:

| Scenario | Evidence Pattern | Recommended Action |
| :--- | :--- | :--- |
| **1. Traffic Dilution** | Channel-specific CRs stable; overall drop due to low-intent source mix. | **Marketing Fix:** Pause low-quality ad sets; refine targeting. Do not change the page. |
| **2. Design Confusion** | Universal CR drop; high drop-off at `select_plan_clicked`; surveys cite confusion. | **Design Fix:** A/B test a simplified comparison table or revert the "Pro" highlight immediately. |
| **3. Checkout Friction** | Plan selection stable; drop-off at `checkout_started`. | **Tech Fix:** Investigate login/payment bugs or form length; unrelated to pricing layout. |
