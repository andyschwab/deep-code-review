**Recommendation: Adopt Monthly Recurring Revenue (MRR) as your sole North Star Metric.**

For a Series B raise requiring proof of 20% quarter-over-quarter growth, MRR is the only metric that directly aligns operational behavior with your immediate survival and funding objectives. While the decline in Net Promoter Score (NPS) from 60 to 45 is a critical risk, elevating NPS to the North Star creates a misalignment between your daily activities and the specific financial thresholds investors will scrutinize.

**The Investor Narrative**
Investors at the Series B stage prioritize scalability and predictable revenue trajectories. Your deck must demonstrate a clear path to hitting the 20% QoQ growth target. If you designate NPS as the North Star, your internal narrative shifts toward satisfaction initiatives, which often have lagging and non-linear impacts on revenue. By choosing MRR, every team—from product to sales—optimizes for actions that directly influence the top-line number investors care about most. You can still present the NPS data in the deck as a "risk mitigation" slide, showing you are aware of the churn correlation, but the strategic compass must point to revenue growth to secure the valuation you need.

**Operational Focus and Behavior**
Choosing MRR forces the organization to solve the revenue equation directly: Acquisition + Expansion – Churn. The data you provided (95% renewal for NPS >50 vs. 70% for NPS <50) does not argue for NPS as the leader; rather, it defines a specific financial constraint within an MRR framework.

If you choose NPS as the North Star, teams may prioritize delight features or support overreach that improves sentiment but fails to move the revenue needle quickly enough for your Series B timeline. The translation time from "happier customers" to "renewed contracts" is too uncertain for your current runway.

Conversely, an MRR North Star allows you to treat the NPS drop as a churn problem, which is a mathematical component of MRR. You can set aggressive MRR targets that implicitly require fixing the retention leak. For example, if your growth target is 20%, and your current churn (driven by low NPS) caps you at 12%, the MRR metric forces an immediate, quantifiable intervention to stop the bleeding. This creates urgency without losing focus on the financial outcome.

**Strategic Implication**
Prioritizing MRR acknowledges that customer satisfaction is a *means* to revenue stability, not the end goal itself. Your founding team should frame the NPS slip not as a reason to change the North Star, but as the primary obstacle preventing the MRR North Star from reaching its full potential. This keeps the focus on the financial result while directing resources to fix the specific retention issues causing the NPS decline.

Do not split focus. In a fundraising crunch, clarity of purpose wins. MRR provides the only direct line of sight to the Series B check.
