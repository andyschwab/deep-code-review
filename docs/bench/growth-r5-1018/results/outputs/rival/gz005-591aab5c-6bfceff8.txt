# Search Ranking Impact Analysis Plan

**Objective:** Determine if the 8% CTR gain justifies the 15% Average Order Value (AOV) drop by isolating whether users are buying cheaper books or if premium titles are being suppressed.

### Step 1: Segment Performance by Price Tier (Immediate)
Break down search results and conversions into three price buckets: Low (<$10), Medium ($10–$25), and High (>$25). Compare pre-launch vs. post-launch metrics for each bucket:
*   **Impression Share:** Are high-priced books receiving significantly fewer impressions?
*   **CTR by Tier:** Did CTR increase only for low-priced books, or across all tiers?
*   **Conversion Rate by Tier:** Did conversion rates improve for cheap books enough to offset the volume loss of expensive ones?

*Hypothesis Check:* If high-priced book impressions dropped >20% while their CTR remained flat, the algorithm is burying premium inventory. If low-priced book CTR spiked 30%+ with stable conversion, users are simply finding affordable options faster.

### Step 2: Calculate Net Revenue Per Search (RPS)
Do not evaluate CTR and AOV in isolation. Compute the single metric that matters for the VP's decision: **Revenue Per Search**.
*   *Formula:* `Total Search-Attributed Revenue / Total Search Sessions`
*   Compare RPS pre-launch vs. post-launch.
*   **Decision Rule:** If RPS is down by more than 5%, the engagement gain is vanity; the algorithm is harming profitability. If RPS is flat or up, the lower AOV is compensated by higher volume, and the change may be beneficial long-term.

### Step 3: Analyze "Position vs. Price" Correlation
Sample the top 20 search results for your top 50 highest-volume queries (e.g., "bestseller," "fiction," specific genres).
*   Map the average price and average rating of results in positions 1–5 vs. positions 6–20.
*   **Diagnostic:** If positions 1–5 now average $12 (previously $18) and positions 6–20 hold the $25+ books, confirm if the conversion rate for those buried premium books has collapsed. Users rarely scroll past position 5; if premium books are pushed down, their sales will vanish regardless of quality.

### Step 4: Check Basket Composition
Analyze orders originating from search to see if basket size (items per order) changed.
*   **Scenario A:** Users are buying *more* cheap books per order (basket size up, AOV down). This indicates successful discovery of affordable series/bundles.
*   **Scenario B:** Users are buying *fewer* items total (basket size flat/down, AOV down). This suggests the algorithm is failing to surface premium add-ons or gift-worthy titles.

### Recommendation Framework for VP
Present the findings using this matrix to drive the rollback decision:

| Metric Trend | Interpretation | Recommendation |
| :--- | :--- | :--- |
| **RPS Down >5%** | Engagement is misleading; revenue loss is real. | **Rollback Immediately.** Do not risk weekend peak. |
| **RPS Flat/Up** | Users buy more cheap books; volume offsets price drop. | **Keep Live.** Monitor margin impact; consider tweaking rank to re-introduce some premium titles. |
| **Premium Impressions -20%+** | Algorithm aggressively suppresses high-price items. | **Hybrid Approach:** Adjust weighting to ensure at least 2 premium titles appear in top 5 results, then monitor. |

**Immediate Action:** Run the RPS calculation first. If revenue per search is materially negative, recommend an immediate rollback before the weekend traffic surge amplifies the loss. If RPS is stable, propose a "tuned" version that balances rating priority with a price floor for the top 5 slots.

*Note: This analysis relies solely on existing quantitative logs; no user surveys are required.*
