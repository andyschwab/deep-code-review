**Recommended North Star Metric: Weekly Successful Matches**

For BookSwap PL, your North Star Metric should be **Weekly Successful Matches** (defined as a buyer and seller agreeing to exchange, verified by chat initiation or contact detail swap).

While "Total Listings Created" measures supply effort, it is a vanity metric in this context. Optimizing for raw listings encourages spam, duplicate posts, and irrelevant books that clutter the marketplace, directly harming the buyer experience and reducing actual liquidity. In a two-sided marketplace, liquidity is not just about volume; it is about the probability of a transaction occurring. A marketplace with 10,000 low-quality listings that yield zero trades is less liquid than one with 2,000 high-intent listings that yield 500 trades.

**Evaluation of Potential Metrics**

| Metric | Pros | Cons | Verdict |
| :--- | :--- | :--- | :--- |
| **Total Listings Created** | Easy to track; encourages seller activity. | Incentivizes spam/low quality; ignores demand; does not correlate to revenue (take rate). | **Reject** |
| **Search Volume** | Indicates demand interest. | Can be high even if inventory is poor; doesn't measure outcome. | **Secondary Only** |
| **Weekly Successful Matches** | Directly correlates to revenue (12% take rate); balances buyer/seller needs; measures actual liquidity. | Requires robust tracking of the "match" event. | **Adopt** |

**Trade-offs and Behavior Influence**

Choosing "Successful Matches" aligns the homepage redesign toward **conversion efficiency** rather than just **content volume**.

1.  **Seller Behavior:** Instead of incentivizing users to dump every book they own onto the platform, this metric pushes the design to guide sellers toward listing *in-demand* titles. The homepage should highlight "Books Needed by Students" rather than just "Recent Uploads." This reduces low-quality posts because sellers realize that obscure books won't generate matches.
2.  **Buyer Behavior:** The metric forces the product team to prioritize search relevance and filtering. If buyers can't find books quickly, matches drop. This ensures the homepage serves as a functional discovery engine, not just a bulletin board.
3.  **The Liquidity Balance:** By focusing on the *connection* rather than the *listing*, you optimize for the health of the ecosystem. High match rates signal to sellers that the platform works, organically driving higher quality supply over time without needing to game the system with spam.

**Preventing Spam and Low-Quality Posts**

To ensure this metric does not inadvertently encourage gaming (e.g., users fake-matching to boost numbers), implement these guardrails in your definition and design:

*   **Strict Definition of "Match":** Do not count a match until a specific high-friction action occurs, such as the buyer clicking "Reveal Contact Info" or sending the first message in the internal chat. This prevents automated scripts from inflating numbers.
*   **Quality Gates for Sellers:** Redesign the listing flow to require specific metadata (ISBN, condition, course code) before a book goes live. This increases the effort to list, naturally filtering out low-effort spam, while ensuring high-quality listings have a higher probability of matching.
*   **Monitor the Ratio:** Track the **Match Rate** (Matches / Total Listings) as a health metric. If total listings spike but matches stay flat, your quality controls are failing, and you can intervene before the user experience degrades.

**Presentation Strategy for Partners**

When presenting to university partners, frame "Weekly Successful Matches" as **Student Success**. Explain that your goal isn't just to host ads for books, but to ensure students actually *get* the materials they need before February 10th. This metric proves you are solving the accessibility problem, not just hosting a database.

*Note: Ensure your analytics team can track the specific "match" event before the February 10th launch.*
*Note: Consider adding a secondary guardrail metric for "Listing Quality Score" if spam becomes an issue post-launch.*
