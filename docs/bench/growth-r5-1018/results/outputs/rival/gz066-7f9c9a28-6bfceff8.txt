### Retention Window Framework for RioNews

To align content strategy with user habits and accurately measure the impact of your push notification changes, you must move from a single "retention rate" to a **Multi-Horizon Stickiness Model**. News consumption is habitual but episodic; therefore, different time windows answer different strategic questions.

**1. The Pulse Check (24-Hour Window)**
*   **Definition:** Percentage of users who open the app within 24 hours of a specific trigger (e.g., the 7 AM push).
*   **Purpose:** Measures immediate reaction to breaking news and push notification efficacy.
*   **Application:** This is your primary metric for evaluating the March 10th schedule change. The drop from 40% to 28% indicates the new timing or content mix fails to interrupt the morning commute routine.
*   **Verdict:** Keep this window to optimize send-times, but do not use it as the sole definition of long-term health.

**2. The Habit Loop (7-Day Rolling Window)**
*   **Definition:** Percentage of users who open the app at least once in any 7-day period.
*   **Purpose:** Measures weekly habit formation. For news, a user who reads deeply on Monday and Saturday is highly valuable, even if they ignore mid-week pushes.
*   **Application:** This solves your "weekend user" dilemma. If a user returns only on weekends, they are **retained** in the 7-day window but **churned** in the 24-hour window. This segment represents "Deep Divers" rather than "Headline Scanners."
*   **Verdict:** Adopt this as your primary definition of "Active Retained User" for editorial planning. It captures the full news cycle without penalizing users who binge content.

**3. The Lifecycle View (28-Day / 4-Week Window)**
*   **Definition:** Percentage of users returning at least once over four weeks.
*   **Purpose:** Identifies true churn vs. natural lulls. In news, users may travel or have busy work weeks.
*   **Application:** Use this to distinguish between a failed notification strategy (short-term drop) and a fundamental product disconnect (long-term decline).

**Logic for Boundary Decisions**

To decide if a specific user is "retained," apply the **Intent-Based Classification**:

*   **Class A: Daily Commuters (High Frequency)**
    *   *Behavior:* Opens app 5+ days/week, primarily mornings.
    *   *Retention Logic:* Must satisfy the **24-Hour Window** at least 4 times in 7 days.
    *   *Strategy:* The 7 AM push is critical here. The current drop suggests you are losing this cohort.

*   **Class B: Weekend Analysts (Low Frequency, High Value)**
    *   *Behavior:* Opens app 1-2 times/week, longer session duration.
    *   *Retention Logic:* Satisfies the **7-Day Window** consistently, even if 24-hour metrics are zero for 5 days.
    *   *Strategy:* Do not count these users as "lost" due to the 7 AM push failure. They are retained. Aggressive daily pushing may actually annoy them.

*   **Class C: Event-Driven (Sporadic)**
    *   *Behavior:* Returns only for major events (elections, crises).
    *   *Retention Logic:* Measured only on the **28-Day Window**.
    *   *Strategy:* Irrelevant for daily push optimization; relevant for server capacity and breaking news protocols.

**Recommendation for the Editorial Director**

Present retention as a layered metric: "Our **Daily Stickiness** (24h) has dropped, indicating the 7 AM slot is missing the commute window. However, our **Weekly Stickiness** (7d) will reveal if we are merely shifting *when* users read, or if we are losing them entirely. A user returning only on weekends is retained for our long-term ad revenue but is currently invisible to our morning push strategy. We should A/B test a secondary push at 8:30 AM or 12:30 PM to recapture the Daily Commuters without alienating the Weekend Analysts."

*Note: Calculate the 7-day metric immediately to see if the 12% drop in daily returns is offset by increased weekend engagement. If both drop, the issue is content relevance, not just timing.*
