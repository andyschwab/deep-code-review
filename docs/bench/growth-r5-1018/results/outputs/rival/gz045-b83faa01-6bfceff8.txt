**Analysis of FitTrack Day-30 Retention Cliff**

The divergence between improved Day-1 retention (45% → 55%) and degraded Day-30 retention (25% → 18%) following the February 1 onboarding update indicates a "false start" phenomenon. The new flow successfully lowers the barrier to entry but fails to establish the long-term habits required to sustain usage beyond the initial novelty window. The sharp drop-off after three weeks of high activity (4 workouts/week) suggests the value proposition is front-loaded rather than compounding.

Based on the engagement pattern described, three primary structural causes are likely:

1.  **Content Exhaustion vs. Habit Formation:** The new onboarding may front-load the most engaging content or easiest workouts. Users consume this "low-hanging fruit" over 21 days, hitting a content wall just as the novelty fades. If the app has not transitioned the user from "consuming curated content" to "building a personal routine" by week four, disengagement is inevitable.
2.  **The "Scaffold Removal" Effect:** The engaging new onboarding might act as a heavy scaffold (e.g., daily guided prompts, high-touch notifications) that is abruptly removed or significantly reduced after the initial setup phase. Users become dependent on this external structure; when it recedes to a standard cadence in week four, they lack the internal trigger to continue.
3.  **Misaligned Success Metrics:** The new flow may optimize for "setup completion" rather than "second-week activation." By making the initial setup too comprehensive or time-consuming, you may be attracting users who feel they have already "done the work" of getting fit by completing the profile, leading to psychological closure rather than opening.

**Critical Data Points for Root Cause Confirmation**

To isolate the specific driver before the roadmap review, investigate the following data intersections:

*   **Cohort Content Depth Analysis:** Compare the variety of workouts accessed by the Feb 1+ cohort vs. pre-Feb 1 cohorts at Day 10, Day 20, and Day 30. If the new cohort shows a sharp decline in *unique* workout types accessed after week three while the old cohort maintained variety, content exhaustion is the primary driver.
*   **Feature Dependency Correlation:** Isolate users who engaged with specific "new onboarding" features (e.g., goal setting wizards, introductory challenges). Correlate their Day-30 retention against those who skipped them. If high engagement with the *new* flow correlates with *lower* Day-30 retention, the flow itself is creating a dependency trap.
*   **The "Week 4 Trigger" Audit:** Analyze the drop in push notification open rates and session initiation sources between Week 3 and Week 4. If external triggers (push/email) drop off while the user is still active, the app is failing to hand off agency to the user. If external triggers remain constant but sessions drop, the content itself is no longer compelling.
*   **Social Graph Maturity:** Segment the churned users by whether they connected a friend or joined a challenge in Week 1. If users without social connections comprise 80%+ of the Day-30 churn, the lack of social stickiness is the gap; if social users are also churning at 18%, the issue is core content or utility, not community.
*   **Completion Bias Check:** Measure the percentage of users who hit "100% profile complete" or finished the "introductory challenge" in the new flow. If this group has a statistically lower Day-30 retention than those who left the profile partially complete, the onboarding is inadvertently signaling that the "work" is finished.

**Note:** Ensure all rate metrics compare closed observation windows (e.g., Feb 1 cohort at Day 30 vs. Jan 1 cohort at Day 30) to avoid skewing data with partial periods. Do not aggregate active users with churned users when analyzing feature usage; the behavior of the churned cohort specifically holds the answer.
