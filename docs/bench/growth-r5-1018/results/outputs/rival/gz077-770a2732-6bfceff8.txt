### Success Framework for SafeHaiti Connect: Outcome Over Activity

Given the constraints of intermittent connectivity in rural Haiti, success must be redefined from "digital engagement" to "operational continuity." The following three definitions prioritize field coordination outcomes over app interaction metrics, providing your funding partners with a realistic viability assessment.

**1. The "Last-Mile Confirmation" Rate**
*Definition:* The percentage of registered volunteers who physically arrive at their assigned location and initiate a task, regardless of whether they digitally confirmed receipt of the assignment beforehand.
*Rationale:* This metric decouples success from the unreliable "message received" signal. In this model, the act of showing up and starting work serves as the ultimate confirmation. If a volunteer arrives without having clicked "confirm," the system records the task as "accepted via action" rather than "ignored." This shifts the success criterion from network-dependent acknowledgment to physical presence, validating that the coordination logic holds even when the communication layer fails.

**2. The "Offline-to-Online" Sync Efficiency**
*Definition:* The time delta between a volunteer completing a field action (e.g., distributing supplies, assessing damage) and the moment that data successfully syncs to the central dashboard once connectivity is restored.
*Rationale:* In intermittent networks, immediate data transmission is an unrealistic success metric. Instead, success is defined by the system's ability to queue actions locally and synchronize them accurately when a signal becomes available. A short sync window indicates the tool effectively bridges the connectivity gap, allowing coordinators to see a near-real-time picture of operations despite infrastructure limitations. This measures the resilience of the data architecture rather than the stability of the cellular network.

**3. The "Task Completion Ratio" (Assigned vs. Executed)**
*Definition:* The ratio of tasks marked as "completed in the field" to total tasks "assigned by coordinators," measured over a fixed operational window (e.g., 24 hours post-disaster).
*Rationale:* This outcome-based metric bypasses the ambiguity of the "450 confirmations" entirely. It focuses strictly on the throughput of the response effort. If 1,200 volunteers are assigned tasks and 1,000 are eventually marked complete (whether confirmed digitally or verified later), the system is successful. A low ratio indicates a breakdown in coordination logic or resource allocation, whereas a high ratio proves the tool facilitates effective response even if the intermediate "receipt" step is opaque. This directly answers the funders' question about real-world viability: did the work get done?
