### Executive Summary: Do Not Roll Out Yet
The aggregate 10% reduction in cancellations is masking a critical failure among your highest-value segment (users >18 months). While the new flow successfully retains price-sensitive new users, it is actively alienating loyal subscribers. Rolling this out to 100% of users risks increasing long-term churn and reducing Lifetime Value (LTV), even if short-term cancellation volume drops.

### Interpreting the Distributional Shift
This is a classic case of **segmentation bias** where an aggregate win hides a localized loss. The data suggests two distinct behavioral responses:

1.  **New Users (<18 months):** The "discounted pause" acts as a effective safety net. These users are likely price-sensitive or uncertain about value; the offer successfully converts a hard "cancel" into a "pause," driving the aggregate win.
2.  **Loyal Users (>18 months):** The 10% *increase* in cancellation for this group indicates the intervention is backfiring. Your hypothesis that they feel "insulted" is plausible. Long-term subscribers often view themselves as valued partners; presenting them with a discount typically reserved for at-risk churners can signal that the company undervalues their loyalty or assumes they are about to leave. Alternatively, the "extra step" friction may disproportionately annoy users who expect a seamless exit due to their tenure.

### Recommended Immediate Actions

**1. Halt the Rollout**
Do not expose the remaining 90% of users to this flow. The risk of accelerating churn among your most profitable cohort outweighs the gains from new user retention.

**2. Isolate the Mechanism (Qualitative & Quantitative)**
Before reverting completely, run a rapid diagnostic to distinguish between "Insult" vs. "Friction":
*   **Exit Survey:** Add a single optional question to the cancellation flow for the treatment group: *"What influenced your decision today?"* with options including "Offer felt irrelevant," "Process was too long," or "Just wanted to cancel."
*   **Time-on-Page Analysis:** Check if loyal users in the treatment group spent significantly less time on the offer page than new users. If they bounced faster, it suggests immediate rejection (insult/confusion) rather than frustration with length.

**3. Strategic Segmentation (The Path Forward)**
The solution is likely not a binary "revert all" or "launch all," but a **targeted rollout**:
*   **Hypothesis:** The pause offer should only be shown to users with tenure <12 months.
*   **Next Test:** Design an A/B test where the treatment logic includes a tenure gate.
    *   *Variant A (Tenure <12mo):* See discounted pause offer.
    *   *Variant B (Tenure >12mo):* See a "We're sorry to see you go" message with a direct cancellation path or a high-touch retention offer (e.g., "Talk to us" or "Switch to annual") rather than a discount.

### Decision Framework for the Retention Lead
*   **If LTV of >18mo users is >3x that of <6mo users:** Revert immediately for the long-tail segment. The aggregate 10% win is mathematically misleading if it trades low-value retention for high-value churn.
*   **If the increase in long-term churn is statistically significant (p<0.05):** Treat this as a critical bug in the user experience for loyalists, not a neutral result.

**Conclusion:** The aggregate metric is lying to you. You have a winning tactic for new users and a losing tactic for loyalists. Segment the experience by tenure immediately.

*Note: Ensure your sample size for the >18-month segment was sufficient to declare statistical significance before acting on the negative trend. If the segment size was small, treat this as a strong warning signal requiring a dedicated follow-up test rather than definitive proof of harm.*
