**Experiment Summary: Primary CTA Color Scheme (Blue vs. Green)**
**Date:** August 24, 2024
**Audience:** Chief Product Officer
**Status:** Inconclusive – Recommendation to Iterate

**Overview**
From August 10–24, we A/B tested a new blue primary CTA against the existing green across 10,000 iOS users. The hypothesis was that a vibrant blue would improve visual hierarchy and drive end-to-end conversion.

**Key Findings**
*   **Engagement Lift:** The blue variant increased button clicks by **4%**.
*   **Conversion Drop:** Subsequent form completion rates decreased by **2%**.
*   **Net Impact:** The disconnect suggests the color change successfully attracted attention but failed to signal intent, potentially increasing "curiosity clicks" from users not ready to convert.

**Analysis of Drop-off**
The 2% decline in form completion raises two possibilities:
1.  **Design-Induced Anxiety:** The blue hue may clash with our trust-based branding, causing hesitation at the form stage.
2.  **Statistical Noise:** With 10,000 users, the sample size may be insufficient to confirm if a 2% drop is significant or random variance.

**Recommendation: Do Not Roll Out; Iterate Instead**
We recommend **against** a full rollout of the current blue design and **against** an immediate revert to green without further learning. A binary go/no-go now risks either deploying a leaky funnel or discarding a valid engagement lever.

**Next Steps**
1.  **Run a Follow-Up Test (Priority):** Launch a second iteration testing a modified blue (higher contrast or adjusted saturation) to isolate whether the specific shade causes anxiety.
2.  **Qualitative Validation:** Deploy a micro-survey on the form page for the blue variant to ask users if they experienced hesitation or confusion.
3.  **Extend Quantitative Analysis:** If resources allow, extend the current test duration by one week to increase sample size and confirm statistical significance on the completion metric.

**Decision Required**
Approval to proceed with **Iteration Plan #1** (Modified Blue Test) for the next two-week sprint.

***

*Note: Ensure the next test pre-calculates the required sample size to detect a 2% change with 95% confidence to avoid ambiguity.*
