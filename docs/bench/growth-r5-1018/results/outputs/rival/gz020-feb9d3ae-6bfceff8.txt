### Analytical Approach: Free Shipping Threshold Evaluation

To determine the net financial impact and separate signal from seasonal noise, execute the following three-step analysis using your existing order-level data. Do not rely on aggregate averages alone.

**1. Isolate the Treatment Effect (Signal vs. Noise)**
Since you cannot re-run the A/B test, use a **Difference-in-Differences (DiD)** approach to remove seasonal noise.
*   **Test Group:** Orders during the two-week test window in October.
*   **Control Group:** The identical two-week period in the previous year (October 2023) to account for seasonality, OR a concurrent holdout group if one exists (e.g., a specific geographic region or traffic source where the threshold remained $50).
*   **Calculation:** Compare the *change* in conversion rate and AOV in the test period against the *change* in the control period.
    *   *Formula:* $(Metric_{Test, Current} - Metric_{Test, Prior}) - (Metric_{Control, Current} - Metric_{Control, Prior})$
*   **Behavioral Check:** Segment users by cart size pre-threshold ($50–$64). If conversion dropped primarily here, the friction is real. If conversion held steady for carts >$65, the "adjustment period" theory is weak; customers simply either add items or abandon.

**2. Calculate Contribution Margin per Session (The Decision Metric)**
Stop evaluating AOV and Conversion Rate separately. The only metric that matters is **Profit per Session**.
*   **Define Variables:**
    *   $P$: Average Product Margin (%)
    *   $S$: Average Shipping Cost per Order
    *   $CR$: Conversion Rate
    *   $AOV$: Average Order Value
*   **Formula:** $Profit per Session = CR \times [ (AOV \times P) - (Shipping Cost Incurred) ]$
*   **Comparison:**
    *   *Scenario A ($50 Threshold):* Use baseline CR and AOV. Calculate shipping cost incurred on all orders >$50.
    *   *Scenario B ($65 Threshold):* Use test CR and AOV. Calculate shipping cost incurred on all orders >$65.
*   **Break-Even Analysis:** Determine the exact conversion rate drop required to negate the margin gain from reduced shipping subsidies and higher AOV. If your actual drop (8%) exceeds this break-even point, the change destroys value.

**3. Cohort Retention Analysis**
To address the "adjustment period" concern, analyze **Day 7 and Day 30 return rates** for customers who converted during the test vs. the prior period.
*   If new customers acquired during the test have lower retention, the higher threshold is attracting lower-quality traffic or damaging brand perception.
*   If retention is stable, the conversion drop is a permanent filter of low-value baskets, not a temporary friction.

### Recommendation Framework for the Operations Director

Present the findings using this decision matrix based on the **Profit per Session** calculation:

| Outcome | Profit/Session | Recommendation |
| :--- | :--- | :--- |
| **Positive Lift** | Scenario B > Scenario A | **Implement Immediately.** The margin gain outweighs volume loss. |
| **Neutral/Negative** | Scenario B ≤ Scenario A | **Revert to $50.** Do not wait for "adjustment." Friction is functioning as intended (blocking low-value orders) but too aggressively. |
| **Mixed** | Profit flat, but AOV up | **Test $60.** The optimal threshold likely lies between $50 and $65. Run a segmented test at $60 for the holiday season. |

**Final Note:** Given the 8% conversion drop, it is statistically probable that the loss in volume outweighs the margin gain unless your shipping costs are exceptionally high relative to product margins. Prepare the revert plan as the default unless the DiD analysis proves the seasonal noise was the sole cause of the drop.

*Ensure all shipping cost calculations include variable handling fees, not just carrier rates, to avoid underestimating the savings of the higher threshold.*
