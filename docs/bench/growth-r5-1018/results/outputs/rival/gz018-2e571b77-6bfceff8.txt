# ServiceHub North Star Metric Framework

## Recommended North Star: **Quality-Adjusted Completed Jobs (QACJ)**

Given your tension between volume growth (+15%) and dipping satisfaction, a raw count of transactions is insufficient. Your North Star must penalize volume that erodes trust.

**Formula:**
`QACJ = Total Completed Jobs × Quality Coefficient`

Where **Quality Coefficient** is derived from post-job ratings:
*   5-star rating = 1.0 multiplier
*   4-star rating = 0.8 multiplier
*   3-star rating = 0.5 multiplier
*   <3-star rating = 0.0 multiplier (excluded from success count)

This metric forces the team to realize that a bad match is not just "neutral"—it actively subtracts from your growth engine by destroying repeat usage and referral potential.

## Evaluation of Candidates

| Metric Candidate | Pros | Cons | Verdict |
| :--- | :--- | :--- | :--- |
| **Total Transaction Volume** | Easy to track; clear growth signal. | Ignores quality; incentivizes bad matches to hit numbers. | **Reject** as North Star (keep as secondary). |
| **Net Revenue** | Directly ties to sustainability. | Can be gamed by raising fees, which hurts liquidity in a two-sided market. | **Secondary** (Lagging indicator). |
| **Frequency of Use** | Indicates product stickiness. | In local services, high frequency may indicate poor work quality (repeat repairs). | **Contextual** only. |
| **Quality-Adjusted Completed Jobs** | Balances supply/demand; aligns with long-term retention. | Requires robust rating data integration. | **SELECT** as North Star. |

## Validation Method: The Cohort Correlation Test

To prove to your leadership that QACJ predicts sustainability better than raw volume, perform this retrospective analysis before the offsite:

1.  **Segment Users:** Divide customers acquired 12–18 months ago into quartiles based on their initial experience (e.g., Quartile 1 had mostly 5-star jobs; Quartile 4 had mixed/low ratings).
2.  **Measure Retention:** Calculate the Month-12 retention rate and Lifetime Value (LTV) for each quartile.
3.  **Correlate:** Plot the correlation between "Initial QACJ" and "Month-12 Retention."
4.  **Compare:** Run the same plot for "Initial Raw Volume."
5.  **Result:** You will likely find that QACJ has a significantly higher correlation coefficient with long-term retention than raw volume. This data proves that protecting the quality coefficient is the primary driver of business longevity.

## Socialization Plan for Leadership Offsite

Do not present the metric as a mathematical abstraction. Frame it as a behavioral contract for the company.

**1. The "Why" Narrative (15 mins)**
Present the correlation data above. Show the cost of a "bad match" in terms of lost future revenue. Explicitly state: *"Growth at the expense of quality is not growth; it is churn acceleration."*

**2. The Definition Workshop (30 mins)**
Collaboratively define the "Quality Coefficient" thresholds. Ask Operations and Supply teams: *"Does a 4-star job truly deserve an 0.8 multiplier, or should we be stricter?"* Getting their input on the formula ensures buy-in on the result.

**3. Departmental Translation (20 mins)**
Break out by function to define how QACJ changes their weekly goals:
*   **Supply Team:** Shift from "Onboard 100 contractors" to "Onboard 100 contractors who achieve a 4.5+ avg rating in first 10 jobs."
*   **Demand/Marketing:** Shift from "Maximize leads" to "Maximize leads likely to convert into 5-star matches."
*   **Product:** Prioritize features that improve matching accuracy over features that simply increase transaction speed.

**4. The Dashboard Commitment**
Commit to displaying QACJ as the top-line number on the company dashboard, with raw volume and revenue as supporting metrics below it.

By adopting QACJ, ServiceHub signals that while volume matters, the *health* of the marketplace is the only metric that guarantees survival in Cairo's competitive landscape.

*Note: Ensure your data pipeline can reliably capture post-job ratings before finalizing the coefficient logic.*
*Revisit the weighting coefficients quarterly until the correlation with LTV stabilizes.*
