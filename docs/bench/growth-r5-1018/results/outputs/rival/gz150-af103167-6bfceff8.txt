# Upsell Experiment Post-Mortem: KnotWork

## Executive Summary
The October 5–19 upsell experiment failed catastrophically, reducing conversion from 2.1% to 0.4% and triggering a 12% spike in account deletions. The current data confirms *that* users rejected the offer, but it fails to explain *why*. The measurement approach focused solely on the binary outcome (convert vs. delete) without capturing the user's intent or workflow state at the moment of interruption. Before considering a revenue model pivot, we must diagnose whether the failure was driven by price sensitivity, timing friction, or a fundamental mismatch in perceived value.

## Key Ambiguities in Current Data

**1. The "Why" of Deletion is Unknown**
We know deletions increased by 12% within 24 hours of exposure, but we do not know if users deleted because:
*   They felt the platform was becoming "pay-to-play" and lost trust.
*   The modal blocked a critical workflow step, causing frustration unrelated to price.
*   They were testing the free tier, hit the limit, and naturally churned (coincidental timing).
*   **Missing Data:** Exit survey responses, session replay heatmaps showing rage-clicks on the close button, or support ticket tags related to "pricing" during this window.

**2. Intent vs. Interruption**
The hypothesis assumed "3 projects = serious intent." However, for film producers, the third project might be a sandbox test, a student film, or a pitch deck never intended to go into production.
*   **Missing Data:** We did not track what users did *immediately before* triggering the modal. Did they just upload a budget (high intent) or just create an empty shell (low intent)? Without this, we cannot validate the "serious intent" assumption.

**3. Price vs. Timing Conflation**
Because we tested one variable (the modal) with a fixed price ($18) at a fixed time (project #3), we cannot isolate the culprit.
*   **Missing Data:** We have no data on price elasticity (would $5 have worked?) or timing flexibility (would a banner instead of a modal have reduced churn?).

## Critical Questions to Answer Before Retesting

To determine if a pivot is necessary or if this was simply a poorly executed test, the founding team must answer:

1.  **Workflow Friction:** Did the modal appear *during* a task (e.g., while uploading a file) or *between* tasks? Interrupting a "flow state" causes significantly higher churn than interstitial prompts.
2.  **Value Perception:** Do users understand that "advanced budget tracking" solves their specific pain point at project #3? Or is this feature irrelevant until later stages (e.g., pre-production vs. post-production)?
3.  **Alternative Monetization Signals:** Have users who converted organically (without the modal) shown different behavioral patterns? What was their project count and feature usage before upgrading?
4.  **Churn Attribution:** Of the deleted accounts, what percentage had previously engaged with *any* paid feature or visited the pricing page? This distinguishes between "price shock" and "workflow annoyance."

## Recommended Variables for Future Diagnosis

Do not run another broad A/B test until you implement tracking for these leading indicators:

*   **Interaction Cost:** Time spent on the modal before closing vs. converting. (Long hesitation suggests price consideration; immediate close suggests irritation).
*   **Feature Tease Engagement:** Clicks on a "Preview Feature" link within the modal (measures interest in the tool itself, separate from the paywall).
*   **Workflow State:** The specific action being performed when the modal triggered (e.g., "Creating Task" vs. "Viewing Dashboard").
*   **Soft Conversion:** Users who dismiss the modal but visit the pricing page within 24 hours (indicates interest despite poor timing).

## Immediate Next Steps
Pause all aggressive upsell modals. Implement session recording tools to qualitatively review the user experience at the "Project #3" milestone. Conduct 5–10 exit interviews with users who deleted their accounts during the experiment window to distinguish between price objections and workflow frustration. Only then can you decide whether to adjust the offer or pivot the revenue model.

*Note: The 12% deletion spike suggests an immediate threat to user trust; prioritize qualitative feedback over quantitative testing for the next 14 days.*
