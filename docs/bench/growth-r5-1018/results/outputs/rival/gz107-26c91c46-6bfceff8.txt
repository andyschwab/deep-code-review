# Post-Mortem: Checkout Simplification A/B Test

**Recommendation:** Revert the simplified flow immediately. The 12% decrease in completed bookings represents a statistically significant loss in revenue that outweighs the efficiency gain of a 40-second faster checkout. The hypothesis that "fewer clicks equal higher conversion" failed because it prioritized speed over user confidence.

### Analysis of Failure
The test results indicate a classic "efficiency vs. effectiveness" trade-off error. While the variant successfully reduced friction (time-to-book), it likely removed critical "friction of confidence" required for high-consideration purchases like travel.

Two primary drivers likely caused the 12% drop:
1.  **Missing Information/Options:** If the removed steps contained insurance upsells, seat selection, or cancellation policy details, users may have abandoned the flow due to uncertainty or fear of making an irreversible mistake without reviewing these options.
2.  **Pacing and Trust:** A 40-second reduction is substantial. For travel bookings, this rapid pace can feel rushed, triggering anxiety rather than relief. Users often use intermediate steps to mentally validate their purchase; removing these "pause points" can increase cognitive load at the final payment button, leading to abandonment.

### Required Signals for Deep Dive
To confirm the specific cause before the director's meeting, investigate these quantitative and qualitative signals:

**Quantitative Signals:**
*   **Funnel Drop-off Points:** Compare abandonment rates specifically at the *first* remaining step of the new flow versus the old flow. A spike here suggests users are confused or missing context immediately upon entering the simplified flow.
*   **Support Ticket Volume:** Filter for tickets tagged "checkout," "insurance," or "error" during the test window. A spike in "Where is my insurance option?" confirms the missing content hypothesis.
*   **Payment Method Abandonment:** Check if the drop is uniform across payment types. If high-value transactions (e.g., credit cards) dropped more than low-value ones, it suggests a trust issue rather than a UI confusion issue.
*   **Segment Performance:** Analyze by device. Mobile users often prefer speed, while desktop users may prefer detail. If mobile performed worse, the "rushed" feeling is likely the culprit.

**Qualitative Signals:**
*   **Session Recordings:** Watch 20–30 recordings of users who abandoned the simplified flow. Look for "rage clicks," rapid back-button usage, or long pauses on the final payment page (indicating hesitation).
*   **Exit Surveys:** If an exit survey was triggered on abandonment, analyze text responses for keywords like "missing," "unsure," "too fast," or "insurance."
*   **Customer Support Logs:** Manually review 10–15 recent chat logs regarding checkout issues to identify recurring themes about missing information.

### Next Steps
1.  **Revert:** Restore the original flow to stop revenue bleed.
2.  **Hybrid Hypothesis:** Formulate a new test that keeps the steps but optimizes their content (e.g., pre-select insurance, summarize policies clearly) rather than removing them entirely.
3.  **Qualitative Research:** Conduct 5 user interviews focusing on "What information did you need to feel safe completing the booking?"

*Note: Ensure the 12% figure is adjusted for any external factors (e.g., site outages, holiday effects) during the ten-day window, though the magnitude suggests a treatment effect.*
*Do not re-run the test until the specific missing element (content vs. pacing) is identified via the signals above.*
