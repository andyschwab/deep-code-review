### Recommended Analytics Events for NeueHeimat Hub

To demonstrate efficacy to your board and funders while respecting your limited server capacity and volunteer resources, you should track the following three high-signal events. These move beyond simple "vanity" pageviews to measure active engagement and progression toward integration outcomes.

**1. `resource_guide_downloaded`**
*   **What to track:** A custom event firing only when a user successfully completes a PDF download (e.g., "Housing Rights Guide," "German A1 Checklist").
*   **Properties to include:** `guide_category` (housing, legal, language), `language_version` (German, English, Arabic, etc.).
*   **Why this matters:** You correctly identified this as your current primary proxy for value. By categorizing downloads, you can report specifically on which *types* of aid are most in demand (e.g., "60% of users sought housing support"). This provides concrete evidence of resource distribution to funders. Since downloads are discrete, high-intent actions, they are low-noise data points that correlate strongly with users taking offline action.

**2. `external_link_clicked`**
*   **What to track:** Clicks on outbound links that direct users to third-party services, such as government housing portals, legal aid booking forms, or language school registration pages.
*   **Properties to include:** `destination_type` (government, ngo, private_provider), `service_category`.
*   **Why this matters:** This addresses your concern about users finding value without downloading. For many refugees, the goal is not to read a guide but to *apply* for something. Clicking a link to a housing application is a stronger signal of integration progress than reading a blog post. This event captures the "bridge" moment where your portal facilitates a real-world transaction. It is technically lightweight (a simple click listener) but proves your platform is an active conduit to services, not just a library.

**3. `search_query_submitted`**
*   **What to track:** Every time a user utilizes your site's search bar and hits enter.
*   **Properties to include:** `search_term` (anonymized text), `results_count` (number of results returned).
*   **Why this matters:** This reveals unmet needs and intent. If users frequently search for "emergency shelter" but you have no guides on that topic, you have identified a critical gap in your service offering. Conversely, if users search for "kindergarten" and immediately click a result, you have validated a successful connection. For a board report, this data allows you to say, "We responded to 500 specific requests for legal aid," demonstrating responsiveness to community needs even if no download occurred.

**Implementation Note:**
Configure these events via a lightweight tag manager or simple JavaScript snippets to ensure they do not burden your server; avoid heavy session recording tools. Focus your reporting on the *conversion rate* between these events (e.g., Search → Click → Download) to tell a story of user progression.
