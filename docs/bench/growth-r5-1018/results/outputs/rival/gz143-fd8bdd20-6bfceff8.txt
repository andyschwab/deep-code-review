### Recommended Onboarding Success Framework for MindfulPath

Do not optimize for "booking a session" alone. In mental health, a booked but unattended session represents a failed intervention and potential user harm. Your conflicting signals indicate you have successfully optimized for *friction reduction* but inadvertently reduced *commitment quality*.

To satisfy both your clinical advisory board and investors, you must shift your primary success metric from **Conversion Rate** (Booked/Signups) to **Activated Care Rate** (Attended First Session/Signups).

#### 1. Primary Metric: Activated Care Rate
This is your single source of truth. It combines speed with outcome.
*   **Definition:** Percentage of new signups who *attend* their first scheduled session within 48 hours.
*   **Why:** It aligns investor goals (revenue/retention) with clinical goals (actual care delivery). If this number drops while bookings rise, your new flow is failing regardless of how "smooth" it feels.

#### 2. Secondary Metric: Commitment Quality Score
You need to measure the *intent* behind the booking to diagnose if users are rushing or mismatched. Track these three leading indicators:
*   **Profile Completion Depth:** Did the user fill out optional fields (e.g., "specific trauma history," "preferred therapist gender") during the fast flow? Low completion suggests low investment.
*   **Reschedule/Cancel Rate:** Measure the % of first bookings cancelled or rescheduled within 24 hours. A spike here confirms low initial commitment.
*   **Time-to-Book vs. Time-to-Attend:** Correlate how long a user spent in onboarding with their attendance. If <2 minute onboarding correlates with <50% attendance, you have identified your "danger zone."

#### 3. Guardrail Metric: Clinical Mismatch Flag
To address the "mismatched pairing" fear, track:
*   **Therapist Switch Rate:** How many users switch therapists before the second session?
*   **Session Duration:** Are first sessions with "fast-onboarded" users ending early?
*   **Post-Session Anxiety Delta:** If possible, capture a one-tap survey post-session: "How do you feel now vs. before?" (Better/Same/Worse).

### How to Read the Conflicting Signals

Your data suggests a **False Positive Conversion**. The old flow likely used friction (more questions) as a mechanism to force users to reflect on their needs, increasing psychological commitment before booking. The new flow removed this reflection time.

*   **If Activated Care Rate is flat or down:** The speed gain is an illusion. You are generating "junk bookings."
*   **If Reschedule Rate is up:** Users are booking impulsively to "get it over with" rather than preparing for care.
*   **If Mismatch Flags are up:** The skipped questions contained critical data needed for the matching algorithm.

### Strategic Recommendation

Present a **"Friction Re-Introduction"** strategy to your board. Do not revert to the old long form, but do not stay at the current minimal state.

1.  **Segment the Flow:** Keep the fast track for users who clearly know what they want, but introduce "Smart Friction" for ambiguous cases. If a user skips all optional fields, trigger a gentle nudge: *"Taking 30 more seconds to tell us about your preferences helps us match you with a therapist you'll stick with."*
2.  **Delay the Ask:** If speed is critical, allow the quick booking, but move the deep-dive questions to the *pre-session* preparation phase (between booking and the appointment). This maintains speed while ensuring the therapist has the data before meeting the patient.
3.  **Report the Delta:** Show investors that while top-of-funnel bookings rose by X%, the *quality* of those bookings (attendance) dropped by Y%. Frame the solution as "optimizing for retained revenue, not just booked revenue."

**Note:** Ensure your analytics tool tracks the specific timestamp of "Onboarding Start" to "Booking Complete" to correlate duration with attendance.
**Note:** Explicitly exclude "no-shows" from any revenue projections presented to investors until the Activated Care Rate stabilizes.
