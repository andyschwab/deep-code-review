# A/B Test Analysis: In-App Purchase Prompt Timing (GemQuest)

**Test Period:** May 5 – May 19
**Population:** 100,000 Daily Active Users
**Variable:** Prompt timing (Level 5 vs. Level 10)

## Executive Summary
The earlier prompt (Level 5) failed to meet the primary hypothesis of increasing conversion volume, resulting in a **4% decrease in conversion rate**. However, it successfully captured higher-value transactions, increasing **Average Revenue Per Paying User (ARPPU) by $0.50**. This indicates a shift in user behavior: fewer users purchased, but those who did were willing to spend more.

The concurrent **2-minute drop in session length** presents a critical risk. Without distinguishing between "efficient completion" and "frustrated churn," rolling out this change threatens long-term retention. The data suggests the earlier prompt acts as a friction point that filters out low-intent buyers while potentially alienating casual players.

## Key Findings

### 1. Revenue Trade-off: Volume vs. Value
- **Conversion Rate (-4%):** The assumption that earlier exposure captures "impulse buyers" was incorrect. Instead, the prompt likely interrupted the core gameplay loop before users achieved a "win state" or emotional investment, causing them to dismiss the offer entirely.
- **ARPPU (+$0.50):** The users who *did* convert despite the interruption had higher purchase intent or disposable income. The earlier prompt may have acted as a qualifier, filtering out window shoppers and leaving only serious spenders.
- **Net Revenue Impact:** While ARPPU increased, the drop in conversion volume likely resulted in a net neutral or slightly negative total revenue impact, depending on the baseline conversion rate. The gain per user did not sufficiently compensate for the loss in buyer volume.

### 2. Engagement Risk: The Session Length Drop
The 2-minute reduction in session length is the most concerning metric for a puzzle game reliant on ad impressions and long-term LTV.
- **Scenario A (Frustration):** Users encounter the prompt, feel interrupted, and quit the session early. This predicts higher churn rates and lower Lifetime Value (LTV) over time.
- **Scenario B (Efficiency):** Users purchase earlier, receive boosts, and complete levels faster. This would be a positive outcome, indicating the prompt aids gameplay flow.

Given the drop in conversion rate, Scenario A (frustration) is the more probable driver. Users are not buying their way out of levels; they are leaving because the experience was disrupted.

## Strategic Recommendation

**Do not roll out the Level 5 prompt globally in its current state.**

The test reveals a classic monetization conflict: optimizing for short-term ARPPU at the expense of conversion volume and potential engagement. For a puzzle game, session length and retention are leading indicators of long-term health. A strategy that reduces session time and conversion volume is unsustainable, even with higher ARPPU.

### Next Steps for Decision Making
1.  **Isolate the Cause of Session Drop:** Before any further testing, analyze churn points immediately following the Level 5 prompt. Did users close the app entirely, or did they continue playing without purchasing? This distinction determines if the prompt is a "churn driver" or a "flow accelerator."
2.  **Re-evaluate the Hypothesis:** The data suggests impulse buying in GemQuest is not triggered by *timing* alone but likely by *context*. Users may need to experience a failure state or a specific difficulty spike before feeling the need to purchase.
3.  **Balance Short-term vs. Long-term:** While the $0.50 ARPPU gain is attractive, it does not justify a 4% loss in buyers and reduced engagement unless the session drop is proven to be benign. Prioritize retention metrics in the next iteration to ensure monetization does not cannibalize the player base.

**Conclusion:** The earlier prompt is not currently viable. The risk to long-term engagement outweighs the marginal gain in spender value. Further investigation into user sentiment post-prompt is required before re-testing.

*Note: Total revenue calculation requires baseline conversion rates to determine if the ARPPU gain offset the volume loss. Session drop analysis must differentiate between churn and accelerated completion.*
