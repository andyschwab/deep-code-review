### Executive Briefing: Hazard Alert Feature Trial (March 3–17)

**The Core Conflict**
The trial produced a false positive for engagement. The 18% rise in Daily Active Users (DAU) is likely driven by "thrashing"—users repeatedly triggering the alert due to confusion or sensitivity testing—rather than genuine value creation. This behavior directly correlates with the 575% increase in critical crashes (27 vs. 4), indicating the system is failing under load or misinterpreted usage.

**Decision Framework: Reliability Over Velocity**
For safety-critical software in the construction sector, system stability is not just a technical metric; it is the primary value proposition. A hazard alert tool that crashes during an emergency destroys user trust instantly. Given the CEO's skepticism and the constraint against public failure, **reliability must be the gatekeeper metric.** You cannot trade stability for engagement in this specific domain.

**Recommended Metrics Hierarchy**
To present a clear path forward, reframe your reporting around these three tiers:

1.  **Gatekeeper Metric (Stop/Launch Decision): Crash-Free Session Rate**
    *   *Current State:* Unacceptable. With 27 critical crashes in two weeks, the app is unreliable for field use.
    *   *Threshold:* Define a strict minimum (e.g., 99.5% crash-free sessions) required before any broader rollout. If this isn't met, the feature remains in beta regardless of usage stats.

2.  **Diagnostic Metric (Root Cause): Alerts Per User Per Session**
    *   *Purpose:* Distinguish between "genuine need" and "confusion."
    *   *Analysis:* Calculate the average number of alert triggers per session.
        *   **High Frequency (>3/session):** Indicates confusion, accidental triggers, or users "testing" the button. This validates your hypothesis that the engagement spike is artificial and harmful.
        *   **Low Frequency (1-2/session):** Suggests genuine hazard reporting, implying the crashes are due to backend scaling issues rather than user error.

3.  **Outcome Metric (True Value): Verified Hazard Resolution**
    *   *Purpose:* Measure actual safety impact, not just button clicks.
    *   *Definition:* Track how many triggered alerts resulted in a confirmed safety action or acknowledgment by a supervisor. This filters out noise and proves utility.

**Strategic Recommendation**
Do not roll out the feature yet. The current data suggests the "engagement" is actually a symptom of a usability flaw causing system instability.

**Immediate Action Plan:**
1.  **Pause Broad Rollout:** Communicate to the CEO that the trial successfully identified a critical stability risk before it reached the full market. This frames the trial as a success in risk mitigation, not a failure of the feature itself.
2.  **Isolate the Trigger:** Analyze the 27 crash logs specifically against the "Alerts Per User" metric. If crashes correlate with high-frequency triggering, the fix is UI/UX (e.g., add a confirmation step, debounce the button, or clarify instructions).
3.  **Run a Targeted Fix:** Implement a UI guardrail (such as a "Hold to Confirm" interaction) to prevent accidental spamming. Re-test with a small control group (5–10 users) to see if DAU normalizes while crash rates drop.

**Conclusion for the CEO**
"The trial revealed that our current interface encourages behavior that crashes the app. The 18% usage spike is a warning sign of confusion, not adoption. By prioritizing crash resolution over raw engagement numbers, we can refine the interaction model to ensure the tool is stable when a worker genuinely needs it. We recommend a two-week stabilization sprint focused on preventing accidental triggers before reconsidering launch."

*Note: Ensure your engineering team isolates whether crashes are client-side (app logic) or server-side (overload) to direct the fix correctly.*
*Do not present the 18% DAU increase as a win; contextualize it immediately as a stress-test result.*
