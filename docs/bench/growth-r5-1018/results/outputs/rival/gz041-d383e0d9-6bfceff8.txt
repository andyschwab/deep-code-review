**Executive Summary: Baggage Allowance Clarity A/B Test**

**Verdict: Inconclusive. Do not roll out yet.**

The test results (Control: 22.0% vs. Variant: 23.1%) represent a **1.1 percentage point lift**, but this finding is **statistically unreliable** due to insufficient sample size. With only 8,400 participants, the test lacks the power to distinguish a genuine behavioral shift from random noise.

**Statistical Reliability Assessment**
To detect a lift of this magnitude (approx. 5% relative improvement) with standard confidence (95%), you typically require roughly **30,000–40,000 participants per variant**. Your total sample of 8,400 (approx. 4,200 per group) is critically underpowered.
*   **The Risk:** The wide confidence interval indicates that the true effect could easily be zero, negative, or significantly higher. The observed 1.1% lift is within the margin of error.
*   **The Decision Trap:** Acting on this data risks implementing engineering changes for a "win" that may not exist, or killing a feature that might actually drive double-digit growth if measured correctly.

**Segmentation Hypothesis**
Your intuition regarding leisure vs. business travelers is likely the key to unlocking value here, but the current dataset is too small to segment reliably.
*   **Leisure Travelers:** Often price-sensitive and unfamiliar with baggage rules; clarity likely reduces anxiety and friction.
*   **Business Travelers:** Often have status or fixed allowances; extra data may be irrelevant noise.
*   **Action:** Aggregating these groups dilutes the signal. A real effect in the leisure segment is being masked by the non-effect in the business segment.

**Recommendations**

1.  **Do Not Roll Out Broadly:** Halting the rollout to Croatian and Italian routes is the prudent financial decision. The engineering cost cannot be justified by statistically insignificant data.
2.  **Extend the Test (If feasible):** If seasonal demand allows, extend the test duration to reach a minimum of 15,000 participants *per variant*. This will narrow the confidence interval and provide a definitive answer.
3.  **Pivot to Segmented Analysis:** If extending the test is impossible due to seasonality, do not kill the feature. Instead, instrument the tracking to explicitly tag "leisure" vs. "business" bookings (e.g., by fare class or booking channel) and re-analyze the existing data. If the lift in the leisure segment is >5%, consider a targeted rollout only to leisure-focused routes.
4.  **Low-Cost Alternative:** Before committing engineering resources to a full flow change, test a lightweight copy update on the baggage selection page only, rather than the entire booking flow, to validate the hypothesis with lower technical debt.

**Conclusion**
The data currently shows a promising *direction* but provides no *proof*. Treat this as a "pause and refine" signal rather than a go/no-go decision. Prioritize gathering more data or isolating the leisure segment before committing capital to a full market rollout.
