**Executive Summary: Donate Button Test Analysis**

**Current Performance**
*   **Variant Traffic:** 2,500 visitors
*   **Conversions:** 45 donations
*   **Conversion Rate (CVR):** 1.80%
*   **Total Revenue:** $3,200
*   **Average Donation:** $71.11

**Reliability Assessment**
The current data is **not statistically reliable** for declaring a winner. With only 45 conversions, the margin of error is approximately ±14%. This means the true conversion rate could realistically range from 0.4% to 3.2%. Any observed difference from your control page is likely noise rather than a genuine effect of the button color. Declaring a winner now risks implementing a change that provides no value or, worse, inadvertently lowers donations.

**Recommendation: Do Not Extend; Pivot to Qualitative Insight**
Given the campaign ends December 31st and your budget is tight, **do not extend the test for two weeks.**

1.  **Insufficient Upside:** Even if you doubled traffic to 5,000, you would likely only reach ~90 conversions. This remains below the threshold needed for high-confidence statistical significance (typically requiring 100+ conversions per variant for modest lift detection).
2.  **Opportunity Cost:** Running a potentially underperforming variant for two more weeks risks leaving donations on the table during your peak giving season.
3.  **Strategic Pivot:** Since quantitative certainty is unattainable within your constraints, stop the A/B test immediately. Revert to the control (or the variant *only* if qualitative feedback is overwhelmingly positive) and redirect resources toward high-impact holiday messaging.

**Action Plan for the Board Report**
Frame this not as a failed test, but as a validated learning exercise:
*   **State the Baseline:** "Our current donate button converts at approximately 1.8% under test conditions."
*   **Highlight Constraints:** "Statistical rigor requires more traffic than our paid test could generate within the budget window."
*   **Propose Next Steps:** "We will implement the new button only if user testing confirms better visibility, otherwise we will retain the control and focus budget on driving total traffic volume, which yields higher immediate ROI than marginal conversion tweaks."

**Critical Missing Context**
To refine future decisions, you must retrieve:
1.  **Control Group Data:** What was the conversion rate and traffic volume for the original button during the exact same period? Without this comparison, the 1.8% figure is meaningless.
2.  **Segmentation by Device:** Mobile users often struggle with small buttons. If 80% of your traffic is mobile, a color change might have a vastly different impact than on desktop. Split the data by device immediately to see if the trend holds in one specific environment.

**Note:** Ensure your analytics tool is tracking "donation started" vs. "donation completed" to rule out technical friction in the payment processor itself.
**Note:** For the board, emphasize that increasing total site traffic by 10% will likely generate more revenue than optimizing this button by 1%.
