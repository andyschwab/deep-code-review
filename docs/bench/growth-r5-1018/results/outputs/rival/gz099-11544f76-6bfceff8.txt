# Procurement Workflow Diagnosis Plan for BuildRight

**Objective:** Isolate the root cause of the 2-day cycle time increase (3 to 5 days) in the 1,200 monthly purchase orders (POs) to inform the CEO roadmap.

### Phase 1: Immediate Data Reconstruction (Days 1–2)
Since intermediate timestamps are missing, we must reconstruct the timeline using available data and proxy indicators.

1.  **Segment by Value Threshold:**
    *   Split the 1,200 POs into two groups: **<$10k** (no finance approval required) and **>$10k** (requires finance approval).
    *   Calculate the average cycle time for each group for last month vs. the previous three months.
    *   *Diagnostic Logic:* If the <$10k group also slowed down, the bottleneck is likely upstream (vendor documentation or requisition) or downstream (final processing), not finance. If only the >$10k group slowed, your hypothesis is correct.

2.  **Vendor Document Latency Proxy:**
    *   Cross-reference PO creation timestamps with vendor email receipt timestamps (if available in inboxes) or ERP attachment upload times.
    *   Identify the time delta between "PO Created" and "Documentation Complete."
    *   *Diagnostic Logic:* A growing gap here indicates missing vendor documentation is the primary driver.

3.  **Finance Queue Volume Analysis:**
    *   Plot the volume of >$10k POs submitted per day/week against the finance team's headcount or known capacity.
    *   Look for spikes in submission volume that correlate with the cycle time increase.
    *   *Diagnostic Logic:* If volume remained flat but time increased, the issue is a finance backlog or resource constraint. If volume spiked, the process lacks scalability.

### Phase 2: Qualitative Process Audit (Days 3–4)
Validate quantitative findings with direct observation.

1.  **The "Five-PO" Walkthrough:**
    *   Select five specific >$10k POs from last month that experienced the longest delays.
    *   Manually trace their path: Interview the requisitioner, the vendor manager, and the specific finance approver.
    *   Ask: "At what point did this sit idle, and why?" (e.g., waiting for a signature, missing invoice, system error).

2.  **Finance Team Pulse Check:**
    *   Conduct a 30-minute session with the finance approvers.
    *   Determine if the delay is due to **volume** (too many approvals), **complexity** (missing info requiring back-and-forth), or **tooling** (system slowness/access issues).

### Phase 3: Synthesis and Roadmap Formulation (Day 5)
Compile findings into three potential scenarios for Thomas Greer's presentation:

*   **Scenario A: Vendor Documentation Bottleneck.**
    *   *Evidence:* Large gap between PO creation and document completion; <$10k orders also delayed.
    *   *Recommendation:* Implement a vendor portal for mandatory document upload before PO generation; automate validation checks.
*   **Scenario B: Finance Approval Backlog.**
    *   *Evidence:* Delay isolated to >$10k orders; high correlation with submission spikes; finance team reports capacity issues.
    *   *Recommendation:* Increase approval threshold (e.g., to $15k); delegate preliminary checks to junior staff; implement parallel approvals.
*   **Scenario C: Process Ambiguity.**
    *   *Evidence:* POs bouncing between departments due to unclear ownership.
    *   *Recommendation:* Define SLAs for each stage; automate escalation notifications after 24 hours of inactivity.

### Immediate Next Steps for Thomas Greer
1.  Execute the **Value Threshold Segmentation** immediately to confirm or refute the finance hypothesis.
2.  Schedule the **Finance Team Pulse Check** to gather qualitative context for the CEO presentation.
3.  Draft the roadmap focusing on the single highest-impact lever identified in Phase 1.

*Note: This plan relies on reconstructing missing data through segmentation and proxy metrics. Do not propose new software purchases until the specific bottleneck (vendor vs. finance) is quantified.*
