**Priority Early Signals (First 48 Hours)**

To predict long-term viability for NegevTech, prioritize tracking these specific actions within the first two days of user onboarding:

1.  **Second Transaction Initiation:** The single strongest predictor of a "power user" in fintech is not the first transaction, but the initiation of a second one within 48 hours. This distinguishes users testing the system from those integrating it into a workflow.
2.  **Feature Breadth (3+ Actions):** Users who engage with at least three distinct core features (e.g., linking a bank account, setting a rule/tag, and executing a transfer) show significantly higher retention than those who only complete the mandatory first transaction.
3.  **Support/Help Interaction Pattern:** Distinguish between users who read help docs *before* transacting (high intent/learning) versus those who search for help *after* an error or abandonment (friction point). High correlation exists between pre-transaction help consumption and power user status.
4.  **Session Depth & Return Frequency:** Power users typically exhibit multiple short sessions rather than one long session in the first 48 hours. Track users who return to the app at least twice within this window without a push notification trigger.

**Distinguishing Product Failure vs. Natural Settling**

To determine if the drop-off from 150 to 20 transactions is a critical failure or a natural beta settling period, analyze the following data points immediately:

*   **Cohort Retention Curves:** Do not look at aggregate numbers. Segment your 500 users by signup date (daily cohorts). If the Day 2 and Day 7 retention rates are consistent across cohorts (e.g., every cohort drops by ~80% after the first week), this indicates a natural settling period where initial curiosity faded. If later cohorts show *worse* retention than early ones, this signals a product failure or worsening bug introduction.
*   **Funnel Drop-off Location:** Map exactly where the 480 non-transacting (or one-time) users abandoned the flow.
    *   *Natural Settling:* Users complete the first transaction successfully but never return. This suggests a lack of recurring use cases or value proposition issues, not necessarily bugs.
    *   *Product Failure:* Users abandon *during* the first transaction setup (e.g., at bank linking or confirmation). A high abandonment rate at a specific technical step indicates a bug or UX friction preventing value realization.
*   **Qualitative Exit Signals:** Review support tickets and session recordings for the 480 users.
    *   *Natural Settling:* Users express "nothing else to do" or "will check back later."
    *   *Product Failure:* Users report specific errors, confusion on basic steps, or inability to complete the core loop.

For your seed investor update, frame the narrative around **cohort consistency** rather than raw volume decline. If your daily cohorts show stable retention curves, argue that the initial spike was "beta curiosity" and the current 20 weekly transactors represent your true early adopter baseline. If later cohorts are degrading, acknowledge the friction and present a remediation plan focused on the specific drop-off points identified.
