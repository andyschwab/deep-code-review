**Subject:** Recommendation for GreenFlow North-Star Metric

**Executive Recommendation**
Adopt **"Normalized Water Savings per Active Household"** as the primary north-star metric.

While "weekly engagement" is a vital health indicator for product usage, it is a lagging proxy for the actual value municipalities purchase: water conservation. City managers and mayors are accountable for volume reductions, not app open rates. However, to address the valid concern regarding climate variability and baseline differences, we must not use raw "gallons saved." Instead, we will normalize the data to ensure fair comparison across cities like Springfield and Riverton.

**Comparative Analysis of Potential Metrics**

| Metric | Pros | Cons | Verdict |
| :--- | :--- | :--- | : |
| **Gallons Saved (Raw)** | Directly ties to municipal cost savings; easy to explain to stakeholders. | Unfairly penalizes cities with wet climates or low baseline usage; incentivizes targeting only high-use households. | **Reject** as standalone north-star. |
| **Weekly Engagement %** | Measures behavior change sustainability; immune to weather fluctuations. | Does not guarantee actual water reduction (users can engage without saving); hard to translate into budget impact for mayors. | **Adopt** as secondary "Health Metric." |
| **Normalized Savings %** | Compares actual usage against a climate-adjusted baseline; fair across diverse geographies; focuses on efficiency gains. | Requires robust baseline data modeling; slightly more complex to explain than raw gallons. | **SELECT** as North-Star. |

**The Solution: Normalized Water Savings**
To solve the normalization challenge, the north-star will be calculated as:
*(Actual Usage – Climate-Adjusted Baseline) / Climate-Adjusted Baseline*

This approach accounts for:
1.  **Climate Conditions:** Adjusts baselines based on local rainfall and temperature data (e.g., allowing higher usage in drought-free periods).
2.  **Baseline Usage:** Measures improvement relative to each city's historical average, rather than an absolute gallon target.
3.  **Equity:** Allows Springfield and Riverton to compete on *efficiency gains* rather than absolute volume, aligning incentives regardless of city size or weather.

**Strategic Narrative for the Springfield Mayor**
When meeting the Mayor on Thursday, frame the metric as **"Efficiency Over Volume."**
*   **The Problem:** Raw gallon counts fluctuate with weather, making performance look poor during dry spells even if residents are trying hard.
*   **The GreenFlow Solution:** We measure how much better residents are doing compared to what *should* have been used given the weather.
*   **The Value:** This proves the program's effectiveness in driving behavioral change, insulating the city's performance reports from uncontrollable weather events.

**Implementation Plan**
1.  **Primary Dashboard:** Display "Normalized Savings %" prominently for all 15 cities.
2.  **Secondary View:** Track "Weekly Engagement %" to identify cities where low savings are due to low adoption rather than ineffective conservation.
3.  **Reporting:** Provide both the normalized percentage and the estimated total gallon impact (for budget calculations), but tie success bonuses and goal-setting to the normalized percentage.

This dual approach satisfies the need for tangible volume data while ensuring fair, behavior-focused comparisons across your diverse municipal portfolio.
