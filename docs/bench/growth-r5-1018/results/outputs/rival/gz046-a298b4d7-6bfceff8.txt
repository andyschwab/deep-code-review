# Incident Report: Harvest Prediction Dashboard & Churn Uplift
**To:** CEO, Product Design Lead
**From:** Director of Analytics
**Date:** October 18
**Subject:** Urgent Analysis of 2.1% Churn Rate Post-Feature Launch

### Executive Summary
Between October 3 and October 17, weekly churn among the 450 farmers with access to the new 'harvest prediction' dashboard increased from 1.8% to 2.1%. This represents a 16.7% relative increase in churn. While the feature intended to build trust through transparency, early data suggests it may be driving frustration. Immediate validation is required before the upcoming planting cycle.

### Three Distinct Hypotheses

**Hypothesis 1: The "False Precision" Frustration (Data Accuracy)**
The prediction algorithm failed to account for the recent heavy rainfall, generating overly optimistic yield forecasts. Farmers relying on these numbers for restaurant supply commitments faced immediate operational risk. When reality diverged from the dashboard, trust eroded faster than if no data had been shown at all.
*   *Indicator:* High correlation between churn and users who viewed predictions for crops most affected by rain (leafy greens).

**Hypothesis 2: Cognitive Overload & Interface Confusion (UX Failure)**
The new dashboard introduces complex visualizations without sufficient onboarding. Farmers, who typically rely on simple historical averages, are misinterpreting probability ranges as guarantees. The confusion creates anxiety rather than clarity, prompting users to abandon the platform rather than risk making a mistake based on misunderstood data.
*   *Indicator:* High drop-off rates on the dashboard page and increased support tickets containing keywords like "confusing," "how to read," or "wrong numbers."

**Hypothesis 3: Premature Exposure to Volatility (Timing Mismatch)**
The feature was launched during a high-volatility weather window. By surfacing real-time fluctuation data during an unstable period, the dashboard highlighted risk rather than mitigating it. Farmers who previously felt stable are now seeing daily prediction swings, causing panic and a perception that the system (or their crops) is out of control.
*   *Indicator:* Churn concentrated among long-term, previously stable users who logged in frequently during the rainy week of Oct 7–14.

### Validation Plan: Top Hypothesis (Data Accuracy)
Given the severity of providing incorrect operational data, we must first rule out **Hypothesis 1**. If the numbers are wrong, UX improvements are irrelevant.

**Objective:** Determine if churned users disproportionately interacted with inaccurate predictions during the rainy period.
**Timeline:** 5 Days
**Data Sources:** Existing application logs, weather data records, churn registry.

**Day 1-2: Data Segmentation & Querying**
*   Isolate the 9-10 farmers who churned (2.1% of 450) vs. a control group of 50 retained users.
*   Query logs to identify which specific crop predictions each churned user viewed between Oct 3–17.
*   Cross-reference viewed crops with local weather data to flag "high error" predictions (where predicted yield deviated >20% from likely actuals due to rain).

**Day 3: Correlation Analysis**
*   Calculate the exposure rate: What percentage of churned users viewed "high error" predictions vs. the control group?
*   If >60% of churned users viewed flawed data compared to <20% of retained users, Hypothesis 1 is confirmed.

**Day 4: Qualitative Spot-Check**
*   Review support ticket text from the 450-user cohort for mentions of specific crop failures or "rain" combined with "dashboard."
*   Conduct 3 rapid 15-minute calls with recently churned farmers to ask specifically: "Did the harvest numbers match your field reality?"

**Day 5: Recommendation Decision**
*   **If Confirmed:** Recommend immediate feature rollback or a "Data Paused" banner acknowledging weather volatility.
*   **If Refuted:** Pivot immediately to validating Hypothesis 2 (UX) via session replay analysis.

### Immediate Recommendation
Do not rollback yet. Execute the 5-day validation plan. A rollback admits defeat on the product vision; a fix based on data accuracy restores trust. If the algorithm is at fault, we can patch the weather weighting model rather than removing the feature entirely before the planting cycle.

---
*Note: This analysis assumes logs capture individual crop view events; if not, Day 1 will require engineering support to reconstruct user paths.*
*Churn calculation based on 450 exposed users; absolute volume is small (~9-10 users), making qualitative feedback critical alongside log data.*
