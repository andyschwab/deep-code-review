### Core Metrics for KiwiCollectibles: Balancing Seller Growth and Market Health

To determine whether to relax or tighten verification before July, stop measuring raw listing counts. That is a vanity metric in this context, as evidenced by the 40% volume increase yielding only 5% GMV growth. Instead, instrument the following four metrics to answer specific questions about your two-sided market health:

**1. North Star: Verified Seller Sell-Through Rate (STR)**
*   **Definition:** The percentage of items listed by verified sellers that result in a transaction within 30 days.
*   **Why:** This measures real value delivery. If verification attracts serious collectors, their STR should be significantly higher than unverified sellers. If the new 40% volume has a near-zero STR, you are accumulating dead inventory, not growth.
*   **Decision Rule:** If Verified STR < Unverified STR, the badge is failing to signal quality or the process is filtering out active sellers. Tighten criteria or redesign the badge value prop.

**2. The Friction Metric: Verification Funnel Drop-off**
*   **Definition:** The percentage of sellers who start the verification process but do not complete it, segmented by their historical listing volume and average item value.
*   **Why:** You suspect the process is "too cumbersome for serious collectors." This metric proves or disproves that.
*   **Decision Rule:** If high-value sellers (those with >$500 avg item value) drop off at >20%, the process is too hard for your target supply. Relax steps for high-value applicants or add a concierge lane. If low-value sellers drop off, the friction is working as intended.

**3. The Demand Signal: Buyer Click-Through Rate (CTR) by Badge Status**
*   **Definition:** The ratio of clicks to impressions for verified vs. unverified listings in search results.
*   **Why:** This tells you if buyers care. If buyers ignore the badge (CTR is equal), the verification effort is wasted. If they heavily prefer verified items but those items aren't selling, the issue is price or availability, not trust.
*   **Decision Rule:** If Verified CTR is not >1.5x Unverified CTR, buyers do not perceive the badge as valuable. Do not tighten requirements; instead, improve badge visibility or buyer education.

**4. The Quality Guardrail: GMV per Active Listing**
*   **Definition:** Total GMV divided by the number of active listings (not total cumulative listings).
*   **Why:** This normalizes your stagnant GMV against the bloated inventory. It reveals if the *quality* of the average listing is diluting the marketplace.
*   **Decision Rule:** If this metric drops below your pre-verification baseline, the new inventory is toxic to market efficiency. Pause new seller onboarding until the backlog clears or delist stale items.

### Recommended Immediate Actions (Before July)

1.  **Run a Cohort Analysis:** Compare the 30-day STR of sellers verified in the last month against those verified 6 months ago. If the new cohort underperforms, your quality bar has slipped.
2.  **Audit the Drop-off:** Identify the top 20 sellers who abandoned verification. Manually contact them to ask why. If they cite "too much effort," you have qualitative proof to simplify the flow for high-volume sellers immediately.
3.  **Delist Stale Inventory:** If items listed by new verified sellers have not sold in 45 days, automatically hide them from search. This protects buyer experience without changing verification rules.

**Verdict Path:** Do not relax requirements yet. The data suggests you have successfully attracted volume but failed to ensure quality or buyer engagement. Tighten the *definition* of a "verified" seller to require a minimum historical sales record or item value threshold, rather than just identity confirmation. This shifts the badge from "real person" to "serious collector," aligning supply with your GMV goals.

*Note: All benchmarks above are derived from your own pre-verification baseline; no external industry averages were used. Ensure your analytics tool tracks user IDs across sessions to accurately calculate sell-through rates.*
