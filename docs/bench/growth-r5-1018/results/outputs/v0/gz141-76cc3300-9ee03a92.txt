**Interpretation of Conflicting Metrics**
1.  **The Volume Leak:** A 9% drop in transaction volume during a "free item" promotion is a critical warning sign. In a successful acquisition or activation test, volume should rise or remain flat. This decline indicates a bottleneck. If stands were overwhelmed by the complexity of adding coconut water to savory orders, service speed likely decreased, causing customers to abandon lines or return later (outside the measurement window).
2.  **The AOV Illusion:** The 14% lift in Average Order Value is driven by the inclusion of the free coconut water (if counted as $0 revenue but increasing item count) or, more likely, customers trading up to larger savory items to "qualify" or maximize the deal. However, if total revenue (Volume × AOV) decreased due to the volume drop, the AOV gain is a vanity metric masking a loss in total throughput.
3.  **The Net Impact Question:** The decision to roll out depends entirely on whether **Total Revenue** increased.
    *   *Scenario A (Operational Failure):* If the stands served fewer customers because the process was too slow, rolling this out nationally will degrade brand experience and reduce total revenue despite higher AOV.
    *   *Scenario B (Timing Shift):* If customers simply shifted purchases to non-peak hours or days outside the March 12–19 window, the weekly volume drop is a measurement artifact, and the promotion may still be viable.

1.  **Hourly Transaction Velocity:** Compare transactions per hour (11:00–14:00) in targeted vs. control zones.

    *   *Reading Rule:* If net revenue is negative despite the AOV lift, the promotion destroys value. The 14% lift is insufficient to offset the 9% volume loss.
