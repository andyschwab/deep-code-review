### New Market Launch Measurement Framework: GoShare (Denver, Portland, Seattle)

**North Star Metric:** Weekly Active Users completing a **First Successful Unlock** in the new cities.
*Reject vanity metrics like total app downloads or map views; value is only delivered when a user drives.*

**1. The Funnel (Read Bottom-Up: Retention & Activation First)**
Do not pour marketing spend into acquisition until activation is proven. Diagnose the leak in this specific order for each city:
1.  **Retention of Core Action:** Of those who unlocked a car, did they book a second ride within 14 days? (If no, the product/market fit is weak regardless of onboarding).
2.  **Activation (The "Aha" Moment):** Conversion rate from **Identity Verified** → **First Unlock**.
    *   *Hypothesis Check:* Does "Instant Verification" correlate with higher unlock rates compared to "Delayed Verification"?
3.  **Acquisition:** Cost per Verified User (not Cost per Download).

**2. Event Taxonomy (Instrument Only What Answers a Named Question)**
Track these four specific events with the listed properties. Do not instrument "browsing" unless it leads to a drop-off analysis.

| Event Name | Question Answered | Required Properties |
| :--- | :--- | :--- |
| `app_installed` | Baseline volume | `city`, `install_date`, `campaign_source` |
| `id_doc_uploaded` | Friction at entry | `user_id` (pseudonymous), `city`, `timestamp` |
| `id_verified` | **The Critical Split** | `user_id`, `city`, `verification_latency_minutes`, `status` (instant/delayed/rejected) |
| `vehicle_unlocked` | **Value Delivered** | `user_id`, `city`, `time_since_install_hours`, `vehicle_type` |

**3. The Identifier Seam**
To track the journey from install to unlock across sessions, you must implement a **stable, pseudonymous user_id** persisted on the device and sent with every event. This differs from system logs (which avoid per-user cardinality); here, the user journey is the product. Ensure PII (driver's license images) is never sent to the analytics tool; route storage and consent to your privacy compliance protocol.

**4. Stage-Specific Instrumentation (MVP Phase)**
Since this is a new market launch (MVP stage), ignore revenue optimization or referral loops for now. Focus entirely on the **Install → Unlock** conversion.
*   **Cohort Analysis:** Segment users by `city` and `verification_latency_minutes` (e.g., <5 mins vs >24 hours).
*   **The Drop-off Query:** Calculate the % of users who `id_doc_uploaded` but did not `vehicle_unlocked` within 48 hours, broken down by verification speed.

**5. Experiment Rigor for the June 15th Decision**
You are comparing new cities against a historical baseline (60% conversion). This is an observational comparison, not a randomized A/B test, so beware of confounders (e.g., different user demographics in Seattle vs. the home base).
*   **Primary Metric:** Conversion rate (`id_verified` → `vehicle_unlocked`).
*   **Guardrail Metric:** Average `verification_latency_minutes`. If conversion rises but latency spikes due to manual review bottlenecks, the model is not scalable.
*   **Decision Rule (Go/No-Go):**
    *   **GO:** If conversion rate > 40% (adjusted for new market variance) AND median verification time < 30 mins.
    *   **NO-GO / PAUSE:** If conversion rate < 20% OR if >50% of churn occurs *after* instant verification (indicating the problem is interest/map, not docs).
    *   **FIX & RETRY:** If churn is highly correlated with `verification_latency_minutes` > 2 hours.

**6. This Week's Steps**
1.  **Implement the 4-event taxonomy** above with the pseudonymous `user_id` by May 15th to ensure data collection starts on Day 1 (May 20th).
2.  **Build the "Verification Latency vs. Unlock" dashboard** segmented by city. Set an alert if `id_verified` users do not trigger `vehicle_unlocked` within 48 hours at a rate > 60%.
3.  **Prepare the June 10th Readout:** Pre-commit to reviewing the cumulative conversion data on June 10th (allowing 21 days of data). Do not peek at daily significance; the sample size in new cities will be small, and early noise will lead to false "No-Go" decisions.

*Note: The 60% historical benchmark is for context only; the decision threshold (40%) accounts for typical new-market cold starts. All specific conversion figures must be pulled from your live analytics dashboard once launched.*
