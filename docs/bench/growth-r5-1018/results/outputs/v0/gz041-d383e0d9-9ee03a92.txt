**To:** CFO, Revenue Management Team
**From:** Head of Product
**Date:** April 6
**Subject:** Decision on Baggage Weight Display Test (March 12 – April 5)

**Executive Summary**
The A/B test comparing explicit weight allowance display (Variant) vs. price-only (Control) yielded a **1.1 percentage point lift** in baggage attach rates (22.0% → 23.1%). However, based on the sample size of 8,400 and the observed variance, this result is **statistically inconclusive** and must be treated as **actionable noise**, not a validated win. We recommend **killing the feature for now** and reallocating engineering resources to higher-confidence initiatives.

**Statistical Reliability Assessment**
While the variant showed a directional increase, the confidence interval is too wide to rule out random chance. With only 8,400 participants split between two groups, the test lacked the power to detect a lift of this magnitude with 95% confidence.
*   **The Risk of Peeking/Early Stop:** The test ran for 24 days during low seasonal demand. Stopping or evaluating based on the current snapshot without a pre-committed sample size calculation invites false positives.
*   **False Positive Probability:** A 1.1% lift on this sample size likely falls within the margin of error. Declaring this a "win" and rolling it out risks implementing a change that provides zero revenue impact while incurring permanent maintenance costs across all markets.
*   **Segmentation Signal:** The hypothesis regarding leisure vs. business travelers is plausible but **unverified**. The current aggregate data masks any potential subgroup effects. Without a pre-planned segmentation analysis, drilling down now would constitute "data dredging," increasing the likelihood of finding spurious correlations.

**Financial & Operational Implication**
Rolling this out to Croatian and Italian routes requires engineering effort to modify the booking flow. Given the statistical uncertainty:
1.  **Expected Value:** The probability that the true lift is zero (or negative) is high. The expected revenue gain does not justify the deployment cost and technical debt.
2.  **Opportunity Cost:** Engineering cycles spent on this low-confidence feature are diverted from tests with clearer signals or core reliability work.

**Recommendation**
**Do not roll out.** Kill the feature in its current form. The data does not support a causal claim that clarity drives attachment at this scale.

**Next Steps (This Week)**
1.  **Halt Deployment:** Confirm with Engineering that the variant code is not merged into the main branch for the Croatia/Italy release.
2.  **Recompute Sample Size:** Before re-testing, calculate the required sample size to detect a 1.1% lift with 80% power. If the required N exceeds our quarterly traffic volume, we must either:
    *   Raise the Minimum Detectable Effect (aim for a larger lift, e.g., 3%+), or
    *   Accept that this specific micro-copy change is not a primary growth lever.
3.  **Qualitative Validation:** Instead of a broad A/B test, run targeted user interviews with first-time flyers on Slovenian routes to verify if "weight clarity" is actually a friction point, or if price sensitivity is the sole driver.

**Conclusion**
We cannot justify engineering spend on a trend that may be statistical noise. We will revisit this hypothesis only after securing a larger sample size or qualitative evidence confirming the friction point.
