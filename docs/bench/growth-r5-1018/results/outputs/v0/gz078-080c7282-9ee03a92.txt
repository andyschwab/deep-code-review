# Measurement Strategy: Balancing Parking Efficiency and Commercial Vitality
**To:** City Council Transportation Committee
**From:** Data Lead, Vienna SmartPark
**Date:** [Current Date]
**Subject:** Proposal to Correlate Parking Turnover with Local Economic Health (Innere Stadt Pilot)

## 1. North Star Metric: "Value-Adjusted Turnover"
We reject "total parking sessions" or "average duration" as standalone success metrics, as they are vanity measures that can rise while local commerce suffers. Instead, we define our North Star as **Value-Adjusted Turnover**: the rate of parking space utilization weighted by the likelihood of a commercial transaction occurring.
*   **Input Metrics:** (a) Sensor-detected occupancy duration distribution; (b) Aggregate footfall counts near sensors; (c) District-wide daily retail card transaction volume (aggregated, non-PII).

## 2. Event Taxonomy & Data Correlation (Privacy-First)
We will instrument only events that answer specific questions, avoiding "track everything" noise. No individual driver or shopper will be identified.
*   **Event A: "Session Duration Bucket"**
    *   *Question:* What percentage of parking sessions fall below the hypothesized 45-minute "purchase threshold"?
    *   *Properties:* `duration_bucket` (<15m, 15-44m, 45-90m, >90m), `time_of_day`, `day_of_week`.
    *   *Privacy:* Sensor IDs are hashed; no license plate retention.
*   **Event B: "Aggregate Commercial Pulse"**
    *   *Question:* Does a shift in short-duration parking correlate with a drop in local retail revenue?
    *   *Properties:* `hourly_transaction_count` (total district), `hourly_transaction_value` (total district), `sensor_occupancy_rate`.
    *   *Source:* Aggregated data from local banks/payment processors or anonymized POS aggregates provided by the Merchant Association. **No individual transaction data is accessed.**

## 3. Analysis Method: Bottom-Up Retention of "Commercial Viability"
We apply the AARRR framework bottom-up, prioritizing **Retention of Commercial Viability** over Acquisition (cars entering the district).
*   **Step 1 (Retention):** Analyze the trend of sessions <45 minutes vs. total sessions since March 20th. If short sessions are rising *and* aggregate retail revenue is flat or falling, the hypothesis of harm is supported.
*   **Step 2 (Activation):** Correlate peak "short stay" hours with dips in hourly transaction volumes. If high turnover hours show *higher* transaction counts (more unique customers), efficiency is driving growth. If they show lower values, we have a leak.
*   **Step 3 (Acquisition):** Only if Steps 1 and 2 show negative correlation do we consider restricting acquisition (raising prices to deter short stays).

## 4. Proposed Experiment: Dynamic Pricing Pilot (Pre-Committed Design)
To decide on expansion or pricing adjustments, we propose a controlled A/B test rather than a district-wide change.
*   **Design:** Split Innere Stadt zones into two groups for 4 weeks.
    *   *Control:* Current pricing.
    *   *Treatment:* Slight price increase for stays <30 minutes; discount for stays >60 minutes.
*   **Primary Metric:** Change in `hourly_transaction_value` in Treatment vs. Control.
*   **Guardrail Metric:** `average_search_time` (must not exceed 9 minutes, our pre-pilot baseline).
*   **Stopping Rule:** Pre-committed sample size of 28 days. No peeking at daily significance. Analysis occurs only after the period ends to avoid false positives from daily volatility.
*   **Decision Threshold:** If `hourly_transaction_value` drops >5% in Treatment while `average_search_time` remains <9 mins, we reject the pricing change. If value rises or stays flat while search time improves, we expand.
