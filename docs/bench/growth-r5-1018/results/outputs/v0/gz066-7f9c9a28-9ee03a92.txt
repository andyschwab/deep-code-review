### Retention Window Framework for RioNews

To align content release with user habits and satisfy the editorial director, do not select a single arbitrary window. Instead, define retention through three distinct lenses that answer specific business questions. This approach separates "habit strength" from "content cadence."

**1. The Habit Lens (24-Hour Return)**
*   **Definition:** A user is retained if they open the app within 24 hours of their last session.
*   **Question Answered:** "Is the app part of the user's daily routine?"
*   **Context:** Your drop from 40% to 28% here indicates the March 10th push change broke a **daily habit loop**. For a news product, this is the primary health metric for "stickiness." If users do not return daily, they are likely switching to competitors or social media for news.
*   **Verdict on 7 AM Push:** If the 24-hour rate dropped immediately after moving to 7 AM, the timing may be missing the actual commute window (e.g., users read on the train at 8:15 AM, not at home at 7:00 AM) or the frequency is causing fatigue.

**2. The Cadence Lens (7-Day Return)**
*   **Definition:** A user is retained if they open the app at least once within a rolling 7-day window.
*   **Question Answered:** "Does the user consider us their primary news source for the week?"
*   **Context:** This captures the "weekend-only" user you asked about. A user who only reads on Saturday and Sunday is **retained** under this definition but **churned** under the 24-hour definition.
*   **Logic for Weekenders:** For editorial planning, classify these users as "Weekly Digest Consumers." They do not need breaking news pushes at 7 AM on Tuesdays; they need a curated "Week in Review" push on Friday afternoon. Do not count them as failed daily retainers; count them as a separate cohort with a different content need.

**3. The Decay Lens (Cohort Heatmap)**
*   **Definition:** Measure the percentage of users returning on Day 1, Day 2, Day 3, etc., up to Day 14, for users who installed or were active before March 10th vs. after.
*   **Question Answered:** "How quickly does the value of a single visit decay?"
*   **Context:** News has a short half-life. If Day 1 retention is high but Day 2 collapses, your content is not giving a reason to return tomorrow. If the curve is flat but low, the product isn't hooking anyone initially.

**Decision Logic for the Editorial Director**

Do not ask "Which window is correct?" Ask "What behavior are we trying to drive?"

*   **If the goal is Ad Impressions/Volume:** Prioritize the **24-Hour Window**. The current 28% rate is a critical failure state. The 7 AM push is likely misaligned with the actual "first check" time. Action: A/B test push times against actual open rates (e.g., 6:30 AM vs. 8:00 AM) rather than assuming commute start times.
*   **If the goal is Subscriber Loyalty/Brand:** Prioritize the **7-Day Window**. A user returning 3 times a week is valuable. Action: Segment users by "Daily" vs. "Weekly" patterns. Stop sending daily pushes to Weekly users (reducing churn risk) and send them high-value summaries instead.

Segment your March 10th cohort into "Daily Readers" (open 5+ days/week) and "Casual Readers" (open 1-2 days/week). Calculate the 24-hour retention drop for *each* group separately. If the drop is isolated to Daily Readers, the 7 AM push specifically disrupted the morning routine. If the drop is universal, the change may have affected deliverability or overall relevance.
