### Success Metrics Framework for LexiScan General Release

**North Star Metric: Verified Time-to-Final-Approval**
Reject "Time Saved" as your primary metric; it incentivizes speed over safety and ignores the cost of fixing AI errors. Instead, track **Verified Time-to-Final-Approval**: the elapsed time from document upload to the partner's final signature, *only* for documents where the AI's suggestions required zero critical overrides. This rises only when the tool delivers genuine value (speed without rework).

**Input Metrics (The Efficiency/Accuracy Balance)**
To move the North Star, track these three paired indicators. Do not view them in isolation:

1.  **Acceptance Rate vs. Critical Override Rate**
    *   *Definition:* Percentage of AI-suggested edits accepted by lawyers versus the percentage of suggestions explicitly rejected or heavily modified.
    *   *The Question:* "Is the AI reducing cognitive load or creating noise?"
    *   *Reading Rule:* If Acceptance Rate > 80% but Critical Override Rate > 15%, you are generating false confidence. High overrides indicate the AI is creating work, not saving it.

2.  **Correction Latency**
    *   *Definition:* The time a lawyer spends reviewing and correcting an AI suggestion compared to the time taken to draft that clause manually (based on your beta baseline).
    *   *The Question:* "Is fixing the AI faster than doing it from scratch?"
    *   *Reading Rule:* If Correction Latency exceeds 40% of the manual baseline time, the feature is a net negative for that document type.

3.  **Final Approval Cycle Count**
    *   *Definition:* The number of back-and-forth versions required before a document is finalized.
    *   *The Question:* "Does the AI introduce new errors that delay final sign-off?"
    *   *Reading Rule:* An increase in cycle count post-AI introduction signals that speed is compromising accuracy, validating partner concerns.

**Qualitative Feedback Loops**
Integrate these directly into the workflow to contextualize the numbers:
*   **The "Why" Tag on Overrides:** When a lawyer rejects an AI suggestion, require a one-click tag (e.g., "Legally Incorrect," "Wrong Tone," "Missed Context"). This turns qualitative frustration into quantifiable error categories.
*   **Weekly "False Positive" Review:** A 15-minute sync with power users to review the top 5 most frequent override reasons. This identifies systemic model drift before it impacts retention.

**Instrumentation Plan (MVP Stage)**
Do not instrument everything. Track only events that answer the questions above:
*   `suggestion_viewed` (properties: `suggestion_id`, `clause_type`)
*   `suggestion_accepted` (properties: `suggestion_id`, `time_to_decision`)
*   `suggestion_overridden` (properties: `suggestion_id`, `override_reason_tag`, `time_spent_editing`)
*   `document_finalized` (properties: `total_cycles`, `total_elapsed_time`)

**This Week's Steps**
1.  **Define the Baseline:** Calculate the average `manual_draft_time` and `cycle_count` from your beta data for the top 3 contract types. Without this internal baseline, any new metric is `UNVERIFIED`.
2.  **Implement the "Why" Tag:** Update the UI to force a reason tag on every override before the September 20th launch. Without this, you cannot distinguish between a helpful nudge and a dangerous error.
3.  **Set the Guardrail:** Establish a hard stop rule: if `Critical Override Rate` exceeds 20% for any clause type in the first week of GA, automatically disable AI suggestions for that clause type pending model retraining.

*Note: Do not compare your retention or accuracy rates to industry benchmarks; compare them strictly to your own beta baseline. Any external "good" percentage is invented noise.*
*Ensure user identifiers in these events are pseudonymous to comply with legal data privacy standards.*
