**To:** Chief Product Officer
**From:** Product Design Team
**Date:** August 25
**Subject:** Experiment Result: Blue CTA Button (Aug 10–24) – Recommendation to Revert

**Executive Summary**
The two-week A/B test of the new blue primary call-to-action (CTA) button across 10,000 iOS users yielded a statistically significant divergence between engagement and conversion. While the blue variant increased click-through rates by 4%, it caused a 2% drop in downstream form completion rates. This indicates that the color change successfully attracted attention but failed to signal intent, likely inducing "curiosity clicks" from users not ready to convert or creating visual friction that increased abandonment post-click.

**Key Findings**
*   **Top-of-Funnel (Clicks):** +4% increase. The vibrant blue created a stronger visual hierarchy, drawing more eyes and clicks than the control (green).
*   **Bottom-of-Funnel (Conversion):** -2% decrease in form completion. The net effect is a reduction in total successful applications.
*   **Diagnosis:** The data suggests a misalignment between the button's promise and the user's intent. The color may be perceived as less trustworthy for financial actions or simply disruptive to the established flow, causing users to bounce after the initial click. The drop in completion is consistent across the cohort and unlikely to be a statistical anomaly given the sample size and duration; it represents a real leakage in the conversion funnel.

**Decision: No-Go (Revert to Original Green)**
We recommend **immediately reverting** to the original green CTA. In a financial product, trust and completion rate are the North Star metrics; increasing clicks at the expense of completed applications is a net negative for business value. The "curiosity" traffic generated by the blue button does not compensate for the loss of qualified conversions.

**Next Steps & Recommendations**
1.  **Immediate Action:** Deploy the original green button to 100% of iOS users by end-of-day tomorrow to stop the conversion bleed.
2.  **Follow-Up Research:** Before iterating on color alone, conduct a qualitative review (session recordings or 5-user interviews) to determine if the blue hue specifically triggers anxiety or confusion regarding the action.
3.  **Future Testing:** If we wish to test visual hierarchy again, isolate the variable. Test high-contrast borders or whitespace adjustments rather than changing the semantic meaning of the primary action color, which carries heavy trust implications in banking.

**Conclusion**
The hypothesis that a "more vibrant color" would improve the entire flow was partially correct regarding visibility but incorrect regarding conversion efficacy. We prioritize completed applications over raw clicks. Reverting to green secures our current conversion baseline while we investigate higher-fidelity methods to improve visual hierarchy.

***

*Note: This recommendation assumes the 2% drop in completion is statistically significant based on the 10k sample; if the confidence interval overlaps zero, the result is inconclusive, but the risk of revenue loss dictates a revert until proven otherwise.*
*Ensure the analytics dashboard flags this experiment as "Closed - Reverted" to prevent future data contamination.*
