**Recommendation: Do not adopt either Total Transaction Volume or Average Order Value (AOV) as your primary North Star metric.**

Both proposed metrics are vanity indicators in your current context. Volume rises regardless of whether you lose money on every sale, and your 15% rise in logistics costs proves that scaling volume is currently destroying value, not creating it. AOV ignores the frequency of purchase and the cost to fulfill; a high AOV item with massive shipping overhead can still be a loss leader. Since you explicitly stated you lack granular profitability data per category, neither metric can safely drive daily decisions or investor forecasts without risking further margin erosion.

**Your North Star must be: "Weekly Active Sellers Completing Profitable Transactions."**

This metric shifts focus from "moving boxes" to "sustainable value." It counts only those artisans who return week-over-week to sell items where the margin covers the logistics cost. If this number rises, your marketplace is healthy; if it falls, you are growing hollow.

**Why this fixes your specific problems:**
1.  **Stops the bleeding:** It forces the team to validate unit economics before pushing volume. If logistics costs eat the margin, the transaction does not count toward the North Star, immediately signaling the problem to leadership and logistics partners.
2.  **Aligns incentives:** Operations stops optimizing for raw throughput and starts optimizing for efficient fulfillment that allows artisans to remain active.
3.  **Investor clarity:** Instead of showing "25,000 transactions" (which hides the loss), you report "X active sellers generating sustainable GMV," which is a stronger signal of product-market fit than raw volume.

**Immediate Actions for This Week:**
1.  **Define "Profitable" Proxy:** Since you lack category-level data, establish a conservative threshold immediately (e.g., Order Value > 150k IDR) based on your known average logistics cost + 10% buffer. Treat any transaction below this as "non-core" for the North Star calculation until granular data exists.
2.  **Instrument the Core Event:** Create a single event `seller_completed_profitable_sale` with properties: `seller_id`, `order_value`, `estimated_logistics_cost`, and `is_repeat_weekly`. Do not instrument everything; just this question: "Did the seller make money and come back?"
3.  **Pause Volume Incentives:** Halt any bonuses tied purely to transaction count. Tie Q1 incentives to the retention rate of sellers hitting the profitable threshold.

**Note:** Your assumption that "volume leads to better unit economics" is currently false given the 15% cost increase; scale is amplifying your losses until the unit economics are fixed.
**Note:** Do not attempt to benchmark this against regional competitors; compare your weekly active profitable sellers only against your own baseline from last month.
