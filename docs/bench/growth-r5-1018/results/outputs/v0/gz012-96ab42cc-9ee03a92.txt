**Executive Summary: Social Accountability Feature Long-Term Impact**

**Verdict:** The data indicates a **novelty-driven activation spike** rather than a sustainable habit driver. While the feature successfully lifts early retention (45% vs. 28% at 30 days), the convergence of curves after month four signals that the social mechanic fails to compound value over time. For the 2025 roadmap, **do not double down** on expanding social features until the post-month-four churn mechanism is isolated and fixed.

**Reading the Divergence**
The gap between your treatment and control groups represents the "activation lift" provided by novelty and initial social pressure. When this gap narrows significantly after month four, it means the **retention rate of the engaged cohort has dropped below that of the control group** during months 2–4, or simply reverted to the baseline. This is a classic "leaky bucket" scenario: the feature acquires/activates users who would otherwise have churned immediately, but it does not improve the underlying product value enough to keep them once the novelty of social accountability fades. If the feature drove sustainable habits, the curves would remain parallel or diverge further as the network effect compounded.

**Critical Signals to Investigate (Immediate Actions)**
To confirm the root cause before the board meeting, analyze these three specific signals using your existing data:

1.  **Cohort Decay Rate (Months 2–4):** Calculate the week-over-week retention *slope* for the treatment group specifically between weeks 5 and 16. Compare this slope to the control group. If the treatment slope is steeper (more negative), the feature is creating a "hangover" effect where users feel burdened by social obligations once the initial excitement passes.
2.  **Feature Usage vs. Retention Correlation:** Segment the treatment group by *continued* social engagement. Do users who stop interacting with the social feature after month one churn at the same rate as the control group? If yes, the feature is a one-time activation trigger, not a retention engine.
3.  **The "Silent Churn" Check:** Verify if the treatment group is merely delaying churn (extending lifetime value slightly) or if they are churning with higher dissatisfaction (e.g., lower NPS or higher uninstall rates post-churn). A novelty effect often masks delayed dissatisfaction.

**Recommendation for 2025 Roadmap**
Do not allocate significant engineering resources to expanding social features based on the 30-day metric alone. The current data suggests such an investment would increase acquisition/activation costs without solving the core retention problem. Instead, pivot the Q1 2025 strategy to **isolate the month-four drop-off**. Run a qualitative study or targeted survey on users who churned between months 2 and 4 to understand if the social aspect became a friction point. Only consider scaling social features if you can identify a sub-segment where the curve *does not* converge.
