**Strategic Recommendation for Q3 Budget Allocation**

**Decision:** Shift 60% of the Q3 paid social budget from pure acquisition optimization to **retention and high-LTV prospecting**. Optimize ad sets for **predicted Lifetime Value (pLTV)** rather than lowest Acquisition Cost (CAC).

**Rationale:** Your current unit economics show an LTV:CAC ratio of 4:1 (1,800 SEK / 450 SEK). While healthy, rising CAC indicates diminishing returns on broad prospecting. In a constrained economic climate, cash flow preservation is critical; acquiring low-value customers at a lower upfront cost often yields negative long-term margins if they do not repeat. Furniture is a high-consideration, low-frequency purchase; therefore, daily decisions must prioritize the quality of the acquired user over the cheapness of the click. Optimizing for pLTV aligns daily spend with the 1,800 SEK value ceiling, ensuring you pay more only for users statistically likely to generate that return.

**Reading the Funnel (Retention First):**
Do not pour budget into acquisition until retention is verified. Before scaling spend, confirm that customers acquired in Q1/Q2 are returning or referring. If the core action (furniture purchase) is not retained or does not lead to secondary high-margin purchases (e.g., textiles, decor), lowering CAC is vanity. The North Star for the marketing team should shift from "Cost per Purchase" to **"90-Day Return Rate"** or **"Contribution Margin per Cohort."**

**Data Gaps Skewing Attribution:**
1.  **Cross-Device & Offline Conversion Tracking:** Furniture research often happens on mobile, but purchases may occur on desktop or in-showroom. If your pixel only tracks last-click web conversions, you are undervaluing top-funnel brand campaigns and overvaluing retargeting.
2.  **True LTV Horizon:** A calculated LTV of 1,800 SEK implies a time horizon. If this figure is based on <12 months of data for a category with a 3-year repurchase cycle, it is `UNVERIFIED` and likely understated. You need cohort-based LTV curves, not averages.
3.  **Incrementality:** Your model likely attributes sales to ads that would have happened organically (brand search). Without holdout groups, you cannot distinguish between *assisted* and *driven* revenue.

**Accounting for Seasonality:**
Furniture purchases in Sweden peak in late summer (pre-autumn) and pre-Christmas. Q3 includes the ramp-up.
*   **Action:** Normalize Q3 performance against Q3 historical baselines, not Q2. Do not judge a July campaign by June's conversion rates.
*   **Adjustment:** Apply a seasonality index to your pLTV predictions. A customer acquired in August may have a higher immediate propensity to buy than one acquired in May, altering the optimal bid cap.

**This-Week Steps:**
1.  **Segment Ad Sets:** Split current campaigns into "Broad/Low CAC" and "Lookalike/High pLTV." Cap bids on the former; scale the latter until marginal CAC reaches 600 SEK (maintaining a 3:1 ratio buffer).
2.  **Define Guardrails:** Set a hard stop if the 30-day payback period exceeds 40% of the average order value. If cash flow tightens, this tripwire triggers an immediate budget freeze on prospecting.
3.  **Instrument Cohort Query:** Run a query comparing retention rates of customers acquired via "Low CAC" audiences vs. "High Intent" audiences over the last 6 months. If the latter shows >20% higher retention, reallocate the remaining 40% budget immediately.

*Note: Ensure all user identifiers used for pLTV modeling are pseudonymized and comply with GDPR before feeding data into ad platforms.*
*Verify your LTV calculation includes gross margin, not just revenue, to prevent optimizing for unprofitable volume.*
