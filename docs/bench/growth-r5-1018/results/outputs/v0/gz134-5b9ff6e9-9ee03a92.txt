**Decision: Do not trust the current allocation as a definitive win; restart with a uniform split test for Q2 planning.**

The six-week run using dynamic allocation (multi-armed bandit) optimized for immediate reward but failed as a rigorous experiment for causal inference. Because the algorithm shifted 60% of traffic to Creative C and only 10% to Creative A, the sample sizes are unbalanced. This imbalance invalidates direct statistical comparisons between variants, explaining why confidence intervals for B and D overlap significantly. You cannot determine if B and D failed due to poor creative quality or simply lack of exposure data. Furthermore, the "8% improvement" is an aggregate metric conflating the algorithm's exploitation phase with actual creative lift; it does not isolate the specific performance delta of Creative C against a control under equal conditions.

Regarding seasonality: Since the test ran through the late December holiday period, the baseline and variant performance are likely inflated by seasonal demand. Without a concurrent control group held out from the dynamic shifts (which was not described), you cannot disentangle the "holiday effect" from the "creative effect." Assuming the algorithm minimized regret is plausible for revenue, but dangerous for learning *why* a creative works. If Creative C relies on holiday-specific urgency, it may fail in Q2.

**Recommended Action Plan for Q2 Launch:**

1.  **Run a Fixed-Horizon A/B/n Test:** Restart the test with a **uniform traffic split** (25% each) for a pre-committed duration or sample size. Calculate the required sample size based on your Q2 projected traffic and the minimum detectable effect (MDE) you care about (e.g., a 5% lift in CTR). Do not peek at results before the end date to avoid false positives.
2.  **Define Primary and Guardrail Metrics:**
    *   **Primary:** Click-Through Rate (CTR) per creative.
    *   **Guardrail:** Conversion Rate (CVR) or Cost Per Acquisition (CPA). Ensure the "winning" creative doesn't attract clicks that don't convert.
3.  **Address Seasonality:** If Q2 traffic patterns differ significantly from Q4, treat the previous data as `UNVERIFIED` for Q2 prediction. Use the new uniform test to establish a fresh baseline specific to the non-holiday context.
4.  **Analyze Failure Modes:** Only with equal exposure can you definitively say why B and D failed (e.g., wrong hook, poor visual hierarchy). If they still underperform with adequate data, retire them. If they perform comparably to C, you may have multiple winners to rotate.
