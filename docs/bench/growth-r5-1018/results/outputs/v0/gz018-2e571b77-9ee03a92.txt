### North Star Framework for ServiceHub Leadership Offsite

**Recommended North Star Metric:**
**Weekly Active Users Completing a High-Quality Job (WAU-Q)**
*Definition:* The count of unique homeowners who successfully complete a job with a contractor in a given week, where "High-Quality" is defined as: (1) The job is marked "Completed" by both parties, AND (2) The post-job rating is ≥ 4 stars OR no dispute is filed within 48 hours.

**Why This Over Your Current Candidates:**
*   **Vs. Total Transaction Volume:** Volume is a vanity metric in a two-sided marketplace; it rises even if you are burning cash on bad matches that churn users later. Your dipping satisfaction scores confirm this risk. Volume counts the *action*, not the *value*.
*   **Vs. Net Revenue:** Revenue is a lagging indicator of health, not a driver. Optimizing purely for revenue too early can suppress liquidity (e.g., raising take-rates before supply is dense enough), killing long-term sustainability.
*   **Vs. Frequency of Use:** Home services are often episodic (e.g., plumbing repairs). Forcing frequency misaligns with user intent. Value is delivered when the *need* is met reliably, not when the app is opened often.

**The Logic:**
This metric forces the balance you seek. To grow WAU-Q, the product team must ensure **Supply Availability** (contractors are online and accepting) to enable the match, while simultaneously ensuring **Demand Satisfaction** (the work is done well) to meet the quality threshold. If you maximize matches but quality drops, the metric falls. If you have perfect quality but no matches, the metric stays flat. It is the only single number that requires both sides of the marketplace to function correctly.

**Input Metrics (The Levers):**
To move WAU-Q, track these three inputs weekly:
1.  **Fill Rate:** % of homeowner requests that receive at least one contractor bid within 30 minutes. (Measures supply liquidity).
2.  **Completion Rate:** % of accepted bids that result in a marked "Completed" job. (Measures execution friction).
3.  **Quality Pass Rate:** % of completed jobs meeting the ≥4 star/no-dispute criteria. (Measures match relevance).

**Validation Method: Correlation to Sustainability**
Do not guess; prove the correlation using your existing two years of data before the offsite.
1.  **Cohort Analysis:** Segment users by their first month. Calculate their WAU-Q in Month 1.
2.  **Retention Plot:** Plot Month 1 WAU-Q against Month 6 Retention (did they book again?).
3.  **Revenue Plot:** Plot Month 1 WAU-Q against Lifetime Value (LTV).
4.  **The Test:** If the cohort with the highest WAU-Q also shows the highest retention and LTV, the metric is validated. If "Total Volume" correlates better with churn than retention, you have mathematical proof to discard it.

**Socialization Plan for the Offsite:**
1.  **The "Bad Growth" Story:** Start by showing the last quarter's 15% volume growth alongside the dipping satisfaction scores. Ask: "Did we actually grow, or just get louder?"
2.  **The Definition Vote:** Present the WAU-Q definition. Ask the leadership team: "If this number goes up, but revenue stays flat for a month, are we healthier?" (The answer must be yes for a leading indicator).
3.  **The Commitment:** Assign one owner per Input Metric (Fill Rate, Completion, Quality). No one owns "North Star"; they own the levers that move it.

**This Week's Step:**
Run the historical correlation query described above using your current database logs. If the data shows that users who complete one "High-Quality" job are 3x more likely to return than those who complete a low-rated job, print that chart as the first slide of your presentation. If the data is missing the "dispute" flag, instrument that specific event immediately as `job_dispute_filed` with properties `job_id` and `reason`.

*Note: Do not benchmark these rates against global marketplaces; Cairo's service density and user behavior create a unique baseline. Compare only against your own historical trends.*
*Ensure the "High-Quality" threshold (4 stars) is reviewed quarterly to prevent grade inflation from rendering the metric useless.*
