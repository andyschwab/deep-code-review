### Three Outcome-Based Definitions of Success for SafeHaiti Connect

**1. The "Verified Handshake" Rate (Activation Integrity)**
*Definition:* The percentage of registered volunteers who complete a verified two-way confirmation loop (Task Assigned → Acknowledgement Received) within a defined operational window (e.g., 4 hours), regardless of the number of transmission attempts required.
*Why it matters:* This shifts success from "message sent" (vanity) to "coordination achieved" (value). It explicitly accounts for intermittent connectivity by measuring the system's ability to eventually synchronize, rather than penalizing the initial delay. If the rate is low, the failure is in the protocol's resilience, not just the network.
*Measurement:* Count unique volunteers with a `task_confirmed` event linked to their `task_assigned` ID. Do not count raw SMS logs.

**2. The "Field Execution" Ratio (Retention of Utility)**
*Definition:* The percentage of confirmed tasks that result in a verified field outcome (e.g., "Site Arrived," "Supply Delivered," or "Status Updated") within 24 hours of assignment.
*Why it matters:* This isolates the tool's actual impact on disaster response. A volunteer might confirm receipt (high activation) but fail to act due to confusion or lack of resources. This metric ignores the "click" and measures the physical coordination event. It distinguishes between a communication breakdown and an operational breakdown.
*Measurement:* Track the transition from `status: confirmed` to `status: action_verified`. If data transmission is impossible in real-time, this metric relies on the next successful sync; the timestamp of the *action* (not the sync) is the critical data point.

**3. The "Network Resilience" Cohort (Infrastructure Independence)**
*Definition:* The consistency of the "Verified Handshake" rate across different geographic zones (e.g., Southern Peninsula vs. Port-au-Prince) when normalized for reported network outages.
*Why it matters:* This defines success as "equitable functionality." If the tool works in high-connectivity zones but fails completely in low-connectivity ones, it is not viable for rural deployment. Success is defined as maintaining at least 60% of the baseline handshake rate even in zones with known infrastructure deficits.
*Measurement:* Segment users by `region_id`. Compare the `Verified Handshake` rate of the "low connectivity" cohort against the "high connectivity" cohort. The gap between them is the measure of system fragility.

**Implementation Note for Funding Partners:**
Present these metrics as "Sync-Agnostic Outcomes." Emphasize that the system is designed to queue actions locally and verify them upon the next available connection, making the *time-to-verify* the variable, not the *success-of-verification*.
