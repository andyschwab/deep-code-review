**Deliverable: Early Viability Signals & Diagnosis Plan for NegevTech Investors**

**1. Priority Early Signals (First 48 Hours)**
To predict long-term viability without complex modeling, track these three specific actions occurring within the first two days of signup. Correlation to "power user" status (defined as completing ≥2 transactions in Month 1) should be measured manually against your current cohort:
*   **Second Transaction Completion:** The single strongest predictor. Did the user complete a *second* distinct transaction within 48 hours? This validates recurring utility versus one-off curiosity.
*   **Feature Depth vs. Breadth:** Did the user engage with >1 core feature (e.g., transaction + setting up a recurring rule) versus repeating the same action? Power users typically configure the tool; casual users only execute.
*   **Support/Help Interaction Pattern:** Did the user view help docs *before* failing a transaction, or only after? Proactive education seeking correlates with higher retention than reactive frustration handling.

**2. Distinguishing Product Failure vs. Natural Settling**
Do not assume the drop from 150 to 20 users is "normal excitement fade" without verifying the *cohort behavior*. Apply this diagnostic logic:

*   **Hypothesis A: Product Failure (Bug/Friction)**
    *   *Signal:* High drop-off occurs at the *exact same step* for both Week 1 and Week 2 users (e.g., 90% fail at "Confirm Payment").
    *   *Indicator:* Users who *did* complete the transaction in Week 2 show identical time-to-complete and error rates as Week 1. If the product is broken, the success rate per attempt remains low regardless of "excitement."
    *   *Verdict:* If the conversion rate *per attempt* is flat and low, this is a blockage, not a settling period.

*   **Hypothesis B: Natural Settling (Lack of Recurring Need)**
    *   *Signal:* Week 1 users completed transactions easily but simply had no reason to return. Week 2 users show high success rates *when they try*, but fewer total attempts.
    *   *Indicator:* Look at the "Time Between Transactions" for the 150 Week 1 users. If 130 of them have not returned by day 10, the issue is utility frequency, not bugs.
    *   *Verdict:* If the *ability* to transact is high but the *frequency* is low, the product works but the use case is not habitual.

**3. Immediate Action Plan (Before Month-End Update)**
*   **Segment the 150 Week 1 Users:** Split them into "One-and-Done" (120 users) vs. "Repeaters" (30 users). Manually interview 5 from each group. Ask the "One-and-Dones" specifically: "What event would trigger you to open the app again?" If they cannot name one, the issue is market fit, not bugs.
*   **Instrument the "Second Action" Event:** Ensure your analytics explicitly track `transaction_completed_count` per user ID. Calculate the % of new users who hit count ≥2 within 7 days. This is your leading indicator for the investors, not total signups.
*   **The Investor Metric:** Report the **Week-2 Retention of the Core Action** (20/150 = 13%). Contextualize it: "13% of early users found a recurring need immediately. Our focus for the next month is increasing this to 25% by [specific fix or hypothesis test], rather than acquiring more users."

**Note:** Do not cite industry benchmarks for "good" retention; compare only against your own Week 1 baseline. If you cannot distinguish between "bug" and "no need" via the steps above, label the cause as `UNVERIFIED` in your report until user interviews confirm it.
