# Q3 Referral Incentive A/B Test: Executive Summary for CFO

**Decision Context:** The $10/$10 incentive test (Aug 1–28) increased referral volume (+25% invites) but degraded immediate efficiency (Cost Per Acquired Rider rose from $18 to $24). The hypothesis that volume would lower blended acquisition cost via organic spillover is currently **unverified** by the data.

### 1. The Trade-off: Volume vs. Unit Economics
Present the results not as a failure of volume, but as a shift in the cost structure that requires LTV validation.
*   **The Efficiency Gap:** The immediate CPA increased by 33% ($6 absolute increase). To break even against the control group ($18 CPA), the new cohort must demonstrate a **Lifetime Value (LTV) at least 33% higher** or a significantly higher retention rate to justify the upfront cost.
*   **The Spillover Question:** The anticipated "organic spillover" (secondary referrals or brand lift) is currently an `UNVERIFIED` metric. If the tracking taxonomy does not explicitly link second-degree conversions to the original referrer ID, this value is invisible in the current $24 CPA calculation.

### 2. Forward-Looking Metrics & Report Structure
Do not project LTV using industry benchmarks. Construct the projection strictly from SwiftRide's historical cohort data.

**A. Cohort Retention Curve (The Primary Validator)**
*   **Metric:** Compare Week 1, Week 4, and Week 8 retention of the *Test Cohort* ($10 credit) vs. the *Control Cohort* ($5 credit).
*   **Decision Rule:** If the Test Cohort's retention curve does not sit materially above the Control Cohort's curve by Week 4, the higher incentive is attracting lower-quality users (gaming the system), and the unit economics are fundamentally flawed.

**B. Payback Period Analysis**
*   **Metric:** Calculate the number of rides required for the $24 CPA to be recovered by gross margin.
*   **Formula:** `Break-even Rides = $24 / (Avg Fare × Margin %)`.
*   **Comparison:** Overlay this against the historical average time-to-break-even for the $18 CPA cohort. If the payback period extends beyond the company's cash-flow constraint (e.g., >6 months), the program threatens Q4 liquidity regardless of total volume.

**C. Fraud & Gaming Audit (The "Leak" Check)**
*   **Metric:** Percentage of referred users who complete only the minimum required rides to unlock the credit and then churn (zero organic activity).
*   **Action:** If this rate exceeds the control group by >10%, the incentive structure is being exploited, and the "volume" is vanity.

### 3. Recommended Next Steps (This Week)
1.  **Freeze Full Rollout:** Do not scale the $10/$10 incentive globally. The current read indicates a negative ROI unless LTV proves otherwise.
2.  **Extend Observation Window:** Commit to a fixed read date (e.g., September 28) to capture the full 30-day retention and secondary referral data. Do not peek at interim LTV projections to avoid false positives.
3.  **Instrument Second-Degree Tracking:** Verify that the event taxonomy captures `referral_source_id` on *all* subsequent rides by the referee. Without this, the "spillover" hypothesis cannot be tested.

**Final Call:** Treat the $24 CPA as the new baseline for this segment until the 30-day LTV data confirms a compensating increase in user value. If the 30-day LTV does not exceed the control group's LTV by at least 33%, terminate the $10 incentive and revert to the $5 baseline or test a non-monetary reward.

*Note: All LTV projections must be derived from SwiftRide's internal historical cohort data; no external benchmarks were used. The "spillover" effect remains UNVERIFIED until second-degree attribution is confirmed in the analytics pipeline.*
