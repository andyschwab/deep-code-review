### Data Extraction Plan & Board Recommendation Framework

**1. Immediate Data Extraction (Run these queries today)**
To isolate the root cause before Tuesday, extract the following from your analytics using a stable per-user identifier. Do not rely on aggregates; you need cohort segmentation.

*   **Segmented Retention of Core Action:** Compare the 7-day retention rate of "daily irrigation log completion" for users aged **<40**, **40–55**, and **>55** before vs. after the badge launch.
    *   *Question answered:* Is the drop universal or isolated to the older demographic?
    *   *Reading rule:* If the <40 cohort retention is flat or up while >55 drops, the feature creates a generational divide. If all drop, the UI clutter is likely the culprit, not the gamification logic itself.
*   **Activation Funnel by Age:** Measure the percentage of users in each age bracket who clicked a badge, viewed the leaderboard, and then *successfully logged data* within the same session.
    *   *Question answered:* Does engagement with the new feature correlate with the core action, or does it distract from it?
    *   *Reading rule:* A high click-through on badges but low subsequent logging indicates "vanity engagement" that harms utility.
*   **Session Depth vs. Completion:** Calculate the average number of screens visited per session for each age group pre- and post-launch.
    *   *Question answered:* Are older users getting lost in navigation (high screens, low completion) or leaving early (low screens, low completion)?
*   **Qualitative Triage Sample:** Identify 5 users from the <40 cohort with increased session time and 5 with decreased time. Flag these for immediate phone interviews.
    *   *Question answered:* Quantitative data shows *what* is happening; these calls will reveal *why* younger users might also be frustrated despite higher engagement metrics.

**2. Framing Findings for the Board**
Structure your presentation around **Customer Value**, not feature effort. The board invested in gamification to drive data density; if it reduces the core metric, the strategy must pivot.

*   **The North Star Context:** Open with the change in your North Star Metric (e.g., "Weekly Active Farmers Logging Data"). State clearly: "A 12% drop in completion directly threatens our planting season data models."
*   **The Segmented Reality:** Present the age-segmented retention chart.
    *   *Scenario A (Isolated to >55):* Frame as a "Segmentation Risk." The feature works for growth but alienates your experienced base. Recommendation: **Iterate** by making badges optional or hidden for users >50.
    *   *Scenario B (Universal Drop):* Frame as a "UX Failure." Visual clutter is degrading utility for everyone. Recommendation: **Kill** the visual overlay immediately; retain only the underlying data tracking if it adds value without UI noise.
*   **The Financial/Operational Stake:** Tie the completion rate to the planting season. "Every 1% drop in logging reduces our yield prediction accuracy by X%, impacting farmer trust and future renewals."

**3. This-Week Steps**
1.  **Run the segmented retention query** by age cohort immediately to confirm if the drop is isolated or universal.
2.  **Prepare two mockups** for the board: one with an "Opt-Out" toggle for gamification (if segmented) and one reverting to the pre-badge UI (if universal).
3.  **Schedule 3 farmer calls** (mix of ages) for Monday morning to validate the quantitative hypothesis before the Tuesday meeting.

*Note: All metrics above must be compared to your pre-launch baseline; do not use industry benchmarks for "good" gamification rates as they do not apply to rural agricultural contexts.*
*If your analytics lack age segmentation, you must manually tag recent sessions or rely on the qualitative calls as your primary evidence, labeling the quantitative gap as `UNVERIFIED`.*
