**Executive Summary & Immediate Call**
Do not make the change permanent yet. The current data is `UNVERIFIED` due to seasonal noise and a lack of pre-committed success criteria. The 8% conversion drop is a leading indicator of revenue loss that likely outweighs the margin gain from higher Average Order Value (AOV), but we cannot confirm this without isolating the signal. We must extend the analysis using a "Profit Per Visitor" guardrail rather than AOV alone.

**1. The Core Metric: Profit Per Visitor (PPV)**
Stop evaluating based on AOV or Conversion Rate in isolation. These are input metrics that can move in opposite directions. The only North Star for this decision is **Profit Per Visitor**.
*   **Formula:** `(Conversion Rate × AOV × Gross Margin %) - (Conversion Rate × Shipping Cost Subsidy)`
*   **Why:** AOV rising from $58 to $67 is vanity if the volume of buyers drops enough to reduce total profit. We need to know if the *extra $9 margin per order* covers the *8% fewer orders*.

**2. Separating Signal from Noise (The Analytical Approach)**
Since the test ran during October with "seasonal noise," we cannot trust a simple before/after comparison. We must reconstruct the baseline to isolate the threshold impact.
*   **Action:** Run a cohort analysis comparing the test group (threshold $65) against a holdout group (threshold $50) if one exists. If no holdout exists, compare the **week-over-week trend** of the test period against the **same week-over-week trend** from the previous year (YoY) to strip out seasonality.
*   **The Calculation:**
    1.  Calculate the expected AOV and Conversion Rate for October based on last year's growth trend (the counterfactual).
    2.  Subtract the actual observed metrics from this expected baseline.
    3.  The difference is the true lift/drag caused by the threshold change, not the season.
*   **Verdict Rule:** If the adjusted PPV is negative or within the margin of error (<2% lift), the test fails. Do not assume customers will "adjust"; in e-commerce, friction at checkout is usually permanent unless the value proposition changes.
