**Deliverable: LTV Validation & Cash Flow Logic for CFO**

To validate your lifetime value (LTV) model against actual cash flow and inform the Q3 budget, calculate these three specific metrics using your existing signup (1,200) and revenue data:

1.  **Cohort-Specific Retention Curve (Months 1–4):**
    *   **Data Required:** Count of the 1,200 premium users who placed an order in Month 1, Month 2, Month 3, and Month 4.
    *   **Calculation:** (Users active in Month $X$) / (Total 1,200 signups).
    *   **Why:** Your assumption relies on survival past Month 3. If retention drops sharply before Month 3, the "5x revenue" multiplier never triggers, and the LTV model is inflated. This is the primary validator.

2.  **Net Revenue Per Cohort (Excluding Referral Distortion):**
    *   **Data Required:** Total cash collected from the premium cohort minus the cash cost of referral bonuses redeemed.
    *   **Calculation:** (Gross Subscription Revenue) – (Cash Value of Referral Bonuses Issued).
    *   **Why:** You noted uncertainty about tracking referral bonuses in the first box. If these are treated as revenue but represent a future liability or immediate cash cost, your "actual cash flow" is overstated. Separate the *subscription fee* from the *referral credit value* to see true cash intake.

3.  **Contribution Margin by Month:**
    *   **Data Required:** Net Revenue (from #2) minus the Cost of Goods Sold (COGS) and fulfillment for that specific month.
    *   **Why:** LTV is useless if the cost to serve exceeds revenue in early months. This tells the CFO if early churners are losing money or just breaking even.

**Interpretation of a 10% Drop in Average Order Value (AOV) in Month 2:**

If AOV drops 10% in Month 2, do not assume this is normal attrition. Interpret it through these two lenses before approving budget:

*   **The "Referral Hangover" Hypothesis:** If the first box included a high-value referral bonus that inflated Month 1 AOV, the Month 2 drop may simply be a reversion to the true baseline price.
    *   *Action:* Recalculate Month 1 AOV *excluding* the monetary value of the referral bonus. If the adjusted Month 1 AOV matches Month 2, the drop is an accounting artifact, not a behavioral problem. The LTV model holds.
*   **The "Downsell/Churn Signal" Hypothesis:** If Month 1 AOV (adjusted) is still higher than Month 2, users are actively spending less.
    *   *Action:* This invalidates the "5x revenue" assumption. Users who reduce spend in Month 2 rarely survive to Month 3.
    *   *CFO Decision Rule:* If the drop is real (not just the referral artifact), **freeze Q3 acquisition budget** for the premium tier. Pouring money into acquisition while Month 2 value leaks destroys cash flow. Fix the Month 2 experience first.
