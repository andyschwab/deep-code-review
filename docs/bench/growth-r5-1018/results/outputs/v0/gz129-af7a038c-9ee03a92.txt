### Behavioral Cohort Breakdown for AncestryIS Retention Strategy

**North Star Metric:** Weekly Active Users completing a "Meaningful Tree Expansion" (defined as adding ≥3 nodes or 1 photo in a session). This rejects vanity metrics like total signups or raw pageviews, focusing strictly on the core value action: building family history.

**Cohort Definitions & Diagnostic Questions**
To differentiate between "stuck" users and "satisfied" small-tree users, segment your 12,000 users into these three behavioral cohorts based on existing data (35% photo upload rate) and proposed event tracking:

1.  **The "Stalled Builders" (Hypothesis: Privacy/Records Friction)**
    *   **Definition:** Users who initiated a tree, reached 2–9 nodes, and have not returned in 14 days.
    *   **Diagnostic Event:** `tree_build_abandoned` (Properties: `node_count`, `last_action_type`, `error_code_if_any`).
    *   **Differentiation Signal:** High drop-off specifically at the "Search Historical Records" step suggests missing data; drop-off at "Invite Family" suggests privacy friction.
    *   **Action:** If >40% of churn occurs at "Search," allocate budget to content acquisition partnerships. If at "Invite," test privacy-first copy or granular sharing controls.

2.  **The "Satisfied Gardeners" (Hypothesis: Value Realized)**
    *   **Definition:** Users with ≤10 nodes who return weekly to view or edit existing nodes/photos but do not expand.
    *   **Diagnostic Event:** `tree_view_session` (Properties: `duration`, `nodes_viewed`, `photos_uploaded`).
    *   **Differentiation Signal:** High retention (>20% Week-4 retention) despite low node count indicates satisfaction. These users value preservation over discovery.
    *   **Action:** Do not force expansion. Monetize via "Memory Book" exports or photo enhancement features rather than premium search access.

3.  **The "Deep Divers" (Hypothesis: Premium Candidates)**
    *   **Definition:** Users who have crossed the 10-node threshold.
    *   **Diagnostic Event:** `tree_milestone_reached` (Property: `node_count=10`).
    *   **Validation:** Track conversion to `subscription_started` within 7 days of this event.
    *   **Action:** If conversion is <5%, the 10-node assumption is flawed; interview this cohort immediately.

**Funnel Analysis Approach (Retention First)**
Do not pour the $8,000 budget into acquisition. Analyze the retention leak in this order:
1.  **Retention:** Are "Stalled Builders" returning after 7 days? If no, the core loop is broken.
2.  **Activation:** What percentage of new signups reach 3 nodes?
3.  **Acquisition:** Only optimize once the above hold.

**Experiment Plan for $8,000 Budget**
Run one pre-committed A/B test targeting the "Stalled Builders" at their highest drop-off step (to be identified via the `tree_build_abandoned` event).
*   **Primary Metric:** % of stalled users adding 1+ node within 7 days of intervention.
*   **Guardrail Metric:** Unsubscribe rate or support ticket volume (to ensure we aren't annoying users).
*   **Design:** Fixed horizon of 21 days. Do not peek at results before day 21 to avoid false positives.
*   **Intervention:** If drop-off is at "Records," test a "Notify Me When Records Found" button (low cost) vs. generic "Search Again" prompt.

3.  **Decide:** If retention for 6–10 node users is flatlining while 11+ users spike, validate the 10-node threshold. If retention is high for small trees, pivot the premium offer away from "more nodes" to "richer media."
