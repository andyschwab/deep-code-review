**Subject:** Recommendation on Homepage Layout Experiment – Do Not Roll Out Globally

**Executive Decision:**
**Do not roll out globally yet.** The observed 40% drop in session duration (from 8 minutes to ~4.8 minutes) is a critical guardrail failure that invalidates the 12% satisfaction lift as a standalone success metric. While the hypothesis of "efficient discovery" is plausible, the magnitude of the time reduction suggests users may be abandoning the app after a single transaction rather than entering a state of deep engagement or exploration. In a music discovery product, time spent often correlates directly with ad inventory, catalog exploration, and long-term retention. A trade-off where users leave 40% faster is unacceptable without proof that retention and lifetime value (LTV) remain stable or improve.

**Analysis of Conflicting Signals:**
The divergence between higher satisfaction and lower duration presents two distinct risks:
1.  **The "Efficiency" Trap:** Users find one song quickly and leave. If the core business model relies on multiple streams per session or ad impressions, this change reduces revenue per user even if the immediate experience feels "better."
2.  **The "Failure" Mask:** The satisfaction survey may be capturing relief at finding *something* quickly after previous frustration, while the drop in duration indicates users are failing to find a second or third song they like, leading to early churn.

Without pre-committed data on retention cohorts or revenue per session, this result is **UNVERIFIED** as a net positive. We cannot assume the satisfaction score predicts long-term value when the behavioral signal (duration) flashes red.

**Immediate Action Plan (Before Friday):**
1.  **Segment by User Maturity:** Request an immediate breakdown of these metrics for **New Users (Day 0-7)** vs. **Power Users (30+ day active)**.
    *   *Hypothesis:* New users may benefit from reduced friction (activation), while Power Users may be frustrated by the removal of deep-dive features (retention risk).
    *   *Decision Rule:* If Power Users show a >20% drop in duration with no offsetting increase in Day-7 retention, restrict the rollout to New Users only.
2.  **Analyze "Songs Streamed per Session":** Duration is a proxy; count is the truth.
    *   If *Songs Streamed* dropped proportionally to duration (e.g., also ~40%), the layout is killing consumption. **Stop.**
    *   If *Songs Streamed* remained flat while duration dropped, the "efficiency" hypothesis holds merit, but we must verify Day-30 retention before proceeding.
3.  **Check Revenue/Ad Impressions:** Calculate revenue per session for the test group. If this metric declined by >15%, the financial cost outweighs the satisfaction gain.

**Recommendation for Friday's Review:**
Propose a **targeted rollout to New Users only** as a compromise, contingent on the "Songs Streamed" data holding steady. For Power Users, revert to the control or initiate a new test that preserves deep-engagement pathways. Do not claim a win on satisfaction alone; frame this as a "high-risk efficiency test" requiring validation of long-term retention before full adoption.

**Note:** The 12% satisfaction lift is based on post-session surveys which can be biased by recency; do not treat this as a causal driver of LTV without correlating it to return visits.
**Note:** Ensure the data engineering team isolates bot traffic, as automated sessions often skew duration metrics downward artificially.
