### Funnel Leakage Diagnosis Framework for DevTools Inc

**Executive Summary:** The data indicates a **mix-shift event**, not necessarily a performance failure. The 35% increase in demo requests coupled with a drop in close rate (22% → 14%) and average deal size ($18k → $12k) suggests the content campaign successfully attracted a new, smaller customer segment that differs from your historical baseline. The "leakage" is likely the natural attrition of large enterprises who no longer fit the profile of the inbound traffic, while the sales team may be applying an enterprise sales motion to SMB prospects (or vice versa).

To isolate the problem and resolve the Marketing vs. Sales conflict, slice the existing data by the following dimensions before collecting new feedback:

**1. Lead Source & Content Asset Correlation**
*   **Action:** Segment demo requests by the specific content asset or campaign channel that generated them.
*   **Hypothesis:** The new campaign attracts "do-it-yourself" users or smaller teams, whereas previous channels attracted enterprise decision-makers.
*   **Decision Rule:** If close rates for *legacy* channels remain at ~22% while the *new* channel sits at ~14% with lower deal sizes, Marketing succeeded in volume but shifted the Ideal Customer Profile (ICP). The issue is not lead quality, but ICP alignment.

**2. Company Size vs. Conversion Matrix**
*   **Action:** Cross-reference demo request volume and close rates against company employee count or revenue band (e.g., <50 employees, 50–200, 200+).
*   **Hypothesis:** The influx is predominantly from the <50 employee segment, which historically has a lower close rate or smaller contract value for your product.
*   **Decision Rule:** If the conversion drop is isolated entirely to the smallest company tier, the sales process may be too heavy for these accounts, or the product pricing/packaging is mismatched. This validates the "different type of prospect" assumption.

**3. Sales Cycle Duration & Stage Velocity**
*   **Action:** Compare the time spent in each funnel stage (Demo → Proposal → Close) for the last two months vs. the prior six months, segmented by deal size.
*   **Hypothesis:** Sales reps are spending enterprise-level effort on SMB leads, causing bottlenecks, or SMB leads are stalling because they lack budget authority.
*   **Decision Rule:** If small deals are taking as long as large deals to close, the sales motion is inefficient for the new mix. If small deals are moving fast but dying at the proposal stage, the issue is pricing or product fit for that segment.

**4. Win/Loss Reason by Segment**
*   **Action:** Aggregate existing CRM loss codes (e.g., "Price," "No Decision," "Competitor") by the new vs. old lead segments.
*   **Hypothesis:** Large deals are being lost to "Competitor" or "Feature Gap," while small deals are lost to "Price" or "Timing."
*   **Decision Rule:** Distinct loss patterns confirm two different buying motions are colliding. Uniform loss reasons across all sizes would indicate a broader product or market issue.

**Immediate Next Step:**
Run the **Company Size vs. Conversion Matrix** immediately. If the data confirms the volume spike is entirely in the small-business segment with proportionally lower close rates, the "miss" is a mathematical inevitability of the new mix, not a failure of execution. The decision then becomes strategic: either pivot the content to re-target enterprises, or adapt the sales motion (and revenue targets) to accommodate a high-volume, lower-touch SMB model.

*Note: Do not calculate a "blended" close rate target; benchmark the new campaign only against its own specific segment history if available, or treat the current 14% as the new baseline for that specific cohort until further data is gathered.*
