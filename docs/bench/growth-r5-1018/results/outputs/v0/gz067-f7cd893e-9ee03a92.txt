### Cohort Segmentation and Comparative Analysis Plan

To isolate the effect of the new cat food line and validate your multi-pet hypothesis, structure your data extraction using the following grouping logic and comparative metrics. This approach prioritizes retention behavior over vanity acquisition numbers.

**1. Grouping Logic (The Segments)**
Do not analyze the January cohort as a single block. Slice the data into a matrix of four primary segments for both the **December Cohort** (baseline, pre-launch) and the **January Cohort** (treatment, post-launch):
*   **Pet Type:** Dog-only vs. Cat-only vs. Multi-pet (mixed or same species).
*   **Acquisition Source:** Organic vs. Campaign-specific (to control for marketing noise).
*   **Delivery Milestone:** Pre-delivery, Post-Delivery 1, Post-Delivery 2, Post-Delivery 3.

**2. Comparative Metrics (The Questions)**
Compare the January segments against the December baseline using these specific rates. Do not use raw counts; use percentages to normalize the cohort sizes.
*   **Retention at Delivery 3:** The percentage of subscribers still active *after* the third shipment. This is your primary indicator of product-market fit for the new line.
    *   *Comparison:* (Jan Cat-Only Retention D3) minus (Dec Cat-Only Retention D3).
*   **Multi-Pet Lift:** The difference in retention between multi-pet and single-pet owners within the same pet-type category.
    *   *Comparison:* (Jan Multi-Pet Retention D3) minus (Jan Single-Pet Retention D3).
*   **Churn Velocity:** The time-to-cancel distribution. Identify if cancellations cluster immediately after Delivery 1 (activation failure) or Delivery 3 (value exhaustion).

**3. Interpreting the "High Spend, Fast Churn" Scenario**
If the data shows dog owners cancel faster but spend more per order, interpret this as a **high-friction, high-value mismatch** rather than a pure success.
*   **The Diagnosis:** These users likely have an urgent, short-term need (e.g., a specific dietary trial or emergency stock-up) rather than a recurring subscription habit. The high average order value (AOV) masks a broken retention loop.
*   **Supply Chain Implication:** Do not forecast inventory based on the high AOV alone. If churn accelerates after Delivery 3, the "lifetime" of these customers is shorter than the standard model assumes. Forecasting based on standard LTV will lead to overstocking in Quarter 3.
*   **Actionable Insight:** The revenue is real, but the model is transactional, not subscription-based. Consider shifting these users to a "flex" plan or larger one-time purchases rather than forcing a monthly cycle that triggers cancellation.

**4. Supply Chain Forecasting Inputs**
For the upcoming quarter, provide the supply chain manager with two distinct volume projections:
*   **Base Case:** Applies the December cohort's retention curve to the January acquisition volume.
*   **Adjusted Case:** Applies the specific "Dog-Owner Churn Velocity" if the new data shows a statistically significant deviation from the baseline.
*   **Critical Variable:** The "Multi-Pet" retention rate. If multi-pet owners retain 20% longer than single-pet owners, weight the inventory forecast toward the multi-pet consumption profile (higher volume per household) for the retained portion of the cohort.

**Note:** Ensure user identifiers are pseudonymized before analysis to maintain privacy compliance. If the January cohort size (2,500) yields fewer than 30 cancellations in a specific segment, mark that specific retention rate as `UNVERIFIED` due to low statistical power.
