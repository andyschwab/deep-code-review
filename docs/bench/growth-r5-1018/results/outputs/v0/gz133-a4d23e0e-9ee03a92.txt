**Recommendation: Redefine Conversion and Measurement Strategy for SunLease Australia**

**1. Redefine the North Star Metric**
For SunLease, a completed application form is a **vanity metric** in its current state because it no longer correlates reliably with revenue. Your data proves this: traffic rose 20%, but qualified site visits (the actual value driver) dropped from 25% to 15% of submissions.
*   **New North Star:** **Weekly Qualified Site Visits.** This metric only rises when a homeowner completes the process *and* meets the criteria for installation. It aligns directly with revenue potential.
*   **Primary Input Metric:** **Lead Quality Rate** (Qualified Site Visits / Form Submissions). This measures the efficiency of your funnel, not just the volume.

**2. Diagnose the Funnel (Bottom-Up)**
Do not optimize for more form completions (Acquisition/Activation) until the leak in Retention/Revenue is plugged. Pouring ad spend into a funnel where 85% of leads are unqualified wastes budget.
*   **Current State:** You are maximizing the top of the funnel while the bottom leaks. The redesign likely removed friction for unqualified users, increasing noise.
*   **Action:** Shift focus from "Conversion Rate" (forms) to "Qualification Rate" (visits).

**3. Event Taxonomy & Instrumentation**
Stop measuring "Form Submitted" as a success event. Implement the following specific events to answer named questions:
*   **Event:** `application_started` (Property: `entry_source`) → *Question: Where are low-quality leads coming from?*
*   **Event:** `application_submitted` (Property: `completion_time`, `device_type`) → *Question: Does rushing the form correlate with disqualification?*
*   **Event:** `site_visit_qualified` (Property: `days_since_submission`, `lead_score_if_available`) → *Question: What is the true lag between submission and value?*
*   **Event:** `pre_qual_step_viewed` (if implemented) → *Question: Does adding friction filter bad leads without deterring good ones?*

**4. Strategic Decision: Optimize for Quality**
Your assumption that "conversion = form completion" is incorrect for this stage. You must introduce a **pre-qualification step** or re-weight your optimization goals.
*   **Hypothesis:** Adding a high-friction question (e.g., "Do you own the roof?" or "What is your monthly electricity bill?") before the final submit will lower total volume but increase the Lead Quality Rate back toward 25%+.
*   **Measurement Plan:** Run an A/B test where Variant B includes one mandatory pre-qualification field.
    *   **Primary Metric:** Lift in `site_visit_qualified` rate.
    *   **Guardrail Metric:** Total `site_visit_qualified` volume (ensure you don't filter out *too* many good leads).
    *   **Design Rule:** Pre-commit to a sample size based on your current traffic to avoid "peeking" at early results. Do not declare a winner until the pre-committed duration ends.

**5. Financial Alignment for the CRO**
To adjust the advertising budget:
*   Calculate **Cost Per Qualified Visit (CPQV)** instead of Cost Per Lead (CPL).
*   If Channel A has a CPL of $10 but a 10% quality rate, and Channel B has a CPL of $20 but a 40% quality rate, Channel B is actually cheaper per revenue-generating event.
*   **Immediate Action:** Re-allocate budget away from channels driving the highest volume of unqualified forms, even if their CPA looks low.
