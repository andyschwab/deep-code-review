### Experimental Design: Discounted Pilot Acceleration Test

**Objective**
Validate whether a 20% pilot discount increases pilot-to-contract conversion from 30% to 45% without degrading deal quality (LTV) or attracting high-churn segments, specifically for the VP of Sales board presentation on the 15th.

**1. Cohort Structure**
To isolate the impact of price sensitivity and industry verticals, randomize qualified leads at the **pilot proposal stage** into three distinct cohorts. Randomization must occur before the price is revealed to prevent selection bias.

*   **Control Cohort (A):** Standard pricing. No discount offered.
    *   *Purpose:* Establish the baseline 30% conversion rate and standard LTV/churn profile.
*   **Treatment Cohort (B):** 20% discounted pilot.
    *   *Purpose:* Test the primary hypothesis (conversion lift to 45%).
*   **Stratified Sub-Cohorts (Vertical Analysis):** Within both A and B, tag leads by **Industry Vertical** (e.g., Finance, Healthcare, Retail).
    *   *Purpose:* Determine if price sensitivity varies by sector. This addresses the unknown variable of whether specific industries respond disproportionately to discounts, which could skew overall results.

**2. Leading Indicators (Pre-Revenue Proxies)**
Given the nine-month sales cycle and 60-day pilot duration, waiting for final revenue or churn data is not feasible for the initial read. Monitor these leading indicators to detect early signals of "low-quality" leads or devaluation:

*   **Pilot Activation Rate:** Percentage of signed pilots that actually onboard and configure the tool within the first 14 days.
    *   *Signal:* If the Discount Cohort signs more deals but has a lower activation rate, the discount is attracting non-serious buyers.
*   **Core Action Frequency (Weeks 1–4):** Average number of core value-delivering actions per user during the pilot.
    *   *Signal:* A significant drop in engagement depth in the Discount Cohort suggests the perceived value of the product has been diminished by the lower price point.
*   **Stakeholder Breadth:** Number of distinct stakeholders (e.g., engineers, managers, CIO) engaging with the platform during the pilot.
    *   *Signal:* Enterprise deals require broad buy-in. If discounted pilots show narrow usage (single user), they are at higher risk of failing the final CIO approval, regardless of initial sign-off.
*   **Pilot-to-Extension Request Rate:** Frequency of requests to extend the pilot beyond 60 days.
    *   *Signal:* High extension requests in the Control group vs. low requests in the Discount group may indicate that discounted leads feel less urgency to prove value or commit.

**3. Decision Framework for the Board**
Present the experiment as a "Gatekeeper" model. The final go/no-go decision will rely on a matrix of the primary metric (conversion) against the quality guardrails:

*   **Ship (Roll out discount):** Conversion increases significantly **AND** Activation/Core Action Frequency remains within 5% of the Control cohort.
*   **Stop (Reject discount):** Conversion increases, but Activation or Stakeholder Breadth drops by >10% (indicating lead quality degradation).
*   **Segmented Rollout:** Conversion lifts only in specific Industry Verticals with no quality drop. Recommendation: Apply discount only to those verticals.

**4. Immediate Next Steps (Before the 15th)**
1.  **Instrumentation Check:** Verify that the analytics platform can tag users by "Cohort ID" and "Industry Vertical" at the moment of pilot agreement.
2.  **Baseline Calculation:** Extract the last 12 months of pilot data to confirm the current 30% conversion baseline and calculate the average "Core Action Frequency" for successful vs. failed pilots. This establishes the threshold for the quality guardrails.
3.  **Sample Size Estimation:** Calculate the number of pilot starts required to detect a lift from 30% to 45% with sufficient confidence, ensuring the 60-day pilot window provides enough data points before the next board review.

*Note: Do not interpret mid-pilot conversion spikes as final wins; the 60-day pilot completion is the minimum unit of analysis. Ensure legal/compliance reviews the discount structure to avoid precedent issues in enterprise contracts.*
