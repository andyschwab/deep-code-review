**Recomputed Impact & Confounders**
You reported a 2% lift in checkout completion and a 5% drop in page views. Without raw counts, we cannot verify if the absolute number of checkouts increased or if the conversion rate simply improved on a smaller, self-selected pool of determined buyers.
*   **The Trap:** If page views dropped by 5% but checkouts only rose by 2%, your total revenue likely decreased. Conversion rate is a ratio; if the denominator (views) shrinks faster than the numerator (checkouts), the business loses.
*   **Confounding Variables:** The "surprise" drop contradicts the assumption that trust signals uniformly help. This suggests **UI clutter** (badges obscuring key product info) or **segmentation friction** (e.g., European buyers mistrusting specific verification types or finding them intrusive). The two-week duration starting Feb 1st may also include post-holiday normalization, but since this is an A/B test, seasonality should cancel out unless the badge interacts with specific seasonal buying behaviors.

**Required Data Slices (The "This-Week" Step)**
Before Friday's council meeting, run these three specific queries on your existing data. Do not instrument new events yet; slice what you have.

1.  **Funnel Drop-off Location:**
    *   *Query:* Compare the click-through rate (CTR) from Product Page → Add to Cart for Control vs. Variant.
    *   *Decision Rule:* If the drop is entirely at "View → Click," the badge is causing **UI clutter** or distraction. If the drop is later, the badge is working as a filter but reducing browsing volume.
    *   *Action:* If UI clutter is confirmed, redesign the badge placement (e.g., move below the fold) rather than killing the feature.

2.  **Segmented Reaction by Buyer Geography & Seller Tier:**
    *   *Query:* Break down page view drops by buyer country (e.g., Germany vs. France) and seller verification level (e.g., ID-only vs. ID + Business License).
    *   *Decision Rule:* If the drop is isolated to specific countries or low-tier sellers, the issue is **trust mismatch** (e.g., the badge looks "cheap" or suspicious to high-value markets).
    *   *Action:* Roll out only to segments where page views remained flat or grew, and pause for others.

3.  **Net Revenue Per Visitor (RPV) Check:**
    *   *Query:* Calculate `(Total Checkout Value / Total Unique Visitors)` for both groups.
    *   *Decision Rule:* This is your true North Star. If RPV is lower in the variant, the 2% conversion lift is a vanity metric driven by a shrinking funnel.
    *   *Action:* If RPV is negative, stop the rollout immediately. If RPV is positive, the reduced browsing is a feature (users find what they need faster), and you can proceed.
