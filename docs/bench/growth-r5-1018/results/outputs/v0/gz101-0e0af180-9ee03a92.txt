**Recommendation: Redefine Activation to "First Value Realized," Not "Five Tickets Resolved"**

Your current definition (resolving five tickets) is a **lagging indicator** that likely occurs too late to prevent the 30% drop-off you see after day three. If teams leave before resolving five tickets, your metric counts them as "never activated," masking whether they actually found value earlier. For a B2B ticketing platform, "activation" should be the moment the customer perceives the core value: **successfully routing and resolving a single critical issue.**

**Proposed North Star Input Metric:**
Replace "5 tickets resolved" with **"First Ticket Resolved by an Invitee Other Than the Admin."**
*   **Why:** This confirms three critical adoption steps: the admin invited the team (invitation), the team logged in (activation), and the workflow was configured correctly to allow resolution (value).
*   **Hypothesis:** Accounts that resolve *one* ticket within the first 48 hours will have significantly higher 90-day retention than those stuck at the invitation or configuration phase, regardless of whether they ever reach five tickets.

**Validation Plan (Using Existing Data Only)**
Do not build new dashboards yet. Use your current database logs to run a one-time retrospective cohort analysis before next Tuesday:

1.  **Segment Last Month's 4,500 Accounts:**
    *   **Group A:** Resolved ≥1 ticket within 48 hours of signup.
    *   **Group B:** Invited agents but resolved 0 tickets within 48 hours.
    *   **Group C:** Did not invite agents within 48 hours.
2.  **Measure Retention:** Check the login status of these groups at Day 30 and Day 90.
3.  **Compare Correlations:** Calculate the correlation between "Time to First Ticket Resolved" and "Contract Renewal/Retention" versus "Time to Five Tickets" and "Retention."

**Expected Outcome & Decision Rule:**
*   **If Group A's retention is >2x Group B's:** Your new activation metric is "First Ticket Resolved." The 5-ticket threshold is indeed too high and delays your intervention window.
*   **If retention is similar across groups:** The issue is not the threshold but the initial onboarding flow; the metric remains valid, but the product experience is failing earlier.

**Immediate Actions for Next Tuesday:**
1.  **Run the SQL Query:** Extract `signup_date`, `first_invite_sent`, `first_ticket_resolved`, and `last_login_date` for the 4,500 accounts.
2.  **Plot the Curve:** Visualize retention rates against "Days to First Ticket Resolved." Identify the steepest drop-off point.
3.  **Present the Shift:** Show your director that moving the activation definition earlier expands the addressable "at-risk" population from 0% (those who didn't hit 5) to ~30% (those who didn't hit 1), allowing Customer Success to intervene days sooner.

**Note:** Do not assert a specific "good" retention percentage; compare these groups only against your own historical baseline. If the data shows smaller teams resolve fewer tickets overall but renew at high rates, the "5-ticket" rule is definitively broken for that segment.
