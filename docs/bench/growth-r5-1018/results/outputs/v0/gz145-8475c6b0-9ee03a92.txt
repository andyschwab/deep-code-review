### Revised Activation Definition & Measurement Plan

**North Star Metric:** Weekly Active Enterprises completing a "Value-Realization Event" (defined below), not cumulative storage volume.

**Diagnosis:** Your current definition (50GB + 3 shares) measures *effort*, not *value*. The data proves that high-volume uploaders who do not establish a recurring workflow (automated backups) are treating CloudVault as a temporary archive, leading to churn. Conversely, the 10GB automated backup users have integrated CloudVault into their daily operations, creating stickiness. Volume is a vanity metric in this context; **workflow integration** is the value driver.

**New Activation Criteria (Hypothesis to Validate):**
A customer is "Activated" when they complete **one** of the following "Value-Realization Events" within the first 14 days:
1.  **Automation Established:** Successful configuration and execution of at least one automated backup or sync job.
2.  **Collaborative Loop:** A file shared externally is accessed or edited by the recipient (proving the share created value, not just noise).
3.  **Multi-Seat Adoption:** At least 3 distinct user seats perform an action (upload/download/edit) within a 48-hour window.

**Event Taxonomy (Instrument Only These):**
Do not instrument "everything." Track only these events to answer specific questions:
*   `automation_configured` (Properties: `source_type`, `schedule_frequency`) → *Question: Does setting up automation predict renewal?*
*   `external_share_accessed` (Properties: `file_type`, `recipient_domain`) → *Question: Do shares that are actually opened correlate with retention?*
*   `active_seat_count_weekly` (Properties: `count`, `action_type`) → *Question: Is breadth of adoption more critical than depth of storage?*
*   `core_action_retention_cohort` (Weekly) → *Question: Are users returning to the specific action that defined their activation?*

**How to Read the Data (Bottom-Up Funnel):**
1.  **Retention First:** Ignore new signups until you verify if the "Automation Established" group retains at 90% vs. the "Volume Only" group at 40%. Segment your existing 200 clients by these new behaviors to confirm the correlation.
2.  **Activation Second:** Measure the conversion rate from "Signup" to "Automation Established." If this rate is low, your onboarding guidance is failing to teach the *right* behavior (automation), regardless of how much data they upload.
3.  **Acquisition Last:** Only optimize acquisition channels once the activation path leads to verified retention.

**Experiment Design for Onboarding Redesign:**
To test the new onboarding emails/guidance, do not peek at results daily.
*   **Pre-commit:** Run an A/B test for 4 weeks or until 500 enterprises reach the decision point.
*   **Primary Metric:** % of users completing "Automation Established" within 14 days.
*   **Guardrail Metric:** Total support tickets per user (ensure simplified guidance doesn't increase confusion).
*   **Rule:** If Variant B shows a lift in automation setup but a spike in support tickets, the win is invalid.

**This Week's Steps:**
1.  **Query Existing Data:** Run a cohort analysis on the 200 clients comparing `renewal_status` against `automation_configured` vs. `total_gb_uploaded`. Confirm the hypothesis that automation is the leading indicator.
2.  **Define the Event:** Finalize the technical definition of `automation_configured` in your analytics tool (e.g., specific API call or UI click sequence).
3.  **Draft the Test:** Design the A/B test where the treatment group receives onboarding focused solely on setting up one automated backup, ignoring storage volume targets entirely.

*Note: All benchmark comparisons must be made against your own historical baseline (the 40% vs 90% split you already observed); do not seek external industry averages for "good" activation rates.*
*Ensure user identifiers in these events are pseudonymous and comply with your privacy governance before instrumentation.*
