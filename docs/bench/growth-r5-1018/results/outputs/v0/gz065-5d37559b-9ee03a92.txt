**Executive Summary: Price Experiment Read-Out**

**Verdict:** Do not roll out the price globally next month. The current data is **unverified** for causal impact due to extreme noise and missing context. Acting now risks a costly error.

**How to Read the Results Without False Conclusions**

The wild daily swings (200 to 900 units) indicate that external factors are overpowering the price signal. In this environment, daily revenue is a vanity metric; it moves regardless of your pricing strategy. To find the truth, you must shift your focus from daily fluctuations to aggregated stability and relative performance.

1.  **Aggregate to the Full Period, Not Daily Snapshots**
    Ignore the day-to-day chart. It is designed to mislead in high-variance environments. Instead, sum the total units sold and total revenue for the entire 14-day period for the test villages. Compare this single aggregate number against a comparable 14-day baseline period from before the experiment (e.g., April 1–14) or against similar villages where the price did not change. If the total volume over two weeks is statistically indistinguishable from the baseline, the price change had no meaningful effect, regardless of daily spikes.

2.  **Isolate the "Core Action" Rate**
    Revenue is a lagging indicator influenced by weather-driven foot traffic. To isolate the price effect, you must measure the **conversion rate**: the percentage of people who entered the store (or visited the product page) who actually bought the jacket.
    *   If bad weather caused low sales, both foot traffic and total sales should drop, but the *conversion rate* might remain stable.
    *   If the price was the driver, you will see the conversion rate shift even if foot traffic fluctuates.
    *   **Action:** You must retrieve foot traffic counts for those 14 days. Without this denominator, you cannot separate "no one came because of rain" from "no one bought because of price."

3.  **Demand the Missing Context (The Weather Gap)**
    Your assumption that weather is the driver is logical but currently **unverified**. You cannot smooth out noise you cannot measure. Before any global decision, you must obtain granular weather data (precipitation, temperature, wind) for those specific villages for May 1–14. Overlay this data with your sales volume. If the days with 200 units sold correlate perfectly with storm events, and 900-unit days correlate with clear skies, the price variable is likely irrelevant. If sales remain flat despite perfect weather, the price is the blocker.

**Decision Framework for the VP**

*   **If** the 14-day aggregate conversion rate is within 5% of the baseline **AND** low sales days align with poor weather data: **Hold.** The price is likely neutral; the variance is environmental. Do not roll out yet; wait for a clearer season or more locations to confirm.
*   **If** the aggregate conversion rate dropped significantly compared to the baseline **REGARDLESS** of weather: **Stop.** The price is too high for this market.
*   **If** you cannot retrieve foot traffic counts or local weather data by next week: **Stop.** You are flying blind. Rolling out globally without verifying the cause of the variance is a gamble, not a strategy.
