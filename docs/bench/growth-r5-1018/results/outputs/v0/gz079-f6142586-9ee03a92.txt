**Executive Summary: Do Not Roll Out Globally Yet**

The observed 25% lift is **UNVERIFIED** as a causal result of the feature. Because a massive external shock (market-wide shift to remote work) occurred simultaneously with the test window, the experiment is contaminated. The "treatment effect" is currently indistinguishable from the "market effect." Rolling out globally based on this data risks allocating engineering resources to a feature that may provide negligible value once market conditions stabilize, or worse, masking usability issues that will surface when the external tailwind fades.

**Recommended Approach to Isolate Impact**

To provide the CEO with a realistic assessment, we must abandon the claim of a 25% feature lift and instead execute the following three-step isolation strategy before any global decision:

1.  **Establish a Counterfactual Baseline (The "No-Feature" Trend)**
    Compare the treatment group's growth rate against a **holdout cohort** that did *not* receive the feature but was exposed to the same market shock.
    *   *Action:* Identify a segment of users (e.g., a specific geographic region or enterprise tier) where the feature was technically disabled or not deployed during April 1–14.
    *   *Calculation:* If the holdout group grew by 20% due to the market shock, and the treatment group grew by 25%, the **isolated feature lift is approximately 5%**, not 25%. If no internal holdout exists, we cannot mathematically separate the signals; the result remains `UNVERIFIED`.

2.  **Analyze Retention of the Core Action (Bottom-Up Diagnosis)**
    Vanity metrics (Daily Active Users) will naturally spike during a crisis as everyone logs in. This does not prove the *new collaboration feature* creates value.
    *   *Action:* Measure **retention** of the specific core action enabled by the new feature (e.g., "completed a shared edit session") for users who activated it, compared to their pre-shock baseline.
    *   *Logic:* If users are logging in due to the crisis but *not* using the new collaboration tools at a higher rate than before, the feature itself is not driving the engagement. Real value is proven only if the *depth* of usage increases, not just the *breadth* of logins.

3.  **Extend the Test or Run a Staggered Rollout**
    Since the April 1–14 window is compromised, we need a cleaner signal.
    *   *Action:* Do not stop the test. Extend the duration until the market volatility stabilizes, or initiate a **staggered rollout** (group-sequential design) starting next week.
    *   *Method:* Release the feature to a small, randomized 10% of traffic *now*. Compare this new cohort's performance against a concurrent control group over the next 14 days. This new window will have a consistent "remote-first" baseline for both groups, allowing the feature's true delta to emerge without the confounding "shift-to-remote" noise.
