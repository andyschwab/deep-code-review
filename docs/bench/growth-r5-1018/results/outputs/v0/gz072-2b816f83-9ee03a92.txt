**Subject:** Q2 Budget Decision Framework: Engagement Signals for Renewal

To decide between funding more classes or equipment maintenance, we must stop measuring "attendance" (a vanity metric that rises regardless of value) and start measuring **value realization**. Our North Star for Q2 will be **"Weekly Active Makers Completing a Project,"** defined as a member who finishes a print job they started. Attendance alone does not predict renewal; successful output does.

We will instrument only the following three behavioral signals to answer specific questions about financial sustainability. All metrics are currently `UNVERIFIED` until we attach tracking to our point-of-sale and door systems.

**1. The "Second-Visit" Conversion Rate (Activation)**
*   **Question:** Does attending a second workshop within 30 days actually correlate with higher filament spend, or is it just social loitering?
*   **Event to Track:** `workshop_attended` (properties: `member_id`, `date`, `workshop_type`) linked to `purchase_made` (properties: `item_type`, `amount`).
*   **Decision Rule:** If members with 2+ visits spend <10% more on filament/rentals than one-time visitors, the "advocate hypothesis" is false. **Action:** Stop budgeting for more classes; shift funds to equipment maintenance to improve the experience for high-spenders. If they spend significantly more, **Action:** Allocate budget to classes to drive that second visit.

**2. Project Completion vs. Abandonment Ratio (Retention)**
*   **Question:** Are members renewing because they finish things, or are they churning because machines are down when they need them?
*   **Event to Track:** `print_job_started` and `print_job_completed` (properties: `member_id`, `machine_id`, `duration`). Calculate the % of started jobs that finish successfully.
*   **Decision Rule:** If completion rates are low (<70%) due to machine errors or downtime, **Action:** 100% of Q2 budget goes to maintenance. No amount of new classes will fix retention if the core utility (printing) is broken. If completion is high but renewals are low, **Action:** Investigate pricing or community engagement, not maintenance.

**3. Revenue Per Active Maker (RPAM) by Cohort**
*   **Question:** Which behavior drives the $120 renewal: high-frequency renting or high-volume filament purchases?
*   **Event to Track:** Aggregate `rental_session` and `filament_purchase` per `member_id` over 90-day cohorts.
*   **Decision Rule:** Compare the RPAM of members who renewed last year vs. those who didn't. If renewers are primarily heavy renters, **Action:** Prioritize machine uptime (maintenance). If renewers are primarily filament buyers, **Action:** Prioritize classes that teach complex designs requiring more material.

**This Week's Steps:**
1.  **Define the Schema:** By Wednesday, finalize the `member_id` pseudonymous key to link door check-ins, workshop logs, and POS transactions. Without this stable ID, we cannot track individuals across sessions.
2.  **Baseline Query:** Run a manual retroactive query on January–March data: "Of the 350 weekly check-ins, how many have a corresponding filament purchase or rental >$20?" Use this baseline to set our Q2 target.
3.  **Hold Budget:** Do not commit Q2 funds until the "Second-Visit" signal is verified. If the data shows no spend lift from repeat attendees, the class expansion hypothesis fails immediately.

*Note: We are comparing these metrics only against MotorCity Makers' own historical baseline; no external industry benchmarks for "good" retention exist for niche hobby hubs. Any metric without a linked transaction ID remains `UNVERIFIED`.*
