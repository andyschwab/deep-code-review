### Candidate Retention Metrics for Maple Meal Prep

**1. Weekly Active Subscription Rate (Frequency-First)**
*Definition:* The percentage of users who place at least one order in a given calendar week, measured over a rolling 8-week window.
*Pros:* Directly measures habit formation and product usage frequency. It clearly distinguishes between a user who pauses for a week (temporary) and one who stops ordering entirely (churn). It is simple to calculate and easy for the team to visualize.
*Cons:* Ignores monetary value. A user downgrading from 5 meals to 1 meal counts as "retained," masking revenue erosion. It may not capture the financial impact of price sensitivity if users stay but drastically reduce order size.

**2. Rolling 30-Day Revenue Retention (Value-First)**
*Definition:* The ratio of revenue generated by a cohort in their current 30-day window compared to their baseline 30-day window prior to the price change.
*Pros:* Directly ties retention to business health and cash flow. It immediately captures the net effect of churn, downgrades, and pauses. If students are price-sensitive, this metric will show a decline even if they haven't fully canceled, highlighting the need for a cheaper plan.
*Cons:* Conflates usage frequency with price changes. A drop in this metric could be due to the price hike itself (mathematical) rather than behavioral churn, making it harder to isolate whether the issue is "too expensive" vs. "not eating here anymore." It is less effective at distinguishing temporary pauses from permanent exits without additional segmentation.

**3. Hybrid "Core Action" Retention (Recommended)**
*Definition:* A user is "retained" only if they maintain ≥40% of their pre-change meal frequency AND ≥50% of their pre-change spend over a rolling 4-week window. Users falling below both thresholds are "churned"; those below only one are "at-risk/downgraded."
*Pros:* Explicitly separates temporary pauses (low frequency, high intent to return) from permanent churn (zero frequency) and value erosion (high frequency, low spend). This directly answers your question about portion sizes vs. cost: if frequency stays high but spend drops, it's price/portion sensitivity. If frequency drops to zero, it's a fit issue. It provides a clear "at-risk" segment to target with the proposed smaller meal plan.
*Cons:* More complex to define and track initially. Requires setting specific thresholds (e.g., the 40%/50% splits) which may need calibration based on historical data.

**Recommendation for Strategy Review:**
Adopt the **Hybrid "Core Action" Retention** metric. It allows you to segment the post-February 1st spike: if the "at-risk" group (high frequency, low spend) is growing, the issue is price/portion, validating the smaller plan. If the "churned" group (zero frequency) is growing, the issue is fundamental fit or sticker shock requiring a different intervention.

*Note: Ensure your analytics tool can track individual user IDs across weeks to calculate these cohorts accurately; do not rely on aggregate signup counts.*
*Final decision on thresholds should use your pre-February baseline data to avoid setting arbitrary targets.*
