### Presentation Framework: Checkout Speed vs. Accessibility Compliance

**1. The Core Trade-off Matrix**
Present the conflict as a direct comparison of verified outcomes against stated constraints. Do not normalize the numbers; display them as raw tensions.
*   **Gain (Primary Metric):** 4% lift in sales conversion. Frame this as revenue impact based on your 4,200 monthly active users. Calculate the absolute number of additional transactions this represents over a month to make the gain tangible.
*   **Cost (Guardrail Failure):** Contrast ratio dropped from 4.8 (pass) to 2.9 (fail). Explicitly state this violates the pre-test assumption that visual clarity would remain above the minimum threshold.
*   **Secondary Signal:** Average session time reduced by 18 seconds. Label this `UNVERIFIED` as a pure efficiency gain until correlated with task completion rates for low-vision users; faster failure is not an improvement.

**2. The Data Gap & Risk Exposure**
Highlight exactly what is missing that prevents a final decision. This shifts the conversation from "guessing" to "measuring risk."
*   **Segmentation Blind Spot:** The 4% lift is an aggregate. You lack the conversion rate specifically for users relying on screen readers or high-contrast modes. The gain may be driven entirely by low-vision users abandoning the cart due to illegibility, masked by power users checking out faster.
*   **Compliance Status:** The 2.9 contrast ratio is a definitive fail against standard accessibility guidelines (WCAG). Frame this not as a design preference but as a legal and ethical exposure. The "assumption" was a guardrail; breaking it invalidates the test's success criteria regardless of sales lift.

**3. Decision Options for Leadership**
Present three distinct paths, each tied to a specific next action and data requirement. Do not recommend one; define the trigger for each.
*   **Option A: Immediate Rollback.**
    *   *Trigger:* If legal risk or brand reputation for inclusivity outweighs the projected revenue of the 4% lift.
    *   *Action:* Revert to the 4.8 contrast ratio baseline immediately.
    *   *Data Needed:* None. This prioritizes compliance over unverified growth.
*   **Option B: Partial Fix (Iterate).**
    *   *Trigger:* If the team believes the contrast can be raised above the threshold (e.g., to 4.5) without losing the majority of the speed gain.
    *   *Action:* Deploy a hotfix adjusting contrast only, then re-measure conversion.
    *   *Data Needed:* A rapid design sprint estimate on how much speed is lost per point of contrast gained.
*   **Option C: Targeted Analysis (Wait).**
    *   *Trigger:* If leadership requires proof that the 4% lift includes low-vision users before acting.
    *   *Action:* Keep the change live for 48 more hours solely to instrument and query conversion by assistive-technology usage.
    *   *Data Needed:* Immediate engineering support to tag sessions by screen-reader usage. If this data cannot be retrieved instantly, this option is unavailable.

**4. The "This-Week" Step**
Conclude the presentation by assigning the immediate next move based on the chosen path.
*   If **Rollback**: Schedule the revert for tonight; announce the commitment to accessibility standards to the team.
*   If **Partial Fix**: Commission a design review by tomorrow noon to propose specific color tokens that meet the 4.5 threshold.
*   If **Targeted Analysis**: Define the exact database query needed to isolate the low-vision cohort and set a deadline for those results (e.g., "Data ready by 10 AM tomorrow").

**Note:** The 4% lift is `UNVERIFIED` as a net positive until the exclusion cost is quantified. Do not present the sales gain as a "win" while the guardrail is broken.
**Note:** Ensure the CEO understands that "faster session time" is a vanity metric if it results from users leaving because they cannot read the buttons.
