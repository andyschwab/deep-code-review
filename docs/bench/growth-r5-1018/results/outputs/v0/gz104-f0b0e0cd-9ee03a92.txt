### Executive Breakdown: Isolating Price Sensitivity vs. Operational Friction

**Diagnosis Strategy**
Do not treat the 9% churn as a single number. You must isolate the **price shock** from the **logistics change** by slicing your existing data along three specific dimensions before collecting new data.

1.  **Cohort Separation (The "When" Test)**
    *   **Action:** Split users into two cohorts: those who subscribed *before* the logistics partner change and those who joined *after*.
    *   **Reading Rule:** If churn spiked equally in both cohorts immediately after Jan 15, the driver is **price**. If churn spiked only in the "post-logistics" cohort (or existing users experienced a service dip coinciding with the bill), the driver is **operational**.
    *   **Status:** If you cannot separate users by their exposure to the old vs. new logistics provider, this variable is `UNVERIFIED`. You must join subscription dates with logistics rollout dates immediately.

2.  **Segmentation by Tenure and Plan (The "Who" Test)**
    *   **Action:** Break down the 9% churn by **customer tenure** (<6 months vs. >2 years) and **plan tier**.
    *   **Reading Rule:**
        *   *Price Sensitivity:* High churn in newer, lower-tier plans; stable retention in legacy/high-tier loyalists.
        *   *Logistics Failure:* Uniform churn across all price points, or concentrated in geographic zones where the new partner operates.
    *   **Decision Trigger:** If churn >7% in the <6-month cohort but remains <4.5% in the >2-year cohort, the issue is **acquisition pricing**, not loyalist rebellion.

3.  **The "Silent" Churn Signal**
    *   **Action:** Analyze cancellation reasons (if captured) and support ticket volume regarding "delivery" vs. "cost" in the 14 days post-hike.
    *   **Reading Rule:** A spike in "delivery delay" tickets coinciding with churn confirms the logistics confounder. If tickets are flat but cancellations rise, it is pure price elasticity.

**Immediate Actions (This Week)**
1.  **Recompute the Baseline:** Calculate the churn rate for the specific segment of users *not* affected by the logistics change (if any exist). If no such group exists, you cannot statistically isolate the variables yet; label the cause `UNVERIFIED` and recommend a targeted survey to the churned cohort immediately.
2.  **Run a "Win-Back" Test:** Before the board meeting, launch a controlled offer to 10% of the churned users: half get a price rollback, half get a "guaranteed delivery" badge. Measure re-subscription rates. This is the only way to causally prove which lever moves the needle.
3.  **Prepare the Board Narrative:** Present the churn not as a failure of loyalty, but as a data gap. Show the segmented breakdown. If the data shows high churn in price-sensitive segments, recommend a tiered pricing structure rather than a rollback.

**Note:** Do not cite industry retention benchmarks; compare February's 9% only against your own historical 4% baseline. If logistics rollout dates are not mapped to user IDs, you cannot prove causality—state this limitation clearly to the board.
