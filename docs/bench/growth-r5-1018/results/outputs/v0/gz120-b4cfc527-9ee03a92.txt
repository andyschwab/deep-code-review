### Core Conversion Funnel Definition for DesignHub

**Funnel Start Point:** The funnel must start when a **client posts a job**.
Starting at "portfolio view" ignores the primary friction point: job creation. If clients do not post, there is no inventory for freelancers to bid on, rendering portfolio views irrelevant to transaction volume. Your current data (4,000 posts → 800 hires) confirms the bottleneck lies within the post-to-hire flow, not pre-post browsing.

**Proposed Event Taxonomy & Drop-off Measurement**
Instrument only these four events to answer specific questions about your 20% overall conversion rate. Do not track "pageviews" as funnel steps; they are vanity metrics here.

| Stage | Event Name | Question Answered | Drop-off Calculation |
| :--- | :--- | :--- | : |
| **1. Activation** | `job_posted_success` | Did the client complete the listing? | N/A (Baseline = 4,000) |
| **2. Liquidity** | `first_bid_received` | Did the job attract *any* market interest? | `(Posted - First Bid) / Posted` |
| **3. Consideration** | `proposal_threshold_met` | Did the client receive enough bids to choose? (e.g., ≥3 bids) | `(First Bid - Threshold Met) / Posted` |
| **4. Conversion** | `freelancer_hired` | Did the interaction result in revenue? | `(Threshold Met - Hired) / Posted` |

**Diagnosing the Conflict (Quality vs. Quantity)**
Your team argues "proposal quality"; clients argue "not enough bids." This funnel resolves the debate empirically:
*   **If Stage 2 (Liquidity) drop-off is high:** Clients are correct. Jobs are sitting empty. The bottleneck is **supply distribution** (freelancers not seeing or ignoring the post). Fix: Improve job matching algorithms or notify relevant freelancers faster.
*   **If Stage 2 is healthy but Stage 3/4 drop-off is high:** Your team is correct. Clients get bids but don't hire. The bottleneck is **proposal quality** or **client decision paralysis**. Fix: Surface top-rated proposals, hide low-quality bids, or assist clients in selection.

**Measurement Rules**
*   **Baseline Only:** Compare these rates only against DesignHub's own historical monthly trends. Do not compare to external "industry standards" as market dynamics in Ukraine differ from global averages.
*   **Cohort Analysis:** Measure these drop-offs weekly. If `first_bid_received` time exceeds 24 hours, the "not enough bids" complaint is likely a latency issue, not a volume issue.

**This Week's Steps**
1.  **Instrument:** Implement tracking for `first_bid_received` immediately. Without this timestamp, you cannot distinguish between "no bids" and "slow bids."
2.  **Query:** Run a cohort analysis on last month's 4,000 jobs: What % received zero bids within 24 hours? If >30%, prioritize supply-side notifications over proposal quality tools.
3.  **Align:** Present the Stage 2 vs. Stage 3 drop-off rates to product and marketing. Assign ownership: Marketing owns Stage 2 (liquidity); Product owns Stage 3 (selection efficiency).

*Note: Ensure user identifiers are pseudonymous when tracking across sessions to comply with privacy standards. Do not fabricate a "good" conversion rate; your goal is to improve the 20% baseline, not hit an arbitrary external number.*
