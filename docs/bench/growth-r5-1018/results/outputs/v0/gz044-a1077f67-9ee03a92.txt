**Incident Report: Real-Time Collaboration Module (Flag Test Oct 2–9)**

**Executive Summary**
The A/B test for the new real-time collaboration module delivered strong user engagement but failed critical performance guardrails, creating an immediate risk to Service Level Agreements (SLAs) and enterprise client retention. While 40% of the exposed cohort adopted the feature daily, the architectural assumption of <50ms added latency was violated by a factor of 2.4x (observed +120ms). This degradation triggered a 3.5% error rate spike during peak loads on October 6, directly correlating with formal complaints from our largest enterprise client.

**Quantified User Experience Degradation**
The trade-off between adoption and stability is currently unsustainable. The high engagement rate (40% daily active usage within the test group) confirms the feature solves a genuine user need; however, the delivery mechanism is actively degrading the core service.
*   **Latency Breach:** The +120ms average latency exceeds the internal threshold by 70ms. In real-time collaboration, this lag disrupts the "flow state" of editing, likely causing the reported user friction.
*   **Reliability Failure:** The 3.5% error rate during peak hours represents a significant availability drop. For an enterprise client relying on continuous uptime, this translates to measurable productivity loss and potential contract penalties.
*   **Scope Uncertainty:** Due to incomplete logging, we cannot yet confirm if the latency is geographically isolated or systemic. However, the presence of enterprise complaints suggests the impact is affecting high-value segments disproportionately.

**SLA and Business Risk Assessment**
The current performance metrics place DataFlow Systems in breach of typical enterprise SLAs, which often mandate 99.9% availability and strict latency caps. The 3.5% error rate alone jeopardizes our compliance standing. Continuing the test at the current 10% exposure amplifies this liability linearly; scaling to 100% without remediation would likely cause a systemic outage and trigger mass churn among premium accounts. The "vanity" success of high adoption masks a fundamental "value" failure: users cannot derive value from a tool that fails one in every 30 requests during critical work windows.

**Recommendation**
The data indicates a clear conflict: the feature logic is desirable, but the infrastructure implementation is unstable. The risk to SLA compliance and enterprise trust outweighs the benefit of further data collection on adoption rates, which are already proven high.

**Decision Required:**
1.  **Immediate Action:** Disable the feature flag for the 10% cohort to halt SLA bleeding and stabilize the environment for the enterprise client.
2.  **Next Step:** Authorize a targeted investigation to isolate the latency source (geographic vs. systemic) using the existing incomplete logs before any re-enablement or hotfix deployment is attempted.
