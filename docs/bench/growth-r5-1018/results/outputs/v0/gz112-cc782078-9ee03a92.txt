### WriteWise Q1 Measurement Plan: From Activity to Revenue

**1. The North Star: Weekly Active Teams (WAT)**
*Definition:* Count of companies where ≥2 distinct users each generate ≥1 document within a 7-day window.
*Why:* Rejects "cumulative signups" (vanity) and single-user trials. This metric only rises when the core value hypothesis—team collaboration driving stickiness—is realized.
*Investor Link:* Directly correlates to your observation that inviting colleagues predicts retention. A rising WAT curve proves product-market fit for the B2B motion.

**2. Primary Input Metric: Collaboration Depth Ratio**
*Definition:* `(Users who invite a colleague) / (Total Active Users)` measured at Day 3 of the trial.
*Interpretation:*
*   **Shift Up:** Validates the "team collaboration" hypothesis. Immediate action: Move the invite prompt earlier in the onboarding flow.
*   **Shift Flat/Down:** Suggests the product is being used as a single-user tool. Immediate action: Interview non-inviting trials to identify friction; do not optimize for individual document count yet.

**3. The Funnel Leak: Trial-to-Paid Conversion by Cohort**
*Definition:* Split your 12 converted companies into two groups: those who invited a colleague during trial vs. those who did not. Compare conversion rates.
*Interpretation:*
*   If "Inviters" convert at >20% and "Solo" users <5%, your engineering priority is exclusively features that facilitate sharing/inviting.
*   If both groups convert equally, the hypothesis is wrong; revert to optimizing individual document generation speed.

**4. Guardrail Metric: Time-to-First-Invite**
*Definition:* Average hours from account creation to the first successful colleague invitation.
*Interpretation:* If this number exceeds 48 hours, the trial period (14 days) is too long relative to the value realization cycle. Shortening the trial or triggering intervention emails at hour 24 becomes the lever.

**How to Read These Numbers Next Quarter**
Do not compare these figures to industry benchmarks (which are unavailable and often misleading for niche B2B AI). Compare them strictly to your **current baseline**:
*   **Baseline:** 12/200 conversion (6%).
*   **Success Signal:** If you increase the "Collaboration Depth Ratio" by 10% and the "Trial-to-Paid" rate for inviters holds steady, total revenue should rise proportionally without new acquisition spend.
*   **Failure Signal:** If document volume rises but "Weekly Active Teams" stays flat, you are building a vanity dashboard. Stop feature work on generation speed and fix the collaboration loop.

**This Week's Execution Steps**
1.  **Instrument One Event:** `invite_sent` (properties: `user_id`, `days_since_signup`). Do not track "page views" or generic clicks.
2.  **Run One Query:** Segment last month's 200 trials by "invited vs. solo" and calculate the exact conversion delta.
3.  **Investor Narrative:** Present the "Collaboration Depth Ratio" as the leading indicator for next quarter's revenue, replacing "total documents generated" as the primary success metric.

*Note: All metrics above rely on your existing data; no new engineering overhead is required beyond tagging the `invite_sent` event. If the segmentation query reveals no statistical difference between inviters and solo users, the "team collaboration" hypothesis is UNVERIFIED and requires user interviews before further instrumentation.*
