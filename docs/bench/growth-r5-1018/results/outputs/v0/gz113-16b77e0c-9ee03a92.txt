**Executive Summary: Do Not Roll Out Yet**

The observed lift from 2.1% to 2.3% represents a relative increase of roughly 9.5%, but with only 5,000 visitors over three days, this result is **statistically unverified**. The wide confidence interval indicates the test is underpowered; the "win" could easily be random noise caused by peeking at the data early or natural traffic variance. Rolling out globally based on this snapshot risks implementing a change that provides no real value or, worse, negatively impacts revenue in larger markets.

**Immediate Decision:** Extend the test to a pre-committed sample size; do not aggregate with other countries yet.

**How to Read the Current Results**
Treat the current 2.3% figure as a hypothesis, not a fact. Because the test was likely monitored daily ("peeking"), the false-positive rate is inflated far above the standard 5%. A result that looks significant on day 3 often regresses to the mean if left running. Furthermore, you have assumed no impact on average donation size without data. If the green button attracts more clicks but from lower-intent donors (reducing average gift), the net revenue could drop despite the conversion lift. Until you verify the guardrail metric (average donation size), the experiment is incomplete.

    Your assumption that "color has no negative impact on average donation size" is an unverified risk.
    *   *Action:* Query the current data for **Average Donation Amount** and **Revenue Per Visitor** for both Blue and Green groups.

    Aggregating Norway with Sweden or Denmark introduces "mix shift" bias. User behavior, trust signals, and device usage vary by country. If Sweden has higher baseline conversion, blending the data could mask a negative result in Norway or create a false positive.
