**Analysis of Retention Cliff for FitTrack Head of Product**

The divergence between improved Day-1 retention (45% → 55%) and degraded Day-30 retention (25% → 18%) following the February 1 onboarding update indicates a **activation-retention mismatch**. The new flow successfully lowers the barrier to entry but appears to fail in establishing a sustainable value loop beyond the novelty phase. The sharp drop after three weeks, specifically among users averaging 4 workouts/week, suggests the product is delivering immediate utility but failing to transition users from "guided novelty" to "autonomous habit."

Based on the engagement pattern (high activity for 21 days, then sudden cessation), the root cause is likely not content exhaustion (which usually manifests as a gradual decline) but rather a **structural dependency on the onboarding scaffolding**. Users may be completing a specific 3-week challenge or guided plan introduced in the new flow, upon completion of which they encounter a "what now?" void. Without a clear next step or a transition to social/self-directed modes, the perceived value drops to zero, causing the cliff.

**Critical Data Points to Investigate**

To confirm the root cause without proposing tactics, investigate the following specific correlations and cohort splits:

1.  **Onboarding Path vs. Drop-off Date:** Segment the post-February 1 cohort by the specific onboarding track they completed. Correlate the exact date of drop-off with the **completion date of their initial guided plan**. If the drop-off date aligns precisely with the end of a 21-day program, the issue is a lack of post-program engagement triggers, not content exhaustion.
2.  **Feature Usage Decay:** Analyze the usage frequency of specific features (e.g., workout logging, progress tracking, social sharing) in Week 3 versus Week 4 for the churned cohort. A simultaneous drop across all features suggests a total disengagement (novelty end), whereas a drop only in "guided" features while "self-log" features remain flat suggests a transition failure.
3.  **Cohort Comparison (Pre- vs. Post-Update):** Compare the Day-30 retention of users who launched pre-February 1 but were active during the same calendar weeks. This controls for seasonality (e.g., New Year's resolution fade) to isolate whether the drop is unique to the new onboarding logic.
4.  **Exit Survey Correlation:** If available, cross-reference the drop-off cohort with any in-app exit survey responses or support tickets filed in Week 4. Look for keywords related to "finished," "what's next," or "bored" versus technical issues.
5.  **Social Feature Adoption Timing:** Measure the percentage of retained users who engaged with any social feature (even passively) before Day 21 versus those who did not. Determine if the "cliff" is exclusive to non-social users, which would validate the lack of social stickiness hypothesis.

**Immediate Next Step**
Run a cohort analysis grouping users by their "Day 0" (onboarding start date) and overlay their "Last Active Date" against the duration of their assigned initial program. This will confirm if the retention cliff is mechanically tied to program completion.
