**North Star Metric: 48-Hour Completed Care Cycle**
Redefine success from "Booking Made" to "First Session Attended within 48 Hours of Signup." Booking is a vanity metric in this context; it rises even when care quality falls. Your data confirms that speeding up onboarding increased bookings but decreased attendance, indicating a leak in real value delivery. The North Star must only rise when a user receives actual care.

**Input Metrics to Track**
1.  **Activation Rate:** % of users who book a session within 48 hours (current focus).
2.  **Fulfillment Rate:** % of booked users who actually attend the first session.
3.  **Quality Guardrail:** User-reported anxiety reduction (or session satisfaction) measured 24 hours post-attendance. Do not optimize speed if this guardrail drops.

**How to Read the Conflicting Signals**
Read the funnel bottom-up. Your current "win" (higher booking rate) is illusory because the retention/fulfillment bucket is leaking.
*   **The Diagnosis:** The correlation between "fast onboarding" and "low attendance" suggests the shortened flow is failing to establish commitment or ensure therapist-match fit. Users are signing up impulsively but not following through.
*   **The Rule:** Do not compare your 12% or 18% targets to industry benchmarks. Compare your new Fulfillment Rate strictly against your own historical baseline. If bookings rise to 18% but attendance drops below the historical equivalent of 12% of total signups, the redesign is a net failure regardless of the booking spike.
