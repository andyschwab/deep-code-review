### Recommended Analytics Events for NeueHeimat Hub

To demonstrate efficacy to your board and funders while respecting server constraints, stop measuring "downloads" as your primary success metric. Downloads are a vanity metric in your context; they measure file transfer, not integration. Instead, instrument these three specific events that correlate directly with successful outcomes (housing secured, classes enrolled, legal issues resolved):

1.  **`resource_action_completed`**
    *   **What it measures:** A user clicking a "Copy Email," "Click to Call," or "Get Directions" button on a housing, legal, or course listing.
    *   **Why this over downloads:** This captures the *intent to act* and the bridge to the real world. A refugee securing an apartment requires contacting a landlord, not just reading a PDF. This event answers the question: "Did the user take the step required to secure the resource?"
    *   **Data properties:** `resource_type` (housing/legal/course), `action_type` (call/email/map), `device_type`.

2.  **`returning_user_core_task`**
    *   **What it measures:** A unique user ID returning to the site within 7 days and performing a second search or viewing a detailed listing.
    *   **Why this over unique visitors:** Integration is a process, not a one-time visit. High unique visitor counts with low return rates indicate users are browsing but not finding what they need to proceed. Retention is the strongest proxy for "ongoing support" and trust. This answers: "Are users relying on us for more than a single lookup?"
    *   **Data properties:** `days_since_first_visit`, `previous_resource_viewed`.

3.  **`external_success_signal`**
    *   **What it measures:** A click on a specific "Mark as Found" or "I enrolled" button placed next to key resources, or a redirect to a partner's application form that you control the link for.
    *   **Why this over anecdotes:** This provides direct, self-reported confirmation of the outcome you care about. Even if only 10% of users click it, it gives you a verified baseline of success rates to extrapolate from, replacing guesswork with data. This answers: "What percentage of engaged users actually achieved the goal?"
    *   **Data properties:** `outcome_type` (apartment_found/class_enrolled), `time_to_outcome`.

**Implementation Note:** Do not track pageviews or time-on-site. With limited server capacity, every byte counts; store only these three events with their properties. Use a stable, pseudonymous ID for users to track retention without storing personal data (PII), adhering to privacy standards.

**Board Narrative:** Frame the annual report around "Actions Taken" and "Users Returning" rather than "Pages Viewed." This shifts the narrative from "we had traffic" to "we facilitated steps toward integration."

*Note: Since you cannot fabricate benchmarks, compare these metrics month-over-month against your own baseline starting now. Do not claim industry averages.*
