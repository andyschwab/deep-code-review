**Subject:** Recommendation for GreenFlow North Star Metric

**Executive Recommendation**
Adopt **"Weekly Active Households Completing a Conservation Action"** as the primary North Star Metric. Do not use "Gallons Saved" or "Percentage Engaging" as the single top-line metric.

"Gallons Saved" is a **vanity metric** in this context because it is heavily skewed by external factors you cannot control, such as local climate, seasonal rainfall, and pre-existing infrastructure efficiency. A dry summer in Springfield will artificially inflate savings regardless of user behavior, while a wet season in Riverton will suppress them, making cross-city comparison impossible without complex, disputed normalization models. Furthermore, this metric measures *outcome* rather than *product value*; if the app works perfectly but it rains all week, the metric drops, falsely signaling product failure.

"Percentage of Residents Engaging Weekly" is closer to behavior but risks measuring low-value activity (e.g., opening the app without acting).

The recommended metric—**Weekly Active Households Completing a Conservation Action**—isolates the specific user behavior that drives value: the act of logging a fix, setting a smart schedule, or completing an educational challenge. This rises only when users derive real value from the platform, independent of weather. It aligns product teams to improve the "aha" moment and gives city partners a clear count of active participants, which is often their primary political KPI.

**Comparative Analysis**

| Metric | Pros | Cons | Verdict |
| :--- | :--- | :--- | : |
| **Gallons Saved** | Directly ties to municipal cost savings. | **Unverifiable as a product metric.** Cannot normalize for climate/baseline without external data sources. Fluctuates with weather, not product quality. | **Reject** as North Star. Use as a secondary impact report only. |
| **% Residents Engaging** | Highlights adoption breadth; good for political "reach" narratives. | Vulnerable to vanity (clicks without action). Does not guarantee behavior change. | **Reject** as North Star. Use as an acquisition/activation input. |
| **Weekly Active Households Completing Action** | **Normalizes across cities.** Measures consistent behavior change. Directly correlates to long-term retention. | Requires clear definition of "Action." | **Adopt** as North Star. |

**Input Metrics to Move the North Star**
To drive the North Star up, track these inputs:
1.  **Activation Rate:** % of new households completing their first conservation action within 7 days.
2.  **Retention Cohort:** % of users who complete an action in Week 2 after doing so in Week 1.
3.  **Action Diversity:** Average number of *different* conservation actions taken per active household (prevents gaming the system with one repetitive task).

**Implementation Note for Springfield Meeting**
When speaking with the Mayor, frame the metric as **"Active Participation."** Explain that while total gallons fluctuate with the weather, the number of *residents taking action* is the true measure of community engagement and program health. This shields the partnership from blame during wet seasons and highlights success during dry ones based on effort, not luck.
