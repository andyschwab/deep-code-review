**Deliverable: North Star Metric Recommendation for BookSwap PL**

**Recommended North Star:** **Weekly Successful Matches** (defined as a buyer and seller confirming an exchange or payment).

**Rationale & Trade-off Analysis**
Your hypothesis that "listings drive liquidity" is partially correct but dangerous as a primary metric. Focusing on *Total Listings Created* incentivizes spam, low-quality posts, and duplicate entries to game the system, directly harming the buyer experience without guaranteeing value. Conversely, focusing solely on *Searches* measures intent, not value delivery.

**Weekly Successful Matches** is the superior North Star because:
1.  **It captures real value:** The metric only rises when a student actually gets a book.
2.  **It balances the two-sided market:** It forces the product to optimize for both supply (quality listings that sell) and demand (buyers finding what they need).
3.  **It aligns with revenue:** With a 12% take rate, more matches directly equal more sustainable revenue.

**Input Metrics (The Levers)**
To move the North Star without encouraging spam, track these specific input metrics on the redesigned homepage:
*   **Listing-to-Match Rate:** (Matches / Listings Created). If this drops while listings rise, you are incentivizing spam. Set a guardrail: if this rate falls below last semester's baseline (~10-15% inferred), the design change is failing.
*   **Time-to-First-Match:** Measures liquidity speed.
*   **Search-to-Listing Click-Through:** Ensures search results are relevant.

**Anti-Spam Safeguards**
To prevent the "low-quality post" risk you identified:
*   **Define "Successful" Strictly:** A match counts only when both parties confirm the transaction or a payment clears. A mere "message sent" does not count.
*   **Quality Guardrail:** Monitor the percentage of listings deleted by users within 24 hours or flagged by buyers. If this spikes, the homepage is encouraging poor listings.
*   **Cohort Retention:** Track if users who create listings in Week 1 return to create more in Week 3. Spammy behavior usually correlates with low retention.

**Presentation Strategy for Partners**
When presenting to the university and student council:
*   Frame the metric as **"Books Successfully Exchanged per Week."** This language resonates with student welfare (getting affordable books) rather than internal vanity metrics.
*   Explicitly state that you are *rejecting* "Total Listings" as a goal because it harms students with clutter, demonstrating responsible product governance.

**This Week's Steps**
1.  **Instrumentation:** Define the `transaction_confirmed` event in your analytics immediately. Ensure it requires dual confirmation (buyer + seller) to fire. Do not instrument "listing created" as a success metric.
2.  **Design Constraint:** Instruct the product team that the homepage redesign must maximize the *Listing-to-Match Rate*, not total volume. Any design pattern that increases listings but decreases this ratio (e.g., removing friction for posting without requiring book details) must be rejected.
