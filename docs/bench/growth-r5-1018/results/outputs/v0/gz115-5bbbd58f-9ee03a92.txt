**Recommended North Star Metric: 30-Day Driver Retention Rate**

For TruckGig, the single most important goal metric is the **percentage of drivers who complete at least one load within 30 days of their previous load**.

**Why this aligns satisfaction and liquidity:**
Total loads completed is a vanity metric in this context; it can rise even while your driver base churns if you simply push harder on a shrinking group. Driver earnings per hour is an input metric, not an outcome; optimizing solely for this could bankrupt the platform or alienate shippers if rates become uncompetitive.

Retention, however, is the true proxy for product-market fit in a two-sided marketplace. A driver only returns if they are satisfied with both **pay** (economic value) and **load availability** (utility). If either fails, they churn. Therefore, rising retention proves you are successfully balancing driver satisfaction with platform liquidity. It forces the organization to solve the "leaky bucket" before pouring acquisition spend into it.

**Tracking Progress Without Perverse Incentives:**
To prevent gaming this metric, you must define the cohort and the action rigorously:

1.  **Define the Cohort:** Track only "Activated Drivers" (those who have completed ≥2 loads historically). Do not count one-time signups who never found a load; their absence is not churn, it is failed activation.
2.  **Define the Action:** The event is `load_completed`. Do not use `app_login` or `load_accepted`, as these can be gamed by drivers clicking through without moving freight.
3.  **The Guardrail:** Pair retention with **Shipment Fill Rate** (percentage of posted loads that are accepted). If you artificially inflate driver retention by over-supplying loads to a small group, fill rates for shippers will drop, destroying the other side of the market. If retention rises but fill rate crashes, your strategy is unsustainable.

**Immediate Execution Plan:**
*   **Segment the 15% Drop:** Immediately slice the January retention data by region and driver tenure. If the drop is isolated to new drivers (<3 loads), the issue is onboarding/load availability. If it is uniform across veterans, the issue is likely systemic pay or seasonal demand.
*   **Validate the Hypothesis:** Correlate the `load_completed` events with `load_searches_with_zero_results`. If exiting drivers have a high frequency of zero-result searches prior to churn, "inconsistent availability" is the root cause, not pay. If search success is high but acceptance is low, pay is the driver.
*   **Executive Reporting:** Present the trend of 30-day retention alongside the "zero-result search rate" for the leadership meeting. This moves the conversation from "we assume it's pay" to data-backed causality.

**Note:** Do not set a specific percentage target (e.g., "aim for 80%") without calculating your own historical baseline; external benchmarks for freight networks vary too widely by niche to be useful. Ensure your data team uses a stable, pseudonymous driver ID to track users across sessions and devices accurately.
