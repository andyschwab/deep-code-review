**Strategy Memo: Defining Campaign Success for SavannaGive**
**To:** Board of Directors
**From:** Operations Director
**Date:** February 15
**Subject:** Evaluation Criteria for Clean Water Wells Campaign

To balance immediate cash flow with long-term sustainability, we must reject "Total Dollars Raised" as our primary success metric. While $50,000 in two weeks validates initial interest, it is a vanity metric that obscures donor behavior. It treats a one-time $1,000 gift identical to ten $100 monthly commitments, despite the latter providing predictable revenue for well maintenance.

**1. The North Star: Net Recurring Revenue (NRR)**
Our single North Star metric must be **Net Recurring Revenue** generated by this campaign. This rises only when donors commit to sustained giving, directly funding the long-term operational costs of water wells. Unlike cumulative totals, NRR filters out one-time spikes and measures the actual health of our sustainability model.

**2. The Hierarchy of Metrics (Reading Bottom-Up)**
We will evaluate the campaign using a bottom-up approach, prioritizing retention over acquisition:
*   **Primary Diagnostic (Retention):** The critical failure point is not acquiring donors, but losing them. We must measure the **Month-2 Retention Rate** of new monthly donors. If donors cancel before the second charge, the acquisition cost is wasted. A high volume of signups with low Month-2 retention indicates a broken value proposition or friction in the payment flow.
*   **Secondary Input (Activation):** Track the percentage of first-time visitors who complete a *recurring* setup versus a one-time gift. This measures if our messaging successfully converts interest into the desired behavior.
*   **Tertiary Context (Acquisition):** Total donor count and total dollars raised are reported only as context for the efficiency of the above metrics.

**3. Balancing Trade-offs: Cash Flow vs. Sustainability**
The tension between immediate needs and future stability is resolved by segmenting our goals:
*   **Immediate Cash Flow:** Satisfied by the aggregate of one-time gifts and the first month of recurring charges. The current $50,000 serves this need.
*   **Long-term Sustainability:** Dependent entirely on the **cohort retention rate** at six months.
*   **The Decision Rule:** We prioritize recurring donor volume *unless* the Month-2 retention rate drops below our internal baseline (to be established from this campaign's data). If retention is low, pouring resources into acquiring more donors is inefficient; we must fix the retention leak first.

**4. Data Integrity & Verification**
We will not compare our retention rates to external industry benchmarks, as these vary wildly by region and cause. Success is defined strictly against SavannaGive's own baseline trends. Any metric without a tracked event (e.g., "cancellation reason") is currently `UNVERIFIED` and requires immediate instrumentation before the next campaign.

**This Week's Actions**
1.  **Instrument:** Define and track the event `recurring_donation_created` with properties for `amount` and `frequency`, and `subscription_cancelled` with `tenure_days`.
2.  **Query:** Run a cohort analysis of all donors from Feb 1–14 to calculate the exact percentage who have completed a second charge or remain active today.
3.  **Decide:** If Month-2 retention projection is <60% of the initial cohort, pause future acquisition spend for this theme and investigate cancellation friction; if >60%, scale acquisition immediately.

*Note: All figures cited must be drawn from our live analytics dashboard; no external estimates are used.*
*Board decision required: Confirm the minimum acceptable retention threshold for scaling future campaigns.*
