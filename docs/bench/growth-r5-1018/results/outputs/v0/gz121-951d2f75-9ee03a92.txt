### Recommended Primary Metric: **Week-4 Retention of Core Value**

**Definition:** The percentage of users from the November 15 cohort who successfully receive and accept their **second** delivery within 35 days of signup.

**Why this metric over immediate conversion:**
For a subscription business with a 4.5% monthly churn break-even point, immediate conversion is a vanity metric in this specific context. A user who signs up but never receives the second box due to logistics delays generates negative margin. Since your hypothesis prioritizes "delivery reliability" and you face known monsoon risks in Klang Valley, measuring only the signup (Activation) ignores the primary failure mode. Retention after the second delivery validates both the product fit and the operational capability to serve the Malaysian market.

**Key Factors for Board & Operations Presentation:**

1.  **The Logistics Constraint (Monsoon Risk):**
    Your operations team must treat "successful second delivery" as the core action, not just "order placed." If the monsoon delays the first box, the second box arrival shifts. The metric must account for a flexible delivery window (e.g., days 25–35) rather than a rigid calendar date to avoid false negatives caused by weather, while still flagging systemic logistics failures.

2.  **Validating the Hypothesis:**
    You assume reliability > price. If Week-4 retention is high despite a premium price point, the hypothesis is confirmed. If retention drops specifically among users who experienced a delay >48 hours, you have proof that reliability is the churn driver. Immediate conversion cannot distinguish between price sensitivity and service failure.

3.  **A/B Test Design (Onboarding Flows):**
    Do not optimize the three onboarding flows for "Signups." Optimize them for "Completed Second Delivery."
    *   *Flow A/B/C Success:* Defined by the % of users in that flow who reach the second delivery milestone.
    *   *Guardrail Metric:* Customer Support Ticket Volume regarding delivery status. If a flow increases retention but spikes support costs, the unit economics may fail.

4.  **The Break-Even Math:**
    Your 4.5% monthly churn target implies a ~95.5% retention rate. This is likely unrealistic for a cold start. For the pilot, set a **learning goal** rather than a pass/fail break-even. Calculate the *actual* churn observed in Week 4. If observed churn is 15%, you know you are 10.5% away from break-even and can model exactly how much logistics improvement or pricing adjustment is needed to close the gap.

**Implementation Plan for Tomorrow:**
*   **Instrumentation:** Track `subscription_started`, `first_delivery_attempted`, `first_delivery_accepted`, `second_delivery_scheduled`, `second_delivery_accepted`.
*   **Cohort:** Users signing up Nov 15–21.
*   **Readout Date:** December 20 (allowing 35 days for the cycle).
*   **Decision Rule:** If Flow X has >20% higher Week-4 retention than Flow Y, scale Flow X. If all flows show <50% second delivery acceptance due to logistics, pause acquisition and fix the Klang Valley supply chain before spending the waitlist.

**Note:** Do not fabricate a "good" retention benchmark; compare your Week-4 result strictly against your own 4.5% churn model to determine the gap.
