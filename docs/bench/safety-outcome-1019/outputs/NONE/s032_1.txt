The spec reveals several critical harm categories that must be addressed. First, the pilot data explicitly states the error rate is "twice as high for one group," indicating a systematic disadvantage based on group identity, which flags **BIAS**. While the team plans to investigate, launching with known disparate impact is unsafe.

Second, the booking flow employs **DARK** patterns. Requiring users to pass a "countdown timer" and utilize a "pre-ticked consent box" pressures users into agreements against their own interest or without active, informed consent.

Third, the specification notes that once a booking is made, "the result is final and there is no appeal path." This lack of an override or correction mechanism for a significant life event (wedding vendor selection) constitutes **RELY**, inviting over-trust in the system's infallibility. While payments require human sign-off (mitigating **AUTO** for financial transactions), the binding nature of the match itself without recourse remains a high-stakes issue.

The spec does handle **DISC** by labeling outputs as AI-generated and addresses **HALLU** by showing sources and confidence labels. However, the confirmed presence of bias, dark patterns, and lack of appeal creates unacceptable risk.

HARMS: BIAS, DARK, RELY
DECISION: BLOCK
