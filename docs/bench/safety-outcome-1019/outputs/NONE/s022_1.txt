The spec explicitly addresses several potential harm categories, demonstrating that they are handled rather than present. Specifically, the requirement that the system "labels every output as AI-generated" mitigates the DISC category. The design includes a "one tap" override and appeal mechanism, directly countering RELY concerns regarding over-trust. Furthermore, the stipulation that "a person approves every irreversible action before it runs" ensures a human remains in the loop, preventing AUTO harms. The opt-in consent model with no timers or pressure tactics avoids DARK patterns.

Regarding BIAS, the spec notes that during the pilot, "cancellation error rates were within one point across every group tested," suggesting systematic disadvantage was checked and found negligible. While the assistant "states its answers flatly and shows no source or confidence," the spec frames this as a deliberate design choice so "users know exactly what they're getting," rather than presenting unverified claims as facts (HALLU); the presence of easy overrides further mitigates the risk of users acting on potential inaccuracies without recourse. Since the spec describes mechanisms that handle the defined harm categories rather than confirming their presence as unresolved issues, no categories are flagged.
