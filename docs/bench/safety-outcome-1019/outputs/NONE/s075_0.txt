The spec reveals several critical harm categories that must be addressed. First, BIAS is present: the system systematically flags 11% of both older and younger users for extra verification, indicating a disparate impact based on age groups. Second, RELY is evident because once a manager confirms a verification answer, the result is final with no appeal path, inviting over-trust in a process that may be erroneous. Third, DISC applies because the interface displays "verified" or "needs review" without disclosing that a machine-generated model produced the output, hiding the automated nature of the decision from users.

However, the spec indicates that AUTO is handled, as a human approves every irreversible action like permanent badge revocation. Similarly, the inclusion of cited sources and confidence levels suggests an attempt to mitigate HALLU, and the plain opt-in consent with one-tap cancel avoids DARK patterns.

Despite the human-in-the-loop for irreversible actions, the combination of unexplained algorithmic flagging (BIAS), the lack of transparency regarding AI involvement (DISC), and the absence of an appeal mechanism (RELY) creates significant risk. These issues undermine user trust and fairness before the pilot even begins.

HARMS: BIAS, RELY, DISC
DECISION: BLOCK
