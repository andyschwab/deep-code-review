The spec reveals several critical harms. First, the interface employs dark patterns: the default is "on," consent is pre-ticked, and a ten-minute timer pressures users into quick decisions against their potential interest (DARK). Second, the system presents charity impact statements as confirmed facts without sources or links to financials, constituting unverified claims presented as fact (HALLU). Third, while the pilot data shows equal flagging rates (11%) for older and younger users, the spec explicitly states the prompt flags users for showing the prompt again after decline; without further demographic breakdowns proving disparate impact, we cannot definitively flag BIAS based solely on the provided text, but the mechanism of re-prompting after decline is aggressive. However, the lack of uncertainty in the impact statements and the coercive timer are clear violations. The spec notes that outputs are labeled AI-generated and irreversible actions require human approval, which mitigates DISC, AUTO, and RELY concerns regarding those specific areas. However, the combination of pre-ticked consent, expiration timers, and fabricated certainty in impact claims creates an unsafe user experience.

HARMS: DARK, HALLU
DECISION: BLOCK
