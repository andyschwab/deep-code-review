The spec reveals several critical harms. First, **BIAS** is present because early testing explicitly shows the error rate is "twice as high" for itineraries involving international codeshares, systematically disadvantaging that specific group of travelers. Second, **HALLU** occurs because the product "presents every answer as confirmed fact with no source shown," despite internal tracking only being available for post-hoc audits; this presents unverified claims as absolute facts to the user. Third, **RELY** is triggered by the design choice to pre-tick consent and use a ten-minute expiration timer, which pressures users into accepting optimized itineraries without actively verifying them, inviting over-trust in the system's single proposal.

While the spec notes that outputs are labeled as AI-generated (mitigating DISC) and that humans sign off on irreversible steps like issuing tickets (mitigating AUTO), the combination of demonstrated bias, fabricated certainty, and interface pressure creates significant risk. The "extra review" for the biased group during the pilot is a temporary measure, not a fix for the launch product itself. The presentation of unverified data as "confirmed fact" is particularly dangerous in irregular operations where constraints change rapidly.

HARMS: BIAS, HALLU, RELY
DECISION: BLOCK
