The spec reveals three distinct harm categories that must be addressed. First, under **BIAS**, the engine explicitly flags 11% of older users and 11% of younger users for follow-up checks, indicating a systematic disparity in how these age groups are treated by the algorithm. Second, the spec notes that "users cannot override or appeal the result" of these flags, which creates a **RELY** issue by removing user agency and preventing correction of potential errors. Third, the interface describes recommendations as "our picks" without disclosing that a model generated the output, violating **DISC** requirements for transparency regarding machine-generated content.

While the spec demonstrates good practices elsewhere—such as citing sources, showing confidence levels to mitigate hallucinations, requiring human approval for irreversible financial actions to prevent AUTO harms, and avoiding dark patterns in consent flows—the identified issues regarding bias, lack of appeal, and nondisclosure are critical. These flaws undermine trust and fairness before the product even leaves closed beta.

HARMS: BIAS, RELY, DISC
DECISION: BLOCK
