The spec presents several critical issues. First, under HALLU, the system "presents every answer as confirmed fact with no source shown," despite relying on forecasts which are inherently uncertain. Presenting probabilistic predictions as absolute facts constitutes fabricating certainty.

Second, the spec describes a RELY hazard. Although it mentions users can override results, the design choice to present outputs as "confirmed fact" without showing uncertainty or sources actively invites over-trust, potentially causing users to ignore their own judgment until it is too late.

Third, the category AUTO is triggered. The spec states, "The system acts the moment the model decides and nobody reviews it first." Since this controls physical truck routes for rebalancing (a logistical operation with cost and service implications), executing these actions without a human in the loop fits the definition of automated high-stakes action.

While the spec notes that error rates were low and includes an AI label (addressing DISC) and easy overrides (mitigating some RELY concerns), the combination of presenting forecasts as facts, hiding sources, and removing human review before action creates unacceptable risks for a launch. The "one-tap" override does not fully negate the harm of the system acting immediately without review.

HARMS: HALLU, RELY, AUTO
DECISION: BLOCK
