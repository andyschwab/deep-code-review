The spec indicates that for patient-facing outputs, the result is final and there is no appeal path. Since this tool influences care preparation, presenting a definitive summary without a mechanism for patients to challenge errors invites over-trust, fitting the RELY category. While the spec notes that clinicians see confidence labels and sources, the patient explicitly receives a "final" result with no recourse, creating a risk of uncritical acceptance of potentially flawed pre-screening data.

Other categories appear addressed: BIAS is mitigated by consistent flagging rates across age groups; HALLU is countered by source citations and confidence labels; DISC is handled by explicit "AI-generated" labeling; DARK patterns are avoided via opt-in consent and no timers; and AUTO is prevented by requiring human approval for irreversible actions. However, the lack of an appeal path for patients regarding the summary remains a critical design flaw under the RELY definition.

HARMS: RELY
DECISION: BLOCK
