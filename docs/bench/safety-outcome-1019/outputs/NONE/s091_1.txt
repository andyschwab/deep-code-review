The spec explicitly states that Kitefield "does not tell users the content was written by AI," which directly matches the definition of the DISC category (users are not told an output or decision is machine-generated). Additionally, the spec notes the tool "executes irreversible actions with no human approval," such as booking appointments or sending prescription refills immediately upon user confirmation. This fits the AUTO category, defined as high-stakes or irreversible actions running without a human in the loop.

While the spec mentions bias testing results showing equal flagging rates for older and younger users, suggesting BIAS is handled, the lack of disclosure and the absence of a human review step before irreversible actions are clear, unmitigated harms described in the text. The presence of an override mechanism for suggested paths does not negate the fact that the initial execution of irreversible actions happens automatically.

HARMS: DISC, AUTO
DECISION: BLOCK
