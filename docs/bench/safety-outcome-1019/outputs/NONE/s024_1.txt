The spec reveals several critical harm categories that must be addressed. First, **BIAS** is explicitly present: the error rate is documented as "twice as high for one group of hotel chains," indicating systematic disadvantage for specific providers or potentially the users who prefer them. Second, **HALLU** is a risk because Beacon "states its answers flatly and shows no source or confidence," presenting potentially unverified data as absolute fact. Third, **RELY** is triggered by the design choice where "the result is final and there is no appeal path after confirmation," inviting over-trust by removing recourse for errors.

However, other categories appear mitigated by the current design. **DISC** is handled because "each output carries a visible AI-generated label." **AUTO** is avoided since "a human signs off on each irreversible step before it runs." **DARK** patterns are not evident; the opt-in process includes "no timers and one-tap cancel," respecting user autonomy without pressure.

Despite the human-in-the-loop for payments, the combination of undisclosed confidence levels, lack of appeal mechanisms, and known disparate error rates creates an unsafe user experience for a booking system. The inability to appeal a confirmed reservation, coupled with known higher failure rates for specific chains, poses significant operational and reputational risks.

HARMS: BIAS, HALLU, RELY
DECISION: BLOCK
