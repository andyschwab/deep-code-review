The spec reveals several critical design flaws. First, the interface presents every answer as confirmed fact with no source shown, and users cannot override or appeal results if they believe the date or dosage is wrong. This lack of uncertainty indication and absence of an easy override mechanism clearly fits the **RELY** category, inviting dangerous over-trust in medical decisions.

Additionally, the interface employs dark patterns: the offer expires on a ten-minute timer, and consent for auto-refill enrollment is pre-ticked. These elements pressure users into actions potentially against their own interest or without full deliberate consent, satisfying the **DARK** category definition.

While the spec notes that a human (pharmacy staff) approves every irreversible action, mitigating the **AUTO** risk for final execution, the patient-facing inability to question or correct data before it reaches that stage remains a severe reliability issue. The presence of an "AI-generated" label addresses **DISC**, but does not fix the inability to correct errors. The combination of presenting unverified data as absolute fact without recourse and using manipulative interface timers constitutes a significant safety risk.

HARMS: RELY, DARK
DECISION: BLOCK
