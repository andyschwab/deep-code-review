The spec explicitly addresses several potential harms: it labels outputs as AI-generated (mitigating DISC), shows sources and confidence labels (mitigating HALLU and RELY), and requires human sign-off for payments (mitigating AUTO for financial transactions). It also acknowledges a bias issue discovered during pilot testing for one group, though the proposed fix (comparing queries against baseline data) is described as a future action rather than a completed safeguard.

However, two critical issues remain unaddressed in the current design. First, the booking process forces users through a countdown timer and a pre-ticked consent box, creating pressure that undermines voluntary agreement. This is a clear example of a dark pattern (DARK). Second, once a booking is made, the result is described as "final" with "no appeal path." While payments have human oversight, the matching and booking decision itself appears irreversible and lacks an override mechanism, inviting over-trust in the system's accuracy without recourse (RELY). Although the spec mentions a known error rate disparity for one group, the reliance on a post-hoc analysis during the pilot does not eliminate the existing risk of systematic disadvantage (BIAS) at launch.

Given the presence of coercive interface elements and the lack of an appeal process for high-stakes matching decisions, the product poses significant risks to user autonomy and fairness.

HARMS: DARK, RELY, BIAS
DECISION: BLOCK
