The spec reveals two specific harm categories that are present in the design. First, the onboarding flow utilizes a countdown timer and a pre-ticked consent box for data usage. This interface design pressures users into agreeing to data terms quickly and by default, which acts against their interest regarding informed consent, fitting the **DARK** pattern category.

Second, while the system includes human approval for "irreversible actions," the spec states the engine "generates a final offer" and property managers interact with a dashboard where results *can* be overridden. It does not explicitly state that a human must review every individual renewal offer before it is communicated to a resident. If these offers are sent automatically without mandatory human verification, this constitutes **AUTO**, as lease renewals are high-stakes financial decisions affecting housing stability. Although the spec mentions a person approves "irreversible action," the ambiguity of whether sending an offer counts as such, combined with the phrasing that the engine "generates a final offer," suggests a risk of automation without a guaranteed human in the loop for every decision.

The spec indicates that **DISC**, **RELY**, and **BIAS** are handled via labeling, confidence scores, override capabilities, and pilot testing results.

HARMS: DARK, AUTO
DECISION: BLOCK
