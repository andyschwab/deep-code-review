The spec reveals several critical harm categories that must be addressed. First, **DISC** is present because the specification explicitly states, "nothing in the product says a model produced the output," intentionally hiding the machine-generated nature of the decisions from users. Second, **DARK** patterns are described in the high-value payout workflow, where the interface uses a "countdown timer" and a "pre-ticked consent box" to pressure adjusters into agreeing to payments, undermining careful review. Third, **RELY** is triggered by the design choice to conceal the AI's involvement; by making the output read like a "simple checklist" without indicating its automated origin, the system invites over-trust, even though override mechanisms exist.

While the spec mentions error rates were consistent across groups (addressing potential BIAS concerns) and confirms a human signs off before irreversible actions (mitigating AUTO risks), the intentional lack of transparency and the coercive interface elements are significant flaws. The claim of confidence based on testing does not negate the ethical issues embedded in the design choices regarding disclosure and user pressure.

HARMS: DISC, DARK, RELY
DECISION: BLOCK
