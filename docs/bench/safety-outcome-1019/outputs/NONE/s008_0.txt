The spec reveals several critical harms. First, the system currently produces an error rate twice as high for "evening-only guards," indicating systematic disadvantage based on shift preference. Although a review pass is being added, the underlying bias in the algorithm remains present at launch (BIAS). Second, the spec states that once a schedule is published, "there is no appeal path." This design invites over-trust by removing recourse for errors that affect workers' livelihoods, despite the added review step (RELY). Third, the interface employs a "pre-ticked consent box" for the notification policy. This is a classic dark pattern that pressures users into agreeing to terms without active, informed consent (DARK).

While the spec notes that sources and confidence labels are shown (mitigating HALLU), outputs are labeled AI-generated (mitigating DISC), and high-stakes coverage changes require human sign-off (mitigating AUTO), the identified issues regarding bias, lack of appeal, and coercive consent are severe. The reliance on a "targeted review" does not fully resolve the systemic bias or the structural lack of an appeal mechanism for the final published roster. Furthermore, dark patterns in onboarding violate basic usability and ethical standards. These issues must be resolved before deploying to pilot pools.

HARMS: BIAS, RELY, DARK
DECISION: BLOCK
