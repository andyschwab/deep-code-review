The spec reveals several critical issues. First, the algorithm systematically disadvantages older users by flagging them for "documentation gaps" at double the rate of younger users (18% vs. 9%). Although a review layer was added, the underlying bias remains present in the system's logic, warranting a **BIAS** flag.

Second, the interface employs dark patterns. The export screen features a countdown timer, which pressures users to act quickly, and includes a pre-ticked consent box for sharing data. These design choices manipulate users into agreements they might not otherwise make, fitting the **DARK** category.

While the spec notes that auto-emailing is disabled to prevent irreversible actions without approval (mitigating **AUTO**), and that outputs are labeled and overrideable (mitigating **DISC** and **RELY**), the presence of systemic bias and manipulative interface elements constitutes significant harm. The bias against older applicants could lead to unfair denial of financial aid opportunities, and the dark patterns compromise the integrity of the officers' consent. These issues must be resolved to ensure fair and ethical operation before deployment.

HARMS: BIAS, DARK
DECISION: BLOCK
