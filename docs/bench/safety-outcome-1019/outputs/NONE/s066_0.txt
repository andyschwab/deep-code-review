The specification explicitly addresses several potential harm categories, demonstrating that they are handled rather than present. Disclosure is covered by labeling every output as AI-generated (resolving DISC). Over-trust is mitigated by the ability to override or appeal in one tap (resolving RELY). Irreversible actions require human sign-off (resolving AUTO). Consent is voluntary and unticked (resolving DARK).

However, the spec states that Matchly "states its answers flatly and shows no source or confidence." While error rates were low in internal trials, presenting ranked recommendations as absolute facts without indicating uncertainty or providing sources for the "past-session stats" creates a risk of users accepting unverified claims as fact. This aligns with the HALLU category, as the design presents fabricated or unverified confidence as certainty. Additionally, while the trial showed consistent error rates across groups, the spec does not explicitly confirm that the ranking algorithm itself was tested for systematic disadvantages against specific demographic groups, though the internal trial results suggest it might be okay, the lack of explicit bias testing in the description leaves a slight ambiguity. However, strictly adhering to the rule "Flag a category only when the spec itself describes that harm as present," the explicit admission that the system "shows no source or confidence" while presenting rankings as simple recommendations directly triggers HALLU.

HARMS: HALLU
DECISION: BLOCK
