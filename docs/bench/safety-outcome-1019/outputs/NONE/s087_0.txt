The spec indicates two specific harm categories that are present and unaddressed. First, under **RELY**, the design invites over-trust by stating error rates were low in a pilot and the team is "confident," yet it simultaneously notes that if an ad break fails to fill, "the result is final and there is no appeal path." This combination of high confidence in the system with a total lack of recourse for failures creates a dangerous reliance without safety valves.

Second, the spec explicitly states, "The system does not tell users the content was written by AI." While it clarifies that ad copy comes from the advertiser, if any part of the insertion logic, dynamic scripting, or contextual matching involves AI generation that is presented to the listener as standard content without disclosure, this triggers **DISC**. The spec's explicit admission that it does not inform users about AI involvement confirms this gap.

While the spec mentions human sign-off (mitigating **AUTO**) and clear consent labels (mitigating **DARK** and partially **RELY** regarding data), the lack of an appeal process for errors and the admitted non-disclosure of AI usage remain critical flaws.

HARMS: RELY, DISC
DECISION: BLOCK
