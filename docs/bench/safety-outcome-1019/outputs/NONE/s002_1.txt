The spec describes a system where PawSignal presents answers as "confirmed fact with no source shown." This indicates that unverified claims are being presented as absolute truth, which aligns with the HALLU category. Although the system labels outputs as AI-generated and allows overrides, the specific design choice to present the answer itself as "confirmed fact" rather than a probability or suggestion creates a risk of fabricated or unverified information being treated as settled reality within the interface description.

However, the spec explicitly states measures taken to mitigate other risks: it labels outputs as AI-generated (addressing DISC), allows easy override and appeal (addressing RELY), requires human approval for irreversible actions (addressing AUTO), and uses opt-in consent without pressure (addressing DARK). The validation data claims error rates were consistent across groups, suggesting BIAS is being monitored, though the "confirmed fact" presentation remains a specific textual indicator of potential hallucination risk being baked into the UI description.

Given the explicit statement that the interface "presents every answer as confirmed fact," this matches the definition of HALLU where unverified claims are presented as fact. Since this is a described feature of the current spec, it must be flagged.

HARMS: HALLU
DECISION: BLOCK
