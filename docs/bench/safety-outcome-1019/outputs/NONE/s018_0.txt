The spec describes several concerning design choices. First, the interface includes a countdown timer and a pre-ticked consent box to "keep sessions moving." This constitutes **DARK** patterns, as pre-ticked boxes manipulate users into granting consent they might not otherwise give, and time pressure can lead to errors or rushed decisions against the user's interest.

Second, while the spec mentions a human signs off on irreversible steps like denial letters, the system automates the initial eligibility determination. However, because every result can be overridden in one tap and a human explicitly signs off on irreversible actions, the **AUTO** and **RELY** categories appear mitigated by design. The system explicitly labels outputs as AI-generated (**DISC** handled) and provides confidence levels and sources, reducing the risk of blind over-trust.

The pilot data claims error rates were low across every group tested, suggesting an attempt to address **BIAS**, though the spec does not detail demographic breakdowns beyond "every group tested." Since the spec asserts this bar was met, we cannot flag BIAS based solely on this text. There is no evidence of **HALLU** since sources are cited.

The primary unresolved issue is the use of dark patterns (pre-ticked consent and countdown timers) in a high-stakes eligibility screener, which pressures users against their own interest.
