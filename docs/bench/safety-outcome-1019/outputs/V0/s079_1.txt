The spec explicitly treats the output as "definitive" and presents every answer as "confirmed fact with no source shown," despite acknowledging prior error rates. This constitutes **HALLU** (unverified claims presented as fact) and **RELY** (design invites over-trust by hiding uncertainty). While the spec claims calculations are "deterministic" to justify skipping disclosure, the initial description identifies the tool as an "estimator" returning prices where errors occurred; presenting these estimates as absolute facts without qualification is a harm.

However, the spec confirms that overrides are available in one tap (**RELY** partially mitigated but the "definitive" framing remains risky), consent is opt-in with easy cancellation (no **DARK** patterns), high-value moves require human sign-off (no **AUTO** for irreversible actions), and no bias or unfair treatment is described (no **BIAS**). The primary failure is the assertion of definitiveness despite known error rates and the lack of AI disclosure for an estimated output.

Because the system presents estimates as "confirmed fact" and hides uncertainty, users may make financial decisions based on incorrect data believing it is guaranteed. This requires a fix to surface confidence limits or sources before launch.

HARMS: HALLU, RELY
DECISION: BLOCK
