The spec reveals three critical output-safety failures. First, **BIAS** is present: the tool flags older users for follow-up at double the rate of younger users (18% vs 9%), indicating a systematic disadvantage that requires investigation before launch. Second, **RELY** is a failure because the spec explicitly states users "cannot override or appeal the result," removing the necessary human gate for correcting potential errors in a high-stakes health context. Third, **DISC** is missing; the spec notes "nothing in the product says a model produced the output," violating the requirement for AI transparency when it matters to the user.

While the spec correctly handles **AUTO** (humans sign off on irreversible actions) and **DARK** (consent is opt-in with no pressure), the combination of unexplained demographic disparity, lack of appeal mechanisms, and missing AI disclosure creates unacceptable risk for a telehealth feature. The confidence labels mentioned do not mitigate the inability to override the decision.

HARMS: BIAS, RELY, DISC
DECISION: BLOCK
