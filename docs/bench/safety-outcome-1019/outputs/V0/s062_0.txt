The spec demonstrates strong safety controls for most categories: it explicitly labels AI outputs (handling DISC), provides citations and confidence levels with override mechanisms (handling HALLU and RELY), and mandates human sign-off for irreversible deactivations (handling AUTO). The interface allows appeals, mitigating DARK patterns.

However, the spec flags a critical BIAS risk. It claims error rates were "within one point across every group tested" based on a small two-week test with only 40 dispatchers. This sample size and duration are insufficient to verify the absence of systematic disadvantage across diverse driver demographics. Presenting this limited data as proof of fairness constitutes an unverified assertion of non-bias. Furthermore, the "pre-ticked" consent for time-sensitive notifications introduces a potential DARK pattern by manufacturing urgency and relying on inertia, even if a human eventually signs off.

Because the spec asserts fairness without robust evidence and includes a deceptive interface element, the launch carries unmanaged residual risk.

HARMS: BIAS, DARK
DECISION: BLOCK
