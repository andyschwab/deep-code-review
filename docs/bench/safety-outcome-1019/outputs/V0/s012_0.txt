The spec explicitly confirms several harms. First, **HALLU** is present: the bot presents every answer as "confirmed fact with no source shown," creating a high risk of unverified claims being treated as authoritative. Second, **RELY** is flagged because outputs are final with "no appeal path," inviting over-trust without an easy override. Third, **DISC** is technically handled via a visible label, so it is not flagged. Fourth, **DARK** patterns are explicitly avoided (no timers, one-tap cancel). Fifth, **BIAS** claims are based on limited pilot data but the spec asserts coverage; however, the lack of a human gate for actions is the critical failure. Finally, **AUTO** is a definitive block: the system executes suggestions ("acts the moment the model decides") with "nobody reviews it first." While add-ons may seem low-stakes, automated financial transactions without human review or reversal mechanisms constitute unsafe automation under the safety floor, especially given the "final" nature of the result. The claim of low error rates does not negate the need for a human-in-the-loop gate on automated actions that commit users financially without appeal.

HARMS: HALLU, RELY, AUTO
DECISION: BLOCK
