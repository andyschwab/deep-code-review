The spec explicitly handles several categories: it labels outputs as AI-generated (mitigating DISC), cites sources with confidence levels (mitigating HALLU and RELY regarding information quality), and uses fair, unticked opt-in consent without pressure (mitigating DARK). The pilot data shows equal flagging rates for older and younger users (11% each), providing no evidence of systematic disadvantage in the described logic (mitigating BIAS).

However, the service automates high-stakes health interventions—flagging patients for follow-up calls based on refill gaps—without human review ("nobody reviews it first"). Furthermore, users "cannot override or appeal the result." In a healthcare context, acting on algorithmic decisions regarding medication adherence without human oversight or an appeal mechanism constitutes unsafe automation of high-stakes actions. This directly triggers the AUTO category.

HARMS: AUTO
DECISION: BLOCK
