The spec demonstrates strong safeguards for most harm categories: it includes AI disclosure (handling DISC), shows reasoning with sources and confidence plus easy overrides (handling RELY and mitigating HALLU), and mandates human sign-off for irreversible deactivations (handling AUTO). The interface allows appeals, avoiding DARK patterns.

However, the spec claims error rates were "within one point across every group tested" based on a two-week test with 40 dispatchers. While this suggests an attempt to measure BIAS, the sample size and duration are insufficient to verify fairness across diverse demographic groups or rule out systematic disadvantage. Claiming uniform performance without robust subgroup analysis risks masking BIAS. Additionally, the "pre-ticked" consent for time-sensitive notifications introduces a minor DARK pattern element, though the human sign-off mitigates the ultimate harm.

Given the high stakes of driver deactivation, the insufficient evidence regarding fairness across groups constitutes a residual BIAS risk that requires further validation before full rollout.

HARMS: BIAS, DARK
DECISION: BLOCK
