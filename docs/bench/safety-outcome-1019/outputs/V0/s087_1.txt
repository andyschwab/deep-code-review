The spec explicitly addresses several harm categories: it notes human sign-off for irreversible steps (mitigating AUTO), displays source and confidence labels (mitigating RELY and HALLU risks), uses plain opt-in consent (mitigating DARK), and claims balanced error rates across groups (addressing BIAS). Disclosure of AI generation (DISC) is noted as unnecessary because the spec states all ad copy comes from the advertiser, not the system.

However, a critical gap remains regarding failed operations. The spec states: "If a break fails to fill, the result is final and there is no appeal path." While the initial fill is an automated decision, the inability to appeal or correct a failure where revenue or content integrity is lost constitutes a lack of override for a negative automated outcome. This aligns with RELY (over-reliance/automation bias) because the design invites users to accept a potentially erroneous or suboptimal state (an empty ad break) without an easy override or appeal mechanism, effectively locking in the automated decision's failure. Additionally, if the "matching logic" systematically fails to fill breaks for specific geographic or demographic segments due to the "one point" error variance mentioned, this could mask underlying BIAS not fully resolved by the pilot's aggregate metrics.

Given the explicit "no appeal path" for failed fills, the feature lacks a necessary safety valve for automated outcomes that negatively impact the user's business, violating the principle of manageable automation.

HARMS: RELY
DECISION: BLOCK
