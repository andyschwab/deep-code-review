The spec demonstrates active mitigation for most harm categories. It explicitly includes an "AI-generated label" (addressing DISC), allows overrides/appeals in "one tap" with plans treated as a "draft" (addressing RELY), and mandates that "a person approves every irreversible action" (addressing AUTO). The pilot data shows equal flagging rates (11%) for older and younger users, suggesting no evident BIAS in the described outcome. Consent flows lack timers and allow easy cancellation, avoiding DARK patterns.

However, the spec states Loop "states its answers flatly and shows no source or confidence." While operators currently treat the plan as a draft due to workflow culture, the system itself provides no uncertainty indicators or sourcing for its forecasts and move proposals. If operators rely on these flat statements without verifying underlying demand data, unverified claims could be acted upon as fact. This presents a residual HALLU risk where fabricated or inaccurate forecasts are presented with an authority the evidence (hidden from the user) does not support within the interface itself. Because the system lacks built-in confidence tiers or source attribution to prevent over-reliance on potentially hallucinated data points, this specific gap must be addressed before launch to ensure safety relies on interface design, not just user habit.

HARMS: HALLU
DECISION: BLOCK
