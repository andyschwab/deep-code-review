The spec explicitly addresses most harm categories: it labels outputs as AI-generated (handling DISC), requires human approval for irreversible actions (handling AUTO), provides one-tap override and appeal (handling RELY), and uses unticked consent without pressure (handling DARK). It also cites pilot data showing consistent error rates across groups, indicating BIAS was measured and found acceptable within the pilot scope.

However, the spec states the assistant "states its answers flatly and shows no source or confidence." By presenting recommendations as absolute facts without indicating uncertainty or providing sources for rate comparisons and upgrade negotiations, the feature invites users to trust fabricated or unverified claims as truth. This directly matches the definition of HALLU (unverified or fabricated claims presented as fact). Since the design intentionally omits confidence indicators, this risk remains present despite the other controls.

HARMS: HALLU
DECISION: BLOCK
