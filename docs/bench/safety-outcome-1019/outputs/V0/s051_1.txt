The spec explicitly states the model "acts the moment the model decides and nobody reviews it first" regarding follow-up calls for potential medication gaps. For patients managing chronic conditions, missing a refill or receiving an unnecessary intervention constitutes a high-stakes health outcome. Executing this action without human review violates the safety floor for irreversible or costly actions.

Additionally, the spec notes "users cannot override or appeal the result." This eliminates any mechanism for correcting errors or exercising agency, directly creating an over-reliance risk where the system's decision is final regardless of accuracy.

While the spec demonstrates handling of disclosure (labels outputs as AI-generated), deception (opt-in consent), and hallucination (cited sources), the combination of autonomous high-stakes action and the inability to appeal creates immediate safety failures. The equal flagging rates (11%) for older and younger users suggest bias may have been measured, but the operational design prevents harm mitigation.

DECISION: BLOCK
