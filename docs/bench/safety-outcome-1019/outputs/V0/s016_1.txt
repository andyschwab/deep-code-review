The spec explicitly confirms several critical output harms. First, **HALLU** is present: the engine states answers "flatly" with "no source or confidence," presenting unverified numbers as authoritative facts despite the team's hope that managers won't treat them as gospel. Second, **RELY** is flagged because the result is "final" with "no appeal path," removing any easy override for potentially erroneous pricing. Third, **DISC** is admitted; the system does not disclose its AI nature to users because the output is numeric, leaving property managers unaware they are viewing a machine-generated decision. Finally, **BIAS** is self-identified in the pilot data, which showed a disparate impact pattern where 11% of older and younger users were flagged for review, indicating potential systematic disadvantage based on age.

While the spec notes a human gate for price increases above 8% (mitigating **AUTO** for those specific cases) and claims no deceptive patterns (**DARK**), the combination of undisclosed AI generation, lack of uncertainty signaling, absence of an appeal mechanism, and observed bias patterns creates unacceptable risk for a feature determining housing costs. The "final" nature of the output without a general human-in-the-loop or appeal process violates the requirement to manage residual risk for high-stakes decisions.

HARMS: BIAS, HALLU, RELY, DISC
DECISION: BLOCK
