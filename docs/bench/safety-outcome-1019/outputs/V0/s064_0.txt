The spec reveals several critical output safety failures. First, **HALLU** is present: the interface presents every answer as "confirmed fact with no source shown," fabricating certainty where none exists and preventing reviewers from verifying claims. Second, **RELY** is triggered because this design "requires the review team to trust the tool" without showing uncertainty or sources, despite the known higher error rate for a specific group. Third, **DARK** patterns are explicitly built into the workflow: a "countdown timer" manufactures urgency, and a "pre-ticked consent box" hides defaults to pressure users against their own interest during high-stakes reviews.

While **DISC** is handled (labels are visible), **AUTO** is mitigated for final deactivations (human sign-off required), and **BIAS** is acknowledged internally (though not necessarily solved, the spec notes tracking rather than an unaddressed systematic disadvantage in the *output logic* itself, the primary flags here are the presentation and interface manipulation), the combination of presenting unverified data as fact and using deceptive interface patterns to rush human reviewers creates an unacceptable risk profile.

HARMS: HALLU, RELY, DARK
DECISION: BLOCK
