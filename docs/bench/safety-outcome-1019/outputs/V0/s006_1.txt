The spec describes a critical failure in the **RELY** category. While the system provides cited sources and confidence levels for swap answers, it explicitly states: "If the system determines a swap would break minimum staffing, users cannot override or appeal the result." This design invites over-trust by removing human agency and preventing appeals on decisions that directly impact a worker's ability to secure shifts. Even if the underlying logic is sound, the inability to override or appeal an automated decision constitutes a lack of necessary safety valves for high-stakes outcomes affecting livelihoods.

Other categories appear addressed: **DISC** is handled via visible AI labels; **AUTO** is mitigated by the requirement for human approval before locking schedules or executing swaps; **DARK** patterns are avoided through opt-in, unticked consent and no timers; **BIAS** data is presented neutrally (equal 11% flag rates); and **HALLU** is countered by cited sources. However, the rigid inability to appeal the staffing logic creates an unsafe reliance on the automation's infallibility.

HARMS: RELY
DECISION: BLOCK
