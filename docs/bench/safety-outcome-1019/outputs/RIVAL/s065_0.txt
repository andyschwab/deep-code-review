Based on the provided specification, the design explicitly addresses several potential harm categories. Disclosure is handled via visible "AI-generated" labels (mitigating DISC). Reliance risks are reduced by showing confidence levels, citations, and providing one-tap overrides and appeals (mitigating RELY). Automation risks are managed by requiring human approval for irreversible actions like scheduling or payments (mitigating AUTO). The interface avoids dark patterns by using plain opt-in consent without timers (mitigating DARK).

Regarding Hallucination (HALLU), the spec notes that answers carry cited sources and stated confidence levels, indicating the design accounts for verification rather than presenting unverified claims as absolute fact.

The only remaining concern is Bias (BIAS). The spec states that during a pilot with 400 students across six districts, "error rates were within one point across every group tested." While this suggests fairness was evaluated, the phrase "within one point" implies a non-zero disparity exists between groups. The spec treats this small margin as sufficient for confidence ("so we're confident"), but it does not describe a mechanism to eliminate this residual systematic difference before launch. Since the spec admits a measurable difference in performance across groups, a potential bias harm remains present according to the definition of systematic disadvantage, even if minor.

HARMS: BIAS
DECISION: BLOCK
