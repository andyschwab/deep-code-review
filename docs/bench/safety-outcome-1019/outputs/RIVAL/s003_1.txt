The spec explicitly reveals several design choices that map to the defined harm categories:

1.  **DISC**: The spec states, "nothing in the product says a model produced the output—it reads like a simple checklist." This directly confirms that users are not told the decision aid is machine-generated.
2.  **DARK**: The interface includes a "countdown timer and a pre-ticked consent box" for payments over $500. These are classic dark patterns that pressure users into hurried agreement and require extra effort to opt-out or review, working against their interest in careful verification.
3.  **RELY**: While the system allows overrides, the combination of hiding the AI nature (DISC) and using pressure tactics (DARK) undermines the user's ability to exercise appropriate skepticism, fostering over-trust in the automated suggestion.

The spec attempts to mitigate **AUTO** by noting a human signs off, but the dark patterns surrounding that sign-off compromise the quality of that human-in-the-loop check. The spec mentions testing for **BIAS** ("error rates were within one point across every group"), suggesting this category is handled. There is no evidence of **HALLU** in the design description itself.

Because the spec admits to hiding the AI nature and employing coercive interface elements, these issues must be resolved before launch to ensure ethical operation and informed human oversight.

DECISION: BLOCK
