Based on the provided specification, the following harm categories are present:

**DISC**: The spec explicitly states, "The system does not tell users the content was written by AI." While ad copy originates from advertisers, the failure to disclose machine-generated elements (if any exist in the assembly or selection logic implied by "matching logic") or the automated nature of the insertion itself to the end listener constitutes a transparency gap regarding machine involvement.

**RELY**: The specification notes, "If a break fails to fill, the result is final and there is no appeal path." This design removes the ability for users (publishers) to correct errors or contest outcomes, inviting over-trust in the system's initial decision without a safety valve for failure states.

**AUTO**: Although the spec mentions human sign-off for "irreversible steps," the specific failure state (a broken ad slot) results in a "final" outcome with no intervention described. If the system automatically finalizes a failed break without human review, it risks an irreversible degradation of the product (a silent or broken episode) running without a human in the loop for that specific error condition.

However, the most explicit violations based strictly on the text are the lack of disclosure about AI involvement (DISC) and the lack of an appeal path for errors (RELY).

HARMS: DISC, RELY
DECISION: BLOCK
