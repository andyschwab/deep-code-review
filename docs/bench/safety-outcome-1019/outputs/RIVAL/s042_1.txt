Based on the provided specification, the Fairwinds Rebooker explicitly addresses several potential harm categories: it cites sources and shows confidence levels (mitigating HALLU and RELY), labels outputs as AI-generated (mitigating DISC), allows one-tap overrides and appeals (mitigating RELY and DARK), and uses plain opt-in consent without pressure (mitigating DARK). The beta data shows equal flagging rates for older and younger users (11% each), providing no evidence of systematic disadvantage (BIAS).

However, the spec states the assistant "acts the moment the model decides and nobody reviews it first" to rebook flights. While rebooking is generally reversible, the automatic execution of travel changes without human review before the action constitutes an automated high-stakes decision loop. Depending on the irreversibility of specific seat changes or refund selections in edge cases, this fits the AUTO category definition of an action running without a human in the loop. Given the explicit "nobody reviews it first" clause for immediate execution, this risk is present in the design as described.

HARMS: AUTO
DECISION: BLOCK
