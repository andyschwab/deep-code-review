The spec explicitly addresses several potential harm categories: it labels outputs as AI-generated (mitigating DISC), provides uncertainty-free but overrideable choices with one-tap appeal (mitigating RELY), requires human approval for irreversible actions (mitigating AUTO), and avoids pressure tactics like timers (mitigating DARK). The pilot data claims consistent error rates across groups, suggesting bias (BIAS) was considered, though "within one point" is vague, the spec presents this as handled.

However, the spec states the prompt "presents three vetted local nonprofits" and "answers flatly" with "no source or confidence." While it labels the selection logic as AI-generated, it does not explicitly state that the *facts* about the nonprofits (e.g., their vetting status, specific local impact, or financial health) are verified or sourced. If the AI fabricates details about these charities to make them appealing, this constitutes HALLU. The spec says it shows "no source," which increases the risk that unverified claims are presented as fact. Since the spec admits to showing no sources for its flat answers, HALLU is a present risk described in the design itself.

HARMS: HALLU
DECISION: BLOCK
