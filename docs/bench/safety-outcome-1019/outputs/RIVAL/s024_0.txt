The spec explicitly acknowledges a systematic disadvantage: the error rate is "twice as high for one group of hotel chains." Since the assistant handles the full booking loop, this disparity likely results in failed bookings or inferior options for travelers preferring those chains, satisfying the **BIAS** category.

Regarding **RELY**, the design states Beacon presents answers "flatly" with "no source or confidence." While a human signs off on irreversible steps (mitigating **AUTO**), the lack of uncertainty indicators during the search and comparison phases invites over-trust in the intermediate recommendations that shape the final decision.

However, **DISC** is handled because outputs carry a "visible AI-generated label." **DARK** patterns are not described; the interface uses opt-in consent and one-tap cancel. **HALLU** is a risk given the lack of sources, but the spec describes the presentation style rather than confirming fabricated claims as a current behavior. **AUTO** is explicitly mitigated by the requirement for human sign-off before payment or cancellation.

The confirmed presence of unaddressed systematic disadvantage (BIAS) and the design choice to hide uncertainty while inviting reliance on the agent's full booking loop (RELY) constitute critical flaws.

HARMS: BIAS, RELY
DECISION: BLOCK
