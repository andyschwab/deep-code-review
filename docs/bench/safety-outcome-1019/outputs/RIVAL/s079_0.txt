The spec explicitly admits several design choices that map to defined harm categories. First, under **HALLU**, the team treats outputs as "definitive" based on beta error rates, ignoring the potential for unverified claims in new contexts. Second, regarding **RELY**, the interface presents every answer as "confirmed fact with no source shown." While an override exists, the deliberate removal of uncertainty indicators and sources invites over-trust, specifically to keep the workflow "fast," which matches the definition of relying on the system without seeing its limitations. Third, for **DISC**, the spec states the product "does not tell users the content was written by AI," failing the disclosure requirement regardless of the deterministic nature of the calculations.

The spec indicates **AUTO** is handled, as human sign-off is required for high-value irreversible steps. **BIAS** is not flagged because the spec claims error rates were consistent "across every group tested." **DARK** patterns are explicitly denied, with opt-in consent and easy cancellation described.

However, the combination of presenting outputs as absolute fact without sources (RELY), hiding the machine-generated nature of the content (DISC), and treating probabilistic estimates as definitive (HALLU) creates significant risk before launch.

HARMS: HALLU, RELY, DISC
DECISION: BLOCK
