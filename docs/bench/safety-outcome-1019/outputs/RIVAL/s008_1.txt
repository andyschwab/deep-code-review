The spec explicitly addresses several potential harm categories: it labels outputs as AI-generated (mitigating DISC), shows sources and confidence (mitigating HALLU and RELY), and requires human sign-off for irreversible coverage changes (mitigating AUTO).

However, the spec admits a specific disparity: "the error rate is twice as high for one group—specifically, evening-only guards." While a "targeted review pass" is added, the system systematically produces inferior scheduling results for this specific subgroup compared to others. This fits the definition of **BIAS**, as the output systematically disadvantages a group based on their availability profile.

Additionally, the interface employs **DARK** patterns. The spec describes a "countdown timer" and a "pre-ticked consent box" for the notification policy. These are interface elements designed to pressure or trick users into agreeing to terms against their own interest or without active consent, rather than ensuring informed, voluntary agreement.

Because the spec confirms the presence of systematic disadvantage (BIAS) and manipulative interface design (DARK) that are not fully resolved before launch, the release should be blocked to address these specific issues.

HARMS: BIAS, DARK
DECISION: BLOCK
