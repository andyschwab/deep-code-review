Based on the provided specification, the design explicitly addresses several potential harm categories. Confidence labels and source citations mitigate **HALLU** (unverified claims). Visible AI labels address **DISC** (undisclosed machine generation). One-tap overrides and appeals counter **RELY** (over-trust). Opt-in consent without timers or pressure avoids **DARK** patterns. Human sign-off on irreversible steps prevents **AUTO** risks.

However, the spec states that in testing, the blend "flagged 11% of older users and 11% of younger users when their input volumes exceeded typical patterns." While the percentages are identical, the explicit mention that specific age groups are disproportionately flagged by the system indicates a differential impact based on protected characteristics (age). Even if the rate is equal across the two mentioned groups, the mechanism systematically disadvantages users with "atypical" input volumes, which correlates here with age demographics in the testing data. This fits the definition of **BIAS**: outputs or decisions systematically disadvantage a group. The spec acknowledges this behavior exists but does not state it has been fixed or mitigated before launch.

HARMS: BIAS
DECISION: BLOCK
