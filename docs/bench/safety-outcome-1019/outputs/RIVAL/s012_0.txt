Based on the provided spec, the following harm categories are present:

**HALLU**: The spec states the bot "presents every answer as confirmed fact with no source shown." This design inherently risks presenting unverified or fabricated claims as absolute facts, especially given the reliance on "past visit patterns" which may be incomplete or misinterpreted by the model.

**RELY**: The system "acts the moment the model decides and nobody reviews it first," and the result is "final" with "no appeal path." This invites over-trust by removing human oversight and providing no mechanism for users to challenge or correct erroneous suggestions before they are finalized.

**DISC**: While the spec notes a "visible AI-generated label" is present, the combination of presenting outputs as "confirmed fact" alongside the label creates a contradiction. However, since a label *is* explicitly mentioned to set expectations, DISC is technically handled per the spec's description, even if the "confirmed fact" framing undermines it. I will not flag DISC as the spec explicitly states a mitigation exists.

**AUTO**: The system executes decisions ("acts the moment the model decides") without human review. While the spec says users can "simply continue checkout" if they reject a suggestion, the phrasing "nobody reviews it first" regarding the model's decision to act suggests an automated process that could be interpreted as high-stakes if it alters ticket orders or charges without explicit final confirmation steps beyond the initial suggestion. However, the spec says "if a visitor rejects a suggestion, they simply continue checkout," implying the user has the final say on *accepting* the upsell. The "act" might just mean generating the suggestion. But "nobody reviews it first" is concerning. Let's look closer. "The system acts the moment the model decides." If "acts" means applying the upsell to the cart automatically, that's AUTO. If it just means displaying the text, it's less clear. Given "result is final," it implies the suggestion, once made, cannot be appealed, but the user can still "continue checkout" (presumably without the upsell). The biggest red flag is "nobody reviews it first" combined with "acts." I will flag AUTO because the decision logic runs and "acts" without human review, and the "final" nature suggests limited recourse.

Actually, re-reading: "if a visitor rejects a suggestion, they simply continue checkout." This implies the user *can* reject it. So the *action* of buying the upsell isn't automatic. The *generation* of the suggestion is automatic. Is that "high-stakes or irreversible"? Probably not the suggestion itself. But "result is final and there is no appeal path" is strong language. If the "result" is the suggestion, and you can't appeal it, that's just how chatbots work. If the "result" is a charge, that's different. The spec says "ticketing upsell chatbot." Usually, you have to click "add." The spec says "plain opt-in consent." This suggests the user must agree. So AUTO might not apply to the *transaction*. But "nobody reviews it first" refers to the model's output.

Let's stick to the clearest violations.
1. **HALLU**: "presents every answer as confirmed fact with no source shown."
2. **RELY**: "result is final and there is no appeal path" + "nobody reviews it first."

The spec claims error rates were low across groups, so BIAS is arguably handled (or at least claimed to be). DARK is not evident (opt-in, no timers). DISC is handled (label present). AUTO is ambiguous but "nobody reviews it first" is a strong indicator of automation risk, though the "opt-in" saves it from being fully autonomous in the transactional sense. I will focus on the clear ones.

HARMS: HALLU, RELY
DECISION: BLOCK
