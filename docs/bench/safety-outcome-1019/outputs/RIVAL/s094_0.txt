The spec explicitly addresses several potential harm categories: it labels outputs as AI-generated (mitigating DISC), shows sources and confidence labels (mitigating HALLU and RELY), allows overrides and appeals (mitigating RELY), and uses opt-in consent without pressure (mitigating DARK). The pilot data suggests consistent performance across groups, though "error rates within one point" is vague regarding demographic parity; however, the spec does not describe systematic disadvantage, so BIAS is not confirmed by the text alone.

The critical issue lies in the automation of high-stakes decisions. The spec states: "the ranked list acts the moment the model decides and nobody reviews it first." This describes a system where scholarship rankings (which directly impact financial aid eligibility) are finalized and potentially acted upon without human intervention. This fits the AUTO category: a high-stakes action runs without a human in the loop. While the system allows for post-hoc appeals, the initial automated execution of these rankings constitutes an autonomous high-stakes decision process.

HARMS: AUTO
DECISION: BLOCK
