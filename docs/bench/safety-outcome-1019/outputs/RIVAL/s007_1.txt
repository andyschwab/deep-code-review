The spec reveals several harms requiring attention before launch.

First, **DISC** is present: the interface "reads like a simple scheduling form" and "nothing in the product says a model produced the output," failing to inform users that decisions are machine-generated.

Second, **DARK** patterns are explicitly described: the offer "expires on a ten-minute timer," creating artificial urgency, and "consent is pre-ticked," manipulating users into agreements they did not actively choose.

Third, **RELY** is a concern despite some mitigations. While confidence levels and overrides exist, the lack of disclosure (DISC) combined with the high-stakes nature of scheduling and the pressure tactics (DARK) undermines the effectiveness of the provided overrides, inviting over-trust in the system's suggestions.

Regarding **BIAS**, the spec notes that 11% of older and 11% of younger users are flagged equally. Since the disparity is equal across age groups, the spec does not describe systematic disadvantage against a specific group, so BIAS is not flagged based on these facts.

**AUTO** and **HALLU** are handled; humans sign off on irreversible steps, and answers include cited sources.

HARMS: DISC, DARK, RELY
DECISION: BLOCK
