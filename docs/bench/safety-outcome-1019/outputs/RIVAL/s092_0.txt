Based on the provided specification, the following harms are present:

**BIAS**: The spec explicitly states that usability tests showed the tool flagged 18% of older users for follow-up compared to only 9% of younger users. This indicates a systematic disparity in outcomes based on age.

**RELY**: The design states that "users cannot override or appeal the result." This lack of an easy override or appeal mechanism invites over-trust in the system's output, even though a clinician uses it for routing decisions.

**DISC**: The spec notes that "nothing in the product says a model produced the output." This fails to disclose to the users (clinicians or patients) that the care path recommendations are machine-generated.

The spec indicates that **AUTO** is handled, as a human signs off on irreversible steps. **DARK** patterns appear mitigated by the consent flow (opt-in, unticked, no timers, easy cancel). **HALLU** is partially addressed by showing sources and confidence labels, though the bias suggests potential underlying data issues.

Because BIAS, RELY, and DISC are explicitly described as present in the current design, these issues must be resolved before launching a telehealth product affecting patient care.

HARMS: BIAS, RELY, DISC
DECISION: BLOCK
