**Immediate Risk Mitigation Plan for Legal Tech Launch**

**1. Implement a Hard "Red Flag" Blocklist (Immediate)**
Before generating any clause, the system must check the user's input against a hardcoded list of prohibited concepts specific to New York and general consumer protection laws. For liability waivers, explicitly block generation if the prompt includes terms like "gross negligence," "willful misconduct," "personal injury," or "waiver of statutory rights." If these triggers are detected, the system must refuse to draft the clause and display a static warning: "This type of waiver may be unenforceable under NY law. Please consult an attorney." This prevents the specific error you encountered from recurring without needing complex external validation.

**2. Deploy Mandatory Static Disclaimers**
Modify the output template to inject a prominent, non-editable disclaimer at the top of every generated document. It must state: "This document is a draft for informational purposes only and does not constitute legal advice. Laws vary by jurisdiction and fact pattern. This waiver may not be enforceable in New York regarding consumer rights, gross negligence, or statutory protections. Use at your own risk." This does not fix the legal flaw but significantly reduces your startup's liability exposure by ensuring the end-user is warned that the output is not vetted legal counsel.

**3. Conduct a "Adversarial" Internal Review Sprint**
With no budget for external counsel, assign two team members to act as "attackers" for the next 48 hours. Their sole task is to attempt to break the system using high-risk prompts derived from known NY legal pitfalls (e.g., "Write a waiver that lets me ignore safety laws" or "Draft a clause that stops customers from suing for injuries"). Compare these outputs against a manually curated "Golden Set" of 5–10 correct, safe clauses written by a knowledgeable team member or sourced from reputable public domain templates. Any deviation where the AI suggests waiving non-waivable rights is a critical failure that requires adding that specific scenario to your blocklist in Step 1.

**4. Restrict Scope and Add Human-in-the-Loop**
Narrow the beta launch scope. Disable the generation of high-risk documents like liability waivers, employment contracts, or non-competes until you can secure legal review. Limit the tool initially to lower-risk clauses such as "Confidentiality," "Term," or "Governing Law" where the risk of harming consumer rights is lower. For any remaining high-risk features you must keep active, implement a mandatory manual review step where a human team member must approve the clause before it is shown to the user, even if this slows the process slightly.

**5. Establish a Rapid Takedown and Feedback Mechanism**
Create a visible "Report an Error" button on the interface. If a user flags a clause as legally suspect, have a protocol to immediately investigate and, if necessary, remotely disable that specific template type. Log all generated clauses involving liability or waivers so you can proactively audit them post-launch if a pattern of errors emerges.

**Note:** This plan prioritizes harm reduction and liability containment over comprehensive legal accuracy, which is impossible to guarantee without counsel.
**Note:** You must consult a qualified attorney immediately after funding is secured to validate these measures and address the previously generated flawed waiver.
