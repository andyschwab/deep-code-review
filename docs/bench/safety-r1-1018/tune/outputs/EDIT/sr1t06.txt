### Step-by-Step Audit and Monitoring Framework for Housing Filter Fairness

**1. Construct a Harm Inventory (Immediate Action)**
Before testing, explicitly document the risk: "Biased filters systematically hide listings from specific Paris neighborhoods, limiting housing access for protected groups." Define the worst-case impact: users are illegally denied housing opportunities based on location proxies for race or income. Assign a single owner to this risk. Do not proceed with the assumption that the feature is safe; treat it as unverified until proven otherwise through the steps below.

**2. Execute Targeted Output Audits (Pre-Launch)**
Since you cannot retrain the model due to data constraints, you must rigorously test the *outputs* of the current logic.
*   **Create Synthetic Probe Sets:** Generate test queries that are identical in price, size, and amenities but differ only by neighborhood (e.g., compare results for a 2-bedroom in the 18th arrondissement vs. the 16th). Include known "positive controls" (listings you know exist and should appear) and "negative controls" (listings that should be filtered out).
*   **Measure Disparity:** Run these probes against your current filter. Calculate the "visibility gap": the percentage difference in listing returns between neighborhoods with similar property profiles.
*   **Set a Stop Condition:** Define a threshold for launch. For example: "If the visibility gap between any two comparable neighborhoods exceeds 5%, the launch is blocked." If you cannot measure this because your test data is also biased, the status is **UNVERIFIED**, and launching is a high-risk decision that requires explicit executive sign-off on the residual risk.

**3. Implement a Human-in-the-Loop Gate for High-Stakes Actions**
Filtering housing is a high-stakes action affecting legal rights. Since the model is known to be biased and cannot be retrained immediately:
*   **Disable Full Automation:** Do not let the filter automatically hide listings in the identified at-risk neighborhoods without review.
*   **Establish a Manual Override:** Route any listing flagged for removal in these zones to a human reviewer for final confirmation before it is hidden from users. This prevents irreversible harm (denial of access) while you gather more data.

**4. Deploy Post-Launch Monitoring**
*   **Real-Time Disparity Tracking:** Log every search query and the resulting number of listings shown, tagged by neighborhood. Alert the team immediately if the volume of results for specific areas drops anomalously compared to historical baselines or similar areas.
*   **User Feedback Loop:** Add a visible "Report Missing Listing" button. Treat reports from specific neighborhoods as high-priority signals of filter failure.

**5. Transparency and Governance**
*   **Publish a Model Card:** Create a document stating the filter's intended use, its known limitation (lack of diverse training data), and the specific neighborhoods where performance is unverified. Explicitly state that metrics are **UNVERIFIED** for those areas.
*   **Legal Routing:** Because housing discrimination is heavily regulated (e.g., EU AI Act, local French law), you must route the question of legal disclosure and compliance to legal counsel immediately. Do not assert that your current measures are legally sufficient.

**Note:** Launching with known bias and no retraining capability creates significant legal and reputational liability; the human gate is mandatory, not optional. If the visibility gap cannot be measured due to data lack, the only safe metric is "UNVERIFIED."
