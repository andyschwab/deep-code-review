### Safety Assessment Plan: Size Recommendation Engine

**1. Immediate Harm Inventory & Risk Mapping**
Before deployment, formally document the specific failure mode: "Systemic under-sizing leading to physical discomfort or minor injury." Identify affected groups, specifically checking for bias against body types underrepresented in your training data (e.g., non-standard proportions, specific ethnic demographics common in Paris vs. global training sets). The worst-case scenario is not just a return, but physical harm and reputational damage. An empty inventory is not a pass; record these risks explicitly.

**2. Measurement Strategy (Runnable Eval Plan)**
You cannot certify safety without testing outputs, not just code. Construct an evaluation set immediately:
*   **Probe Set:** Curate a diverse dataset of photos representing varied body shapes, sizes, and demographics, including edge cases where the model previously failed.
*   **Controls:** Include "known-bad" inputs (images known to trigger under-sizing) and "known-good" inputs (clear cases with correct historical data).
*   **Metric & Threshold:** Define a pass/fail metric based on the rate of under-sizing recommendations. Set a **zero-tolerance threshold** for recommendations that carry a risk of physical restriction. If the model cannot distinguish between "snug" and "restrictive" with high certainty, it fails the eval.
*   **Stop Condition:** If the error rate for under-sizing exceeds 0% in the high-risk category, the launch must be blocked. Do not invent a "typical" acceptable failure rate for physical safety.
*   **Result Reporting:** If you cannot measure performance across specific subgroups due to data gaps, label those metrics as **UNVERIFIED**. Do not fabricate an accuracy percentage.

**3. Management Controls: Human Gate & Confidence Tiers**
*   **High-Stakes Gate:** Given the reports of injury, this is a high-stakes automation. You must implement a **human-in-the-loop gate** or a hard software fallback for any prediction where the model's confidence is not in the highest tier. Fully autonomous execution of a recommendation that could cause physical harm is the failure to prevent.
*   **Confidence as Defined Tiers:** Do not display raw probability scores (e.g., "87% fit") to users, as this implies false precision. Instead, implement defined coarse tiers:
    *   **Confirmed:** High correlation with past purchases and clear image data.
    *   **Corroborated:** Supported by similar body types in the dataset.
    *   **Unverified:** Low confidence, ambiguous image, or outlier body type.
    *   **Action:** If the output is "Unverified," the system must **withhold the specific size recommendation** and instead suggest consulting a size chart or customer support.
*   **Disclosure:** Clearly disclose that the suggestion is AI-generated and based on limited data. State the limitation: "This tool estimates fit but cannot guarantee comfort or safety."

**4. Governance & Transparency Artifact**
Create a **Model Card** before launch. This document must list:
*   **Intended Use:** Sizing suggestions only, not medical or safety guarantees.
*   **Limitations:** Explicitly state the risk of under-sizing and the groups where performance is UNVERIFIED.
*   **Owner:** Name the specific individual responsible for monitoring injury claims post-launch.
*   **Data Provenance:** Describe the dataset used and acknowledge gaps in body type diversity.

**5. Legal Routing**
Regarding the injury claims and potential regulatory duties (e.g., product safety laws in the EU), route this immediately to legal counsel. Do not assert compliance internally; let counsel determine if this feature triggers specific regulatory obligations under the EU AI Act or consumer safety statutes.

**Deliverable Status:** Plan outlined.
**Note:** Do not deploy the automated feature into the checkout flow until the "Unverified" tier successfully blocks risky recommendations and the Model Card is published.
