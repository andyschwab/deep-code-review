**Immediate Action Plan: Critical Gaps & Mitigation**

Your current protocol has a fatal gap: it lacks a **human-in-the-loop gate** for high-stakes, irreversible outputs. Generating legally binding clauses that can expose users to lawsuits is a high-stakes action. Fully automating this without review is the failure mode you must prevent immediately.

**1. Implement a Mandatory Human Gate (Do Not Skip)**
You cannot launch with fully automated drafting for legal clauses. Before release, every generated clause must trigger a mandatory review step.
*   **Action:** Modify the workflow so the tool produces a "Draft for Review" rather than a "Final Document." The user (or an internal team member if offering a concierge MVP) must explicitly confirm the output before it is considered final.
*   **Constraint:** If your funding timeline prevents building a complex UI for this, the system must output a clear disclaimer: *"This is an AI-generated draft. It has not been verified by an attorney and may not comply with current laws. Do not use without legal review."* This shifts the liability expectation and prevents over-reliance.

**2. Deploy Targeted "Red Team" Probes (Measure, Don't Assert)**
You cannot certify the system is "accurate" or "unbiased" without evidence. Since you cannot hire counsel, you must rigorously test known failure modes internally.
*   **Action:** Create a test suite of 20–30 adversarial prompts specifically designed to trigger illegal or unenforceable clauses (e.g., "Write a waiver that bans all consumer rights in New York," "Draft a clause that ignores state labor laws").
*   **Metric:** The pass threshold for any prompt generating an illegal clause is **zero**. If the model generates even one unenforceable clause in this set, the feature must not ship in its current autonomous form.
*   **Reporting:** Do not invent an accuracy percentage (e.g., "99% accurate"). If you haven't tested against every state law, the status is **UNVERIFIED**. Document this residual risk clearly.

**3. Transparency Artifacts (Model Card)**
Ship with a visible "Model Card" or limitation statement embedded in the product.
*   **Content:** Explicitly state the intended use (drafting assistance only), known limitations (may generate non-compliant clauses), and the fact that outputs are AI-generated.
*   **Requirement:** Never claim the tool replaces a lawyer. Absence of this disclosure is a transparency failure that increases your legal exposure.

**4. Confidence Calibration**
Do not display raw confidence scores (e.g., "87% confident") to users, as this implies a precision the model does not possess.
*   **Action:** If you show any confidence indicator, use defined coarse tiers like "Unverified" or "Needs Review" rather than percentages. Better yet, default all outputs to "Needs Legal Review."

**Prioritized Summary for Launch:**
1.  **Stop full automation.** Insert a mandatory human confirmation step or a bold, unavoidable disclaimer on every output.
2.  **Run adversarial tests** immediately on high-risk clauses (liability, waivers, employment). If failures occur, disable those specific clause types until fixed.
3.  **Publish limitations** clearly in the UI and terms of service. State that the tool is an assistant, not a lawyer.
4.  **Route legal duties to counsel** as soon as funding allows. You cannot internally verify regulatory compliance for all jurisdictions; acknowledge this gap as **UNVERIFIED** rather than guessing.

Launching without these controls risks harming your users and ending your startup via litigation before you gain traction. The cost of a delayed feature is lower than the cost of a lawsuit.

*Note: This plan identifies product safety gaps; it does not constitute legal advice. Regulatory obligations regarding automated legal advice must be determined by qualified counsel.*
