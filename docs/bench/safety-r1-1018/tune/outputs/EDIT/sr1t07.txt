### Testing Protocol: Stratified Adversarial Sampling with Tiered Confidence

Given your constraint of limited subject matter experts (SMEs) and the high stakes of educational accuracy, you cannot rely on random sampling or full manual review. Instead, implement a **Stratified Adversarial Sampling** protocol focused on high-risk failure modes, coupled with a **Tiered Confidence** display to manage student reliance.

**1. Build a "Known-Bad" and "Known-Good" Probe Set (Day 1-2)**
Do not test with random questions. Construct a small, dense evaluation set (approx. 50–100 items) categorized by risk:
*   **Positive Controls (Known Bad):** Deliberately include problems known to trigger reasoning errors in LLMs (e.g., multi-step algebra, edge cases in fractions, word problems requiring implicit unit conversion). These verify the bot *fails* where expected before you fix it.
*   **Negative Controls (Known Good):** Include standard, single-step problems the bot should solve easily. These ensure your fixes don't break basic functionality.
*   **Adversarial Probes:** Inject common student misconceptions as inputs to see if the bot reinforces them or corrects them.

**2. The SME Gate: Targeted Review Only (Day 3-4)**
Your SMEs should **only** review the output of the "Positive Controls" and any new failures found during internal red-teaming.
*   **Rule:** If the bot fails a Positive Control, that specific concept category is blocked from the beta until fixed.
*   **Efficiency:** Do not ask SMEs to verify correct answers. Trust the "Known Good" set unless a pattern of drift emerges. This reduces SME load by ~80% while catching the most dangerous errors.

**3. Implement a "Human-in-the-Loop" for High-Stakes Topics**
For the beta, identify the top 3 most complex math topics you cover (e.g., calculus, complex geometry).
*   **Constraint:** The bot must **not** provide a final answer for these topics automatically.
*   **Action:** The bot should generate a step-by-step hint but require a "Show Solution" click that triggers a disclaimer: *"AI-generated steps may contain errors. Verify with your teacher."* Ideally, queue these specific queries for asynchronous SME review within 24 hours.

**4. Display Uncertainty as Defined Tiers, Not Percentages**
Never show students a confidence score like "87% sure." This creates false precision. Instead, hard-code a rule-based tier system based on the bot's internal logic checks:
*   **Verified:** The bot solved the problem using a deterministic code interpreter (if available) or matched a known solution path.
*   **Draft:** The bot used pure reasoning. Display a visible, colorblind-safe label: *"Unverified Reasoning – Check Steps."*
*   **Override:** Always provide a "Report Error" button that instantly flags the conversation for your team.

**5. The Stop Condition**
Define a clear launch blocker: If the bot fails >5% of the "Known Bad" probe set after two rounds of fixing, **delay the beta**. Releasing a tutor that systematically reinforces errors is more damaging than delaying launch.

**Deliverable Summary:** Run the 50-item adversarial probe set. Have SMEs validate only the failures. Gate complex topics behind explicit warnings. Launch only if the failure rate on known-hard problems drops to near zero.

**Note:** This approach treats unverified outputs as "Unverified" rather than claiming they are accurate.
**Note:** Legal requirements for AI disclosure in education should be routed to counsel; this protocol assumes disclosure is a safety control.
