**Do not launch the automated rejection feature next week.**

Your current plan to automatically reject candidates based on name patterns presents an unmitigated, high-stakes risk of illegal discrimination and severe reputational damage. In the EU and Germany, automated decisions that significantly affect individuals (such as employment rejection) are strictly regulated under the GDPR and the upcoming EU AI Act. There is no checklist or "quick fix" that can validate fairness for a system fundamentally designed to profile protected characteristics (names) within your current timeline.

**Immediate Actions Required:**

1.  **Disable the Automated Rejection Gate:** Immediately decouple the "reject" action from the bot's logic. The system may *flag* applications for human review based on criteria you can legally justify, but it must not execute a rejection without a human in the loop. Fully automated high-stakes decisions require a level of bias testing and legal validation that cannot be completed before Friday.
2.  **Implement a Human-in-the-Loop Gate:** Route every decision the bot would have made to a human recruiter. The bot can serve as a decision-support tool (e.g., summarizing resumes), but the final "reject" or "advance" decision must be made by a person who can account for context and nuance.
3.  **Halt Name-Based Pattern Matching:** Stop using name patterns as a proxy for qualification or fit. This mechanism is inherently prone to bias against specific cultural, ethnic, and national origins. Continuing to use this logic, even with human oversight, exposes the company to significant legal liability under German anti-discrimination laws (AGG).
4.  **Route to Legal Counsel Immediately:** Do not attempt to self-assess regulatory compliance. You must engage counsel to determine if your intended use case qualifies as a "high-risk" AI system under the EU AI Act and to assess GDPR obligations regarding automated individual decision-making. This is a legal determination, not a technical one.
5.  **Draft a Transparency Artifact (Model Card):** Before any beta release, document the system's intended use, the fact that it uses name-based heuristics (and why this is being paused/changed), known limitations, and the lack of validated fairness metrics. If you cannot measure bias because you haven't tested it, explicitly state the metric as **UNVERIFIED**. Do not fabricate a "0% bias" claim.

**Why this cannot be delayed:**
You cannot measure what you have not tested. Validating fairness requires adversarial testing with diverse datasets, statistical analysis of error rates across subgroups, and iterative remediation. This process takes weeks or months, not days. Launching without this evidence means you are deploying a system with **UNVERIFIED** harm potential. If the system disproportionately rejects candidates from specific backgrounds, the harm is immediate, potentially irreversible for the applicants, and legally actionable against your startup.

**Revised Path Forward:**
Launch the beta with the bot in "assistive mode" only (no auto-rejects). Use the beta period to collect data (with consent) and run the necessary fairness evaluations. Once you have measurable evidence of equity and legal sign-off, consider reintroducing automation with strict human oversight gates.
