### Emergency Pricing Override Protocol

**1. Emergency Zone Identification (Data Feeds & Triggers)**
Implement a real-time ingestion layer that monitors specific public data feeds. If any trigger fires, the zone is flagged as an "Emergency Zone" immediately.
*   **Official Alerts:** Ingest RSS/API feeds from local emergency management (e.g., Toronto Emergency Management Office, Environment Canada) for keywords: "Evacuation," "Shelter-in-place," "State of Emergency," "Flash Flood," "Severe Storm."
*   **Anomalous Demand Spikes:** Detect demand surges exceeding 300% of the 4-week rolling average within a 15-minute window, specifically when coupled with a drop in driver supply (indicating potential evacuation or danger).
*   **Geofenced Events:** Manually or automatically activate based on known disaster polygons (e.g., flood plains) when weather severity indices hit critical thresholds.

**2. Automatic Pricing Caps (The Hard Gate)**
Once a zone is flagged, bypass the dynamic pricing model entirely for that geographic polygon.
*   **Hard Cap:** Set the surge multiplier to a maximum of **1.0x** (base fare only) or a fixed "crisis flat rate" regardless of demand intensity. Do not allow the model to output a multiplier >1.0 in these zones.
*   **Duration:** The cap remains active until the official alert is cleared or the anomalous demand pattern normalizes for 60 consecutive minutes.
*   **Fallback Logic:** If the data feed fails or returns ambiguous data, default to the capped state (fail-safe) rather than risking exploitative pricing.

**3. Monitoring & Human-in-the-Loop Alerts**
*   **Real-Time Dashboard:** Create a visible "Crisis Mode" indicator on the operations dashboard showing active emergency zones and the override status.
*   **Alerting:** Send immediate high-priority notifications (SMS/PagerDuty) to the on-call data scientist and product lead when:
    *   An emergency zone is activated.
    *   The pricing engine attempts to calculate a multiplier >1.0 in a flagged zone (indicating a logic failure).
    *   Demand exceeds supply by >500% while capped (to monitor service availability risks).
*   **Manual Override:** Provide a "Kill Switch" for operations staff to manually expand emergency zones if public data lags behind ground reality.

**4. Implementation Plan (5-Day Rollout)**
*   **Day 1-2:** Develop the rule-engine middleware to intercept model outputs and apply the hard cap. Integrate public alert APIs.
*   **Day 3:** Simulate historical disaster scenarios (e.g., past storms) to verify the triggers fire and caps engage correctly.
*   **Day 4:** Deploy to a staging environment; conduct a "game day" exercise with the team to test manual activation and alert response.
*   **Day 5:** Deploy to production with the system defaulting to "monitoring only" for the first 2 hours of the festival, then switching to "active enforcement" once confidence is verified.

**Note:** This approach treats the potential for harm as a binary gate (human-defined rules) rather than relying on the model's probabilistic output during high-stakes events. Ensure legal counsel reviews the specific definition of "emergency" used in the triggers to align with local regulations before launch.
