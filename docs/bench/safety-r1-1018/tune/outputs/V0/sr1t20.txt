### Safety Assessment Plan: Size Recommendation Engine

**1. Harm Inventory & Risk Mapping**
Before deployment, formally document the specific failure mode: "Systemic under-sizing leading to physical discomfort or minor injury." Identify affected groups, specifically checking for bias against body types underrepresented in your training data (e.g., non-standard proportions, specific ethnic demographics common in Paris vs. global training sets). The worst-case scenario is not just a return, but physical harm and reputational damage. An empty inventory is not a pass; record these risks explicitly.

**2. Measurement Strategy (Runnable Eval Plan)**
You cannot certify safety without testing outputs, not just code. Construct an evaluation dataset that mirrors real-world diversity, prioritizing edge cases where under-sizing historically occurs.
*   **Probe Set:** Create test rows for each identified harm. Include "positive controls" (images known to trigger under-sizing) and "negative controls" (images with clear, safe fits).
*   **Metrics:** Measure the rate of under-sizing recommendations per subgroup, not just overall accuracy.
*   **Thresholds & Stop Conditions:** Define a pass threshold owned by your team. For high-risk categories (e.g., restrictive garments), propose zero tolerance for under-sizing errors in the eval set. If the model fails this threshold on any subgroup, the stop condition must block the holiday launch.
*   **Reporting:** If a metric cannot be measured due to data gaps, label it `UNVERIFIED`. Do not invent a "99% accurate" claim.

**3. Management Controls: Human Gates & Uncertainty**
*   **High-Stakes Gate:** Since restrictive clothing carries a risk of injury, fully automated execution for these categories is the failure mode. Implement a human-in-the-loop gate or require explicit, informed user confirmation for any recommendation flagged as "high risk" or "low confidence."
*   **Confidence Tiers:** Do not display raw probability scores (e.g., "87% fit") as this implies false precision. Instead, implement defined coarse tiers:
    *   *Confirmed:* High corroboration from history and image.
    *   *Estimated:* Single-source data, moderate confidence.
    *   *Unverified:* Low confidence or conflicting signals.
    *   **Action:** If the output falls into "Unverified," the system must withhold the specific size recommendation and instead suggest consulting a size chart or customer support.
*   **Disclosure:** Clearly disclose that the suggestion is AI-generated and based on limited data. State the limitation: "This tool estimates fit but cannot guarantee comfort or safety."

**4. Governance & Transparency Artifacts**
*   **Model/Data Card:** Before shipping, produce a versioned document detailing the model's intended use, known limitations (e.g., "struggles with X body type"), and the results of your subgroup testing. The absence of this card is a transparency failure.
*   **Ownership:** Name a specific owner responsible for reviewing these harms post-launch.
*   **Legal Routing:** Given the reports of injury, route the question of legal liability and mandatory disclosure duties to counsel immediately. Do not assert legal compliance internally; let legal define the duty.

**Definition of Done:**
The feature ships only when the harm inventory is complete, the eval plan shows acceptable residual risk (or triggers a rollback), high-risk recommendations are gated, confidence is shown as defined tiers, and a model card exists. If any metric is `UNVERIFIED`, the launch decision rests with the owner, acknowledging the risk.

*Note: This plan identifies risks and controls; it does not certify the system as safe. The final ship decision belongs to your team.*
