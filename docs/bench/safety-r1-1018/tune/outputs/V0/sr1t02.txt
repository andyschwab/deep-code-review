### Critical Preparation Strategy: Image Analysis Claim Denial

**Immediate Halt Required:** Do not deploy an automated denial mechanism for insurance claims without a human-in-the-loop gate. Automating irreversible financial rejections based on an unverified algorithm constitutes a high-stakes failure mode that risks severe regulatory penalty and user harm, regardless of your deadline.

**1. Implement a Mandatory Human Gate (Day 1)**
Re-architect the feature immediately: the algorithm must **recommend** denial, but a human adjuster must **execute** it.
*   **Action:** Modify the workflow so any claim flagged by the algorithm enters a "Review Queue" rather than triggering an automatic denial letter.
*   **Rationale:** This satisfies the safety floor for irreversible actions. It converts a potential systemic bias incident into a manageable operational workflow while you gather evidence.

**2. Construct a "Synthetic Stress-Test" Dataset (Days 2–5)**
Since you lack diverse real-world data, you must aggressively probe the model's failure modes using your existing engineering resources. Do not attempt to prove the model is "fair"; attempt to break it.
*   **Lighting Simulation:** Write scripts to programmatically alter your existing seed images. Apply filters to simulate:
    *   Low-light/night conditions (common in accident reporting).
    *   High-glare/noon sun (common in Mumbai outdoors).
    *   Monsoon/overcast diffusion (critical for your region).
*   **Damage Perturbation:** Use image editing tools or code to introduce artifacts that mimic common non-damage features:
    *   Dirt/mud splashes vs. dent shadows.
    *   Reflections on wet roads vs. scratch marks.
    *   Old rust vs. fresh impact damage.
*   **Execution:** Run these modified images through your algorithm. Record every instance where the model denies a claim for a known-valid image due to these artificial conditions.

**3. Define "Unverified" Metrics and Stop Conditions (Days 6–8)**
You cannot calculate a true accuracy or bias rate without a labeled ground-truth dataset. Any such number you generate now would be fabricated.
*   **Reporting Standard:** Label all performance metrics as **UNVERIFIED**.
*   **Stop Condition:** Define a specific failure threshold for your human gate. For example: "If the algorithm flags >10% of our synthetic 'clean' images as inconsistent, the auto-recommendation feature is disabled, and 100% of claims require human review."
*   **Documentation:** Draft a **Model Card** artifact. Explicitly state:
    *   **Intended Use:** Assistance tool only; not a decision maker.
    *   **Limitations:** "Performance across diverse lighting conditions and damage types is UNVERIFIED due to lack of labeled diverse data."
    *   **Data Gaps:** Explicitly list the missing demographics and environmental conditions (e.g., monsoon lighting) not present in your training set.

**4. Regulatory Filing Strategy (Days 9–10)**
When filing your regulatory requirements:
*   **Disclosure:** Clearly disclose that the decision logic is AI-assisted but human-gated.
*   **Risk Acknowledgement:** Submit your "Unverified" status and the specific data gaps (lighting, regional diversity) as part of your risk management plan. Regulators penalize hidden risks and fabricated certainty far more heavily than acknowledged limitations with mitigation controls (the human gate).

**Summary of Deliverables:**
1.  Code change enforcing human approval for all denials.
2.  A script-generated suite of adversarial images (lighting/dirt/reflection variants).
3.  A Model Card stating "UNVERIFIED" for bias/accuracy with listed data gaps.
4.  A formal routing of the legal disclosure requirement to your counsel.

**Note:** This plan prioritizes preventing irreversible harm over meeting the deadline with an unsafe feature.
**Note:** Do not assert "bias-free" status in any documentation; use the term "UNVERIFIED" until diverse labeled data is obtained.
