### Emergency Pricing Safety Protocol

**1. Emergency Zone Identification (The "Red Zone" Trigger)**
Establish a real-time ingestion pipeline for the following public data feeds. If **any** two signals activate for a specific geohash, flag it as a Red Zone:
*   **Official Alerts:** RSS/API feeds from local emergency management (e.g., Toronto Emergency Management Office, Environment Canada severe weather warnings). Keywords: "Evacuation," "Shelter-in-place," "State of Emergency."
*   **Infrastructure Status:** Real-time transit shutdowns or road closure APIs from city transport authorities.
*   **Anomalous Demand Spikes:** A demand surge >300% above the 4-week rolling average in a specific zone within a 15-minute window, *without* a corresponding scheduled event (festival/concert) in the database.

**2. Rule-Based Pricing Overrides (Hard Caps)**
Once a Red Zone is flagged, bypass the dynamic pricing model entirely for that zone and apply these deterministic rules:
*   **Price Freeze:** Immediately cap the surge multiplier at **1.0x** (base fare) or the current active rate, whichever is lower. Do not allow any increase.
*   **Service Availability Toggle:** If the model predicts driver supply will drop to zero due to the cap, trigger a "Safety Mode" notification to drivers offering a flat **emergency stipend** per completed trip in the zone, funded from a reserved operational budget, rather than passing costs to riders via surge.
*   **Duration:** The override remains active until the official emergency alert is cleared or the anomalous demand spike normalizes for 30 consecutive minutes.

**3. Monitoring and Human-in-the-Loop Alerts**
*   **Real-Time Dashboard:** Create a dedicated view showing all active Red Zones, the triggered data signals, and the active price cap status.
*   **Escalation Alert:** If the system flags a Red Zone, immediately page the on-call Product Lead and Operations Manager. They must manually verify the context within 10 minutes.
*   **Post-Incident Review:** Log every trigger event, the data sources involved, and the revenue impact. This log is mandatory for the post-festival review to refine the detection logic.

**4. Implementation Constraints & Rollout Adjustment**
*   **No Retraining Required:** This protocol sits as a middleware layer *after* the model generates a price but *before* the user sees it. It requires only configuration changes, not model retraining.
*   **Rollout Recommendation:** Given the high stakes of exploitative pricing during emergencies and the proximity to a major festival (which increases noise in demand data), **delay the city-wide rollout by 48 hours** to dry-run this override logic in a staging environment using historical disaster data. Launching without testing the "kill switch" creates an unacceptable risk of reputational and ethical harm.

***

**Note:** This protocol treats the prevention of price gouging during crises as a non-negotiable safety gate; if the override mechanism cannot be verified before the festival, the feature should not go live.
**Note:** Legal counsel should review the specific wording of the "emergency stipend" to ensure it complies with local labor regulations before implementation.
