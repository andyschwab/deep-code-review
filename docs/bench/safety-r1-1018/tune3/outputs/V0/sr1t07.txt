### Testing Protocol: Targeted Adversarial Sampling with Tiered Human Gates

Given your constraint of limited subject matter experts (SMEs) and high stakes for student learning, you cannot rely on random sampling or full manual review. Instead, implement a **Targeted Adversarial Sampling** protocol focused on high-risk failure modes, paired with a **Tiered Human Gate** that scales review intensity based on risk.

**1. Construct a "High-Risk" Probe Set (The Sample)**
Do not test generic questions. Build a small, dense dataset (50–100 items) specifically designed to trigger reasoning errors:
*   **Adversarial Edge Cases:** Multi-step problems where early errors cascade, problems with irrelevant distractors, and non-standard phrasing common in student queries.
*   **Positive Controls:** 10 known-correct problems the bot *must* pass. If it fails these, the system is unstable.
*   **Negative Controls:** 10 known-problematic patterns (e.g., specific algebraic traps) where the bot previously failed.
*   **Subgroup Slicing:** Ensure coverage across the specific math topics your beta will cover (e.g., if testing Algebra I, ensure linear equations and inequalities are both represented).

**2. The Measurement Plan (One Row Per Harm)**
For the specific harm of **"Hallucination surfaced as fact"** (incorrect math presented as truth):
*   **Metric:** Accuracy Rate on High-Risk Probe Set.
*   **Threshold:** Set by your team, but consider **zero tolerance** for incorrect final answers on Positive Controls. For Adversarial cases, define a maximum acceptable error rate (e.g., <5%).
*   **Stop Condition:** If the bot fails any Positive Control or exceeds the error threshold on Adversarial cases, **block the beta launch**. Do not proceed until the specific failure mode is patched and re-tested.
*   **Reviewer:** One SME reviews only the failures and the positive controls, not the entire set.

**3. Manage Residual Risk: The Human-in-the-Loop Gate**
Since you cannot verify every possible query in production:
*   **Uncertainty Tiers:** Do not display raw confidence percentages (e.g., "87% sure"). Instead, configure the bot to output defined coarse tiers for its reasoning steps: **"Verified"** (matches known solution paths), **"Corroborated"** (multiple internal checks agree), or **"Unverified"** (novel reasoning path).
*   **Actionable Override:** For any output tagged "Unverified" or involving high-stakes concepts (e.g., foundational definitions), the bot must **refuse to give a final answer** and instead say: *"I'm not fully confident in this solution. Let's break it down together,"* prompting a step-by-step scaffold rather than a direct answer. This prevents cementing incorrect concepts.
*   **Feedback Loop:** Include a one-click "Report Incorrect Answer" button in the beta. Route these reports directly to your SME queue for immediate patching.

**4. Transparency Artifact (Mini Model Card)**
Before launch, create a one-page document for your internal team and beta testers stating:
*   **Intended Use:** Tutoring support for [Specific Math Topics].
*   **Known Limitations:** Explicitly list the types of problems where the bot struggles (e.g., "May struggle with multi-step word problems involving fractions").
*   **Validation Status:** Mark metrics as **UNVERIFIED** for any topic not covered in your probe set. Do not invent an overall accuracy number.

**Note:** This protocol prioritizes preventing harm over comprehensive coverage; it assumes some errors will slip through and focuses on making them reversible and detectable.
**Note:** If your bot provides step-by-step reasoning, prioritize verifying the *logic flow* on the probe set, as a correct answer with flawed reasoning is still a learning hazard.
