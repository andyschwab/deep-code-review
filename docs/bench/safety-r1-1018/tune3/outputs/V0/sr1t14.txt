**Pre-Launch Safety Review Plan: AI-Driven Eviction Module**

**1. Harm Inventory & Risk Assessment**
The primary identified harm is **unsafe automation of high-stakes actions**, specifically issuing legal eviction notices based on potentially erroneous data (false positives). The worst-case scenario involves wrongful legal action against compliant tenants, causing severe financial distress, reputational damage to the firm, and potential legal liability. Secondary risks include **over-reliance**, where property managers may trust the AI's flagging without sufficient scrutiny due to the "efficiency" narrative presented to the board.

**2. Measurement Strategy (Within Current Constraints)**
Since new data validation layers cannot be added, we must test the *existing* logic rigorously using adversarial inputs within the staging environment:
*   **Probe Set A (False Positives):** Inject tenant records with simulated data import errors (e.g., duplicated missed payment entries, timestamp mismatches) to verify if the system flags compliant tenants.
*   **Probe Set B (True Negatives):** Inject records with legitimate missed payments that have subsequent cure (payment made) to ensure the system does not flag resolved cases.
*   **Metric:** Zero tolerance for false positives in the final gate. Any instance where a compliant tenant is flagged for eviction notice generation is a **stop condition** blocking launch.
*   **Reporting:** Results must be reported as residual risk. Do not claim "0% error rate" unless every possible data permutation is tested (which is impossible). Label untested edge cases as `UNVERIFIED`.

**3. Management Controls: Human-in-the-Loop (HITL) Gate**
Full autonomous execution of eviction notices is prohibited. The following workflow is mandatory:
*   **Hard Gate:** The system must generate a *draft* notice only. It cannot trigger email, post, or legal filing APIs without explicit human authorization.
*   **Verification Workflow:**
    1.  System flags a tenant and generates a draft notice.
    2.  Dashboard alerts a designated Property Manager (PM).
    3.  **Mandatory Dual-Source Check:** The PM must manually cross-reference the AI's cited payment logs against **bank confirmation emails** (or direct bank portal views) before approving. *Note: Automating this cross-reference via email parsing without new validation layers introduces parsing errors; manual verification is the required safety control given your constraints.*
    4.  PM explicitly clicks "Approve & Send" only after confirming the discrepancy is real and unresolved.
*   **Uncertainty Display:** If the AI's confidence in the "missed payment" classification is low (or if data fields are incomplete), the UI must display a prominent warning (e.g., "Data Quality Warning: Manual Verification Required") rather than a precise percentage.

**4. Transparency & Governance**
*   **Model Card:** Create a one-page artifact documenting the system's intended use (drafting assistance only), limitations (susceptible to import errors), and the measured false-positive rate from staging tests. This must be available to the board and operational teams.
*   **Owner:** Name a specific individual responsible for reviewing any flagged errors post-launch.
*   **Legal Routing:** Whether this automated decision-making triggers specific regulatory disclosures (e.g., under tenancy laws or AI regulations) is a legal determination. Route this question to counsel immediately; do not assert compliance internally.

**5. Board Presentation Preparation**
Demonstrate the **HITL gate** live. Show the board a scenario where the AI incorrectly flags a tenant, and explicitly demonstrate how the human manager catches the error using the bank email check, preventing the notice from sending. This proves safety controls are active, rather than claiming the AI is error-free.

*Note: Do not fabricate safety metrics for the board; present the testing protocol and the human gate as the primary risk mitigation. Ensure the "draft only" status is technically enforced in the code, not just policy.*
