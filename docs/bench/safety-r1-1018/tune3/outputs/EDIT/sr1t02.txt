**Strategy: Risk-Gated Simulation and Human Review for Claim Denials**

**1. Immediate Control: Halt Autonomous Denials**
Do not deploy the algorithm to automatically deny claims. Given the unverified nature of the model and the high stakes (financial loss, regulatory breach), fully automated irreversible actions are unsafe. Implement a **human-in-the-loop gate**: the algorithm may flag claims as "Inconsistent" for priority review, but a human adjuster must make the final denial decision. This satisfies the safety floor regardless of testing completeness.

**2. Simulation Plan (Using Existing Resources)**
Since new data collection is impossible, generate synthetic variance from your existing unlabeled photos using deterministic code transformations. Do not retrain the model; test its robustness against these inputs:
*   **Lighting Stress Tests:** Programmatically alter brightness, contrast, and color temperature on existing images to simulate Mumbai monsoon gloom, harsh noon sun, and night flash photography.
*   **Occlusion & Angle Noise:** Apply random crops, rotations, and synthetic overlays (e.g., mud, rain streaks, blur) to mimic poor user capture conditions common in field accidents.
*   **Adversarial Probes:** Create "positive controls" (clear damage photos artificially degraded) and "negative controls" (clean photos with noise added). If the model denies the degraded clear damage, it is over-sensitive to lighting rather than actual damage.

**3. Measurement & Metrics**
Run the image logic against these transformed sets. Measure the **False Denial Rate** (valid claims flagged as inconsistent) across your simulated conditions.
*   **Threshold:** Set a zero-tolerance stop condition for autonomous denial if the False Denial Rate exceeds 0% on your positive controls.
*   **Reporting:** Document results as **UNVERIFIED** for real-world fairness. Explicitly state that without a diverse labeled dataset, bias regarding specific vehicle types, regional damage patterns, or demographic factors cannot be measured. Do not invent an accuracy percentage.

**4. Transparency & Disclosure**
*   **Model Card:** Draft a versioned artifact stating the intended use (flagging inconsistencies), the limitation (unverified on diverse lighting/regions), and the known gap (no labeled diverse dataset).
*   **User Disclosure:** Inform users when an AI system assists in the review. Do not assert legal compliance; route the specific regulatory filing requirements regarding automated decision-making to legal counsel immediately.

**5. Execution Timeline (10 Days)**
*   **Days 1-3:** Build deterministic image transformation scripts. Run initial probes.
*   **Days 4-6:** Analyze failure modes. Refine the "flagging" threshold to minimize false positives, accepting that some false negatives (missed fraud) are safer than false denials.
*   **Days 7-8:** Integrate the human-review workflow. Ensure no claim is denied without human confirmation.
*   **Days 9-10:** Finalize the Model Card and internal risk memo. Submit regulatory filing with the explicit caveat that the system is "AI-assisted with human oversight" due to unverified autonomous performance.

**Cost to Goal:** This approach meets the filing deadline by shifting the claim from "autonomous processing" to "AI-assisted triage," which reduces regulatory risk while acknowledging the data gap.

**Note:** This plan prioritizes preventing harm over achieving full automation. The lack of diverse data makes certifying fairness impossible; the human gate is the only viable control.
