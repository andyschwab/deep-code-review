**Testing Protocol: Tiered Sampling with Human-in-the-Loop Gates**

Given your constraint of limited subject matter experts (SMEs) and the high stakes of incorrect math instruction, do not attempt to verify every query. Instead, implement a **Tiered Confidence Gate** combined with **Adversarial Sampling**.

**1. Define Output Tiers (No Raw Percentages)**
Stop the bot from displaying raw confidence scores (e.g., "87% sure"), as this invites over-reliance. Replace them with three defined tiers visible to your internal review team:
*   **Verified:** The bot's reasoning chain matches a known, pre-validated template or textbook solution.
*   **Corroborated:** The bot arrived at the answer via multiple internal reasoning paths that agree.
*   **Unverified:** The reasoning is novel, complex, or the internal paths disagree.
*Action:* Any output falling into "Unverified" must **never** reach a student without human review.

**2. The Sampling Strategy (The "Red Team" Set)**
Since you cannot test everything, build a static "Golden Set" of 50–100 math problems before launch. This set must include:
*   **Positive Controls:** 20 standard problems with known correct answers (to ensure the bot still works).
*   **Negative Controls (Adversarial):** 30 problems specifically designed to trigger reasoning errors (e.g., multi-step algebra, edge cases with zero/negative numbers, word problems with irrelevant data).
*   **Subgroup Slices:** Ensure problems cover different grade levels and topic areas your bot claims to support.
*Run this set every time the model or prompt changes. If the bot fails >0% of the Negative Controls, the feature is blocked from launch.*

**3. The Human-in-the-Loop (HITL) Gate**
For the beta launch, enforce a strict gate on **irreversible learning moments**.
*   **High-Stakes Filter:** If a student asks a question flagged as "Unverified" or involving a new topic cluster, the bot must respond: *"I'm working on this problem. To ensure you get the right concept, a tutor will verify this step shortly,"* and queue it for your limited SMEs.
*   **Low-Stakes Flow:** For "Verified" or "Corroborated" outputs on standard topics, allow immediate display but log the interaction for random audit.

**4. Measurement Plan**
Create a single spreadsheet row for your primary harm: **Hallucination Surfaced as Fact**.
*   **Metric:** % of "Unverified" responses accidentally shown to students.
*   **Threshold:** 0% (Zero tolerance).
*   **Stop Condition:** If any unverified math solution reaches a student, pause the beta immediately.
*   **Owner:** Assign one team member to review the "Unverified" queue daily.

**5. Transparency Artifact**
Draft a one-page **Model Card** for your internal team and beta partners. It must state:
*   **Intended Use:** Supplemental tutoring for grades X–Y.
*   **Limitations:** Explicitly list topics where the bot is known to struggle (e.g., "Complex word problems").
*   **Status:** Mark accuracy metrics as **UNVERIFIED** for any topic not covered by your Golden Set. Do not invent an accuracy percentage.

By gating the unknown and rigorously testing only the most dangerous edge cases, you protect students from learning incorrect concepts while respecting your SME bottleneck.

**Note:** This protocol treats mathematical errors as a high-stakes safety issue; do not relax the human gate for "Unverified" outputs regardless of launch pressure.
**Note:** Legal requirements for AI disclosure in Australian edtech should be routed to counsel; this plan recommends disclosure as a safety control, not a legal verdict.
