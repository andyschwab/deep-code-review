### Immediate Safety Plan: Rule-Based Guards and Human Gate

Given the critical risk of contraindicated prescriptions and the inability to retrain the model before launch, the following plan implements a deterministic safety net and a mandatory human review layer. This approach treats the AI output as a draft only, never a final decision.

**1. Deterministic Rule-Based Guards (The "Hard Stop")**
Deploy a post-processing filter that runs immediately after the bot generates a recommendation but before it is displayed to any human or patient. This filter uses static logic, not the AI model, to intercept known failure modes:
*   **Contraindication Lookup:** Integrate a local, versioned database of drug-disease and drug-drug interactions. If the bot's suggested medication appears in this list for the patient's recorded conditions or current meds, the system automatically blocks the output.
*   **Keyword Triggers:** Flag and block outputs containing high-risk phrases (e.g., "prescribe," "dosage," specific controlled substance names) if the patient profile lacks required verification fields (e.g., age, weight, allergy status).
*   **Fallback Response:** When a rule is triggered, the system must not show the AI's text. Instead, it returns a standardized message: "Complex case detected. A licensed physician will review your symptoms shortly."

**2. Mandatory Human-in-the-Loop (HITL) Gate**
Since the feature involves high-stakes, irreversible health actions, full automation is unsafe. Implement a "Review-Then-Release" workflow for the launch:
*   **Universal Review:** For the initial launch period (minimum 4 weeks), *every* prescription recommendation generated by the bot must be queued for approval by a licensed medical professional within your team.
*   **Workflow Design:** The bot sends the draft recommendation + patient context to a secure clinician dashboard. The clinician must explicitly click "Approve" or "Reject/Modify." Only upon approval is the message sent to the patient or EHR.
*   **Staffing Adjustment:** With a team of eight, designate specific shifts where at least one clinician is dedicated solely to reviewing bot outputs to ensure no backlog delays care.

**3. Uncertainty Signaling & Override**
*   **Confidence Tiers:** Do not display numerical confidence scores (e.g., "85% sure"). Instead, map internal scores to coarse, defined tiers: "Verified" (matches guidelines exactly), "Needs Review" (partial match or missing data), and "Unverified" (low confidence or novel symptom combination). Force "Needs Review" status for any output where the rule-based guard found ambiguous data.
*   **Easy Override:** Ensure the clinician dashboard allows one-click rejection with a mandatory reason code (e.g., "Contraindication missed," "Hallucinated symptom") to build a dataset for future model fixing.

**4. Launch Governance**
*   **Owner Assignment:** Name one clinical lead as the owner of this safety gate. They are responsible for daily review of all rejected cases.
*   **Kill Switch:** Implement an immediate "off-switch" for the bot feature. If the rate of human rejections exceeds a pre-set threshold (e.g., 5% of cases), the system automatically disables bot generation and routes all triage to humans manually.
*   **Transparency Artifact:** Create a brief "Model Limitation Note" for internal staff explaining that the bot is prone to misinterpreting symptoms and that its outputs are unverified drafts. Do not claim the system is "safe" or "bias-free."

**Note:** This plan defers the legal determination of whether specific AI disclosures are required for patients to your legal counsel; however, internal transparency with your clinical team is immediate and mandatory.
**Note:** Do not fabricate a safety metric (e.g., "99% safe"); report the rejection rate from the HITL process as the only valid measure of residual risk.
