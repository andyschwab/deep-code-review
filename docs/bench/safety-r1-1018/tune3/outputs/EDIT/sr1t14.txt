**Pre-Launch Safety Review Plan: AI-Driven Eviction Module**

**1. Harm Inventory & Risk Assessment**
The primary identified harm is **unsafe automation of high-stakes actions**, specifically issuing legal eviction notices based on potentially erroneous data (false positives). The worst-case scenario involves wrongful legal threats against compliant tenants, causing severe distress, reputational damage, and potential legal liability. Secondary risks include **over-reliance**, where property managers might trust the AI's flagging without sufficient scrutiny due to the "efficiency" narrative presented to the board.

**2. Measurement Strategy (Within Current Constraints)**
Since new validation layers are prohibited, we must test the *existing* data pipeline and output logic rigorously:
*   **Adversarial Probe Set:** Construct a test dataset within the current cloud environment containing known "false positive" scenarios (e.g., tenants with missed payments in the system but confirmed bank receipts in email logs, or timing mismatches).
*   **Negative Control:** Include a set of tenants with perfect payment history to ensure zero false flags.
*   **Metric & Threshold:** The pass threshold for any automated flagging logic must be **zero tolerance** for false positives in the high-stakes category of "notice generation." If the system flags a tenant in the negative control or fails to reconcile a known bank confirmation in the probe set, the feature cannot proceed to even limited release.
*   **Stop Condition:** Any detection of a false positive in the test run triggers an immediate block on notice generation capabilities until the specific data discrepancy logic is resolved.

**3. Management Controls: Human-in-the-Loop Gate**
Given the irreversible and high-stakes nature of eviction notices, **full autonomous execution is prohibited.** The following workflow is mandatory:
*   **Mandatory Human Verification:** The system must be configured to *only* generate a draft notice. It cannot send, schedule, or finalize any communication without explicit, individual approval from a designated property manager.
*   **Uncertainty Disclosure:** The interface presented to the property manager must clearly label the notice as "AI-Generated Draft – Requires Verification." It must display the specific data points triggering the flag (e.g., "Missed payment dated X") alongside a direct link to the raw payment log and a manual upload/view field for the bank confirmation email.
*   **Confidence Tiering:** Do not display a raw probability score (e.g., "95% likely overdue"). Instead, use defined coarse tiers: **"Confirmed Discrepancy"** (system found missed payment AND no matching bank record) vs. **"Data Conflict"** (system found missed payment BUT a bank email exists requiring manual review). This prevents automation bias.

**4. Governance & Transparency**
*   **Owner Assignment:** A specific individual must be named as the owner of this output harm, responsible for reviewing any flagged errors post-launch.
*   **Transparency Artifact:** A brief "Model Card" summary must be prepared for the board and internal teams, explicitly stating the system's limitation: it relies on existing import data which is known to have error rates, and therefore human review is the primary safety control, not the algorithm itself.
*   **Legal Routing:** The requirement for legal disclosure to tenants that an AI assisted in generating the notice, or whether this constitutes a regulated automated decision under local tenancy laws, must be routed immediately to legal counsel. Do not assert compliance; request a determination.

**5. Immediate Next Steps for Board Presentation**
Demonstrate the **Human-in-the-Loop workflow** live. Show the system flagging a test case, generating the draft, and stopping. Then, show the property manager interface where the conflict is resolved manually. This proves efficiency *and* safety. Do not claim the system is "bias-free" or "error-proof"; present the residual risk and the control mechanism.

*Note: This plan identifies risks and controls; it does not certify the system as safe. The final ship decision rests with the product owner.*
*Legal determination on disclosure duties is required before launch.*
