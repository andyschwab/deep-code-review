### Step-by-Step Audit and Monitoring Framework for Housing Filter Fairness

**1. Map the Harm Inventory (Immediate Action)**
Before testing, explicitly document the specific risk: "Systematic exclusion of listings from specific neighborhoods due to biased filters." Define the affected groups (users seeking housing in those areas, landlords in those areas) and the worst-case realistic impact (reinforced segregation, financial loss for landlords, reduced housing access for tenants). An empty inventory is not a pass; write this down now.

**2. Measure with Adversarial Probes (Days 1–5)**
Since you cannot retrain the model, you must test the current logic rigorously. Do not rely on aggregate accuracy.
*   **Construct Test Rows:** Create a dataset of synthetic listing profiles that are identical in price, size, and quality but differ *only* by neighborhood identifier. Include known "bad" positives (listings in excluded neighborhoods that should appear) and "good" negatives (listings that should correctly be filtered for other reasons).
*   **Run the Audit:** Execute these probes against your current filtering logic. Measure the "visibility gap": the percentage difference in display rates between target neighborhoods and control neighborhoods.
*   **Set a Stop Condition:** Define a threshold for launch blocking. For example: "If visibility in target neighborhoods is >5% lower than the control group without a valid, non-discriminatory reason, the launch is halted." Do not invent a "safe" rate; set this based on your risk tolerance for housing discrimination.
*   **Check for Injection/Leakage:** Ensure user search queries cannot manipulate the filter to expose hidden data or that the filter isn't leaking private landlord data.

**3. Manage Residual Risk with Human Gates (Pre-Launch)**
*   **Human-in-the-Loop for Edge Cases:** If the filter confidence is low or a listing falls into a borderline category for a target neighborhood, route the decision to a human reviewer rather than auto-hiding it. High-stakes decisions (hiding housing) require human confirmation by default.
*   **Surface Uncertainty:** If the system cannot verify a listing's eligibility with high confidence, do not hide it silently. Flag it for review or display it with a neutral "verification pending" status, rather than excluding it.
*   **Transparency Artifact:** Draft a "Model Card" for this feature. Document the intended use, the known limitation (lack of diverse training data), the measured visibility gaps from your audit, and the owner of this risk. If a metric was not measured, label it `UNVERIFIED`—do not fabricate a fairness score.

**4. Post-Launch Monitoring (Day 10+)**
*   **Continuous Sampling:** Implement a daily automated run of your adversarial probe set against the live system.
*   **Drift Detection:** Monitor for changes in visibility rates. If the gap widens beyond your stop condition, trigger an automatic rollback or alert the nine-person team immediately.
*   **Feedback Loop:** Create a clear channel for users to report missing listings. Treat these reports as high-priority data points to refine your probes.

**5. Governance**
Assign one person on your nine-person team as the explicit owner of this harm inventory and the monitoring dashboard. Re-review the filter logic if the underlying data source changes or if new neighborhoods are added.

**Note:** This framework identifies and measures risk; it does not certify the system as "unbiased" or "fair," as no test can prove a negative. If legal obligations regarding housing discrimination disclosures exist, route that determination to legal counsel immediately.
