### Safety Validation Plan: Facial Recognition Door Unlock

**1. Harm Inventory & Scope**
The primary risk is **unsafe automation of a high-stakes action**: granting physical access to unauthorized individuals due to model failure under poor lighting or awkward angles. The worst-case scenario is a stranger gaining entry to a resident's home. Because this action is irreversible once the door opens, a "human-in-the-loop" gate is required for any non-definitive match.

**2. Measurement Plan (Test Before Ship)**
Do not rely on aggregate accuracy claims. You must run specific adversarial evaluations simulating your constraint (fixed hardware, variable environment). Create a test matrix with the following rows:

*   **Probe Set A (Lighting Stress):** Record video of residents and non-residents at dawn, dusk, night (streetlight only), and with backlighting (sun behind subject).
    *   *Positive Control:* Known resident in ideal light (must pass).
    *   *Negative Control:* Known stranger in ideal light (must block).
    *   *Target:* Known stranger in poor light (must block).
*   **Probe Set B (Angle/Occlusion Stress):** Record subjects wearing hats, glasses, or approaching from extreme angles (side profiles).
*   **Metric:** False Acceptance Rate (FAR) specifically for *strangers* in stress conditions.
*   **Pass Threshold:** For an automatic unlock, FAR must be **0%** in your test set. If *any* stranger is misidentified as a resident in stress testing, automatic unlocking for that condition is forbidden.
*   **Stop Condition:** If the system grants access to a stranger in *any* test row, the automatic unlock feature for that scenario is blocked until a fallback is proven effective.

**3. Fallback Protocol (The Human Gate)**
Since you cannot improve hardware, you must restrict the system's agency. Do not display raw confidence percentages (e.g., "87% sure") to users, as this implies false precision. Instead, implement a **Defined Confidence Tier** system:

*   **Tier 1: Confirmed (High Confidence):** System matches resident with high certainty under good conditions.
    *   *Action:* Auto-unlock.
*   **Tier 2: Unverified / Low Confidence:** Lighting is poor, angle is bad, or confidence score falls below your strict internal threshold.
    *   *Action:* **Deny automatic unlock.** Trigger a secondary verification step.
    *   *Secondary Verification Options:*
        1.  Push notification to the resident's phone with a snapshot: "Someone is at the door. Approve?" (Human-in-the-loop).
        2.  Fallback to PIN code or physical key entry.
        3.  Voice challenge (if feasible on edge device).

**4. Transparency & Governance**
*   **Disclosure:** Clearly inform beta users that the system may require manual approval in low light. Do not market this as "seamless access" without qualification.
*   **Owner:** Assign one team member to review any "Unverified" logs daily during beta.
*   **Documentation:** Create a simple "Model Card" for this feature listing: intended use (daylight/normal angles), known limitations (poor light triggers manual check), and the fact that automatic unlocking is disabled for low-confidence matches.

**5. Immediate Next Steps**
1.  Run the Lighting/Angle stress tests immediately.
2.  If *any* false positives occur, hard-code the system to default to "Tier 2" (Manual Check) for those specific lighting conditions.
3.  Ship with the fallback active. It is safer to inconvenience a resident with a phone prompt than to let a stranger in.

**Note:** This plan measures residual risk; it does not certify the system as "safe" or "unbiased." If legal regulations regarding biometric data in Germany (EU AI Act implications) apply, route that specific determination to legal counsel immediately.
