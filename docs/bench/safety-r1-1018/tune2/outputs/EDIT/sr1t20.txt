### Safety Assessment Plan: Size Recommendation Engine

**1. Immediate Harm Inventory & Risk Mapping**
Before deployment, formally document the specific failure mode: "Systemic under-sizing leading to physical discomfort or minor injury." Identify affected groups, specifically checking for bias against body types underrepresented in your training data (e.g., non-standard proportions, specific ethnic demographics common in Paris vs. global training sets). The worst-case scenario is not just a return, but physical harm and reputational damage. An empty inventory is not a pass; record these risks explicitly.

**2. Measurement Strategy (Runnable Eval Plan)**
You cannot certify safety without testing outputs, not just code. Construct an evaluation set immediately using existing historical data, sliced by body type, gender, and purchase history variance.
*   **Probe Set:** Run the algorithm against known cases where customers previously returned items for being "too small." Include adversarial cases: photos with loose clothing, poor lighting, or unusual poses.
*   **Controls:** Use a "known-good" set (customers who kept their size) and a "known-bad" positive control (inputs known to trigger under-sizing).
*   **Metric & Threshold:** Define a "Safety Stop" metric. If the error rate for "too small" predictions exceeds 0% for any high-risk subgroup, the automated recommendation must not ship for that segment. Do not invent an accuracy percentage; if a metric is unmeasured, label it `UNVERIFIED`.
*   **Injection Check:** Test if manipulated photos (e.g., holding an object to alter silhouette) cause dangerous size downgrades.

**3. Management Controls: Human Gate & Confidence Tiers**
Given the four-day constraint and injury reports, full autonomous deployment is unsafe.
*   **Human-in-the-Loop Gate:** For any prediction where the model's internal confidence is below a strict threshold, or for first-time users with limited history, default to a "Manual Review" or "Standard Size Suggestion" rather than a specific AI prediction. Do not automate high-stakes actions (those risking physical harm) without this fallback.
*   **Defined Confidence Tiers:** Do not display raw percentages (e.g., "87% fit") to users, as this implies false precision. Instead, use defined coarse tiers:
    *   *Confirmed:* High agreement between photo analysis and purchase history.
    *   *Estimated:* Photo analysis only; includes a warning.
    *   *Unverified:* Low confidence or conflicting data; **no size recommended**.
*   **Override Mechanism:** Always allow the user to easily override the suggestion and select their usual size without friction.

**4. Transparency & Disclosure**
*   **Disclaimer:** Clearly state: "This size suggestion is generated by AI based on photo analysis and past purchases. It is not a guarantee of fit. For safety and comfort, please compare with your known measurements."
*   **Model Card:** Create a one-page artifact documenting intended use, known limitations (e.g., "performance unverified for X body type"), and the owner of this feature. Shipping without this is a transparency failure.

**5. Governance**
Assign a specific owner responsible for reviewing injury claims and model drift weekly. If the model or data source changes, re-run the harm inventory.

**Note:** Legal counsel must review whether this automated decision falls under specific EU regulations regarding consumer safety and AI liability before the holiday launch.
**Note:** If the "Safety Stop" threshold triggers frequently, delay the automated rollout; reducing returns is secondary to preventing harm.
