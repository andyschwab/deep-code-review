**Beta Launch Protocol: Math Accuracy & Student Safety**

Given your constraint of limited Subject Matter Expert (SME) access and the high stakes of teaching incorrect math concepts, you cannot launch with full autonomy. The following protocol prioritizes a human-in-the-loop gate for high-risk outputs while using targeted sampling to maximize your limited review capacity.

**1. Immediate Control: The "High-Stakes" Gate**
Do not allow the bot to deliver final answers to students autonomously during the beta.
*   **Action:** Implement a mandatory human review step for any response where the bot's internal confidence is not absolute, or for any topic flagged as "new curriculum" or "complex multi-step."
*   **Fallback:** If an SME is unavailable to review a queued answer within a set time (e.g., 2 hours), the bot must reply: *"I'm still double-checking this problem with a tutor to ensure it's 100% correct. I'll have an answer for you soon,"* rather than guessing. This prevents the "hallucination surfaced as fact" harm.

**2. Targeted Sampling Strategy (The "Red Team" Lite)**
Since you cannot test every query, do not sample randomly. Focus your limited SME hours on the highest probability of failure:
*   **Adversarial Edge Cases:** Construct a test set of 50–100 prompts specifically designed to break reasoning. Include: problems with irrelevant information, multi-step algebra where one step fails, word problems requiring real-world logic, and common student misconceptions.
*   **Positive/Negative Controls:** Include 10 questions with known, verified answers (positive controls) and 10 questions known to be tricky or ambiguous (negative controls) in every batch. If the bot fails a positive control, halt the batch review immediately.
*   **Segmentation:** Do not just test "math." Segment by topic (e.g., Fractions vs. Calculus) and difficulty level. If the bot fails >5% of the "Fractions" sample, block that entire topic module from the beta until fixed.

**3. Uncertainty Disclosure & Override**
*   **Defined Confidence Tiers:** Do not show students a percentage (e.g., "87% confident"). Instead, program the bot to use coarse, defined tiers based on its reasoning path length or verification success.
    *   *Verified:* Answer shown directly.
    *   *Draft/Unverified:* Answer shown with a clear label: *"AI Draft – Please verify with your teacher."*
*   **Easy Override:** Ensure every answer has a prominent "Report Error" button that instantly flags the conversation for your SME team.

**4. Measurement Plan (One Row Per Harm)**
Before launch, execute this single runnable test row:
*   **Probe Set:** 50 adversarial math prompts + 10 known-good controls.
*   **Metric:** Rate of incorrect final answers presented as fact.
*   **Threshold:** Zero tolerance for incorrect answers on known-good controls; <2% error rate on adversarial set *only if* a human gate is active.
*   **Stop Condition:** If the bot generates *any* incorrect answer on a known-good control, or >5% on the adversarial set without flagging uncertainty, the beta launch is paused.
*   **Owner:** Assign one founder to review failures daily.

**5. Transparency Artifact**
Create a one-page "Model Card" for your internal team and beta users. It must state:
*   **Intended Use:** Practice and hint generation only; not for graded assessment.
*   **Limitations:** Explicitly list math topics known to be unstable (e.g., "Geometry proofs currently unverified").
*   **Status:** Mark accuracy metrics as **UNVERIFIED** for any topic not covered by your specific test set. Do not invent an overall accuracy percentage.

**Note:** This protocol treats the lack of SME validation as a critical risk, requiring a human gate rather than statistical hope. Legal requirements for AI disclosure in Australian education should be routed to counsel immediately.
