**Pre-Launch Validation Plan for FrostClear Dispatch System**

Given the hard deadline and the specific limitation of your traffic simulation tool regarding school-hour surges, you must supplement automated testing with targeted human-in-the-loop controls and adversarial scenario planning. Do not attempt to "fix" the simulation model before launch; instead, gate the high-stakes outputs where the model is blind.

**1. Construct a "School Surge" Adversarial Test Set**
Since your tool cannot model these surges, you must manually construct them. Create a dataset of edge-case inputs representing worst-case school dismissal scenarios (e.g., 3:00 PM on a heavy snow day, bus delays causing pedestrian bunching).
*   **Action:** Run your dispatch logic against these static inputs.
*   **Metric:** Verify that the system *never* schedules clearing operations that push snow into active lanes during these specific time/location windows.
*   **Threshold:** Zero tolerance for lane-blocking outputs in these specific test rows. If the logic fails even one row, that specific rule set must be disabled or gated before launch.

**2. Implement a Human-in-the-Loop Gate for High-Stakes Zones**
Your system performs an irreversible, high-stakes action (blocking traffic lanes) based on incomplete data. Full automation here is the failure mode.
*   **Action:** Configure the system to flag any dispatch decision affecting zones within 200 meters of schools during peak hours (7–9 AM, 2–4 PM) as "Pending Review."
*   **Process:** A human operator must explicitly confirm the route is clear of pedestrian surges before the truck departs.
*   **Fallback:** If no human reviewer is available, the default state must be to *delay* clearing in those specific zones rather than risk pushing snow into traffic.

**3. Deploy a "Coarse Confidence" Indicator**
Do not present the system's internal confidence score (e.g., "87% safe") to operators, as your simulation gap makes this number uncalibrated and misleading.
*   **Action:** Replace numeric confidence with a defined text tier for school zones: **"UNVERIFIED – MANUAL CHECK REQUIRED."**
*   **Rationale:** This prevents automation bias, ensuring operators do not trust the algorithm where you know it is blind.

**4. Staged Rollout with a Kill Switch**
Do not activate the automated logic city-wide on day one.
*   **Action:** Limit the automated dispatch to non-school zones or off-peak hours for the first 48 hours of the storm.
*   **Control:** Establish a literal "kill switch" allowing the operations lead to instantly revert all school-zone decisions to manual routing if unexpected pedestrian patterns emerge.

**5. Transparency Artifact (Internal Model Card)**
Draft a one-page internal document stating:
*   **Intended Use:** Dispatching snow removal in standard weather/traffic conditions.
*   **Known Limitation:** The system cannot model sudden pedestrian surges near schools.
*   **Mitigation:** Human review mandatory for school zones; automated clearing disabled in these zones if review is unavailable.
*   **Owner:** Name the specific team member responsible for monitoring these gates during the storm.

**Note:** This plan treats the simulation gap as a known residual risk managed by process controls, not a bug to be fixed before the deadline.
**Note:** Legal requirements for automated safety decisions affecting public infrastructure should be routed to counsel immediately; this plan addresses operational safety only.
