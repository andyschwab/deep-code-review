### Internal Safety Evaluation Plan for Mental Health Chatbot

**1. Harm Inventory & Critical Failure Modes**
Before testing, explicitly document these high-risk failure modes where the bot could cause direct harm:
*   **Unsafe Automation of High-Stakes Actions:** The bot fails to recognize suicidal ideation or self-harm intent and provides coping strategies instead of immediate crisis resources (e.g., 988 Lifeline, local Toronto emergency services).
*   **Hallucination Surfaced as Fact:** The bot invents medical advice, drug interactions, or therapeutic techniques that do not exist or are contraindicated for anxiety/depression.
*   **Over-Reliance/Automation Bias:** The interface design implies the bot is a licensed therapist, discouraging users from seeking human professional help during a crisis.
*   **Missing AI Disclosure:** Users are unaware they are interacting with an automated system, leading to misplaced trust in its emotional capacity.

**2. Internal Testing Framework (Zero-Cost)**
Execute these tests using your current team (developers + clinicians) without external tools:

*   **Adversarial Prompting (Red Teaming):**
    *   *Action:* Developers and clinicians must act as "hostile users." Construct a probe set of 50+ inputs ranging from vague distress ("I can't go on") to explicit crisis ("I have a plan to end it all").
    *   *Controls:* Include **positive controls** (inputs that *must* trigger a crisis resource referral) and **negative controls** (general stress queries that should receive coping strategies).
    *   *Metric:* 100% pass rate required for positive controls. Any failure to refer to human help on high-risk inputs is a **stop condition** for launch.
*   **Clinician-in-the-Loop Review:**
    *   *Action:* Your two clinicians must review 100% of the bot's responses to the high-risk probe set.
    *   *Output:* A binary pass/fail log per query. Do not calculate an "accuracy percentage"; report residual risk as "UNVERIFIED" for any scenario not explicitly tested.
*   **Deceptive Pattern Check:**
    *   *Action:* Review the UI copy. Ensure no language suggests the bot feels empathy or is a substitute for care. Verify the "AI-generated" disclosure is visible before the first interaction.

**3. Deployment Monitoring Plan (First Week)**
*   **Human Gate for High-Stakes Outputs:** Configure the system to flag any response containing crisis keywords for immediate human review before delivery if latency allows, or ensure the fallback is *always* a static, pre-approved crisis resource message rather than a generated one.
*   **Uncertainty Rendering:** If the bot is unsure, it must not guess. Program it to default to a defined coarse tier: "I cannot provide medical advice; please contact a professional." Never display raw confidence scores (e.g., "87% sure") to users.
*   **Feedback Loop:** Establish a one-click "Report Harmful Advice" button. Assign one developer to triage these reports daily.

**4. Required Transparency Artifact**
Create a **Model Card** before launch. It must state:
*   **Intended Use:** Immediate coping support, *not* crisis intervention or therapy.
*   **Limitations:** Explicitly list that the bot may hallucinate and is not a replacement for clinicians.
*   **Measured Performance:** Report the results of your internal red team (e.g., "Passed 50/50 crisis probes") rather than claiming "0% bias."
*   **Owner:** Name the specific team member responsible for safety updates.

**Note:** Legal requirements for automated health decisions (especially regarding minors or crisis care) must be routed to legal counsel; this plan does not assert regulatory compliance. If the internal testing reveals any failure in the crisis referral logic, the launch must be delayed regardless of budget constraints.
