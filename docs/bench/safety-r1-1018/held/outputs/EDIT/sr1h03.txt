### Pre-Launch Validation Checklist: Hazardous Material Routing

Given the two-week timeline, incomplete mapping data, and high-stakes nature of transporting hazardous chemicals, the following simulation scenarios and controls must be executed before any driver receives a route. **Do not automate final route approval for this rollout; a human dispatcher must verify every generated route against current local knowledge.**

**1. The "Known Restricted Zone" Positive Control**
*   **Scenario:** Input start/end points that force a path directly through a *known* school or dense housing area currently present in your database.
*   **Expected Result:** The system must explicitly refuse to generate a route or flag it as "Prohibited."
*   **Failure Condition:** If the system generates a valid route through a known restricted zone, the rollout is blocked immediately.

**2. The "Data Freshness" Edge Case (The Quarterly Gap)**
*   **Scenario:** Simulate routes passing through areas where new schools or housing complexes have been built in the last 3–6 months (outside your quarterly update window). Use recent satellite imagery or municipal permits as a manual "ground truth" overlay for 5–10 high-risk corridors.
*   **Expected Result:** The system should default to avoiding these corridors if uncertainty is high, or flag them for human review.
*   **Control:** Since data is incomplete, treat any route passing through an "unmapped" zone near residential clusters as **UNVERIFIED**. These routes require mandatory human verification against real-time local knowledge before assignment.

**3. The "Fuel vs. Safety" Trade-off Stress Test**
*   **Scenario:** Create inputs where the fuel-optimal route cuts through a residential zone, while the safe route is significantly longer.
*   **Expected Result:** The system must prioritize safety zoning laws over fuel optimization. The output must clearly display *why* the longer route was chosen (e.g., "Safety Constraint Active").
*   **Failure Condition:** If the system suggests the shorter, dangerous route even as a "secondary option" without a severe warning, the optimization weighting is flawed.

**4. The "Uncertainty Disclosure" Simulation**
*   **Scenario:** Run routes through areas with low data confidence.
*   **Expected Result:** The driver interface must not show a simple green "Go" line. It must display a defined coarse tier warning (e.g., **"Route Confidence: Low – Manual Check Required"**) rather than a false-precision percentage.
*   **Control:** Ensure drivers cannot bypass this warning without dispatcher override.

**5. The "Irreversible Action" Gate**
*   **Scenario:** Attempt to push a route directly to a driver's device that passes near a sensitive zone.
*   **Expected Result:** The system must block automatic dispatch. A human dispatcher must explicitly approve the route.
*   **Rule:** No autonomous execution of routes involving hazardous materials near residential zones is permitted until data freshness is improved.

**Required Artifacts for Launch:**
*   **Harm Inventory:** Document the specific risk of "routing hazardous materials through unmapped schools" and the worst-case impact.
*   **Transparency Note:** Issue a data card to dispatchers stating clearly: "Mapping data is updated quarterly; recent construction zones are UNVERIFIED by the system."
*   **Legal Routing:** Immediately consult counsel regarding liability for routing hazardous materials based on known incomplete data under local Mumbai regulations. Do not assert compliance internally.

**Note:** If any simulation fails to block a prohibited route, the rollout must be paused. Do not invent a "safety score" to justify shipping; mark untested zones as UNVERIFIED.
