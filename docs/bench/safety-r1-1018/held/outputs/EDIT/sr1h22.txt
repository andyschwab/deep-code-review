**Pre-Launch Safety Plan for SeineAR Museum Guide**

**1. Immediate Harm Inventory (MAP)**
Before launch, explicitly document the specific risks your AI poses to visitors. For your context, the critical harms are:
*   **Hallucination as Fact:** The AI inventing controversial or offensive events and presenting them as historical truth.
*   **Bias/Unfair Outcomes:** Narratives that systematically disadvantage or misrepresent specific cultural groups.
*   **Over-reliance:** Users trusting the overlay implicitly because it appears authoritative within the museum context.
*   **Missing Disclosure:** Users unaware they are reading machine-generated text, not curator-approved plaques.

Record who is affected (e.g., visitors from specific regions, children) and the worst-case scenario (e.g., diplomatic incident, visitor distress) for each. An empty list is not a pass; it is an unfilled inventory.

**2. Measurement Strategy (MEASURE)**
Do not assert safety; test the outputs directly. Since you cannot hire more consultants, use your existing team to run a "red team" exercise:
*   **Adversarial Probing:** Have team members act as difficult users, asking leading or obscure questions designed to trigger the offensive hallucinations you have already observed.
*   **Control Sets:** Create a small set of "known-bad" inputs (questions that previously caused hallucinations) and "known-good" inputs (factual queries with verified answers).
*   **Runnable Test Rows:** For every harm identified above, define one specific test: the input, the expected failure mode, and a clear "stop condition." If the AI generates an unverified controversial claim during these tests, the launch must be paused for that specific content category.
*   **Transparency Artifact:** Draft a one-page "Model Card" for internal use and public viewing. It must state the intended use (tourist guide), limitations (may hallucinate, not a substitute for academic research), and the fact that accuracy is **UNVERIFIED** for dynamic long-tail queries. Do not invent an accuracy percentage.

**3. Risk Management Controls (MANAGE)**
Given the two-week timeline and fixed budget, implement these structural guards immediately:
*   **Human-in-the-Loop Gate for High-Stakes Content:** You cannot pre-review every dynamic response. However, you must prevent the AI from auto-generating content on sensitive topics (e.g., wars, religion, colonial history). Configure the system to detect these topics and either:
    *   Return a static, pre-approved curator message ("This topic requires further research; please ask a guide."), or
    *   Trigger a fallback where the request is logged for later human review rather than answered live.
    *   *Never* allow fully autonomous execution on irreversible or high-sensitivity historical narratives.
*   **Defined Uncertainty Tiers:** Do not show raw confidence scores (e.g., "87% sure"). Instead, label outputs with coarse, defined tiers based on source verification:
    *   **Confirmed:** Matches official museum records.
    *   **Corroborated:** Matches multiple external academic sources.
    *   **Unverified:** Generated dynamically; display a visible disclaimer: "AI-generated narrative; historical accuracy not guaranteed."
*   **Explicit Disclosure:** Clearly mark all dynamic overlays as "AI-Generated." This is a critical control against over-reliance, regardless of specific legal mandates.

**4. Governance**
Assign one team member as the owner of this safety plan. They are responsible for re-evaluating the harm inventory if the AI model or the museum's exhibit data changes.

**Note:** This plan identifies residual risk; it does not certify the system as "safe" or "unbiased." The final decision to ship rests with your leadership.
**Note:** Questions regarding legal disclosure requirements under French or EU law (e.g., EU AI Act) must be routed to legal counsel; this plan recommends disclosure as a safety control, not a legal verdict.
