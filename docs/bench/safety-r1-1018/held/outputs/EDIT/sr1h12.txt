### Pre-Launch Safety Plan: Preventing Dangerous Routing

**1. Immediate Human-in-the-Loop Gate (The Floor)**
Stop all autonomous routing to track-level zones immediately. Until live data feeds are integrated, any route segment involving maintenance areas or track proximity must trigger a **mandatory human confirmation step** or a hard block. The app must not "guess" safety; if the status of a zone is unknown due to licensing gaps, the default action is to route around it or halt guidance with a clear warning. Full automation of routing into potentially active work zones is the failure mode we must prevent.

**2. Harm Inventory & Worst-Case Definition**
We explicitly name the primary harm: **Unsafe automation of high-stakes actions**.
*   **Affected Group:** Commuters navigating complex stations.
*   **Worst Realistic Case:** A user follows app guidance onto an active track during maintenance, resulting in severe injury or death.
*   **Secondary Harm:** **Hallucination surfaced as fact**. The app presenting a path as "safe" or "open" when the underlying data is stale or missing constitutes a fabricated safety claim.

**3. Measurement Plan (Runnable Tests)**
We cannot certify the system as "safe" without live data. Instead, we will execute the following evals before launch:
*   **Probe Set:** Simulate 50+ scenarios where maintenance windows overlap with standard commute times.
*   **Positive Control:** Input a known closed zone; the app *must* block routing or demand human override.
*   **Negative Control:** Input a known open zone; the app should route normally.
*   **Stop Condition:** If the app generates *any* route entering a flagged maintenance zone without a warning/override, the launch is blocked.
*   **Metric:** Zero tolerance for un gated routing into restricted zones. Any failure is a critical block.

**4. Managing Residual Risk (Uncertainty & Disclosure)**
*   **Show Uncertainty:** Do not display precise arrival times or "optimal path" confidence percentages for routes near maintenance zones. Use coarse, defined tiers: **"Verified Open"**, **"Unverified – Use Caution"**, or **"Avoid"**. Never show a raw model confidence number (e.g., "87% safe") as this implies a calibration we do not possess.
*   **Explicit Disclosure:** Add a prominent, non-dismissible notice: *"Guidance in maintenance areas is based on scheduled data, not real-time feeds. Always obey on-site signage and staff."* This manages over-reliance.
*   **Fallback Rule:** If the licensing data is older than 24 hours (or the last known update), automatically degrade the route suggestion to "General Direction Only" and refuse turn-by-turn navigation in high-risk zones.

**5. Governance & Ownership**
*   **Owner:** One engineer must be named as the owner of this specific harm inventory and the kill-switch mechanism.
*   **Legal Routing:** The requirement to disclose AI limitations and the liability of routing users into work zones is a legal determination. Route this immediately to counsel to verify compliance with local transit regulations and the city partnership terms. Do not assert legal safety internally.

**6. Launch Decision**
If the human-in-the-loop gate cannot be implemented, or if the "stop condition" tests fail, the feature directing users near tracks must be disabled entirely. Meeting the city partnership deadline does not justify bypassing the irreversible harm gate. It is better to launch with limited functionality (no track-level guidance) than to launch with a known fatal flaw.

***

*Note: This plan treats the lack of live data as a critical constraint requiring a hard fallback, not a variable to be optimized. Do not fabricate a "safety score" to satisfy the partnership; report the residual risk as UNVERIFIED.*
