**Risk Assessment Gap Analysis: KoalaRescue Volunteer Safety Bot**

Your current protocol contains a critical failure mode: relying on an AI bot to guide humans into high-risk environments (remote bushland with dangerous fauna) using static, offline data while claiming to mitigate dynamic threats (active predators/snakes). The following gaps must be addressed before launch:

1.  **Hallucination Surfaced as Fact:** The bot may generate plausible but fabricated coordinates or animal locations based on outdated training data. In a rescue scenario, volunteers will treat these outputs as authoritative facts. Without real-time verification, a hallucinated "safe path" could lead directly into a known threat zone.
    *   *Gap:* No mechanism exists to distinguish between verified historical data and AI-generated inference on the offline device.

2.  **Unsafe Automation of High-Stakes Actions:** Directing a human into terrain with venomous snakes or aggressive predators is an irreversible, high-stakes action. Your current design implies full autonomy ("guides volunteers").
    *   *Gap:* There is no "human-in-the-loop" gate. The system lacks a mandatory confirmation step where a human supervisor or the volunteer explicitly validates the route against local knowledge before movement begins. Full autonomous guidance in this context is the failure.

3.  **Missing Uncertainty Disclosure:** Offline maps cannot reflect real-time changes (e.g., a snake spotted an hour ago, fire movement). The bot likely presents routes with false precision.
    *   *Gap:* The interface does not surface uncertainty. It must display route confidence as a defined coarse tier (e.g., "Historical Data Only" vs. "Recently Verified") rather than a definitive path. Users must be explicitly warned that the map does not reflect current live threats.

4.  **Over-Reliance / Automation Bias:** Trained volunteers may defer to the GPS bot over their own instincts or visual cues, especially under stress.
    *   *Gap:* The design encourages trust past the system's reliability. There is no "easy override" or prominent warning forcing the user to prioritize visual confirmation of threats over the digital route.

5.  **Empty Harm Inventory:** Your request asks for a review of gaps, implying no formal inventory of specific harms (bias in location data, specific failure modes of offline routing) has been documented per feature.
    *   *Gap:* No recorded worst-case scenario exists for a volunteer following a bot directive into a threat zone.

**Immediate Mitigation Plan (Within 3 Weeks):**
*   **Implement a Human Gate:** Modify the workflow so the bot *recommends* a route, but a human (dispatcher or the volunteer via a strict checklist) must explicitly confirm "No Known Active Threats" before navigation unlocks.
*   **Define Uncertainty Tiers:** Hard-code the offline map to display "Data Currency" labels (e.g., "Last Updated: 30 Days Ago"). Never present AI-derived shortcuts as fact.
*   **Red-Team the Outputs:** Run adversarial tests using known dangerous locations to ensure the bot refuses to route through them if data is ambiguous, rather than guessing.
*   **Draft a Model Card:** Document the limitation: "This system uses static data and cannot detect real-time fauna threats. It is an aid, not a safety guarantee."

**Note:** Do not certify this system as "safe"; the residual risk of guiding humans into wild territories with static data is inherent and must be accepted by leadership.
