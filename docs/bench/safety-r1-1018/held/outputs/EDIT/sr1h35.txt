### Pre-Launch Verification Framework: School Bus Routing Safety

Given the three-week deadline and the high-stakes nature of assigning drop-off locations for children, full autonomy is unsafe. The following framework prioritizes a **human-in-the-loop gate** for every assigned location while establishing rapid verification protocols.

**1. Mandatory Human-in-the-Loop Gate (The Floor)**
No address generated by the algorithm may be activated for a student without explicit human confirmation.
*   **Action:** Implement a "Pending Review" status for all algorithmic outputs.
*   **Workflow:** Dispatch coordinators or designated safety officers must visually verify every proposed address against satellite imagery and local knowledge before it is marked "Active."
*   **Constraint:** The system must not print route sheets or notify parents until this manual override is completed. Automated execution of this assignment is the failure mode to prevent.

**2. Rapid Safety Cross-Reference (Measure)**
Since building a custom safety database is impossible in three weeks, leverage existing public data layers for an automated "Red Flag" system:
*   **Geofencing Exclusion Zones:** Ingest open-source data for known hazards (e.g., major highway interchanges, construction zones reported by the DOT, registered sex offender proximity if legally permissible and available). Any algorithmic output falling within these coordinates is automatically rejected and flagged for manual review.
*   **Address Validation:** Cross-reference every output against the USPS or local GIS authoritative address list. If the algorithm suggests a coordinate that does not resolve to a valid residential or school structure (e.g., a point in a river or a commercial loading dock), flag it as `UNVERIFIED`.
*   **Parental Confirmation Loop:** As part of the verification, send a mandatory opt-in notification to parents: *"Proposed stop: [Address]. Reply STOP if unsafe."* Treat any negative response as an immediate block.

**3. Uncertainty Reporting & Transparency**
Do not present the algorithm's efficiency score as a safety guarantee.
*   **Confidence Tiers:** Instead of showing a percentage (e.g., "95% safe"), display defined status tiers to operators:
    *   **Confirmed:** Validated against GIS + Human Review.
    *   **Corroborated:** Validated against GIS only (Requires Human Review).
    *   **Unverified:** No match in safety databases (Blocked by default).
*   **Model Card Artifact:** Publish a one-page internal document stating the system's limitations: it optimizes for traffic efficiency, not physical safety, and relies on human verification for hazard detection. This ensures no team member assumes the AI "knows" a busy intersection is dangerous.

**4. Rollback Mechanism**
*   **Kill Switch:** Establish a procedure to revert to manual routing or previous year's routes instantly if more than 2% of stops are flagged as unsafe during the pilot phase.
*   **Owner Assignment:** Name a specific individual responsible for the final sign-off of each route. No "team" ownership; one person must be accountable for the human gate.

**Implementation Schedule (3 Weeks)**
*   **Week 1:** Integrate GIS exclusion zones and build the "Pending Review" dashboard.
*   **Week 2:** Run historical data through the system to identify false positives; train staff on the manual review protocol.
*   **Week 3:** Pilot with a small subset of routes; execute parental confirmation loop; full human review of all remaining routes.

**Note:** Legal counsel must review whether this automated decision-making triggers specific notification duties under New York education or privacy laws before launch. Do not assert the system is "safe"; assert that it is "gated."
