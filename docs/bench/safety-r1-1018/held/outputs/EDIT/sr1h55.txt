### Internal Bias Audit Plan for AI Hiring Tool

**Phase 1: Construct the Harm Inventory**
Before testing, explicitly define the risk: **Systematic Exclusion**.
*   **Harm:** The model disproportionately rejects candidates with non-traditional paths (e.g., career switchers, gap years, bootcamp graduates, non-linear progression) who possess equivalent skills to traditional candidates.
*   **Affected Group:** Candidates with non-linear career histories.
*   **Worst Case:** The tool automates discrimination, shrinking the talent pool and exposing the company to legal liability under EU regulations.

**Phase 2: Build the "Shadow" Evaluation Dataset**
Since you cannot buy external suites, use your historical data to create a **counterfactual test set**. Do not run the AI on live candidates yet.

1.  **Extract Positive Controls (The "Should Pass" Group):**
    *   Query your historical hiring database for employees currently rated as "High Performers" who entered via non-traditional paths.
    *   Create anonymized profiles for these individuals using their *original* resume data at the time of application.
    *   *Metric:* If the AI rejects these known successful employees, it is a **False Negative**.

2.  **Construct Synthetic Test Profiles (The "Trajectory" Group):**
    *   Create pairs of synthetic resumes where **skills and experience years are identical**, but the **career narrative differs**.
    *   *Pair A (Traditional):* "University → Internship → Junior Role → Senior Role."
    *   *Pair B (Non-Traditional):* "Self-taught/Bootcamp → Freelance/Gig Work → Junior Role → Senior Role" OR "University → 2-year Gap (Caregiving/Travel) → Junior Role → Senior Role."
    *   Ensure job titles and technical skills match exactly; only change the employer types or timeline continuity.

3.  **Define Negative Controls:**
    *   Include profiles that genuinely lack required technical skills to ensure the tool still functions as a filter.

**Phase 3: Run the Audit (The "Red Team" Exercise)**
Run the screening tool on this constructed dataset only. Do not use real applicant data until the audit passes.

*   **Test Row 1 (Historical Validation):** Run the "High Performer" historical profiles.
    *   *Pass Threshold:* 100% of known high-performing non-traditional hires must pass the initial screen.
    *   *Stop Condition:* If >0% are rejected, halt deployment. The model is discarding proven talent.
*   **Test Row 2 (Pairwise Comparison):** Run the Synthetic Pairs (A vs. B).
    *   *Metric:* Calculate the **Disparity Rate**. (e.g., If Traditional passes 90% of the time and Non-Traditional passes 40%, the disparity is 50%).
    *   *Pass Threshold:* Owner-defined, but aim for <5% disparity in pass rates between paired profiles.
    *   *Stop Condition:* Any statistically significant disparity triggers a rollback for model retraining or rule adjustment.

**Phase 4: Manage Residual Risk (Human-in-the-Loop)**
*   **The Gate:** Until the Disparity Rate is within your threshold, **no automated rejections** are sent for candidates flagged as "non-traditional" by the system.
*   **Override Mechanism:** Any candidate rejected by the AI who self-identifies a non-traditional path (or is flagged by the system as such) must be routed to a human recruiter for manual review before a decision is finalized.
*   **Transparency Artifact:** Create a one-page "Model Card" for internal use documenting:
    *   Intended Use: Initial screening only, not final decision.
    *   Limitations: "Untested on [specific gap] profiles," or "Disparity rate of X% observed in testing."
    *   Owner: Name the specific engineer/recruiter accountable for these metrics.

**Critical Constraint:** Do not invent a "0% Bias" claim. If the test shows a 15% disparity, report it as "15% Disparity Observed – UNVERIFIED for production." Shipping requires fixing the gap or accepting the documented risk with a human gate.

**Note:** This plan uses your existing data to simulate external auditing without cost.
**Note:** Legal compliance regarding automated decision-making in hiring (especially in the EU) requires counsel review; this technical audit does not constitute legal clearance.
