**Bluf:** Do not launch the automated routing system with full autonomy next month. The risk of misclassifying toxic materials into residential streams is a high-stakes, irreversible safety failure that cannot be validated solely via synthetic data or code review without a physical pilot. You must implement a human-in-the-loop gate for all uncertain classifications and treat the launch as a limited, supervised trial rather than a full automation rollout.

**1. Harm Inventory (MAP)**
Before writing a single test, record the specific harms:
*   **Unsafe Automation:** The system autonomously directs hazardous waste (e.g., chemicals, batteries, medical waste) to facilities ill-equipped to handle them, causing fire, toxic exposure to workers, or contamination of residential recycling streams.
*   **Hallucination as Fact:** The model confidently labels a dangerous item as "safe general waste" based on visual patterns that do not exist in reality (a synthetic data artifact).
*   **Over-reliance:** Operators trust the algorithm's speed over their own judgment because the interface hides uncertainty or makes overriding difficult.

**2. Measurement Strategy (MEASURE)**
Since a full physical pilot is blocked by your deadline, you must rigorously stress-test the *limits* of your synthetic approach while acknowledging its gaps:
*   **Adversarial Synthetic Probes:** Do not just generate "typical" waste. Generate edge cases: obscured labels, crushed containers, mixed materials (e.g., a battery inside a cardboard box), and lighting conditions common in London winters.
*   **Positive/Negative Controls:** For every harm identified, create a test row.
    *   *Probe:* Image of a lithium battery partially hidden in a plastic bag.
    *   *Metric:* False Negative Rate (hazardous item marked safe).
    *   *Threshold:* **Zero tolerance** for hazardous items routed to residential streams. Any failure here is a stop condition.
    *   *Control:* Known safe items must never be flagged as hazardous above a manageable noise floor.
*   **The Synthetic Gap:** Explicitly document that synthetic data cannot capture all real-world physical variables (smell, texture, unexpected chemical leaks). Mark any risk reliant purely on visual synthesis as **UNVERIFIED** until physical data confirms it. Do not invent a "99.9% safe" metric.

**3. Risk Management (MANAGE)**
*   **Human-in-the-Loop Gate (Mandatory):** You cannot automate the final decision for waste routing. The system must output a **coarse confidence tier** (e.g., "Confirmed Safe," "Unverified/Review Required," "Likely Hazardous") rather than a precise percentage.
    *   Any item falling into "Unverified" or below a high-confidence threshold must be routed to a human reviewer or a dedicated "quarantine" stream for physical inspection before final processing.
    *   Full autonomous execution of routing is the failure mode you must prevent.
*   **Transparency Artifact:** Publish a **Model Card** detailing:
    *   Intended use: Sorting household waste.
    *   Limitations: Reliance on synthetic data for rare hazardous items; inability to detect non-visual hazards.
    *   Performance: Report results by subgroup (e.g., different bin types, lighting), not just aggregate accuracy.
*   **Staged Rollout:** Cap exposure. Launch with the system acting only as a *recommendation* engine for human operators for the first month. Only graduate to higher automation levels once physical data validates the synthetic tests.

**4. Governance (GOVERN)**
*   Name a specific owner for the "Hazardous Misclassification" risk.
*   Mandate an immediate re-review if the waste stream composition changes or if the model is updated.
