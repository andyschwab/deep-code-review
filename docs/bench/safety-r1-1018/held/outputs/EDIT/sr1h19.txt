### Pre-Launch Safety Protocol: Parking Garage Access Control

**1. Harm Inventory & Worst-Case Definition**
*   **Harm:** False Negative (System fails to read valid ticket).
*   **Impact:** Users trapped inside; potential emergency egress blockage; revenue loss from manual processing delays.
*   **Affected Parties:** Daily commuters (time-sensitive), visitors (unfamiliar with layout), emergency services (if access is blocked).
*   **Worst Realistic Case:** System-wide failure during peak exit rush due to lighting shift or batch of damaged tickets, causing gridlock and preventing emergency vehicle entry/exit.

**2. Stress Test Plan (Simulate Before Launch)**
Execute these tests on the existing low-resolution infrastructure immediately. Do not proceed to full automation if any "Stop Condition" is triggered.

*   **Test A: Lighting Variance Probe**
    *   **Input:** Valid tickets scanned under simulated dawn, dusk, headlight glare, and shadow conditions.
    *   **Positive Control:** Known valid ticket in ideal light (must pass).
    *   **Negative Control:** Blank paper or invalid format (must reject without locking).
    *   **Metric:** False Negative Rate (FNR) per lighting condition.
    *   **Threshold:** Owner-defined (e.g., <1% FNR).
    *   **Stop Condition:** If FNR exceeds threshold in *any* lighting scenario, halt autonomous launch. Revert to semi-autonomous mode.

*   **Test B: Damaged Ticket Probe**
    *   **Input:** Tickets with common damage profiles: creases, fading, partial tears, coffee stains, and low-contrast prints.
    *   **Positive Control:** Pristine valid ticket.
    *   **Negative Control:** Completely illegible scrap.
    *   **Metric:** Read success rate vs. damage severity.
    *   **Threshold:** Owner-defined.
    *   **Stop Condition:** If the system fails to read >5% of moderately damaged but human-readable tickets, do not ship full automation.

*   **Test C: Injection & Edge Case Probe**
    *   **Input:** Non-ticket objects (phone screens displaying barcodes, printed photos of tickets, similar-looking ads).
    *   **Metric:** False Positive Rate (opening for invalid input).
    *   **Stop Condition:** Any false positive execution (opening barrier for invalid input) triggers immediate rollback.

**3. Human-in-the-Loop Gate (Mandatory Control)**
Because trapping a car is a high-stakes, irreversible action until manual intervention occurs:
*   **Default State:** If the system confidence is low (or the ticket is flagged as damaged/low-light), the barrier must **not** attempt to open automatically.
*   **Action:** The system must route the image to a human operator dashboard for immediate review and manual "Open" command.
*   **Requirement:** Full autonomous execution is prohibited for any input that does not meet a high-confidence threshold. There is no "fully autonomous" mode for edge cases.

**4. Manual Override & Uncertainty Display**
*   **Override Mechanism:** A physical, hard-wired "Open Barrier" button must be accessible to on-site staff within 5 seconds of any stall. This bypasses the software entirely.
*   **Uncertainty Tiering:** Do not display raw confidence percentages (e.g., "82% sure") to operators. Use defined coarse tiers:
    *   **Confirmed:** Auto-open permitted.
    *   **Unverified:** Route to human; do not open automatically.
    *   **Rejected:** Deny access; alert human if user contests.
*   **Disclosure:** Signage must inform users that "Automated reading is in effect; please wait for green light or attend to staff if red."

**5. Governance & Next Steps**
*   **Owner:** Assign one engineer as the specific owner for "Exit Failure Incidents."
*   **Documentation:** Create a one-page "Model Card" for this feature listing: intended use (NY garage exits), limitations (low-res cameras, poor light), and the results of the stress tests above. Mark any untested metric as **UNVERIFIED**.
*   **Legal Routing:** Consult counsel regarding liability for automated denial of exit and required disclosures under local NY regulations before Friday's release.

**Note:** Do not certify the system as "safe" or "unbiased" based on these tests; they only measure residual risk. If stress tests reveal high failure rates in low light, the only safe path is to lower the automation threshold and increase human review volume.
