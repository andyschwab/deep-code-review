### Lightweight Offline Triage Validation Strategy

**1. Construct the "Golden Set" of Simulated Records**
Before deployment, your seven doctors must curate a fixed dataset of 50–100 simulated patient records on a local USB drive or tablet. This set must be heavily weighted toward edge cases:
*   **Positive Controls (Critical):** 20 cases that *must* trigger an emergency referral (e.g., chest pain with diaphoresis, stroke symptoms, severe trauma). If the system routes any of these to a general practitioner, it is a critical failure.
*   **Negative Controls (Routine):** 20 cases that *must* route to general practice (e.g., mild fever, routine checkup).
*   **Ambiguous/Edge Cases:** 10–20 records with missing data or conflicting symptoms to test how the system handles uncertainty.

**2. Execute the "Blind Dry Run" Protocol**
Local clinic staff will run these simulated records through the scheduler on the offline device without knowing the expected outcome.
*   **Input:** Staff enters the symptom data exactly as they would for a real patient.
*   **Output Recording:** Staff records the system's recommended destination (Emergency vs. General) on a simple paper checklist next to the case ID.
*   **Comparison:** Once the batch is complete, the lead doctor compares the recorded outputs against the known correct answers from the Golden Set.

**3. Define the "Stop Condition" (Human Gate)**
Establish a zero-tolerance rule for high-stakes errors:
*   **Failure Criterion:** If *any* Positive Control (critical case) is misrouted to a general practitioner, the system **must not** be used for real patients.
*   **Action:** The rollout halts immediately. The specific failure cases are flagged for manual rule adjustment by the development team before re-testing.
*   **Success Criterion:** 100% accuracy on Positive Controls is required to proceed. Accuracy on Negative Controls should be high, but missing a critical case is the only non-negotiable blocker.

**4. Implement Uncertainty Tiers and Overrides**
Since connectivity prevents real-time model updates, the interface must explicitly show confidence levels using defined text tiers, not percentages:
*   **"Confirmed Emergency":** Direct route to specialist.
*   **"Unverified/Unclear":** If symptoms are ambiguous, the system must default to the higher level of care (Emergency) or flag for immediate human review, rather than guessing.
*   **Manual Override:** Staff must have a clearly visible button to override the system's suggestion and force an emergency referral if their clinical judgment disagrees. The system should log this override for later review but never block it.

**5. Documentation and Ownership**
Create a one-page "Triage Limitations Card" to stay with the device. It must list:
*   Known failure modes (e.g., "System may underestimate severity in pediatric cases with vague symptoms").
*   The name of the lead doctor responsible for reviewing any overrides or misroutes weekly.
*   A clear statement that the tool is a decision *support* system, not a replacement for clinical judgment.

This approach requires no cloud connectivity, relies on local expertise for validation, and places a hard human gate on the most dangerous failure mode: downgrading an urgent case.

**Note:** This strategy measures output harm locally but does not certify the system as "safe" or "unbiased"; residual risk remains until real-world performance is monitored. Legal requirements for AI disclosure in medical settings in India should be verified by counsel before rollout.
