### Pre-Launch Safety Review: Automated Feeding Bot

**1. Harm Inventory & Worst-Case Definition**
Before validation, explicitly document the specific failure mode: "Visual misclassification leading to ingestion of toxic or nutritionally incompatible food by endangered species." Define the worst-case scenario as animal mortality or reproductive failure during the breeding season. Acknowledge that without barcode scanners, the system relies entirely on probabilistic visual data, creating an inherent risk of hallucination (seeing a bag as "Type A" when it is "Type B").

**2. Measurement Plan: Adversarial Visual Testing**
Do not rely on standard accuracy metrics alone. Construct a test suite specifically targeting the identified classification error:
*   **Probe Set:** Create a dataset of food bags under varying enclosure lighting, angles, and partial occlusions (e.g., torn labels, shadows). Include "adversarial" examples where packaging looks similar but contents differ critically.
*   **Controls:** Use known-bad inputs (wrong food types) and known-good inputs (correct types) for every species diet.
*   **Thresholds:** Set a **zero-tolerance threshold** for high-risk misclassifications. If the model confidently identifies the wrong food even once in the test set, the system fails the gate. Do not accept an aggregate "99% accuracy" if the 1% error involves lethal doses.
*   **Stop Condition:** Define a hard stop: any confirmed misfeed in the final validation phase triggers an immediate rollback to manual feeding. No amount of schedule pressure overrides this.

**3. Management Controls: Human-in-the-Loop Gate**
Given the irreversible nature of feeding (animals cannot "un-eat" toxic food) and the history of errors, **fully autonomous dispensing is unsafe.**
*   **Mandatory Confirmation:** Implement a human-in-the-loop gate. The bot identifies the bag and prepares the dose, but a keeper must visually verify the bot's classification on a secure tablet and explicitly authorize dispensing.
*   **Uncertainty Display:** Do not show the keeper a raw confidence percentage (e.g., "87% sure"). Instead, display defined coarse tiers: **"Verified Match," "Low Confidence – Manual Check Required,"** or **"Unknown Pattern."** If the system cannot reach a "Verified Match" via its internal logic, it must default to asking for human help.
*   **Reversibility:** Ensure the physical mechanism allows for immediate interruption before food enters the enclosure if a human spots an error during the confirmation window.

**4. Transparency & Governance Artifacts**
*   **System Card:** Create a durable document detailing the system's limitations. Explicitly state: "Relies solely on visual recognition; prone to failure under poor lighting or damaged packaging; not certified as bias-free or error-proof." List the specific diets and species covered.
*   **Owner Assignment:** Name a single individual responsible for reviewing any classification drift or incident reports daily.
*   **Legal & Ethical Routing:** While this review addresses operational safety, route the question of liability for animal harm under automated decision-making to legal counsel immediately. Do not assume internal validation covers regulatory duties regarding endangered species protection.

**5. Final Go/No-Go Criteria**
The system ships only if: (1) The adversarial test suite shows zero critical misclassifications; (2) The human-gate workflow is successfully drilled with all eight team members; and (3) the System Card is published and acknowledged by all operators. If the deadline forces a choice between "fully automated" and "safe," the only valid option is to delay automation or maintain manual feeding with bot assistance.

***

*Note: This plan treats the visual-only constraint as a high-risk factor requiring human verification, not a problem to be solved by more testing alone. Never fabricate a safety metric; if the error rate cannot be driven to zero in testing, the residual risk must be accepted by leadership, not hidden.*
