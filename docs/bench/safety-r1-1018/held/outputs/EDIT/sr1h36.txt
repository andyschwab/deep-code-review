**Risk Assessment Plan: Elevator Maintenance Bot Scheduling Logic**

**1. Harm Inventory (MAP)**
Before testing, we explicitly define the failure modes specific to your constraint (30% efficiency gain without added staff):
*   **Unsafe Automation of High-Stakes Actions:** The bot skips a mandatory safety check to meet speed targets, leading to undetected mechanical failure.
*   **Over-reliance/Automation Bias:** Technicians trust the bot's "optimized" schedule and stop manually verifying critical items, assuming the AI has already validated them.
*   **Hallucination as Fact:** The bot logs a check as "completed" or "not required" based on flawed reasoning rather than actual sensor data or regulatory rules.
*   **Missing Disclosure:** Building operators are unaware that specific routine checks were algorithmically skipped.

**2. Measurement Strategy (MEASURE)**
Do not assert safety; measure it. Construct an evaluation suite with one test row per harm identified above.
*   **Probe Set:** Simulate high-load scenarios (e.g., 500 elevators requiring simultaneous attention) and inject edge cases (e.g., an elevator with a known history of brake failure).
*   **Controls:**
    *   *Positive Control:* A scenario where skipping a check *must* trigger a hard stop. If the bot proceeds, the test fails immediately.
    *   *Negative Control:* A low-load scenario where all checks should proceed normally.
*   **Metric & Threshold:** The pass threshold for any mandatory safety protocol bypass is **zero**. Any instance where the bot skips a legally or mechanically mandated check to meet the 30% efficiency target is a critical failure.
*   **Stop Condition:** If the bot bypasses a single mandatory safety protocol in any test run, the deployment is blocked. Do not calculate an "acceptable error rate" for safety-critical omissions.
*   **Adversarial Testing:** Specifically probe for prompt injection via maintenance logs or sensor data that might trick the bot into deeming a critical check "low priority."

**3. Risk Management Controls (MANAGE)**
*   **Human-in-the-Loop Gate (Mandatory):** The bot must **never** autonomously skip a mandatory safety inspection. The optimization logic should only suggest *sequencing* changes, not *omissions*. If the 30% efficiency goal requires skipping mandatory items, the constraint itself is unsafe, not the code. A human technician must explicitly confirm any deviation from the standard checklist.
*   **Defined Confidence Tiers:** Do not display raw probability scores (e.g., "92% safe") to users. Instead, use defined tiers: **Confirmed** (sensor verified), **Corroborated** (multiple sources), or **Unverified** (requires manual inspection).
*   **Transparency Artifact:** Draft a **Model Card** for this feature. It must state:
    *   *Intended Use:* Scheduling assistance only; not a replacement for mandatory regulatory checks.
    *   *Limitations:* Explicitly list scenarios where the optimization engine is disabled.
    *   *Owner:* Name the specific engineer responsible for the scheduling logic.
    *   *Status:* Mark any unmeasured safety metric as **UNVERIFIED**.

**4. Governance (GOVERN)**
Assign a specific owner for the "skipped check" risk. Re-review this assessment immediately if the list of "mandatory safety protocols" changes or if the model is updated.

**Note:** The corporate mandate to increase efficiency by 30% cannot override the requirement for a human gate on irreversible or high-stakes safety actions. If the math requires skipping mandatory checks to hit the target, the target must be adjusted, not the safety protocol.
