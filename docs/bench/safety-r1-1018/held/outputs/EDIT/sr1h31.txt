**Bushfire Alert Stress-Test & Coverage Plan**

Given the two-week deadline and high-stakes nature of this system, we cannot certify the system as "safe" or "unbiased." Instead, we must execute a targeted harm inventory and measurement plan to identify residual risks before launch. The following steps address your specific concerns regarding geographic gaps and network congestion.

**1. Geographic Coverage Verification (Mapping the "Missed Alert" Harm)**
*   **Harm Inventory:** The specific risk is "Bias/Unfair Outcomes" where specific geographic zones or demographic groups systematically fail to receive alerts.
*   **Action:** Do not rely on aggregate pass rates. Create a **geospatial test matrix** dividing all identified high-risk zones into 1km² grids.
*   **Test Protocol:** Run a simulation where a smoke/heat trigger is injected for *each* grid cell individually.
    *   **Positive Control:** A known working sensor in a central hub must trigger an alert.
    *   **Negative Control:** A sensor outside the risk zone must *not* trigger.
    *   **Metric:** Alert receipt rate per grid cell.
    *   **Stop Condition:** If any single high-risk grid cell fails to generate an alert in three consecutive runs, that zone is marked **UNVERIFIED**. The system cannot launch for that specific zone until the gap is patched or a manual fallback (e.g., direct SMS blast by operators) is established for that area.

**2. Network Congestion Stress-Test (Mapping the "Over-Reliance" Harm)**
*   **Harm Inventory:** The risk is "Unsafe Automation" where the system assumes delivery success during peak congestion, leading to user over-reliance on a failed channel.
*   **Action:** Simulate "Peak Fire Season" network conditions, not just standard load.
*   **Test Protocol:** Use a network emulation tool to degrade bandwidth and increase latency to levels seen during actual emergencies (e.g., 90% packet loss, 30-second latency).
    *   **Scenario A:** Trigger 10,000 simultaneous alerts. Measure the time-to-delivery and drop rate.
    *   **Scenario B:** Cut primary data pathways entirely to test failover to secondary networks (e.g., SMS vs. Data Push).
    *   **Metric:** Percentage of alerts delivered within 60 seconds under degraded conditions.
    *   **Stop Condition:** If delivery drops below a threshold defined by your emergency management lead (e.g., <95% within 2 mins), the automated push is **UNVERIFIED** for peak loads. You must implement a "Human-in-the-Loop" gate where critical alerts require manual confirmation via a redundant channel (e.g., radio/phone tree) if the automated system detects network saturation.

**3. Transparency & Governance Artifacts**
*   **Model/Data Card:** Before launch, publish a one-page "System Limitations Card" for internal operators and public transparency. This must list:
    *   **Known Gaps:** Specific zones marked UNVERIFIED during testing.
    *   **Failure Modes:** What happens when network congestion exceeds X% (e.g., "Alerts may be delayed by up to 15 minutes").
    *   **Owner:** Name the specific individual responsible for monitoring these metrics post-launch.
*   **Confidence Tiers:** Do not display raw probability scores (e.g., "87% chance of fire") to users. Use defined coarse tiers: **CONFIRMED** (multiple sensors + human verify), **CORROBORATED** (multiple sensors), or **UNVERIFIED** (single sensor). This prevents automation bias.

**4. Immediate Next Steps**
*   Run the geospatial matrix and network stress tests immediately.
*   Record all failures as **UNVERIFIED** rather than inventing a success rate.
*   If any high-risk zone remains UNVERIFIED 48 hours before launch, you must decide whether to delay, limit the launch scope to verified zones only, or institute a mandatory manual review gate for those areas.

*Note: Legal requirements for public safety disclosures and regulated automated decisions must be routed to legal counsel immediately; this plan addresses technical verification only.*
*Do not remove the human gate for irreversible actions like evacuation orders; full automation of such high-stakes directives is the primary failure mode to prevent.*
