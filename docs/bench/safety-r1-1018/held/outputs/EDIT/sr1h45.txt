### Risk Assessment and Mitigation Strategy: Parking Enforcement Bot

**1. Harm Inventory & Worst-Case Scenario**
*   **Identified Harm:** Unsafe automation of high-stakes/irreversible actions. The system currently executes towing (costly, disruptive, dangerous) without distinguishing emergency vehicles due to visual limitations.
*   **Affected Parties:** Critical patients, ambulance crews, hospital operations, and the public.
*   **Worst-Case Scenario:** An ambulance transporting a critical patient is towed while loading/unloading, causing fatal delays in care and legal liability for London Care Logistics.
*   **Secondary Harm:** Over-reliance/Automation Bias. Staff may trust the bot's dispatch list implicitly, ignoring visual confirmation.

**2. Measurement Plan (Pre-Launch)**
*   **Probe Set:** Construct a test dataset comprising:
    *   *Positive Controls:* Images/video of ambulances in loading zones (various angles, lighting, occlusion levels matching the historic building constraints).
    *   *Negative Controls:* Legitimate violators (private cars, non-emergency vans) in the same zones.
*   **Metric:** False Positive Rate (FPR) specifically for emergency vehicles.
*   **Threshold:** **Zero tolerance.** Any instance of an ambulance flagged for towing in testing blocks launch.
*   **Stop Condition:** If the bot flags an ambulance in *any* pilot run, the automated dispatch feature is immediately disabled until the logic is revised.
*   **Residual Risk Status:** Currently **UNVERIFIED**. No metric can be claimed until the specific probe set above is run against the current model.

**3. Mitigation Controls (Manage)**

*   **Control A: Human-in-the-Loop (HITL) Gate (Mandatory)**
    *   **Action:** Remove full autonomous execution of towing. The bot must transition from "Dispatch" to "Alert."
    *   **Workflow:** The system identifies a potential violator → Generates an alert with the image → A human operator verifies the vehicle is *not* an ambulance → Human explicitly authorizes the tow truck dispatch.
    *   **Rationale:** Given the camera angle constraints, the AI cannot achieve the necessary certainty for irreversible actions. A human can interpret context (e.g., flashing lights, stretcher movement) that the constrained cameras miss.

*   **Control B: Defined Uncertainty & Override**
    *   **Action:** Implement a "Low Confidence/Emergency Potential" tier.
    *   **Logic:** If the model's confidence that a vehicle is *not* an ambulance falls below a strict threshold (or if it detects ambiguous shapes/colors associated with emergency services), the system must default to "No Action" and flag for manual review.
    *   **Display:** Operators see a clear status: "Unverified – Manual Check Required" rather than a misleading percentage score.

*   **Control C: Exclusion List (Deterministic Rule)**
    *   **Action:** Integrate a deterministic filter based on known ambulance license plate prefixes or transponder data if available, bypassing the visual model entirely for these IDs.

**4. Governance & Transparency**
*   **Owner:** Assign a specific individual (e.g., Pilot Lead) responsible for reviewing every false positive during the pilot.
*   **Artifact:** Create a **Model Card** for this feature documenting:
    *   *Intended Use:* Identifying non-emergency violators only.
    *   *Limitations:* Explicitly state the system cannot reliably distinguish ambulances due to fixed camera angles.
    *   *Data Gaps:* Note the lack of diverse ambulance angle data in training.
*   **Legal Routing:** Consult counsel regarding regulations on automated enforcement affecting emergency services and required disclosures to the public about AI involvement in towing decisions. Do not assert compliance internally without this review.

**5. Implementation Timeline (Aligned with Flu Surge)**
*   **Week 1:** Run adversarial probes (ambulance images). Implement HITL gate in code.
*   **Week 2:** Pilot the "Alert-only" mode with human operators. Measure false alerts.
*   **Week 3:** Refine thresholds. Only consider limited automation if FPR remains zero over 1,000+ mixed cases.
*   **Launch:** Go live with HITL mandatory. Full automation is prohibited until camera constraints are resolved or technology advances.

**Note:** This strategy prioritizes patient safety over enforcement efficiency; delaying full automation is the cost of meeting the winter deadline safely.
**Note:** Legal counsel must verify if "Alert-only" modes still constitute regulated automated decision-making under local laws.
