### Pre-Launch Safety Intervention Plan for ParisBake

**Objective:** Prevent toxic substance orders while maintaining supply chain velocity during the 5-week pilot, given the constraint that the translation engine cannot be replaced.

**1. Immediate Harm Inventory & Triage (Days 1–2)**
*   **Identify High-Risk Targets:** Compile a static "Blocklist" of known toxic industrial chemicals often confused with food ingredients (e.g., specific cleaning agents, preservatives banned in food, non-food grade additives). This list must be hardcoded, not reliant on the AI.
*   **Define the Gate:** Any order containing an ingredient on the Blocklist, or any order where the AI's confidence in the chemical name translation is below a strict threshold, triggers a mandatory human review.

**2. The Human-in-the-Loop (HITL) Workflow (Staged Rollout)**
*   **Tier 1: Automated Pass (Low Risk):** Orders containing only standard bakery staples (flour, sugar, yeast, common flavorings) bypass human review and proceed automatically. This preserves speed for 90%+ of volume.
*   **Tier 2: Mandatory Human Gate (High Risk):** Orders triggering the Blocklist or low-confidence flags are paused.
    *   **Action:** A designated staff member (rotating duty among the 8 team members) receives an alert.
    *   **Verification Task:** The human must manually verify the chemical name against a trusted food-safety database or contact the supplier directly before approving the purchase.
    *   **Override Protocol:** No high-risk order ships without explicit human confirmation. The system defaults to "blocked" if no action is taken within 4 hours.

**3. Operational Constraints & Budget Adherence**
*   **No New Tools:** Use existing communication channels (email/Slack) for alerts. Do not procure new software.
*   **Labor Allocation:** With 8 staff, assign one "Safety Officer" per day on a rotating basis. Their primary role remains normal duties, but they are responsible for clearing the HITL queue within the 4-hour window.
*   **Kill Switch:** If the queue exceeds 20 pending items or if a toxic slip-through occurs, the automated purchasing feature pauses entirely until manual review of all pending orders is complete.

**4. Transparency & Disclosure**
*   **Internal Logging:** Record every blocked order, the reason (Blocklist hit vs. low confidence), and the human verifier's name. This creates an audit trail for the pilot.
*   **Supplier Notification:** Inform key suppliers that "new verification protocols" may cause slight delays on unusual chemical requests, framing it as a quality assurance upgrade.
*   **User Disclosure:** While legal counsel must determine final obligations, proactively note in internal pilot documentation that purchasing decisions are "AI-assisted with human verification for non-standard ingredients."

**5. Measurement & Stop Conditions**
*   **Metric:** Zero toxic substances ordered.
*   **Stop Condition:** If *any* toxic substance is ordered and received during the pilot, the automated purchasing feature is immediately disabled for all categories until the root cause is fixed, regardless of the holiday rush timeline.
*   **Review Cadence:** Weekly 30-minute standup to review blocked items and refine the Blocklist based on near-misses.

**Residual Risk Statement:**
This plan reduces but does not eliminate risk. Translation errors on items *not* on the initial Blocklist remain possible. The "UNVERIFIED" status applies to the AI's overall accuracy rate; we are measuring success solely by the absence of toxic incidents and the efficacy of the human gate.

**Note:** Legal counsel must review the "toxic substance" definition and any regulatory disclosure requirements for automated food-supply decisions before launch.
**Note:** The Blocklist must be updated immediately if new confusing chemical names are identified during the weekly reviews.
