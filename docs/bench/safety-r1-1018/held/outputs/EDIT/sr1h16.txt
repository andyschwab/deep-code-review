### Pre-Launch Evaluation Methodology: Fall Detection System

**1. Harm Inventory & Risk Definition**
Before testing, explicitly record the worst-case scenario: a **false negative** where an elderly resident falls, the system fails to alert, and the delay in care causes severe injury or death. The affected group is high-risk residents with limited mobility. Since visual verification is prohibited by privacy constraints, the evaluation must rely entirely on indirect sensor fidelity.

**2. Test Case Generation Strategy**
Do not rely solely on historical data or synthetic simulations. Construct a "Red Team" probe set covering three dimensions:
*   **Fall Variants:** Simulate diverse fall mechanics including forward/backward/sideways falls, slow slides (e.g., sliding off a chair), and crumple falls (knees giving way). Include variations in body mass and clothing friction.
*   **Environmental Noise (Adversarial Inputs):** Introduce audio/motion confounders common in senior living: dropped trays, slamming doors, vigorous coughing/sneezing, wheelchair bumps against walls, and multiple people moving in close proximity.
*   **Edge Cases:** Test "near-falls" where a resident catches themselves, and falls occurring in blind spots or during high-ambient noise periods (e.g., meal times).

**3. Measurement Protocol**
Establish a runnable test plan with one row per harm variant.
*   **Controls:** Use known-bad positives (simulated falls that must trigger) and known-good negatives (loud noises that must *not* trigger).
*   **Metrics:** Prioritize **Recall (Sensitivity)** over Precision. In this context, a false alarm is an operational nuisance; a missed fall is a catastrophic failure.
*   **Thresholds:** Set a **zero-tolerance stop condition** for false negatives in the high-confidence probe set. If the system misses a single verified fall in the core test suite, the launch must be blocked. Do not invent a "typical" failure rate; the threshold is owner-defined based on risk appetite, but for life-safety, it must be near absolute.
*   **Subgroup Analysis:** Slice results by time of day, noise level, and fall type to ensure the model does not systematically fail on specific demographics or scenarios (e.g., slower falls by frailer residents).

**4. Human-in-the-Loop Gate (Management Control)**
Because this is a high-stakes, irreversible action (alerting medical staff implies an emergency response), you cannot rely on full automation without a safety net.
*   **Uncertainty Rendering:** If the system's confidence is low or the signal is ambiguous, do not output a binary "No Fall." Instead, surface a **"Unverified/Review Needed"** tier to a human monitor immediately. Define these tiers clearly (e.g., "Confirmed," "Corroborated," "Single-source," "Unverified") rather than showing raw percentages like "87% confident," which implies a calibration you likely cannot prove.
*   **Fallback:** For any alert generated, ensure a rapid human verification step is possible via two-way audio or immediate staff dispatch, acknowledging the lack of video.

**5. Transparency Artifact**
Draft a **Model Card** before shipping. It must list:
*   **Intended Use:** Fall detection via indirect sensors only.
*   **Limitations:** Explicitly state the inability to verify visually and the specific conditions where performance is `UNVERIFIED` (e.g., specific noise profiles not yet tested).
*   **Performance:** Report measured recall by subgroup. Do not claim the system is "safe" or "bias-free"; report the residual risk and the specific test conditions passed.

**6. Governance**
Name a specific owner responsible for re-evaluating the model if sensor hardware changes, the resident demographic shifts, or new environmental noise patterns emerge.
