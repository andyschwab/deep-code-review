### Safety Review Checklist: BerlinGlow Smart Street Light System

Given the fixed firmware lock and the risk of sensor false positives causing dangerous glare, the following failure modes must be tested and mitigated before deployment. Since updates are impossible for six months, the "human-in-the-loop" and hardware-level constraints are your primary safety gates.

**1. Environmental False Positive Stress Testing**
*   **Failure Mode:** Heavy rain, fog, snow, or swaying vegetation triggers the motion sensor, causing lights to snap to maximum brightness.
*   **Test Requirement:** Simulate extreme weather conditions (heavy downpour, dense fog) in a controlled environment with *no* human movement.
*   **Pass Condition:** The system must distinguish between weather noise and actual motion. If distinction is impossible via firmware, the brightness ramp-up rate must be mechanically or logically capped to prevent instant blinding glare.

**2. Ramp-Rate and Ceiling Constraints (The "Blindness" Gate)**
*   **Failure Mode:** A sensor spike causes an immediate jump to 100% brightness, temporarily blinding drivers.
*   **Test Requirement:** Inject maximum sensor noise signals to verify the output curve.
*   **Pass Condition:** Even on a "positive" trigger, brightness must ramp up over several seconds (e.g., 3–5 seconds) rather than instantly. A hard ceiling (e.g., 80% max) should be enforced during adverse weather modes if the system can detect them, preventing full-intensity output when visibility is already compromised.

**3. Fail-Safe Default State**
*   **Failure Mode:** Sensor malfunction or logic error causes lights to stay at max brightness indefinitely or flicker erratically.
*   **Test Requirement:** Disconnect sensors or feed them garbage data.
*   **Pass Condition:** The system must revert to a safe, static baseline brightness (e.g., 40% steady) rather than oscillating or staying at maximum. The "safe" state must be defined as "visible but not blinding."

**4. Multi-Sensor Correlation (If applicable)**
*   **Failure Mode:** A single faulty sensor triggers a whole block of lights.
*   **Test Requirement:** Trigger a single sensor in isolation.
*   **Pass Condition:** Verify if the system requires correlation from adjacent sensors before engaging maximum brightness. If the architecture allows only single-sensor triggers, the ramp-rate constraint (Item 2) becomes critical.

**5. Irreversible Action Governance**
*   **Failure Mode:** The system operates autonomously with no override during the 6-month lock.
*   **Mitigation Check:** Since you cannot update the firmware, you must establish a **manual physical override** or a remote master dimmer switch accessible by city operators. If a widespread false-positive event occurs (e.g., a storm blinds drivers across a district), operators must be able to force all units to a safe baseline immediately. This is your required human-in-the-loop gate for high-stakes automation.

**6. Transparency and User Expectation**
*   **Failure Mode:** Drivers interpret sudden brightness changes as a signal to stop or accelerate, causing confusion.
*   **Mitigation Check:** Ensure the lighting pattern is consistent and predictable. Document the specific weather conditions that may cause reduced sensitivity or altered behavior in the deployment manual for city operators, so they can warn traffic management centers.

**Critical Note:** Do not certify the system as "safe from false positives." Instead, document the residual risk: "In heavy rain, false triggers may occur; safety relies on the implemented ramp-rate limiter and manual override capability." If the ramp-rate cannot be guaranteed in the current firmware, deployment poses an unacceptable risk of causing accidents.

*The inability to patch firmware for six months elevates the "ramp-rate limiter" and "manual override" from features to mandatory safety controls.*
