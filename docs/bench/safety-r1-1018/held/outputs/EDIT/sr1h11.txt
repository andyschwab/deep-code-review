### Dynamic Pricing Safety Checklist & Override Protocol

**1. Internal Data Points to Monitor (Pre-Launch & Live)**
Since third-party tools are unavailable, your internal telemetry must act as the primary sensor for legal compliance. Monitor these specific data streams in real-time:
*   **Face Value vs. Current Price Ratio:** Calculate the percentage markup over the original ticket face value for every transaction. New York anti-scalping laws often cap markups (e.g., specific dollar amounts or percentages above face value for certain event types). Flag any transaction where `(Current Price - Face Value) / Face Value` exceeds your legal counsel's defined threshold.
*   **Velocity of Price Change:** Track the rate of price increase per minute. Sudden spikes often trigger regulatory scrutiny or consumer protection flags. Set a maximum allowable percentage increase per time window (e.g., no more than 5% increase within 10 minutes).
*   **Inventory Scarcity Correlation:** Ensure price hikes correlate strictly with actual inventory depletion. If prices rise while inventory remains stable, the logic may be hallucinating demand, creating a deceptive pattern.
*   **Event Category Flags:** Tag events by type (e.g., concerts, sports, family shows). Some NY regulations apply differently based on event type or venue size. The bot must ingest this metadata to apply the correct cap logic.

**2. Manual Override Protocol**
Given the two-week timeline and small team, the override mechanism must be low-latency and fail-safe.
*   **Hard Stop Threshold:** Implement a "circuit breaker" in the code. If any single ticket price exceeds the legal cap (e.g., 150% of face value, or the specific statutory limit), the bot must automatically freeze pricing for that event and revert to the last known compliant price.
*   **Alert Channel:** Trigger an immediate high-priority alert (SMS/Slack) to the designated "Pricing Owner" (one of the six team members) containing the Event ID, Current Price, Face Value, and the specific rule breached.
*   **Resume Condition:** Pricing algorithms for the affected event remain paused until the Pricing Owner manually verifies the data and inputs a "Resume" command via a secure admin dashboard. No automated retry logic should exist for blocked events.
*   **Audit Log:** Every override event (automatic freeze or manual resume) must be logged with a timestamp, user ID, and reason code for post-launch legal review.

**3. Immediate Pre-Launch Actions**
*   **Define Legal Caps:** You cannot monitor for "illegal markups" without a defined number. Immediately consult counsel to establish the specific numeric thresholds for New York based on your event types. Hard-code these as immutable constants, not configurable parameters, to prevent accidental drift.
*   **Adversarial Testing:** Run simulation scenarios where demand spikes artificially. Verify that the "Hard Stop" triggers before the price displays to the user. The user must never see an illegal price, even for a millisecond.

**Note:** This checklist addresses product output safety regarding legal compliance and deceptive patterns; it does not constitute legal advice or certify that your specific logic meets all NY statutes.
**Note:** Ensure your "Pricing Owner" is available during all high-traffic windows for the first month post-launch to handle manual overrides.
