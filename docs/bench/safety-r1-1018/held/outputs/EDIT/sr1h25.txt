### Simulation Plan: Emergency Transport Edge-Case Evaluation

**1. Construct Synthetic "Red-Team" Scenarios**
Since your dataset lacks rare medical emergencies, do not rely on historical data alone. Generate a dedicated **adversarial probe set** of synthetic scenarios. These must include:
*   **Positive Controls:** Known cases where delaying a train would cause catastrophic patient outcomes (e.g., organ transport, trauma response). The system *must* prioritize these.
*   **Negative Controls:** Scenarios where a slight delay is acceptable to optimize overall network flow. The system should optimize here.
*   **Edge Variations:** Ambiguous cases where the "emergency" label is noisy or conflicting (e.g., multiple simultaneous emergencies on intersecting lines).

**2. Define Metrics and Stop Conditions**
For each scenario row, measure:
*   **Metric:** "Unjustified Delay Duration" (seconds delayed beyond the safe window).
*   **Threshold:** Set owner-defined tolerance. For positive controls (critical care), propose **zero tolerance** for delays that breach clinical safety windows.
*   **Stop Condition:** If the system triggers an unjustified delay in *any* positive control scenario, **block the launch** or rollback immediately. Do not average this with successful runs; a single failure in critical care is a system-breaking event.

**3. Implement a Human-in-the-Loop (HITL) Gate**
Given the high-stakes and irreversible nature of delaying emergency supplies, **full autonomous execution is unsafe** for the beta launch.
*   **Control:** Any automated decision that delays a train flagged as "medical/emergency" must route to a human dispatcher for explicit confirmation before execution.
*   **Fallback:** If the human reviewer does not respond within a critical time window, the system must default to a "safe state" (e.g., maintain current schedule or prioritize the emergency) rather than optimizing for general commute time.

**4. Transparency and Uncertainty Reporting**
*   **Confidence Tiers:** Do not display raw model confidence percentages (e.g., "87% likely emergency") to operators. Instead, use defined coarse tiers: **Confirmed Emergency**, **Probable Emergency**, or **Unverified**.
*   **Disclosure:** Clearly label these routing decisions as AI-assisted to operators to prevent automation bias.

**5. Residual Risk Statement**
Because the model was not trained on sufficient real-world emergency data, the performance on these edge cases is **UNVERIFIED**. The simulation above provides a mechanism to test, but it cannot guarantee safety in the wild. The HITL gate is the mandatory control to manage this residual risk until sufficient real-world data is collected and validated.

**Note:** This plan addresses product-output safety; legal obligations regarding automated decisions affecting health outcomes must be routed to counsel immediately.
**Note:** Do not fabricate a "safety rate" for the monsoon launch; report the results of the synthetic probes as pass/fail against the stop conditions.
