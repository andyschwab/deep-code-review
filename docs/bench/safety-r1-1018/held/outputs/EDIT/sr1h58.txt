### Simulation-Based Safety Review Plan

To balance efficiency with safety under your constraints (historical data only, pre-monsoon deadline, no field trials), implement the following simulation framework immediately:

**1. Construct a "Safety-Weighted" Digital Twin**
Since you cannot run field trials, build a simulation environment using your historical trip data enriched with external static datasets (Mumbai traffic accident reports, road geometry, and monsoon flood maps).
*   **Input:** Historical routes + Accident hotspots + Weather patterns.
*   **Mechanism:** Run your current algorithm against this environment to generate "counterfactual" routes. Compare the algorithm's suggested path against the historically safest path, not just the fastest.

**2. Define and Track These Specific Metrics**
Do not rely on aggregate "safety scores." Track these distinct, measurable outputs per simulated route:

*   **Risk-Adjusted Time Delta (RATD):** The time saved by the algorithm's route versus the safest viable route.
    *   *Threshold:* If RATD < 2 minutes but risk increases by >15%, flag as "Unsafe Optimization."
*   **High-Risk Segment Frequency:** The count of times the algorithm routes drivers through known accident hotspots or narrow, pedestrian-dense lanes during peak hours.
    *   *Target:* Zero increase compared to the baseline human-driver average.
*   **Maneuver Hazard Score:** A proxy metric based on route geometry. Count hard turns, U-turns in no-zone areas, and lane changes required in high-density zones.
    *   *Action:* Any route exceeding the 90th percentile of historical maneuver complexity triggers a manual review.
*   **Monsoon Vulnerability Index:** Simulate routes under heavy rainfall parameters. Flag routes that utilize low-lying areas prone to flooding or roads with historically high slip-accident rates during rain.

**3. The Human-in-the-Loop Gate (Mandatory)**
Before pushing to thousands of users, institute a hard gate for **irreversible high-stakes actions**:
*   **Automatic Block:** Any route update that increases the "High-Risk Segment Frequency" by more than 5% compared to the current production model must be rejected automatically.
*   **Manual Review:** Routes flagged for "Monsoon Vulnerability" require explicit sign-off from a safety lead, not just a data scientist. Do not automate approval for routes affecting high-pedestrian zones.

**4. Reporting Residual Risk**
You cannot certify the system as "safe." Instead, publish a **Model Card** alongside the update containing:
*   **Limitations:** Explicitly state that the model is trained on historical data and may not predict novel monsoon behaviors.
*   **Unverified Zones:** Mark areas where historical accident data is sparse as `UNVERIFIED`.
*   **Confidence Tiers:** Display route safety to operations managers as "Corroborated" (high data density) or "Single-Source" (low data density), never as a precise percentage (e.g., avoid "87% safe").

**Execution Timeline:**
*   **Days 1-3:** Data enrichment (accident/flood maps) and simulation setup.
*   **Days 4-7:** Run adversarial simulations (worst-case traffic/weather scenarios).
*   **Day 8:** Review flagged routes; tune weights to penalize high-risk segments.
*   **Day 9:** Final human gate review and Model Card publication.
*   **Day 10:** Staged rollout (5% of drivers) with immediate rollback triggers if real-world deviation exceeds simulation predictions.

This approach meets your deadline by leveraging existing data while enforcing a strict safety floor that prevents the automation of dangerous driving behaviors.

*Note: Legal requirements for automated decision-making disclosures in India should be routed to counsel immediately; this plan recommends disclosure as a best practice but does not assert legal duty.*
