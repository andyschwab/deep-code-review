### Contingency Plan: Monsoon Flood Prevention for Mumbai WaterWise

**Immediate Logic Override (The "Safe-Fail" Gate)**
Since real-time sensors are unavailable and predictive models are unreliable, you must shift the system's default state from "collect unless predicted full" to **"do not collect unless verified safe."** Implement the following deterministic rules immediately in your deployment logic:

1.  **Hard Cap on Intake Duration:** Regardless of rainfall prediction, hard-code a maximum cumulative intake window per storm event (e.g., 45 minutes) based on the known volume of your smallest tank relative to maximum historical rainfall intensity in Mumbai. Once this window closes, valves remain shut until a manual reset or a prolonged dry period (e.g., 6 hours of no predicted rain).
2.  **Prediction Confidence Thresholding:** Do not act on a single model output. Require consensus from at least two distinct weather data sources predicting *below* critical thresholds. If models disagree or confidence is low, the system defaults to **valves closed**.
3.  **Mandatory Human-in-the-Loop for High-Risk Events:** If the predictive model forecasts heavy rainfall (>50mm/hr), the system must **pause automation** and send an alert to a designated building manager for explicit confirmation before opening any valves. No autonomous action is permitted under high-risk forecasts.

**Operational Contingencies (Non-Code Controls)**
1.  **Manual Override Protocol:** Install physical, clearly labeled emergency shut-off switches accessible to security staff or residents. Distribute a one-page "Flood Emergency" card to building management detailing exactly when to cut power to the valve system.
2.  **Staged Rollout with Exposure Caps:** Do not deploy to all complexes simultaneously. Start with one building that has the highest basement flood risk. Monitor this single node manually during the next rain event. If the logic fails, the blast radius is contained to one location.
3.  **Transparency Artifact:** Issue a "System Limitation Notice" to all participating apartment complexes. Explicitly state: *"This system operates on predictive models only, lacks real-time tank level verification, and may fail to prevent flooding during extreme weather. Manual monitoring is required during heavy monsoons."*

**Residual Risk Statement**
Because you lack real-time feedback (water level sensors), **zero risk of flooding cannot be guaranteed.** The current architecture relies on open-loop control, which is inherently unsafe for irreversible actions like filling tanks to capacity. The measures above reduce the probability of failure but do not eliminate it. The only way to certify safety is to install low-cost ultrasonic sensors or float switches; until then, the system must operate under the assumption that predictions will be wrong.

***

**Note:** This plan prioritizes preventing basement flooding over maximizing water collection; you will harvest less rainwater, but you will avoid property damage.
