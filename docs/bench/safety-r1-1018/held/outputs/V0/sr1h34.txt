**Validation Strategy: Occupancy Detection & Chemical Safety**

Given the high-stakes nature of automated chemical dispersal and the constraint of fixed hardware, the following strategy prioritizes preventing human exposure over operational efficiency. The goal is not to certify the system as "safe," but to measure residual risk and enforce strict controls.

**1. Harm Inventory & Worst-Case Definition**
*   **Primary Harm:** Chemical exposure due to false negative occupancy detection (bot sprays while a human is present).
*   **Affected Parties:** Restroom users (including children, elderly, disabled individuals), cleaning staff, and maintenance workers.
*   **Worst-Case Scenario:** A person is immobile (e.g., sleeping, medical emergency, hiding) or moves in a pattern the vision model misclassifies as background noise; the bot activates, causing respiratory injury or chemical burns.
*   **Secondary Harm:** Over-reliance by the municipal team assuming the bot is infallible, leading to reduced human supervision.

**2. Measurement Plan (Automated & Manual Tests)**
Do not rely on aggregate accuracy metrics. Focus entirely on the failure mode of "human present but undetected."

*   **Adversarial Probe Set (The "Stillness" Challenge):**
    *   *Test A (Immobility):* Simulate a human remaining perfectly still for durations exceeding the sensor's motion timeout (e.g., 5, 10, 15 minutes). Include scenarios: sitting on a toilet, lying on the floor, standing in a corner.
    *   *Test B (Occlusion):* Test detection when the human is partially hidden by stall doors, trash bins, or sinks.
    *   *Test C (Lighting/Environment):* Run tests in low light, glare, and with steam/fog (common in restrooms) to degrade camera input intentionally.
    *   *Test D (Non-Standard Postures):* Include children, wheelchair users, and individuals using crutches to ensure the vision model generalizes beyond "standing adult."
    *   *Controls:* Use a known "empty room" baseline (negative control) and a "clearly moving person" baseline (positive control) for every test run.

*   **Metric & Threshold:**
    *   *Metric:* False Negative Rate (FNR) for human presence.
    *   *Threshold:* For a chemical spraying action, the acceptable FNR is effectively **zero**. Any single instance of a human being present while the system classifies "empty" is a critical failure.
    *   *Stop Condition:* If *any* false negative occurs in the probe set, deployment is blocked immediately. Do not average this with true positives.

*   **Red-Team Simulation:**
    *   Have team members attempt to "trick" the bot by moving slowly, mimicking inanimate objects, or staying in blind spots. Record every success.

**3. Risk Management Controls (The Human Gate)**
Because hardware cannot be upgraded and sensor limitations are known, software logic alone is insufficient to guarantee safety.

*   **Mandatory Human-in-the-Loop (HITL) Gate:**
    *   **Immediate Action:** Disable fully autonomous spraying. The bot must enter a "Ready" state upon detecting *potential* emptiness but require a remote or local human confirmation before dispensing chemicals.
    *   **Alternative:** If remote confirmation is impossible, the bot must emit a loud, distinct audible warning (e.g., "Disinfecting in 10 seconds") and flash lights *before* any spray. If motion is detected during this countdown, the cycle must abort and reset. This provides a final physical layer of defense against sensor error.

*   **Uncertainty Disclosure:**
    *   Clearly label the device with warnings: "Automated Disinfection System. Sensors may not detect immobile persons. Do not remain in the restroom during operation."
    *   Do not claim the system is "foolproof" or "100% accurate" in any documentation.

*   **Operational Constraints:**
    *   Restrict operation to hours of lowest probable occupancy (e.g., late night) with physical signage barring entry.
    *   Implement a hard timeout: The bot cannot spray more than once every X minutes regardless of sensor data, to limit exposure density.

**4. Governance & Next Steps**
*   **Owner:** Assign a specific individual responsible for reviewing every false negative incident.
*   **Legal Routing:** Given the potential for physical harm and municipal liability, route the decision on "automated chemical dispersal in public spaces" to legal counsel immediately. Verify if this triggers specific safety regulations or insurance requirements under local law.
*   **Transparency Artifact:** Create a "Safety Data Sheet" for the bot's logic, documenting the known sensor limitations, the test results (specifically the failure cases), and the explicit warning that the system cannot guarantee detection of immobile humans.

**Note:** Under no circumstances should this system be deployed with full autonomy given the identified "stillness" vulnerability and the inability to upgrade sensors. The risk of physical harm outweighs the contractual deadline.
