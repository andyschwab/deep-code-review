### Safety Review Checklist: BerlinGlow Smart Street Light System

**1. Environmental False-Positive Stress Testing**
*   **Failure Mode:** Weather artifacts (heavy rain, fog, snow) interpreted as human/vehicle movement, triggering maximum brightness.
*   **Test Requirement:** Simulate extreme weather conditions in a controlled environment matching deployment zones. Measure the rate of "max brightness" triggers when no target is present.
*   **Acceptance Criteria:** Zero instances of full-intensity activation caused solely by weather. If false positives occur, the system must default to a "safe dim" state rather than "max bright."

**2. Gradient Ramp Verification (Anti-Blinding Control)**
*   **Failure Mode:** Instantaneous jump from low/off to 100% brightness, causing temporary blindness (glare) to drivers.
*   **Test Requirement:** Validate firmware logic enforces a minimum ramp-up time (e.g., 2–5 seconds) regardless of sensor input urgency.
*   **Acceptance Criteria:** Brightness increases linearly or in small steps; no instantaneous spikes to maximum lumen output.

**3. Sensor Fusion & Logic Gating**
*   **Failure Mode:** Single noisy sensor reading triggering a system-wide high-beam event.
*   **Test Requirement:** Inject noisy data streams mimicking sensor drift or electrical interference. Verify that a single outlier does not trigger max brightness without corroboration (e.g., sustained detection over X milliseconds).
*   **Acceptance Criteria:** System requires consecutive positive confirmations before escalating brightness beyond a safe baseline.

**4. Fail-Safe State Definition (The "Frozen Firmware" Constraint)**
*   **Failure Mode:** Unresolvable sensor error leads to undefined behavior (random flashing or stuck max brightness).
*   **Test Requirement:** Simulate sensor disconnection, power fluctuation, and data corruption. Since updates are locked for six months, the hardware must have a hard-coded physical or logical fallback.
*   **Acceptance Criteria:** In any error state, lights revert to a constant, moderate "safety glow" (not off, not max) until manual intervention.

**5. Cumulative Exposure & Over-Reliance**
*   **Failure Mode:** Frequent false alarms cause drivers to ignore the lights or experience fatigue, reducing reaction times when a real threat appears.
*   **Test Requirement:** Run extended duration tests (simulating weeks of operation) with intermittent false positives.
*   **Acceptance Criteria:** False positive rate remains below a threshold where driver habituation becomes a safety risk. If the rate exceeds this, the sensitivity must be manually lowered pre-deployment.

**6. Human-in-the-Loop for Calibration**
*   **Failure Mode:** Deployment team accepts "good enough" sensor tuning that fails under rare but severe weather combinations.
*   **Test Requirement:** A designated safety owner must sign off on the specific sensitivity settings used for the firmware lock, based on the worst-case weather data from the specific installation city.
*   **Acceptance Criteria:** Documented sign-off confirming settings were tuned against local historical weather extremes, not just lab standards.

**Note:** Because your firmware is locked for six months, you cannot patch issues post-installation; any failure mode identified above must be resolved or mitigated via hardware configuration before the lock engages. Do not certify the system as "safe"; instead, document the residual risk of weather-induced glare and ensure the ramp-up logic is physically incapable of blinding a driver instantly.
