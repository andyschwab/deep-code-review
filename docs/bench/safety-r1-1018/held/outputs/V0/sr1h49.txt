### Immediate Contingency Plan: "Fail-Safe" Logic for Rainwater Harvesting

Given the critical risk of basement flooding and the absence of real-time water level sensors, your current reliance on predictive rainfall models is insufficient. Predictive models are probabilistic, not deterministic, and cannot serve as the sole gatekeeper for irreversible physical actions like opening valves. To prevent flooding during the monsoon, you must implement a **human-in-the-loop gate** and a **conservative default state** immediately.

**1. Implement a "Closed-by-Default" State with Explicit Override**
Stop the system from automatically opening intake valves based solely on a rainfall prediction. The default state of the valves must be **CLOSED**. The system should only open valves if two conditions are met:
*   The predictive model indicates rain with high confidence.
*   A human operator (facility manager) explicitly confirms via a mobile alert that the storage tanks have verified capacity.
Without real-time sensors, you cannot algorithmically guarantee tank availability. Therefore, the decision to open the valve must be a conscious human act, not an automated inference. This shifts the liability from a flawed algorithm to a verified human observation.

**2. Establish a Manual Verification Protocol (The Human Gate)**
Since you lack sensors, you must substitute them with a procedural check. Before any predicted rain event:
*   The system sends an alert to the facility manager: "Rain predicted. Please verify tank levels manually."
*   The manager must physically check the tanks or use a simple manual dipstick measurement.
*   Only upon receiving a digital confirmation ("Tanks Verified < 80% Full") from the manager does the system authorize the valve to open.
If no confirmation is received within a set window (e.g., 30 minutes before predicted rain), the valves remain closed. It is safer to miss a rainwater collection opportunity than to flood a basement.

**3. Deploy Low-Cost Physical Fail-Safes (Immediate Hardware Patch)**
While software logic is being updated, you must address the physical vulnerability. Since you cannot deploy expensive IoT sensors in time:
*   Install simple, mechanical float switches or overflow alarms in the storage tanks and basement intake points. These do not need to be networked; they can trigger a local audible alarm or even a hard-wired circuit breaker that cuts power to the intake valves if water reaches a critical height.
*   This creates a hardware-level "circuit breaker" that operates independently of your AI model's accuracy.

**4. Transparency and Disclosure to Residents**
You must disclose the system's current limitation to the apartment complex management and residents. Issue a notice stating: "The automated harvesting system is currently operating in 'Manual Verify' mode due to monsoon risks. Automatic collection is disabled to prevent flooding." This manages expectations and ensures that if a failure occurs, it is not due to hidden automation bias where users trust the system to "know" the tank levels.

**5. Post-Monsoon Measurement Plan**
Once the immediate monsoon threat passes, you must build the missing measurement layer.
*   **Harm Inventory:** Record "Unsafe Automation" as a critical risk.
*   **Evaluation:** Do not claim the system is "safe" based on simulation. You must test the new manual protocol with dry runs.
*   **Data Card:** Document that the current system relies on human verification due to sensor gaps. Never publish a "flood prevention rate" without empirical data from the upcoming season; mark such metrics as **UNVERIFIED** until proven.

**Summary of Actions for Deployment:**
1.  **Disable** automatic valve opening based purely on AI prediction.
2.  **Require** human confirmation of tank levels before any valve opens.
3.  **Install** mechanical overflow switches as a hard stop.
4.  **Disclose** the manual operating mode to all stakeholders.

*Note: This plan prioritizes harm prevention over collection efficiency. Do not attempt to "tune" the model to be more accurate; the problem is the lack of ground truth (sensor data), which no amount of model tuning can solve.*
