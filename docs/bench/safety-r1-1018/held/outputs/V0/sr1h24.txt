### Pre-Launch Validation Strategy for FrostClear Dispatch System

Given the hard deadline and the specific limitation of your traffic simulation tool regarding pedestrian surges, you cannot rely solely on automated modeling to validate safety. Your validation strategy must shift from pure simulation to a hybrid approach combining adversarial data injection, strict human gating, and transparent uncertainty reporting.

**1. Construct Adversarial "Surge" Test Sets (Measure)**
Since your simulator cannot model sudden surges, you must manually create them as input data. Build a specific evaluation dataset that overrides normal traffic patterns with "worst-case" school-hour scenarios:
*   **Positive Controls:** Inject historical or synthetic data spikes representing 300% increases in pedestrian volume at 8:00 AM and 3:00 PM near schools. Verify the system *never* dispatches trucks to clear these zones during the spike.
*   **Negative Controls:** Use normal low-traffic data to ensure the system still operates efficiently when no surge is present.
*   **Metric:** Define a pass threshold of **zero** instances where a truck is dispatched into a zone flagged as "active surge." Any failure here is a stop condition for launch.

**2. Implement a Human-in-the-Loop Gate (Manage)**
Because this feature controls physical machinery affecting pedestrian safety (a high-stakes, irreversible action), full automation is unsafe given the known modeling gap.
*   **The Control:** Configure the system to enter a "Review Required" state whenever weather triggers a dispatch during school hours or when confidence in traffic data is low.
*   **The Action:** A human operator must explicitly confirm the route is clear of pedestrian surges before the truck moves. Do not attempt to automate this decision until the simulation gap is closed.

**3. Surface Uncertainty, Not False Precision**
Do not display a specific confidence percentage (e.g., "87% safe") to your operators, as your model cannot calibrate this against unmodeled surges.
*   **The Display:** Use defined coarse tiers for route status: **"Confirmed Clear,"** **"Corroborated,"** or **"Unverified – Manual Check Required."**
*   **The Logic:** If the input data falls outside normal parameters (e.g., sudden temperature drop coinciding with school start), force the status to "Unverified" automatically.

**4. Draft a Transparency Artifact (Model Card)**
Before launch, produce a one-page document for your team and city stakeholders listing:
*   **Known Limitation:** Explicitly state that the system has not been validated against sudden pedestrian surges via simulation.
*   **Mitigation:** Detail the human-in-the-loop gate and the adversarial test sets used.
*   **Owner:** Name the specific individual responsible for monitoring dispatch logs during the first month.

**5. Red-Team the Edge Cases**
Conduct a "pre-mortem" session with your team of ten. Ask: "It is next month, a blizzard hits, and a truck blocks a school crossing. How did our logic fail?" Use the answers to add specific rows to your test set. If the simulation tool cannot run these scenarios, run them as manual logic checks on the decision engine.

**Note:** Do not claim the system is "safe" or "unbiased"; report the residual risk that unmodeled surges remain a threat mitigated only by human review. If legal questions arise regarding liability for automated decisions affecting public safety, route those immediately to counsel.
