### Pre-Launch Evaluation Methodology: Fall Detection System

**1. Harm Inventory & Risk Definition**
Before testing, explicitly record the worst-case scenario: a resident falls, the system fails to trigger (false negative), and medical aid is delayed. Given the high-stakes, irreversible nature of this failure, **no fully autonomous "all-clear" decision should ever be made without a human-in-the-loop gate.** The system must default to alerting staff when confidence is low, rather than silently dismissing ambiguous events.

**2. Test Case Generation Strategy (The "Probe Set")**
Since video verification is prohibited, you must construct a "Golden Dataset" of non-visual ground truth.
*   **Controlled Physical Simulation:** Engage staff and safety-trained actors to perform staged falls on instrumented mats (pressure sensors) that provide independent, non-visual confirmation of impact time and location.
*   **Fall Taxonomy:** Generate cases covering distinct vectors: forward, backward, lateral, and vertical (slumping into a chair). Include "slow falls" where the resident slides down a wall, as these are often missed by acceleration-only models.
*   **Environmental Noise Injection:** Record and mix in facility-specific audio: dropped trays, shouting, TV volume spikes, wheelchair bumps, and cleaning equipment. Create "adversarial" mixes where a fall sound is masked by high-decibel noise.
*   **Subgroup Segmentation:** Ensure test cases account for residents with different mobility aids (walkers, canes) and varying body weights, as these alter impact signatures.

**3. Measurement Plan & Metrics**
Do not rely on aggregate accuracy. You must measure performance by subgroup and scenario type.
*   **Primary Metric:** False Negative Rate (FNR) per fall type.
*   **Thresholds:** Set a **zero-tolerance stop condition** for any fall type where the FNR exceeds a pre-defined, owner-set limit (e.g., if *any* slow-fall scenario is missed in 100 trials, the feature cannot launch).
*   **Confidence Reporting:** Do not display raw probability scores (e.g., "87% chance of fall") to staff. Instead, implement defined coarse tiers:
    *   **Confirmed:** High-confidence match to known fall signatures → Auto-alert.
    *   **Unverified:** Ambiguous signal (possible fall or loud noise) → Immediate human review required (page a nurse to check).
    *   **Normal:** Clear non-fall activity.
*   **Transparency Artifact:** Draft a **Model Card** documenting the intended use (fall detection only), the limitations (cannot detect silent medical events like heart attacks without movement), the data composition (simulated falls, not real patient data), and the measured FNR by fall type. Mark any untested scenarios as `UNVERIFIED`.

**4. Governance & Legal Routing**
*   **Human Gate:** Because this involves high-stakes health outcomes, the system must be designed so that ambiguous detections trigger a mandatory human check. Fully automated dismissal of potential falls is a critical failure mode.
*   **Regulatory Duty:** Whether this system classifies as a regulated medical device or triggers specific AI disclosure duties under local Toronto/Ontario law or broader frameworks (like the EU AI Act if you expand) is a legal determination. **Route this immediately to counsel.** Do not assert compliance internally; have legal name the specific regime and required disclosures.

**5. Execution**
Run the probe set against the current model. If the system fails to alert on a single known fall in the "slow fall" or "masked noise" categories, halt the launch. Refine the model or adjust the sensitivity thresholds to prioritize catching falls over reducing false alarms, as the cost of a missed fall outweighs the cost of a staff check.

***

*Note: This plan assumes simulated data is permissible under your privacy constraints; verify with legal that staged actor data does not require the same consent as resident data. Never fabricate a "0% error rate" claim; report residual risk honestly.*
