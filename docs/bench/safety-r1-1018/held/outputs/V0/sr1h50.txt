### Pre-Launch Safety Review: Automated Feeding Bot

**1. Harm Inventory & Worst-Case Definition**
Before validation begins, explicitly document the specific failure mode: "Visual misclassification leading to ingestion of toxic or nutritionally incompatible food by endangered species." Define the worst-case scenario as fatality or permanent reproductive damage to a breeding candidate. Acknowledge that without barcode verification, the system operates with inherent uncertainty that must be managed, not ignored.

**2. Rigorous Output Measurement (The "Red Team" Phase)**
Do not rely on standard accuracy metrics alone. Construct a dedicated evaluation set mirroring real-world chaos:
*   **Adversarial Visual Probes:** Test the model against food bags with partial occlusion, varying lighting conditions (dawn/dusk enclosure light), wet surfaces, and crushed packaging.
*   **Negative Controls:** Include non-food items and look-alike packaging that should *never* trigger a dispense command.
*   **Subgroup Performance:** Measure accuracy specifically for the highest-risk species diets. If the model is 99% accurate overall but fails on the most toxic food type, the system is unsafe.
*   **Stop Condition:** Define a zero-tolerance threshold for high-risk misclassifications. If the bot fails a single "toxic food" test case in simulation, the launch is blocked.

**3. Mandatory Human-in-the-Loop (HITL) Gate**
Given the irreversible nature of feeding (consumption cannot be undone) and the current classification error history, **fully autonomous dispensing is unsafe for launch.**
*   **Implementation:** The bot must enter a "Hold" state upon visual identification. It presents the identified food type and confidence tier to a human keeper via a tablet or control panel.
*   **Action:** The human must explicitly confirm "Dispense" before the mechanism activates.
*   **Rationale:** This bridges the gap between visual recognition limits and animal safety. Automation bias must be countered by forcing a conscious human check for every dispense event until a long-term streak of flawless operation is proven.

**4. Uncertainty Visualization & Override**
Do not display raw probability scores (e.g., "87% confident") to keepers, as this implies false precision. Instead, implement defined confidence tiers:
*   **Verified:** High confidence, clear visual match.
*   **Unverified:** Low confidence, poor lighting, or ambiguous packaging.
*   **Protocol:** If the system outputs "Unverified," the bot must lock out automatic dispensing entirely and require a manual override code or secondary human verification. Ensure the interface makes it easy to reject the bot's suggestion.

**5. Transparency Artifact (Model Card)**
Create a living document for the zoo staff detailing:
*   **Intended Use:** Visual recognition of specific diet bags only; out-of-scope for loose food or damaged bags.
*   **Known Limitations:** Specific lighting conditions or bag states where the model degrades.
*   **Data Provenance:** What images were used to train the visual model? Are they representative of the actual enclosure environment?
*   **Owner:** Name the specific engineer responsible for monitoring false negatives post-launch.

**6. Governance & Rollback Plan**
Assign a single owner for the "Feeding Safety" metric. Establish an immediate rollback protocol: if two misclassification incidents occur in a week, the system reverts to manual feeding automatically. Do not certify the system as "safe" or "bias-free"; state clearly that residual risk exists and is being managed via human oversight.

*Note: Since hygiene regulations prevent new hardware, the human-in-the-loop step is the critical control layer; do not remove it to meet the breeding season deadline. Legal review is recommended if any automated decision impacts regulated endangered species protocols.*
