### Simulation-Based Safety Review Plan for Mumbai Food Delivery Algorithm

**1. Harm Inventory & Scope**
The primary risk is **unsafe automation of high-stakes actions**: the algorithm may systematically prioritize speed over safety, encouraging dangerous driving behaviors (speeding, ignoring traffic signals, risky maneuvers) in Mumbai's crowded streets, especially during monsoon conditions. This affects drivers, pedestrians, and other road users. The worst-case scenario involves a significant increase in traffic accidents, injuries, or fatalities directly linked to algorithmic routing suggestions.

**2. Simulation Design (Given Constraints)**
Since new field trials are prohibited, you must construct a **digital twin** of Mumbai's traffic environment using your historical trip data.
*   **Data Enrichment:** Augment historical trip logs with publicly available Mumbai traffic accident reports, monsoon rainfall data, and road topology maps.
*   **Risk Modeling:** Create a "Safety Cost Function" that runs parallel to your existing "Speed Cost Function." This function should assign high penalty scores to routes with historical accident clusters, narrow lanes, school zones, or areas prone to monsoon flooding.
*   **Adversarial Testing:** Run the new algorithm against extreme scenarios (e.g., heavy monsoon downpours, peak festival traffic) to see if it suggests physically impossible or highly dangerous maneuvers to meet delivery times.

**3. Metrics to Track (Measure)**
Do not assert safety; measure residual risk. Track these specific metrics in your simulation:
*   **Safety-Efficiency Trade-off Curve:** Plot delivery time (efficiency) against the aggregated "Safety Cost" of the route. If the curve shows that marginal time gains require disproportionate spikes in risk, the algorithm is failing.
*   **High-Risk Route Frequency:** The percentage of suggested routes that pass through known high-accident zones or violate simulated safety constraints (e.g., suggesting U-turns in no-U-turn zones).
*   **Monsoon Degradation Index:** Compare route safety scores in dry vs. simulated wet conditions. A safe algorithm should automatically reroute away from flood-prone areas even if it increases delivery time.
*   **False Negative Rate:** The frequency with which the algorithm fails to flag a route that historical data identifies as high-risk.

**4. Management & Governance (Manage)**
*   **Human-in-the-Loop Gate:** Before full deployment, implement a hard gate where any route suggestion exceeding a specific "Safety Cost" threshold requires manual review or is automatically blocked, regardless of efficiency gains. **Do not automate high-risk routing decisions.**
*   **Uncertainty Disclosure:** If the simulation confidence in a route's safety is low (e.g., due to lack of historical data for a new road), the system must display a "Caution" tier to the driver rather than a precise confidence percentage.
*   **Transparency Artifact:** Create a **Model Card** for this update. It must document:
    *   **Intended Use:** Routing for food delivery in Mumbai.
    *   **Limitations:** Reliance on historical data only; potential blind spots in unrecorded accident zones.
    *   **Measured Performance:** Safety metrics broken down by zone (e.g., South Mumbai vs. Suburbs) and weather condition.
    *   **Owner:** Name the specific lead data scientist responsible for the safety model.

**5. Critical Boundaries**
*   **No Certification:** This process will not certify the algorithm as "safe" or "unbiased." It will only provide a residual risk report. The decision to ship rests with your leadership.
*   **Legal Routing:** Whether these safety optimizations meet local Mumbai traffic regulations or labor laws regarding driver safety is a legal determination. Route this question to legal counsel; do not assume compliance based on simulation results.
*   **No Fabricated Metrics:** If a specific risk metric cannot be measured due to data gaps, label it **UNVERIFIED** in your report. Do not invent a "0% accident risk" figure.

**Note:** Given the monsoon deadline, prioritize the "High-Risk Route Frequency" metric first, as it offers the clearest signal of immediate danger. Ensure your simulation explicitly models the reduced friction and visibility of monsoon rains, as dry-season data will underestimate risk.
