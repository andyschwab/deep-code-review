### Internal Safety Evaluation Framework for Mental Health Chatbot

**1. Harm Inventory & Critical Failure Modes**
Before testing, explicitly document these high-risk failure modes specific to mental health:
*   **Unsafe Automation of High-Stakes Actions:** The bot fails to recognize suicidal ideation or self-harm intent and provides coping strategies instead of immediate crisis resources (e.g., 988 Lifeline, local Toronto emergency services).
*   **Hallucination Surfaced as Fact:** The bot invents medical advice, drug interactions, or therapeutic techniques that are clinically invalid or dangerous.
*   **Over-Reliance/Automation Bias:** The interface design implies the bot is a licensed therapist, discouraging users from seeking human professional help.
*   **Missing AI Disclosure:** Users are unaware they are interacting with an automated system during a vulnerable state.

**2. Internal Testing Framework (Pre-Launch)**
Execute these steps using your existing team (developers + clinicians) without external auditors:

*   **Adversarial Prompt Construction (Red Teaming):**
    *   **Action:** Developers and clinicians co-create a "probe set" of 50–100 inputs.
    *   **Content:** Include direct statements of suicidal intent, subtle cries for help, requests for medication advice, and scenarios involving self-harm methods.
    *   **Controls:** Include "known-bad" inputs (explicit suicide plans) that *must* trigger a crisis referral, and "known-good" inputs (general anxiety) that should receive coping strategies.
*   **Clinician-in-the-Loop Review:**
    *   **Action:** Run the probe set against the current model.
    *   **Evaluation:** Your two clinicians must review every output for the high-risk probes. Do not rely on automated metrics.
    *   **Pass Threshold:** Zero tolerance for failure on high-stakes inputs. If the bot fails to provide crisis resources for *any* explicit suicidal input, the feature cannot launch.
*   **Uncertainty & Disclosure Check:**
    *   **Action:** Verify that every response includes a clear disclaimer that the bot is not a doctor and provides crisis hotline numbers prominently.
    *   **Confidence Rendering:** Do not display numerical confidence scores (e.g., "87% sure"). Instead, ensure the bot uses defined tiers: if uncertainty exists regarding safety, it must default to the crisis referral protocol.

**3. Deployment Monitoring Plan (First Week)**
*   **Human Gate for High-Risk Flags:** Implement a rule where any user input containing keywords related to suicide, self-harm, or violence triggers an immediate alert to a human clinician on-call before any automated response is finalized, or ensures the automated response is strictly limited to crisis resources.
*   **Real-Time Log Review:** Your clinicians must review 100% of conversations flagged by keyword filters daily. Do not sample; review all potential high-risk interactions.
*   **Stop Condition:** Define a clear rollback trigger. If *one* instance occurs where the bot provides harmful advice to a user expressing active intent to self-harm, the feature must be paused immediately for re-evaluation.

**4. Transparency Artifact (Model Card)**
Create a simple, versioned document accessible to your team and users containing:
*   **Intended Use:** Immediate coping support, *not* therapy or crisis intervention.
*   **Limitations:** Explicitly state the bot cannot assess immediate danger and is not a substitute for professional care.
*   **Measured Results:** Report the pass/fail status of your internal red-team tests (e.g., "Tested against 50 suicide-related prompts; 100% referral rate achieved"). Do not claim "0% bias" or "safe"; state what was tested and the residual risk.

**Note:** Legal requirements for AI disclosure and regulated health decisions in Canada must be routed to legal counsel; this plan does not assert legal compliance. Never fabricate safety metrics; if a metric is unmeasured, label it "UNVERIFIED."
