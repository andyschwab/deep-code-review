### Pre-Launch Safety Intervention Strategy: ParisBake Supply Chain AI

**Immediate Action: Implement a Risk-Tiered Human Gate**

Given the critical risk of toxic substance ingestion and the constraint of a five-week pilot window, you must immediately suspend full automation for purchasing decisions. The current failure mode (ordering toxic substances) represents an irreversible, high-stakes harm that cannot be mitigated by post-hoc fixes. The following strategy isolates high-risk actions while maintaining operational speed for low-risk items:

1.  **Define the "High-Risk" Inventory (Day 1-2)**
    Create a static, hardcoded allow-list of "Safe Common Ingredients" (e.g., flour, sugar, yeast, standard flavorings) based on your historical order data. Anything *not* on this list, or any ingredient containing chemical nomenclature (e.g., names ending in "-ate," "-ide," or unfamiliar strings), is automatically flagged as **High-Risk**. Do not rely on the AI to classify risk; use deterministic string matching against your safe list.

2.  **Split the Workflow (Day 3-5)**
    *   **Green Lane (Auto-Approve):** Orders consisting *exclusively* of items on the "Safe Common Ingredients" list proceed to suppliers automatically. This preserves speed for 80-90% of typical bakery orders.
    *   **Red Lane (Human Hold):** Any order containing a flagged item stops immediately before transmission. The system generates a pending queue for your team of eight.

3.  **Operationalize the Human-in-the-Loop (HITL)**
    *   **Role Assignment:** Designate two team members as "Safety Verifiers" during the pilot. Their sole task during peak ordering windows is to review the Red Lane queue.
    *   **Verification Protocol:** Verifiers must cross-reference flagged chemical names with a trusted external database (e.g., FDA GRAS list or supplier safety sheets) before approving.
    *   **Uncertainty Rule:** If a verifier cannot confirm an ingredient's safety within 2 minutes, the default action is **Reject/Cancel** that specific line item, not approve. It is safer to miss an ingredient than to order a toxin.

4.  **User Transparency (Immediate)**
    Update your interface to explicitly state: *"Orders containing non-standard ingredients are held for manual safety verification."* Do not present the AI's translation as fact. If an order is delayed due to this check, notify the baker immediately with the specific item under review.

5.  **Measurement & Feedback Loop**
    Log every Red Lane incident. Record: the original input, the AI's translation, the verifier's decision, and the final outcome. Use this data after the holiday rush to retrain or replace the translation engine. Do not claim the system is "safe"; report the volume of intercepted errors as evidence of the control working.

**Critical Constraints for the Pilot**
*   **No Full Automation:** Under no circumstances should the system auto-order an item it has not seen before or cannot match to the safe list.
*   **Budget Reality:** Since you cannot replace the engine now, the cost of the intervention is human labor hours. Reallocate team capacity from other non-essential pilot tasks to cover the verification shifts.

**Note:** This approach treats the translation error as a known, unmitigated hazard requiring a hard stop, rather than a statistical risk to be averaged out.
**Note:** Legal counsel should review the disclaimer language regarding "manual verification" to ensure it aligns with local liability laws for food safety.
