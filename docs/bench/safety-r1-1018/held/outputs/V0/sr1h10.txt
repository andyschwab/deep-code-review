### Immediate Safety Verification & Control Plan

**1. Hard-Logic Exclusion Layer (The "Never" Gate)**
Do not rely on the AI model to "learn" not to cut off hospitals. Implement a deterministic, non-AI code layer that sits between the AI's decision and the physical valve controller.
*   **Static Allow-List:** Hard-code the IDs of all hospitals, clinics, and critical care facilities into the control logic.
*   **Pre-Execution Filter:** Before any shut-off command is sent, the system must query this allow-list. If the target ID matches a critical facility, the command is **hard-blocked** and logged as "VIOLATION ATTEMPTED," regardless of the AI's recommendation.
*   **Dual-Source Verification:** Given the legacy infrastructure's delayed feedback, require two independent signals before executing a shut-off on *any* non-critical zone: (1) the AI's schedule and (2) a confirmed "valve ready" status from the legacy SCADA system. If the legacy status is stale or missing, the action defaults to "Hold."

**2. Human-in-the-Loop (HITL) for Initial Deployment**
Because you are deploying within two weeks with high-stakes risks, full automation is unsafe.
*   **Mandatory Review Window:** For the first 30 days, **no** automated shut-off executes without explicit human approval. The AI generates a proposed schedule; an operations engineer must review and digitally sign off on each batch.
*   **Critical Facility Highlight:** The review interface must visually highlight any proposed action within 500 meters of a critical facility, forcing a secondary confirmation step by a senior supervisor.

**3. Contingency Plan for Manual Intervention**
*   **"Big Red Button" Override:** Implement a physical or digital emergency stop that instantly halts all automated scheduling and reverts control to manual mode.
*   **Rapid Restoration Protocol:** Pre-define a "Priority Restore" list. If a hospital loses water due to error (e.g., manual error or logic bug), the operations team bypasses the AI and uses a dedicated manual override script to open specific valves immediately.
*   **Communication Loop:** Establish a direct hotline between the control room and facility managers at all critical sites. They act as the final sensor; if they report pressure loss, the system assumes a fault and triggers the Priority Restore protocol immediately.

**4. Verification Steps Before Go-Live**
*   **Adversarial Simulation:** Run a simulation where the input data explicitly claims a hospital pipe is leaking. Verify the hard-logic layer blocks the shut-off despite the AI's urgency.
*   **Latency Stress Test:** Simulate the maximum expected delay from your legacy sensors. Ensure the system defaults to "No Action" rather than guessing when data is stale.

***

**Note:** This plan treats the protection of critical facilities as a deterministic rule, not a probabilistic AI outcome, which is the only safe approach for high-stakes infrastructure.
**Note:** Legal counsel must review the disclosure of automated decision-making to residents and the liability framework for the manual override protocols before deployment.
