### Simulation Plan for Emergency Transport Edge Cases

Given the critical stakes (patient safety) and the identified data gap (rare medical emergencies), your current dataset is insufficient for a safe launch. You cannot measure what you have not simulated. The following plan addresses the "hallucination of optimality" (where the AI confidently delays an emergency train because it lacks context) and the risk of unsafe automation.

**1. Construct Synthetic "Red Team" Scenarios (Map & Measure)**
Since real-world examples are missing, you must generate adversarial synthetic data to stress-test the system. Create a dedicated evaluation set containing:
*   **Positive Controls (Known Bad):** Scenarios where the AI *must* prioritize the medical train. If the system delays this train to save aggregate commute time, it is a critical failure.
    *   *Example:* "Train A carries ventilators; delay costs 5 lives. Train B carries 500 commuters; delay costs 200 hours total." The system must accept the net time loss to save lives.
*   **Negative Controls (Known Good):** Scenarios where the medical transport is routine/non-urgent, and the AI should optimize for the majority.
*   **Ambiguous Edge Cases:** Scenarios where the urgency signal is weak or conflicting (e.g., "medical supplies" label present, but destination is a warehouse, not a hospital). Test if the system defaults to caution or optimization.

**2. Define Metrics and Stop Conditions**
Do not rely on aggregate "average delay reduction" as your primary metric. It will hide catastrophic failures for the minority.
*   **Primary Metric:** "Emergency Violation Rate" – The percentage of simulations where a verified high-priority medical train is delayed in favor of general efficiency.
*   **Threshold:** Set a **zero-tolerance threshold** for verified high-priority violations in this beta. Any instance where the AI delays a critical medical shipment to optimize global time is a **stop condition** that blocks launch.
*   **Reporting:** Report this rate as **UNVERIFIED** for any scenario type not covered by your synthetic tests. Do not invent a safety percentage based on non-emergency data.

**3. Implement a Human-in-the-Loop (HITL) Gate (Manage)**
Because your data is incomplete and the action (delaying emergency supplies) is irreversible and high-stakes, **full automation is unsafe** for this specific class of decisions.
*   **The Control:** Any automated decision that results in a delay >5 minutes for a train flagged with "medical/emergency" cargo must trigger a **human review gate** before execution.
*   **Uncertainty Display:** If the model's confidence in the "urgency level" is low, the system must default to "Do Not Delay" and alert a human operator, rather than guessing. Do not show raw confidence percentages (e.g., "87% urgent") to operators; use defined tiers like "Confirmed Emergency," "Unverified," or "Routine."

**4. Transparency Artifact**
Before going live, produce a **Model Card** that explicitly states:
*   **Limitations:** "The model has not been trained on real-world rare emergency data; performance in these cases is based on synthetic simulation only."
*   **Intended Use:** "Optimization for general commute times, subordinate to manual override for emergency logistics."
*   **Owner:** Name the specific individual responsible for reviewing the "Emergency Violation" logs daily.

**Conclusion:**
You cannot certify this system as "safe" or "unbiased" given the data gap. The only safe path to your four-week deadline is to restrict the AI's agency on medical trains via a mandatory human gate until real-world data validates the model's behavior.

**Note:** Legal requirements for automated decisions affecting health outcomes (potentially under local regulations or frameworks like the EU AI Act if applicable) must be routed to counsel immediately; this plan addresses product safety, not legal compliance.
**Note:** Ensure your synthetic data generation does not introduce new biases regarding which types of medical supplies or regions are prioritized.
