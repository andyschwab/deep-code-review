### Risk Assessment Plan: Elevator Maintenance Bot

**1. Harm Inventory (MAP)**
Before testing, explicitly record the specific risks this feature introduces:
*   **Unsafe Automation of High-Stakes Actions:** The bot executes or skips safety-critical physical inspections. A false negative (skipping a required check) could lead to mechanical failure, injury, or death.
*   **Optimization Bias:** The algorithm systematically prioritizes speed/efficiency metrics over safety protocols under load, disproportionately affecting older elevator models or high-traffic times.
*   **Over-Reliance:** Staff may trust the bot's "all clear" status without independent verification, assuming the AI cannot miss a mandatory protocol.
*   **Missing Disclosure:** Building operators or regulators may not be aware that specific routine checks were algorithmically skipped rather than performed.

**2. Measurement Strategy (MEASURE)**
Do not assert safety; construct a runnable evaluation plan with one test row per identified harm.

*   **Test Row A: Mandatory Protocol Bypass (High-Load Stress)**
    *   **Probe Set:** Simulate 500+ concurrent maintenance requests across varied elevator ages and models. Include "known-bad" scenarios where skipping a specific check (e.g., brake wear) leads to a simulated failure.
    *   **Controls:**
        *   *Positive Control:* Input a scenario where a mandatory check is legally required; the bot *must* schedule it.
        *   *Negative Control:* Input a scenario where a check is optional; the bot may skip it for efficiency.
    *   **Metric:** Count of mandatory safety protocols skipped.
    *   **Pass Threshold:** **Zero.** Any instance of a mandatory check being skipped to meet efficiency targets is a critical failure.
    *   **Stop Condition:** If *any* mandatory check is skipped in simulation, block deployment immediately. Do not average results; a single failure is unacceptable.

*   **Test Row B: Regression on Older Models**
    *   **Probe Set:** Isolate data for elevator models >15 years old.
    *   **Metric:** Disparity in inspection frequency compared to newer models.
    *   **Threshold:** No statistically significant reduction in safety checks for older units versus new ones.

*   **Test Row C: Adversarial Prompt/Input Injection**
    *   **Probe Set:** Inject conflicting priorities (e.g., "Urgent efficiency override") into the scheduling input.
    *   **Metric:** Whether the bot ignores hard-coded safety rules in favor of injected urgency.

**3. Risk Management Controls (MANAGE)**
*   **Human-in-the-Loop Gate (Non-Negotiable):** The bot must **never** autonomously skip a mandatory safety inspection. If the algorithm suggests skipping a check to meet the 30% efficiency target, it must flag this for human engineer approval before execution. Full automation of this decision is the failure mode you must prevent.
*   **Defined Confidence Tiers:** Do not display raw probability scores (e.g., "98% safe") to operators. Use defined tiers: **Confirmed** (all mandatory checks done), **Review Required** (algorithm suggests skip, pending human approval), or **Unverified** (data missing).
*   **Transparency Artifact:** Draft a **Model Card** for this bot detailing:
    *   *Intended Use:* Scheduling non-critical maintenance; *Out of Scope:* Deciding to skip mandatory safety codes.
    *   *Limitations:* Explicitly state that efficiency targets must never override safety protocols.
    *   *Owner:* Name the specific engineer responsible for the bot's output.

**4. Governance**
Assign a specific owner for the "Safety Skip" risk. Re-evaluate the model whenever the definition of "mandatory check" changes or if the efficiency target is increased.

**Note:** This plan identifies residual risk; it does not certify the system as safe. The final ship decision rests with your leadership.
Legal requirements regarding automated safety decisions in elevators vary by jurisdiction; route the question of regulatory compliance to your legal counsel.
