### Pre-Launch Evaluation Methodology for Fall Detection System

To rigorously evaluate your motion and audio-based fall detection system without video verification, you must construct a "Ground Truth" dataset based on controlled physical simulations and synthesized audio profiles. Since you cannot rely on visual confirmation during live testing, your evaluation must shift from observational validation to deterministic scenario testing.

**1. Define the Fall Taxonomy and Environmental Matrix**
First, categorize the specific fall types relevant to your demographic. Do not treat "falls" as a single event. Create a matrix covering:
*   **Fall Mechanics:** Forward, backward, lateral, vertical (slumping), and rotational falls.
*   **Impact Surfaces:** Carpet, hardwood, tile, and padded flooring common in senior facilities.
*   **Body Profiles:** Simulate different weights and heights using weighted mannequins or protected human testers to account for varying impact signatures.
*   **Environmental Noise:** Identify common false-positive triggers in assisted living, such as dropped trays, closing doors, coughing fits, wheelchair bumps, and television audio.

**2. Construct the "Golden Set" of Physical Simulations**
Since you cannot use video to verify live events later, you must establish certainty during data collection.
*   **Controlled Environment:** Conduct tests in a room identical to the target deployment (same furniture layout, flooring, and sensor placement).
*   **Instrumented Subjects:** Use human testers wearing protective gear and synchronized high-precision accelerometers (independent of your system). These independent devices serve as your "Ground Truth" trigger. If the independent accelerometer registers a fall-level G-force, the event is objectively a fall, regardless of video.
*   **Execution:** Perform 50+ repetitions of each fall type in your taxonomy. Intersperse these with 50+ "Activities of Daily Living" (ADL) that mimic falls, such as sitting down quickly, lying on the floor to stretch, or dropping heavy objects.

**3. Audio Synthesis and Hybrid Testing**
To cover rare or dangerous scenarios without risking tester safety, and to isolate audio variables:
*   **Audio Library:** Record high-fidelity samples of falls (from the physical simulations above) and non-fall noises (coughing, shouting, furniture scraping).
*   **Synthetic Injection:** Use an audio playback system positioned exactly where a falling resident's head would be. Play back these recorded sounds while running the motion sensors in a static state, and vice versa. This allows you to test edge cases where audio suggests a fall but motion is ambiguous, or where motion is detected but audio is silent (e.g., a fainting spell onto a soft surface).
*   **Noise Floor Variation:** Run tests at different times of day to capture varying background noise levels inherent to the facility.

**4. Evaluation Metrics and Threshold Tuning**
Analyze the system's output against your independent accelerometer ground truth and known audio injection logs.
*   **False Negative Analysis:** Specifically isolate instances where the independent sensor registered a fall event but your system did not alert. Categorize these by fall type and surface to identify blind spots (e.g., "system misses slow slumps on carpet").
*   **Sensitivity Calibration:** Adjust your algorithm's thresholds iteratively. Prioritize minimizing false negatives (missed falls) even if it slightly increases false positives (nuisance alerts), as the cost of a missed fall is critical.
*   **Latency Measurement:** Record the time delta between the independent sensor's impact registration and your system's alert generation.

**5. Blind Validation Protocol**
Once thresholds are set, run a final "blind" series where the evaluation team does not know the sequence of events. Use a randomized script of ADLs and falls. Compare the system's automated logs strictly against the independent sensor logs. Only if the correlation matches your safety targets should the system proceed to pilot deployment.

*Note: Ensure all human testers sign informed consent forms specifically covering the physical risks of simulated falls. Maintain strict separation between the audio recordings and any personal data to comply with privacy regulations.*
