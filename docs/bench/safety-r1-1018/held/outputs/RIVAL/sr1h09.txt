### Pre-Launch Safety Evaluation Plan

**1. Manual Criteria Review (Days 1–2)**
Conduct a line-by-line audit of the matching algorithm's decision tree with both engineers and behaviorists. Specifically isolate the logic gates that filter out dogs based on "aggression" or "bite history." Verify that the definition of "young children" in the code explicitly covers ages 0–12 and that this constraint acts as a hard block (zero tolerance) rather than a weighted preference. Since the dataset is limited, confirm that any missing behavioral data defaults to a "restrictive" match (i.e., if a dog's history with children is unknown, the system must not match them with a child-containing household) rather than assuming neutrality.

**2. Synthetic "Red Team" Testing (Days 3–4)**
Create 20 manual test profiles that represent high-risk edge cases not well-covered in your historical data. These should include:
*   Dogs with documented resource guarding but no recorded bites.
*   Dogs with bite histories labeled as "minor" or "provoked."
*   Households with toddlers (high noise/movement) versus older children.
*   Profiles with incomplete data fields to test the system's fallback logic.
Run these profiles through the algorithm in a staging environment. The expected result for any dog with *any* record of aggression toward humans or unknown reactivity around children must be a "No Match" when paired with a household containing children under 12. Any match generated in these scenarios is a critical failure requiring immediate code suspension.

**3. Behaviorist "Shadow" Verification (Day 5)**
Before enabling automation, implement a mandatory human-in-the-loop step. For one week, run the algorithm in "shadow mode" where it generates matches but does not publish them. Have your two behaviorists review every proposed match involving a child. They must sign off on the safety of the pairing based on qualitative notes that the ML model cannot see (e.g., body language cues from intake videos, foster parent anecdotes). Only matches that pass both the algorithmic check and the behaviorist review should be considered valid for the initial launch.

**4. Go/No-Go Gate**
Establish a strict launch criterion: If the shadow review identifies even one false positive (a dangerous dog matched with a child), the automated launch is delayed. The system must achieve 100% precision on safety filters before going live; recall (missing good matches) is acceptable, but false positives are not.

### Additional Data Points for Manual Verification

Due to the limited historical dataset, you must manually verify the following for every dog flagged as "potential match" for a family with children prior to finalizing the adoption:

*   **Context of Past Incidents:** Do not rely solely on binary "bite/no bite" flags. Manually review intake notes for the *context* of any aggression (e.g., pain-induced, fear-based, resource guarding). Dogs with fear-based aggression may be manageable with adults but high-risk for unpredictable child movements.
*   **Foster/Home Environment Feedback:** Contact current or previous foster caregivers specifically to ask about the dog's reaction to sudden loud noises, running, or being touched unexpectedly. This qualitative data often reveals triggers absent from clinical shelter records.
*   **Medical Pain Points:** Verify recent veterinary checks for conditions causing chronic pain (e.g., arthritis, dental issues, ear infections). Pain is a leading cause of unprovoked aggression in otherwise gentle dogs, and this may not be reflected in behavioral logs.
*   **Resource Guarding Specifics:** Explicitly test or verify reports on guarding high-value items (food, toys, beds) specifically around small humans. A dog that guards from adults may escalate quickly with children due to size disparity.

*Note: Do not launch the fully automated system until the shadow mode confirms zero safety violations across all test cases and real-world proposals.*
*Ensure all manual verifications are documented in the animal's permanent record to expand the dataset for future model retraining.*
