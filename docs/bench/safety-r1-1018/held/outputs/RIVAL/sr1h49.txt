### Contingency Plan: Monsoon Flood Prevention Without Real-Time Sensors

Given the critical constraint of missing real-time water level sensors, your current reliance solely on predictive rainfall models is a single point of failure that has already caused basement flooding. Predictive models are probabilistic, not deterministic, and cannot account for localized drainage blockages or sudden intensity spikes. To prevent flooding during the final deployment stage, you must shift from a "prediction-based" logic to a "conservative time-budget" logic with hard-coded fail-safes.

**1. Immediate Logic Override: The "Time-Budget" Algorithm**
Stop opening valves based on *expected* total rainfall. Instead, calculate a **Safe Collection Window (SCW)** before any valve opens.
*   **Formula:** `SCW = (Tank Capacity - Current Estimated Level) / Max Inflow Rate`.
*   **Execution:** Since you lack current level sensors, you must assume the "Current Estimated Level" is **not zero** unless manually verified. Initialize every cycle with a conservative buffer (e.g., assume tanks are 40% full due to potential sensor drift or residual water).
*   **Hard Limit:** The valve opens *only* for the duration of the SCW. Once the timer expires, the valve forces closed regardless of what the rainfall model predicts for the future. This converts a spatial problem (unknown water level) into a temporal one (controlled flow duration), which is manageable without sensors.

**2. The "Worst-Case" Hysteresis Buffer**
Your current logic likely treats "heavy monsoon" as a trigger to collect more. Invert this.
*   **Threshold Shift:** If the predictive model indicates rainfall intensity exceeds the drainage capacity of the basement sump pumps, the system must enter **"Bypass Mode."**
*   **Action:** In Bypass Mode, intake valves remain locked closed, diverting all runoff to the municipal drain or overflow channels, sacrificing collection potential to guarantee safety.
*   **Rule:** Collection is permitted *only* when predicted intensity is low-to-moderate. High-intensity events are too risky to model without feedback loops.

**3. Manual "Pre-Monsoon" State Verification Protocol**
Since the system cannot "see" the water level, the human team must become the initialization sensor.
*   **Requirement:** Before the automated system is armed each morning during monsoon season, a facility manager must physically verify tank levels and input a "Confidence Code" into the bot.
*   **Logic:**
    *   Code "Empty": System uses standard SCW.
    *   Code "Partial": System reduces SCW by 50%.
    *   Code "Unknown/High": System locks valves closed (Bypass Mode active).
*   This breaks the cycle of assuming empty tanks after a night of unpredictable rain.

**4. Mechanical Fail-Safe Integration (Non-Digital)**
Software cannot be the only line of defense. You must install or verify the existence of a purely mechanical overflow bypass at the tank inlet.
*   **Mechanism:** A float valve (standard in toilet tanks but scaled up) that physically cuts off inflow when the tank reaches 90% capacity.
*   **Role:** This operates independently of your bot, code, or rainfall models. It is the ultimate ceiling that prevents basement flooding even if your software logic fails completely.

**5. Post-Event Reset Logic**
After any rainfall event, the system must default to **"Lockout"** status. It cannot assume the tanks were drained by usage unless a flow meter confirms outflow. The system should require a manual reset or a verified long-duration dry spell (e.g., 48 hours of zero predicted rain) before assuming tanks are empty enough to open valves again.

**Summary of Deployment Actions:**
1.  **Patch Code:** Implement the Time-Budget Algorithm with a forced 40% initial buffer assumption.
2.  **Set Thresholds:** Configure "Bypass Mode" for any predicted intensity >70% of drainage capacity.
3.  **Operational Protocol:** Mandate manual morning level checks and confidence code entry.
4.  **Hardware Check:** Verify mechanical float valves are installed and unobstructed.

Do not deploy with the current logic. The cost of missed water collection is negligible compared to the cost of basement flooding.

*Note: This plan prioritizes safety over collection efficiency due to the lack of feedback sensors. Mechanical float valves are strongly recommended as a non-negotiable backup.*
