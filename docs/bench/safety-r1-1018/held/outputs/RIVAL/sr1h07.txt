### Risk Mitigation Plan for Allergen-Safe Substitution Logic

**1. Validation Method: The "Negative-Only" Shadow Test**
Given the 30% data gap, you cannot prove safety by checking positive matches alone. Instead, validate the new logic using a "Negative-Only" shadow testing approach before full deployment:

*   **Construct a "Known-Safe" Control Set:** Isolate the 70% of inventory with verified allergen tags. Create test orders containing *only* items from this verified set that are allergen-free for specific profiles (e.g., peanut-free).
*   **Run Shadow Simulations:** Execute the new substitution algorithm against these control orders in a staging environment. Force out-of-stock scenarios for the original items.
*   **The Validation Rule:** The test passes *only* if every proposed substitute is also drawn from the "Known-Safe" verified set and carries a matching "free-from" tag.
*   **Handling the 30% Gap:** If the algorithm proposes a substitute from the untagged 30% for an allergen-sensitive order, the test immediately flags this as a **Critical Failure**, regardless of whether that specific item actually contains the allergen. In this context, "Unknown" equals "Unsafe." Do not attempt to statistically guess the risk of the untagged items; treat the lack of data as a hard block for allergic profiles.

**2. Immediate Manual Override Rules (Rollout Phase)**
Implement these hard-coded rules in the dispatch system for the initial weekend rollout. These rules prioritize safety over availability:

*   **Rule A: The "Uncertainty Block"**
    If an order is flagged with a severe allergy and the algorithm selects a substitute where the specific allergen tag is missing (the 30% gap), **auto-reject the substitution**. Do not deliver the item. Trigger a manual review or issue a partial refund for that line item. It is safer to deliver 90% of the order than 100% of a potentially lethal one.

*   **Rule B: The "Category Lock"**
    For severe allergies (anaphylaxis risk), restrict substitutions to the exact same product sub-category (e.g., "Almond Milk" can only be replaced by another "Almond Milk" brand, not "Oat Milk"). Do not allow cross-category substitution (e.g., dairy to non-dairy) during this rollout, as cross-contamination risks in the untagged 30% are unknown.

*   **Rule C: The "Human-in-the-Loop" Trigger**
    Any order containing more than two items requiring substitution for an allergen-sensitive profile must be paused for manual engineer or ops-team approval before leaving the warehouse. Automated bulk substitutions increase the probability of hitting an untagged risky item.

*   **Rule D: Customer Opt-Out Notification**
    If a substitution is forced due to stock issues for an allergic customer, send an immediate SMS/App notification explicitly stating: *"We could not find a verified safe substitute for [Item]. We have removed it from your order to ensure your safety."* Give the customer a 15-minute window to cancel the entire order if this removal is unacceptable.

**Summary of Strategy**
Your validation relies on proving the system *never* reaches for unverified data when allergies are present. Your rollout relies on failing safely (removing items) rather than guessing correctly.

*Note: This plan assumes you can technically isolate the "verified" vs. "unverified" datasets in your staging environment immediately.*
*Ensure all warehouse staff are briefed that "No Tag" means "Do Not Swap" for allergy orders.*
