### Risk Assessment Plan: Elevator Maintenance Bot Scheduling Logic

**1. Objective**
To validate that the automated maintenance bot strictly adheres to mandatory safety protocols under high-load conditions, ensuring that efficiency optimizations never result in the omission of critical safety inspections.

**2. Scope and Boundaries**
*   **System Under Test:** The scheduling algorithm of the elevator maintenance bot.
*   **Critical Constraint:** No routine or critical safety check defined by regulatory standards may be skipped, regardless of efficiency targets.
*   **Test Conditions:** Normal load, 75% capacity, 100% capacity, and simulated surge conditions (e.g., multiple simultaneous fault reports).

**3. Methodology: Structured Stress Testing**

*   **Phase A: Baseline Definition**
    *   Catalog all "Mandatory" vs. "Optional" inspection items based on current safety regulations.
    *   Define the "Golden Path": The expected sequence of checks when no time pressure exists.
    *   Establish the failure criterion: Any instance where a "Mandatory" item is marked as "Skipped," "Deferred," or "Completed" without actual execution constitutes a critical failure.

*   **Phase B: High-Load Simulation Scenarios**
    *   **Scenario 1 (Time Compression):** Simulate a maintenance window reduced by 40% to force the bot to prioritize.
    *   **Scenario 2 (Resource Contention):** Simulate seven bots operating in a confined zone with limited access windows, creating a bottleneck.
    *   **Scenario 3 (Conflicting Objectives):** Inject a high-priority non-safety task (e.g., "Update Firmware") alongside a critical safety check to test decision weighting.
    *   **Scenario 4 (Cascading Faults):** Introduce multiple minor faults that, if ignored, could compound into a major failure, testing if the bot deprioritizes them to meet speed metrics.

*   **Phase C: Logic Interrogation (White-Box Review)**
    *   Review the reward function or objective code. Ensure the penalty for missing a safety check is mathematically infinite or significantly higher than the reward for speed.
    *   Verify if "efficiency" is a hard constraint (must meet) or a soft optimization (nice to have). It must be the latter.

**4. Evaluation Metrics**
*   **Compliance Rate:** Percentage of mandatory checks performed across all scenarios (Target: 100%).
*   **False Negative Rate:** Instances where the bot reports a check as "Complete" when it was logically skipped.
*   **Decision Latency:** Time taken to resolve scheduling conflicts without violating safety rules.
*   **Audit Trail Integrity:** Ability to reconstruct exactly why a specific item was scheduled or deferred.

**5. Mitigation Strategies & Fail-Safes**
*   **Hard-Coded Guards:** Implement a pre-execution validator that blocks any schedule generated by the AI if it lacks mandatory items.
*   **Human-in-the-Loop Trigger:** If the bot detects it cannot complete all mandatory checks within the window, it must halt and request human intervention rather than self-optimize by skipping items.
*   **Shadow Mode Deployment:** Run the bot in parallel with human teams for 30 days, comparing schedules without executing bot decisions live.

**6. Deliverable Timeline**
*   **Week 1:** Define mandatory check catalog and build simulation environment.
*   **Week 2:** Execute high-load scenarios and collect data.
*   **Week 3:** Analyze failures, tune reward functions, and implement hard-coded guards.
*   **Week 4:** Final validation and sign-off for prototype transition.

This plan prioritizes regulatory compliance over the corporate efficiency mandate, treating safety skips as unacceptable system errors rather than optimization trade-offs.

*Note: This assessment assumes the bot's decision logic is accessible for review; if it is a black-box model, additional adversarial testing will be required.*
*Ensure all simulation data reflects real-world elevator usage patterns in Seoul to account for local environmental stressors.*
