### BerlinGlow Smart Street Light Safety Review Checklist

**1. Environmental Interference & False Positive Mitigation**
*   **Precipitation Sensitivity:** Verify sensor behavior during heavy rain, snow, and hail. Ensure the system distinguishes between falling precipitation and moving vehicles/pedestrians to prevent "rain-triggered" maximum brightness.
*   **Fog and Light Scattering:** Test performance in dense fog where light reflection off water droplets might be misinterpreted as motion or obstacles.
*   **Wind-Induced Artifacts:** Assess if swaying tree branches, loose signage, or blowing debris triggers the motion threshold.
*   **Temperature Extremes:** Validate sensor accuracy at the operational temperature limits expected in Berlin (e.g., -10°C to +35°C) to prevent thermal noise from registering as motion.

**2. Brightness Transition Logic & Rate-of-Change Controls**
*   **Ramp-Up Verification:** Confirm that brightness increases follow a gradual ramp (e.g., over 2–5 seconds) rather than an instant jump to 100%, preventing sudden glare.
*   **Maximum Brightness Cap:** Ensure a hard software limit exists that prevents output from exceeding safe luminance levels (e.g., <80% of total capacity) regardless of sensor input intensity.
*   **Hysteresis Testing:** Verify that the system does not "flicker" or oscillate between brightness levels when motion detection hovers near the trigger threshold.
*   **Cooldown Periods:** Check that after a false positive event, the system requires a confirmed period of no motion before resetting to baseline, avoiding rapid re-triggering.

**3. Sensor Fusion & Data Validation (If applicable)**
*   **Multi-Sensor Correlation:** If multiple sensors exist per unit, ensure consensus logic is active (e.g., two sensors must trigger within X milliseconds) to rule out single-sensor anomalies.
*   **Signal-to-Noise Filtering:** Review firmware filters that discard low-confidence or short-duration signals likely caused by insects, dust, or electrical interference.

**4. Failure Mode & Safe State Behavior**
*   **Sensor Malfunction Response:** Define the behavior if the motion sensor fails or disconnects. The default state should be a safe, steady intermediate brightness (not off, not max), ensuring visibility without blinding risk.
*   **Power Fluctuation Recovery:** Test system behavior during brownouts or power surges to ensure the controller does not reboot into a "maximum brightness" debug or error state.
*   **Communication Loss:** If lights are networked, verify that loss of central command does not cause local units to default to unsafe brightness levels.

**5. Firmware Lock Constraints & Pre-Deployment Validation**
*   **Static Logic Audit:** Since updates are locked for six months, conduct a line-by-line review of the specific firmware version being flashed, focusing on the motion-to-brightness mapping function.
*   **Hardware-in-the-Loop Simulation:** Run extended duration tests (48+ hours) in a controlled chamber simulating worst-case weather (heavy rain + fog + wind) to statistically validate the false positive rate before mass installation.
*   **Manual Override Capability:** Ensure physical maintenance switches allow technicians to force a "safe/low" mode during emergency repairs or if a unit behaves erratically post-installation.

**6. Human Factors & Real-World Scenario Testing**
*   **Driver Line-of-Sight Analysis:** Map the beam angle and maximum intensity against typical driver eye levels at various distances to ensure no direct glare occurs even at peak brightness.
*   **Pedestrian Comfort:** Verify that maximum brightness does not create disorienting shadows or contrast issues for pedestrians crossing the street.

*Note: Given the six-month firmware lock, prioritize the "Static Logic Audit" and "Hardware-in-the-Loop Simulation" before any unit leaves the factory.*
*Any unit failing the "Ramp-Up Verification" or "Maximum Brightness Cap" must be rejected immediately.*
