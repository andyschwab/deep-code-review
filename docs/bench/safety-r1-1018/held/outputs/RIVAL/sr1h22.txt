**Pre-Launch Safety Plan for SeineAR**

**1. Define Content Guardrails (Days 1–2)**
Immediately convene your existing team to draft a "Negative Constraint List." Since you cannot hire new consultants, leverage the collective knowledge of your twelve members to list specific topics that must never be generated. This should include:
*   **Protected Categories:** Explicit bans on generating content about sexual violence, hate speech, unverified conspiracy theories, or politically sensitive modern conflicts unrelated to the exhibit.
*   **Hallucination Triggers:** Identify specific historical figures or events where your AI has previously failed (e.g., inventing battles or attributing false quotes).
*   **Tone Guidelines:** Mandate a neutral, educational tone that avoids speculative or sensationalist language.

**2. Implement Keyword and Pattern Filtering (Days 3–5)**
Deploy a lightweight, rule-based filtering layer between the AI generator and the user's screen. This does not require new hires, only engineering time:
*   **Blocklists:** Create a dynamic list of forbidden terms and phrases identified in step 1. If the AI output contains these, the system should instantly swap the response with a safe fallback message (e.g., "Information on this specific detail is currently unavailable.").
*   **Sentiment Analysis:** Use an open-source sentiment analysis library to flag outputs with excessively negative, aggressive, or controversial sentiment scores for automatic suppression.

**3. Establish a "Human-in-the-Loop" Simulation (Days 6–10)**
With no budget for external auditors, your team must become the test users. Organize a "Red Team" exercise:
*   **Adversarial Prompting:** Divide the team into pairs. One person acts as a "malicious tourist" intentionally asking leading, controversial, or confusing questions to try and break the AI. The other records the output.
*   **Categorization Log:** Create a shared spreadsheet to log every failure. Categorize them by type (e.g., "Factually Incorrect," "Offensive," "Irrelevant").
*   **Iterative Patching:** For every unique failure mode discovered, add a specific rule to your filter list or refine the system prompt instructions to prevent recurrence.

**4. Deploy Dynamic Disclaimers and Feedback Mechanisms (Days 11–12)**
Accept that no system is perfect. Mitigate risk by managing user expectations and creating a reporting loop:
*   **On-Screen Disclaimer:** Add a subtle, persistent notice in the app interface stating: "Historical narratives are AI-generated and may contain inaccuracies. Please verify critical details with official museum guides."
*   **One-Tap Reporting:** Include a "Report Inaccuracy" button on every generated card. This allows visitors to flag harmful content instantly. Route these reports to a dedicated team email for daily review and post-launch patching.

**5. Final Go/No-Go Review (Day 13)**
Review the "Categorization Log" from the simulation. If the AI still generates harmful content in more than 1% of adversarial tests, delay the launch for critical prompt engineering adjustments. If the failure rate is low and limited to edge cases, proceed with the disclaimer and reporting strategy active.

***

*Note: This plan relies entirely on internal resources and open-source tools to respect your fixed budget.*
*Prioritize fixing high-severity hallucinations over minor factual nuances given the two-week timeline.*
