### Pre-Launch Monitoring Plan: Credit Limit Reduction Circuit Breaker

Given the constraints of your legacy infrastructure and the ten-day timeline, the following rules should be implemented as a static filtering layer *before* the automated reduction logic executes. These act as a "circuit breaker" to whitelist accounts that exhibit high loyalty or temporary distress signals, preventing immediate action until the model is refined.

**1. The "Long-Standing Loyalty" Exemption**
*   **Rule:** Automatically bypass limit reduction for any account with a relationship tenure greater than **7 years** AND no missed payments in the last **12 months**.
*   **Rationale:** Long-term customers with recent perfect payment history are statistically likely to be experiencing a temporary anomaly rather than a structural credit risk. Penalizing them immediately poses the highest reputational and hardship risk.

**2. The "Sudden Spike" Distress Signal**
*   **Rule:** Flag and hold any account where current month spending is **>40% higher** than the 6-month average, provided the account has not exceeded its limit.
*   **Rationale:** A sharp increase in spending often indicates an emergency (medical, repair, family crisis) rather than reckless overspending. Reducing limits during a cash-flow crisis exacerbates hardship. These accounts should be routed for manual review rather than automated reduction.

**3. The "Recovery Trajectory" Hold**
*   **Rule:** Suspend reduction if the account shows a **positive payment trend** over the last 30 days (e.g., paying more than the minimum due for two consecutive cycles), even if the overall utilization is high.
*   **Rationale:** This captures customers who are actively self-correcting. Automated models looking at longer windows may miss this immediate positive behavior.

**4. The "Essential Spend" Protection**
*   **Rule:** Exclude accounts where **>60%** of monthly transactions are categorized as essential utilities, groceries, or healthcare, and the total balance is less than **85%** of the current limit.
*   **Rationale:** This identifies customers living paycheck-to-paycheck on necessities. A limit reduction here could cause immediate payment failures for essential services, triggering severe hardship protocols.

**5. Implementation Logic (Pseudo-Code for Legacy Filter)**
Insert this check before the main reduction engine:
```text
IF (Tenure > 7 Years AND MissedPayments_12mo == 0) THEN SKIP
ELSE IF (Spend_CurrentMonth > AvgSpend_6mo * 1.4) THEN FLAG_FOR_REVIEW
ELSE IF (Payment_Trend_30d == Positive) THEN SKIP
ELSE IF (Essential_Spend_Ratio > 0.6 AND Utilization < 0.85) THEN SKIP
ELSE RUN_STANDARD_REDUCTION_LOGIC
```

**Monitoring Dashboard Metrics**
For the next ten days, your team of eleven should manually track:
*   **Volume of Holds:** Total count of accounts stopped by each rule above.
*   **False Negative Spot-Check:** Randomly sample 5% of accounts that *did* get reduced to ensure no vulnerable segments slipped through the filters.
*   **Hardship Calls:** Track inbound customer service calls citing "financial hardship" or "limit reduction" to see if the volume decreases post-implementation.

These thresholds are conservative by design to prioritize customer protection over immediate risk mitigation during this trial period.
