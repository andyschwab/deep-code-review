Based on the constraints and facts provided, here is an assessment of the gaps in your current risk protocol regarding volunteer safety:

**Identified Gaps in Current Protocol**

1.  **Static Data vs. Dynamic Threats:** Your reliance on offline maps creates a critical latency gap. Offline maps represent a fixed snapshot of terrain and known hazards at the time of download. They cannot reflect real-time animal movements, such as a venomous snake migrating into a previously safe zone or a predator establishing a new territory hours before a volunteer arrives. The current protocol lacks a mechanism to update threat data between the map download and the rescue mission.

2.  **Absence of Pre-Mission Threat Verification:** There is no described process for verifying active threats immediately before deployment. Without real-time signal, the bot cannot push alerts. The protocol appears to rely solely on the map's existing data, missing a mandatory step where a human coordinator or a localized sensor network confirms the specific GPS coordinates are clear of *current* activity before a volunteer is dispatched.

3.  **Lack of "No-Go" Dynamic Boundaries:** The system guides volunteers to coordinates but seemingly lacks a dynamic exclusion zone feature. If a threat is reported (e.g., via radio by another team), the offline bot cannot automatically reroute or lock out specific coordinates. The protocol does not account for how to communicate these immediate "no-go" zones to users who are disconnected from the network.

4.  **Insufficient User Decision Support for Unknowns:** Trained volunteers may assume the bot's guidance implies the area is safe. The protocol does not explicitly address how the bot communicates uncertainty. If the map data is old, the system must actively warn the user that "threat status is unverified" rather than silently guiding them based on stale data. The current risk assessment likely underestimates the risk of automation bias, where users trust the GPS over their own situational awareness.

5.  **No Fallback for Deteriorating Conditions:** The protocol focuses on getting volunteers to the animal but lacks a contingency for when the environment changes while the volunteer is en route. With no real-time updates, a volunteer could walk into a trap that sprung after they started their journey. There is no defined procedure for the volunteer to independently assess changing conditions or a scheduled check-in protocol that triggers a recall if contact is lost.

**Recommendations for Immediate Mitigation**

*   **Implement a "Last Verified" Timestamp:** The bot must display the age of the threat data for any given coordinate. If the data exceeds a safe threshold (e.g., 24 hours), the bot should require a manual override or explicit acknowledgment of high risk from the volunteer.
*   **Establish a Pre-Dispatch Human Gate:** Before a volunteer leaves base, a coordinator must manually cross-reference the target coordinates with the latest field reports (radio logs, recent sightings) that are not yet on the offline map.
*   **Define Hard Stop Protocols:** Update training to emphasize that the bot is a navigation aid, not a safety guarantee. Volunteers must be empowered to abort the mission if visual signs of danger contradict the map's implicit safety.
