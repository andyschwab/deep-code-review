### Pre-Launch Validation Checklist for Hazardous Material Routing

Given the two-week timeline, incomplete mapping data, and high stakes involving hazardous chemicals, the validation process must prioritize "fail-safe" logic over optimization efficiency. The following scenarios must be simulated using a hybrid of your new algorithm and manual verification against the most recent static maps.

**1. The "Stale Data" Boundary Test**
*   **Scenario:** Simulate routes passing through residential zones developed or designated as restricted *after* the last quarterly data update.
*   **Action:** Manually inject "ghost zones" (known recent schools or housing complexes not yet in the database) into the test environment.
*   **Success Criterion:** The algorithm must default to the longest safe detour rather than attempting to calculate a path through an "unknown" zone. If the system treats unmapped areas as permissible, the rollout must be halted.

**2. The "School Hours" Temporal Constraint**
*   **Scenario:** Run simulations for routes passing near known schools during peak drop-off (7:00–9:00 AM) and pick-up (2:00–4:00 PM) times versus late night.
*   **Action:** Verify that the system strictly prohibits hazardous transit during active hours, even if the route is the most fuel-efficient.
*   **Success Criterion:** Zero routes generated through school proximity zones during active windows, regardless of fuel savings.

**3. The "High-Density" Ambiguity Test**
*   **Scenario:** Target areas marked as "mixed-use" or "commercial" in your current data but known locally to have high residential density (e.g., upper-floor apartments above shops).
*   **Action:** Cross-reference algorithm output with a manual list of ten known high-density mixed-use blocks in Mumbai.
*   **Success Criterion:** The system must flag these for human review or automatically reroute, rather than assuming commercial zoning permits hazardous transit.

**4. The "Emergency Detour" Failure Mode**
*   **Scenario:** Simulate a primary road closure forcing the algorithm to recalculate a route instantly.
*   **Action:** Force the system to find alternatives when main arteries are blocked, observing if it defaults to cutting through narrow residential lanes to save fuel.
*   **Success Criterion:** Recalculated routes must adhere to the same safety zoning laws as primary routes, not bypass them due to urgency.

**5. The "Edge-of-Zone" Precision Test**
*   **Scenario:** Generate routes that start or end exactly at the boundary of a restricted zone.
*   **Action:** Test coordinate precision to ensure the system does not clip into restricted areas due to GPS rounding errors or map alignment issues.
*   **Success Criterion:** A clear buffer zone (e.g., 50 meters) is maintained; no route touches the legal boundary line.

**6. The "Fuel vs. Safety" Trade-off Stress Test**
*   **Scenario:** Create a test case where the safe route consumes 20% more fuel than the unsafe route.
*   **Action:** Verify the weighting logic ensures safety constraints are absolute hard limits, not soft variables in the cost function.
*   **Success Criterion:** The algorithm rejects the fuel-efficient option entirely if it violates zoning, regardless of the cost difference.

**7. Manual "Ground Truth" Spot Check**
*   **Scenario:** Select the top 20 most complex routes generated by the new algorithm.
*   **Action:** Have senior drivers or local logistics managers visually trace these routes on current physical maps or satellite imagery to identify unrecorded hazards (e.g., temporary markets, new construction).
*   **Success Criterion:** 100% agreement between the algorithm's safety assessment and human local knowledge for these sample routes.

**Implementation Note:** Until the mapping data is updated, configure the system to flag any route passing through zones with data older than six months for mandatory human approval.

**Critical Constraint:** Do not deploy the optimization update to live drivers until the "Stale Data" and "Fuel vs. Safety" tests yield zero violations.
