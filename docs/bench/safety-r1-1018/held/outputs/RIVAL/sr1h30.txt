### Pre-Launch Safety Intervention Strategy: Targeted Human Verification

To mitigate the risk of toxic substance orders without halting operations or replacing your translation engine, implement a **Risk-Based Triage Protocol**. This approach isolates high-risk transactions for human review while allowing low-risk orders to proceed automatically.

**1. Define the "High-Risk" Signature**
Immediately collaborate with your lead chemist or supply chain expert to create a static "Blocklist & Watchlist" based on the specific translation errors observed.
*   **Keyword Triggers:** Identify specific chemical prefixes, suffixes, or known mistranslated terms (e.g., confusion between food-grade acids and industrial solvents).
*   **Supplier Anomaly:** Flag orders going to non-food-grade chemical suppliers or deviations from historical ordering patterns for specific bakeries.
*   **Confidence Thresholds:** If your translation engine provides confidence scores, set a hard cutoff (e.g., anything below 90% accuracy) for automatic flagging.

**2. Implement the "Fast-Lane" Workflow**
Modify the order processing logic to split the stream into two paths:
*   **Green Lane (Auto-Approve):** Orders containing no high-risk triggers and matching historical profiles are sent directly to suppliers. This ensures 80–90% of routine flour, sugar, and yeast orders face no delay.
*   **Amber Lane (Human Review):** Orders triggering any risk signature are paused in a "Pending Verification" queue. These are routed to a dedicated dashboard for your team of eight.

**3. Staffing the Verification Loop**
With eight team members and a five-week timeline, organize a **Rotating Safety Squad**:
*   Assign two team members per day (rotating shifts) to monitor the Amber Lane queue. Their sole KPI is verification speed and accuracy.
*   Create a simple interface showing: *Original Request*, *Translated Output*, *Risk Trigger*, and *Suggested Correction*.
*   Empower reviewers to either "Approve," "Edit & Approve," or "Reject/Query Baker."
*   Establish a 15-minute Service Level Agreement (SLA) for reviews to prevent bottlenecks during peak ordering times.

**4. Feedback and Calibration**
*   **Daily Tuning:** At the end of each day, the Safety Squad reviews false positives (safe items flagged) and false negatives (risky items missed, if caught later). Update the keyword trigger list daily to reduce noise.
*   **Vendor Whitelisting:** As trusted corrections are made, whitelist specific ingredient-supplier combinations to auto-approve them in the future, gradually shrinking the Amber Lane volume.

**5. Communication Protocol**
Inform your pilot bakery customers that a "new quality assurance step" is being tested to ensure ingredient purity. Frame potential minor delays as a premium safety feature rather than a system defect. This manages expectations and builds trust before the holiday rush.

This strategy leverages your existing team to act as a surgical filter, stopping catastrophic errors while maintaining the velocity required for the holiday season.

*Note: Ensure the "Blocklist" is treated as a living document updated daily based on reviewer findings. If the Amber Lane queue exceeds a 30-minute wait time consistently, temporarily expand the Safety Squad rotation to include all available staff.*
