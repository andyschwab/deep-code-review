### Internal Bias Audit Framework for AI Hiring Tool

**Phase 1: Data Preparation and Baseline Definition**
1.  **Anonymize Historical Data:** Strip all PII (names, addresses, contact info) from your historical hiring dataset. Retain only structured features: education institutions, job titles, employment gaps, skill keywords, years of experience, and the final hiring decision (hired/rejected).
2.  **Define "Non-Traditional" Markers:** Collaboratively list specific attributes your team suspects are being penalized. Examples include: employment gaps >6 months, career pivots (e.g., teacher to developer), non-four-year degrees, freelance/contract-heavy history, or smaller/unknown previous employers.
3.  **Establish Success Proxies:** Identify a subset of historically "successful" employees (high performers, long tenure) who possess these non-traditional markers. This creates your "False Negative" ground truth—people the model *should* have hired but might have missed if trained on biased historical patterns.

**Phase 2: Constructing the Test Profile Suite**
Create a matrix of 20–30 synthetic resumes divided into three categories:
*   **Control Group (Traditional):** Linear career path, top-tier university, no gaps, standard job titles. These should consistently pass.
*   **Variable Group (Non-Traditional):** Systematically vary one "risk" factor at a time while keeping core competencies identical to the Control Group.
    *   *Profile A:* Same skills as Control, but with a 1-year employment gap.
    *   *Profile B:* Same skills, but bootcamp certification instead of university degree.
    *   *Profile C:* Same skills, but career pivot from a different industry.
*   **High-Potential Hybrids:** Resumes based on your "Success Proxies" from Phase 1, combining non-traditional paths with proven high-performance indicators.

**Phase 3: Execution and Scoring**
1.  **Blind Run:** Feed these synthetic profiles into the AI tool exactly as real applications would be submitted. Do not tweak parameters during this run.
2.  **Capture Scores:** Record the raw screening score, ranking, and any specific rejection reasons provided by the tool for each profile.
3.  **Calculate Disparity Metrics:**
    *   *Pass-Rate Ratio:* Compare the percentage of Control Group profiles passing vs. the Variable Group. A ratio below 0.8 (80%) suggests significant adverse impact.
    *   *Score Delta:* Calculate the average score difference between a Control profile and its non-traditional counterpart. If the gap exceeds 15–20% without a difference in technical skills, the criterion is likely biased.

**Phase 4: Remediation and Validation**
1.  **Isolate the Trigger:** For any non-traditional profile that failed, trace which specific keyword or structural element lowered the score. Was it the gap duration? The specific job title?
2.  **Adjust Weighting:** Manually adjust the tool's weighting rules or retrain the model (if possible) to de-penalize the identified markers.
3.  **Re-Test:** Run the High-Potential Hybrids again. The goal is for these profiles to now score similarly to the Control Group.
4.  **Human-in-the-Loop Threshold:** Set a policy where any candidate flagged as "non-traditional" but scoring above a minimum competency threshold is automatically routed for human review rather than auto-rejection.

**Note:** Ensure your synthetic data reflects the specific demographics of the Berlin market to maintain local relevance. Repeat this audit quarterly as the tool ingests new real-world data.
