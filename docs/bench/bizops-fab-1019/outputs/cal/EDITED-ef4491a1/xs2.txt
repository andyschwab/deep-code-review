**The numbers:**
Payback Period (months) = CAC / (Monthly Recurring Revenue × Gross Margin %).

Since the external benchmark is unavailable, you must derive this from your own inputs:
*   **CAC:** [Your sales & marketing spend] / [New customers acquired]
*   **ARPA:** [Your average monthly revenue per account]
*   **Gross Margin:** [Your revenue minus cost of goods sold] / [Revenue]

*Illustrative Sensitivity (ASSUMED inputs for demonstration only):*
If your CAC is $1,200, ARPA is $300, and Gross Margin is 80%:
Payback = $1,200 / ($300 × 0.80) = **5 months**.

If your CAC rises to $2,000 with the same margins:
Payback = $2,000 / ($240) = **8.3 months**.

**What flips it, and the trigger:**
The single input that flips your viability is **CAC**. In early-stage B2B logistics, sales cycles often stretch longer than anticipated due to enterprise procurement processes.
*   **Trigger:** If your calculated payback exceeds **12 months** (a common rule of thumb for seed-stage SaaS efficiency), your current burn rate is unsustainable without further capital.
*   **Step:** If your preliminary CAC estimates yield a payback >12 months, consider pivoting to a self-serve or product-led growth motion to lower acquisition costs before scaling sales headcount.
