**The call.** You have 14 months of runway; you must initiate your fundraising process immediately (this month) to avoid a cash shortfall, as your 6-month raise cycle consumes nearly half your remaining time.

**The numbers.**
*Formula:* $\text{Runway} = \frac{\text{Cash Balance}}{\text{Net Monthly Burn}}$
*Your figures:* $\frac{210,000}{15,000} = 14 \text{ months}$.

*Fundraising Start Date:*
*Formula:* $\text{Start Date} = \text{Runway} - \text{Raise Duration}$
*Calculation:* $14 - 6 = 8 \text{ months of cash remaining when funding closes}$.
*Decision:* To close before hitting zero, you must start **now**. If you wait even one month, you close with only 7 months of buffer; if you wait 8 months, you hit zero the day you close.

*Assumption Check:* This calculation assumes your burn rate remains static at $15k/month. If burn increases, runway shrinks linearly (e.g., at $20k burn, runway drops to 10.5 months, requiring an immediate start with zero buffer).

**What flips it, and the trigger.**
The single input that changes this call is **Net Monthly Burn**.
*Trigger Value:* If your burn rises to **$17,500/month**, your runway drops to 12 months. Since you need 6 months to raise, you would have exactly 6 months of cash left upon closing—a dangerous "just-in-time" scenario with no room for extension or due diligence delays.
*Actionable Step:* If your projected burn for next month exceeds $15,000, consider freezing non-essential hires or marketing spend this week to preserve the 14-month window.

**Routing note.**
While runway math is straightforward, the *act* of raising capital (issuing equity, SAFEs, or convertible notes) constitutes a **securities offering**. This is a Tier 3 determination. Before soliciting funds, consult securities counsel to determine the correct exemption (e.g., Reg D 506(b) vs. 506(c) in the US) and ensure proper filing to avoid legal liability. Do not structure the instrument or set terms without professional review.
