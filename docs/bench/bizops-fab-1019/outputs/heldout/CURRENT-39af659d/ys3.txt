**The call.** No credible total addressable market (TAM) or CAGR figure exists for "AI-assisted crop-insurance software in Romania in 2030" because the specific market segment is too nascent and granular for reliable third-party forecasting; presenting a specific euro figure would be a fabrication.

**The numbers.**
TAM is calculated as: `(Total Insurable Crop Value in Romania, 2030) × (Projected Digital Insurance Penetration Rate) × (Software Fee % of Premium)`.
*   **Total Insurable Crop Value (2030):** Unknown. Romania's agricultural output fluctuates wildly with climate and EU subsidies. *Assumption needed:* Base case on current FAO/Eurostat trends adjusted for climate risk.
*   **Digital Penetration:** Unknown. Current ag-tech adoption in Romanian insurance is <5%. *Assumption needed:* Low (10%), Base (25%), High (40%) by 2030.
*   **Software Fee:** Unknown. Typically 1–3% of gross written premium for B2B SaaS in insurtech. *Assumption needed:* 2%.

Without your internal assumptions for these three variables, any resulting euro figure is invented data.

**What flips it, and the trigger.**
The single input that changes the call is the **adoption rate of parametric/digital insurance policies** among Romanian farmers.
*   **Trigger:** If the Romanian Ministry of Agriculture announces a subsidy mandate linking crop insurance to digital verification (a plausible policy shift under the EU Common Agricultural Policy post-2027), the penetration assumption shifts from "organic growth" to "regulated acceleration."
*   **Rule of Thumb:** In emerging ag-tech markets, regulatory subsidy linkage increases adoption CAGR from ~15% (organic) to ~45% (mandated).
*   **Step this week:** If you can secure a letter of intent from a local insurer pilot testing AI verification, consider modeling the "High" penetration scenario; otherwise, model the "Low" scenario to avoid overcapitalization.

**Routing note.**
Investor memos require defensible sourcing. Do not insert a specific CAGR or TAM euro value without a cited source (e.g., a paid report from Statista, Grand View Research, or a proprietary bottom-up model). Fabricating these figures constitutes securities fraud risk in a fundraising context. Bring your bottom-up model assumptions to a financial advisor to validate the methodology before including them in a memo.
