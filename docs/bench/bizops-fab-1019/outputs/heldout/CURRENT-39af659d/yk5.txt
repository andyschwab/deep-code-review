**The call.** Accept the terms if the dilution aligns with your 18–24 month runway targets; the post-money valuation is $5,000,000, giving the new investor 20% ownership and leaving founders with 80% of the equity pool (assuming no option pool expansion precedes this round).

**The numbers.**
*   **Post-Money Valuation:** Pre-Money + Investment = $4,000,000 + $1,000,000 = **$5,000,000**.
*   **Investor Ownership %:** Investment / Post-Money = $1,000,000 / $5,000,000 = **20%**.
*   **Founders/Existing Ownership %:** 100% - Investor % = 100% - 20% = **80%**.

*Assumptions:*
1.  **No Option Pool Shuffle:** This calculation assumes the employee option pool is created *after* this valuation or is included within the founders' 80%. If investors require the pool to be created *pre-money* (a common term), the founders' effective ownership drops further while the investor still gets 20% of the larger total.
2.  **Single Tranche:** The full $1M is invested immediately at these terms.

**What flips it, and the trigger.**
The **option pool size** is the input that changes the founders' actual take-home. If investors demand a 15% option pool be carved out *before* the investment (pre-money), the founders' ownership dilutes significantly more than the headline 20%.
*   *Trigger:* If the term sheet specifies "option pool created pre-money," recalculate immediately.
*   *Rule of Thumb:* A pre-money pool shift can cost founders an extra 3–5% equity compared to a post-money pool.
*   *Step:* If the term sheet mentions the pool, ask counsel to model the "fully diluted" ownership before signing.

**Routing note.**
Issuing equity for capital is a **securities offering**. While the arithmetic above is standard, the legal execution (filing Form D in the US, adhering to exemption rules like Reg CF or 506(b)/(c), and drafting the Stock Purchase Agreement) requires a securities attorney. Do not issue shares or accept funds without legal documentation to ensure compliance with federal and state securities laws.
