**The call.** Your current 65% gross margin is healthy for a $120 price point; however, a 20% discount slashes margin to 56.7%, so only deploy that discount if it unlocks volume that strictly offsets the per-unit profit loss or clears excess inventory.

**The numbers.**
Formula: $\text{Gross Margin \%} = \frac{\text{Price} - \text{COGS}}{\text{Price}} \times 100$

*Scenario A: Current Pricing*
*   Price: $120
*   COGS: $42
*   Calculation: $(120 - 42) / 120 = 0.65$
*   **Result:** 65.0% Gross Margin ($78 contribution per unit)

*Scenario B: 20% Discount*
*   Discounted Price: $120 \times 0.80 = $96
*   COGS: $42 (ASSUMED constant)
*   Calculation: $(96 - 42) / 96 = 0.5625$
*   **Result:** 56.3% Gross Margin ($54 contribution per unit)

*Sensitivity Check (Volume Required to Maintain Total Profit)*
To earn the same total gross profit dollars after the discount, you must sell more units.
*   Formula: $\text{Required Volume Increase} = \frac{\text{Old Margin \$}}{\text{New Margin \$}} - 1$
*   Calculation: $(78 / 54) - 1 = 0.444$
*   **Result:** You need a **44.4% increase in unit volume** just to break even on total profit.

**What flips it, and the trigger.**
The deciding input is **elasticity of demand** (how many extra units you actually sell).
*   **Trigger:** If your sales volume does not increase by at least 45% during the discount period, the promotion destroys net profit compared to selling at full price.
*   **Rule of Thumb:** Never discount deeper than your target CAC payback allows. If your CAC is $30, the discounted contribution ($54) still covers it, but leaves less for R&D and overhead.
*   **Step this week:** Run a limited A/B test on 10% of your traffic. If conversion rate does not lift by ~45% relative to the control, kill the 20% discount offer immediately.
