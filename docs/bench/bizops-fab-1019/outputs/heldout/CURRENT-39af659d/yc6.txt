There is no original study, journal article, or DOI for the "Abernathy Discount Fatigue effect" in SaaS pricing because **it does not exist**.

The term appears to be a conflation of three distinct concepts:
1.  **The Abernathy-Utterback Model:** A seminal innovation theory (Abernathy & Utterback, *Research Policy*, 1978) describing the lifecycle of product innovation from fluid to specific. It addresses R&D and production dynamics, not pricing psychology or discount fatigue.
2.  **Discount Fatigue:** A well-documented phenomenon in retail and SaaS where excessive promotions erode brand value, train customers to wait for sales, and compress margins. While widely discussed in industry literature (e.g., by ProfitWell, Price Intelligently, or McKinsey), it is not attributed to a single academic study by an author named Abernathy.
3.  **SaaS Pricing Metrics:** The specific mechanics of how discounts impact LTV:CAC and churn are standard unit economics calculations, not a singular named "effect" with a foundational paper.

**The Call:** Stop searching for this specific citation; it is likely an AI hallucination or an internal industry term misremembered as academic canon.
