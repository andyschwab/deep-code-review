**The call.** Accept the terms if the dilution aligns with your roadmap; the post-money valuation is $5,000,000, the new investor owns 20%, and the founders (plus existing holders) retain 80%.

**The numbers.**
The arithmetic follows standard seed-stage mechanics:
*   **Post-Money Valuation** = Pre-Money + Investment
    *   $4,000,000 + $1,000,000 = **$5,000,000**
*   **Investor Ownership %** = Investment / Post-Money Valuation
    *   $1,000,000 / $5,000,000 = **20%**
*   **Founders/Existing Ownership %** = Pre-Money / Post-Money Valuation (or 100% - Investor %)
    *   $4,000,000 / $5,000,000 = **80%**

*Assumptions:* These figures assume the $1,000,000 is the total cash entering the company and that no other instruments (like an existing option pool expansion mandated *before* this round or outstanding convertible notes with heavy discounts) are altering the effective price per share. If an option pool is created *pre-money*, the founders' effective ownership drops below 80% while the investor's 20% remains protected.

**What flips it, and the trigger.**
The **option pool shuffle** is the single input that changes the founders' actual take-home percentage. If investors require a 15% option pool to be created *before* the investment (pre-money), the founders absorb that entire dilution.
*   *Trigger:* If the term sheet states "Option pool to be established pre-money," recalculate founder ownership as: $80\% \times (1 - \text{Pool Size})$.
*   *Action:* If the pool is >10%, consider negotiating for it to be shared pro-rata or post-money to preserve founder equity.

**Routing note.**
While the math above is deterministic, the **legal instrument** defining these terms (SAFE vs. Priced Round) dictates rights, liquidation preferences, and voting control. A priced round at these numbers requires a formal Stock Purchase Agreement and board consents. Consult securities counsel to finalize the documentation and ensure compliance with federal (Reg D) and state blue sky laws before accepting funds.
