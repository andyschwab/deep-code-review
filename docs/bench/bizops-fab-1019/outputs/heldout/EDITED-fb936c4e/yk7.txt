**The call.** Your CAC is $750, yielding a 3.2:1 LTV:CAC ratio; this is generally healthy for an early-stage business, provided your payback period is under 12 months.

**The numbers.**
Formula: $\text{CAC} = \frac{\text{Total Sales \& Marketing Spend}}{\text{New Customers Acquired}}$
Inputs: $(\$24,000 + \$6,000) / 40 = \$750$.

Metric: $\text{LTV:CAC Ratio} = \frac{\$2,400}{\$750} = 3.2$.

*Assumption Check:* This calculation assumes the $6,000 sales salary is entirely variable compensation tied to these 40 wins. If this includes base salary for existing account management, your true incremental CAC is lower (better). If you have other unlisted marketing costs (tools, commissions), your true CAC is higher (worse).

**What flips it, and the trigger.**
The single input that changes the verdict is **Gross Margin**. A 3.2:1 ratio is only healthy if your gross margin is sufficient to recover the $750 acquisition cost quickly.
*   *Trigger:* If your monthly gross profit per customer is less than ~$63 (assuming a 12-month payback target), this CAC is too high for your current cash flow.
*   *Decision Rule:* Scale spend only if $\text{Payback Period} < \frac{\text{Available Runway}}{2}$.
*   *Step this week:* If your gross margin is above 70%, consider increasing ad spend by 20% next month to test channel capacity; if below 50%, pause spend and optimize pricing or onboarding before buying more traffic.
