**The call.** Your current 65% gross margin is healthy for a software-enabled or physical product business; however, a 20% discount collapses your margin to 56%, which may dangerously erode your ability to cover fixed costs and marketing if your volume does not increase proportionally. Do not discount below your contribution margin threshold without a verified customer acquisition strategy.

**The numbers.**
Gross Margin % = $(Price - COGS) / Price$.

*Scenario A: Current Pricing*
*   Price: $120
*   COGS: $42
*   Calculation: $(120 - 42) / 120 = 78 / 120$
*   **Gross Margin:** 65%

*Scenario B: 20% Discount*
*   Discounted Price: $120 \times (1 - 0.20) = \$96$
*   COGS: $42 (assumed constant per unit)
*   Calculation: $(96 - 42) / 96 = 54 / 96$
*   **Gross Margin:** 56.25%

*Impact Analysis*
You are giving up 9 percentage points of margin. To maintain the same total gross profit dollars as selling 100 units at full price ($7,800), you would need to sell approximately 144 units at the discounted price ($54 margin/unit). This requires a 44% increase in volume just to break even on profit.

**What flips it, and the trigger.**
The deciding input is your **volume elasticity** (how many extra units you sell because of the discount).
*   **Trigger:** If a 20% price cut does not drive at least a 44% increase in unit sales, the discount destroys value compared to holding price.
*   **Action:** Run a limited-time A/B test this week. If the conversion rate lift is <44% relative to the price drop, consider bundling value-adds instead of cutting price to preserve the 65% margin.
