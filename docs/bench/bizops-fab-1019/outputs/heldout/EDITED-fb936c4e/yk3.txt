**The call.** You need 500 customers monthly to break even; therefore, 400 customers by June is insufficient to cover costs and will result in a monthly loss.

**The numbers.**
The break-even formula is: $\text{Break-Even Units} = \frac{\text{Fixed Costs}}{\text{Price} - \text{Variable Cost}}$.

Using your figures:
*   Fixed Costs: $18,000
*   Price: $50
*   Variable Cost: $14
*   Contribution Margin: $50 - $14 = $36

Calculation: $18,000 / 36 = 500$ customers.

At 400 customers (your June target):
*   Revenue: $400 \times \$50 = \$20,000$
*   Total Variable Costs: $400 \times \$14 = \$5,600$
*   Contribution: $\$20,000 - \$5,600 = \$14,400$
*   Net Result: $\$14,400 - \$18,000 = -\$3,600$ (Monthly Loss)

**What flips it, and the trigger.**
The single input that changes this call is **volume**. The trigger is reaching **500 active paying customers**. Until you hit 500, every additional customer reduces your loss by $36; after 500, every customer adds $36 to profit. If you cannot reach 500 customers by June, you must either raise prices, lower variable costs, or secure enough cash runway to cover the $3,600 monthly shortfall until volume increases.

**Actionable step.**
If your customer acquisition rate suggests you will only hit 400 by June, consider launching a "good-better-best" pricing tier immediately to increase the average price per user above $50, which would lower the break-even point without needing more customers.
