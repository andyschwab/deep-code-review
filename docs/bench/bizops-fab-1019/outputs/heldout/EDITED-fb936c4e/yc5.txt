**The Numbers**
Because the specific data point is unavailable, you cannot plug a verified coefficient into your model. Instead, you must derive this locally using your own figures:
*   **Formula:** $\text{LTV}_{\text{bundle}} = \frac{\text{ARPU}_{\text{bundle}} \times \text{Gross Margin}}{\text{Churn}_{\text{bundle}}}$ vs. $\text{LTV}_{\text{single}} = \frac{\text{ARPU}_{\text{single}} \times \text{Gross Margin}}{\text{Churn}_{\text{single}}}$
*   **Missing Input:** $\text{Churn}_{\text{bundle}}$ and $\text{Churn}_{\text{single}}$.
*   **Action:** Run an A/B test or cohort analysis on your existing subscribers. Compare the churn rate of members who receive mixed packs (bundled) versus single-SKU shipments.
*   **Decision Rule:** If $\text{Churn}_{\text{bundle}} < \text{Churn}_{\text{single}} \times (\frac{\text{ARPU}_{\text{bundle}}}{\text{ARPU}_{\text{single}}})$, then bundling increases LTV.

**What Flips It, and the Trigger**
The deciding input is **churn elasticity**. Bundling often raises Average Revenue Per User (ARPU) but can increase churn if customers feel overwhelmed by variety or receive styles they dislike.
*   **Trigger:** If your bundled cohort churn exceeds your single-SKU cohort by more than 15% within the first 90 days, the LTV benefit of higher ARPU is likely negated.
*   **Step this week:** Segment your last 6 months of shipping data by SKU count per box. Calculate the 90-day retention rate for 1-SKU vs. 3+ SKU boxes. If the multi-SKU retention is lower, consider allowing "style preference" filters to reduce churn.
