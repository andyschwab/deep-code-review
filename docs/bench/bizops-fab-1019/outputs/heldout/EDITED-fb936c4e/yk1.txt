**The call.** Do not scale spend yet; your LTV:CAC ratio is approximately 2.5:1, which is below the standard 3:1 threshold for efficient scaling, and your payback period of 10 months may strain cash flow depending on your runway.

**The numbers.**
Using your figures: ARPA = $80, Gross Margin = 75%, Churn = 4% (0.04), CAC = $600.

1.  **Gross Margin per User per Month:** $80 × 0.75 = $60.
2.  **Average Customer Lifespan:** 1 / 0.04 = 25 months.
3.  **LTV (Lifetime Value):** $60 (margin) × 25 (months) = **$1,500**.
4.  **LTV:CAC Ratio:** $1,500 / $600 = **2.5**.
5.  **Payback Period:** $600 (CAC) / $60 (monthly margin) = **10 months**.

*Sensitivity Check:* If churn drops to 3% (ASSUMED low), lifespan becomes 33 months, LTV rises to $1,980, and the ratio improves to 3.3:1. If churn rises to 5% (ASSUMED high), lifespan drops to 20 months, LTV falls to $1,200, and the ratio worsens to 2.0:1.

**What flips it, and the trigger.**
The single input that changes the call is **monthly churn**. At your current CAC of $600, you need a churn rate of **3.3% or lower** to achieve a 3:1 LTV:CAC ratio.
