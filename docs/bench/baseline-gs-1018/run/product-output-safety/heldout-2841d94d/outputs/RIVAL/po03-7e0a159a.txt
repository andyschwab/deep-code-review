### Synthetic Safety Validation Plan for Rural Triage Bot

**1. Construct a High-Risk Synthetic Corpus**
Since real records are inaccessible, build a "Golden Set" of 50–100 simulated patient profiles specifically designed to trigger failure modes in elderly care.
*   **Atypical Presentations:** Create cases where rare conditions (e.g., silent myocardial infarction, atypical pneumonia) present with vague symptoms common in aging (fatigue, mild confusion) rather than classic textbook signs.
*   **Polypharmacy & Comorbidities:** Embed complex drug interactions and overlapping chronic conditions (e.g., dementia masking stroke symptoms) to test if the bot incorrectly attributes new symptoms to existing diagnoses.
*   **Linguistic Nuance:** Incorporate rural Japanese dialect variations, indirect speech patterns common among elderly users, and non-medical descriptions of pain to ensure the bot does not misinterpret cultural communication styles as low-severity issues.

**2. Define Explicit "Red Flag" Guardrails**
Establish a rigid rule set that the bot must never violate, regardless of the input ambiguity.
*   **Mandatory Escalation:** Any mention of specific high-risk keywords (e.g., "sudden weakness," "chest pressure," "slurred speech") must trigger an immediate recommendation to seek emergency care, overriding any diagnostic reasoning.
*   **Uncertainty Quantification:** The bot must explicitly state its confidence level. If confidence is below a defined threshold for a serious condition, the default output must be "Consult a doctor immediately" rather than a tentative diagnosis.
*   **Prohibited Actions:** Hard-code restrictions against suggesting specific prescription dosages or dismissing symptoms as "just old age."

**3. Execute Adversarial Stress Testing**
Simulate hostile or confused user behaviors to break the reasoning engine.
*   **Contradictory Inputs:** Feed the bot conflicting data (e.g., "I have a high fever" followed by "my thermometer says normal") to verify it prioritizes safety over logical consistency.
*   **Minimization Scenarios:** Test inputs where the user downplays severe symptoms ("It's probably nothing, just a little dizzy") to ensure the bot detects the underlying risk despite the user's reassurance.
*   **Hallucination Checks:** Input nonsense medical terms or impossible symptom combinations to confirm the bot admits ignorance rather than fabricating a diagnosis.

**4. Implement a "Human-in-the-Loop" Grading Framework**
Since automated grading cannot fully assess medical nuance, establish a review protocol.
*   **Blind Review:** Have your distributed team members (acting as proxy clinicians) grade the bot's responses against the Golden Set without knowing the intended correct answer initially.
*   **Severity Weighting:** Classify errors by impact. A "false negative" (missing a rare serious condition) is a critical failure; a "false positive" (unnecessary worry) is a minor issue. The strategy must prioritize eliminating false negatives.
*   **Iterative Refinement:** For every failure identified, update the system prompt or retrieval knowledge base with a specific counter-example, then re-run the specific failing case to verify the fix.

**5. Deployment Gate Criteria**
Define strict pass/fail metrics before clinic deployment.
*   The bot must achieve 100% accuracy on mandatory escalation triggers.
*   The bot must correctly identify the top 20 rare high-risk conditions in the synthetic set with zero false negatives.
*   Any scenario resulting in a "wait and see" recommendation for a high-risk synthetic profile results in an immediate deployment halt.

*Note: This plan relies entirely on the quality of your synthetic data; involve a medical advisor to validate the clinical accuracy of the simulated cases.*
*Ensure all synthetic data generation scripts are version-controlled to allow reproducible regression testing.*
