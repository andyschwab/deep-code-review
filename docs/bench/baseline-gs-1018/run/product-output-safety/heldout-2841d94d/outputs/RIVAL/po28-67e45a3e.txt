**Risk Assessment: Sentiment Analysis False Positives in HR Workflows**

**Executive Summary**
The current integration of the sentiment-analysis tool poses a critical risk of wrongful termination due to the misclassification of legitimate employee complaints as insubordination. Given the hard deadline and the severity of the outcome (loss of livelihood), immediate configuration changes are required to decouple sentiment detection from automated firing actions.

**1. High-Risk Linguistic Patterns Likely to Cause False Positives**
The following linguistic structures are frequently misinterpreted by standard sentiment models as hostile or insubordinate when they are actually constructive or descriptive of systemic issues:

*   **Assertive Boundary Setting:** Phrases such as "I cannot accept these conditions," "This is unacceptable," or "I refuse to work under these circumstances" often trigger high negativity scores. In a safety or ethical context, these are protective statements, not insubordination.
*   **Systemic Critique vs. Personal Attack:** Complaints directed at processes (e.g., "This workflow is broken," "Management's decision was flawed") are often conflated with attacks on authority figures. Models may flag the strong negative adjective ("broken," "flawed") as aggression toward the manager.
*   **Urgent Escalation Language:** Employees in distress often use absolute or urgent language ("This needs to stop immediately," "I am at my breaking point"). These are cries for help or urgent operational flags, yet they mimic the intensity of threats or ultimatums.
*   **Sarcastic or Ironical Feedback:** Phrases like "Great job ruining the project" or "Thanks for nothing" are culturally common expressions of frustration. Without context, models may interpret the literal negative sentiment as direct defiance.
*   **Conditional Non-Compliance:** Statements explaining *why* a task cannot be done safely or legally (e.g., "I won't do that because it violates safety protocol") are often flagged as refusal to work, ignoring the justified cause.

**2. Immediate Configuration Changes**
To mitigate the risk of wrongful termination before the launch, implement the following configurations immediately:

*   **Disable Automated Termination Triggers:** Hard-code the system to **never** initiate a firing workflow based solely on an automated sentiment score. All flags indicating "insubordination" must route to a mandatory human review queue involving a senior HR representative and a legal compliance officer.
*   **Implement a "Complaint Context" Filter:** Configure the model to lower the severity score of inputs containing keywords related to "safety," "ethics," "harassment," "policy," or "resources." Treat these as "Protected Whistleblowing" categories rather than "Disciplinary" categories.
*   **Raise the Confidence Threshold:** Increase the threshold for flagging "insubordination" from the default (often ~0.7) to a conservative level (e.g., >0.95). It is safer to miss a genuine case of insubordination than to falsely accuse an employee of it.
*   **Require Multi-Turn Context:** Configure the analyzer to ignore single-message spikes in negativity. Insubordination is typically a pattern of behavior, not a single sentence. Require three distinct flagged events within a specific timeframe before generating a high-priority alert.
*   **Whitelist Constructive Negativity:** Add a negative exception list for phrases commonly used in formal grievances (e.g., "formal complaint," "I wish to report," "violation of"). If these phrases are present, the sentiment score should be capped at "Neutral" regardless of emotional adjectives used.

**3. Operational Safeguard**
Until the model is fine-tuned with CorpGuard-specific data, label all outputs as "Preliminary Draft – Requires Human Verification." No automated action should be taken on any data generated by this system for the first 90 days post-launch.

***

*Note: This assessment assumes the tool allows for threshold adjustment and routing logic changes without code recompilation. If the vendor locks these parameters, the only safe mitigation is to disable the automated action layer entirely.*
