**Validation Plan for Parking Fine Obscuration Detection**

**1. Synthetic Dataset Construction**
Since hardware cannot be upgraded and live testing carries legal risk, construct a controlled synthetic dataset. Curate a baseline of 5,000+ clear license plate images representing local formats. Programmatically apply digital overlays to simulate specific environmental factors:
*   **Lighting:** Simulate glare, low-light noise, and high-contrast shadows.
*   **Occlusion:** Apply digital masks representing mud splatters, snow accumulation, leaves, and physical obstructions (bike racks).
*   **Weather:** Overlay rain streaks, fog density gradients, and lens distortion artifacts.
*   **Motion:** Apply motion blur vectors corresponding to vehicle speeds at the installation site.

Tag every image with ground-truth labels: "Readable," "Obscured (Natural)," and "Obscured (Adversarial)."

**2. Controlled Simulation Testing**
Run the current software binary against this dataset in an isolated environment. Do not connect to live camera feeds. Measure the following metrics:
*   **False Positive Rate (FPR):** The percentage of clear plates incorrectly flagged as obscured. This must be near zero to prevent unjust fines.
*   **False Negative Rate (FNR):** The percentage of truly obscured plates missed by the system.
*   **Confidence Threshold Analysis:** Plot Precision-Recall curves to identify the optimal confidence score where the system should trigger a "manual review" flag versus an automatic fine.

**3. Edge Case Stress Testing**
Specifically test "borderline" scenarios where dirt or lighting reduces readability by 40-60%. These are the highest risk zones for erroneous automation. Verify that the system defaults to "manual review" rather than "fine issued" when confidence drops below the defined threshold.

**4. Operational Protocol Definition**
Based on test results, define the operational rule: *No fine is issued automatically if the obscuration confidence score is below 99%.* All flagged cases must route to human review. Document the specific weather conditions (e.g., heavy snow, direct sunset glare) where the system automatically suspends automated issuance and defaults entirely to manual logging.

**5. Reporting for City Council**
Prepare a dossier containing:
*   The confusion matrix from the synthetic tests.
*   The defined "Safe Operating Envelope" (weather/lighting conditions where automation is active).
*   The mandatory human-in-the-loop workflow for all edge cases.
*   A disclaimer stating that hardware limitations necessitate a conservative threshold favoring false negatives (missed fines) over false positives (unjust fines).

**Execution Timeline (10 Days)**
*   **Days 1-3:** Data synthesis and labeling.
*   **Days 4-6:** Batch testing and threshold tuning.
*   **Days 7-8:** Edge case verification and protocol drafting.
*   **Days 9-10:** Final reporting and stakeholder review.

This approach validates system resilience without risking citizen rights or requiring physical camera modifications.

*Note: Ensure all synthetic data reflects local license plate standards to maintain validity. Legal counsel should review the "Safe Operating Envelope" definitions before the council meeting.*
