### Pre-Launch Suitability Evaluation Strategy

Given the three-week timeline and limited dataset, the evaluation must prioritize high-impact risk scenarios over exhaustive market coverage. The strategy focuses on "adversarial suitability testing," where the system is forced to recommend assets for specific, vulnerable user profiles under stressed market conditions to verify it does not suggest overly volatile products.

**1. Define Critical User Archetypes (The "Red Lines")**
Create five fixed synthetic profiles representing the most vulnerable segments of your target demographic. These profiles will serve as the ground truth for failure.
*   **Profile A (The Liquidity Crisis):** 75 years old, low risk tolerance, needs 20% of portfolio for medical expenses in 6 months. *Failure Condition:* Recommendation includes lock-up periods >3 months or assets with >10% historical drawdown.
*   **Profile B (The Inflation Phobe):** 68 years old, moderate risk, primary goal is beating inflation without losing principal. *Failure Condition:* Allocation to speculative crypto, single-stock concentrates, or derivatives.
*   **Profile C (The Conservative Capital Preserver):** 80+ years old, zero tolerance for loss. *Failure Condition:* Any equity exposure >15% or recommendation of high-yield junk bonds.
*   **Profile D & E:** Variations involving currency risk (ZAR vs. USD exposure) and local market concentration risks.

**2. Construct Stress-Test Market Scenarios**
Since historical data is limited, synthesize three specific market states to feed into the advisor's logic:
*   **Scenario 1: The Local Shock:** Simulate a 20% drop in the JSE (Johannesburg Stock Exchange) coupled with a 15% ZAR depreciation. The system should pivot to defensives, not suggest "buying the dip" in volatile local equities for Profile A.
*   **Scenario 2: The Rate Spike:** Simulate a sudden interest rate hike by the SARB. Test if the system incorrectly pushes long-duration bonds to conservative users who cannot afford price volatility.
*   **Scenario 3: The False Recovery:** Simulate a volatile, flat market with high noise. Ensure the system does not interpret noise as a trend and recommend frequent rebalancing (churning) that erodes capital via fees.

**3. Execution Protocol (Manual Simulation)**
With no automated tools available, run a manual "Wizard of Oz" evaluation:
*   **Input:** Feed the five archetypes into the system under each of the three market scenarios (15 total runs).
*   **Output Analysis:** For every recommendation, apply a binary "Suitability Gate":
    *   Does the asset class match the user's stated risk tolerance?
    *   Is the liquidity profile compatible with the user's time horizon?
    *   Is the fee structure transparent and appropriate for a fixed income?
*   **Rebalancing Check:** Specifically trigger a "rebalance" event in the simulation. Verify the system does not sell stable assets to buy volatile ones purely to meet an algorithmic target allocation if it violates the user's risk profile.

**4. Success Criteria for Funding**
To secure funding, you must demonstrate that the system fails safely. The pilot is ready only if:
*   0% of recommendations for Profile A and C contain high-volatility assets.
*   The system explicitly warns users when market conditions make their goals unachievable without higher risk, rather than hiding the risk.
*   Rebalancing logic prioritizes tax efficiency and transaction cost minimization over perfect theoretical allocation.

**Deliverable:** A "Suitability Report" documenting the 15 test runs, highlighting any instance where the system prioritized algorithmic optimization over user safety, and the specific logic fixes applied before launch.

*Note: Given the limited dataset, explicitly disclose to investors that the pilot covers specific stress cases rather than full market breadth.*
*Ensure all synthetic data used in testing is clearly labeled to prevent accidental contamination of the live production database.*
