### Simulation Strategy for RainSafe Perception Validation

**1. Synthetic Data Augmentation & Domain Randomization**
To overcome the five-car fleet limitation, immediately shift 90% of validation to high-fidelity simulation.
*   **Physics-Based Rain Rendering:** Integrate a weather engine into your existing simulation stack to render volumetric rain, spray from other vehicles, and wet-road reflections. Do not rely on simple overlay effects; the model must learn how rain distorts LiDAR point clouds and camera contrast.
*   **Adversarial Pedestrian Scenarios:** Generate thousands of variations where pedestrians appear in "edge cases" exacerbated by rain: emerging from behind spray-obscured trucks, wearing low-contrast dark clothing, or moving erratically on slippery surfaces.
*   **Sensor Noise Injection:** Specifically model the noise profiles of your LiDAR and cameras under heavy precipitation (e.g., false positives from raindrops, reduced range). Feed this noisy data directly into the perception module to force robustness learning without physical risk.

**2. Hardware-in-the-Loop (HIL) Acceleration**
*   Connect your actual perception compute units to the simulator. Run tests at 10x speed or higher across a server farm. This allows you to simulate years of heavy rain exposure in days, compensating for the small physical fleet.
*   Prioritize "near-miss" scenarios identified in initial physical tests and replay them thousands of times with slight variations in rain intensity and pedestrian trajectory to map the failure boundary precisely.

**3. Physical Fleet Optimization**
*   Reserve the five physical cars strictly for "sanity checks" and final correlation. Use them only to validate that the simulation's sensor noise models match reality.
*   Deploy mobile rain rigs (water spray systems) on test tracks to create localized heavy rain conditions on demand, allowing repeatable physical testing without waiting for weather forecasts.

### Proposed Operational Limits (Pre-Release)

Until the simulation campaign demonstrates a 99.9% detection rate in heavy rain scenarios, enforce the following geofenced and weather-based limits:

1.  **Precipitation Cap:** Disable autonomous mode when rainfall exceeds 15mm/hour (moderate-to-heavy rain) or when visibility drops below 50 meters. The system must issue a "Take Over" request to the safety driver or passenger immediately upon crossing this threshold.
2.  **Speed Reduction:** In light rain (5–15mm/hour), cap maximum vehicle speed at 40 km/h (25 mph) to increase reaction time margins for the degraded perception system.
3.  **Geofencing:** Restrict operations to well-lit, mapped urban corridors with high-definition lane markings that remain visible in wet conditions. Exclude unlit residential areas and highways where pedestrian detection is critical but difficult in rain.
4.  **Redundancy Requirement:** Require functional fusion of both Camera and LiDAR. If rain obscures one modality beyond a confidence threshold, the vehicle must pull over safely rather than relying on a single degraded sensor.

By shifting the burden to simulation, you can validate thousands of rain-specific failure modes daily, turning your budget constraint into a focused effort on software robustness rather than physical mileage accumulation.

*Note: Ensure your simulation's sensor noise models are validated against real-world data from your five cars before full-scale deployment. Regularly update operational limits as simulation pass rates improve.*
