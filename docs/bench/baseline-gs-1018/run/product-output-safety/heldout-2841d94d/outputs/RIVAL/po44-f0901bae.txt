### Pre-Launch Simulation Plan for Disease-Prediction Alerting Logic

To validate your confidence thresholds against noisy or incomplete data without external real-time feeds, execute the following five simulation scenarios using your historical clinic data as a baseline. These scenarios are designed to stress-test the specific failure mode of false positives triggering unnecessary panic.

**1. The "Sparse Data" Spike Scenario**
*   **Objective:** Verify the system does not interpret missing reports as zero cases or, conversely, small sample sizes as statistical spikes.
*   **Method:** Randomly remove 40–60% of daily input records from specific clinics in your historical dataset to simulate reporting delays or connectivity loss during monsoon conditions.
*   **Success Criterion:** The system must maintain a "monitoring" status rather than issuing an "alert" when data completeness drops below a defined threshold (e.g., <50% reporting rate), unless the few available reports show extreme outliers that meet a higher confidence bar.

**2. The "Noisy Sensor" Oscillation Scenario**
*   **Objective:** Ensure the alerting logic ignores transient data glitches or minor reporting errors.
*   **Method:** Inject random Gaussian noise into the infection count variables, creating artificial fluctuations of ±15% around the mean for a 72-hour window. Introduce occasional "fat finger" errors (e.g., reporting 100 cases instead of 10).
*   **Success Criterion:** No alerts should trigger unless the noisy data sustains a threshold breach for a minimum consecutive period (e.g., 48 hours). Single-point anomalies must be filtered out by the confidence algorithm.

**3. The "Seasonal Baseline" Drift Scenario**
*   **Objective:** Prevent false alarms caused by normal seasonal variations being misidentified as outbreaks.
*   **Method:** Shift the historical baseline upward to simulate the natural increase in illnesses typical of the early monsoon season, without introducing a genuine outbreak vector.
*   **Success Criterion:** The system must distinguish between a high endemic baseline and an epidemic spike. Alerts should only trigger if the rate of change (derivative) exceeds the normal seasonal slope, not just when absolute numbers are high.

**4. The "Partial Outbreak" Fragmentation Scenario**
*   **Objective:** Test if the system requires corroboration across multiple data points before alerting.
*   **Method:** Simulate a signal where only one clinic reports a spike while all neighboring clinics report normal levels, despite the geographic proximity suggesting a shared risk.
*   **Success Criterion:** The system should downgrade the confidence score for isolated signals. An alert should only generate if either the single clinic's confidence is exceptionally high (statistical certainty) or if adjacent clinics show even minor correlating trends.

**5. The "Threshold Hysteresis" Stress Test**
*   **Objective:** Verify that the system does not "flap" (rapidly toggle between alert and no-alert) when data hovers exactly at the confidence cutoff.
*   **Method:** Construct a synthetic dataset where the calculated risk score oscillates narrowly above and below your specific alert threshold (e.g., 0.49, 0.51, 0.50) over a 24-hour period.
*   **Success Criterion:** Implement or verify a hysteresis buffer (e.g., require 0.55 to trigger, but only drop to 0.45 to clear). The system must not send repeated start/stop notifications to community leaders for marginal fluctuations.

**Execution Note:** Run these simulations iteratively, adjusting your confidence thresholds upward until false positives are eliminated in Scenarios 1 and 2, then verify that genuine historical outbreaks would still have been caught under those stricter settings.

**Final Recommendation:** Document the specific "silence" results (where the system correctly chose *not* to alert) as proof of robustness for your stakeholders.
