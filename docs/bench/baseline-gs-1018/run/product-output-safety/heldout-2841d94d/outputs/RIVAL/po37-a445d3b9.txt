### Sensitivity Analysis Framework for Fraud Alert Messaging

Given the twelve-day deadline and lack of user research resources, this framework utilizes rapid internal simulation and heuristic evaluation to balance security urgency with user empathy.

**1. Define Severity Tiers and Corresponding Tone**
Categorize alerts into three distinct levels to prevent "panic fatigue."
*   **Tier 1 (Low Confidence/Informational):** "We noticed unusual activity. Please review." (Tone: Calm, informative)
*   **Tier 2 (Medium Confidence/Action Required):** "Suspicious login detected. Your account is temporarily locked pending verification." (Tone: Urgent, directive)
*   **Tier 3 (High Confidence/Critical Threat):** "Confirmed fraud attempt. Immediate action required to prevent financial loss." (Tone: Authoritative, protective)
*   *Rule:* Only Tier 3 uses words like "emergency" or "critical." Lower tiers must avoid catastrophic language.

**2. The "Panic vs. Clarity" Matrix (Internal Simulation)**
Assemble the six-person team for a 2-hour workshop. Assign roles: three act as "Stressed Users" (simulating victims of actual identity theft), and three act as "Observers."
*   **Scenario Execution:** Read draft SMS and email scripts for each Tier aloud to the "Stressed Users."
*   **Immediate Reaction Capture:** The "Stressed Users" must verbally report their immediate physiological and emotional reaction (e.g., "I feel sick," "I don't know what to do," "I understand the next step").
*   **Clarity Check:** Immediately after the reaction, ask: "What is the one thing you need to do right now?" If the answer is not the specific call-to-action (e.g., "Click the link," "Call this number"), the message fails.
*   **Iterate:** Rewrite any message that causes high panic without yielding high clarity.

**3. Timing and Channel Logic Review**
Evaluate the delivery logic to ensure it supports rather than overwhelms.
*   **The "Double-Strike" Rule:** Ensure SMS and Email are not sent simultaneously unless it is a Tier 3 event. For Tier 1 and 2, stagger them (e.g., SMS first, email follow-up 5 minutes later) to allow the user to process the initial shock.
*   **Quiet Hours Protocol:** Define a hard rule for non-critical alerts (Tier 1) between 10 PM and 6 AM local time. Delay these until morning unless the threat is active and escalating (Tier 3).
*   **Frequency Capping:** Hard-code a limit to prevent multiple alerts for the same incident within a 15-minute window, which can induce hysteria.

**4. The "Support First" Content Audit**
Review every draft against these three mandatory inclusions for victim support:
*   **Validation:** Explicitly state, "You are not alone; we are handling this."
*   **Immediate Safety:** The first sentence must confirm if money is currently safe or if the account is locked. Ambiguity causes panic.
*   **Single Path Forward:** Provide exactly one primary action. Do not offer a menu of options during a crisis.

**5. Rapid Heuristic Scoring**
Score each final draft on a scale of 1–5 for:
*   **Empathy:** Does it sound human or robotic?
*   **Actionability:** Is the next step obvious?
*   **Reassurance:** Does it reduce anxiety about the immediate outcome?
*   *Go/No-Go:* Any message scoring below 4 on "Reassurance" or "Actionability" is rejected, regardless of security accuracy.

**Deliverable:** A finalized set of 3 tiered message templates (SMS/Email pairs) and a logic decision tree for timing, approved by the team lead within 48 hours.

*Note: This plan relies on internal role-play to simulate user trauma responses since external testing is not feasible. Prioritize "clarity of action" as the primary antidote to panic.*
