# Internal Legal Validation Guide for OzWill Prep

**Phase 1: Establish the "Source of Truth" Repository**
Before testing, centralize the rules. Assign one team member to compile the current Succession Acts and Wills Acts for all eight Australian states and territories into a single, searchable digital repository. Focus specifically on sections regarding:
*   Formal execution requirements (witnessing, signing).
*   Testamentary capacity thresholds.
*   Specific prohibitions (e.g., witnesses who are beneficiaries).
*   Mandatory clauses for specific asset types.
Do not rely on general summaries; use the raw legislative text and official government explanatory memoranda. This document becomes the absolute benchmark against which all outputs are measured.

**Phase 2: Create a "Golden Set" of Test Scenarios**
Develop 20–30 standardized user profiles that cover high-risk edge cases rather than simple wills. These must include:
*   Blended families (step-children vs. biological children).
*   Users with potential capacity concerns (e.g., early-stage dementia indicators in the input data).
*   Cross-border assets (property in one state, residence in another).
*   Specific bequests that often fail (e.g., gifts to witnesses, lapsed gifts).
*   Intestacy scenarios where partial validity is tested.
For each profile, manually draft the *correct* legal outcome based on your Phase 1 repository. This creates your answer key.

**Phase 3: The Deterministic Rule-Check Layer**
Implement a non-AI, code-based validation layer that runs before any document is shown to a user. This script should hard-code the binary rules that cannot be subjective, such as:
*   "If State = NSW and Witness = Beneficiary, flag as FATAL ERROR."
*   "If Age < 18 and no marriage exemption, flag as FATAL ERROR."
*   "If signature block does not match State X statutory wording, block generation."
This layer acts as a guardrail to catch clear statutory violations that the generative model might miss or hallucinate around.

**Phase 4: Blind Comparative Audit**
Run your "Golden Set" inputs through the generator. Have two team members independently compare the generated clauses against your manual "answer key" from Phase 2. They must not know which specific test case is running to avoid bias.
*   **Marking Criteria:** Any deviation from the statutory requirement is a "Fail." Any ambiguity that a layperson could misinterpret is a "Warning."
*   **Documentation:** Log every failure with the specific section of the Act it violates. Do not fix the prompt yet; just catalog the failure modes.

**Phase 5: The "Layperson" Capacity Simulation**
Since your users are seniors who may lack legal capacity to spot errors, recruit five individuals outside the legal tech space (ideally matching your user demographic) to review the *output* documents. Ask them only: "Is anything confusing?" and "Does this match what you asked for?" If they cannot identify the beneficiary or the executor clearly, the clause is too complex and risks being challenged on capacity grounds later. Simplify any language that triggers confusion.

**Phase 6: Iterative Prompt Confinement**
Based on the failures in Phase 4, rewrite your system instructions to explicitly forbid the identified error patterns. Instead of asking the AI to "write a valid will," instruct it to "apply [Specific State] Section [X] regarding witnesses." Re-run the Golden Set. Repeat this cycle until the pass rate is 100% on binary rules and 95%+ on complex scenarios.

**Phase 7: The "Human-in-the-Loop" Launch Protocol**
For the first three months post-launch, mandate that every generated will be reviewed by a qualified internal team member against a simplified checklist derived from Phase 3 before it is finalized. Do not fully automate the final delivery until you have processed 100+ live cases without a critical error.

**Note:** This process mitigates risk but does not replace professional legal liability coverage; ensure your terms of service clearly state the tool provides drafting assistance, not legal advice. Prioritize updating the Phase 1 repository immediately if any state legislation changes during your launch window.
