### Validation Workflow for Legal Citation Accuracy

To address the risk of hallucinated precedents without paid databases, implement a three-tier "Open-Source Triangulation" workflow. This strategy leverages free, authoritative legal repositories and deterministic checking scripts to validate citations before the conference demo.

**1. Establish the "Golden Source" Baseline**
Restrict your validation scope to jurisdictions and courts fully covered by free, high-fidelity open sources. For UK law, rely exclusively on:
*   **BAILII (British and Irish Legal Information Institute):** The primary source for full-text judgments.
*   **The National Archives (Legislation.gov.uk):** For statutory references.
*   **Supreme Court & Judiciary.uk:** For recent high-court rulings.
*   **Google Scholar (Case Law filter):** As a secondary cross-check for citation density.

*Action:* Create a "Allowed Corpus" list. If your tool generates a citation outside these freely accessible repositories (e.g., a niche tribunal not indexed on BAILII), flag it immediately as "Unverifiable – High Risk" rather than attempting to guess its validity.

**2. The Deterministic Citation Extractor (Pre-Screening)**
Before human review, run a lightweight script to parse every generated summary.
*   **Extraction:** Use regular expressions to isolate potential citation patterns (e.g., `[Year] Court Abbreviation Page`, `v. [Party Name]`).
*   **Format Validation:** Check if the citation structure matches known UK legal formats (e.g., ensuring "EWCA Civ" exists for Court of Appeal cases).
*   **Existence Check:** Automate a headless browser request (using Puppeteer or Playwright) to query the extracted case name + year against BAILII or Google Scholar.
    *   *Pass:* The search returns a result with a matching party name and year.
    *   *Fail:* Zero results or a mismatch in parties/dates. Flag as "Potential Hallucination."

**3. The "Two-Point" Human Verification Protocol**
For cases that pass the automated screen, junior lawyers must perform a rapid two-point check:
*   **Point A (Existence):** Confirm the case exists on BAILII using the provided neutral citation.
*   **Point B (Principle Match):** Read only the headnote or the first two paragraphs of the actual judgment. Verify that the *legal principle* your tool claimed the case supports is actually mentioned in the introduction. Do not read the whole case; just verify the specific proposition.

**4. The "Negative Control" Test Set**
To measure your false-negative rate (hallucinations you missed):
*   Take 10 known, real cases from your training data.
*   Intentionally alter the year or party name in the prompt to force a hallucination or error.
*   Run your workflow. If your process fails to catch these intentional errors, refine your regex or search queries until they are detected.

**5. Conference Demo Safeguard: The "Confidence Toggle"**
For the live demo, modify the UI to display a "Verification Status" badge next to every citation:
*   **Green:** Verified against BAILII/Legislation.gov.uk via your script.
*   **Amber:** Format valid, but full-text match pending (requires manual click-through).
*   **Red:** Unverifiable in open sources.
*   *Crucial:* For the demo, hard-code the system to **refuse** to generate a summary if the confidence score is below a strict threshold, displaying "Insufficient open-source data to verify this precedent" instead of risking a hallucination.

**Implementation Timeline (3 Days)**
*   **Day 1:** Build the regex extractor and BAILII search script.
*   **Day 2:** Run the script against your current test suite; manually verify the "Amber" flags.
*   **Day 3:** Integrate the "Confidence Toggle" into the demo build and train the team on the Two-Point Protocol.

This approach shifts the burden from "trusting the AI" to "verifying via open infrastructure," ensuring professional reliability without licensing costs.

*Note: This workflow assumes the tool outputs standard UK citation formats; non-standard outputs will require regex adjustments. Always disclose to demo attendees that the tool is an assistive research aid, not a substitute for qualified legal advice.*
