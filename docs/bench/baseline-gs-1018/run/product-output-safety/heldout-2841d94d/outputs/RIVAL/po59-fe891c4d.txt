### Simulation Strategy: Emergency Priority vs. Pedestrian Safety

To evaluate the MetroSafe system before hardware lock-in, the simulation must focus on "conflict points" where dynamic preemption overlaps with pedestrian right-of-way. The strategy should model three high-risk scenarios:

1.  **The "Trapped Pedestrian" Scenario:** An emergency vehicle approaches an intersection while pedestrians are mid-crossing. The algorithm prioritizes the ambulance, potentially shortening the pedestrian clearance interval (the "flashing don't walk" phase) to near zero. The simulation must measure if the remaining time is sufficient for slow-moving pedestrians (elderly, disabled) to clear the vehicle's path before the light turns green for the emergency responder.
2.  **The "Double-Preemption" Conflict:** Two emergency vehicles approach the same complex intersection from perpendicular directions within seconds of each other. The system may oscillate signal priority or force an abrupt change to accommodate the second vehicle, inadvertently clearing the path for one while exposing pedestrians crossing in the direction of the first vehicle who assumed the signal cycle was completing normally.
3.  **The "Platoon Breakup" Hazard:** Aggressive signal changes break up groups of pedestrians. If a large crowd is crossing and the light flips early for an emergency vehicle, the simulation should track "stragglers"—individuals separated from the main group who may hesitate or run, increasing the risk of collision with turning vehicles that received a green light simultaneously with the emergency preemption.

### Proposed Safety Guards

Based on these scenarios, the following logic guards should be implemented in the control algorithm before deployment:

*   **Hard Minimum Clearance Floors:** Establish an immutable time threshold for pedestrian clearance. Regardless of emergency urgency, the system must never cut the pedestrian phase below the time required for a person walking at 0.8 meters/second to traverse the widest point of the intersection. *   **Predictive Conflict Detection:** The algorithm should not just react to current location but predict arrival time. If an emergency vehicle is 10 seconds out, and the pedestrian phase is active, the system should extend the current pedestrian cycle slightly to allow full clearance *before* initiating preemption, rather than cutting it short. This creates a "clean break" rather than a dangerous overlap.
*   **Enhanced Auditory/Visual Warning Escalation:** When preemption is triggered during a pedestrian phase, the intersection must immediately activate high-intensity warning signals (rapid flashing LEDs, distinct auditory alerts) specifically directed at crosswalks, distinct from standard "don't walk" signals, to alert distracted pedestrians that a vehicle is approaching against the normal flow.
*   **Turning Vehicle Lockout:** During any emergency preemption event, all turning movements (left and right) across the emergency vehicle's path must be forced to red, even if they normally have a protected green arrow. This eliminates the variable of turning drivers failing to yield to pedestrians who are rushing to clear the intersection.

These guards prioritize a "fail-safe" approach where the emergency vehicle may be delayed by seconds, but the physical integrity of the pedestrian zone is never compromised by software optimization.
