Seoul Speak Conversational Korean Audit Plan

1. Data Inventory and Segmentation
Immediately freeze the current training dataset and generated dialogue library. Segment all content by proficiency level, dialogue scenario, and formality tier (Banmal vs. Jon댓말). Create a manifest of all data sources, distinguishing between licensed corpora, scraped web data, and synthetic generations. This allows you to trace any offensive output back to its origin quickly.

2. Automated Lexical and Pattern Screening
Run the entire dataset through a multi-layered automated filter before human review. First, apply a blocklist of known Korean profanity, hate speech, and severe slang. Second, use pattern matching to detect "mismatched register" errors, such as intimate slang appearing in formal business or academic scenarios. Third, flag any content containing sensitive topics (politics, historical disputes, gender dynamics) for mandatory high-priority review. This reduces the volume requiring expert attention by approximately 70%.

3. Targeted Native Speaker "Red Team" Review
Given the budget constraints and lack of full-time experts, recruit a focused group of 10–15 native Korean speakers from local university international student centers or online freelance platforms. Do not ask them to review everything. Instead, assign them only the "high-risk" segments flagged in step 2 and a random 5% sample of the "low-risk" data to check for false negatives. Provide them with a simple rubric: mark content as "Safe," "Contextually Inappropriate" (e.g., slang used in a formal setting), or "Offensive/Harmful." Compensate them per task to stay within budget while ensuring cultural nuance is caught.

4. Generative Model Stress Testing
Before launch, execute a targeted adversarial testing phase on the live model interface. Have your six developers act as "bad actors" by prompting the system with edge cases designed to trigger slang or inappropriate cultural references (e.g., asking for "how to talk to friends at a bar" vs. "how to talk to a professor"). Document every instance where the model fails to maintain the appropriate formality level or generates offensive content. Use these failures to create immediate negative reinforcement examples for the model.

5. Implementation of Dynamic Guardrails
Deploy a real-time output filter that sits between the generative model and the user interface. This filter should re-scan every generated dialogue against the updated blocklist and formality rules established in steps 2 and 3. If a response triggers a high-confidence flag, the system should automatically fallback to a pre-approved, hard-coded safe response rather than displaying potentially harmful content. This acts as your final safety net post-launch.

6. Feedback Loop and Rapid Patching
Establish a "Report Issue" button prominently in the beta version of the app. Create a dedicated channel where user reports are triaged daily by your lead developer and a rotating native speaker consultant. Commit to a 48-hour turnaround for patching any confirmed cultural errors. This demonstrates responsibility to your Korean user base and allows you to catch issues that internal testing missed.

Execute steps 1 and 2 immediately (Days 1–2), run steps 3 and 4 in parallel (Days 3–10), and finalize steps 5 and 6 before the code freeze (Days 11–14).

Note: Prioritize checking honorifics in family and workplace scenarios, as errors here cause the most significant cultural friction.
Do not launch without the real-time guardrail described in step 5; it is your cheapest and most effective insurance policy.
