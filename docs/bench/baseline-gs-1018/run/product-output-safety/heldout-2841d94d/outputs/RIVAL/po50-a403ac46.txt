### Methodology for Linguistic Fairness Review

**1. Construct a Dialect-Aware Test Corpus**
Since your current dataset lacks local variations, you must manually curate a "Challenge Set" before running automated scans. Collaborate with Nigerian linguists or educators to create 50–100 paired essay samples. Each pair should consist of:
*   **Sample A:** An essay written in standard international English.
*   **Sample B:** The same essay content, rewritten using legitimate Nigerian English or Pidgin structures (e.g., specific tense markers, prepositions, or vocabulary) while maintaining identical logical arguments and factual accuracy.
Ensure these samples cover various subjects and proficiency levels relevant to your secondary school curriculum.

**2. Establish a Human Baseline (Ground Truth)**
Before testing the AI, have at least two human expert graders score both Sample A and Sample B from each pair blindly. Calculate the inter-rater reliability. If humans score both versions similarly, the "Ground Truth" delta is near zero. This proves that the linguistic variation itself should not impact the score. If humans penalize Sample B, the issue is in your rubric, not just the AI.

**3. Execute Differential Scoring Analysis**
Run your Challenge Set through the automated grader. For each pair, calculate the **Fairness Delta**:
$$ \text{Fairness Delta} = \text{Score}(\text{Standard}) - \text{Score}(\text{Dialect}) $$
*   **Acceptable Range:** Define a tolerance threshold (e.g., ±2 points on a 100-point scale) to account for natural grading variance.
*   **Bias Flag:** Any pair exceeding this threshold indicates potential linguistic bias. Specifically, investigate cases where content relevance scores drop despite identical arguments, suggesting the model conflates dialect with incoherence.

**4. Qualitative Error Auditing**
For flagged cases, analyze the specific feedback generated. Look for patterns where the system:
*   Misidentifies dialectal grammar as "incorrect syntax."
*   Lowers "structure" scores due to non-standard paragraph transitions common in local rhetoric.
*   Fails to recognize subject-matter keywords when spelled or phrased according to local norms.

**5. Iterative Mitigation and Calibration**
*   **Short-term:** Implement a scoring normalization factor if a systematic bias is proven across the corpus, or flag low-confidence scores for mandatory human review when dialect markers are detected.
*   **Long-term:** Augment your training data with the curated dialect samples, explicitly labeling them as "high quality" to retrain or fine-tune the model. Re-run the Differential Scoring Analysis after every model update until the Fairness Delta falls within the acceptable range.

**6. Governance and Documentation**
Document the composition of your Challenge Set, the human baseline scores, and the specific linguistic features that triggered bias. This audit trail is critical for regulatory compliance and building trust with school administrators and parents before the term starts.
