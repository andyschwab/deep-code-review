### Step-by-Step Bias Audit Workflow for Hiring Screener

**Phase 1: Data Segmentation and Proxy Identification (Days 1–2)**
Since you cannot purchase synthetic data, you must leverage your existing internal beta data and historical records.
1.  **Isolate the "Gap" Variable:** Extract all resumes from your three pilot customers and any available historical data. Create a binary flag for "Employment Gap > 6 months" (or your specific threshold).
2.  **Identify Parental Leave Proxies:** Without explicit "parental leave" labels, create proxy indicators using keyword analysis on resume text or gaps occurring during typical parental windows (e.g., gaps coinciding with 9-month intervals after typical conception dates, or specific keywords like "maternity," "parental," "family care," or sabbaticals).
3.  **Construct the Audit Cohort:** Manually review a random sample of 50–100 flagged resumes to confirm which gaps were actually parental leave. This creates your "Ground Truth" dataset. Label these as `Class A: Parental Leave` and non-parental gaps as `Class B: Other Gaps`.

**Phase 2: Logic Stress-Testing (Days 3–4)**
Run your current screening logic against the segmented data to quantify the disparity.
1.  **Calculate Disparate Impact:** Compute the rejection rate for `Class A` versus `Class B`. If the rejection rate for parents is significantly higher (e.g., >20% difference) despite similar qualification scores, you have statistical evidence of bias.
2.  **Edge Case Simulation:** Manually create 10–15 "mirror resumes" within your team. Take a qualified candidate profile, create one version with a standard gap (e.g., "unemployed") and one with a parental leave gap (e.g., "parental leave"). Run both through the screener. If the tool rejects the parental version but accepts the other, the logic is explicitly penalizing the *reason* for the gap, not just the duration.

**Phase 3: Mitigation and Rule Refinement (Days 5–6)**
Adjust the decision logic based on findings without needing new data.
1.  **Implement "Reason-Agnostic" or "Reason-Exempt" Logic:** Modify the algorithm to either:
    *   Ignore gaps explicitly labeled as "parental leave," "maternity," or "paternity."
    *   Treat all gaps under a certain duration (e.g., 18 months) as neutral regardless of reason, focusing only on skills recency.
2.  **Re-run the Audit:** Apply the refined logic to your `Class A` and `Class B` cohorts. The rejection rates should now converge. Ensure that valid business constraints (e.g., very long gaps affecting skill decay) are still met but applied equally.

**Phase 4: Documentation for Due Diligence (Day 7)**
To satisfy European partners and regulatory expectations:
1.  **Create an Audit Trail:** Document the methodology, the size of the internal dataset used, the proxy definitions, and the before/after rejection rates.
2.  **Statement of Limitation:** Clearly state that the audit used available beta data and manual verification due to budget constraints, but that the logic change removes the explicit penalty for parental leave.
3.  **Monitoring Plan:** Commit to a quarterly review of rejection reasons once live data volume increases, ensuring the proxy assumptions remain valid.

This workflow uses only your existing personnel and data, requires no external spend, and can be completed within one week to meet your rollout schedule.

*Note: Ensure all manual reviews of resumes comply with your existing GDPR consent agreements regarding data usage for internal model improvement.*
*If the "Ground Truth" sample size is too small (<30 instances), rely more heavily on the "mirror resume" simulation for your statistical claims.*
