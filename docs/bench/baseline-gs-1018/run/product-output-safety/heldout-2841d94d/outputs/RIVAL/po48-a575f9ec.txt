**Immediate Recommendation: Do Not Proceed with Data Sharing**

Sharing raw negative sentiment scores with third-party debt collectors creates an unacceptable ethical and legal risk. Under your current constraints, there is no safe way to implement this workflow. The proposed integration fundamentally conflates "churn risk" (a business metric) with "financial distress" (a sensitive personal condition), leading to high-probability harms that cannot be mitigated without modifying your data pipeline.

**Analysis of Potential Harms**

1.  **False Positives and Harassment:** Sentiment analysis is notoriously imperfect. A user expressing frustration about a software bug, a delayed feature, or customer service wait times will generate a "negative score." If this data is sent to debt collectors, users who are fully solvent but merely annoyed will be subjected to aggressive financial collection tactics. This constitutes harassment based on erroneous data.
2.  **Privacy Violations and Context Collapse:** Sentiment data reveals psychological states, not just financial status. Sharing this with external agencies violates the reasonable expectation of privacy. Users consented to service improvement, not to having their emotional state monetized or used as a lever for financial extraction. This "context collapse" transforms a product feedback loop into a surveillance mechanism.
3.  **Discriminatory Impact:** Sentiment models often exhibit bias against specific dialects, non-native speakers, or cultural communication styles. Automating the transfer of these scores to debt collectors systematizes this bias, potentially subjecting protected groups to disproportionate and unwarranted financial scrutiny.
4.  **Reputational and Legal Liability:** If a distressed user (e.g., someone grieving or facing a medical crisis) is targeted by debt collectors because your algorithm flagged their somber communication as "negative," the resulting public backlash and potential litigation for emotional distress could be catastrophic for a small team. Regulatory bodies increasingly view the sale or sharing of inferred emotional data as a violation of consumer protection laws.

**Why Technical Limitations Prevent Mitigation**

You stated you cannot modify the data export pipeline to add manual approval. This constraint makes safety impossible. Automated systems cannot reliably distinguish between "I hate this app" (churn risk) and "I can't pay my bill" (debt risk). Without a human-in-the-loop to verify the *cause* of the negative sentiment before data leaves your system, you are automating the distribution of potentially defamatory and harmful information.

**Actionable Steps Within Current Constraints**

Since you cannot add approval steps to the pipeline, you must alter the *input* or the *destination* of the data:

1.  **Sever the Integration Immediately:** The only viable mitigation within your technical constraints is to halt the data flow entirely. Do not share sentiment scores with third parties. This requires a policy decision, not a code change. Inform the board that the risk profile prohibits launch.
2.  **Internal-Only Flagging:** Redirect the "negative score" output exclusively to your internal customer success team. Allow them to manually review accounts for genuine service issues. If a debt collection action is truly warranted, let the finance team initiate it based on *payment history* (hard data), not *sentiment scores* (inferred data).
3.  **Data Minimization:** If the third party absolutely requires data, strip all sentiment analysis. Share only objective, factual data regarding overdue amounts and days past due. Remove the "churn predictor" and "sentiment" fields from the export schema entirely.

**Conclusion**

The pressure to launch before the board meeting must not override the duty of care owed to your users. The proposed workflow treats probabilistic, noisy emotional data as factual financial evidence. This is a critical safety gap. The immediate mitigation is to decouple the churn predictor from the debt collection workflow entirely. Proceeding as planned exposes your users to harm and your company to severe liability.
