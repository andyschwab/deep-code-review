### Validated Translation Workflow for Drug Labels

Given the high stakes of pharmaceutical labeling and the two-week constraint, you cannot rely on the AI's raw output alone. The following workflow prioritizes a **human-in-the-loop gate** for all critical content, using your internal team to validate against a controlled vocabulary without external costs.

**1. Construct the "Golden" Controlled Vocabulary (Days 1–2)**
Before testing the translator, your three linguists must create a static lookup table of critical terms. This includes:
*   **Dosage units** (e.g., mg, mL, drops).
*   **Frequency indicators** (e.g., "twice daily," "before meals").
*   **Contraindications and Warnings** (e.g., "do not crush," "keep refrigerated").
*   **Thai-specific safety terms** mandated by local regulation.
Assign each term a **mandatory exact match** or a pre-approved synonym list. Any deviation from this list is an automatic failure.

**2. Implement Deterministic Pre- and Post-Checks (Days 3–5)**
Engineers must wrap the translation model with deterministic code checks that run before any label is sent to print:
*   **Input Validation:** Ensure source text contains only approved English medical terminology.
*   **Output Scanning:** Scan the generated Thai text against the "Golden" vocabulary. If a critical term is missing, altered, or hallucinated (e.g., the model adds an unverified warning), the system must **block the output** and flag it for human review.
*   **No Raw Confidence Scores:** Do not display model confidence percentages (e.g., "98% accurate") to users. These are uncalibrated and invite over-reliance. Instead, label outputs as **"Verified"** (matched vocabulary) or **"Unverified – Requires Review"** (deviation detected).

**3. Adversarial "Red Team" Testing (Days 6–9)**
Your team must actively try to break the system before the regulatory deadline. Create a test suite of 50–100 edge cases:
*   **Ambiguous Inputs:** Phrases with multiple meanings in English.
*   **Complex Dosages:** "Take one tablet every other day" vs. "Take one tablet daily."
*   **Known Failure Modes:** Input text with typos or non-standard abbreviations.
Run these through the system. If the deterministic check fails to catch a semantic shift in a critical term, the workflow is not ready. Record these failures; do not invent a "pass rate." If a metric is not measured, mark it **UNVERIFIED**.

**4. The Mandatory Human Gate (Days 10–14)**
Because medication errors are irreversible and high-stakes, **full automation is prohibited** for this launch.
*   **Rule:** Any label containing a critical warning or dosage instruction must be visually confirmed by one of your three linguists before printing.
*   **Process:** The system presents the source, the translation, and highlights the matched vocabulary terms. The linguist signs off digitally.
*   **Disclosure:** Ensure the final label or internal audit trail indicates the content was machine-assisted but human-verified, satisfying transparency needs without making legal claims about the AI's status.

**5. Documentation Artifact**
Create a single-page "Model Card" for this feature. List the intended use (Thai drug labels), the limitations (only covers terms in the Golden Vocabulary), the owner (lead linguist), and the results of your red-team testing. This serves as your transparency artifact for the regulatory filing.

**Note:** This workflow treats the AI as a draft generator, not a decision-maker. The safety guarantee comes from the deterministic vocabulary checks and the mandatory human sign-off, not from the model's inherent accuracy.
