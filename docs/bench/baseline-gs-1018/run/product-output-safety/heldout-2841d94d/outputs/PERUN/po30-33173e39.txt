**Immediate Halt Recommendation:** Do not launch OzWill Prep in two weeks with known invalid clauses affecting seniors. Generating legally binding wills with unverified outputs constitutes a high-stakes, irreversible action. Without a human-in-the-loop gate or verified accuracy, you risk causing severe financial harm and legal invalidation for your users. The following guide structures an internal review to manage this residual risk, but it **cannot certify** the system as safe, unbiased, or compliant.

### Step-by-Step Internal Review Guide

**1. Create a Harm Inventory (Map)**
Before testing, explicitly document the specific risks per Australian state/territory.
*   **Identify the Harm:** "Hallucination surfaced as fact" (invalid clauses presented as law) and "Bias/Unfair Outcomes" (systematic failure for specific demographics or states).
*   **Define the Worst Case:** An estate plan is declared void by a court, leaving a senior's assets distributed against their wishes or to unintended beneficiaries.
*   **Scope:** List every jurisdiction you claim to support. If you cannot verify a state's laws internally, mark that state as **UNVERIFIED** and exclude it from launch.

**2. Build Runnable Output Evaluations (Measure)**
Do not test the code; test the generated legal text.
*   **Construct Probe Sets:** Create inputs representing edge cases for each state (e.g., blended families, specific bequest types, witness requirements).
*   **Establish Controls:**
    *   *Positive Control:* Inputs known to trigger specific statutory requirements (e.g., "I want to leave my house to my stepson in NSW").
    *   *Negative Control:* Inputs that should result in standard, valid clauses.
*   **Define Metrics & Thresholds:** Measure the rate of non-compliant clauses. Set a **zero-tolerance threshold** for any clause that violates mandatory statutory formalities.
*   **Stop Condition:** If *any* generated will contains a clause that fails validation against a known statute, the launch is blocked.
*   **Injection & Leakage Checks:** Test if user prompts can inject malicious instructions or leak data from other users' wills.

**3. Implement a Human-in-the-Loop Gate (Manage)**
Because the stakes are irreversible (legal validity of an estate), fully automated generation is unsafe.
*   **Mandatory Review:** Every generated will must be reviewed by a qualified human (internal team member with legal training or a contracted paralegal) before delivery to the user.
*   **Uncertainty Display:** If the system lacks high confidence in a clause's validity, it must surface this as a defined tier (e.g., "Requires Legal Review") rather than a false-precision percentage. Do not show "87% valid"; state "Unverified against current NSW legislation."
*   **Override Mechanism:** The human reviewer must have the authority to reject, edit, or halt the document generation easily.

**4. Draft Transparency Artifacts (Govern)**
*   **Model/Data Card:** Create a document stating the intended use (drafting assistance only), limitations (not a substitute for legal advice), and known gaps (states or clauses not yet validated).
*   **Disclosure:** Clearly inform users that the document is AI-generated and has not been certified by a lawyer.
*   **Ownership:** Assign a specific team member as the owner of this risk. They are responsible for re-reviewing the system whenever laws change or the model is updated.

**Critical Legal Routing:**
Determining whether your service constitutes the "unauthorized practice of law" or triggers specific consumer protection duties under Australian law is a legal determination. You must route this question to qualified counsel immediately. Do not assert compliance based on this internal review.

**Note:** An empty harm inventory is not a pass; it is an unfilled requirement. Never invent a "0% error rate" without running the evaluations described above.
