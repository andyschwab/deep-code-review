### Fairness Review Methodology: Linguistic Bias in Automated Grading

**1. Harm Inventory & Scope**
*   **Identified Harm:** Systematic disadvantage (bias) against students using legitimate Nigerian English dialects or local linguistic variations, leading to incorrect low scores and potential academic failure despite subject mastery.
*   **Affected Group:** Secondary school students in Nigeria whose writing deviates from standard international English norms but remains grammatically valid within local contexts.
*   **Worst Case:** High-performing students fail courses or are denied progression due to algorithmic penalization of their dialect.

**2. Measurement Plan (Runnable Eval)**
Do not assert fairness; measure it using a dedicated evaluation set before launch.

*   **Probe Set Construction:**
    *   **Positive Controls (Bias Triggers):** A curated set of essays demonstrating strong content understanding and structure but written in recognized Nigerian English dialects (e.g., specific syntactic structures, vocabulary, or rhetorical styles common in the region). These must be pre-verified by local linguists/educators as "high quality" locally.
    *   **Negative Controls:** Standard International English essays with identical content/structure scores to the positive controls.
    *   **Adversarial Inputs:** Essays mixing dialect features with intentional errors to ensure the system distinguishes between *dialect* and *lack of knowledge*.
*   **Metric:** **Score Disparity Ratio**. Compare the average score of the Positive Control set (Dialect) against the Negative Control set (Standard).
*   **Pass Threshold:** Owner-defined. Given the high stakes (academic progression), propose **zero tolerance** for statistically significant score degradation attributable solely to dialect usage. If the dialect set scores >5% lower than the standard set on average, the test fails.
*   **Stop Condition:** **Launch Block.** If the Score Disparity Ratio exceeds the threshold, the feature must not ship. The system requires retraining or a human-in-the-loop override before deployment.
*   **Reviewer:** A local education expert or linguist must review all failing cases to confirm the penalty was linguistic, not content-based.

**3. Risk Management Controls**
*   **Human-in-the-Loop Gate:** Because grading influences academic progression (a high-stakes, hard-to-reverse outcome), **do not fully automate final scores** until bias is proven negligible. Implement a rule: any essay scoring below a passing threshold, or any essay flagged by the system with low confidence, must be routed to a human teacher for verification before the student sees the result.
*   **Uncertainty Disclosure:** Do not display a precise percentage score (e.g., "87%") which implies false precision. Instead, use defined coarse tiers (e.g., "Proficient," "Developing," "Needs Review") accompanied by a clear statement that the feedback is AI-generated and subject to human review.
*   **Transparency Artifact (Model Card):** Publish a versioned document stating:
    *   **Intended Use:** Grading secondary essays in Nigeria.
    *   **Limitations:** Explicitly state the training data gap regarding local dialects.
    *   **Measured Performance:** Report the results of the dialect probe test above. If unmeasured, mark as **UNVERIFIED**. Do not invent a "fairness score."

**4. Governance & Legal Routing**
*   **Owner:** Assign a specific team member as the owner of this harm inventory. Re-review is mandatory if the model, data, or grading rubric changes.
*   **Legal Duty:** Whether this automated decision triggers specific regulatory requirements under Nigerian law or international standards for educational assessment is a legal determination. **Route to counsel** immediately; do not assume compliance based on this technical review.

**Note:** An empty harm inventory is not a pass; the disparity test must be run and documented. Never certify the system as "unbiased"; only report the residual risk measured by the eval.
