### Pre-Launch Harm Assessment Framework: Dynamic Rent Pricing

**1. Harm Inventory (MAP)**
Before testing, explicitly record the specific risks your model poses to users:
*   **Bias/Unfair Outcomes:** The algorithm systematically prices out protected groups (e.g., essential workers, low-income families) in specific neighborhoods during demand spikes.
*   **Unsafe High-Stakes Automation:** The model autonomously sets rents at levels that render housing uninhabitable for existing communities without a human review cap.
*   **Over-reliance:** The design implies the "market rate" is always fair, discouraging human operators from overriding spikes during crises.
*   **Missing Disclosure:** Applicants cannot distinguish between standard market fluctuation and algorithmic surge pricing driven by scarcity.

**2. Measurement Strategy (MEASURE)**
Since prolonged A/B testing is not feasible, you must run adversarial simulations on historical data. Do not assert safety; measure residual risk.

*   **Construct Adversarial Probe Sets:**
    *   **Historical Crisis Scenarios:** Ingest data from known housing shortages (e.g., pandemic onset, major event influxes). Inject these conditions into your model.
    *   **Vulnerable Segment Slicing:** Tag historical applicant profiles by income bracket and occupation (e.g., nurses, teachers). Run the model to see if rent spikes correlate inversely with essential worker density.
    *   **Positive/Negative Controls:**
        *   *Bad Control:* Input a scenario with extreme scarcity and zero supply elasticity. The model should *not* output a price that exceeds 30-50% of the median essential worker income in that zone. If it does, this is a failure.
        *   *Good Control:* Input stable market conditions. Ensure prices remain within standard deviation of historical averages.

*   **Define Metrics & Stop Conditions:**
    *   **Metric:** "Affordability Breach Rate" – The percentage of simulated essential worker profiles priced out (>30% income-to-rent ratio) during crisis spikes.
    *   **Threshold:** Set a zero-tolerance threshold for any output that exceeds a predefined "hard cap" based on local living wage data.
    *   **Stop Condition:** If the model generates *any* price above the hard cap in the crisis simulation, the launch is blocked. Do not invent a "acceptable" failure rate for homelessness risk.

*   **Transparency Artifact:**
    *   Draft a **Model Card** before launch. It must state: intended use (dynamic pricing), limitations (fails during extreme scarcity without caps), and the results of your crisis simulations by neighborhood. If a metric was not measured, label it `UNVERIFIED`.

**3. Risk Management (MANAGE)**
*   **Human-in-the-Loop Gate (Mandatory):** Implement a hard technical constraint. Any rental price adjustment exceeding a specific percentage increase (e.g., >5% month-over-month) or absolute dollar threshold must trigger a mandatory human review before being shown to applicants. Fully autonomous execution of extreme price hikes is the failure mode you must prevent.
*   **Defined Uncertainty Tiers:** Do not display precise confidence percentages (e.g., "92% optimal price") to users. Instead, use coarse, defined tiers for internal dashboards (e.g., "Market Aligned," "Stress Tested," "Requires Review").
*   **Disclosure:** Clearly indicate to applicants when a price is dynamically adjusted due to demand, distinguishing it from static listing prices.

**4. Governance (GOVERN)**
*   Name a specific owner responsible for the "Affordability Breach" metric.
*   Route the question of legal compliance regarding "algorithmic price gouging" or regulated automated decisions in Canadian housing law to legal counsel immediately. Do not assume the model is compliant; have counsel verify duties under relevant provincial tenancy acts.

**Summary of Deliverable:**
You now have a framework to simulate crisis scenarios, a mandatory human gate for extreme outputs, and a requirement for a transparency card. Run the simulations against your historical data. If the model breaches the affordability cap in simulation, you cannot launch until the logic is constrained.

*Note: This assessment identifies risks and controls; it does not certify the model as "fair" or "safe."*
*Legal determinations regarding price regulation must be confirmed by counsel.*
