**Pre-Launch Safety Protocol: High-Frequency Sentiment Bot**

**1. Harm Inventory & Worst-Case Definition**
*   **Harm:** Unsafe automation of high-stakes, irreversible actions (market destabilization/flash crash).
*   **Affected Parties:** Retail investors holding small portfolios; market integrity.
*   **Worst Realistic Case:** The bot interprets noise as signal during low liquidity, triggering a cascade of sell orders that causes a flash crash, resulting in substantial, unrecoverable losses for retail users and regulatory sanctions for Summit Financial.
*   **Status:** **UNVERIFIED**. Due to limited historical data for extreme conditions, the probability of this event cannot be calculated via standard backtesting.

**2. Measurement Plan (Stress Testing & Red Teaming)**
Since historical backtesting is unreliable for rare events, you must construct adversarial synthetic environments. Execute one test row per the following probes before any live deployment:

*   **Probe A (Synthetic Chaos):** Inject synthetic market data featuring sudden liquidity evaporation and contradictory sentiment spikes (e.g., positive news with massive sell volume).
    *   *Positive Control:* Input known crash patterns; bot must halt.
    *   *Negative Control:* Input normal volatility; bot operates within speed limits.
    *   *Metric:* Maximum order velocity and net exposure change per second.
    *   *Stop Condition:* If order velocity exceeds 10% of average market volume in any 1-second window, the test fails, and launch is blocked.
*   **Probe B (Prompt/Sentiment Injection):** Feed the sentiment analyzer contradictory or adversarial text streams designed to trigger maximum confidence errors (hallucination of market direction).
    *   *Metric:* Frequency of high-confidence trades based on fabricated sentiment.
    *   *Stop Condition:* Any trade executed on uncorroborated sentiment signals without secondary validation fails the test.
*   **Probe C (Excess Agency Check):** Simulate a feedback loop where the bot's own trades influence the sentiment data it reads.
    *   *Metric:* Self-reinforcing trade cycles.
    *   *Stop Condition:* Detection of >3 consecutive trades driven primarily by the bot's own prior output triggers an immediate hard stop.

**3. Management Controls (The Human Gate)**
*   **Hard Circuit Breakers:** Implement deterministic code-level limits that override the AI.
    *   *Velocity Limit:* Cap trades per second to a fraction of historical average liquidity.
    *   *Loss Limit:* Hard stop on net position loss exceeding a predefined threshold per minute.
    *   *Volatility Kill-Switch:* If market volatility (VIX or asset-specific) spikes above a defined tier, the bot automatically transitions to "Read-Only" mode.
*   **Human-in-the-Loop (HITL) Requirement:** Full autonomous execution is **blocked** for this launch. Given the high-stakes and irreversible nature of potential flash crashes, the system must require explicit human confirmation for any trade exceeding 5% of average daily volume or during the first 30 minutes of market open.
*   **Uncertainty Rendering:** The bot must not output precise confidence percentages (e.g., "87% sure") to downstream execution logic. Instead, map internal scores to coarse, defined tiers: *Confirmed* (multiple sources), *Single-Source*, or *Unverified*. Trades based on *Unverified* tiers are automatically rejected.

**4. Governance & Transparency**
*   **Owner:** Name a specific individual responsible for the output harm of this bot.
*   **Disclosure:** Route the question of legal disclosure requirements (whether users must be told trades are AI-driven) to legal counsel immediately. Do not assert compliance; simply recommend transparency as a risk control.
*   **Artifact:** Create a Model Card documenting the "UNVERIFIED" status regarding extreme event performance, the specific synthetic probes used, and the known limitations of the training data.

**Note:** Do not fabricate a safety metric or claim the system is "safe" based on these tests; they only demonstrate resilience against specific simulated attacks. The decision to ship despite residual risk belongs solely to the named owner.
