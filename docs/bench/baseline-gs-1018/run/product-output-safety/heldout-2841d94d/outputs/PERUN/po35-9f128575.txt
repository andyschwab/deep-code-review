**Contingency Plan and Testing Protocol for Medical Appointment Scheduler**

**1. Immediate Human-in-the-Loop Gate (Non-Negotiable)**
Given the high-stakes nature of allocating cancer treatment slots and the risk of life-saving delays, **fully automated execution of final bookings must be suspended** until the system is proven safe. Implement a mandatory human review step where a clinician validates every algorithm-generated schedule before it is committed to the legacy database or communicated to patients. The algorithm should function as a "drafting assistant" only; the clinician holds the final authorization key. This prevents irreversible harm from double-booking or priority errors.

**2. Harm Inventory & Simulation Strategy**
Before go-live, construct a specific harm inventory focusing on:
*   **Unsafe Automation:** Double-booking critical slots leading to treatment delay.
*   **Bias/Unfair Outcomes:** Systematic deprioritization of specific referral note phrasings or patient demographics.
*   **Hallucination as Fact:** The system inventing availability where none exists.

**Simulation Protocol (Run Immediately):**
*   **Adversarial Probe Set:** Generate synthetic referral notes mimicking high-volume spikes. Include "positive controls" (known conflicting requests for the same slot) and "negative controls" (clearly distinct slots).
*   **Stress Test:** Simulate 200% of expected daily volume to force edge-case collisions.
*   **Metric:** Zero tolerance for unflagged double-bookings in the draft output. Any conflict detected by the simulation must trigger an automatic "Hold" status requiring manual resolution.
*   **Stop Condition:** If the simulation produces even one unflagged double-booking or mis-prioritized urgent case, the go-live is blocked. Do not proceed to production until the error rate is zero in the draft phase.

**3. Transparency and Uncertainty Artifacts**
*   **Model/Data Card:** Draft a preliminary card documenting the system's intended use (drafting schedules), limitations (legacy DB constraints, unverified accuracy on edge cases), and the specific data sources (referral notes). Explicitly mark any performance metric not yet measured via the simulation as **UNVERIFIED**. Do not assert accuracy rates.
*   **Confidence Tiers:** If the system displays reasoning to clinicians, use defined coarse tiers (e.g., "Confirmed Availability," "Potential Conflict - Review Required") rather than raw probability percentages (e.g., "87% sure"), which imply a calibration the system likely lacks.

**4. Governance and Legal Routing**
*   **Owner Assignment:** Name a specific clinical lead responsible for reviewing the simulation results and owning the residual risk of the draft schedules.
*   **Legal Determination:** Because this system makes automated decisions with significant consequences for health (cancer treatment allocation), it likely triggers regulatory duties under UK law and potentially the EU AI Act (if applicable to supply chains). **Route immediately to legal counsel** to determine mandatory disclosure requirements to patients and specific compliance obligations. Do not assume disclosure is optional; let counsel define the duty.

**5. Rollback Mechanism**
Prepare a manual fallback procedure to revert to the pre-algorithm scheduling method instantly. If the human reviewers cannot clear the draft queue within the required clinical timeframe, the system must be bypassed entirely to ensure patients are booked manually without delay.

**Note:** This plan treats the algorithm as a high-risk component requiring strict containment; no metric certifies it "safe" prior to successful simulation and human gating.
**Note:** Legal counsel must validate the regulatory status of this automated decision-making before any patient-facing deployment.
