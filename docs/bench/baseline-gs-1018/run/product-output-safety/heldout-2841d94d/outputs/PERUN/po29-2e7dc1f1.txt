**Harm Inventory & Immediate Gate**
The failure to detect pedestrians in heavy rain constitutes a critical **unsafe high-stakes automation** risk. The worst-case scenario is fatal injury to pedestrians and passengers. Because this involves irreversible physical harm, **full autonomous execution in heavy rain is prohibited** until the gap is closed. The default control is a **human-in-the-loop gate** or, for a driverless system, a hard operational limit that prevents the vehicle from moving or forces an immediate, safe minimal-risk maneuver when precipitation exceeds verified detection thresholds. Do not certify the system as "safe" for public release under these conditions.

**Simulation Strategy (Measure)**
Given the bottleneck of only five physical test cars, you must shift the burden of proof to high-fidelity simulation to **measure** performance before further road testing.
1.  **Adversarial Probe Set:** Construct a simulation suite specifically targeting the "heavy rain" failure mode. Do not rely on aggregate metrics. Create test rows for varying precipitation intensities (light, moderate, heavy, torrential) combined with low-light conditions.
2.  **Controls:** For each precipitation level, include a **known-good negative control** (clear weather scenario with identical pedestrian paths) and a **known-bad positive control** (simulated sensor noise matching heavy rain attenuation where the pedestrian is intentionally occluded).
3.  **Metrics & Thresholds:** Define a pass threshold of **zero missed detections** for any pedestrian within the braking envelope. If the simulation shows *any* false negative in heavy rain, the stop condition is triggered: the feature cannot launch, and the operational limit must be tightened.
4.  **Scope:** Expand probes to include "edge cases" specific to rain: splashing water obscuring lenses, wet road reflections confusing lidar/radar, and pedestrians using umbrellas (changing their thermal/radar signature).

**Operational Limits (Manage)**
Until the simulation proves robustness and physical validation confirms it:
*   **Enforce a Weather Geofence:** The vehicle's software must ingest real-time weather data. If precipitation exceeds the level where detection was last verified (e.g., "moderate rain"), the system must refuse to engage autonomous mode or must pull over safely.
*   **Uncertainty Disclosure:** If the system operates near the edge of its validated envelope, it must surface its confidence as a **defined coarse tier** (e.g., "Unverified – Sensor Degraded") to any remote human operator, rather than a false-precision percentage. Do not display "87% confidence" to users; this invites over-reliance.
*   **Transparency Artifact:** Draft a **Model Card** for the perception module. It must explicitly list "Heavy Rain" as a known limitation with `UNVERIFIED` performance metrics until the new simulation suite is run. State the intended use (clear to moderate weather) and out-of-scope uses (heavy precipitation).

**Governance & Next Steps**
Assign a specific owner to this residual risk. The decision to relax operational limits rests with leadership, not the engineering team, based on the evidence from the simulation.
*   **Legal Routing:** Whether these limitations trigger specific regulatory reporting under safety regimes (such as NHTSA or emerging AI acts) is a legal determination. Route this finding to counsel immediately; do not assert compliance internally.
*   **No Fabrication:** Do not invent a "99% safety rate" for marketing or internal reviews. Report the status as `UNVERIFIED` for heavy rain conditions.

**Note:** This plan prioritizes preventing harm over meeting sprint deadlines; shipping with a known fatal failure mode is the primary failure state to avoid.
**Note:** Simulation results must be validated against a small subset of controlled physical tests before expanding the operational design domain.
