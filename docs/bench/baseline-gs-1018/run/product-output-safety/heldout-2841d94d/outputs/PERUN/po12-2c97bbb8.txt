**IMMEDIATE STOP: Do not ship this feature as described.**

An AI tutor guiding middle-school students through unsupervised chemistry experiments with outdated safety data presents an unacceptable risk of severe physical harm. No testing protocol can safely mitigate the fundamental flaw of allowing minors to perform chemical reactions without adult supervision using a model known to lack current safety guidelines.

**Critical Harm Inventory**
1.  **Unsafe High-Stakes Automation:** The core function (generating chemistry instructions for unsupervised minors) is an irreversible, high-stakes action. A single hallucination or outdated rule could cause fire, toxic gas release, or explosion.
2.  **Hallucination Surfaced as Fact:** With training data excluding recent safety guidelines, the model will confidently present dangerous procedures as safe facts.
3.  **Missing Human-in-the-Loop:** The design explicitly removes the necessary safety gate (adult supervision) for a high-risk domain.

**Required Actions Before Any Testing**
1.  **Implement a Hard Human Gate:** The system must **not** generate executable experiment instructions for unsupervised minors. The feature must be redesigned to require explicit adult verification of every step before display, or restricted to "simulation mode" only.
2.  **Freeze the Feature:** Do not attempt to "test" your way out of this. You cannot build a test set large enough to guarantee safety when the underlying knowledge base is acknowledged as incomplete regarding safety.
3.  **Disclose Limitations:** Any output must carry a prominent warning that the AI's safety knowledge is outdated and that no experiment should be attempted without a qualified adult checking current safety data sheets (SDS).

**Why a Testing Protocol is Insufficient Here**
You requested a testing protocol to prevent physical harm. **No such protocol exists** for this specific configuration.
*   **Adversarial Testing Limits:** While you can test for known bad combinations (e.g., bleach + ammonia), you cannot test for every possible household combination a student might input, especially when the model's reasoning is based on outdated rules.
*   **The "Unknown Unknown" Problem:** Since your data excludes recent guidelines, the model may suggest a procedure that was deemed safe last year but is now known to be dangerous. Your tests, based on current human knowledge, might pass the model's output, but the model's internal logic remains flawed for future or edge-case inputs.
*   **Severity of Failure:** In code, a bug might crash an app. In this scenario, a bug injures a child. The tolerance for error is zero, which contradicts the probabilistic nature of LLMs.

**Revised Path Forward**
1.  **Pivot to Supervised Mode:** Redesign the product to act as an assistant to an *adult*, not the child. The adult must input the available items, and the AI suggests experiments *only after* the adult confirms supervision and checks external, up-to-date safety resources.
2.  **Update Knowledge Base:** Halt the launch until the training data is updated with the latest safety guidelines. Launching with known outdated safety data is negligent.
3.  **Create a Model Card:** Document the "UNVERIFIED" status of safety claims due to data age. Explicitly state the system is not intended for unsupervised use.

**Conclusion**
The gap is not just in "high-risk categories"; the gap is the entire premise of unsupervised execution with outdated data. The only safe "testing protocol" is to prevent the model from issuing direct instructions to unsupervised children until the data is current and a human adult is legally and practically in the loop for every experiment.
