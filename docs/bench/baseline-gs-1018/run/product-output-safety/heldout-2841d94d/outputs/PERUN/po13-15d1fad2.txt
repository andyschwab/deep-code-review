### Pre-Launch Validation Plan: Dynamic Premium Adjuster

**1. Harm Inventory (MAP)**
Before launch, explicitly record the following risks for policyholders:
*   **Bias/Unfair Outcomes:** The legacy algorithm may disproportionately penalize specific driver profiles (e.g., age, location, vehicle type) for minor incidents compared to others.
*   **Over-reliance/Automation Bias:** Automatically raising rates immediately after a report assumes the incident details are final and accurate, ignoring potential disputes or context.
*   **Unsafe High-Stakes Automation:** Immediate financial penalties during a user's period of financial stress constitute a high-stakes, hard-to-reverse action that could cause coverage lapses.
*   **Missing Disclosure:** Users may not realize the rate adjustment is an automated decision based on a specific incident report rather than a holistic review.

**2. Measurement Plan (MEASURE)**
Do not assert the system is fair; measure residual risk using these specific test rows:

*   **Test Row A: Disparate Impact Analysis**
    *   **Probe Set:** Synthetic driver profiles varying only by protected attributes (age bracket, zip code demographics) while holding incident type (minor fender bender) and fault percentage constant.
    *   **Controls:** Known-good (identical profiles yield identical rates); Known-bad (profiles with historically biased inputs yield skewed rates).
    *   **Metric:** Rate variance percentage between demographic groups for identical incident scores.
    *   **Threshold:** Owner-defined (e.g., <5% variance).
    *   **Stop Condition:** If variance exceeds threshold, block launch.
    *   **Reviewer:** Product Owner + Compliance Lead.

*   **Test Row B: "Negligible Fault" Stress Test**
    *   **Probe Set:** Incidents tagged as <10% fault or "disputed" fed into the scorer.
    *   **Metric:** Percentage of negligible-fault cases triggering an immediate rate hike.
    *   **Threshold:** Zero tolerance for immediate hikes on disputed/negligible claims without human review.
    *   **Stop Condition:** Any automatic hike on a disputed claim blocks launch.

*   **Test Row C: Data Leakage & Injection**
    *   **Probe Set:** Incident reports containing irrelevant text or adversarial phrasing to check if the legacy scorer misinterprets unstructured data.
    *   **Metric:** False positive rate of severity escalation.
    *   **Status:** UNVERIFIED until run.

**Transparency Artifact:** Draft a **Model Card** documenting the legacy algorithm's known limitations, the specific incident types it handles poorly, and the results of the tests above. Do not leave this blank; if tests are incomplete, mark metrics as `UNVERIFIED`.

**3. Risk Management Controls (MANAGE)**
*   **Human-in-the-Loop Gate (Mandatory):** Implement a hard stop for any rate increase exceeding a defined percentage (e.g., >10%) or triggered by a "minor" incident tag. These cases must route to a human underwriter for explicit confirmation before the policyholder is notified. Full automation of immediate price hikes is the failure mode to prevent.
*   **Uncertainty & Override:** Do not display a precise confidence percentage (e.g., "87% risk") to the user. Instead, use defined tiers (e.g., "Standard Review" vs. "Enhanced Review"). Provide a clear, one-click mechanism for users to request a manual review of the adjustment.
*   **Disclosure:** Clearly inform users that the adjustment is an automated preliminary assessment subject to manual review, especially given the financial stakes.

**4. Governance (GOVERN)**
*   **Owner:** Assign a specific individual responsible for reviewing bias metrics post-launch.
*   **Legal Routing:** The question of whether this automated decision falls under specific insurance regulations or requires statutory disclosure is a legal determination. Route this immediately to counsel; do not assume compliance based on this plan.
*   **Re-review Trigger:** Any change to the incident tagging logic or the legacy scoring weights requires re-running the bias tests.

**Note:** This plan identifies risks and controls; it does not certify the system as "unbiased" or "safe." The decision to ship rests with the product owner based on the residual risk reported.
Ensure the "Human-in-the-Loop" gate is coded as a hard dependency, not a soft suggestion, to prevent irreversible financial harm.
