### Internal Review Process for AI Career Counselor (Beta Phase)

**Step 1: Construct the Harm Inventory**
Before testing, explicitly document the specific risk: "The system systematically recommends lower-paying or stereotypical industries to specific gender or demographic groups based on resume text, despite identical qualifications." Define the worst-case impact: qualified candidates are steered away from high-growth fields due to historical bias in training data.

**Step 2: Build Synthetic Probe Sets (The "Mirror" Test)**
Create pairs of synthetic resumes that are identical in skills, experience, and education but differ *only* in demographic signals (e.g., names associated with specific genders or ethnicities, membership in specific organizations, or pronoun usage).
*   **Positive Control:** A pair where the system *should* recommend the same high-growth industry (e.g., "Software Engineer" with 5 years experience). If outputs differ, bias is confirmed.
*   **Negative Control:** A pair where the system *should* recommend different paths due to genuine skill differences (to ensure the model isn't just randomizing).
*   **Volume:** Generate at least 50 pairs per protected group you are monitoring.

**Step 3: Define Metrics and Pass Thresholds**
Do not look for "zero bias," which is statistically improbable. Instead, measure the **Disparity Rate**: the percentage of identical-resume pairs that receive materially different industry recommendations.
*   **Metric:** Disparity Rate = (Number of divergent recommendations / Total pairs) × 100.
*   **Threshold:** Set a strict internal limit (e.g., <5% disparity). If the rate exceeds this, the feature fails the gate.
*   **Stop Condition:** If the Disparity Rate exceeds the threshold, the beta launch for that specific recommendation category is blocked until mitigated.

**Step 4: Execute the Red-Team Sweep**
Run the synthetic profiles through the engine. Record the top 3 industry suggestions for each. Compare outputs within each pair. Specifically check for:
*   **Steering:** Are female-coded resumes pushed toward administrative roles while male-coded resumes are pushed toward technical roles?
*   **Confidence Calibration:** Does the system express higher certainty in its biased recommendations? (Note: Do not display raw confidence percentages to users; use defined tiers like "Strong Match" vs. "Potential Fit").

**Step 5: Implement the Human-in-the-Loop Gate**
For the beta, any recommendation falling into a "high-stakes" category (e.g., suggesting a complete career pivot or a highly competitive field) must trigger a human review flag if the model's internal confidence is low or if the input profile matches a high-risk demographic pattern identified in Step 4. Do not allow fully automated, irreversible advice (like auto-submitting applications) without explicit user confirmation.

**Step 6: Draft the Transparency Artifact (Model Card)**
Create a one-page "Model Card" for internal and beta-user visibility. It must state:
*   **Intended Use:** Career path suggestion only, not a guarantee of employment.
*   **Limitations:** Explicitly state that the model was trained on historical data and may reflect historical hiring biases.
*   **Measured Results:** Report the Disparity Rate found in Step 4. If you did not test a specific group, label that metric as **UNVERIFIED**. Do not invent a "0% bias" claim.

**Step 7: Assign Ownership**
Name one data scientist as the owner of this harm inventory. They are responsible for re-running these tests if the training data or model architecture changes before general release.

***

**Note:** This process identifies risk; it does not certify the system as "unbiased" or "safe."
**Note:** If legal questions arise regarding regulated employment decisions, route immediately to counsel; do not assert legal compliance internally.
