**Safety Protocol: Autonomous Drone Delivery with GPS Limitations**

**Critical Finding:** You cannot safely proceed with fully autonomous residential drops given known GPS drift that causes landing on private property. Full automation of this high-stakes action (physical intrusion, potential property damage) without a human-in-the-loop gate is the primary failure mode. The following plan manages residual risk but does not certify safety.

**1. Harm Inventory (MAP)**
*   **Unsafe Automation:** Drones landing on lawns, driveways, or roofs due to drift.
    *   *Affected:* Residents, property owners.
    *   *Worst Case:* Property damage, injury to pets/children, privacy violations, regulatory shutdown.
*   **Over-reliance:** Operators trusting the "autonomous" label despite known drift.
*   **Missing Disclosure:** Residents unaware a machine is making the final landing decision.

**2. Operational Controls (MANAGE)**
*   **Human-in-the-Loop Gate (Mandatory):**
    *   **Procedure:** Drones must hover at a safe altitude (e.g., 15m) upon reaching the target zone. The final descent and release command requires explicit human confirmation via remote operator review of live video feed.
    *   **Constraint Check:** This requires staffing, not hardware. Reallocate budget from marketing to 24/7 remote pilot shifts.
    *   **Stop Condition:** If video feed is lost or visibility is poor, the drone must Return-to-Home (RTH) automatically. No blind drops.
*   **Defined Drop Zones (Geofencing):**
    *   Restrict drops to large, pre-verified open areas (e.g., community parks, specific driveway centers) rather than precise "doorstep" coordinates. Expand the acceptable landing radius to accommodate drift, provided the area is cleared of obstacles.
*   **Confidence Tiers (No False Precision):**
    *   Do not display "95% accuracy" to users. Use coarse tiers: **Verified** (Human confirmed visual lock), **Unverified** (GPS only – *Do Not Drop*).

**3. Measurement Plan (MEASURE)**
*   **Eval Design:** Run adversarial tests simulating worst-case GPS drift scenarios (signal loss, multipath errors) in your test neighborhood.
    *   *Positive Control:* Inject known GPS offset; verify drone halts and requests human help.
    *   *Negative Control:* Normal GPS; verify successful human-confirmed drop.
    *   *Metric:* Rate of unauthorized landings (Target: 0%).
    *   *Threshold:* Any unconfirmed landing on private property triggers an immediate pause and review.
*   **Transparency Artifact:** Create a **Model/Data Card** for this deployment.
    *   *Content:* Explicitly state "GPS accuracy limited; all drops require remote human verification." List known failure modes (drift in urban canyons).
    *   *Status:* Mark any untested drift scenarios as **UNVERIFIED**. Do not invent a safety percentage.

**4. User Notification & Disclosure**
*   **Pre-Delivery:** Notify recipients that "A remote operator will verify the landing spot before release."
*   **During Delivery:** If a drop is aborted due to drift, notify the user immediately with a reschedule option.
*   **Legal Routing:** Consult counsel regarding liability for trespassing and local aviation regulations regarding "beyond visual line of sight" (BVLOS) and human oversight requirements. Do not assert compliance internally.

**5. Governance**
*   **Owner:** Assign a specific Safety Lead responsible for reviewing every incident of GPS drift.
*   **Review Cycle:** Re-evaluate the protocol weekly during the trial. If the rate of human-intervention requests exceeds staffing capacity, pause operations.

**Definition of Done for Launch Readiness:**
1.  Human-in-the-loop software gate is active and tested.
2.  Zero unconfirmed landings in the last 50 trial runs.
3.  Model Card published internally and shared with regulators.
4.  Legal counsel has reviewed the trespassing mitigation plan.

*Note: This plan manages risk; it does not eliminate it. If staffing for human oversight breaks your financial model, the only safe path is to delay launch until sensors are upgraded.*
*Fabricating a "safe" autonomous mode to meet the timeline is explicitly prohibited by this protocol.*
