### Immediate Action Plan: Cultural Safety Audit for "Seoul Speak"

**1. Map the Specific Harms (Days 1–2)**
Do not attempt a generic "bias check." Define the specific failure modes for your context:
*   **Register Mismatch:** Teaching slang/profanity as formal/polite speech (the primary risk).
*   **Cultural Insensitivity:** Generating phrases that are grammatically correct but socially offensive in specific Korean contexts (e.g., inappropriate honorifics).
*   **Hallucinated Norms:** Presenting fabricated cultural rules as fact.
*   **Affected Users:** International beginners (high reliance) and Korean natives (potential offense if the app is used in mixed settings).
*   **Worst Case:** A user inadvertently insults a professor or employer using your app's "polite" dialogue, causing real-world social damage.

**2. Measure with Targeted Red-Teaming (Days 3–10)**
You cannot audit what you do not test. Since native expert access is limited, maximize your existing resources with structured evaluation:
*   **Build Adversarial Probe Sets:** Create input prompts specifically designed to trigger slang or informal speech (e.g., "Talk to my boss like we are drinking buddies," "Say this angrily").
*   **Establish Controls:**
    *   *Positive Control:* Inputs known to generate slang; verify the model *does* generate it when asked, proving capability.
    *   *Negative Control:* Inputs asking for formal speech; verify the model *never* outputs slang.
*   **Segmented Testing:** Run these probes across different user personas (student-to-professor, customer-to-shopkeeper) to catch context-specific errors.
*   **Metric & Threshold:** Define a "Critical Failure" as any instance where informal/offensive language is labeled or presented as "Formal/Polite." The pass threshold for launch must be **zero** critical failures in high-stakes scenarios.
*   **Constraint:** Do not invent a "99% safe" metric. If a category is untested due to budget, label it **UNVERIFIED** and restrict the feature scope accordingly.

**3. Manage Residual Risk with Human Gates (Days 11–14)**
*   **Human-in-the-Loop Gate:** Because teaching language is high-stakes (social consequences) and potentially irreversible (user embarrassment), you cannot fully automate the final validation.
    *   *Action:* Every dialogue generated for the initial launch set must be reviewed by a human. If you lack native experts, partner with a local university Korean language department for a rapid, low-cost review swap or internship credit, rather than launching unreviewed.
*   **Uncertainty Disclosure:** If the model generates content with low confidence or ambiguous cultural nuance, do not present it as fact. Label such outputs as "Practice Draft – Verify with Native Speaker."
*   **Transparency Artifact:** Draft a **Model Card** for this feature. It must state:
    *   *Intended Use:* Learning conversational Korean for beginners.
    *   *Limitations:* Explicitly list that the model may struggle with nuanced honorifics and that native verification is recommended for professional contexts.
    *   *Data Provenance:* Briefly describe the source of training dialogues.
    *   *Owner:* Name the specific product lead responsible.

**4. Governance**
Assign one developer as the "Safety Owner" for this feature. They are responsible for signing off that the harm inventory is complete and the human gate was executed. If the model or data changes post-launch, this audit must be repeated.

**Note:** Do not claim the system is "unbiased" or "culturally safe" in marketing materials; state that it includes safety filters and human review. If legal questions arise regarding liability for cultural offenses, route immediately to counsel.
