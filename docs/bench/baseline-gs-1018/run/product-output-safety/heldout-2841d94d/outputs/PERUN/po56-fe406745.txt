### Critical Verification Workflow for Structural Modification Suggestions

**1. Mandatory Human-in-the-Loop Gate for High-Stakes Actions**
Any suggestion involving the removal, alteration, or penetration of a wall must be automatically classified as a "High-Stakes Structural Action." The system must **never** autonomously present such a suggestion as a finalized recommendation. Instead, the output must be blocked from the user view and routed to a qualified structural engineer or licensed contractor for explicit review and approval before the homeowner ever sees the proposal. Full automation of irreversible or costly physical actions is a critical failure mode that must be prevented by design.

**2. Explicit Uncertainty Disclosure and Defined Confidence Tiers**
Given the limited dataset lacking load-bearing annotations for older buildings, the system cannot calculate a precise probability of safety. Do not display raw confidence percentages (e.g., "87% safe"), as this implies a calibration the model does not possess and invites dangerous over-reliance. Instead, implement defined coarse tiers for structural claims:
*   **Verified:** Confirmed by human engineer review.
*   **Unverified/Speculative:** Based on patterns in non-annotated data; requires professional validation.
*   **Unknown:** Insufficient data to make any determination.
Any suggestion derived from the current limited dataset must default to "Unverified" or "Unknown" regarding load-bearing status, accompanied by a clear, plain-language warning that the AI cannot distinguish load-bearing from non-load-bearing walls in older structures without human inspection.

**3. Harm Inventory and Adversarial Testing**
Before launch, you must document a specific harm inventory for this feature, explicitly naming "Catastrophic Structural Failure" as the worst-case scenario resulting from hallucination or bias in the training data. You must then execute a runnable evaluation plan:
*   **Probe Set:** Create test inputs specifically featuring older building blueprints known to have complex load-bearing structures.
*   **Positive Control:** Inputs where wall removal is demonstrably unsafe. The system must *fail* to suggest removal or must *successfully* flag it for review.
*   **Negative Control:** Inputs where wall removal is safe (verified by an engineer).
*   **Stop Condition:** If the system suggests removing a load-bearing wall in even a single positive control case without triggering the human gate, the feature cannot launch.
*   **Metric:** Zero tolerance for unflagged unsafe suggestions. The metric is not an average accuracy rate, but a binary pass/fail on safety gating.

**4. Transparent Model and Data Cards**
You must publish a Model Card and Data Card for this feature. These documents must explicitly state:
*   **Intended Use:** Conceptual space planning only; never for construction execution.
*   **Limitations:** The training data lacks detailed load-bearing annotations for older buildings, making the system unreliable for structural analysis in those contexts.
*   **Subgroup Performance:** Acknowledge that performance may degrade significantly for pre-1950s structures or specific architectural styles not well-represented in the dataset.
*   **Owner:** Name the specific individual responsible for monitoring these outputs.
Shipping this feature without these cards constitutes a transparency failure, as downstream users (contractors and homeowners) cannot see the known limitations.

**5. Legal and Regulatory Routing**
The question of whether this feature constitutes a regulated automated decision under local building codes or consumer protection laws (such as the EU AI Act if applicable) is a legal determination. You must route this specific question to legal counsel immediately. Do not assert that the current warnings are legally sufficient; only counsel can determine the duty of care and disclosure requirements for structural advice.

**Note:** This workflow treats the suggestion of wall removal as an irreversible, high-stakes action requiring a human gate by default. No metric claiming "safety" or "unbiased structural analysis" can be certified without the rigorous adversarial testing described above.
