### Step-by-Step Bias Audit Workflow for Hiring Screener

**1. Map the Specific Harm (Inventory)**
Immediately document the specific failure mode: "Systematic disadvantage against candidates with parental leave gaps." Define the affected group (parents, disproportionately women) and the worst-case realistic outcome (qualified candidates permanently excluded from the hiring pipeline, leading to reputational damage and potential legal liability under EU non-discrimination norms). Do not proceed to testing until this inventory is written down and owned by a specific team member.

**2. Construct Internal Probe Sets (Measure)**
Since you cannot purchase synthetic data, you must construct a "golden set" from your existing internal data and pilot logs:
*   **Positive Controls (Known Bad):** Manually identify 20–30 resumes in your current dataset where the only differentiator is a gap attributable to parental leave (look for keywords like "maternity," "parental," or standard gap durations coinciding with birth dates if available). Verify these candidates are otherwise qualified.
*   **Negative Controls (Known Good):** Identify 20–30 resumes with similar employment gaps explicitly labeled as non-parental (e.g., "sabbatical," "travel," "education") or no gaps at all, with equivalent qualifications.
*   **Adversarial Probes:** Create variations of the same qualified resume where the *only* change is the labeling of the gap (e.g., changing "Parental Leave" to "Career Break" or removing the label entirely) to test if the model penalizes the *label* or the *time delta*.

**3. Define Metrics and Stop Conditions**
Run your screener against these probe sets.
*   **Metric:** Disparate Rejection Rate. Calculate the percentage of "Positive Controls" rejected vs. "Negative Controls."
*   **Threshold:** Set a zero-tolerance threshold for disparate impact on the protected attribute. If the rejection rate for parental leave gaps exceeds the rate for other gaps by any statistically significant margin, the test fails.
*   **Stop Condition:** If the model rejects any "Positive Control" candidate that a human reviewer would have advanced, **block the rollout**. Do not average this out with overall accuracy; this specific failure is a gate.

**4. Implement Management Controls (Manage)**
*   **Human-in-the-Loop Gate:** Until the bias is mathematically eliminated, any resume rejected solely due to an employment gap must be routed to a human reviewer for final confirmation. Fully automated rejection of candidates with gaps is unsafe and must be disabled.
*   **Uncertainty & Disclosure:** Do not present the rejection as a definitive fact. If the system flags a gap, surface this to the recruiter as "Potential Gap – Requires Review" rather than "Unqualified."
*   **Transparency Artifact:** Draft a one-page "Model Card" for your pilot customers. Explicitly state: "Intended Use: Initial screening. Limitations: Known sensitivity to employment gaps; currently undergoing bias mitigation. Measured Performance: [Insert results from Step 3]." If you have not yet run the test, mark the performance metric as **UNVERIFIED**. Do not invent a safety percentage.

**5. Governance and Legal Routing**
Assign an owner to re-run this audit whenever the screening logic or data source changes. Regarding your European partners: **Do not assert** that this audit makes you compliant with the EU AI Act or local labor laws. That is a legal determination. Route the question of "regulated automated decision-making" and mandatory disclosures to legal counsel immediately, naming the EU AI Act as the relevant regime they must verify.

**Notes**
*   An empty harm inventory is not a pass; if you cannot find internal examples of parental leave gaps, your test data is insufficient, and the risk remains UNVERIFIED.
*   Never certify the system as "unbiased" based on this small sample; report the residual risk and the specific controls (human gate) put in place.
