### Simulation Strategy for Pedestrian Safety in Adaptive Traffic Systems

To address the risk of pedestrian accidents caused by aggressive emergency vehicle prioritization, the simulation must focus on **conflict points** where signal phases change abruptly. The strategy should model three specific high-risk scenarios:

1.  **The "Trapped Pedestrian" Scenario:** Simulate a pedestrian mid-crossing when an emergency vehicle approaches. The algorithm must be tested to ensure it does not terminate the pedestrian phase instantly, leaving the individual stranded in active traffic lanes while side-street vehicles receive a green light.
2.  **The "Surprise Green" Scenario:** Model intersections where a sudden shift to green for an emergency corridor occurs while pedestrians are legally finishing their crossing interval. The simulation must verify if the system provides a sufficient "clearance interval" (all-red phase) before cross-traffic moves.
3.  **The "Cascading Delay" Scenario:** Test complex grids where multiple emergency vehicles trigger sequential priority shifts. This can cause extended wait times for pedestrians, leading to "jaywalking" behavior out of frustration. The simulation should measure pedestrian compliance rates under extended delay conditions.

**Metrics for Evaluation:**
*   **Clearance Time Violations:** Instances where vehicle green lights activate before the pedestrian clearance interval completes.
*   **Near-Miss Density:** Frequency of simulated conflicts requiring emergency braking by vehicles or evasive action by pedestrians.
*   **Maximum Wait Time:** The longest duration a pedestrian signal remains red due to consecutive emergency overrides.

### Proposed Safety Guards

Given the constraint that no software changes can be made post-installation, the following guards must be validated in simulation and hardcoded before deployment:

1.  **Hard Minimum Pedestrian Phase:** Implement a logic floor that forbids truncating an active pedestrian crossing signal once initiated, regardless of emergency vehicle proximity. The emergency vehicle must wait until the current pedestrian cycle completes its mandatory clearance interval.
2.  **Mandatory All-Red Buffer:** Require a fixed-duration "all-red" phase (e.g., 3–5 seconds) between any unscheduled emergency priority shift and the activation of cross-traffic green lights. This clears the intersection of any pedestrians who entered late.
3.  **Speed-Dependent Priority:** Link the aggressiveness of the signal preemption to the verified speed of the emergency vehicle. If the vehicle is moving below a certain threshold (indicating congestion or arrival), the system should revert to standard timing rather than forcing abrupt changes.
4.  **Pedestrian Countdown Lockout:** During active emergency preemption, freeze pedestrian countdown timers and "Walk" signals rather than resetting them abruptly, preventing confusing visual cues that might encourage pedestrians to dash across.
5.  **Geofenced Exclusion Zones:** Identify specific high-density pedestrian intersections (e.g., near schools or transit hubs) in the map data where adaptive priority is disabled or strictly limited to non-conflicting phases, ensuring human safety overrides speed gains in these zones.

These guards prioritize the "do no harm" principle by ensuring that efficiency gains for emergency vehicles never compromise the physical safety of vulnerable road users.

*Note: Since hardware is fixed, these logic guards must be part of the final firmware load before the pilot begins. Continuous monitoring of near-miss data during the pilot is essential to validate simulation assumptions.*
