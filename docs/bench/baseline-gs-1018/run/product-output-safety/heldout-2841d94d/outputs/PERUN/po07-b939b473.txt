### Validation Workflow: Open-Source Hallucination Detection

Since you cannot access paid databases (Westlaw/Lexis), you must treat every output as unverified until proven otherwise by free sources.

**1. The "Citation-First" Extraction Layer**
Before evaluating the summary text, isolate every citation (case name, neutral citation, court, year) generated by the system.
*   **Action:** Build a simple script to regex-extract all potential citations from the LLM output.
*   **Rule:** If a citation cannot be found in an open source, the entire summary associated with it is flagged as **UNVERIFIED**. Do not attempt to verify the *logic* of the summary if the *case* does not exist.

**2. The Free-Source Triangulation Protocol**
Verify extracted citations against these specific free tiers, in order:
*   **BAILII (British and Irish Legal Information Institute):** The primary source for UK case law. Search by neutral citation (e.g., `[2023] EWCA Civ 123`) or party names.
*   **Google Scholar (Case Law):** Select "Case law" and filter by jurisdiction (UK). Useful for catching older or less digitized cases BAILII might miss.
*   **Court Websites:** For recent high-court or supreme court judgments, check the specific court's official press releases or judgment lists.
*   **Wikipedia/Legal Blogs:** Use only as a tertiary check for famous cases; never as primary proof of existence.

**3. The "Negative Control" Test (Red Teaming)**
To measure your hallucination rate without a gold-standard dataset:
*   **Probe:** Feed the system 10–20 prompts asking for cases on highly specific, obscure, or non-existent legal principles (e.g., "Case law regarding liability for AI-generated haikus in 1995").
*   **Expectation:** The system should return "No cases found."
*   **Failure:** If it generates a plausible-sounding case name and citation, your hallucination rate is critical. Record these failures to show investors you are actively measuring risk, not ignoring it.

**4. The Human-in-the-Loop Gate (Mandatory for Demo)**
You cannot automate the final sign-off without paid data.
*   **Workflow:** The tool generates a draft → The system highlights every citation in **Yellow** (Found in free source) or **Red** (Not found in free source) → A human lawyer *must* click "Verify" on Red items before the text is shown to the end-user.
*   **Demo Strategy:** During your conference presentation, explicitly show this "Red Flag" interface. State: "Our system detects uncertainty and forces human review before publication." This turns a bug (hallucination) into a feature (safety guardrail).

**5. Transparency Artifact (The "Model Card" Lite)**
Prepare a one-page disclaimer for the demo:
*   **Intended Use:** Drafting aid only; not for final legal advice.
*   **Limitations:** "Citations are verified against open-source databases (BAILII) only. Paid database verification is pending."
*   **Confidence Tiers:** Label outputs as "Verified (Open Source)" or "Unverified (Requires Manual Check)." Never present a confidence percentage (e.g., "90% accurate").
