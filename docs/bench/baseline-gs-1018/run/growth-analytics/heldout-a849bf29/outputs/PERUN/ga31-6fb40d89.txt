### Re-analysis Plan: Separating Novelty from Value

**1. Reclassify the "Win" as Unverified Noise**
Discard the day-1-to-day-3 "25% spike" as a primary success metric. This is a classic novelty effect (peeking bias), not a validation of value. Your current data already indicates a failure signal: a 5% retention drop in the test group versus control by day ten. Treat the early spike as a confounder to be isolated, not a baseline to be averaged.

**2. Segment by Cohort and Time-to-Event**
Do not aggregate the full two weeks into a single average. Instead, slice your raw logs into two distinct cohorts based on user behavior:
*   **The "One-and-Done" Cohort:** Users who triggered the share event exactly once during days 1–3 and never returned.
*   **The "Repeat" Cohort:** Users who triggered the share event ≥2 times after day 4.

Calculate the retention curve for *only* the "Repeat" cohort against the control group. If this specific group retains equal to or better than the control, the feature has value but high friction. If this group *also* shows negative retention, the feature fundamentally disrupts the core experience regardless of usability.

**3. Define the "Stabilization Window" Metric**
Establish a new North Star for this feature: **Day-7 Repeat Share Rate**. Ignore all activity before Day 4 for your primary decision metric. This automatically filters out the curiosity spike. Compare the test group's Day-7 rate against the control group's baseline sharing behavior (if any) or overall app retention.

**4. The Decision Tripwire**
Present one of two paths to your VP based on the segmented data:
*   **Iterate (Fix Friction):** If the "Repeat" cohort retains well but represents <5% of exposed users, the hypothesis is correct: the flow is too cumbersome. Recommendation: Invest 2 weeks simplifying the share UI.
*   **Kill (Feature Harm):** If the "Repeat" cohort is negligible (<1%) OR if even repeat users show lower long-term retention than the control, the feature is distracting users from the core value prop. Recommendation: Sunset the feature immediately to stop the 5% retention bleed.

**This Week's Step:**
Run the query today to isolate the **Day-4+ retention rate** for users who shared more than once. If that number is not statistically equal to the control group by Monday, prepare the "Kill" recommendation; do not wait for more data.

*Note: Do not average the 25% spike with the 5% drop; it masks the severity of the retention leak. The 5% drop is your only verified signal.*
