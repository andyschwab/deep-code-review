### Proposed Operational Funnel for QuickBite Germany (Munich & Berlin)

To validate your assumption that "order-to-driver assignment" is the primary bottleneck, and to isolate drop-offs without relying on French benchmarks, structure your daily dashboard around these five specific transition points. Measure the **conversion rate** and **median time delta** for each step.

**1. Order Received → Restaurant Accepted**
*   **The Question:** Are German restaurants accepting orders as quickly as French partners, or is there friction in the new partnership model?
*   **Metric:** % of orders accepted within 60 seconds; Median time from `order_placed` to `restaurant_accepted`.
*   **Why Monitor:** If this stalls, the bottleneck is partner readiness, not driver density.

**2. Restaurant Accepted → Food Ready (Preparation Phase)**
*   **The Question:** Is the "preparation speed" assumption correct? Do German kitchens take longer to prep than anticipated?
*   **Metric:** Median time from `restaurant_accepted` to `food_ready_confirmed`.
*   **Why Monitor:** This isolates kitchen efficiency. If this duration spikes, your assumption about driver assignment being the *only* bottleneck is false; the kitchen is the leak.

**3. Food Ready → Driver Assigned (The Critical Hypothesis)**
*   **The Question:** Is driver density in Munich/Berlin sufficient to pick up food immediately upon readiness?
*   **Metric:** % of orders assigned within 2 minutes of `food_ready_confirmed`; Median time from `food_ready` to `driver_assigned`.
*   **Why Monitor:** This is your specific hypothesis test. If times here are high while Step 2 is low, your assumption holds: you need more drivers, not faster kitchens. If both are high, the issue is systemic.

**4. Driver Assigned → Driver Arrived at Restaurant**
*   **The Question:** Once assigned, how long does it take a driver to physically reach the pickup point in German traffic conditions?
*   **Metric:** Median time from `driver_assigned` to `driver_arrived_pickup`.
*   **Why Monitor:** Distinguishes between "no driver available" (Step 3) and "driver too far away" (Step 4).

**5. Driver Arrived → Order Picked Up (Handoff Friction)**
*   **The Question:** Is there operational friction at the restaurant counter (e.g., language barriers, confusing pickup zones) delaying the handoff?
*   **Metric:** Median time from `driver_arrived_pickup` to `order_picked_up`.
*   **Why Monitor:** A spike here indicates process failure at the merchant location, not a supply/demand mismatch.

**Implementation Rule:** Do not set absolute "good" thresholds (e.g., "must be under 5 mins") based on French data. Instead, track the **daily trend** of these medians against your own Day 1 baseline. A rising trend in Step 3 specifically validates your driver density concern.

**Note:** Ensure event timestamps are generated client-side or at the exact moment of state change to avoid logging latency skewing these deltas.
**Note:** Tag every event with `city` (Munich/Berlin) to detect geographic variances immediately.
