### Quantitative Framework: Net LTV Impact of Notification Frequency

To advise the CEO, you must move from "order volume" (a vanity metric in this context) to **Net Lifetime Value (LTV)**. The 15% order increase is irrelevant if the cost of acquired users via uninstalls exceeds the margin from those extra orders.

**1. Define the Core Trade-off Equation**
Calculate the **Net Revenue Delta** over your 6-month horizon using this formula:
$$ \Delta Net = (N_{treated} \times \Delta ARPU_{6mo}) - (N_{uninstalls} \times LTV_{churned}) $$
*   $N_{treated}$: 50,000 users.
*   $\Delta ARPU_{6mo}$: The *incremental* average revenue per user over 6 months, not just the 14-day test spike. You must verify if the 15% lift sustains or decays (novelty effect).
*   $N_{uninstalls}$: The absolute count of excess uninstalls (Test Uninstalls minus Control Uninstalls).
*   $LTV_{churned}$: The projected 6-month value of a user who uninstall today. This is likely higher than your average user if your assumption about the "silent majority" is correct (i.e., you are churning users who *would* have ordered occasionally but now refuse the noise).

**2. Correct the Experiment Read (Guardrails & Peeking)**
You observed a 2.5% increase in uninstalls. Before modeling, validate the statistical significance of this guardrail metric.
*   **Check for Peeking:** If you monitored uninstalls daily and stopped or flagged the test the moment significance appeared, your p-value is inflated. Treat the result as **UNVERIFIED** unless you pre-committed to a 14-day read or used a sequential testing bound.
*   **Segment the Lift:** Your hypothesis is that power users drive the volume. Segment the 15% order lift by user tenure and prior frequency. If the lift comes entirely from users who already ordered 4+ times/week, the marginal gain is low compared to the risk of churning low-frequency users.

**3. Model the Scenarios (The CEO Decision Matrix)**
Present three scenarios based on your data, not external benchmarks:
*   **Base Case:** Assume the 15% lift decays by 50% after month 1 (habituation) and the uninstall rate remains constant. Does $\Delta Net$ remain positive?
*   **Bear Case:** The "silent majority" churn accelerates. If uninstall rates double in month 2 due to accumulated annoyance, at what point does the cohort become net negative?
*   **Bull Case:** The extra orders are from previously dormant users reactivating without causing churn elsewhere. (Unlikely given your data, but worth modeling).

**4. Immediate Action Plan (This Week)**
*   **Query 1:** Calculate the *exact* number of excess uninstalls in the test group vs. control. Do not use percentages; use absolute counts for the revenue model.
*   **Query 2:** Pull the 6-month historical LTV for users who uninstalled in the past year. Use this as your $LTV_{churned}$ variable. Do not guess this number.
*   **Decision Rule:** If $(Excess Uninstalls \times Historical LTV) > (Incremental Orders \times Average Margin)$, the test is a failure regardless of the volume lift. Recommend **stopping the global rollout** and testing a segmented approach (e.g., only high-intent users) instead.

**Note:** Do not present the 15% order lift in isolation to the CEO; it is a trap that ignores the cost of customer loss.
**Note:** If the uninstall difference is not statistically significant at the 95% level after correcting for multiple looks, label the churn risk as `UNVERIFIED` but model the worst-case scenario anyway.
