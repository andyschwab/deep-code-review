**North Star Metric: Weekly Active Teams Completing Core Async Workflows**
Define a "Core Async Workflow" as a team where ≥3 members post status updates or upload files within a 5-day window, resulting in a task state change (e.g., To Do → Done). Measure the count of unique teams hitting this threshold weekly. This rejects vanity metrics like total logins or individual message counts, which encourage performative activity without proving value. It aligns with the Canadian tech sector's emphasis on outcome-based productivity over presence, focusing on whether the tool facilitates actual work completion rather than just communication volume.

**Input Metric 1: Async-to-Sync Ratio per Team**
Calculate the ratio of asynchronous actions (comments, file uploads, task updates) to synchronous actions (video call links clicked within tasks) per active team. Your hypothesis states async frequency predicts productivity; this metric validates that assumption. A declining ratio may indicate a team struggling with remote coordination, relying too heavily on meetings to resolve ambiguity—a common friction point in distributed Canadian teams spanning multiple time zones. This helps CS identify accounts drifting away from your core value proposition before they churn.

**Input Metric 2: Collective Completion Velocity (Team-Level Only)**
Measure the average number of tasks moved to "Done" per week per team, strictly aggregated at the team level. Do not track individual completion rates. This respects privacy and remote work culture by evaluating collective output rather than monitoring individual employee activity, avoiding the "surveillance" feel that damages trust in remote-first organizations. It directly answers whether the team is delivering value together. A drop in velocity while async activity remains high signals a process blockage rather than a tool adoption issue, guiding CS to offer workflow consulting rather than feature training.

**Input Metric 3: Cross-Timezone Collaboration Index**
Track the percentage of async interactions (comments/uploads) occurring outside of standard overlapping business hours (e.g., 9 AM–12 PM EST) for teams with members in different Canadian time zones (PST, MST, CST, EST, NST). High scores indicate healthy asynchronous handoffs, a critical success factor for distributed teams. Low scores suggest teams are forcing synchronous habits or experiencing delays due to poor async documentation. This metric specifically targets the unique dynamic of Canadian geography, where remote teams often span 4.5 hours of time difference, making async efficiency a survival skill.

**Guardrail Metric: Privacy-Compliant Activity Floor**
Monitor the percentage of teams with zero recorded activity for 14 consecutive days. This is a binary health check, not a performance score. It ensures you are measuring *value delivery* (activity exists) without quantifying *how much* any single person works. If this metric rises, it indicates total disengagement, triggering immediate CS outreach. It prevents the system from optimizing for noise; if the North Star rises but this guardrail worsens, you are likely measuring performative spam rather than real work.

These indicators focus entirely on team-level outcomes and workflow health, avoiding individual surveillance. They validate your async hypothesis while respecting the privacy norms essential to remote work culture.

*Note: All metrics must be computed using pseudonymous team identifiers; route any raw user ID handling to your privacy compliance protocol. Do not benchmark these rates against external industry averages; compare each account only to its own historical baseline to detect meaningful drift.*
