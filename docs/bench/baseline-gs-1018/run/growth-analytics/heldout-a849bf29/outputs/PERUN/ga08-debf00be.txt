**Primary Success Indicator: Week-2 Retention of Players Who Complete a Cooperative Mission Within 72 Hours**

Do not track guild creation rates or chat message volume as your primary success metric. These are vanity metrics in this context; they measure activity, not value. A player can join a guild and send ten messages without ever deriving enough value from the game to return or pay. Similarly, cooperative mission completion is a strong candidate, but only if tied to retention.

Your North Star must be a **customer-value metric**, not an output metric. For a free-to-play strategy game relying on in-app purchases, revenue is a lagging indicator of retention. Players do not pay for games they abandon. Therefore, the specific metric to instrument is: **The percentage of new users who complete at least one cooperative mission within their first 72 hours and are still active in Week 2.**

**The Logic:**
1.  **Retention Precedes Revenue:** Your monetization depends on players staying long enough to encounter purchase opportunities. If the social update drives mission completion but those players churn by Day 5, the feature is a cost center, not a growth engine. Reading the funnel bottom-up (Retention → Activation → Acquisition) prevents wasting budget on social features that do not stick.
2.  **Validating the "Guild" Hypothesis:** Your assumption is that guild joining drives purchases. However, joining a guild is a low-friction action. Completing a cooperative mission is a high-friction "aha moment" that proves the social loop is working. By measuring retention *conditional* on this specific action, you isolate whether the social mechanic actually delivers value. If mission completers do not retain better than non-completers, the social feature is broken regardless of how many guilds are created.
3.  **Alignment with Financial Goals:** Once you establish that "Mission Completion → Week-2 Retention" is the leading indicator, you can correlate this cohort's retention rate against your known Average Revenue Per Paying User ($14). An increase in this specific retention rate is the only reliable predictor of increased lifetime value (LTV) from the social update.

**Instrumentation Plan:**
*   **Event:** `cooperative_mission_completed` (Properties: `user_id`, `timestamp`, `guild_id`).
*   **Cohort Query:** Users triggering this event within 72 hours of install.
*   **Metric:** % of that cohort active in Days 8–14.
*   **Guardrail:** Monitor server latency and crash rates during cooperative sessions to ensure the new load does not degrade the core experience.

**This Week's Step:**
1.  Define the `cooperative_mission_completed` event schema and ensure it captures a stable, pseudonymous user ID for cross-session tracking.
2.  Run a historical query on existing data: Compare Week-2 retention for users who joined a guild but *did not* complete a mission versus those who did. If the difference is not statistically significant, pivot the update design before launch.

*Note: Do not fabricate a "good" retention percentage; compare the result strictly against your current baseline for non-social players. Ensure user IDs used for this cohort analysis are pseudonymized and routed to your privacy compliance protocols.*
