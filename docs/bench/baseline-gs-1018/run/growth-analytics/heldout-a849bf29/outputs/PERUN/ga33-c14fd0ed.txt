**Proposed North Star: Weekly Active Learners Demonstrating Fluency Growth (WAL-FG)**

**Definition:** A user counts toward this metric only if, within a rolling 7-day window, they complete at least three conversation sessions AND demonstrate a net reduction in repeated grammatical errors compared to their personal baseline from the previous week.

**Why this replaces "Minutes Spent":**
"Minutes spent" is a vanity metric in your context because it rewards rambling and penalizes efficiency. A user struggling for 20 minutes to form a sentence generates high volume but low value. Your proposed "error correction ratio" is a strong *input* metric but fails as a North Star because, as your February pilot revealed, raw correction volume can drive churn if users feel criticized rather than supported. The new North Star shifts the focus from *exposure* to corrections (which frustrated users) to *evidence of learning* (which retains users). It measures the outcome: the user actually made fewer mistakes on things they previously got wrong.

**Implementation Plan (Engineering & Data):**
1.  **Event Taxonomy (Instrument only these):**
    *   `conversation_session_end`: Properties include `session_duration`, `total_sentences`, `unique_errors_flagged`.
    *   `error_corrected`: Properties include `error_type`, `sentence_id`, `user_acknowledged` (did the user retry the sentence correctly?).
    *   `repeat_error_detected`: Triggered when a user makes the same error type within 48 hours of a correction.
2.  **Derived Signal Logic:**
    *   Calculate a **Fluency Score** per user per week: `(Total Errors Last Week - Repeated Errors This Week) / Total Attempts This Week`.
    *   If the score is positive (fewer repeated errors), the user is "Demonstrating Fluency Growth."
3.  **Dashboard View:**
    *   Primary: Count of WAL-FG.
    *   Input Metrics: Average corrections per session, % of users acknowledging corrections.
    *   Guardrail: Churn rate of users receiving >5 corrections/session (to ensure we aren't overwhelming them).

**Addressing the February Pilot Dip:**
The 3% retention dip likely occurred because the pilot optimized for *correction volume* rather than *correction absorption*. By changing the metric to "reduction in repeated errors," you incentivize the AI to prioritize high-impact corrections that stick, rather than nitpicking every minor mistake. This aligns the product behavior with long-term value: users stay because they feel themselves improving, not because they are being graded.
