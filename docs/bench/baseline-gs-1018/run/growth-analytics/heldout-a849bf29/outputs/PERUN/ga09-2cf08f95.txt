**Critical Factors for the VP of Product**

*   **The Peeking Trap:** The temptation to "extend until significant" is dangerous. If you check results daily and stop the moment p < 0.05, your actual false-positive rate skyrockets well above 5%. You must calculate the required sample size for your Minimum Detectable Effect (MDE) based on current variance, commit to that number, and read the result **once** at the end.
*   **Guardrail Metrics:** Before extending, define a guardrail (e.g., time-to-first-class, support ticket volume). If the new flow improves completion but degrades activation quality or increases support load, the "win" is illusory.
*   **Power vs. Cost:** A 2% lift requires a massive sample size to detect with high power. Calculate if the engineering cost of another month of testing and maintenance is justified by the projected revenue of a 2% gain. If the required N is 50,000 users and you only get 3,000/month, the test may be impractical.
*   **Cognitive Load Assumption:** Your hypothesis is qualitative ("reduces cognitive load"). Ensure your event taxonomy captures *how* users fail (e.g., drop-off at specific steps) rather than just binary completion. This diagnostic data is more valuable than a marginal p-value shift.
