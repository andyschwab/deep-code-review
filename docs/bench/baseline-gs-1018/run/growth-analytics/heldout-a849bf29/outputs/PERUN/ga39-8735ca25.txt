**Recommendation: Optimize for "Contribution Margin" with a "Repeat Booking Rate" Guardrail**

Do not choose between GMV and Take Rate as your primary North Star. GMV is a vanity metric in your current context because it rises even when you lose money on every transaction (as evidenced by your 20% GMV growth vs. 5% revenue growth). Conversely, optimizing strictly for Take Rate is dangerous for a two-sided marketplace; if you raise fees too high, you reduce liquidity, causing homeowners to find no providers and providers to find no jobs, eventually collapsing the GMV base.

Your single North Star must be **Contribution Margin** (Revenue minus variable costs/subsidies per transaction). This metric forces the product team to balance volume and margin automatically. It answers your specific strategic tension: a job only "counts" toward your goal if the fee collected exceeds the cost of acquiring and subsidizing that job. This shifts the roadmap from "growth at all costs" to "sustainable unit economics," which is the critical narrative for your upcoming funding round. Investors will penalize a marketplace that scales top-line GMV while bleeding margin.

**Guardrail Metric: Repeat Booking Rate (Retention)**
While Contribution Margin ensures you don't lose money on individual transactions, it does not guarantee long-term health. A team optimizing for margin might inadvertently raise prices or reduce service quality to a point where users never return. Therefore, your guardrail is **Repeat Booking Rate** (the percentage of homeowners who book a second service within 90 days).
*   **The Rule:** If Contribution Margin rises but Repeat Booking Rate falls below your historical baseline, the initiative is a failure, regardless of the margin gain. This prevents short-term monetization from killing long-term marketplace liquidity.

**Why this fits your stage:**
You are past the "prototype" phase (you have $8M GMV) and entering the "growth/maturity" phase where unit economics matter more than raw expansion. The data shows your current strategy (lowering take rate) is inefficient; you are buying volume that doesn't convert to proportional revenue. By switching to Contribution Margin, you stop subsidizing low-value jobs that distort your GMV.
