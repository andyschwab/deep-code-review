Do not optimize for either Daily Active Users (DAU) or average session length as your primary driver. Both are vanity metrics in your current state. Your Day-1 retention of 18% is a critical failure signal that renders acquisition volume (DAU) and engagement duration (session length) irrelevant. If 82% of users never return after the first day, increasing their session time or counting them in a daily total only masks a broken core loop.

Your single North Star for this sprint must be **Day-1 Retention of users who complete the Core Action**. In a fantasy RPG, the "Core Action" is likely completing the first dungeon, winning the first PvP match, or reaching a specific narrative beat—the moment the game delivers its promised value. You cannot determine if longer sessions cause burnout or if frequency drives monetization until users survive past day one. The industry benchmark for mobile RPG retention is significantly higher than 18%; while I cannot fabricate a specific number for your market, your own baseline proves the current experience fails to hook users immediately.

Optimizing for session length now is dangerous. If your core loop is frustrating or confusing, forcing users to stay longer (22 minutes) may actually increase churn by amplifying friction before they find the "fun." Conversely, optimizing for DAU by pouring traffic into a leaky bucket wastes resources. The 22-minute average session is misleading; it likely includes a small group of stuck users or testers, not a healthy cohort.

**Immediate Actions for the Next Sprint:**

1.  **Define and Instrument the "Aha" Event:** Identify the specific in-game action that correlates with users returning on Day 2. Instrument this single event. Do not track "time played" as a success metric yet.
2.  **Cohort Analysis by Core Action Completion:** Split your Day-1 retention data into two cohorts: those who completed the Core Action vs. those who did not. If retention among completers is still low, the core loop itself is flawed. If retention is high for completers but they are rare, the onboarding funnel is broken.
3.  **Stop the "Longer Session" Hypothesis Test:** Do not run A/B tests aimed at increasing session duration. Any result from such a test is `UNVERIFIED` because the population is churning too fast to measure long-term effects like burnout or LTV.

**Decision:** Pause all design iterations focused on extending playtime or increasing daily frequency. Dedicate 100% of the next sprint to fixing the onboarding flow and core loop clarity to lift Day-1 retention above a viable threshold (e.g., doubling it to ~36% as an internal milestone). Only when Day-1 retention stabilizes should you revisit session length or DAU as optimization targets.

*Note: This recommendation relies strictly on your provided baseline data; no external market benchmarks were invented. The "Core Action" must be defined by your team based on qualitative user feedback from the prototype phase.*
