**Proposed North Star Metric: Contribution Margin per Available Car Hour (CM/ACH)**

For UrbanWheels, the single metric that balances operational efficiency with financial sustainability is **Contribution Margin per Available Car Hour**. This is calculated as: *(Revenue per Booking – Variable Costs per Booking) / Total Available Fleet Hours*.

Variable costs must explicitly include the pro-rated insurance cost per hour, cleaning, fueling/charging, and depreciation wear-and-tear specific to trip distance.

**Why This Outweighs Utilization Rate and Revenue/ACH**

Your current debate frames a false choice between volume (Utilization) and top-line yield (Revenue/ACH). Both are vanity metrics in your specific context because they ignore the cost structure that threatens your sustainability.

1.  **Utilization Rate is a Trap:** Your data shows 85% weekday utilization but 70% weekend idleness. If you optimize purely for utilization, you will be tempted to slash weekend prices to fill that idle capacity. However, if those discounted rentals do not cover the high insurance and operational costs per hour, every additional weekend booking actively burns cash. High utilization with negative contribution margin accelerates failure.
2.  **Revenue per Available Car Hour is Incomplete:** While better than raw utilization, this metric ignores your "high insurance costs." A high-revenue hour that incurs disproportionate insurance claims or wear-and-tear costs less profitable than a moderate-revenue hour with low variable costs. Finance is correct to worry about profitability, but Revenue/ACH does not isolate the *variable* efficiency of the asset.

**CM/ACH** forces the organization to recognize that an idle car on a weekend is only a problem if the cost to rent it out is lower than the margin it generates. It aligns the growth team (who want bookings) with finance (who want profit) by making "profitable usage" the only definition of success.

**Shift in Daily Priorities**

Adopting CM/ACH will fundamentally change how your team operates next week:

*   **Dynamic Pricing Strategy:** Instead of dropping prices blindly to fix the 70% weekend idle rate, pricing algorithms will have a hard floor: *Price must exceed Variable Cost + Target Margin.* If demand at that price point is low, the car sits idle. This stops the cannibalization of profitability.
*   **Fleet Deployment:** Operations will stop moving cars simply to increase zone density. Cars will only be repositioned if the expected CM/ACH in the new location exceeds the cost of the repositioning trip.
*   **Marketing Focus:** Acquisition campaigns will shift from "maximize bookings" to "acquire users who generate high-margin trips." You may stop targeting weekend leisure users if their price sensitivity forces margins below zero, and instead focus on weekday commuters who accept standard pricing.
*   **Insurance & Risk Review:** Since insurance is a key variable cost, the product team will prioritize features that reduce risk (e.g., stricter driver verification, telematics-based pricing) directly to lower the denominator in your cost calculation, instantly lifting the North Star.

This metric tells the board that UrbanWheels is no longer chasing hollow growth. It demonstrates a mature strategy where every vehicle hour is judged on its ability to fund the company's future, not just its ability to look busy.

*Note: Ensure your data pipeline can attribute variable costs (especially insurance) to specific trips before launching this metric to avoid estimation errors.*
*Do not set a universal "good" threshold for CM/ACH; benchmark strictly against your own monthly trend to measure improvement.*
