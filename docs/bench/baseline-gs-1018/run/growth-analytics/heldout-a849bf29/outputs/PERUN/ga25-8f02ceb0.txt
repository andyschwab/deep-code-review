### Pilot Success Framework: The "Value Realization" Scorecard

To satisfy divergent stakeholder requirements without diluting focus, do not report a single average. Instead, present a **three-tiered scorecard** that maps specific metrics to each client's definition of success, anchored by a unified "North Star" of business impact. This approach validates the 80% completion assumption while proving value to the assessment-focused client.

**1. The North Star Metric (Unified Business Value)**
*   **Metric:** **Certified Competency Rate**.
*   **Definition:** The percentage of enrolled employees who *both* complete ≥80% of modules **AND** achieve a passing score on the post-training assessment.
*   **Why:** This rejects "vanity completion" (clicking through without learning) and "theoretical knowledge" (knowing the material but not finishing the program). It proves the training actually happened and stuck.

**2. Client-Specific Input Metrics (The "Satisfiers")**
Report these individually per client to address their specific biases, then aggregate them only for the total program health.

*   **For Client A (Completion Focused):**
    *   **Metric:** **Module Completion Velocity**.
    *   **Measurement:** % of users hitting the 80% threshold by Week 6, Week 10, and Week 14.
    *   **Success Signal:** Hitting 80% completion by the pilot end date. If velocity stalls, it signals engagement friction, not content failure.

*   **For Client B (Assessment Focused):**
    *   **Metric:** **Skill Lift Delta**.
    *   **Measurement:** Average percentage point increase from pre-training baseline to post-training assessment.
    *   **Success Signal:** A positive delta >15% (or your historical benchmark). This proves knowledge transfer regardless of whether they clicked every single module button.

*   **For Client C (The Hybrid/Implicit Requirement):**
    *   **Metric:** **Active Application Rate** (Proxy for behavior change).
    *   **Measurement:** If your platform allows, track logins *after* course completion or usage of job-aids downloaded during training. If not, use a "Manager Verification" survey score sent to supervisors in Week 15.
    *   **Success Signal:** Correlation between high "Certified Competency" users and positive manager feedback.

**3. The Funnel Diagnosis (Retention First)**
Do not just report top-line numbers. Diagnose the pilot health bottom-up to prevent false positives:
*   **Retention Check:** Of those who started, what % returned in Week 2? (If this is low, completion goals are impossible).
*   **Activation Check:** Of those who completed Module 1, what % finished Module 3? (Identifies content drop-off points).
*   **Conversion Check:** Of those with high "Skill Lift," did they complete the full path? (Validates if the assessment aligns with the curriculum).

**4. Reporting Structure for the April 12th Review**
Present the data as a **Cohort Analysis**, not a cumulative total.
*   **Slide 1:** The "Certified Competency Rate" for the total population (The North Star).
*   **Slide 2:** Three distinct columns showing how each client performed on their specific priority metric (Completion Velocity vs. Skill Lift).
*   **Slide 3:** The Correlation Matrix. Show that users who completed 80% *also* had higher assessment scores. This validates your 80% assumption as a leading indicator for their success.

2.  **Define the "Passing Score" threshold** with Client B now; do not wait until April to decide what a "good" score is.
3.  **Draft the Manager Verification survey** for Client C to ensure you have behavioral data if platform telemetry is limited.
