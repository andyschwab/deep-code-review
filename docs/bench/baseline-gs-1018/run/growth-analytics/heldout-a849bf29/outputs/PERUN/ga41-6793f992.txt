Based on your data, the current 12% share rate suggests your assumed activation bar is likely too high for an early signal, while the 60% upload rate is too low to predict value. Here are three potential activation definitions tailored to PixelCloud's workflow, analyzed for predictive power and risk.

**1. The "Collaborative Handshake" (Upload + Share Invite Sent)**
*   **Definition:** A user is activated when they upload a project folder AND send at least one share invitation (regardless of whether the recipient accepts).
*   **Behavior Captured:** This isolates the *intent* to collaborate, which is the core value proposition of PixelCloud over simple personal storage. It acknowledges that the friction often lies with the recipient, not the uploader.
*   **Risk:** It may overstate success if invitees consistently ignore or reject invitations. If users send invites to test the feature but never establish a working workflow, retention may not follow. You must track the "Invite Acceptance Rate" as a guardrail metric to ensure these invites result in actual collaboration.

**2. The "First Successful Sync" (Upload + One External View/Download)**
*   **Definition:** A user is activated when they upload a folder AND a second distinct user successfully views or downloads a file from that folder.
*   **Behavior Captured:** This confirms the *realization* of value: the file has successfully moved from creator to collaborator. It proves the platform works end-to-end and the user has established at least one active working relationship.
*   **Risk:** This bar might be too restrictive for your current beta stage, potentially shrinking your "activated" cohort to single digits (<5%). If marketing spend increases based on this narrow definition, you may under-invest in top-of-funnel acquisition because the conversion rate appears artificially low. It delays the signal needed to iterate on onboarding.

**3. The "Repeat Creator" (Two Uploads + One Share Attempt within 7 Days)**
*   **Definition:** A user is activated when they perform the core loop twice (uploading two separate projects) and attempt to share at least one of them within their first week.
*   **Behavior Captured:** This measures *habit formation* rather than a one-off event. It filters out users who tried the platform once for a specific task and left, focusing on those integrating PixelCloud into an ongoing workflow.
*   **Risk:** This definition ignores the "share" component for the second project, potentially activating users who are only using the tool for personal storage. Given your data shows a massive drop-off between upload (60%) and share (12%), this metric might inflate activation numbers with users who never truly collaborate, leading to poor long-term retention predictions.

**Recommendation for the Strategy Meeting:**
Do not select a single definition yet; instead, propose running a **cohort analysis** comparing the 45-day retention of users who hit each of these three milestones. The definition that correlates most strongly with retention *without* shrinking the cohort to statistical insignificance is your true North Star for activation. Until that analysis is complete, treat the "Invite Sent" (Option 1) as your leading indicator for product iterations, but use "External View" (Option 2) as the hard gate for declaring product-market fit before scaling ad spend.

Note: Ensure your event taxonomy explicitly distinguishes between "Invite Sent" and "Invite Accepted" to avoid data noise. Do not benchmark these rates against industry averages; compare them strictly against your own January–March baseline.
