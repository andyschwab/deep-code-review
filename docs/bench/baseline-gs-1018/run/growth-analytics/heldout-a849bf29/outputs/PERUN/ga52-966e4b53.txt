**SwiftRide Geneva Launch: Metric Prioritization & Compliance Strategy**

**1. The North Star: Compliant Value Delivered**
Do not use "Total Rides" or "Driver Signups" as your primary metric; these are vanity metrics that rise even if you are losing your license. Your North Star must be **"Weekly Active Drivers Completing Compliant Rides."** This only increases when a driver accepts a trip, completes it without safety incidents, and adheres to the 8-minute wait time law. If this number stalls, growth is irrelevant.

**2. The Compliance Guardrails (Leading Indicators)**
Since you lack historical baselines for Geneva, do not invent thresholds. Instead, instrument these specific events to detect breaches *before* they trigger legal action:
*   **The 7-Minute Breach Rate:** Track the percentage of rides where estimated wait time exceeds 7 minutes. This is your leading indicator for the 8-minute legal hard limit. If this trends up, you are imminent risk of violation.
*   **Pre-Trip Cancellation Ratio:** Instrument cancellations by *who* canceled and *when*. A spike in driver cancellations post-acceptance signals supply friction; a spike in passenger cancellations due to "long wait" signals demand-supply mismatch.
*   **Safety Incident Frequency per 1,000 Rides:** This is a strict guardrail. Any upward trend here overrides growth targets immediately.

**3. Reading the Funnel: Retention Before Acquisition**
Your assumption that driver retention is critical is correct, but do not measure it in isolation. Read your funnel bottom-up:
*   **Retention First:** Are drivers who onboarded in Week 1 still completing rides in Week 4? If retention leaks, pouring $2M into acquiring 500 drivers is wasted capital.
*   **Activation Second:** Do new drivers complete their first 10 compliant rides? If they drop off before then, your onboarding or initial dispatch logic is failing.
*   **Acquisition Last:** Only scale driver recruitment once the retention and activation buckets are not leaking.

**4. Handling Unknown Baselines**
You stated you have no historical data for Geneva. Therefore:
*   **No Fabricated Benchmarks:** Do not set a "good" cancellation rate based on other regions or industry averages. Compare every metric strictly to **SwiftRide Geneva's own Week 1 baseline**.
*   **UNVERIFIED Metrics:** Any metric you cannot currently instrument (e.g., "driver sentiment") is `UNVERIFIED`. Do not estimate it. Instrument the specific event (e.g., "driver app close event after rejected ride") to get the real number.

**5. Weighting Safety vs. Growth**
Safety incidents are not a "metric to balance"; they are a **tripwire**.
*   **The Rule:** If the Safety Incident Rate rises above your Week 1 baseline for two consecutive days, **freeze all growth marketing spend** automatically.
*   **The Dashboard:** Display Safety and Wait-Time Compliance at the top in red/green status. Growth metrics (new signups) belong at the bottom. A "win" on growth that coincides with a breach in wait-time compliance is a failure, not a success.

**6. This-Week Steps**
1.  **Instrument the "7-Minute Warning":** Define and log the event `ride_wait_time_exceeded_7min` immediately to create your leading indicator buffer.
2.  **Define the Cohort Query:** Build a query for "Drivers onboarded Day 1–7 still active in Week 4" to establish your true retention baseline before spending the full $2M.
3.  **Pre-commit the Experiment Design:** If you test incentive bonuses to reduce wait times, pre-declare the sample size and duration. Do not peek at results after 48 hours and declare a win; this inflates false positives and risks non-compliant scaling.
