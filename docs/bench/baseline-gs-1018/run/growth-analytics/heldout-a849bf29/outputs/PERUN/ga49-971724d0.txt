### Success Definition & Balanced Scorecard for SwiftPay P2P

**North Star Metric:** Weekly Active Splitters completing a second transaction within 14 days.
*Reasoning:* Total volume and raw transaction counts are vanity metrics here. Value is realized only when the social payment becomes a repeated habit, not a one-off novelty. If users return to split again, the feature is sticking; if they do not, the low average transaction value ($12) indicates a dead-end use case.

**Input Metrics (The Scorecard)**
Track these specifically to move the North Star:
1.  **Frequency:** % of users with 3+ transactions in their first 30 days. (Tests the "habit" hypothesis).
2.  **Value Progression:** Median transaction value of the *second* and *third* transaction per user compared to the first. (Tests if small entries lead to larger bills).
3.  **Retention Cohort:** Day-30 retention of users who performed a split vs. users who only viewed the feature.
4.  **Activation Rate:** % of users who invite at least one new recipient within 48 hours of signup. (Social features die without network effects).

**User Segmentation Strategy**
Do not analyze all 50,000 transactions as one block. Segment by:
*   **The "Coffee" Cohort:** Users with >4 transactions but average value <$15. Analyze if their total monthly volume eventually rivals traditional transfer users.
*   **The "Bill" Cohort:** Users with <2 transactions but average value >$40. Analyze drop-off after the first large split; did they fail to make it a habit?
*   **The "Bridge" Users:** Users who started in the "Coffee" cohort and migrated to values >$30 within 60 days. This is your success pattern; isolate their behavior.

**Critical Compliance & Risk Note**
For risk officers, add a guardrail metric: **Failed/Disputed Transaction Rate by Segment**. High-frequency small transactions may attract different fraud patterns than large transfers. Ensure your event taxonomy captures the *recipient relationship* (new vs. recurring) to detect structuring or money-muling behaviors early.

**Identifier Seam:** Ensure these cohorts track a stable, pseudonymous user ID across sessions to follow the "Coffee" to "Bill" journey, while routing any PII (names, phone numbers) strictly to your privacy compliance systems. Do not use raw PII for analytics queries.

**This Week's Steps**
1.  **Instrument:** Define the `split_completed` event with properties `transaction_value`, `recipient_is_new`, and `user_cohort_date`. Do not instrument "page views" of the split screen yet; they answer no specific question.
2.  **Query:** Run a cohort analysis comparing the Day-30 retention of the "Coffee" cohort vs. the "Bill" cohort.
3.  **Decide:** If the "Coffee" cohort shows 20% higher Day-30 retention than the "Bill" cohort, prioritize features that increase frequency (e.g., recurring splits). If retention is flat for both, the low value is a profitability leak, and you must pivot to driving larger transaction sizes immediately.

*Note: No external benchmarks for "good" retention are used here; success is defined strictly by SwiftPay's internal baseline trends. The $12 vs $45 comparison is a hypothesis to be validated by the "Value Progression" metric, not a verdict.*
