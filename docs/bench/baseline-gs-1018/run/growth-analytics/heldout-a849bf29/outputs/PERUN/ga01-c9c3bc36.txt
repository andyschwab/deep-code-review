**Recommendation: Adopt "Percentage of Completed Prescriptions" as your North Star Metric.**

For SwasthConnect, this metric directly measures the core value promise: improved health outcomes through medication adherence. The alternative, "number of follow-up video calls," is a vanity metric in your specific context. Given the spotty internet connectivity and 4-minute average session duration in rural Maharashtra and Karnataka, video calls are a fragile proxy for care. A patient might complete their treatment via SMS reminders, voice notes, or in-person clinic visits facilitated by your platform, none of which would be captured if you only count successful video streams. Conversely, a high volume of video calls could occur while patients still fail to finish their medication, creating a false sense of success while the actual health outcome deteriorates.

**Trade-off Analysis:**

*   **Completed Prescriptions (Recommended):**
    *   *Pros:* It aligns perfectly with your assumption that health outcomes depend on finishing the course. It is outcome-oriented rather than output-oriented. It remains valid regardless of the communication channel used (video, audio, text), making it resilient to your infrastructure constraints.
    *   *Cons:* It is a lagging indicator. You will not know a prescription is "completed" until the full course duration has passed. It requires reliable data entry from clinics or patients to mark the status, which demands discipline in your operational workflow rather than just passive tracking.

*   **Follow-up Video Calls:**
    *   *Pros:* It is an immediate, real-time signal of engagement. It is easier to automate tracking technically since it relies on server handshake logs.
    *   *Cons:* It is heavily biased against your target demographic. In areas with poor connectivity, a low call count may reflect network failure rather than a lack of patient need or provider effort. Optimizing for this metric might inadvertently push your team to prioritize users with better internet over those with the greatest medical need, or to force video interactions where simpler, more reliable methods would suffice. It measures "platform usage," not "health improvement."

For your Series A board meeting, investors will prioritize evidence that your model delivers actual health results, not just technical activity. "Completed Prescriptions" proves you are solving the medical problem; "Video Calls" only proves you are running a video service. Given your pilot size of 1,200 users, you have sufficient data to establish a baseline for completion rates immediately.

**Note:** Ensure your data collection method for prescription completion is robust against offline gaps, perhaps relying on clinic-side verification rather than patient-side app events. Do not fabricate industry benchmark percentages for completion rates; present your own pilot trend line to the board as the sole baseline for future growth.
