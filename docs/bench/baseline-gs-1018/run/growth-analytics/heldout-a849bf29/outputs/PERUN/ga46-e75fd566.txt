**Decision: Do not ship the new layout yet; treat the result as UNVERIFIED.**

The 15% lift is statistically indistinguishable from noise. Your confidence interval (-2% to +32%) crosses zero, meaning there is a plausible scenario where the new layout actually *reduces* conversion by 2%. With only 300 visitors per variant over three days, you have likely fallen victim to "peeking" or simply insufficient power. Implementing a change with a known risk of negative impact (-2%) contradicts the prime directive of protecting your baseline, especially when inventory is limited and every lost conversion is unrecoverable revenue.

**Risk Analysis**
*   **Implementing Now:** You risk a confirmed conversion drop (up to -2%) during your critical promotion. Given the limited inventory, you cannot afford to waste traffic on a potentially inferior experience. The "win" is likely a false positive caused by small sample variance.
*   **Waiting/Stopping:** Since you cannot extend the test due to inventory constraints, the rigorous choice is to revert to the control (the known baseline) for the main promotion. The opportunity cost of missing a potential 32% upside is lower than the certainty of risking a downturn during a sell-out event.

**Alternative Validation Strategies**
Since quantitative significance is unreachable, pivot to qualitative and historical proxies to inform the merchandising team:
1.  **Historical Segmentation:** Slice the existing 300-user data by device type or traffic source. Did mobile users drive the lift while desktop dragged it down? If the signal is consistent across high-intent segments (even if not significant), it strengthens the hypothesis without needing more N.
2.  **Rapid Qualitative Check:** Run a 24-hour "five-second test" or quick user interviews (n=10) focusing solely on the new layout's clarity. Ask: "What is the primary benefit of this mat?" If users articulate the value proposition faster with the new layout, it validates the *mechanism* even if the conversion metric is noisy.
3.  **Guardrail Check:** Did the new layout increase returns or customer support tickets in the small sample? If yes, kill the variant immediately regardless of conversion.

**This-Week Step**
Present the control layout as the primary driver for the upcoming promotion to secure inventory sell-through. Propose a **post-promotion validation**: once inventory is replenished, re-run the test with a pre-committed sample size calculated to detect a 15% lift with 80% power, ensuring you do not peek before the target N is reached. If the merchandising team insists on testing live, limit the exposure to <5% of traffic as a guardrail, not a full rollout.

The baseline remains your only reliable benchmark; do not gamble limited inventory on an unverified trend.
