### Growth Analysis: Checkout Redesign & Retention Collapse

**Diagnosis: The Vanity Trap**
Your 5.2% completion rate is a vanity metric in this context because it rose while the business health (90-day retention) collapsed. You are successfully acquiring low-value transactions that do not represent real customer value. The "frictionless" checkout likely removed necessary confirmation steps, decoupling the *action* of buying from the *intent* to keep the product. Do not revert the entire flow yet; instead, instrument the specific disconnect between acquisition and retention.

**1. Immediate Data Slices (The "Who" and "What")**
Stop looking at aggregate averages. Slice your existing analytics by these dimensions to isolate the leak:
*   **Cohort by Product Category:** Compare pre- and post-March 1st retention for "Impulse" items (decor, small accessories) vs. "Considered" purchases (sofas, tables). If the drop is isolated to small items, the issue is impulse regret. If it affects large items, the checkout removed critical expectation-setting information.
*   **New vs. Returning Users:** Did the new flow attract entirely new, low-intent traffic, or did it degrade the experience for your existing loyal base? A drop in *returning* user retention indicates a broken experience for known customers; a drop in *new* user retention suggests a targeting or expectation mismatch.
*   **Return Rate by Checkout Path:** Correlate the specific checkout path (e.g., guest checkout vs. logged-in, mobile vs. desktop) with return rates within 14 days. High returns + low repeat purchase = expectation mismatch. Low returns + low repeat purchase = product quality or post-purchase engagement failure.

**2. Hypotheses to Test (The "Why")**
Formulate these as falsifiable statements before querying data:
*   **H1 (Expectation Mismatch):** The removed friction eliminated a "confirmation moment" where users reviewed delivery timelines or product specs, leading to buyer's remorse. *Test:* Compare return reasons and support ticket volume for "wrong item" or "didn't match description" pre- and post-redesign.
*   **H2 (Impulse Quality Signal):** The new flow disproportionately converted low-intent browsers who would never have purchased under the old flow. *Test:* Analyze the Average Order Value (AOV) and margin of the *retained* 8% vs. the lost 14%. If the retained cohort has significantly higher AOV, the new flow is filtering for low-quality users, not breaking the product.
*   **H3 (Post-Purchase Disconnect):** The streamlined checkout skipped an opt-in for post-purchase engagement (e.g., account creation, newsletter), leaving new buyers without a channel for re-engagement. *Test:* Measure email capture rates and account creation rates pre- and post-March 1st.

**3. This Week's Action Plan**
*   **Query:** Run a cohort analysis of users who purchased in March vs. January, segmented by product category, tracking their status at Day 30 and Day 90.
*   **Instrument:** If not already tracked, add an event `checkout_expectation_confirmed` (e.g., time spent on review page, scroll depth on shipping info) to see if skipping this correlates with non-return.
*   **Decision Trigger:** If return rates for "Impulse" items exceed 25% post-redesign (vs. <10% baseline), implement a targeted "soft friction" step (e.g., a summary modal) only for those categories before November. Do not revert the global flow unless *considered* purchase retention also drops.
