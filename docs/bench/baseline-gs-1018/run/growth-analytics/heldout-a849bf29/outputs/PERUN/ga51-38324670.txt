This acts as a proxy for "learning improvement" without needing private grade data. A rising trend in deep acceptance correlates with better writing outcomes without violating privacy constraints.

**Daily Data Points to Track**

**Risks of Focusing on Completion Rates**
Focusing on "essays completed" incentivizes volume over value. Students may generate multiple low-effort drafts to game the metric, inflating your dashboard while actual learning stagnates. This is a classic vanity metric: it rises even if the product fails to help users write better. Furthermore, high completion rates with low retention (your current 40% drop-off) indicate you are efficiently acquiring users who find no lasting value, wasting budget on a leaky bucket.

**Regarding Grade Improvement vs. Time Saved**
Do not attempt to track grade improvement directly yet; the privacy hurdles and lag time (weeks to get grades back) make it unusable for daily operational decisions. "Time saved" is also risky as a primary metric because it is difficult to verify without intrusive monitoring and can be gamed. Stick to **behavioral proxies** (revision depth and return rate) which are immediate, privacy-safe, and strongly correlated with the ultimate outcome of better grades.
