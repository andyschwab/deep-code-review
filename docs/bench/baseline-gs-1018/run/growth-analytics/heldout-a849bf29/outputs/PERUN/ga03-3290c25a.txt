**Decision: Do not roll out. Kill the feature for now and extend investigation.**

A 5% increase in churn is a catastrophic guardrail failure that outweighs a 15% lift in login frequency. In a retention-focused product like FinFlow, losing customers faster than they engage destroys lifetime value regardless of short-term activity spikes. The experiment has failed its primary safety check.

**Causal Mechanisms for High Engagement + High Churn**

The conflicting signals suggest the simplified verification process is altering *who* enters the funnel and *how* they interact, rather than simply improving the experience for existing high-intent users.

1.  **Lower-Quality Acquisition (The "Leaky Bucket" Effect):**
    The reduced friction likely lowered the barrier to entry for low-intent users or bots who were previously filtered out by the stricter verification. These users can easily log in (driving up frequency metrics) but lack genuine intent to use FinFlow's core financial services, leading to rapid abandonment. The "engagement" is superficial noise from users who never intended to stay.

2.  **The "Frustrated Explorer" Pattern:**
    Simplified verification may be allowing users to enter the platform faster, but if the downstream product experience (dashboard, transaction features) does not match the promise of the easy signup, frustration compounds. Users are logging in repeatedly trying to find value or fix an issue they encountered immediately post-signup, only to churn when they cannot resolve it. The high login count represents failed attempts, not successful engagement.

3.  **Security or Trust Triggers:**
    In fintech, overly simplified verification can trigger user anxiety about security. Users might log in frequently to check if their account is secure or if funds are safe, but the perceived lack of robust security protocols drives them to leave for a competitor they trust more. The engagement is driven by worry, not value.

4.  **Hidden Bug in the New Flow:**
    As hypothesized, a specific bug could be causing data corruption or session instability that forces repeated login attempts (inflating frequency) while simultaneously preventing successful completion of key actions, leading to eventual churn.

**Immediate Next Steps (This Week)**

*   **Segment the Churners:** Isolate the 5% higher churn cohort. Analyze their login patterns: are they distinct bursts of failed logins vs. steady usage? Check if they completed any core value actions (e.g., first transfer, card link) before churning.
*   **Quality Audit:** Compare the demographic and behavioral profile of the treatment group signups against the control. Look for spikes in suspicious IPs, incomplete profiles, or zero-value accounts that indicate bot/low-quality influx.
*   **Guardrail Re-evaluation:** Before any re-test, redefine the primary metric. Login frequency is a vanity metric in this context; the primary metric must be "Week 2 Retention of Users Who Completed a Core Financial Action."

**Note:** The December 1–21 window includes holiday noise; ensure any re-test excludes this period or adjusts for seasonality. Do not trust the "15% engagement" lift until the quality of those logins is verified.
