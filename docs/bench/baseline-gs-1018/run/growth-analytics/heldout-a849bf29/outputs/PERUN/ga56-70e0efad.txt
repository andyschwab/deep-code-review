**Decision: Pause new referral invitations immediately; do not kill the program yet, but stop the bleed while you diagnose.**

Your current data indicates a critical failure mode: you are paying $10 to acquire a user who generates -$4.50 in LTV and churns 70% faster than organic. This is not a "low-quality user" problem alone; it is a **unit economics breach**. Continuing to scale this without fixing the leak destroys value.

**1. Isolate the Variable (Onboarding vs. User Intent)**
You cannot distinguish between "bonus hunters" and "broken onboarding" with aggregate data. You must segment the referred cohort by behavior *before* the bonus payout:
*   **The "Cash-Out" Segment:** Users who referred others or completed the referral action immediately upon install but never played a core game loop (e.g., completed a level, joined a guild). If this group comprises >80% of the churn, your hypothesis is correct: the incentive attracted non-players.
*   **The "Drop-off" Segment:** Users who played the core loop but churned before Day 7. If retention here matches organic users, the issue is not user quality but a specific friction point in the referred user's journey (e.g., confusing UI about where the bonus appears, or a technical bug).

**Actionable Step for Thursday:** Run a cohort analysis comparing Day 1 and Day 3 retention of referred users who *did* complete a core game action vs. those who did not. If the "players" retain well, fix the onboarding. If they still churn, the incentive structure is fundamentally misaligned with your product value.

**2. Alternative Metrics for Referral Quality**
Stop tracking "Referral Installs" as a success metric; it is vanity. Replace it with:
*   **Referral Activation Rate:** Percentage of referred users who complete the core game action within 24 hours.
*   **Net Referral LTV:** (LTV of referred user) minus (Cost of Bonus + Cost of Acquisition). This must be positive before scaling.
*   **Organic Lift:** Measure if the *referrer's* retention increases after sending an invite. Often, referrers become more loyal; if their retention drops, the program is cannibalizing your best users.

**3. Immediate Recommendations**
*   **Change Eligibility:** Delay the $10 bonus payout until the referee completes a meaningful action (e.g., reaches Level 5 or plays for 3 days). This filters out pure bonus hunters.
*   **Reduce Bonus or Change Form:** Test a non-cash reward (e.g., exclusive in-game skin) which has high perceived value to players but zero cash cost to you.
*   **Guardrail Metric:** Set a hard stop: if Net Referral LTV remains negative after the eligibility change, kill the program.

**Note:** Do not present "optimization ideas" to the CFO without a pre-committed test design; propose a 2-week pilot of the delayed-payout model with a strict LTV guardrail.
**Note:** Ensure your event taxonomy tracks the specific moment the bonus is claimed to correlate it directly with churn timing.
