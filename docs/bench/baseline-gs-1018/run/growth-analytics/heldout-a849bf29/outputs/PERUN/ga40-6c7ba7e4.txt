**Deliverable: Counterfactual Re-evaluation Plan for Search Ranking Bias**

To isolate the "discovery" signal from "popularity" bias using your existing three-week test data, execute the following four-step analysis immediately. This approach constructs a counterfactual without requiring a new experiment.

**1. Segment CTR by "Author Exposure History"**
Do not analyze the 10% aggregate lift. Instead, split your query logs into two distinct cohorts based on the author's historical impression share in the *control* group over the prior 90 days:
*   **High-Visibility Authors:** Top 20% of authors by historical impressions (your "bestseller" bias group).
*   **Low-Visibility Authors:** Bottom 50% of authors by historical impressions (your "indie/discovery" group).
Calculate the CTR lift separately for each cohort. If the 10% lift is driven entirely by the High-Visibility group while the Low-Visibility group shows flat or negative lift, your hypothesis is confirmed: the algorithm is reinforcing popularity, not improving discovery.

**2. Construct the Counterfactual: "Expected vs. Actual" Discovery Rate**
Define a "Discovery Event" as a click on a book by an author the user has *never* purchased or clicked on in the past 12 months.
*   **Baseline (Counterfactual):** Calculate the historical probability of a Discovery Event for similar queries in the control group during the test window. This represents what would have happened without the new ranking.
*   **Treatment Reality:** Calculate the actual rate of Discovery Events in the treatment group.
*   **The Delta:** If the Treatment Discovery Rate is lower than the Baseline, the new algorithm is actively suppressing diversity despite the higher overall CTR. This metric isolates the specific value you care about (diversity) from the vanity metric (total clicks).

**3. Control for Position Bias via Stratification**
Since the new algorithm changes rank positions, a simple comparison is flawed. Users click more on top results regardless of quality.
*   Stratify your analysis by rank position (e.g., positions 1–3, 4–10, 11–20).
*   Compare CTR for Low-Visibility authors *only* when they appear in the same position in both Control and Treatment.
*   If Low-Visibility authors in the Treatment group receive fewer clicks than Control authors at the *same* rank position, the issue is not just exposure; the model may be surfacing less relevant indie titles, or users are rejecting them when forced into view.

**4. Guardrail Check: Long-Term Value Proxy**
Clicks are immediate; diversity is long-term. Since you cannot wait for long-term data, use "Session Depth" and "Return Rate within Test Window" as proxies.
*   Does the treatment group show a higher rate of single-click bounces?
*   Do users who click "indie" results in the treatment group return to search again within 48 hours (indicating unmet need) compared to control?
If CTR is up but session depth is down, the "lift" is likely clickbait driven by bias, not genuine value.
