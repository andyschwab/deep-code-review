### Core Metrics Definition for GreenDrop Sales Presentations

**1. The North Star: Water-Adjusted Yield Efficiency (WAYE)**
Reject "percentage water reduction" as your sole North Star; it is a vanity metric if yield collapses. Instead, report **Water-Adjusted Yield Efficiency**: *(Crop Yield per Hectare) ÷ (Water Volume Applied)*. This single figure rises only when you save water *without* sacrificing output. If water drops 20% but yield drops 5%, WAYE still improves, proving value. If yield drops disproportionately, WAYE falls, signaling a failure regardless of water savings.

**2. Supporting Input Metrics**
*   **Activation Rate:** Percentage of deployed units successfully transmitting soil moisture data within 7 days of install.
*   **Retention of Core Action:** Percentage of farms where the system autonomously adjusts irrigation schedules (vs. manual override) for 4 consecutive weeks.
*   **Guardrail Metric:** Crop Yield Variance vs. Regional Control Group. Track yield not against a missing historical baseline, but against neighboring farms using traditional timers during the *same* season.

**3. Handling Missing Baseline Data**
Do not attempt to fabricate year-over-year comparisons; without reliable historical soil moisture data, any claim of "X% improvement over last year" is **UNVERIFIED** and risky for sales.
*   **Strategy:** Shift to **Concurrent Cohort Analysis**. Compare the 500 new GreenDrop units against a control group of similar farms in the same drought-prone region using traditional timers *this season*.
*   **Reporting Structure:** Present data as "GreenDrop Cohort vs. Regional Traditional Average." This isolates the variable (your system) while controlling for external factors like weather and drought severity, rendering historical baselines unnecessary for the initial proof of value.

**4. System Uptime Classification**
Classify System Uptime as a **Guardrail Metric**, not a primary success metric.
*   **Reasoning:** High uptime is a prerequisite for operation, not a driver of customer value. A system can have 100% uptime while recommending poor irrigation strategies that hurt yield.
*   **Threshold:** Define a minimum acceptable uptime (e.g., 98%) required for the data to be considered valid for WAYE calculation. If uptime falls below this, the efficacy data is inconclusive, not a "failure of efficiency."

**5. Communicating Algorithmic Risk (Skipped Cycles)**
When the algorithm suggests skipping a watering cycle, frame the communication around **Risk-Adjusted Conservation**:
*   **The Narrative:** "The system detected sufficient root-zone moisture to sustain the crop for 48 hours, avoiding unnecessary evaporation loss."
*   **The Safety Net:** Explicitly state the **Soil Moisture Buffer** used in the decision (e.g., "We only skip if moisture is 15% above the wilting point threshold").
*   **Actionable Insight:** Provide the farmer with the projected water savings *and* the remaining safety margin in days. This shifts the perception from "risk of drought" to "precision optimization."
