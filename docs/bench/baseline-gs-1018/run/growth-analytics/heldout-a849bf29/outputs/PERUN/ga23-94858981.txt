**Executive Summary: ShopSwift Checkout Experiment (Mar 1–14)**

**Verdict: Do not ship. Do not discard. Re-run with adjusted baseline.**

The variant underperformed the control by 18.5% (3.1% vs 3.8%). While the control's 3.8% rate is significantly higher than your historical 2.9% baseline, indicating external noise (the holiday surge), the variant failed to beat even the *historical* average. This suggests the simplification may have removed necessary trust signals or context required during high-intent holiday shopping, rather than the result being purely statistical noise.

**Statistical Validation & Next Steps**

1.  **Reject the "Masked Gain" Hypothesis:** The data does not support the idea that the variant was better but masked. Even if we normalize the control down to the historical 2.9%, the variant's 3.1% represents a negligible ~7% lift, which is likely within the margin of error for a test contaminated by external volatility. More critically, you cannot assume the variant would have risen in lockstep with the holiday surge; different user behaviors during holidays often interact unpredictably with UI changes.
2.  **Do Not Discard the Data Entirely:** The test successfully identified that the "simplified" flow is fragile under high-pressure conditions. This is a valid learning: simplicity may not equal conversion during seasonal peaks. However, the specific lift calculation is `UNVERIFIED` as a causal measure of the feature's value due to the confounding holiday variable.
3.  **Immediate Action Plan:**
    *   **Stop the current analysis:** Cease attempts to mathematically "correct" the 3.8% figure post-hoc; this introduces bias.
    *   **Re-run the test post-holiday:** Schedule a new 14-day test starting after the seasonal effect dissipates (e.g., late March). Use your stable 2.9% historical baseline for power calculations.
    *   **Pre-commit to a Guardrail:** In the next run, set a guardrail metric for "form completion time" or "error rate." If conversion drops again, check if users are rushing and making more errors, or if they are abandoning due to lack of information.
    *   **Sample Size Adjustment:** Since variance is higher during volatile periods, ensure your next test's sample size is calculated to detect a Minimum Detectable Effect (MDE) of at least 5% relative lift, not smaller.
