**Recommendation: Adopt "Weekly Active Users Completing ≥3 High-Intent Actions" as your North Star.**

Do not choose between "time spent" and "tasks completed" in isolation; both fail your specific goal of reducing stress. "Time spent" is a vanity metric here: 45 minutes of planning may indicate deep work for power users, but for others, it signals anxiety-induced over-planning or friction. Since your value proposition is efficiency, higher time expenditure often contradicts your mission. Conversely, raw "tasks completed" is vulnerable to gaming (splitting one project into 20 trivial items) and excludes your "storage-only" users, falsely labeling them as unsuccessful.

Instead, define a **composite North Star** that measures the *frequency* of users deriving value, not the volume of their output. Define a "High-Intent Action" as a specific sequence that correlates with stress reduction, such as: *Create Task → Assign Time/Date → Mark Complete*.

**Why this works:**
1.  **Filters Noise:** It ignores passive "storage" usage where no planning occurs, focusing only on users actively managing their workflow.
2.  **Prevents Gaming:** Requiring a sequence (e.g., scheduling before completing) makes it harder to inflate numbers with trivial, unscheduled items.
3.  **Aligns with Efficiency:** It rewards the *act* of clearing mental load (planning and executing) rather than the duration of the session or the raw count of checkboxes.

**Analysis of Alternatives:**
*   **Time Spent:**
    *   *Pros:* Captures engagement depth.
    *   *Cons:* Directly conflicts with "efficiency." A user finishing their day in 5 minutes is more successful than one taking 45. Measuring this incentivizes features that slow users down.
*   **Tasks Completed:**
    *   *Pros:* Easy to instrument; clearly shows output.
    *   *Cons:* Incentivizes trivialization (breaking projects into tiny steps). It fails to capture the value for users who use the app for storage or long-term roadmap visualization without daily check-offs.

**Addressing the "Storage-Only" Gap:**
Since you cannot currently measure task *quality*, you must infer value from *behavioral patterns*. Users who only store tasks likely derive value from "externalizing memory." Until you can measure impact, treat these users as a separate cohort. Do not force them into the "completion" funnel yet. Instead, instrument a secondary event: *"Task Reviewed"* (opening a task detail view). If a storage user regularly reviews tasks, they are getting value even without checking boxes. Add this as a qualifying action in your North Star definition later once validated.

**Immediate Next Steps (Before Jan 15):**
1.  **Instrument the Sequence:** Define and track the specific event chain for "High-Intent Action" (e.g., `task_created` + `task_scheduled` + `task_completed` within 24 hours).
2.  **Baseline the Cohort:** Calculate how many of your 25,000 DAUs currently perform this sequence weekly. This is your true baseline, not the raw DAU count.
3.  **Validate the Proxy:** Run a quick survey with 50 users who hit this metric and 50 who don't, asking specifically about their stress levels. This verifies if your new metric actually correlates with your goal before locking it in for the quarter.

*Note: Avoid setting absolute benchmarks (e.g., "we need 40% conversion") without your own historical data; compare only against the baseline you establish in step 2.*
*Storage-only users should be tracked via a distinct "Review" event until a quality metric is feasible.*
