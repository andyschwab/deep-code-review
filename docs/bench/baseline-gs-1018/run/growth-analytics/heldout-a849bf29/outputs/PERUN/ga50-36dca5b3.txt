### Analysis Plan: Diagnosing Premium Plus Churn Spike

**1. Isolate the Cohort and Timeline**
Segment users into three distinct groups: (A) Existing Standard users who cancelled post-launch, (B) Existing Standard users who remained, and (C) New users who selected Premium Plus. Compare cancellation rates of Group A against a pre-launch baseline (same window, previous month) to confirm the 7% lift is statistically significant and not seasonal noise.

**2. Analyze Cancellation Survey Data (Qualitative Signal)**
Do not rely on aggregate percentages. Perform text analysis on open-ended feedback from Group A, tagging responses by your three hypotheses:
*   **Confusion:** Keywords like "didn't understand," "too many options," or "mistake."
*   **Re-evaluation:** Keywords like "too expensive," "don't need features," or "downgrading."
*   **Attribution:** Explicit mentions of the "email," "pricing page," or "comparison table."
*   *Action:* Calculate the % of churned users citing "price re-evaluation" vs. "confusion." If >60% cite re-evaluation, the tier exposed a value gap. If >60% cite confusion, the UI/messaging is the blocker.

**3. Audit Session Logs (Behavioral Evidence)**
Correlate the cancellation event with the preceding 48 hours of session data for Group A. Look for these specific friction patterns:
*   **The "Comparison Loop":** Users who visited the pricing page >2 times in one session but did not upgrade. High repetition suggests decision paralysis or confusion.
*   **The "Email Trigger":** Users who clicked the launch email link, viewed the pricing page, and cancelled within 24 hours. This isolates the email's role in triggering re-evaluation.
*   **Feature Exploration:** Did these users check the "Premium Plus" features page before cancelling? If yes, they are comparing value. If no, the price point alone triggered the exit.

**4. Root Cause Determination**
*   **Scenario A (Value Gap):** Logs show users checking features, then cancelling. Survey cites "cost." *Diagnosis:* The new tier made the Standard plan look overpriced for its utility.
*   **Scenario B (UX Confusion):** Logs show rapid toggling between plans or high drop-off on the comparison table. Survey cites "confusing." *Diagnosis:* The naming or table layout is inducing anxiety.
*   **Scenario C (Communication Shock):** Logs show a direct path from Email → Cancel. *Diagnosis:* The email copy aggressively upsold, making users feel their current plan was inadequate.

### Follow-Up Test Structure

Do not run a standard A/B test on price yet. Run a **Messaging/UX Isolation Test** on a small % (5%) of traffic to validate the root cause before the CRO decides.

*   **Hypothesis:** If confusion is the driver, simplifying the comparison table will reduce churn without changing price.
*   **Variant A (Control):** Current pricing page + email flow.
*   **Variant B (Simplified):** Hide the comparison table initially; show only "Most Popular" badges and a simplified 3-bullet difference list. Remove "Premium Plus" from the email subject line.
*   **Primary Metric:** Cancellation rate of existing Standard users (7-day window).
*   **Guardrail Metric:** Conversion rate to *any* paid tier (ensure you don't kill upgrades).
*   **Design Rule:** Pre-commit to a sample size of 10,000 exposed users or 14 days. Do not peek at day 2. If Variant B reduces churn by >2% relative to control with no drop in upgrades, roll out the UX fix globally. If churn remains flat, the issue is structural (price/value), requiring a product strategy shift, not a UI tweak.

**Immediate Next Step:** Pull the cancellation survey text tags and the "Email Click → Pricing View → Cancel" funnel conversion rate by tomorrow morning. These two numbers will tell the CRO if this is a communication fix or a pricing strategy failure.

*Note: All metrics must be compared to your internal pre-launch baseline; do not use industry benchmarks for "acceptable" churn. Ensure user IDs in logs are pseudonymized before analysis.*
