Carlos, because PagoRapido has no historical data and is pre-launch, any specific retention percentage or conversion benchmark would be fabricated and misleading. We cannot assert what "good" looks like yet; we can only define what to measure to establish your own baseline. Your core assumption—that retention correlates with small-value transactions under 50 reais—is a hypothesis to be tested, not a fact to build the dashboard around.

Here are the top five events to instrument, prioritized to validate your hypothesis while respecting limited engineering bandwidth. Each event answers a specific question; do not instrument anything else "just in case."

1.  **`wallet_funded` (Activation)**
    *   **Question:** Did the user complete the minimum viable step to become a customer?
    *   **Reasoning:** In the Brazilian market, trust is the primary barrier to digital wallet adoption. A signup is a vanity metric; a funded wallet is the first signal of intent. Without this, subsequent transaction events are impossible. This is your activation threshold. If users sign up via retailer partnerships but never fund the wallet, your acquisition channel is leaking immediately.

2.  **`transaction_initiated` (with properties: `amount`, `type`, `merchant_id`)**
    *   **Question:** Are users attempting the specific behavior (small-value payments) we believe drives retention?
    *   **Reasoning:** You must capture the attempt, not just the success, to distinguish between product friction and lack of intent. The `amount` property is critical to test your hypothesis: do users transacting <50 BRL return more often than those doing larger transfers? The `type` property (merchant scan vs. P2P) allows you to see which use case sticks without building separate events for each.

3.  **`transaction_completed` (with properties: `amount`, `type`, `duration_ms`)**
    *   **Question:** Is the core value actually delivered, and does speed correlate with return?
    *   **Reasoning:** In high-frequency, low-value contexts (like buying coffee or bus fare in São Paulo), latency kills retention. Comparing `initiated` vs. `completed` reveals your drop-off rate. If users initiate small payments but fail to complete them, your retention will tank regardless of how many users you onboard. This event confirms the "value delivered" moment.

4.  **`app_opened` (with property: `days_since_install`)**
    *   **Question:** Are users returning to the app without a push notification trigger?
    *   **Reasoning:** This is your raw retention signal. By tagging the `days_since_install`, you can build cohorts to see if users who completed small transactions in Week 1 open the app organically in Week 2. Do not over-engineer this; a simple open event mapped to a stable, pseudonymous user ID is sufficient to calculate Day-1, Day-3, and Day-7 retention.

5.  **`onboarding_step_completed` (with property: `step_name`)**
    *   **Question:** Where in the initial flow do we lose users before they can fund the wallet?
    *   **Reasoning:** Since you are relying on retailer partnerships, users may be motivated in-store but drop off due to KYC (Know Your Customer) friction or complex UI. Tracking the specific step where they abandon the process allows you to fix the funnel before scaling acquisition.

**Critical Implementation Notes:**
*   **Identifier Seam:** Ensure every event includes a stable, pseudonymous user ID to track cohorts across sessions. Do not use raw PII (like CPF or phone numbers) in analytics events; route those strictly to your secure backend per privacy compliance standards.
*   **Vanity Trap:** Ignore total "registered users" or "downloads" as success metrics. Your North Star for this phase is **Weekly Active Users who complete at least one transaction <50 BRL**. If this number does not rise, the business is not growing, regardless of how many retailer partnerships you sign.

Your engineering team should finalize the schema for these five events by Friday. Anything beyond this is noise until you have data from your first 1,000 real users.
