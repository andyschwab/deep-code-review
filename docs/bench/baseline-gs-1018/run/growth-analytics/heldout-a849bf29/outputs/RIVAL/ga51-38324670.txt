### Recommended Metrics for ScholarWrite Executive Board

**Primary Metric: Weekly Active Learners (WAL)**
Define a "Weekly Active Learner" as a unique user who completes at least two distinct writing sessions per week where they accept an AI suggestion *and* subsequently edit or refine that suggestion before finalizing the document.
*   **Why this works:** This moves beyond passive consumption (sign-ups) and blind acceptance (low-quality volume). It proves the student is engaging in an iterative process—using the AI as a coach rather than a ghostwriter. It directly addresses your abandonment issue by measuring sustained engagement rather than one-off usage.

**Supporting Metric 1: Suggestion Refinement Rate**
Track the percentage of accepted AI suggestions that are subsequently modified by the user before the document is marked "complete."
*   **Target:** Aim for a refinement rate between 20-40%.
*   **Insight:** A rate near 0% suggests students are blindly copying AI output (risking quality and academic integrity). A rate near 100% suggests the AI suggestions are irrelevant or poor. The "sweet spot" indicates the AI is providing a strong starting point that the student actively improves, signaling genuine learning and co-creation.

**Supporting Metric 2: Draft Iteration Depth**
Measure the average number of distinct save versions or major editing sessions per document before completion.
*   **Insight:** High-quality writing is iterative. If users generate a draft and leave (matching your 40% abandonment stat), this metric stays low. If they return to refine arguments, check citations, or improve flow, this metric rises. This correlates strongly with better final output without needing access to private grade data.

### Daily Data Points to Track
To monitor these metrics effectively for your November 12th board meeting, your dashboard must display:
1.  **New WAL Count:** Unique users meeting the "Active Learner" definition today.
2.  **Abandonment Point Heatmap:** The specific step in the workflow where users drop off (e.g., immediately after the first suggestion vs. during the editing phase).
3.  **Average Refinement Rate:** Rolling 7-day average of how often users edit accepted suggestions.
4.  **Session Duration vs. Output Length:** To ensure time spent correlates with meaningful work, not just idle open tabs.

### Risks of Focusing on Completion Rates
Focusing heavily on "essays completed per week" creates three critical risks for ScholarWrite:
1.  **Gaming the System:** Students may generate low-effort, short, or nonsensical essays just to trigger the "completion" event, inflating your success metrics while degrading the actual value of the tool.
2.  **Academic Integrity Backlash:** If the metric rewards speed and volume over quality, you inadvertently encourage plagiarism. This poses a reputational risk with university administrators who are already skeptical of AI tools.
3.  **Ignoring the Learning Gap:** A completed essay does not equal a learned skill. You could have high completion rates with students who learn nothing because they accepted every AI change without thought. This fails your core mission of "learning improvement."

### Strategic Recommendation on Grade Data
Do not attempt to track grade improvement directly at this stage. The privacy hurdles (FERPA/GDPR) and the lag time between using the tool and receiving a grade make this data too noisy and legally risky for Q4 decision-making. Instead, use **Draft Iteration Depth** and **Suggestion Refinement Rate** as proxy indicators for learning. Research shows that active editing and iteration correlate strongly with improved writing outcomes. Present these behavioral proxies to the board as "Leading Indicators of Academic Success," which are immediate, privacy-safe, and actionable.

*Note: Ensure your engineering team instruments events for "suggestion_accepted" and "text_edited_post_acceptance" immediately to capture the refinement data.*
*These metrics shift the narrative from "how many essays did we write?" to "how many students are learning to write better?"*
