### Re-evaluation Plan: Isolating Discovery from Popularity Bias

To address your concern without extending the experiment, you must shift the primary success metric from aggregate Click-Through Rate (CTR) to **stratified discovery efficiency**. Aggregate CTR is inherently biased toward popularity; a 10% lift there does not prove improved discovery. You can isolate the signal using the existing three-week window by reconstructing the data into specific cohorts and calculating a "Discovery Lift" metric.

**1. Define "Indie" and "Bestseller" Cohorts Retrospectively**
Do not rely on the model's output labels. Instead, use your historical sales data (pre-February) to tag every book in the test inventory as either "Historical Bestseller" (top 10% of all-time sales) or "Long-Tail/Indie" (bottom 80%). Apply these static tags to the February impression logs. This creates a ground truth independent of the new algorithm's ranking behavior.

**2. Calculate Stratified CTR Lift**
Stop analyzing the global average. Split the impression logs into two distinct groups based on the tags above and calculate the CTR lift for each separately:
*   **Bestseller CTR Lift:** Compare CTR for bestsellers in Treatment vs. Control.
*   **Indie CTR Lift:** Compare CTR for indie authors in Treatment vs. Control.

*Interpretation:* If the global 10% lift is driven entirely by the Bestseller cohort (e.g., +15% there) while the Indie cohort shows flat or negative lift (e.g., -2%), your hypothesis is confirmed: the model is reinforcing popularity bias. A true discovery algorithm must show positive lift in the Indie cohort, even if the absolute CTR is lower than bestsellers.

**3. Construct the "Discovery Ratio" Metric**
Create a new efficiency metric for the report: **Share of Clicks on Indie Books**.
*   Formula: `(Clicks on Indie Books) / (Total Clicks)`
*   Compare this ratio between Treatment and Control groups.

If the new algorithm increases total clicks but *decreases* the Share of Clicks on Indie Books, it is successfully optimizing for engagement but failing at diversity. This metric directly answers the editorial team's concern about long-term catalog health.

**4. Positional Bias Correction (Counterfactual Approximation)**
To approximate a counterfactual without a new test, analyze **Position-Normalized CTR**.
*   Group impressions by rank position (e.g., Position 1–3, 4–6, 7–10).
*   Compare Indie CTR at *identical positions* across Treatment and Control.
*   *Logic:* If indie books in the Treatment group receive higher CTR than indie books in the Control group *when both are shown at Position 5*, the lift is due to better relevance/matching (true discovery). If the lift disappears when controlling for position, the gain was purely due to the model moving already-popular items to higher slots.

**Reporting Strategy for Thursday**
Present three numbers to the CTO and Editorial team:
1.  **Global CTR Lift:** +10% (Acknowledge the surface win).
2.  **Indie Cohort CTR Lift:** [Insert calculated value] (The truth about discovery).
3.  **Change in Indie Share of Clicks:** [Insert % change] (The impact on diversity).

Recommendation: Do not deploy globally if the Indie Cohort Lift is neutral or negative. Instead, propose a hybrid ranking that forces a minimum quota of indie impressions in slots 4–10, using this analysis as the baseline for a targeted follow-up test.

*Note: Ensure your "Historical Bestseller" definition uses a time window prior to the test to prevent data leakage. All calculations must be performed on closed-window data from the three-week period only.*
