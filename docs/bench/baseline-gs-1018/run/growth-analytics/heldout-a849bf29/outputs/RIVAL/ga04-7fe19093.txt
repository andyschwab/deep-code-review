# DataBridge Retention Signal Framework

For a portfolio of five enterprise clients, aggregate trends are misleading. A single large account (500 seats) will skew averages, masking distress signals in smaller accounts (40 seats). Instead of seeking statistical significance across the cohort, treat each client as a "market of one." Prioritize signals based on **depth of value** rather than breadth of activity.

### 1. Normalize Metrics by Seat Count
Never compare raw login counts or feature usage totals between clients. All quantitative metrics must be normalized to **Active Seats** or **Total Licenses**.
*   **Bad Metric:** "Client A had 2,000 logins; Client B had 200."
*   **Good Metric:** "Client A has 4 logins/seat/week; Client B has 5 logins/seat/week."
*   **Action:** Build dashboard columns for *Rate per Seat* and *% of Licensed Seats Active*. This allows you to compare engagement intensity fairly between the 500-seat and 40-seat retailers.

### 2. Prioritize "Power Admin" Behaviors
Your hypothesis that admin usage predicts renewal is likely correct. In enterprise logistics, if the internal champion (Admin) stops configuring workflows, the contract is at risk regardless of end-user logins. Track these specific high-intent signals:
*   **Configuration Changes:** Frequency of workflow edits, rule updates, or new route optimizations created by admins.
*   **Data Ingestion Health:** Success rate of automated data uploads (API/file imports). Failed imports often precede churn but go unnoticed if only logins are tracked.
*   **Support Ticket Ratio:** High ticket volume from admins indicates friction; zero tickets from a previously active admin indicates disengagement (the "silent churn" risk).

### 3. The "Red Flag" vs. "Green Light" Matrix
Since you cannot rely on trends, use binary thresholds tailored to each client's baseline established in their first 60 days.

| Signal Type | Metric | Red Flag (Immediate CS Action) | Green Light (Healthy) |
| :--- | :--- | :--- | :--- |
| **Adoption Depth** | % of Seats Active (Weekly) | Drop >20% from client baseline for 2 consecutive weeks. | Stable or growing within ±10% of baseline. |
| **Admin Health** | Admin Config Actions | Zero configuration events in 14 days. | At least 1 config/change per week. |
| **Value Realization** | Critical Workflow Runs | <50% of expected weekly volume (e.g., holiday lulls excepted). | Consistent with seasonal expectations. |
| **Relationship** | Executive Check-ins | No response to QBR invites or status updates for 30 days. | Regular cadence maintained. |

### 4. Qualitative Weighting (The 50% Rule)
In a five-client model, quantitative data should only drive 50% of your health score. The other 50% must be qualitative inputs from your Customer Success team, updated weekly:
*   **Strategic Alignment:** Is the client's internal priority shifting away from supply chain optimization?
*   **Champion Status:** Has your main contact changed roles or lost influence?
*   **Implementation Blockers:** Are there unresolved technical debt issues specific to their unique workflow?

### Implementation for Dashboards
Do not build a single "Average Health Score" for DataBridge. Build **five distinct client views**.
1.  **Set Individual Baselines:** Use the first 60 days of each contract to define "normal" behavior for that specific client.
2.  **Deviation Alerts:** Configure alerts for deviations from *their* baseline, not the cohort average.
3.  **Manual Override:** Allow CS managers to manually adjust the health score based on qualitative intel, forcing a documented reason for the adjustment.

**Note:** With only five clients, a single churn event represents 20% of your revenue; prioritize preventing false negatives (missing a at-risk client) over false positives. Re-evaluate these baselines quarterly as each client matures.
