### LTV Trade-Off Framework: Push Frequency Test

To evaluate whether the 15% order lift justifies the 2.5% uninstall increase, you must model the **Net Revenue Impact (NRI)** over your six-month horizon. Do not rely on the aggregate 14-day volume spike; instead, segment the financial outcome by user fate (Retained vs. Uninstalled).

**1. Define Your Cohorts and Metrics**
Isolate the 50,000 test users and split them into two distinct groups based on the test period outcome:
*   **Group A (The Engaged):** Users who remained installed and generated the +15% order volume.
*   **Group B (The Churned):** The specific users who uninstalled (the +2.5% incremental lift in uninstalls vs. control).

**2. Calculate the Gain (Group A)**
Calculate the incremental gross profit from the increased frequency for retained users.
*   *Formula:* `(Avg Orders/Month per Retained User × Gross Profit/Order × 15% Lift) × 6 Months × Count of Retained Users`
*   *Note:* Ensure you use *gross profit* (revenue minus food/delivery costs), not top-line revenue, to reflect actual business value.

**3. Calculate the Loss (Group B)**
Calculate the Lifetime Value (LTV) lost from the incremental uninstalls. Do not assume these users had zero future value; assume they would have behaved like the control group's average retained user.
*   *Formula:* `(Avg Orders/Month per Control User × Gross Profit/Order × 6 Months) × Count of Incremental Uninstalls`
*   *Critical Adjustment:* If your data shows power users drive the lift, verify if the churned users were low-value or high-value. If high-value users are churning, the loss multiplier increases significantly.

**4. Determine Net Revenue Impact**
*   *Formula:* `Total Gain (Group A) - Total Loss (Group B) = Net 6-Month Impact`

**5. Sensitivity Analysis for the CEO**
Since long-term behavior post-6 months is unknown, present three scenarios based on **churn decay**:
*   **Conservative:** Assume the 2.5% uninstall rate continues monthly beyond the test (compounding loss).
*   **Base Case:** Assume the uninstall spike was a one-time event during the test adjustment, but order frequency lifts persist.
*   **Aggressive:** Assume order frequency lift decays by 5% monthly as users habituate or fatigue, while uninstalls remain a one-time hit.

**Recommendation Logic**
*   **Roll Out Globally:** Only if the *Base Case* NRI is positive AND the *Conservative* scenario doesn't threaten >5% of total active user base LTV.
*   **Scale Back/Segment:** If the *Base Case* is negative, or if the churned users have a higher historical LTV than the gainers. In this case, propose a "Power User Only" segment where frequency increases without triggering the silent majority.
*   **Stop:** If the *Conservative* scenario shows massive long-term erosion of the user base, even if short-term revenue is up.

**Data Execution Steps**
1.  Query the exact count of incremental uninstalls (Test Uninstalls minus Control Uninstall Rate applied to Test size).
2.  Calculate the specific ARPU (Average Revenue Per User) for the churned cohort *prior* to uninstall to avoid underestimating loss.
3.  Apply your company's standard gross margin % to all order figures.

Present the final decision as a single dollar figure: "The net impact of rolling this out globally is projected to be $[X] over six months."
