**Decision Framework: GreenRide Referral Program (Coimbra Pilot)**

**Recommendation: Do not extend the current test parameters. Pivot to a "Pattern-Based" decision using qualitative signals and unit economics rather than statistical significance.**

Given the hard deadline of April 5 and the inherent noise from weather and sample size (n=140), waiting for traditional statistical significance (p < 0.05) is impossible and risky. Instead, use the following three-step framework to make your go/no-go decision:

**1. Calculate the "Break-Even Floor" (Unit Economics)**
Ignore conversion rates for a moment. Focus purely on the cost per acquired rider versus their Lifetime Value (LTV).
*   **Cost:** €10 total payout per successful referral (€5 referrer + €5 referee).
*   **Threshold:** If the 140 referrals generated *any* active riders whose projected LTV exceeds €10, the mechanism is fundamentally viable.
*   **Action:** If the average revenue from referred users is >€10, the program works economically even if volume is low. If it is <€10, no amount of volume will save it without changing the incentive structure.

**2. Isolate the "Power User" Signal (Segmentation)**
Your hypothesis is that power users convert better. With n=140, you cannot prove this statistically, but you can look for directional truth.
*   **Action:** Split the 140 referrals into two groups: those from the top 20% of users (by rides/spend) vs. the rest.
*   **Decision Rule:** If the top 20% group shows a conversion rate even 1.5x higher than the bottom 80%, treat this as a validated pattern. Do not wait for confidence intervals to narrow. This confirms your targeting strategy is correct, even if the total volume was suppressed by rain.

**3. Qualitative "Why" Check**
Since quantitative data is noisy, qualitative data must break the tie.
*   **Action:** Interview 10 users who started but didn't complete a referral, and 10 who did.
*   **Signal:** If drop-offs cite "too hard to share" or "didn't know how," the issue is UX, not the offer. If they cite "€5 isn't enough to bother my friend," the incentive is the problem. If they cite "weather was bad so no one rode," the timing was the issue.

**Final Go/No-Go Matrix for Q2 Budget**

| Scenario | Decision | Q2 Action |
| :--- | :--- | :--- |
| **LTV > €10** AND **Power Users outperform** | **GO** | Roll out to other cities immediately. Target only top 20% users initially to maximize efficiency. Ignore the low total volume; attribute it to rain/seasonality. |
| **LTV > €10** BUT **No user segment difference** | **PIVOT** | Keep the program but change the distribution channel. The offer works, but the viral loop is weak. Test higher incentives (€10/€10) or different triggers. |
| **LTV < €10** | **NO-GO** | Abandon the €5/€5 structure. The unit economics do not support scaling. Reallocate budget to paid acquisition or retention until LTV improves. |

**Summary for the Marketing Director:**
We cannot statistically prove success due to external noise (rain) and small sample size. However, we *can* determine viability based on unit economics and directional user behavior. If the referred users are profitable (LTV > €10) and power users show higher engagement, we proceed with a targeted rollout in Q2, accepting that volume will fluctuate with weather. If the unit economics fail, we kill the program regardless of potential volume.
