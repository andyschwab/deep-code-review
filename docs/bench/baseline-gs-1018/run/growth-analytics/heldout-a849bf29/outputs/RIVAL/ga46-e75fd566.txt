### Executive Summary: Yoga Mat Layout A/B Test

**Recommendation:** Do not implement the new layout site-wide based on current data. The test is statistically inconclusive. Proceed with the **Control (current layout)** for the upcoming promotion to minimize risk, while deploying low-cost qualitative validation immediately.

#### 1. Interpretation of Results
The observed 15% lift is a **point estimate**, not a proven fact. The confidence interval (-2% to +32%) indicates that the true effect of the new layout could range from a slight decrease in conversions to a significant increase. Because the interval crosses zero, we cannot reject the possibility that the result is random noise. With only 300 visitors per variant, the test lacks the statistical power to detect anything less than a massive change, making the 15% figure unreliable for decision-making.

#### 2. Risk Analysis: Implement Now vs. Wait

**Option A: Implement New Layout Now**
*   **Upside:** If the 15% lift is real, you maximize revenue during the limited inventory window.
*   **Downside (High Risk):** There is a meaningful probability the new layout performs worse (up to -2% or potentially lower given the wide variance). With limited inventory and a high-stakes promotion, introducing an unproven variable that could suppress conversion is operationally dangerous. If the layout fails, you cannot "re-run" the test because inventory will be depleted.
*   **Verdict:** Unjustifiable risk given the inability to correct course mid-promotion.

**Option B: Wait for More Data (Standard Approach)**
*   **Upside:** Provides statistical certainty.
*   **Downside:** Impossible in this context. The server outage limited data collection, and the inventory constraint prevents extending the test duration. Waiting means missing the promotion window entirely.
*   **Verdict:** Not feasible.

**Option C: Stick with Control + Qualitative Validation (Recommended)**
*   **Upside:** Protects baseline conversion rates during the critical sales window. Uses the promotion period to gather non-statistical insights.
*   **Downside:** You may miss out on a potential 15% gain if the hypothesis was correct.
*   **Verdict:** The safest path for revenue protection under uncertainty.

#### 3. Alternative Validation Strategies
Since quantitative validation is blocked by inventory and traffic constraints, use these methods to assess the hypothesis without risking the promotion:

*   **Historical Segmentation Analysis:** Isolate traffic from the *previous* month where similar yoga mats were sold. Check if pages with longer descriptions or different layouts historically outperformed standard ones. While not a direct causal link, a consistent pattern in historical data increases confidence in the hypothesis.
*   **Rapid Qualitative Testing (5-User Study):** Recruit 5 potential customers (or use a service like UserTesting) tomorrow. Show them both layouts and ask: "Which makes you feel more confident buying this mat?" and "What information is missing?" If 4/5 users explicitly prefer the new layout's clarity, it supports the persuasive hypothesis without needing statistical significance.
*   **Post-Purchase Survey:** Add a single optional question to the checkout confirmation page during the promotion: "Did the product description help you make your decision? (Yes/No)." While this won't prove the layout caused the sale, a high "No" rate on the control group could indicate a content gap the new layout might solve in the future.

#### Decision for Merchandising Team
Allocate inventory assuming the **Control layout** performance. Treat the 15% lift as an unproven hypothesis. Use the promotion to gather qualitative feedback and post-purchase survey data to inform a definitive re-test when new inventory arrives.

*Note: Present the confidence interval visually to stakeholders to demonstrate why the 15% number is uncertain. Emphasize that protecting the baseline conversion rate is the priority when inventory cannot be replenished.*
