**Recommendation: Track "Packaging-Related Support Ticket Rate" as Your Primary Metric**

Do not focus on time-to-first-open yet; it requires new instrumentation and won't yield data before Tuesday. Instead, immediately measure the **volume of support tickets tagged with "packaging difficulty" or "hard to open" as a percentage of total active subscriptions** for the cohort receiving the new boxes versus the old boxes.

**Why this is the single most important metric:**
Your hypothesis is that frustration drives cancellations. Frustration manifests first as a complaint, not immediately as a churn. If the new packaging is the culprit, you will see a sharp spike in specific support keywords ("hard to open," "can't get in," "box stuck") coinciding exactly with the shipment date of the new eco-friendly boxes. If this metric remains flat while cancellations rise, the cause is likely price sensitivity or product quality, not the box.

**How to Execute Before Tuesday:**

1.  **Isolate the Cohort:** Segment your data into two groups: customers who received the old packaging (pre-change) and those who received the new eco-friendly packaging (post-change).
2.  **Tag & Count:** Manually review or filter support tickets from the last 30 days. Tag every ticket mentioning opening difficulties.
3.  **Calculate the Rate:** Divide the number of "hard to open" tickets by the total number of shipments in that specific cohort.
    *   *Formula:* (Packaging Complaints / Total New Box Shipments) × 100.
4.  **Compare:** Compare this rate against the historical average for the old packaging.

**Interpreting the Signals:**

*   **Signal A (Packaging is the culprit):** The "hard to open" ticket rate spikes significantly (e.g., from <0.5% to >3%) in the new cohort, and these specific tickets have a higher conversion-to-cancellation rate than other ticket types.
    *   *Action:* Revert packaging immediately. The friction is direct and provable.
*   **Signal B (Price/Product is the culprit):** The "hard to open" ticket rate remains negligible (<1%), but cancellation reasons cite "too expensive" or "didn't like items."
    *   *Action:* Do not revert packaging. Investigate pricing strategy or curation quality.
*   **Signal C (Damage vs. Difficulty):** Your 1,200 return shipments need splitting. If >60% are tagged "damaged goods" rather than "opening issues," the eco-friendly material may be failing structurally during transit, not just being hard to open.
    *   *Action:* Engage suppliers on material durability, not just box design.

**Secondary Validation (The "Smell Test"):**
Scan the text of cancellation survey responses from the last month. If "packaging" appears in >15% of free-text responses, this confirms the quantitative support data. If it appears in <5%, look harder at price communications sent last month.

**Decision Framework for the VP:**
Present the **delta** in complaint rates. If the new box generates 5x more opening complaints than the old box, the correlation to the 5% churn jump is strong enough to justify a revert before the holiday rush. If the complaint rate is unchanged, reverting the box will not solve the churn problem and may waste budget.

*Note: Ensure your support team uses consistent tagging immediately to avoid dirty data.*
*Do not wait for a full statistical significance test; the operational risk of holiday churn outweighs the need for perfect p-values.*
