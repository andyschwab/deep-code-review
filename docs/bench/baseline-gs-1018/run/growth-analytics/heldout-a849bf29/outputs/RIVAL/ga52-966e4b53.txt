# SwiftRide Geneva Launch: Metric Prioritization & Compliance Dashboard

**Objective:** Navigate strict Geneva regulations while ensuring business viability for the March 3rd license hearing.
**Primary Constraint:** Passenger wait times must not exceed 8 minutes (legal ordinance).
**Secondary Constraint:** High cost of driver re-recruitment in regulated zones.

## 1. Metric Prioritization Hierarchy

Given the regulatory environment, metrics are weighted by **Compliance Risk** first, then **Operational Viability**, and finally **Growth**.

### Tier 1: Critical Compliance Indicators (Red Zone)
*Failure here risks immediate license revocation.*
1.  **95th Percentile Passenger Wait Time:** Do not track averages. You must monitor the tail end. If the 95th percentile approaches 7 minutes, you are in the breach zone.
    *   *Target:* < 7.5 minutes (providing a 30-second safety buffer).
2.  **Safety Incident Rate (per 1,000 rides):** Any spike here triggers legal scrutiny.
    *   *Target:* 0 tolerance for severe incidents; immediate investigation threshold for minor reports.
3.  **Service Availability Density:** Drivers per square kilometer during peak hours. Low density directly causes wait time breaches.
    *   *Target:* Maintain minimum density required to sustain <8 min waits based on simulation models.

### Tier 2: Operational Viability (Yellow Zone)
*Failure here threatens unit economics and driver supply.*
4.  **Driver Acceptance Rate:** The percentage of requested rides accepted by drivers. Low acceptance directly increases wait times.
    *   *Baseline Strategy:* Without local data, set a provisional baseline of 85%. If this drops below 75%, wait times will likely breach the 8-minute cap.
5.  **Driver Churn Rate (Weekly):** Specifically tracking churn within the first 14 days.
    *   *Target:* < 5% weekly churn. High churn increases recruitment costs and reduces density.
6.  **Cancellation Rate (Driver vs. Passenger):** Distinguish between driver cancellations (supply issue) and passenger cancellations (demand issue).
    *   *Baseline Strategy:* Target < 3% driver cancellation. High driver cancellation indicates friction in the app or unfavorable pricing/conditions.

### Tier 3: Growth & Efficiency (Green Zone)
*Optimize these only when Tiers 1 and 2 are stable.*
7.  **New Driver Onboarding Velocity:** Number of fully vetted, active drivers per day.
    *   *Target:* ~17 drivers/day to hit 500 in 30 days.
8.  **Ride Completion Rate:** Total completed rides vs. total requests.

## 2. Leading Indicators for Regulatory Breach

Since you lack historical data, monitor these **leading indicators** to predict a breach before the 8-minute limit is hit:

*   **Ratio of Active Drivers to Open Requests:** If this ratio drops below 1.2 during peak hours, wait times will spike within 30 minutes.
*   **Average Time-to-Accept:** If drivers take >15 seconds to accept a ride on average, the 8-minute wait time is at risk.
*   **Geographic Heatmap Gaps:** Identify zones with zero driver availability for >5 consecutive minutes. These are imminent breach zones.
*   **Driver App Session Duration:** A sudden drop in average session length suggests drivers are logging off due to low earnings or high friction, predicting a future supply shortage.

## 3. Weighting Safety vs. Growth

For the dashboard presented to the VP and Legal team, apply the following weighting logic:

*   **Safety Incidents:** **Weight 10x.** A single severe safety incident outweighs 10% growth in ride volume. The dashboard should visually "lock" or highlight in red if safety metrics deviate, regardless of growth performance.
*   **Wait Time Compliance:** **Weight 5x.** Breaching the 8-minute ordinance is a binary fail state. Growth targets are irrelevant if the license is revoked.
*   **Growth Targets (Driver Onboarding/Ride Volume):** **Weight 1x.** These are optimization metrics. They should only be pursued aggressively when Safety and Wait Time metrics are green.

**Recommendation for Baselines:**
Since historical data is unavailable, run a **48-hour shadow simulation** using driver GPS data (without accepting rides) to model supply/demand dynamics. Use this to set your initial Acceptance Rate and Cancellation Rate baselines before full launch. Adjust these baselines weekly based on actual performance during the first month.

*Note: Ensure all data collection complies with Swiss privacy laws (FADP) regarding driver and passenger location data.*
