**Proposed North Star Metric: Contribution Margin Per Available Car Hour (CM/ACH)**

**Definition:**
$$ \text{CM/ACH} = \frac{\text{Total Revenue} - \text{Variable Costs (Insurance, Fuel/Charging, Cleaning)}}{\text{Total Fleet Size} \times \text{Hours in Period}} $$

**Why This Metric Outweighs Utilization and Revenue/ACH**

Your current debate frames a false dichotomy between operational efficiency (Utilization) and top-line yield (Revenue/ACH). Both fail to account for UrbanWheels' specific constraint: high, usage-sensitive insurance costs.

1.  **The Utilization Trap:** Optimizing purely for **Vehicle Utilization** encourages filling the 70% weekend idle time at any price. If you discount weekend rates too deeply to boost bookings, you may increase utilization from 30% to 60%, but if the incremental revenue doesn't exceed the variable cost of insurance and wear-and-tear for those extra trips, you are actively destroying value. High utilization with negative marginal contribution accelerates cash burn.
2.  **The Revenue Blind Spot:** Optimizing for **Revenue Per Available Car Hour** captures pricing power but ignores cost structure. In dense urban zones, high-frequency short trips generate high revenue but also trigger higher insurance claim probabilities and cleaning cycles. A metric that ignores these variable costs might favor a high-churn, low-margin user segment that looks profitable on the P&L top line but fails to cover the specific risks of your fleet expansion.

**Contribution Margin Per Available Car Hour** resolves this by forcing every decision to pass a profitability filter before an efficiency filter. It answers the board's core concern: "Are we growing sustainably?" It treats idle time not just as lost opportunity, but as a cost-saving state if the alternative is a loss-making trip.

**Shift in Daily Priorities**

Adopting CM/ACH will fundamentally alter your team's operational rhythm:

*   **Dynamic Pricing Strategy:** Instead of slashing weekend prices to hit utilization targets, the pricing team will model the *minimum* price required to cover variable costs plus a margin threshold. If weekend demand cannot support this price, the priority shifts to reducing variable costs (e.g., negotiating zone-specific insurance or optimizing cleaning routes) rather than chasing bookings.
*   **Fleet Deployment:** Operations will stop measuring success by "cars on the road" and start measuring "profitable hours generated." This may lead to strategically *parking* specific vehicles in low-demand zones during weekends if their projected trip margin is negative, rather than forcing them into the market.
*   **Marketing Focus:** Growth efforts will shift from acquiring "high-frequency users" to acquiring "high-margin users." You may deprioritize segments that book constantly but only during off-peak, low-rate windows, focusing instead on users who book during high-yield weekday windows or accept higher weekend rates.
*   **Insurance & Risk Management:** The finance and ops teams will collaborate directly to correlate trip data with insurance claims, potentially adjusting pricing models in specific downtown zip codes where claim frequency erodes the contribution margin.

By presenting CM/ACH, you demonstrate to the board that UrbanWheels is moving from a "growth at all costs" mindset to a "unit-economic-first" scaling strategy, essential for a capital-intensive business expanding from 50 to 300 vehicles.
