### Recommended Primary Metric: Corrected Sentence Retention Rate (CSRR)

**Definition:** The percentage of sentences containing at least one AI-corrected error where the user successfully avoids repeating that specific error type in their next three consecutive sentences.

**Formula:**
$$ \text{CSRR} = \frac{\text{Sentences with corrections not repeated in next 3 turns}}{\text{Total sentences receiving corrections}} \times 100 $$

**Why this replaces "Minutes Spent":**
"Minutes spent" measures engagement, not improvement. A user can ramble incorrectly for hours. CSRR directly measures **skill acquisition**: it validates that the user heard the correction, understood it, and applied it immediately. It answers your open question by distinguishing between *frustrating* corrections (which users ignore or cause them to quit) and *valuable* corrections (which users integrate).

**Implementation Logic for Engineering (Target: April 1):**
1.  **Input:** Use existing audio transcripts to identify sentences where the AI flags an error (e.g., verb conjugation, gender agreement).
2.  **Tagging:** Tag the specific error type (e.g., `error_type: verb_conjugation`).
3.  **Window Check:** Scan the user's next three spoken sentences.
4.  **Scoring:**
    *   If the specific error type reappears: Score = 0 (Correction failed).
    *   If the error type does not reappear in the next three sentences: Score = 1 (Correction retained).
5.  **Aggregation:** Calculate the daily/weekly average across all active users.

**Investor Narrative:**
"This metric shifts our focus from 'time on app' to 'proven learning.' A high CSRR indicates our AI feedback loop is effective and directly correlates with the fluency users pay for. Unlike raw minutes, this cannot be gamed by rambling; it proves the product works."

**Secondary Guardrail Metric:**
Track **Correction-Induced Churn**: The percentage of users who cancel their subscription within 48 hours of a session where >50% of their sentences received corrections. This monitors the "frustration risk" highlighted by your February pilot.

***

**Note:** Validate this metric against your February pilot data before full rollout; if users who retained corrections still churned, the issue may be UX friction rather than content value.
**Note:** Ensure error categorization is consistent (e.g., grouping all verb errors together) to prevent the denominator from becoming too sparse for statistical significance.
