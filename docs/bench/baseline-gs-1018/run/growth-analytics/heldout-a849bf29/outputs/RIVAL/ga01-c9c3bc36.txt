**Recommendation: Adopt "Percentage of Completed Prescriptions" as your North Star Metric.**

For SwasthConnect's Series A pitch, this metric directly validates your core business hypothesis: that health outcomes depend on medication adherence, not just consultation volume. While follow-up video calls are a valuable operational indicator, they are a proxy measure that can be easily distorted by your infrastructure constraints.

**Trade-off Analysis**

**1. Percentage of Completed Prescriptions (Recommended)**
*   **Alignment with Value:** This metric measures the actual outcome you promise investors and patients: successful treatment. A patient can book five video calls yet fail to take their medicine; conversely, a patient might complete their course with minimal video interaction if they receive effective SMS reminders or community health worker support.
*   **Infrastructure Resilience:** This is a binary, backend-verified event (Prescription Issued vs. Course Completed). It does not rely on real-time connection stability or session duration. In regions with spotty internet where average sessions are only 4 minutes, tracking complex journey steps like "video call completion" risks data loss if the connection drops during the handshake or logging phase. Prescription completion can be verified asynchronously via clinic records or pharmacy pick-ups, making it immune to the 4-minute session ceiling.
*   **Investor Narrative:** For a Series A board, this metric demonstrates "outcome efficacy." It shifts the story from "we facilitate calls" (a commodity) to "we cure patients" (a high-value health intervention).

**2. Number of Follow-up Video Calls (Not Recommended)**
*   **The Proxy Trap:** High call volume does not guarantee adherence. Patients may drop off after the call due to cost, side effects, or lack of medicine availability. Optimizing for calls might inadvertently encourage your team to push unnecessary consultations rather than solving the root barriers to medication access.
*   **Infrastructure Vulnerability:** Given the 4-minute average session duration and spotty connectivity, this metric is highly fragile. If the network fails during a call, does it count? If a patient intends to call but cannot connect, the metric penalizes your platform for infrastructure issues outside your control. This introduces noise that makes it difficult to prove product-market fit to investors.
*   **Misaligned Incentives:** Focusing on call volume could drive up operational costs (bandwidth, doctor time) without correlating to improved health, potentially hurting your unit economics ahead of a funding round.

**Strategic Context for the Board**
By selecting "Percentage of Completed Prescriptions," you acknowledge the reality of rural infrastructure. You are telling the board that SwasthConnect is engineered to deliver results *despite* connectivity challenges, using video calls as one of many tools rather than the definition of success itself. This metric proves that your model works in the difficult conditions of Maharashtra and Karnataka, which is the strongest possible evidence for scalability.

Note: Ensure your data collection for prescription completion relies on clinic-side verification rather than patient-app logging to bypass connectivity gaps.
Use follow-up call rates as a secondary, internal diagnostic metric to optimize doctor engagement, but do not present it as the primary growth driver.
