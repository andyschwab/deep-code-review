# GreenDrop Core Metrics & Reporting Framework

### 1. Primary Success Metric: Water Use Efficiency (WUE) Ratio
Do not report "percentage water reduction" in isolation, as this incentivizes under-watering and yield loss. Instead, report the **Water Use Efficiency Ratio**, which balances conservation against output.

*   **Formula:** `(Crop Yield [kg] / Water Applied [m³]) GreenDrop` vs. `(Crop Yield [kg] / Water Applied [m³]) Control Group`
*   **Why:** This proves you are generating more revenue per drop of water, not just using less water. A 20% water reduction is a failure if yield drops 25%; it is a massive success if yield remains stable or increases.
*   **Sales Narrative:** "GreenDrop doesn't just save water; it optimizes the economic value of every cubic meter applied."

### 2. Secondary Guardrail Metrics
Track these to ensure the primary metric isn't being gamed at the expense of farm health:
*   **Yield Variance:** Percentage difference in total harvest weight/quality compared to the regional average or control plots.
*   **System Uptime (Secondary Metric):** Track this as a **secondary operational metric**, not a primary success metric. High uptime is a baseline requirement for the hardware to function, but 99% uptime means nothing if the algorithm makes poor watering decisions. Report uptime separately as "Service Reliability" to distinguish hardware performance from algorithmic efficacy.
*   **Algorithm Override Rate:** How often farmers manually override a "skip" command. High override rates indicate trust issues or algorithmic errors.

### 3. Handling Missing Baseline Data
Since reliable soil moisture baselines are missing for new sites, **do not attempt year-over-year comparisons** for these specific units. Instead, use a **Concurrent Control Group** strategy:

*   **Structure:** Pair every 10 GreenDrop units with 2 nearby control units running traditional timers on the same crop type and soil profile.
*   **Reporting Window:** Report results as "Season-to-Date Performance vs. Local Control" rather than "Year-over-Year Improvement."
*   **Normalization:** Normalize data by "Days Since Planting" and "Evapotranspiration (ET0) Rates" to account for weather variations between the test and control groups.
*   **Sales Script:** "Because soil conditions vary wildly even within a single farm, we compare GreenDrop directly against your neighbors' traditional timers running side-by-side this season, ensuring an apples-to-apples proof of value."

### 4. Communicating Algorithmic Risk (Skipping Watering)
When the algorithm suggests skipping a cycle, frame the communication around **Risk Mitigation** rather than **Resource Restriction**.

*   **The Narrative:** "The system has detected sufficient root-zone moisture to sustain the crop for another 48 hours. Skipping this cycle prevents root rot and nutrient leaching while preserving water capacity for the forecasted heatwave next week."
*   **Confidence Scoring:** Include a "Confidence Score" (0-100%) with every skip recommendation based on sensor data density and weather forecast reliability.
    *   *High Confidence (>90%):* "Safe to skip."
    *   *Low Confidence (<70%):* "Data insufficient; manual review recommended."
*   **Liability Shield:** Explicitly state in presentations that GreenDrop provides *recommendations* based on probabilistic models, but the final irrigation decision remains with the farm manager. This manages expectations if an unforeseen micro-climate event causes stress.

### Summary for Sales Deck (Feb 1st Launch)
1.  **Headline Metric:** Water Use Efficiency (Yield per m³), not just water saved.
2.  **Comparison Method:** Concurrent Control Groups (Side-by-Side), not historical baselines.
3.  **Reliability:** System Uptime reported as "Operational Stability," distinct from agronomic success.
4.  **Risk Protocol:** Skip recommendations include confidence scores and agronomic justification (disease prevention, strategic reserve).

*Note: Ensure sales teams are trained to explain that "stable yield with less water" is the win condition, not "maximum water reduction."*
*Note: Begin collecting soil baseline data immediately for next season's year-over-year reporting.*
