**Recommendation: Adopt "Meaningful Progress Events" as Your North Star**

Do not choose between "time spent" and "tasks completed" as your sole north star. Both are flawed proxies for your actual goal: reducing user stress through efficiency. Instead, define a composite metric called **Meaningful Progress Events (MPE)**.

An MPE is counted only when a user completes a task *and* has spent at least 2 minutes in the planning session for that day. This simple logic gate solves your immediate dilemmas:
1.  **Prevents Gaming:** It stops users from inflating scores by breaking projects into trivial 10-second items, as these won't meet the time-in-session threshold.
2.  **Captures Planners:** It respects your power users (45 mins) who derive value from the act of organizing, not just checking boxes.
3.  **Excludes Passive Storage:** It naturally filters out users who only use the app for storage without engaging in the planning ritual, ensuring your north star reflects active productivity, not passive hoarding.

**Analysis of Current Options**

*   **Tasks Completed (Pros/Cons):**
    *   *Pros:* Directly aligns with the output of productivity; easy to understand.
    *   *Cons:* High risk of gamification (trivial tasks); fails to capture the value of the 45-minute planning session; ignores the "storage" segment entirely. If you optimize for this, you may inadvertently encourage busywork over deep work, increasing user stress.

*   **Time Spent (Pros/Cons):**
    *   *Pros:* Captures the engagement of power users; acknowledges that planning is work.
    *   *Cons:* Dangerous for an efficiency app. Optimizing for time can lead to bloated features that keep users stuck in the app longer, directly contradicting your value proposition of helping them finish faster and reduce stress. It rewards friction, not flow.

**Handling the "Storage" Segment**
Users who use the app purely for storage without checking items off are currently not realizing your core value proposition of "efficiency." Do not let them skew your north star. Track them as a secondary segment ("Archivers") to understand retention, but exclude them from the primary success metric until you develop features that convert them into active planners. Your Q1 goal is to deepen value for active users, not broaden the definition of activity to include passive usage.

**Implementation for Q1**
Since you cannot yet measure task quality or impact, MPE is the safest leading indicator. It assumes that a user who plans (time) and executes (completion) is experiencing the intended "stress-reducing efficiency."

1.  **Primary Metric:** Daily Meaningful Progress Events (Tasks Completed + Session Duration > 2 mins).
2.  **Guardrail Metric:** Average Session Duration. If this spikes while MPE stays flat, you are creating friction, not value.
3.  **Secondary Metric:** Retention of the "Archiver" segment (to monitor if they eventually convert).

This approach aligns your team around the *behavior* of efficient planning rather than a raw count that can be manipulated or a duration that can be wasted.

*Note: Begin qualitative research immediately to define "task impact" for future quarters, as MPE is a proxy, not a perfect measure of life improvement.*
