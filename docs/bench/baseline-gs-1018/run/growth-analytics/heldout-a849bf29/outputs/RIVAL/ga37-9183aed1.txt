# Executive Briefing: Checkout Flow Experiment Results

**Recommendation:** Do not roll out the new checkout flow as a permanent solution in its current state. The statistical win does not translate to a business win for this quarter. Instead, park this variant and pivot to higher-impact hypotheses.

### The Bottom Line
While the experiment achieved statistical significance (p=0.03), the practical impact is negligible. A 0.4% lift yields approximately **12 extra bookings per day**. At our current engineering maintenance costs, this gain barely breaks even, offering no meaningful contribution to Q1 revenue targets.

### Interpreting the Data
Stakeholders often equate "statistically significant" with "successful." We must correct this framing:
*   **Statistical Significance:** Confirms the 0.4% lift is real and not random noise. We can trust the number.
*   **Practical Significance:** Determines if that number matters. Here, the effect size is too small to justify the technical debt of maintaining a divergent code path.

### Strategic Context: Compounding vs. Ceiling
Your question regarding annual compounding versus a optimization ceiling is critical.
*   **Compounding Reality:** While 12 bookings/day compounds to ~4,380 annually, this assumes zero decay in user behavior and no additional maintenance burden over time. In reality, code complexity increases bug risk and slows future feature velocity.
*   **The Ceiling Signal:** This result likely signals a ceiling on *this specific type* of friction reduction. We have likely extracted the easy gains from checkout layout tweaks. Further micro-optimizations here will yield diminishing returns.

### Decision Framework
We have three paths forward:

1.  **Revert (Recommended):** Remove the new code path. This eliminates maintenance costs and technical debt. We acknowledge the learning: minor UI friction is not the primary barrier to conversion.
2.  **Iterate (High Risk):** Attempt to amplify the effect by combining this change with other variables. However, given the small baseline lift, the probability of finding a combination that clears our ROI hurdle is low.
3.  **Roll Forward (Not Recommended):** Only justifiable if the engineering cost to maintain is effectively zero (e.g., the code is cleaner than the original). If there is *any* ongoing maintenance tax, this option destroys value.

### Presentation Strategy for Leadership
When presenting to the VP of Engineering and CRO on Monday:

*   **Lead with Business Impact, not P-Values:** Start with "The test proved a real lift, but the magnitude is too small to move our revenue needle."
*   **Reframe "Failure":** This is not a failed test; it is a successful elimination of a low-value path. We now know where *not* to invest engineering cycles.
*   **Propose the Pivot:** Recommend shifting experimentation focus from micro-friction (button placement, form fields) to macro-value propositions (payment options, trust signals, post-booking upsells) where the potential lift is 5-10%, not 0.4%.
