### Analysis Plan: Diagnosing Premium Plus Churn Spike

**1. Cancellation Survey Analysis (Qualitative Triage)**
Segment survey responses by user tenure and previous plan type. Filter specifically for "Standard" users who cancelled within 14 days of the Premium Plus launch.
*   **Keyword Clustering:** Tag open-text responses for "confusion," "pricing," "features," "too expensive," or "don't need."
*   **Attribution Mapping:** If the survey asks "What prompted this decision?", isolate mentions of the "new plan," "email," or "pricing page."
*   **Sentiment Split:** Compare sentiment scores between users who mention the new tier vs. those who do not. If >40% of the spike cohort cites "confusion" or explicitly mentions the new tier, the hypothesis of positioning failure is supported.

**2. Session Log Analysis (Behavioral Forensics)**
Correlate cancellation timestamps with user activity in the 48 hours prior.
*   **Page Flow Reconstruction:** Trace the path of churned Standard users. Did they visit the pricing page immediately before cancelling?
    *   *Hypothesis A (Naming/Features):* High dwell time on the feature comparison table, multiple toggles between plans, or repeated visits to the FAQ without conversion.
    *   *Hypothesis B (Email):* A spike in cancellations occurring within 2 hours of the email blast timestamp suggests the messaging triggered the re-evaluation.
*   **Feature Utilization Check:** Compare the feature usage of churned Standard users against the Premium Plus feature set. If these users rarely used features exclusive to Standard (but included in Plus), they may have realized the Standard plan was sufficient, or conversely, felt "priced out" of features they thought they had.
*   **Cohort Comparison:** Calculate the cancellation rate for users who *did not* open the email or visit the pricing page post-launch. If their churn rate remained stable while the "engaged" cohort spiked, the communication/UX is the culprit, not the mere existence of the tier.

**3. Synthesis for CRO**
Create a matrix mapping the friction point to the evidence:
*   **Confusion:** High survey mentions of "confusing" + High dwell time on comparison table.
*   **Re-evaluation:** Survey mentions "don't need" + Low usage of premium features + Stable churn in non-engaged cohort.
*   **Email Trigger:** Churn spike temporally aligned with email send time + Low site engagement prior to cancel.

### Proposed Follow-Up Test Structure

**Hypothesis:** Because the current feature comparison creates cognitive load or perceived obsolescence for Standard users, simplifying the visual hierarchy and clarifying the "Standard" value proposition will reduce cancellation rates by 50% while maintaining Premium Plus adoption.

**Test Design (A/B/n):**
*   **Control:** Current pricing page and email flow.
*   **Variant A (Simplified Naming):** Rename tiers to "Basic," "Pro," and "Ultimate" to reduce perceived complexity. Remove "Plus" which implies the Standard is incomplete.
*   **Variant B (Anchored Value):** Redesign the comparison table to highlight features *lost* if downgrading, rather than features *gained* by upgrading. Add a "Most Popular" badge to Standard to reinforce its sufficiency.
*   **Variant C (No Email):** Suppress the upgrade email to a small holdout group to measure the email's specific impact on churn.

**Metrics:**
*   **Primary:** Cancellation rate of existing Standard users (Guardrail: must not increase).
*   **Secondary:** Conversion rate to Premium Plus; Click-through rate on pricing page.
*   **Sample Size:** Run until statistical significance (p < 0.05) or 14 days, whichever comes first, given the urgency.

**Recommendation:** If Variant B shows a significant reduction in churn without sacrificing Premium Plus signups, implement immediately. If all variants show elevated churn compared to pre-launch baselines, consider pausing the tier launch to re-strategize the value proposition.
