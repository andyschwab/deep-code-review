# Recommendation: Do Not Roll Out Globally Yet

**Decision:** Pause the global rollout. The 5% decline in direct messages (DMs) represents a fundamental degradation of core community health, not a temporary novelty effect. While increased profile views suggest improved discovery, the reduction in deep engagement indicates the new hierarchy successfully attracts attention but fails to convert it into connection.

**Immediate Action Plan:**
1.  **Revert to Control** for 50% of traffic immediately to stabilize DM volume while you analyze further.
2.  **Iterate the Design** to restore the visibility of the message action before re-testing. The "hidden" button hypothesis must be validated and resolved before any layout change is considered successful.

## Interpretation of Conflicting Signals

Your assumption is likely correct: the design optimizes for **passive consumption** (views) at the expense of **active contribution** (messages). In a social network, DMs are a leading indicator of retention and lifetime value; profile views are often a vanity metric unless they convert to action. A 12% gain in views does not mathematically offset a 5% loss in direct conversations if the long-term value of a conversation exceeds the short-term value of a view (which it almost always does).

The drop in messages is unlikely to be a novelty effect. Novelty typically causes a temporary *increase* in interaction as users explore, followed by a回归 to baseline. A sustained *decrease* over 14 days suggests a structural friction point: users literally cannot find or are not prompted to send messages as easily as before.

## Segmentation Strategy for Community Health

To determine the true impact and guide your next iteration, segment your data by the following dimensions immediately:

### 1. User Intent Cohorts (New vs. Returning)
*   **Hypothesis:** New users may browse more (higher views) because they are exploring, while power users (who drive DM volume) are frustrated by the layout change.
*   **Action:** Split results by user tenure (<30 days vs. >90 days). If the DM drop is concentrated in power users, the design alienates your core community.

### 2. The "Over 45" Anomaly
*   **Hypothesis:** The 20% increase in time-on-page for users 45+ likely indicates **confusion**, not utility. Older demographics often struggle with non-standard navigation patterns.
*   **Action:** Cross-reference "time on page" with "bounce rate" and "scroll depth" for this segment. If they spend more time but scroll less or fail to click *any* action, the design is creating friction, not engagement. Conduct 5 rapid user interviews with this demographic to confirm if they feel lost.

### 3. Device Type (Mobile vs. Desktop)
*   **Hypothesis:** Hiding buttons impacts mobile users more severely due to limited screen real estate.
*   **Action:** If the DM drop is significantly worse on mobile, the layout fails the primary use-case for social apps.

## Path Forward for Friday's Decision

Present this narrative to your leads:
*"The experiment proved that highlighting activity drives curiosity (views) but inhibits connection (messages). Since our north star is community health, defined by active relationships, this specific layout fails. However, the insight that 'activity feeds drive discovery' is valuable. We will iterate by keeping the activity highlight but restoring the message button to its original prominence, then re-test to see if we can capture the view lift without the engagement tax."*

Do not frame this as a failure of the team, but as a successful validation of a hypothesis that protected the product from a harmful global change.

*Note: Ensure your tracking distinguishes between "profile view" and "profile interaction" in future tests to catch this imbalance earlier.*
*Consider adding a guardrail metric for "actions per session" in all future UX experiments.*
