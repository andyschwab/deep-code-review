**Recommended North Star Metric: Weekly Active Users Reporting Positive Resolution**

**Definition:** The count of unique students per week who initiate a conversation and explicitly indicate—via a single, optional post-chat prompt—that the interaction was "helpful" or that they feel "better" than when they started.

**Why this metric:**
This metric balances mission alignment with ethical constraints. Unlike raw sentiment analysis, which you correctly identified as vulnerable to manipulation (users feigning positivity to exit) and misinterpretation (missing masked crisis), a direct self-report of resolution respects user agency and provides a clearer signal of perceived value. It avoids the privacy violations of tracking clinical outcomes while remaining actionable for product improvements.

**Trade-offs: Engagement Volume vs. Self-Reported Mood**

| Metric | Pros | Cons | Recommendation |
| :--- | :--- | :--- | : |
| **Engagement Volume** (Total chats/time spent) | Easy to track; indicates reach. | **High risk of perverse incentives.** More chats could mean users are stuck in loops or worsening, not improving. High volume without resolution suggests failure, not success. | **Reject as North Star.** Use only as a guardrail to ensure system capacity. |
| **Self-Reported Mood Scores** (Pre/Post chat rating) | Directly measures emotional shift; aligns with well-being mission. | **Friction reduces data quality.** Asking for two ratings (before/after) increases drop-off. Users in acute distress may lack the cognitive bandwidth to rate accurately. | **Adopt a simplified version.** Ask only one post-chat question: "Did this help?" (Yes/No). This captures the *outcome* without the friction of a full mood scale. |

**Addressing the 'Crisis Escalations' Paradox**

You asked whether a higher number of crisis escalations indicates failure or success. In the context of a mental health triage tool, **a higher number of successful escalations initially indicates success.**

*   **Success Signal:** It means the bot is correctly identifying high-risk language and safely routing students to human care. If this number is zero, the bot is likely missing cries for help (a catastrophic failure).
*   **Failure Signal:** The metric becomes negative only if the *rate* of escalation per conversation spikes unnaturally (suggesting the bot is agitating users) or if the *resolution* of those escalations fails (e.g., students drop off before connecting to help).

**Implementation Strategy for the April 10th Meeting**

1.  **Primary Metric:** Track "Weekly Resolved Users" (users who chat + confirm helpfulness).
2.  **Guardrail Metric:** Monitor "Crisis Escalation Rate." Aim for stability or a slight increase as detection improves, but investigate sudden spikes.
3.  **Ethical Safeguard:** Make the post-chat feedback optional and anonymous to prevent coercion. Do not store the text of the conversation linked to the feedback score.

This approach demonstrates to the board that you prioritize student safety (via escalation tracking) and perceived value (via resolution reporting) without overstepping privacy boundaries.
