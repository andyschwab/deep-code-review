**Recommendation: Do Not Roll Out Yet; Execute a "Conversion-Clarity" Follow-Up Test**

Based on the data provided, rolling out either headline immediately carries significant risk. Your current metrics reveal a classic optimization conflict: Headline A drives volume (sign-ups) but attracts lower-quality traffic (low time-on-page), while Headline B drives engagement but suppresses volume. Without knowing which variant yields more *paid* conversions after the 14-day trial, you cannot determine which drives actual revenue. Since your ultimate goal is Q2 campaign effectiveness (revenue growth), optimizing for intermediate metrics like sign-ups or time-on-page is insufficient and potentially misleading.

**Immediate Action Plan (Next 7 Days)**

1.  **Pause Permanent Implementation:** Maintain the current control or split traffic evenly until the follow-up is ready. Do not commit Q2 creative assets to either variant yet.
2.  **Retroactive Cohort Analysis (If Possible):** Immediately segment your February 5–20 sign-ups by headline variant. Track how many from each group have converted to paid plans *so far*. Even with incomplete 14-day windows for late sign-ups, early conversion velocity often predicts final outcomes. If Headline A's sign-ups are converting at half the rate of Headline B's, the "volume win" is an illusion.
3.  **Design a Follow-Up A/B Test (Not Multivariate):**
    *   **Hypothesis:** "Because Headline A attracts less qualified users, we believe Headline B will generate a higher rate of paid conversions despite lower initial sign-up volume."
    *   **Primary Metric:** Paid conversion rate (Sign-up to Paid) after 14 days.
    *   **Secondary Metric:** Sign-up volume (to monitor top-of-funnel impact).
    *   **Guardrail Metric:** Cost per Acquisition (CPA) if paid traffic is involved.
    *   **Setup:** Run a simple A/B test (Headline A vs. Headline B) with the CTA placement fixed to the version that performed best overall in your previous test.
    *   **Duration:** You must run this for a full 14-day conversion window plus 3–5 days for statistical stabilization. Given 25k visitors over 15 days previously, estimate ~1,600 visitors/day. To detect a meaningful difference in paid conversion (typically 2–5%), you will likely need 2–3 weeks of runtime.

**Strategic Rationale for the Marketing Director**

The conflicting signals in your multivariate test indicate that headline tone acts as a "filter." Headline A casts a wide net, capturing curious but unqualified visitors who bounce quickly. Headline B acts as a qualifier, repelling low-intent users but engaging high-intent prospects. In B2B SaaS, a smaller pool of highly qualified leads often generates more revenue than a large pool of churn-prone trials.

By shifting your success metric from "sign-ups" to "paid conversions," you align the test with business value rather than vanity metrics. The proposed follow-up isolates the headline variable to definitively answer which message drives revenue. This approach ensures your Q2 creative assets are built on proven profitability, not just top-of-funnel activity.
