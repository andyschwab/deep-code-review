# QuickBite Germany: Operational Measurement Funnel (Launch Phase)

**Objective:** Validate the assumption that "Order-to-Driver Assignment" is the primary bottleneck compared to "Preparation" and "Transit" times in Munich and Berlin.

**Implementation Window:** March 1 – March 31
**Audience:** Local General Managers (Munich & Berlin)

## Core Funnel Stages & Transition Points

To isolate drop-offs, every order must be timestamped at these six specific stages. The difference between consecutive timestamps defines your operational health.

### 1. Order Received (`T0`)
*   **Definition:** Customer payment confirmed and order pushed to the restaurant tablet.
*   **Metric:** Total Order Volume.
*   **Why Monitor:** Establishes the baseline denominator for all subsequent rates.

### 2. Restaurant Acknowledged (`T1`)
*   **Definition:** Restaurant staff manually accepts the order on their device.
*   **Transition Metric:** **Acceptance Latency** (`T1 - T0`).
*   **Critical Threshold:** > 2 minutes indicates restaurant onboarding issues or tablet connectivity problems.
*   **Drop-off Risk:** Orders stuck here mean the kitchen never started cooking.

### 3. Food Ready for Pickup (`T2`)
*   **Definition:** Restaurant marks order as "Ready" (driver can collect).
*   **Transition Metric:** **Preparation Time** (`T2 - T1`).
*   **Critical Threshold:** Varies by cuisine, but > 25 minutes for fast casual suggests menu complexity mismatches in the German market.
*   **Drop-off Risk:** Drivers arriving before food is ready (driver idle time) vs. food getting cold.

### 4. Driver Assigned (`T_Assign`)
*   **Definition:** Algorithm successfully matches an available driver to the order.
*   **Transition Metric:** **Assignment Latency** (`T_Assign - T0`).
*   **Critical Threshold:** > 5 minutes validates your hypothesis of a driver density bottleneck.
*   **Drop-off Risk:** Orders waiting indefinitely for a driver while food sits ready.
    *   *Note:* Track this against `T2`. If `T_Assign` > `T2`, you have a critical supply shortage.

### 5. Driver Picked Up (`T3`)
*   **Definition:** Driver scans/confirming pickup at the restaurant.
*   **Transition Metric:** **Pickup Lag** (`T3 - max(T2, T_Assign)`).
*   **Critical Threshold:** > 8 minutes indicates navigation issues, parking difficulties in Berlin/Munich, or restaurant handover friction.

### 6. Order Delivered (`T4`)
*   **Definition:** Customer confirms receipt or driver marks "Delivered."
*   **Transition Metric:** **Transit Time** (`T4 - T3`).
*   **Critical Threshold:** Compare against estimated delivery time (EDT). Consistent overages suggest routing algorithm errors or traffic pattern misunderstandings.

## Daily Dashboard Requirements for GMs

Your daily operational dashboard must visualize three specific derived metrics to test your bottleneck assumption:

1.  **The "Idle Food" Ratio:** % of orders where `T_Assign` occurred *after* `T2`.
    *   *Insight:* If high, the bottleneck is driver supply, not restaurant speed.
2.  **The "Ghost Kitchen" Latency:** Average time between `T0` and `T1`.
    *   *Insight:* If high, the issue is restaurant partner training, not logistics.
3.  **Assignment Failure Rate:** % of orders where no driver is assigned within 10 minutes of `T0`.
    *   *Insight:* Direct measure of driver density sufficiency in specific zip codes.

## Action Protocol

*   **If Assignment Latency > Preparation Time:** Activate surge pricing or expand driver recruitment zones immediately.
*   **If Acceptance Latency > 3 mins:** Trigger manual support calls to the specific restaurant; re-evaluate partnership viability.
*   **If Transit Time varies wildly by zone:** Map specific Berlin/Munich neighborhoods with parking/access issues for driver briefing updates.

*All timestamps must be recorded in UTC locally but displayed in CET for the operations team. Data must be refreshed every 15 minutes during peak hours (11:00–14:00, 18:00–21:00).*
