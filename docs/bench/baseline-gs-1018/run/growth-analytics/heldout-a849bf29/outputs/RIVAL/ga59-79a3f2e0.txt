# Emergency Analysis Plan: HomeHaven Checkout Conversion Decline

## 1. Immediate Quantitative Segmentation (Data to Extract Today)

Since granular funnel data is currently missing, prioritize these specific cuts of your existing analytics to isolate the variable causing the 15% drop:

*   **Device & OS Breakdown:** Segment conversion rate by Desktop vs. Mobile, then further by iOS vs. Android. If the drop is isolated to mobile (especially older OS versions), this points to technical performance or responsive design failures rather than UX psychology.
*   **Entry Point Analysis:** Compare conversion rates for users entering checkout from Product Pages vs. Cart Page vs. Direct Links. A drop specific to one entry point may indicate broken deep linking or context loss.
*   **Traffic Source Quality:** Segment by channel (Paid Social, Organic, Email, Retargeting). If the drop is uniform across all sources, it is likely a universal UX/tech issue. If isolated to one channel, the new design may conflict with specific audience expectations or landing page contexts.
*   **Time-on-Page & Load Metrics:** Correlate conversion rate with page load time buckets (e.g., <2s, 2-4s, >4s). A strong negative correlation in the >4s bucket on mobile confirms a performance bottleneck.
*   **Field-Level Abandonment:** If event tracking exists for form interactions, identify the specific field (Shipping, Payment, Review) where the session ends. If not, measure the drop-off between the three new steps vs. the historical six steps.

## 2. Rapid Qualitative Data Gathering (Execute Within 4 Hours)

*   **Session Replay Sampling:** Watch 20–30 recorded sessions of mobile users who abandoned checkout in the last 48 hours. Look specifically for "rage clicks," rapid back-button usage, or freezing at specific steps.
*   **Intercept Survey:** Deploy a one-question exit-intent survey on the checkout page: *"What almost stopped you from completing your purchase today?"* with multiple-choice options including "Unsure how many steps left," "Page loading slowly," "Concerns about payment security," and "Other."
*   **Support Ticket Triage:** Review customer support chats and emails from the last 10 days tagged with "checkout" or "payment." Look for recurring keywords regarding confusion, errors, or missing information.

## 3. Addressing the AOV Complication

The 5% increase in Average Order Value (AOV) suggests the new design may be successfully encouraging larger baskets (perhaps by reducing friction in item selection earlier in the flow) but failing at the final commitment stage.

*   **Calculate Revenue Per Visitor (RPV):** Do not optimize for conversion rate alone. If RPV is flat or up despite the conversion drop, the issue is less critical. If RPV is down, the volume loss outweighs the basket size gain.
*   **Segment AOV by Completers vs. Abandoners:** Determine if abandoners had higher or lower intended basket values than completers. If high-value carts are abandoning, the risk is greater.

## 4. Recommendation Framework for Tomorrow's Review

Present findings using this decision matrix:

1.  **If Mobile Performance is the culprit:** Recommend immediate hotfixes for load times; do not roll back the design.
2.  **If Progress Bar Anxiety is confirmed (via surveys/replays):** Recommend A/B testing a re-added progress indicator immediately rather than a full rollback.
3.  **If RPV is stable/up:** Recommend keeping the new design and focusing optimization efforts solely on the specific drop-off point identified.
4.  **If RPV is down and no technical cause is found:** Recommend a partial rollback to the previous step count while retaining any successful UI elements from the new design.

**Note:** Ensure all data comparisons use identical 10-day windows year-over-year or week-over-week to rule out seasonality. Prioritize RPV over conversion rate in your final recommendation to the CTO.
