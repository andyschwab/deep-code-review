**Decision Framework: Isolate, Validate, Decide**

**1. Immediate Action: Halt Expansion and Extend the Test**
Do not roll out to 100% traffic yet. The 5% dip in Average Order Value (AOV) against a 10% Click-Through Rate (CTR) gain indicates a likely "bait-and-switch" effect where the algorithm promotes highly clickable but lower-value items. Treat the current 50/50 split as a pre-committed A/B test with **Revenue per Query (RPQ)** as the single North Star metric, not CTR. CTR is a vanity metric here; it rises while business value leaks.

**2. Isolate Algorithm Effect from Seasonality**
To distinguish a structural flaw from a temporary learning phase or seasonal noise, execute a **Difference-in-Differences (DiD)** analysis:
*   **Control Group:** The 50% of traffic on the old algorithm.
*   **Treatment Group:** The 50% on the new algorithm.
*   **Method:** Compare the *change* in RPQ for the Treatment group against the *change* in RPQ for the Control group over the same two-week window. Since both groups experience the exact same seasonal trends and external marketing campaigns, any divergence in the *delta* is attributable to the algorithm, not seasonality.
*   **Verification:** Plot daily RPQ for both cohorts. If the gap widens or stays constant rather than converging, the issue is structural, not a "learning phase."

**3. Diagnose the Funnel Bottom-Up**
The conflict (High CTR, Low AOV) suggests an activation/retention leak in the purchase intent.
*   **Segment by Price Tier:** Break down CTR and Conversion Rate by item price buckets (e.g., <$20, $20-$50, >$50). Hypothesis: The new ranking over-indexes on cheap home goods, inflating CTR but cannibalizing high-margin sales.
*   **Check "Add-to-Cart" vs. "Click":** If clicks are up but add-to-carts are flat, the results are misleading (high relevance to query, low relevance to purchase intent).
*   **Guardrail Metric:** Monitor return rates or customer support tickets for "item not as described." If these rise, the algorithm is optimizing for misleading clicks.

**4. The Call: Ship, Extend, or Revert**
Recompute the net revenue impact immediately using the DiD method.
*   **If Net Revenue (RPQ) is statistically lower:** **Revert** the change before the spring sale. A 5% AOV drop usually outweighs a 10% CTR gain unless conversion volume doubles (which is rare). Do not gamble on a "learning phase" during a peak sales event without evidence of convergence.
*   **If Net Revenue is flat but AOV is down:** **Extend** the test for 7 more days with a **CUPED** variance reduction technique to increase sensitivity, but only if the confidence interval includes a positive upside.
*   **Tripwire:** If RPQ remains negative for 3 consecutive days in the treatment group, trigger an automatic revert.
