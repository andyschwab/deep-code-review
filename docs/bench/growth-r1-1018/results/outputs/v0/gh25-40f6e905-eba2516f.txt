### Evaluation Plan: Net Revenue Impact & Risk Mitigation

**Immediate Status: UNVERIFIED**
The stated "5% lift" and "18% drop" are mid-experiment observations. Without a pre-committed sample size, duration, or sequential testing rule, these figures are susceptible to peeking bias and cannot yet be declared a win. The assumption that lost free users are "low-value" is a hypothesis, not a fact; if those users drive referrals, their loss may degrade long-term revenue more than the immediate subscription lift covers.

**1. Primary Metric & Guardrails**
*   **North Star:** **Net Lifetime Value (LTV) per Cohort**. Do not optimize for immediate subscription revenue alone. Calculate LTV as: *(Subscription Revenue + Ad Revenue from Free Tier) × Retention Curve × Referral Value*.
*   **Primary Decision Metric:** **Total Company Revenue Run Rate** (Subscription + Ad Inventory Value) normalized by new user acquisition cost.
*   **Guardrail Metric:** **Viral Coefficient (k-factor)** and **Brand Sentiment Score** (app store reviews/social mentions). If the viral coefficient drops below 1.0 or sentiment dips >10% from baseline, the "low-value user" assumption is false, and the strategy is eroding the top of the funnel.

**2. Weekly Leading Indicators**
Monitor these weekly to detect negative trends before the six-month mark:
*   **Free-to-Paid Conversion Latency:** Are users converting slower because the paywall creates friction, even if ultimate conversion rises?
*   **Day-1 and Day-3 Retention (Free Tier):** A drop here signals immediate rejection of the value proposition, predicting the Day-7 drop you already see.
*   **Referral Acquisition Rate:** Track the percentage of new sign-ups attributed to existing free users. If this declines, the paywall is killing organic growth.
*   **Churn Reason Taxonomy:** Instrument a specific event `paywall_exit_feedback` to capture *why* users leave. Distinguish between "price objection" (valid) and "feature missing/broken" (fixable).

**3. Segmentation Strategy**
Slice the data to validate or refute the "low-value" assumption:
*   **By Acquisition Channel:** Did organic/social users drop off more than paid ad users? Organic users often have higher viral value; losing them is costlier.
*   **By Geography/Market Maturity:** Compare markets where Melodia is established vs. emerging. Price sensitivity varies; a blanket paywall may work in one region but destroy growth in another.
*   **By Usage Intensity (Pre-Paywall):** Segment the lost free users by their listening hours prior to hitting the paywall. If "high engagement" free users are leaving, they were *not* low-value; they were your best ad inventory and potential converters.
*   **By Device/OS:** Ensure the drop isn't correlated with a specific platform's payment friction (e.g., iOS vs. Android billing flows).

**4. This-Week Steps**
1.  **Define the Stopping Rule:** Commit to a fixed end-date or sample size for this experiment immediately. Do not make a final go/no-go decision based on today's numbers.
2.  **Instrument `referral_source_cohort`:** Ensure every new sign-up is tagged with the status (Free/Paid) of the user who referred them. This is the only way to quantify the viral decay.
3.  **Run a "Holdout" Analysis:** Isolate a small % of new users who *do not* see the paywall. Compare their 30-day LTV against the test group. If the holdout group generates higher total value via ads and referrals, the paywall is a net loss despite the subscription lift.

**Note:** The 18% retention drop is a severe signal; if the viral coefficient declines concurrently, the net revenue impact over six months will likely be negative regardless of short-term subscription gains.
