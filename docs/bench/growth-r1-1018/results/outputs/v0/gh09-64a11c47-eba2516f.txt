### North Star Recommendation: Weekly Active Users Completing a Verified Reduction Action

Your current metric, "logs per week," is a vanity metric; it measures activity, not value. A user can log data without changing behavior, meaning the metric can rise while your actual mission (carbon reduction) stagnates. Similarly, "total carbon saved" is a lagging, cumulative outcome that is difficult to attribute to specific product interactions in the short term and hard to verify without external data.

**The Criteria for Selection**
To select your single North Star, apply these three filters to any candidate:
1.  **Customer Value:** Does the metric only go up when the user actually achieves their goal (reducing footprint)?
2.  **Leading Indicator:** Does it predict future retention and revenue before the financial results appear?
3.  **Actionable:** Can your engineering and product teams directly influence this number through product changes?

**Evaluation of Candidates**
*   **Logs per week:** Fails. Users can log inefficiently or falsely. It rewards noise, not impact.
*   **Total Carbon Saved:** Fails as a primary North Star for daily alignment. It is too aggregate, often relies on estimates rather than verified actions, and moves too slowly for weekly team iteration.
*   **Weekly Active Users Completing a Verified Reduction Action:** **Passes.** This counts users who not only open the app but complete a specific, high-intent behavior (e.g., logging a trip replaced by biking, completing a "meat-free week" challenge, or verifying a utility bill drop). This aligns the team on *behavior change*, which is the precursor to actual carbon reduction.

**Testing "Weekly Carbon Reduced" for Actionability**
You asked how to test if "weekly carbon reduced" is viable given data limitations. Do not guess; instrument the gap.
1.  **Define the Event:** Clearly define what constitutes a "verified reduction." Is it completing a challenge? Is it logging a specific category with a duration?
2.  **The "UNVERIFIED" Check:** If you cannot currently calculate this number because the data isn't captured, the metric is currently `UNVERIFIED`. Your immediate task is not to estimate the value, but to instrument the specific event: `challenge_completed` with property `estimated_co2_saved`.
3.  **Correlation Test:** Once instrumented, run a cohort analysis. Compare the retention rate of users who triggered this event in Week 1 against those who did not. If the "reduction" group retains significantly better, the metric is a valid North Star. If there is no correlation, the metric is not driving value.

**Addressing the Retention vs. Total Saved Debate**
This is a false dichotomy. You must read the funnel bottom-up. If users do not retain (come back to perform reduction actions), "total carbon saved" will flatline regardless of acquisition. Focus on the **frequency of verified reduction actions per active user**. This drives retention (users see impact) and eventually drives the total saved.

**Scaling to Corporate Clients**
This metric scales because it is user-centric. For corporate clients, you simply aggregate the "Verified Reduction Actions" across their employee cohort. The definition of value remains consistent: behavior change.

**This Week's Steps**
1.  **Instrument:** Add tracking for `challenge_completed` and `verified_action_logged` with a carbon estimate property. Do not track "page views."
2.  **Query:** Pull a retention cohort comparing users who performed a verified action in their first week vs. those who only logged data.
3.  **Decide:** If the verified action cohort shows >10% higher Week-4 retention, adopt "Weekly Active Users Completing Verified Reduction" as your North Star for the December 1st meeting.

*Note: Ensure user identifiers used for these cohorts are pseudonymous to comply with privacy standards; route PII handling to your compliance review. Do not fabricate industry benchmarks for "good" retention; compare only against your own baseline once the new events are live.*
