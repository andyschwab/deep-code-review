### Proposed North Star Metric: "Weekly Active Accounts Completing High-Value Report Actions"

**Definition:** The count of unique customer accounts per week where a user generates a report *and* performs a downstream value action (e.g., exporting data, sharing with a team member, or setting a restock alert) within the same session.

**Why this replaces "Reports Generated":**
Your current metric (40 reports/month) is vanity; it rises even if users are frustrated, clicking blindly, or generating useless data. It measures output, not outcome. The proposed North Star measures **realized value**. If a user generates a report but takes no action, they likely derived no value. By tying the metric to a downstream action, you ensure the engineering roadmap prioritizes speed and usability only insofar as they enable that action. Speed is an input, not the goal; the goal is the user successfully acting on their inventory data.

### Framework for Selection & Validation

To align the engineering roadmap before your December 31st fiscal year-end, apply this three-step filter to any proposed metric:

1.  **The Value Test:** Does this metric go up *only* when the customer succeeds? (e.g., If report speed doubles but users still don't export/share, the metric should not rise).
2.  **The Input Link:** Can engineering directly move this via the redesign? (e.g., Reducing load time from 10s to 2s should increase the completion rate of the downstream action).
3.  **The Baseline Reality:** Since you lack historical data linking speed to retention, do not fabricate a benchmark. Establish your baseline *now* using current logs. Measure the current % of reports that result in an action. This is your only valid comparison point for Q3 and Q4 progress.

### Tracking Downstream Effects on Churn (Next Two Quarters)

You cannot wait for annual renewal data to validate the redesign. Instead, construct a **leading indicator funnel** to proxy retention health over the next two quarters:

*   **Step 1: Instrument the "Action" Event.** Immediately define and track the specific event that signifies value (e.g., `report_exported`, `alert_created`). Do not instrument "just in case"; track only what answers: "Did this report drive a decision?"
*   **Step 2: Cohort by Adoption, Not Just Tenure.** Segment users into "High Action" (performing the downstream action >2x/week) vs. "Low Action." Track the retention curve of these two cohorts separately over the next 8 weeks.
*   **Step 3: The Retention Hypothesis.** If your assumption holds (time saved = retention), the redesign should shift users from "Low Action" to "High Action." Monitor if the "High Action" cohort shows significantly lower cancellation intent or support tickets compared to the "Low Action" cohort.
*   **Step 4: The Guardrail.** While optimizing for the North Star, monitor **Report Failure Rate** (technical errors) as a guardrail. A faster system that breaks more often will destroy retention.
