**Executive Recommendation: Diagnose Before Simplifying**

Do not simplify the assessment or add copy yet. The data indicates a **structural friction point**, not necessarily a content problem. Simplifying prematurely risks admitting unqualified candidates who will churn later (retention leak), while motivational copy treats a symptom, not the cause. You must first isolate whether the drop-off is driven by **time-to-complete**, **specific question failure**, or **technical abandonment**.

**1. Micro-Conversions to Extract from Existing Logs**
Since new instrumentation is not an option immediately, query your existing server logs and database timestamps for the period post-March 12th. Isolate these four specific micro-conversions:

*   **Step Entry Rate:** Count of users hitting the `/assessment/start` endpoint vs. the previous step. This confirms if the drop is isolated to the assessment or if users are bailing before even starting.
*   **Time-on-Step Distribution:** Calculate the delta between `start_timestamp` and `end_timestamp` (or `abandon_timestamp`). Segment users into:
    *   *Quick Exits (< 2 mins):* Likely intimidation or technical error.
    *   *Mid-Range (2–15 mins):* Engaged but potentially stuck.
    *   *Long Haul (> 15 mins):* High effort, likely frustrated by length.
    *   *Insight:* If 65% of drop-offs are "Quick Exits," the barrier is perceived difficulty. If they are "Long Haul," the barrier is fatigue/length.
*   **Question-Level Attrition:** If your logs record `question_id` interactions, identify the exact question index where the session ends.
    *   *Insight:* A spike at Question 1 suggests intimidation. A spike at Question 8 suggests fatigue. A spike at a specific complex topic suggests content mismatch.
*   **Error/Exception Rate:** Filter logs for HTTP 500s, JavaScript errors, or timeout events occurring specifically within the assessment window.
    *   *Insight:* If >5% of drop-offs correlate with errors, this is a bug, not a product design issue.

**2. Analysis Structure to Validate Assumption**
Run a **Cohort Retention Analysis** comparing the pre-March 12th cohort (no assessment) vs. the post-March 12th cohort (with assessment). Do not look at signup volume; look at **Day-7 Retention** of those who *did* complete the assessment.

*   **Hypothesis A (Assessment is too rigorous):** If post-assessment completers have significantly *higher* Day-7 retention than the pre-March cohort, the assessment is working as a filter. The 65% drop-off is a feature, not a bug. **Action:** Keep the assessment, but add a progress bar and estimated time copy to reduce anxiety.
*   **Hypothesis B (Assessment is broken/too long):** If post-assessment completers have *flat or lower* Day-7 retention, the assessment is filtering out good candidates or frustrating everyone equally. **Action:** Simplify immediately.

**3. Immediate Next Steps (Before April 1st)**
1.  **Query the "Time-on-Step" distribution** by tomorrow. If the median time exceeds 10 minutes, cut the question count by 50% for the April cohort.
2.  **Check the "Question-Level Attrition."** If one specific question causes 40% of exits, rewrite or remove only that question.
3.  **Implement a "Save & Resume" or Progress Indicator** if none exists. This is a low-code fix that addresses the "fear of the unknown" without lowering standards.

**Verdict:** Do not blindly simplify. If the assessment correlates with higher downstream retention, the friction is valuable. Fix the *experience* (progress, time estimates), not the *rigor*, unless the data proves the rigor destroys retention.

*Note: All metrics above must be compared to your own pre-March 12th baseline; do not use industry benchmarks. If logs lack question-level granularity, the "specific questions" hypothesis remains UNVERIFIED until instrumentation is added.*
