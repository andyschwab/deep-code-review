**Recommendation: Implement a Forward-Looking Normalization Layer with Targeted Historical Correction**

Do not attempt a full retroactive fix on all historical data from the past three months. Instead, deploy an immediate normalization layer for all incoming data effective today, and perform a targeted, sample-based correction only on the specific aggregated reports scheduled for Q4 executive review.

**Risks and Benefits Analysis**

**Option A: Full Retroactive Fix (Not Recommended)**
*   **Benefits:** Theoretically creates a perfectly consistent historical dataset.
*   **Risks:** High operational disruption. Rewriting three months of data across three regions carries a significant risk of introducing new errors during the migration process. It requires substantial engineering downtime, potentially halting current inventory replenishment algorithms while the rewrite occurs. Furthermore, correcting 15% of records lacking millisecond precision is impossible without raw source logs; attempting to infer this data creates "false precision," misleading future analysis. If the aggregated reports already sent to stakeholders contain errors, rewriting the database now does not automatically correct those external communications, creating a version control nightmare between "fixed" internal data and "broken" sent reports.

**Option B: Forward-Looking Normalization + Targeted Historical Correction (Recommended)**
*   **Benefits:** Minimizes immediate operational risk by isolating the fix to the ingestion pipeline. Engineering resources can focus on ensuring 100% accuracy for future data rather than gambling on reconstructing the past. This approach allows GlobalRetail to stabilize Q4 reporting immediately.
*   **Risks:** Historical data remains inconsistent in the raw lake. However, this is mitigated by applying a read-time transformation specifically for the Q4 reports Robert needs to approve.
*   **Strategy for Q4 Reporting:** Since 15% of records lack millisecond precision, exact hourly aggregation for those specific records is unrecoverable. For the Q4 executive report, calculate sales aggregates using the available timestamp data and append a clearly defined confidence interval or footnote stating the margin of error introduced by the missing precision. This is more honest and defensible than presenting fabricated millisecond data.

**Immediate Action Plan for Robert's Approval**
1.  **Engineering Sprint (Days 1-2):** Build and deploy a normalization middleware that converts all incoming timestamps to UTC with mandatory millisecond validation before they hit the warehouse. This stops the bleeding immediately.
2.  **Data Science Task (Days 2-3):** Isolate the specific datasets used for the Q4 stakeholder reports. Apply a best-effort timezone correction to the historical raw logs for these specific subsets only.
3.  **Reporting Protocol:** Generate the Q4 reports using the corrected subset. Where millisecond data is missing, flag the affected aggregates with a quality warning rather than silently guessing. Present this transparently to stakeholders as a "Data Quality Improvement Initiative" rather than hiding the discrepancy.

This strategy secures the integrity of future operations while providing a defensible, transparent path to approve the Q4 numbers without risking a catastrophic database rewrite.

Note: This approach assumes raw logs exist to attempt timezone correction; if raw logs are also corrupted, the historical error margin must be stated as unquantifiable. Ensure legal/compliance reviews the footnote language regarding data precision before distribution.
