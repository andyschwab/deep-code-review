### Holistic Analysis Framework for Guest Checkout Decision

To evaluate the trade-off between the observed 20% immediate conversion lift and the 0% current return rate, you must isolate whether the "guest" behavior is a permanent loss of relationship or a delayed activation. Do not rely on the aggregate 0% figure alone, as it likely includes users who have not yet reached the 30-day mark or those who returned via a different identity.

Run the following four specific cohort analyses to inform the decision:

**1. Identity Resolution & "Hidden" Return Rate Cohort**
*   **Question:** Are guest users returning but remaining anonymous, or are they truly lost?
*   **Action:** Create a cohort of users who checked out as guests between Sept 1–14. Filter this group by those who provided an email address. Cross-reference these emails against all site activity (logins, purchases, cart additions) over the subsequent 30 days, regardless of login status.
*   **Metric to Report:** "Authenticated Return Rate" (users who logged in) vs. "Identified Return Rate" (users who purchased again using the same email, even as a guest). If the Identified Return Rate approaches the 15% baseline, the friction is in account creation, not loyalty.

**2. Email Validity & Marketing Attribution Cohort**
*   **Question:** Is the 0% return rate due to invalid data or failed marketing follow-up?
*   **Action:** Segment the guest checkout cohort by email domain validity (syntax check) and deliverability status (bounce rate). Further split by whether a post-purchase email sequence was successfully delivered.
*   **Metric to Report:** Return rate among guests with *verified, delivered* marketing emails vs. those with bounces or missing emails. This isolates whether the assumption ("capturing emails allows recovery") holds true. If verified emails still yield 0% returns, the channel is ineffective for this segment.

**3. Time-to-Second-Purchase Latency Distribution**
*   **Question:** Is the 30-day window too short to judge guest retention?
*   **Action:** Plot the distribution of days between first and second purchase for both the Guest Cohort (identified by email) and the Registered Cohort.
*   **Metric to Report:** Median time-to-repeat purchase. Fashion retail often has longer repurchase cycles. If registered users typically return on day 45, a 30-day cutoff falsely penalizes the guest variant. Compare the *shape* of the curve, not just the binary 30-day flag.

**4. Lifetime Value (LTV) Bridge Analysis**
*   **Question:** Does the 20% volume increase compensate for lower individual frequency?
*   **Action:** Calculate the total revenue generated per 1,000 visitors for both variants over the test period, including second purchases where identified.
*   **Metric to Report:** Revenue Per Visitor (RPV) = (Conversion Rate × AOV) + (Return Rate × Second Purchase AOV). Compare the RPV of the Guest Variant against the Registered Variant. This single metric mathematically resolves the tension between high acquisition and low retention.

**Required Data Guardrails**
Ensure all email matching is performed on hashed values to maintain privacy compliance. Do not merge these cohorts with operational logs; keep this analysis strictly within the product analytics domain. If the email capture rate during guest checkout is below 90%, the "recovery" assumption is statistically weak regardless of email quality.

Present these four metrics to the marketing director. The decision rests on whether the "Identified Return Rate" and "RPV" of the guest flow meet the business's minimum threshold for sustainable growth, not on the raw 0% figure which likely suffers from identity fragmentation.

*Note: Ensure the email capture field is mandatory during guest checkout to validate the recovery hypothesis; optional fields will render the Email Validity Cohort inconclusive.*
*Do not extend the test duration without pre-committing to a new sample size to avoid statistical peeking errors.*
