### Recommended KPIs for RemoteGear (Next 6 Months)

Given your constraint of two engineers and the critical need to distinguish between product defects and expectation mismatches, stop tracking "total sales" as a primary health metric. It is a vanity metric that rises even if your business is leaking value. Instead, implement these three actionable indicators:

**1. Week-4 Core Usage Retention (The North Star)**
*   **What to measure:** The percentage of customers who report using the desk converter at least 3 times per week, four weeks after delivery.
*   **Why:** Sales prove acquisition; usage proves value. If customers buy but do not use the product by week 4, they will not refer you, and churn is imminent. This separates "buyer's remorse" (expectation mismatch) from "broken product" (defect).
*   **Interpretation:** If usage is high but referrals are low, your product works but lacks a "wow" factor. If usage is low, investigate immediately. Do not guess the number; instrument a single post-purchase email survey or a simple login/event trigger if applicable. Until instrumented, this value is **UNVERIFIED**.

**2. Qualified Referral Rate (The Survival Predictor)**
*   **What to measure:** The number of new sales attributed to a specific existing customer code or link, divided by the total active user base.
*   **Why:** You hypothesize quality drives referrals. This metric validates that hypothesis directly. Unlike raw "support ticket volume" (which only counts unhappy users), this counts happy advocates.
*   **Interpretation:** A rising referral rate with flat marketing spend indicates product-market fit. If this rate is near zero despite 850 units sold, your assumption that "quality drives referrals" is false, and you must pivot to explicit marketing or fix the core experience.

**3. Return Reason Cohort Ratio (The Diagnostic)**
*   **What to measure:** Categorize your existing 8% return rate into two distinct buckets: "Defect/Malfunction" vs. "Not as Expected/Didn't Fit Needs."
*   **Why:** Your engineers cannot fix "returns"; they can only fix specific causes. Aggregating these hides the root cause.
*   **Interpretation:**
    *   If >50% are **Defects**: Engineering must halt feature work to fix quality control. This is an existential threat.
    *   If >50% are **Expectation Mismatch**: Engineering should not touch the hardware. Instead, update product photos, descriptions, or sizing guides. This is a marketing/content fix, saving engineering cycles.

### Implementation Strategy for Two Engineers
Do not build a complex analytics dashboard. For the next quarter, instrument only the data points required to answer these three questions.
*   **Action 1:** Add a mandatory dropdown to the return portal forcing the "Defect" vs. "Expectation" choice. Analyze the current 8% manually if the system allows export; otherwise, tag future returns immediately.
*   **Action 2:** Implement a simple "Refer a Friend" code generation at the point of support resolution or post-delivery email. Track redemptions in your sales ledger.
*   **Action 3:** Send a single automated survey at Day 30 asking: "How many days last week did you use your desk?"

### Board Narrative
Present this shift as moving from "lagging financial outputs" (sales) to "leading behavioral inputs" (usage and referrals). Clarify that the 8% return rate is currently **UNVERIFIED** regarding cause; the first engineering sprint will resolve this classification to direct resources correctly. Profitability by Q4 depends on lowering the cost of acquisition via referrals (KPI #2) and ensuring the product actually stays on desks (KPI #1).

*Note: All benchmarks must be compared against your own Month-1 baseline; do not seek industry averages which vary wildly by hardware category. If data for Week-4 usage is not yet collected, report it as "Pending Instrumentation" rather than estimating.*
