To assess fairness and effectiveness before automation, execute the following three-step framework immediately.

**1. Fairness Audit: Segment by Protected and Behavioral Attributes**
Do not rely on the aggregate 78% accuracy. Slice the model's performance (precision, recall, and false positive rate) across these specific segments to identify bias:
*   **Demographics:** Age bands, gender, and location (specifically comparing France vs. Germany to detect regional training bias).
*   **Subscription Tenure:** New users (<3 months) vs. loyal users (>12 months). High false positives here indicate you are penalizing loyalists with unnecessary discounts.
*   **Usage Intensity:** High-frequency users vs. dormant users.
*   **Plan Type:** Premium vs. Basic tiers.

Calculate the **False Positive Rate (FPR)** for each segment. If loyal, high-value users have a significantly higher FPR than new users, the model is unfairly targeting them for discounts they don't need, eroding margin without saving churn. Report these disparities to the London compliance officer; a variance in FPR >5% between protected groups usually flags a bias risk.

**2. Incremental Impact: The "Ghost" Holdout Design**
You cannot measure the model's true value by simply comparing churn before and after. You must isolate the *incremental* lift of the intervention.
*   **Setup:** From the 15,000 weekly high-risk users, randomly withhold 10–20% (the **Holdout Group**) from receiving the discount email. They are scored as high-risk but receive business-as-usual (no offer).
*   **Measurement:** Compare the churn rate of the **Treated Group** (got discount) vs. the **Holdout Group** (no discount).
*   **Success Metric:** The model is only effective if `Churn(Holdout) - Churn(Treated) > 0`. If both groups churn at the same rate, the discount is wasted spend on users who would have stayed anyway (persuadables vs. sleeping dogs).

**3. Cost-Benefit Analysis: False Positives vs. False Negatives**
Define your loss function to optimize the decision threshold, not just accuracy:
*   **False Positive Cost:** Giving a discount to a user who would *not* have churned. Cost = Discount Amount + Margin Erosion.
*   **False Negative Cost:** Failing to catch a user who *does* churn. Cost = Customer Lifetime Value (LTV) lost.
*   **Decision Rule:** If LTV is high and discount cost is low, tolerate more False Positives. If margins are thin, tighten the threshold to reduce False Positives, even if you miss some churners.
