### Balanced Scorecard for Ontario Business License Renewal Portal

To satisfy both taxpayer accountability and user experience, success must be measured by **value delivered**, not just cost saved. The current focus on reduced call volume is a "vanity metric" in isolation; it rises even if users are abandoning the portal out of frustration. We must shift to a "North Star" metric that only increases when a business successfully renews or applies.

**1. The North Star: Weekly Successful Completions**
*   **Definition:** The count of unique businesses that fully submit a renewal or new application each week.
*   **Why it matters:** This is the only number that proves the portal is working. If call volume drops but this number stays flat or falls, users are not saving time—they are giving up. This directly answers the audit question: "Are citizens getting their licenses?"

**2. Efficiency & Accountability (The "Taxpayer" View)**
*   **End-to-End Completion Rate:** The percentage of users who start a renewal/application and successfully finish it.
    *   *Action:* Segment this by "Legacy Migrated Users" vs. "First-Time Applicants." Your unknown regarding first-time applicants is solved by tracking this specific funnel. If legacy users complete at 80% but new applicants drop to 20%, the onboarding flow is broken.
*   **Average Time-to-Submit:** The median time from landing on the site to hitting "submit."
    *   *Why:* This validates the speed claim. If time-to-submit is high, the "digital transformation" is adding friction, not removing it.

**3. User Experience & Friction (The "Citizen" View)**
*   **Abandonment Rate by Step:** The percentage of users who quit at each specific screen (e.g., document upload, payment).
    *   *Why:* This tells us exactly where the process is failing. A high drop-off at "document upload" indicates a technical barrier, not a lack of intent.
*   **Support Contact Rate per 100 Submissions:** Instead of raw call volume, measure how many calls occur for every 100 *successful* completions.
    *   *Why:* Raw call volume dropped 25%, but if submissions dropped 40%, the *rate* of confusion actually increased. This ratio isolates true usability issues from simple traffic fluctuations.

**4. Implementation Plan for the Quarterly Audit**
Do not instrument "everything." Only track events that answer these specific questions:
*   *Did they finish?* (Track: `renewal_started`, `renewal_submitted`)
*   *Where did they quit?* (Track: `step_abandoned` with the step name)
*   *Who are they?* (Use a stable, pseudonymous ID to distinguish legacy vs. new applicants without exposing personal data).

**Immediate Next Steps:**
1.  **Define the Baseline:** Calculate the current completion rate for the 30,000 migrated accounts using the last 30 days of data.
2.  **Instrument the Unknown:** Implement tracking specifically for the `application_started` to `application_submitted` funnel for non-legacy users to resolve the critical unknown before the audit.
3.  **Set the Guardrail:** Establish a rule that if the "Support Contact Rate per 100 Submissions" rises, no further marketing or migration occurs until the friction point is resolved.

This approach moves the narrative from "we saved money on calls" (which could mean users gave up) to "we successfully processed X% more licenses in less time" (which proves value).

*Note: All metrics must be compared against your own pre-migration baseline or month-over-month trends; do not rely on external industry benchmarks which may not reflect Ontario's specific regulatory context.*
*Ensure all user identifiers used for cohort analysis are pseudonymized and routed to privacy compliance protocols before analysis.*
