**Evaluation: Do Not Ship High-Frequency Defaults**

The proposed change to make "three times daily" the default setting must be **rejected**. While the 15% lift in task completion is positive, the doubling of the unsubscribe rate to 4% within three weeks indicates a fundamental breach of user trust that contradicts your long-term subscription model.

**Recomputed Impact & Trade-off**
You are trading short-term activity for long-term revenue. In a subscription business, retention is the primary driver of Lifetime Value (LTV). A 4% unsubscribe rate in just three weeks projects to an unsustainable annual churn rate that no 15% engagement lift can mathematically offset. You are effectively paying for engagement with your user base size. The "win" on completion rates is likely inflated by a novelty effect or immediate compliance, but the churn signal is a verified verdict that the frequency is perceived as spam.

**Confounding Factors & Risks**
1.  **Peeking Risk:** If the decision to stop or continue was influenced by watching these numbers daily before the month ended, the statistical significance is `UNVERIFIED`. However, even without formal significance testing, a doubling of churn is a massive effect size that demands caution.
2.  **Segmentation Masking:** The aggregate 4% churn may hide that a specific subset of users (e.g., new vs. power users) is driving the exodus.
3.  **Guardrail Failure:** The unsubscribe rate acted as a guardrail metric and failed catastrophically. A win on a primary metric (completion) that breaks a guardrail (retention) is a net loss.

**Decision & Tripwire**
**Call:** Abandon the high-frequency approach as the *default* for all new sign-ups.
**Tripwire:** If any frequency tier exceeds a 1.5% unsubscribe rate in a 14-day window (half your observed spike), that tier is automatically disabled. Your current 4% is nearly triple this safe threshold.
