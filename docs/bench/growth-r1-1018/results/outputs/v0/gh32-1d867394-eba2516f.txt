**Decision: Do not roll out globally. Revert the variant for legacy users immediately and extend the experiment for new users only.**

The 12% overall lift is a net positive that masks a critical failure in your highest-value segment. Because legacy users constitute 40% of revenue, a "significant drop" in their engagement and report generation represents an unacceptable risk of long-term churn that outweighs the short-term conversion gain from newer, unproven users. The experiment design failed to pre-commit a **guardrail metric** specifically for legacy user engagement; without this, the global win is invalid.

**Immediate Actions:**
1.  **Segmented Rollback:** Revert the paywall placement to the dashboard for users with >6 months tenure effective today to stop the bleeding on report generation.
2.  **Isolated Continuation:** Keep the variant active *only* for users <6 months to validate if the 10% conversion lift holds without the noise of legacy user friction.
3.  **Recompute Impact:** Calculate the net revenue impact by applying the observed drop in legacy report generations against their average lifetime value (LTV). If the projected LTV loss exceeds the immediate conversion revenue gain from new users, the variant is a net failure regardless of the conversion rate.

**Tripwire for Final Decision:**
If the isolated test for new users does not show a statistically significant conversion lift (>10%) after reaching the pre-committed sample size (calculated based on your current traffic), stop the test entirely. Do not optimize for new user acquisition at the expense of core revenue stability.

*Note: The conflicting data indicates a segmentation error in the initial hypothesis; future tests must define primary and guardrail metrics per cohort before launch.*
