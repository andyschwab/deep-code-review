To redefine SketchSync's activation event, your user research must isolate the specific moment value is realized by testing three logical hypotheses against your current "board created" baseline. The framework below outlines the factors to evaluate before selecting a new primary event.

**1. The "Empty Room" Hypothesis (Solo vs. Social Value)**
Currently, 40% create a board, but 85% churn. This suggests creating an empty board provides little standalone value. You must determine if the tool's core value is intrinsic (a better whiteboard than competitors) or extrinsic (the network effect of collaboration).
*   **Research Factor:** Distinguish between users who create a board but never invite anyone versus those who invite but receive no response. If both groups churn at similar rates, the activation event cannot be "invite sent." The value likely lies in the *response* (the second person joining) or the *interaction* (real-time editing), not the invitation attempt.

**2. The "First Collaborative Action" Hypothesis (Invitation vs. Interaction)**
You suspect true activation requires a second person, but you must pinpoint whether the value trigger is the *arrival* of the guest or the *act* of collaborating.
*   **Research Factor:** Investigate the delta between "guest joined" and "guest edited." Did the host feel value the moment the cursor appeared (validation of the tool working), or only after marks were made together? If the host derives no value until ink hits the canvas from two sources, your activation event must be "first concurrent edit," not "guest joined." Qualitative interviews should ask: "At what exact second did you feel this tool was necessary for your work?"

**3. The "Time-to-Value" vs. "Completion" Trap**
Activation is often mistaken for completing a setup step. For a collaborative tool, activation is a state of dependency, not a checkbox.
*   **Research Factor:** Analyze the time gap between sign-up and the potential new event. If the "aha" moment requires scheduling a meeting with a colleague, a 24-hour window may be too narrow, artificially lowering activation rates. However, if users who take >48 hours to collaborate never return, the friction is in the invitation flow, not the definition of the event. Research must verify if the delay is due to user intent (planning a future meeting) or friction (unable to find the invite link).

**4. The Retention Correlation Check**
A valid activation event must correlate strongly with long-term retention. Your current metric (board created) fails this, as evidenced by the 15% Day-3 return rate.
*   **Research Factor:** Do not rely on assumptions. Map the retention curves of three distinct cohorts: (A) Board Created only, (B) Guest Joined, and (C) Collaborative Edit Occurred. The correct activation event is the earliest action where the retention curve flattens significantly compared to the others. If Cohort B and C have identical retention, the simpler event (Guest Joined) is preferable for optimization. If Cohort C retains 2x better than B, the complexity of measuring "collaboration" is justified.

**Recommendation for Interview Design**
Structure your qualitative interviews to reconstruct the user's timeline minute-by-minute during their first session. Ask specifically about their emotional state before and after the second person arrived and began interacting. Avoid asking "Was it useful?" which invites rationalization; instead ask, "Would you have canceled your subscription if that second person had never joined?" This reveals whether the value was in the tool or the specific collaborative instance.

*Note: Ensure your analytics can technically distinguish between a passive viewer and an active editor before finalizing the event definition.*
*Do not optimize for the invitation rate until you confirm that an unaccepted invitation yields zero retention value.*
