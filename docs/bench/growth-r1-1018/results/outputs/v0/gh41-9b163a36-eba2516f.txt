### Activation Definitions to Test for GeoCrowd

Based on your data, "adding a point" is a vanity activation metric because it correlates with 40% churn. "Editing a road segment" is a strong signal but too rare (15%) to serve as the primary activation gate for your whole funnel. You need a definition that balances predictive power with frequency. Here are three concrete definitions to test, ranked by recommended priority:

**1. The "Meaningful Contribution" Threshold (Hybrid Action)**
*   **Definition:** A user is activated when they complete **either** one road segment edit **OR** three distinct point-of-interest (POI) additions with attributes (e.g., name + category) within their first 3 days.
*   **Pros:** Captures the high-retention "editor" behavior while allowing "power contributors" who add many POIs to qualify. It filters out the 40% of single-POI dropouts without excluding valid users who haven't found a road to edit yet.
*   **Cons:** Requires slightly more complex event taxonomy to track "distinct" items and attribute completeness.

**2. The "Repeat Value" Moment (Frequency-Based)**
*   **Definition:** A user is activated upon their **second separate session** where they perform *any* contribution (edit or POI add) within the first 7 days.
*   **Pros:** Directly measures the habit formation you are missing. If they return to contribute again, they have already passed the "day 3 churn" cliff. This aligns activation strictly with retention.
*   **Cons:** Delays the "activated" label for users who do everything in one long session (though your data suggests these users may churn anyway). It makes the activation rate lower initially, which can be uncomfortable for stakeholders until retention improves.

**3. The "Depth of Engagement" Score (Composite)**
*   **Definition:** A user is activated when they reach a score of 5 points: 1 point per POI added, 3 points per road edit, and 2 points for adding a photo/tag to an existing item.
*   **Pros:** Accounts for your hypothesis about "diversity of map areas" and effort. It rewards deep interaction over simple clicks, likely correlating strongly with long-term community membership.
*   **Cons:** Hardest to explain to executives ("Why is 5 points the magic number?"). Risks overfitting if the weighting isn't validated against historical retention cohorts first.

**Recommended Next Step for Tuesday's Review**
Do not declare a winner yet. Propose **Option 1** as the primary hypothesis for an A/B test starting immediately.
*   **Experiment Design:** Split new sign-ups. Group A sees onboarding guiding them to "Add a POI" (current). Group B sees onboarding guiding them to "Fix a Road or Add 3 Detailed POIs" (new threshold).
*   **Success Metric:** Retention at Day 7 (not just activation rate).
*   **Guardrail:** Ensure Group B does not have a significantly lower *overall* contribution volume in week 1.
