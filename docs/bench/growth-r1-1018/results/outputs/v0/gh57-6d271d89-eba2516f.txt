### Experiment Readout: Customization Engine Rollout

**1. Recomputed Impact & Current Status**
The observed 15% lower churn among customizers is currently **`UNVERIFIED`** as a causal win. Without a pre-committed test duration or sequential testing rule, this early read risks "peeking" bias (false positives). Furthermore, the 48-hour fulfillment delay introduces a systemic risk to the 80% of users *not* customizing, potentially causing cancellations due to perceived shipping inconsistencies. We cannot declare victory on retention until we confirm the net impact across the entire cohort.

**2. Required Metrics Framework**
To make a valid Go/No-Go decision, you must isolate the **Primary Metric** from the **Guardrail Metrics** using the existing 20% test group vs. the 80% control group.

*   **Primary Metric (North Star Input):** *Net Retention Rate* (Aggregate).
    *   Do not look only at customizers. Calculate the weighted average retention of the *entire* 10,000 subscribers. If the 15% gain in the 20% group is offset by even a 4% lift in churn in the 80% group (due to delayed shipments), the net result is negative.
*   **Guardrail Metric 1 (Operational):** *On-Time Delivery Rate (OTD)* for Non-Customizers.
    *   Track if the 48-hour delay for custom orders is creating a bottleneck that pushes standard orders past their promised window. A drop here is the leading indicator of future churn in the control group.
*   **Guardrail Metric 2 (Financial):** *Fulfillment Cost Per Unit*.
    *   Verify if the manual intervention or system logic for swapping items increases variable costs beyond the lifetime value (LTV) gained from the retained users.

**3. Decision Logic & Tripwires**
Do not rely on the "15% lower churn" headline. Apply the following logic to the aggregated data:

*   **GO (Launch to 100%):** Only if *Net Retention* is positive AND *On-Time Delivery* for non-customizers remains within 2% of the baseline. The gain must outweigh the operational drag.
*   **ITERATE (Hold at 20% or Pause):** If *On-Time Delivery* for non-customizers drops by >2%. This confirms the hypothesis that the delay hurts the majority. The feature creates a "leaky bucket" where acquisition spend is wasted on users who leave due to late standard boxes.
*   **STOP:** If *Net Retention* is flat or negative. The operational cost (48h delay) exceeds the value of the customization feature.

**4. This-Week Steps**
1.  **Query Aggregate Cohorts:** Run a retention query comparing the *total* test group (2k users) vs. the *total* control group (8k users) over the last 7 days. Do not segment by "used feature" yet; look at the group assignment to capture spillover effects.
2.  **Audit Shipping Logs:** Pull the *On-Time Delivery Rate* for the control group specifically. If this number has dipped since the rollout started, the operational delay is already causing damage.
3.  **Set the Tripwire:** Define the break-even point. If the control group churn increases by just 3%, does it erase the 15% gain in the test group? (Math: 0.20 * 15% gain = 3% total pool gain. If control churn rises >3%, you are losing money). Present this specific threshold to the COO.
