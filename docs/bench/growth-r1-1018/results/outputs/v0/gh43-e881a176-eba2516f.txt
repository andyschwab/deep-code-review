### Diagnostic Analysis Plan: Mandatory Verification Churn

**Primary Hypothesis:** The 35% drop-off is driven by **friction and trust deficits** at the point of request, not by the long-term value of verification (proven by higher retention among completers). The "worrying trend" is an activation leak, not a retention failure.

#### 1. Quantitative Segmentation (Slice the Data)
Stop looking at aggregate churn. Slice the 35% drop-off cohort by these specific dimensions to isolate the blocker:

*   **Device & Platform:** Compare drop-off rates between Mobile Web, iOS App, and Android App. If mobile web is significantly higher, the issue is likely UX friction (copy-pasting codes across apps) rather than trust.
*   **Traffic Source:** Segment by acquisition channel (e.g., Paid Ads vs. Organic vs. Referral). Users from high-intent organic search may tolerate friction better than cold traffic from ads. A spike in drop-off for paid traffic suggests a mismatch between ad promise and verification reality.
*   **Time-to-Code:** Analyze the time delta between "Request Code" and "Code Entered." If users abandon specifically after >60 seconds, the root cause is **delivery latency** (SMS/Email delays). If they abandon immediately (<5 seconds), the root cause is **trust/friction** (refusal to provide data).
*   **Failure vs. Abandonment:** Distinguish between users who entered an invalid code multiple times (technical/formatting issue) vs. those who never entered a code (psychological/trust issue).
*   **Geography:** Check if specific regions with stricter privacy norms or known SMS delivery issues show disproportionate drop-off.

#### 2. Qualitative Signals (Listen to the User)
Data tells you *where* they leave; qualitative signals tell you *why*. Gather these immediately:

*   **Session Replays:** Watch 20–30 recordings of users hitting the verification screen. Look for "rage clicks," rapid back-button usage, or switching tabs (indicating they are looking for the code but can't find it).
*   **Support Ticket Tags:** Filter support tickets from Nov 1st onward for keywords: "SMS," "code," "phone," "privacy," "spam," or "why." A cluster of "Why do you need my phone?" tickets confirms a trust deficit.
*   **Exit Survey Micro-intercept:** If possible, trigger a one-question survey on the abandonment event: "What stopped you from verifying?" with options: "Didn't receive code," "Concerned about privacy," "Too much effort," or "Will do later."

#### 3. Immediate Actionable Steps (Before Friday)
*   **Calculate the "Break-Even" Friction:** You noted retained users are stickier. Calculate exactly how many *additional* verified users you need to offset the loss of the 30% who left. If the math shows you need a 50% improvement in conversion to break even on revenue, the current design is unsustainable.
*   **A/B Test a "Soft" Gate (if feasible):** Can you allow browsing/purchasing up to a certain limit before forcing verification? Test if delaying the ask until *after* the first value moment reduces the drop-off.
*   **Trust Copy Audit:** Review the micro-copy on the verification screen. Does it explicitly state *why* the phone number is needed (e.g., "For account recovery only, never for marketing")? Lack of explanation is a primary driver of trust-based abandonment.

**Conclusion for VP:** The data suggests the feature successfully filters for high-quality users (higher retention) but creates a prohibitive barrier to entry. The recommendation is not an immediate rollback, but an urgent **optimization sprint** focused on delivery speed and trust messaging. If the "Time-to-Code" is high, fix the provider. If "Immediate Abandonment" is high, rewrite the value proposition. Roll back only if the break-even math proves the lost volume cannot be recovered.

*Note: Do not compare these rates to industry benchmarks; use your pre-Nov 1st baseline as the only valid comparison. Ensure all user identifiers used in this analysis are pseudonymized per privacy compliance standards.*
