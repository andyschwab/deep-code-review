**Recommended North Star Metric:** **Weekly Active Agencies Completing a Verified Reporting Cycle.**

This metric counts an agency as "active" only when a user completes the full workflow: ingesting sensor data, generating a report, and exporting or submitting it for official use. It replaces "login frequency" (a vanity metric) with evidence of value delivery. If the dashboard reduces reporting time by 30%, the frequency of *completed cycles* should rise, even if logins remain low (e.g., users log in once to finish a week's work efficiently).

**Validation Strategy Against Data Gaps:**
Your current data shows login frequency but lacks visibility into what happens *after* login. To validate this metric without fabricating benchmarks:
1.  **Instrument the "End" Event:** Define and track a specific `report_exported` or `report_submitted` event. Without this, any claim of "time saved" is `UNVERIFIED`.
2.  **Establish a Baseline:** Measure the current rate of completed cycles per agency. Compare this against your internal hypothesis: if users save 30% time, can they complete more cycles or handle more sensors per cycle?
3.  **Cohort Analysis:** Segment by city. If one city has higher completion rates despite similar login stats, investigate their workflow (qualitative follow-up) rather than assuming a global adoption failure.

**Immediate Next Steps:**
*   **This Week:** Add instrumentation for the `report_completed` event to distinguish between "looking at data" and "achieving the outcome."
*   **Next Week:** Run a retention cohort analysis on agencies that completed at least one cycle in November; determine if they returned to complete another cycle in December/January.

**Note:** Do not cite external "good adoption" percentages; compare your completion rate only against your own baseline trend. Ensure user identifiers used for agency cohorts are pseudonymous to comply with civic data privacy standards.
