**Recommendation: Prioritize "Weekly Active Teams Completing a Conflict-Free Merge After a Real-Time Session" as your North Star Metric.**

Do not prioritize concurrent session counts (vanity), raw time-to-merge reduction (noisy), or qualitative satisfaction scores (lagging/subjective) for the first three months.

**Reasoning**
Your stated hypothesis is that real-time collaboration reduces merge conflicts. Therefore, the metric must rise *only* when that specific value is delivered.
*   **Concurrent Sessions:** This is a vanity metric. Teams can edit simultaneously and still generate massive conflicts upon saving. High session counts with high conflict rates mean you are scaling friction, not value.
*   **Time-to-Merge:** This is too noisy for an initial launch. Merge times fluctuate based on code complexity, reviewer availability, and CI pipeline health. Isolating the impact of real-time editing on this aggregate number in the first 90 days will be statistically difficult and likely inconclusive.
*   **Satisfaction Scores:** While useful for qualitative feedback, they do not prove behavioral change. Users may feel "happy" about the feature while still reverting to old, conflict-prone workflows.

**The Proposed North Star**
Define the event: A team initiates a real-time session, edits a file, and subsequently merges a pull request without a recorded merge conflict resolution step (or with a conflict resolution time under a defined threshold, e.g., <5 mins).

This metric directly validates your core business case: saving the four hours per week currently lost to conflicts. If this number rises, the business value is realized. If session counts rise but this metric stays flat, you have a product problem, not a distribution problem.

**Input Metrics to Watch**
To move the North Star, track these specific inputs:
1.  **Activation Rate:** % of daily active teams that start at least one real-time session.
2.  **Retention of Core Action:** % of teams that return to use real-time editing for a second session within 7 days.
3.  **Conflict Avoidance Rate:** The ratio of merges following a real-time session that require zero manual conflict resolution.

**Implementation Note**
Since you are pre-launch, do not instrument "everything." Define the specific event schema for "Real-Time Session Start," "Session End," and "Merge Completed" with a property flagging "Conflict Detected (Y/N)." Ensure you use a stable, pseudonymous team identifier to track cohorts across sessions, routing any PII handling to your privacy compliance protocols.

**This Week's Step**
Before sprint planning tomorrow, define the exact database event or log line that signifies a "Conflict-Free Merge" in your current system. If this data point does not currently exist, your first engineering task is not building the editor, but instrumenting the baseline conflict detection so you can measure the lift in Q1. Without this, any Q1 success claim will be `UNVERIFIED`.

*Note: This recommendation assumes your current CI/CD pipeline can reliably flag merge conflicts. If it cannot, the "Conflict Avoidance Rate" becomes an unmeasurable target until that instrumentation is added.*
