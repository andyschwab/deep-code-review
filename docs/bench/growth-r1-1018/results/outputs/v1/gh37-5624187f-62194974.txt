**Recommendation: Implement a Forward-Looking Normalization Layer Only**

Do not attempt a retroactive fix on the historical data from the past three months. Instead, deploy a normalization layer immediately for all future incoming data and flag the existing Q4 aggregates as "Provisional – Subject to Revision."

**Risks and Benefits Analysis**

**Option A: Retroactive Fix (Not Recommended)**
*   **Risks:** High operational disruption. Rewriting three months of data across London, Tokyo, and New York servers risks referential integrity errors in downstream inventory algorithms that have already executed based on the flawed timestamps. There is a significant danger of "double counting" or losing transactions during the rewrite process. Furthermore, recalculating hourly sales aggregations may invalidate reports already sent to stakeholders, forcing a confusing cycle of re-issuance that erodes trust more than a single transparency notice would.
*   **Benefits:** Theoretical data consistency across the entire quarter. However, given the 15% missing millisecond precision, a perfect restoration is impossible; you can only approximate, which introduces a new layer of uncertainty into the "corrected" data.

**Option B: Forward-Looking Normalization (Recommended)**
*   **Benefits:** Immediate stabilization of Q4 reporting moving forward. This approach isolates the engineering effort to the ingestion pipeline, ensuring all new data entering the system from today until the end of Q4 is accurate. It allows your team to quantify the exact impact of the error by comparing pre-fix and post-fix trends without destabilizing live inventory operations. It minimizes the risk of introducing new bugs into historical records.
*   **Risks:** The historical data (August 20 – Present) remains inconsistent. Stakeholders must be informed that early Q4 trends have a known margin of error. However, since the error is systematic (timezone shifts and missing precision), statistical adjustments can be applied to high-level executive summaries without touching the raw database rows.

**Strategic Execution Plan for CDO Approval**

1.  **Immediate Action:** Engineering to deploy a timezone normalization script and millisecond padding routine in the ingestion layer for all three regions by tomorrow evening.
2.  **Communication Strategy:** Issue a brief addendum to previous stakeholder reports noting a "known timestamp synchronization issue affecting August–October hourly granularity," assuring them that daily and weekly totals remain within acceptable variance limits for decision-making.
3.  **Impact Assessment:** Run a parallel analysis on a sample set of historical data to estimate the maximum deviation in inventory replenishment triggers. If the deviation is below the safety stock threshold, no further action on history is required.
4.  **Resource Allocation:** Direct 100% of approved engineering resources to validating the new normalization layer and building a dashboard that visually separates "Verified" (post-fix) vs. "Unverified" (pre-fix) data periods.

This strategy prioritizes operational continuity and data integrity for the remainder of the critical Q4 period over an unattainable perfection of past records.

*Note: The 15% of records lacking millisecond precision cannot be retroactively recovered; any "fix" would be an estimation, further validating the decision to leave historical raw data untouched.*
