To redefine SketchSync's activation event, your research must isolate the specific moment value transitions from potential to realized. Since your current metric ("board created") yields high initial completion but low Day-3 retention, it measures effort, not value. The logical framework for your interviews should test three competing hypotheses against the user's own behavior patterns, focusing on the "collaborative loop" rather than individual actions.

**1. Test the "Empty Room" Hypothesis (Solo vs. Social Value)**
Your data suggests that creating a board provides no inherent stickiness. The first factor to investigate is whether the tool offers any standalone utility.
*   **The Logic:** If a user creates a board but never invites anyone, do they derive value from organizing their own thoughts? If the answer is no, then "board created" is a vanity metric because the product's core promise (collaboration) remains unfulfilled.
*   **Interview Focus:** Ask users who created boards but didn't return: "What were you hoping to achieve alone?" and "What stopped you from inviting someone?" If they expected immediate collaboration, the friction lies in the invitation flow, not the board creation.

**2. Distinguish Between "Invitation" and "Interaction" (The True Aha Moment)**
You hypothesize that a second person joining is the key, but you must determine if the value trigger is the *act of inviting* or the *act of co-editing*.
*   **The Logic:** An invitation is a hope; a co-editing session is a realized outcome. A user might invite ten people who never join (zero value realized), or one person might join instantly and co-edit for ten minutes (high value realized).
*   **Interview Focus:** Segment your interview candidates into two groups: those who invited but had no joiners, and those who had joiners. Ask the first group if they felt the product worked. Ask the second group specifically about the moment they saw the cursor move or the ink appear from another user. The activation event should be the earliest point where the user *observes* successful collaboration, not just the attempt.

**3. Validate the "Time-to-Value" Window**
Activation is not just an action; it is an action within a specific timeframe. Your 40% stat is within 24 hours, but retention drops after day three.
*   **The Logic:** If the "second person joins" event happens on day 5, the user has likely already churned mentally. The framework must define the maximum acceptable latency between sign-up and the first collaborative interaction.
*   **Interview Focus:** For users who returned, ask: "How long after signing up did you first collaborate?" If the successful cohort consistently collaborates within hour one, while the churned cohort delays collaboration, your activation definition must include a time bound (e.g., "First collaborative session within 24 hours").

**Decision Matrix for the New Event**
Do not select a new metric until qualitative data answers these binary questions:
*   **Is solo usage valuable?** If No → Discard "board created" entirely.
*   **Is an unaccepted invitation valuable?** If No → The event must require a second user's presence, not just an invite sent.
*   **Is passive viewing valuable?** If No → The event must require active co-editing (e.g., two cursors moving or simultaneous edits), not just joining.

Your new activation event will likely shift from a single-user action ("board created") to a multi-user state ("first synchronous edit by ≥2 users"). However, this framework ensures you validate that specific state through user testimony before instrumenting it as your North Star input.

Note: Ensure your interview script explicitly asks about the emotional shift from "setting up" to "working together" to pinpoint the exact psychological trigger.
Remember that without validating the "second person" hypothesis, optimizing for invitations may only increase noise, not retention.
