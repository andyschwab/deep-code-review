**Deliverable: North Star Recommendation & Validation Plan for GreenStep**

**Recommended North Star:** **Weekly Active Users Completing a Verified Reduction Action.**
Reject "logs per week" (vanity/activity) and "total carbon saved" (cumulative/lagging). Your mission is behavior change, not data entry. A user logging 10 times without changing habits creates zero impact. Conversely, a user logging once to confirm a switched commute has delivered value. This metric rises only when a user both engages (active) and achieves the core outcome (verified reduction).

**Selection Criteria for Your All-Hands (Dec 1):**
To resolve the retention vs. impact debate, apply these three filters to any candidate metric:
1.  **Causality:** Does the metric go up *only* when the user gets real value? (Logging fails this; verified reduction passes).
2.  **Leading Indicator:** Does it predict future retention/revenue before the financial result happens? (Weekly active reducers predict LTV better than cumulative totals).
3.  **Scalability:** Can it be calculated consistently for individuals and aggregated for corporate clients without redefinition? (Individual actions aggregate cleanly to corporate dashboards).

**Testing "Weekly Carbon Reduced" for Actionability:**
You suspect data limitations regarding infrequent loggers. Do not guess; instrument the specific question.
1.  **Define the Event:** Create a single event `reduction_verified` with properties: `action_type` (e.g., transit, diet), `estimated_kg_co2`, and `verification_method` (manual vs. API-connected).
2.  **The Cohort Query:** Run a retention cohort analysis comparing two groups over 4 weeks:
    *   Group A: High frequency loggers (>2.4/week) with low `reduction_verified` count.
    *   Group B: Low frequency loggers (<1/week) with high `reduction_verified` count.
    *   *Hypothesis:* If Group B retains better or refers more users, your North Star must weight "verified reductions" over "logins."
3.  **Feasibility Check:** If you cannot currently calculate `estimated_kg_co2` reliably, the metric is **UNVERIFIED**. Your immediate step is not to adopt the metric, but to instrument the `verification_method` property to determine if you can auto-calculate impact or must rely on user estimates.

**This Week's Steps:**
1.  **Instrument:** Add the `reduction_verified` event and `verification_method` property to your tracking plan immediately. Do not track "page views" or generic "logs" unless they tie to this event.
2.  **Query:** Pull the retention curve for users who triggered `reduction_verified` at least once in their first 7 days vs. those who only logged without verifying.
3.  **Decide:** If the "verified" group shows >10% higher Week-4 retention than the "logging only" group, commit to "Weekly Active Reducers" as your North Star on Dec 1. If data is missing, mark the metric as UNVERIFIED and delay the decision until instrumentation is live.

*Note: Ensure user identifiers used for these cohorts are pseudonymous; route any PII handling for corporate reporting to your privacy compliance review. Do not fabricate industry benchmarks for "good" retention; compare only against your own baseline trends.*
