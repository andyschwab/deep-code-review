**Activation Hypotheses for GeoCrowd (QBR Options)**

Based on your data, "adding a point" is a vanity activation metric because it fails to predict retention. "Editing a road segment" is a strong predictor but too rare (15%) to serve as the primary activation gate for the whole cohort. You must test definitions that bridge this gap. Here are three concrete definitions to present:

**Option 1: The "Meaningful Contribution" Threshold**
*   **Definition:** A user is activated when they perform **3 distinct edits** (any type) within their first 48 hours.
*   **Pros:** Filters out "tourists" who add one point and leave, while being more attainable than the single "road edit" requirement. It measures commitment depth rather than a specific feature click.
*   **Cons:** May still exclude users who make one high-quality, complex edit that takes significant time. Requires tuning the number "3" based on historical data.

**Option 2: The "Diversity of Impact" Definition**
*   **Definition:** A user is activated when they touch **2 different map categories** (e.g., adding a point AND editing a road, or editing roads in two different neighborhoods) within the first week.
*   **Pros:** Directly tests your hypothesis about "diversity of map areas." It encourages users to explore the tool's full value proposition, likely driving the "aha" moment deeper than a single action.
*   **Cons:** More complex to instrument and explain in onboarding. Might confuse new users if the UI doesn't clearly guide them to multiple categories quickly.

**Option 3: The "Social Validation" Trigger**
*   **Definition:** A user is activated when they make an edit **AND receive feedback** (acceptance, comment, or upvote) from another mapper within 72 hours.
*   **Pros:** Tests the "social interaction" hypothesis. If community validation is the stickiness factor, this isolates the moment a user feels part of the crowd, not just a data entry clerk.
*   **Cons:** Highly dependent on the activity level of existing mappers in the user's specific region. Hard to scale if the local community is thin; introduces latency between action and activation.

**Recommended Next Step for QBR**
Do not declare a winner yet. Propose an A/B test starting next week where the onboarding flow (launched Oct 12) is tweaked to guide users toward these three specific thresholds respectively. Measure the **Day-7 and Day-30 retention** of users who hit each definition versus those who do not. The definition with the highest correlation to Day-30 retention becomes your new North Star for activation.

*Note: Ensure your analytics track these events with a stable, pseudonymous user ID to follow users across sessions without violating privacy compliance. Do not peek at results before reaching a pre-committed sample size to avoid false positives.*
