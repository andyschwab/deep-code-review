**Analysis Structure to Isolate Algorithm Effect**

To distinguish a structural flaw from a temporary learning phase or seasonal noise, restructure your measurement immediately:

1.  **Redefine the North Star:** Shift from CTR to **Revenue Per Query (RPQ)** or **Gross Margin Per Session**. CTR is a vanity metric here; it rises even if users click wrong items. RPQ captures the net effect of relevance and price.
2.  **Bottom-Up Funnel Diagnosis (Retention First):** Do not analyze acquisition or raw traffic. Analyze **post-click retention**:
    *   *Activation:* Did the user add the clicked item to the cart? If CTR is up but Add-to-Cart is flat/down, the results are misleading (clickbait).
    *   *Retention:* Do users who experienced the new algo return within 7 days? If they bounce permanently, you have damaged trust.
3.  **Isolate Seasonality via Cohort Comparison:**
    *   Compare the **Treatment Group (50%)** against the **Control Group (50%)** for the *exact same days*. Since both groups experience the same external seasonality, any divergence in RPQ is the algorithm's effect.
    *   Slice by **user tenure**: New users vs. returning. If returning users show a sharper AOV drop, the algo is disrupting established buying patterns.
4.  **Event Taxonomy (Instrument Only What Answers a Question):**
    *   `search_result_clicked`: Does this lead to `add_to_cart`? (Question: Is relevance driving intent?)
    *   `search_result_price_bucket`: Are clicks shifting to lower-priced tiers? (Question: Is the algo biasing toward cheap items?)
    *   `session_returned_7d`: Did the user come back? (Question: Did we break long-term value?)
