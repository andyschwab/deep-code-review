**Proposed North Star Metric:** **Weekly Active Accounts Completing a "Decision-Ready" Report.**

This metric replaces "reports generated" (vanity) with a measure of actual value delivery. A "Decision-Ready" report is defined not by speed alone, but by a user action indicating the data was sufficient to act (e.g., exporting the report, sharing it with a stakeholder, or clicking through to an inventory adjustment workflow within 5 minutes of generation).

**Selection Framework & Rationale**
To align your engineering roadmap before the December 31 fiscal year-end, apply this three-step filter to any candidate metric:
1.  **Value Correlation:** Does the metric rise *only* when the customer achieves their goal (making an inventory decision)? If users generate 100 reports but make zero decisions, the metric must not rise.
2.  **Actionability:** Can the engineering team directly influence this number this week? (e.g., by reducing load times, improving data clarity, or adding one-click exports).
3.  **Leading Indicator:** Does this metric predict retention *before* the renewal date? Speed is a feature; "decision made" is the outcome that drives renewal.

**Why "Reports Generated" Fails:** Your current average of 40 reports/month is a volume metric. It increases even if the reports are useless, slow, or ignored. It rewards noise, not value.

**Why "Time Saved" is Insufficient as a North Star:** While your hypothesis links speed to retention, speed is an input, not the outcome. A fast report that lacks critical data saves time but delivers no value. Use "Time to Generate" as a **guardrail metric** (it must stay under X seconds) or an **input metric**, but do not make it the North Star.

**Tracking Downstream Effects on Churn (Next Two Quarters)**
Since you lack historical data linking speed to renewal, you cannot yet assert a benchmark. Instead, construct a **Retention Cohort Analysis** starting immediately:
*   **Segment:** Group accounts by their weekly frequency of "Decision-Ready" reports.
*   **Measure:** Track the renewal rate (or churn rate) of these specific cohorts over the next two quarters.
*   **Hypothesis Test:** Validate if accounts averaging >3 "Decision-Ready" reports/week have statistically lower churn than those with 0–1.

**Immediate Next Steps (This Week)**
1.  **Instrument the "Decision" Event:** Define and tag the specific user action (export, share, or downstream click) that signifies a report was "Decision-Ready." Do not instrument every click; only what answers the question: "Did this report drive an action?"
2.  **Establish Baseline:** Run a query to determine the current % of Active Accounts completing this action weekly. This is your `UNVERIFIED` baseline until instrumented.
3.  **Define the Guardrail:** Set a performance threshold for report generation time (e.g., 95th percentile < 3 seconds) to ensure speed improvements do not degrade data accuracy.

**Note:** Do not fabricate a "good" retention rate or conversion target; compare all future results strictly against the baseline you establish this week. Ensure user identifiers used for cohort tracking are pseudonymous and routed to your privacy compliance protocols.
