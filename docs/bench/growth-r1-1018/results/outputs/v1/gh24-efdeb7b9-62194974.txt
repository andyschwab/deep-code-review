### Holistic Analysis Framework for Guest Checkout Decision

To evaluate the trade-off between the observed 20% immediate conversion lift and the 0% current return rate, you must isolate whether the "guest" behavior is a permanent loss of relationship or a delayed activation. Do not average these metrics; they represent two distinct value streams that require separate cohort analyses before any default change is considered.

**1. The "Email Validity & Activation" Cohort**
Segment the guest checkout users from the September 1–14 window into two sub-cohorts: those who provided an email address and those who did not (if applicable).
*   **Action:** Calculate the **email capture rate** for guest users. Then, run a deliverability check on the captured list to determine the percentage of valid, marketing-consented addresses.
*   **The Question:** Is the 0% return rate driven by a lack of contact information, or by a lack of engagement despite having contact info? If 80% of guest users provided valid emails but still haven't returned, the "guest" mode may fundamentally attract one-time buyers regardless of communication channels.

**2. The "Delayed Activation" Retention Curve**
Your current 0% return rate is based on a window that may not have closed for all users (e.g., a user buying on Sept 14 has not yet reached day 30).
*   **Action:** Plot a **daily retention curve** for the guest cohort vs. the registered baseline, starting from Day 1 post-purchase. Do not wait for Day 30 to read this.
*   **The Question:** Is there a secondary spike in guest returns triggered by order status updates or shipping notifications? If guest users return only for logistical reasons and never browse, their Lifetime Value (LTV) ceiling is structurally lower than registered users who browse catalog updates.

**3. The "Cannibalization" Mix Shift**
Determine if the 20% lift in completed purchases is *new* volume or simply existing customers choosing a faster path.
*   **Action:** Compare the **New vs. Returning visitor ratio** in the guest variant against the control group during the test period.
*   **The Question:** Did mandatory account creation previously force returning customers to register (inflating your baseline registration rate), and did removing it simply let them bypass the form? If the guest variant mostly converted users who would have eventually purchased anyway, the 20% lift is partially an artifact of friction removal, not new demand, making the loss of the 15% long-term retention rate even more costly.

**4. The "Break-Even" LTV Simulation**
Construct a model using your specific numbers rather than industry benchmarks.
*   **Action:** Calculate the **required future conversion rate** for the guest cohort to match the LTV of the registered cohort.
    *   *Formula:* (Registered LTV) = (Guest Immediate Revenue) + (Guest Email Capture Rate × Guest Future Conversion Rate × Average Order Value).
*   **The Question:** Given the 20% immediate gain, what future conversion rate do guest users need to achieve to break even with the registered baseline? If the required rate is historically impossible for your brand (e.g., requiring guest users to convert at 10% when your best segments hit 4%), the trade-off is negative.
