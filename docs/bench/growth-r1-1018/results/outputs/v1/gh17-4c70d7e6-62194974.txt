To diagnose the root cause of ThriftWise's month-two retention cliff, execute the following behavioral investigation plan. This framework isolates whether the drop-off stems from value realization failure, successful task completion, or engagement friction, without prescribing solutions.

**1. Define the Core Value Event and "Aha" Moment**
First, validate if users are actually reaching the point of value. Do not measure "logins" or "budget created." Instead, instrument and measure the **"Weekly Active Budgeter"**: a user who reviews or adjusts their budget categories within 7 days of a transaction occurring.
*   **Investigation:** Segment the January cohort into those who hit this core action at least twice in Month 1 versus those who did not.
*   **Diagnostic Question:** Is the churn concentrated among users who *never* established a rhythm of reviewing their budget? If users who hit the core action twice still churn in Month 2, the issue is likely **value saturation** (they stabilized) or **feature exhaustion**. If they churn before hitting it, the issue is **activation failure**.

**2. Distinguish "Success" Churn from "Failure" Churn**
You hypothesized users might leave because they succeeded. You must prove this by correlating retention with financial health signals, not just app usage.
*   **Investigation:** Create a derived metric for **"Budget Adherence Score"** (actual spend vs. budgeted limit) for the first 45 days.
*   **Diagnostic Question:** Compare the churn rate of users with high adherence (staying under budget) vs. low adherence.
    *   If high-adherence users are churning: The hypothesis of **"successful stabilization"** is supported. They fixed their finances and no longer feel the need to check.
    *   If low-adherence users are churning: The hypothesis is **"value failure."** They aren't seeing results, so they disengage.
    *   *Note:* Do not assume silence equals success; verify it against their spending behavior data.

**3. Deconstruct the "Second Bank Account" Correlation**
The 20% retention lift for users connecting a second account is currently a correlation. You must determine if this is a proxy for **engagement depth** or a **causal driver** of value.
*   **Investigation:** Run a time-to-event analysis. Measure the time between signup and connecting the second account. Overlay this with the time of their last active session.
*   **Diagnostic Question:** Did users connect the second account *before* they started exhibiting retention behaviors, or did highly engaged users simply explore more features?
    *   **Test:** Look at users who connected the second account but *failed* to categorize transactions from it. If they still churned, the account connection itself is not the value driver; it is merely a marker of a power user. The real driver is likely the **complexity of financial view** (seeing a holistic picture), not the technical act of linking.

**4. Analyze the "Silent Exit" Pattern**
To rule out "forgetting," analyze the decay curve of notification interactions and session duration leading up to the churn point.
*   **Investigation:** Plot the **session frequency** and **push notification open rate** week-by-week for the churned cohort.
*   **Diagnostic Question:** Is the drop-off a sudden cliff (user opens app, encounters an error or boring screen, and leaves forever) or a gradual fade (sessions get shorter, notifications go unopened)?
    *   **Sudden Cliff:** Suggests a specific friction point or realization of limited utility at the end of Cycle 2.
    *   **Gradual Fade:** Suggests the app failed to become a habit or was simply forgotten/lowered in priority.

**5. Cohort the "Month-Two" Specifics**
Since the drop happens specifically after the second cycle, isolate what changes in the user experience at that exact moment.
*   **Investigation:** Compare the **feature usage diversity** in Month 1 vs. Month 2 for retained vs. churned users.
*   **Diagnostic Question:** Do retained users discover a *new* use case in Month 2 (e.g., savings goals, debt payoff tracking) that churned users ignore? If churned users only ever used the "basic budgeting" feature, the product may lack a **second-layer value proposition** to sustain interest after the initial setup novelty wears off.

**Deliverable for Roadmap Prioritization:**
Present these findings as a matrix: **High Adherence/High Churn** (Success Exit), **Low Adherence/High Churn** (Value Failure), and **High Engagement/High Churn** (Feature Exhaustion). This classification tells the Head of Product exactly which lever to pull: build re-engagement loops for the "forgotten," new advanced features for the "exhausted," or export/congratulatory flows for the "successful."

*Ensure all user identifiers used in these cohorts are pseudonymized and routed to your privacy compliance protocol before analysis.*
*Do not set arbitrary retention benchmarks; compare these patterns strictly against ThriftWise's own historical baseline.*
