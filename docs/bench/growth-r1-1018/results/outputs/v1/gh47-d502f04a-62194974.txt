**Japan Launch Scorecard: Early Success Indicators**

**1. North Star Metric: Weekly Active Ordering Users (WAOU)**
Reject "Average Order Value" (AOV) as your primary metric for this launch. In a high-frequency, small-basket market like Japan, AOV is a vanity metric that will artificially depress perceived success. Instead, prioritize **Weekly Active Ordering Users** who complete at least **two orders per week**. This measures actual habit formation and customer value delivery, which drives lifetime value in dense urban markets more than single transaction size.

**2. The Funnel: Read Bottom-Up (Retention First)**
Do not optimize for acquisition (signups) until you validate retention. Your current assumption ("3x/week if delivery <20 mins") is **UNVERIFIED** because you have no local data.
*   **Primary Diagnostic:** **Week-2 Retention Cohort**. Measure the percentage of users who ordered in Week 1 and return to order again in Week 2.
*   **Activation Threshold:** Define your "Aha!" moment not by spend, but by **Time-to-Second-Order**. If users do not order a second time within 10 days, the product-market fit hypothesis is failing regardless of delivery speed.
*   **Acquisition:** Only scale marketing spend once Week-2 retention stabilizes above your internal baseline. Pouring users into a leaky bucket is waste.

**3. Event Taxonomy: Instrument Only Named Questions**
Do not track "everything." Instrument only these specific events to answer your critical unknowns:
*   `order_completed`: Properties must include `delivery_time_actual`, `fee_amount`, `restaurant_category`, and `basket_size`.
    *   *Question:* Does delivery time under 20 minutes correlate with higher Week-2 retention?
*   `checkout_abandoned`: Properties must include `abandonment_stage` and `fee_shock_boolean` (did the user leave when fees were displayed?).
    *   *Question:* Is friction driven by fees or lack of restaurant variety?
*   `search_no_results`: Property `search_term`.
    *   *Question:* Are we missing local cuisine preferences?

**4. Benchmarking Strategy: No External Numbers**
You asked how to benchmark against global averages. **Do not do this.**
*   **Rule:** Never assert a benchmark the model cannot source. US/EU AOV ($28) is irrelevant to Japan and will mislead your team.
*   **Action:** Establish a **Japan-Specific Baseline** in Week 1. Compare Week 2 performance strictly against Week 1 Japan data. If Week-2 retention is 5% in Week 1 and 8% in Week 2, you are winning, even if the US retains at 15%.
*   **Status of Assumptions:** Your "3x/week" target is currently **UNVERIFIED**. It becomes a target only after your first month of data confirms the correlation between <20min delivery and frequency.

**5. Experiment Design for Fee Sensitivity**
To resolve the conflicting survey data on fees vs. variety:
*   **Design:** Run an A/B test on delivery fees for new users in a single ward (e.g., Shibuya).
*   **Rigor:** Pre-commit to a sample size and duration (e.g., 14 days). Do not peek at results on Day 2; early significance is often noise.
*   **Metrics:** Primary = Retention Rate; Guardrail = Contribution Margin per Order.
*   **Decision Rule:** If the lower-fee cohort shows >10% higher retention without breaching the margin guardrail, adopt the lower fee structure.
