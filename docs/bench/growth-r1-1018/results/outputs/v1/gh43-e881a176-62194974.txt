**Diagnostic Analysis Plan: Mandatory Verification Churn**

**1. Quantitative Segmentation (Slice by these dimensions immediately)**
*   **Verification Method & Timing:** Split the 35% drop-off by channel (Email vs. SMS) and time-to-complete. Isolate users who abandoned specifically after requesting the code but before entering it. If SMS abandonment is disproportionately high compared to Email, friction or delivery latency is the primary driver.
*   **User Intent/Value Tier:** Segment by pre-verification behavior (e.g., "Browse only" vs. "Added to Cart" vs. "High Lifetime Value"). If high-intent users are churning at the same rate as low-intent browsers, the friction is too high for your core value proposition. If only low-intent users drop off, the feature may be successfully filtering fraud without harming genuine revenue potential.
*   **Device & Geography:** Break down failure rates by mobile vs. desktop and by region. SMS delivery failures are often geographic; mobile keyboard friction differs from desktop. A spike in churn on mobile specifically points to UI/UX friction in the input field.
*   **Return Visitor Status:** Compare churn between new sign-ups and existing users forced to verify upon login. Existing users have higher trust; if they churn at higher rates than new users, the "lack of trust" hypothesis regarding *why* you need the data is likely correct.

**2. Qualitative Signals (Look for these patterns)**
*   **Support Ticket Keywords:** Aggregate tickets from Nov 1st onward. Search for "didn't receive code," "too many steps," "why do you need this," or "privacy." A cluster of "didn't receive" indicates infrastructure delay; "why do you need" indicates a trust/communication gap.
*   **Session Replays (if available):** Watch 20–30 sessions of users who hit the verification page and left. Look for "rage clicks" on the resend button (delivery issue) or long pauses before typing (hesitation/trust issue).
*   **Exit Survey Data:** If an exit survey exists on the verification page, analyze the specific reason codes selected. If none exists, this is a critical gap to flag for immediate implementation.

**3. Hypotheses for the VP Report**
*   **H1 (Friction/Delivery):** The 35% drop-off is driven by SMS delivery latency or mobile input friction, not objection to the principle of verification. *Evidence to seek:* High abandonment specifically in the "waiting for code" state on mobile devices.
*   **H2 (Trust/Communication):** Users understand the step but distrust the request for phone numbers due to poor copywriting or lack of context on *why* it is needed. *Evidence to seek:* High churn among existing loyal users and support tickets citing privacy concerns.
*   **H3 (Fraud Filter Success):** The "churn" is actually successful fraud prevention, removing low-quality bots that previously inflated sign-up numbers but never retained. *Evidence to seek:* The 30-day retention of the *remaining* verified cohort is significantly higher than the pre-change baseline, and the drop-off is concentrated in high-risk geographies or IP ranges.

**Recommended Immediate Action:**
Do not roll back yet. Implement a "soft launch" A/B test where 10% of traffic sees the old flow (or a simplified version) to establish a live control group for revenue impact. Simultaneously, add explanatory micro-copy near the phone number field explaining the security benefit to test H2.

**Note:** Your observation that verified users have *higher* retention strongly suggests the feature improves quality but imposes too high a barrier to entry. The goal is to lower the barrier without losing the quality filter.
