### TutorMatch Metric Hierarchy: Balancing Liquidity and Quality

**1. North Star Metric: Weekly Successful Matches**
Reject "Total Bookings" or "Cumulative Signups" as vanity metrics; they rise even if the marketplace breaks. Adopt **Weekly Successful Matches** (a student booking a tutor who completes the first session) as the single North Star. This metric only rises when *both* sides receive value. If supply grows but matches stall, the North Star flatlines, immediately signaling a liquidity or quality leak regardless of headline booking counts.

**2. Input Metrics: The Two-Sided Funnel**
Decompose the North Star into two synchronized input metrics. Do not optimize one without watching the other:
*   **Student Side (Demand):** Request-to-Book Rate within 24 hours. This validates your "fill rate" hypothesis but must be paired with **Week-4 Retention** (repeat booking rate). If fill rate is high but retention drops, the hypothesis is incomplete: you are filling requests with poor-quality matches or pricing that drives churn.
*   **Tutor Side (Supply):** Acceptance Rate of Requests and Tutor Utilization (hours booked / hours available). A rising supply with falling utilization indicates a liquidity mismatch (tutors in the wrong subjects/locations) rather than a lack of demand.

**3. Diagnostic Layer: Detecting Liquidity vs. Quality**
To answer your open question on price sensitivity versus matching quality, instrument these specific events:
*   **Liquidity Signal:** *Unfilled Request Reason*. Tag requests that expire after 24 hours by subject, location, and price band. If unfilled requests cluster in specific price bands, it is a pricing/liquidity issue. If they are scattered despite high tutor density, it is a matching algorithm or notification latency issue.
*   **Quality Signal:** *Post-Session Repeat Delta*. Compare the repeat booking rate of students matched within 1 hour vs. 24 hours, and by tutor rating tier. If rapid fills lead to lower retention, speed is compromising quality. If high-priced matches have lower retention than mid-tier ones, price sensitivity is the driver.

**4. Operational Guardrails**
Set hard floors to prevent optimizing for one side at the expense of the other:
*   **Student Churn Floor:** If Week-4 retention drops below your current baseline trend, halt aggressive tutor acquisition until match quality improves.
*   **Tutor Frustration Ceiling:** Monitor "Zero-Bookings per Active Tutor" weekly. If this rises while total bookings grow, you are over-supplying low-demand segments, risking tutor churn.
