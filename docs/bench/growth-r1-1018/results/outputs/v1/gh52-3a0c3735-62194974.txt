**RemoteGear Q2–Q3 Survival Scoreboard: Three KPIs**

Given your constraint of two engineers and the critical need to distinguish between product defects and expectation mismatches, stop tracking "total sales" as a primary health metric. It is a vanity metric that rises even if your business is failing due to returns. Instead, instrument these three specific indicators to predict survival and path to profitability:

**1. Net Retention Rate of the Core Action (The "Keep" Metric)**
*   **Definition:** The percentage of customers who still have the desk in use and active 60 days after purchase.
*   **Why this over "Return Rate":** An 8% return rate is ambiguous. If 20% of users struggle but only 8% return, you have a silent churn risk. If returns are due to defects, they happen early (Days 1–14). If due to expectation mismatch, they may happen later or result in the unit sitting in a closet (inactive).
*   **How to Measure (Low Effort):** Do not build complex telemetry. Send a single automated email at Day 45: "Are you still using your RemoteGear desk daily?" with two buttons: "Yes, daily" and "No, rarely/never."
*   **Interpretation:**
    *   **<85% "Yes":** Immediate survival risk. Your product-market fit is leaking. Investigate *why* before spending on acquisition.
    *   **>90% "Yes":** Product quality is likely sufficient; focus shifts to referral mechanics.
    *   **Signal:** If "Yes" is high but returns are 8%, the returns are likely logistical or expectation-based (fixable via marketing copy). If "Yes" is low, you have a product defect or usability crisis.

**2. Verified Referral Conversion Rate (The "Growth" Metric)**
*   **Definition:** The percentage of *verified* happy users (those who answered "Yes" above) who successfully refer a new paying customer.
*   **Why this over "Referral Volume":** Raw referral counts are noise. You need to know the *efficiency* of your happy base. Your assumption is that quality drives referrals; this metric proves or disproves it.
*   **How to Measure (Low Effort):** Implement a simple unique code system. When a "Yes" responder receives a discount code to share, track how many times that specific code converts to a sale.
*   **Interpretation:**
    *   **<5% Conversion:** Your product is good, but it isn't "remarkable" enough to drive organic growth. You will need paid marketing (increasing burn) to survive.
    *   **>15% Conversion:** Your hypothesis is correct. Product quality is your engine. Reinvest engineering time into making the referral frictionless rather than building new features.

**3. Support Ticket Severity Ratio (The "Burn" Metric)**
*   **Definition:** The ratio of "Defect/Breakage" tickets to "How-To/Setup" tickets.
*   **Why this over "Total Ticket Volume":** Total volume grows with sales and tells you nothing about quality. You need to isolate the 8% return cause.
*   **How to Measure (Low Effort):** Add a mandatory dropdown for support agents: "Issue Type: [Defect] [Setup/Expectation] [Shipping]". No new software needed, just a process change.
*   **Interpretation:**
    *   **High Defect Ratio:** The 8% return rate is hardware failure. Survival requires halting new feature work to fix reliability (engineering focus).
    *   **High Setup/Expectation Ratio:** The hardware works, but customers don't understand it. Survival requires better onboarding content or clearer marketing, not engineering changes.

**Board Summary for Path to Profitability:**
To reach profitability by Q4, we must stop guessing why customers leave. These three metrics isolate the leak: **Net Retention** confirms if the product delivers value; **Severity Ratio** identifies if the 8% return rate is a build error or a messaging error; **Referral Conversion** validates if we can grow without burning cash on ads. If Net Retention is below 85%, no amount of marketing will save us; engineering must fix the core experience first.

*Note: All baselines must be established from your first 30 days of this new tracking; do not compare against industry averages which do not exist for your specific niche.*
