**Decision: Do not roll out globally. Revert the variant for legacy users immediately and extend the test for new users only.**

The 12% overall lift is misleading because it masks a critical failure in your highest-value segment. With legacy users comprising 40% of revenue, a "significant drop" in their engagement and report generation represents an unacceptable risk to long-term retention and Lifetime Value (LTV). In growth analytics, we read the funnel bottom-up: retention leaks invalidate acquisition or conversion gains. If the change reduces the core action (report generation) for your most stable cohort, the short-term conversion spike is likely a false positive driven entirely by new users who lack established habits.

Rolling this out globally would trade guaranteed revenue from legacy users for uncertain gains from newer ones. The correct path is a segmented rollout:
1.  **Revert** the paywall placement for users with >6 months tenure to protect the revenue base.
2.  **Isolate** the variant to users with <6 months tenure. The 10%+ lift in this group suggests the hypothesis may be valid for onboarding flows, but this must be verified without the noise of legacy user backlash.
3.  **Recompute** the net impact. Calculate the revenue lost from the legacy drop against the gain from new users. If the net is negative or neutral, the feature fails the business case regardless of the aggregate conversion rate.
