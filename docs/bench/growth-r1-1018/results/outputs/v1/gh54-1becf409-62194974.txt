**Investigative Steps & Hypotheses for StreamLine Leadership**

**Immediate Diagnostic: Read the Funnel Bottom-Up**
Stop analyzing acquisition volume. The 18% DAU growth with flat conversion indicates a retention or activation leak, not a traffic quality issue alone. Pouring more users into a broken monetization flow wastes capital.

**Step 1: Isolate the "Core Action" Retention**
Define the specific action that precedes a subscription (e.g., "watched 3+ episodes in 7 days" or "completed a full movie").
*   **Action:** Segment DAU into cohorts based on whether they performed this core action.
*   **Query:** Compare Week-1 and Week-2 retention rates for users acquired *before* vs. *after* the February content launch.
*   **Hypothesis A (Content Mismatch):** The new library drives high initial views (vanity metric) but fails to trigger the "aha" moment required for long-term value, causing early churn before the paywall is even considered.

**Step 2: Audit the Activation-to-Conversion Seam**
The 4.5% conversion rate is an aggregate that hides friction.
*   **Action:** Map the drop-off rate at every step between the core action and the payment confirmation. Specifically, measure the percentage of users who hit the paywall vs. those who initiate checkout.
*   **Hypothesis B (Monetization Friction):** Users perceive value (high engagement) but encounter a technical or UX barrier at the upgrade prompt (e.g., pricing confusion, limited payment methods, or a broken flow on specific devices).
*   **Hypothesis C (Wrong Audience):** The marketing campaign driving the 18% surge targeted "browsers" rather than "buyers." If the new content is free-heavy, users may be satisfied with the ad-supported or limited tier, perceiving zero marginal value in upgrading.

**Step 3: Validate the "Value" Assumption**
Do not assume engagement equals willingness to pay.
*   **Action:** Correlate "hours watched" with "upgrade probability" for the February cohort specifically.
*   **Hypothesis D (Cannibalization):** The new content library is so compelling on the free tier that it reduces the urgency to upgrade. High engagement is actually *suppressing* revenue.

**This Week's Execution Plan**
1.  **Instrument/Query:** Pull retention cohorts for the "Core Action" (defined above) for Jan vs. Feb/March acquisitions. If this data is not currently tracked, label the insight **UNVERIFIED** and prioritize instrumenting this event immediately.
2.  **Segment Conversion:** Break down the 4.5% conversion rate by traffic source and device type to identify if a specific channel is dragging the average down.
3.  **Decision Tripwire:** If Feb/March cohorts show >15% lower retention on the core action than Jan cohorts, the root cause is **content/product fit** (halt acquisition spend). If retention is stable but paywall drop-off increased, the root cause is **monetization friction** (fix the checkout flow).
