**Executive Summary: Pricing Experiment Signals**

The data indicates a successful segmentation shift in the SMB market but an unverified risk in the Enterprise segment. The 25% volume increase alongside a 15% price hike suggests SMB demand is currently inelastic; customers perceive sufficient value to absorb the increase, and the "filtering" hypothesis holds for lead quality if conversion rates or lifetime value (LTV) for these smaller accounts have improved. However, the 10% decline in enterprise deals introduces a critical ambiguity: it is unclear whether this is a causal effect of the pricing structure (e.g., perceived anchor price shifts, budget threshold breaches) or a non-causal seasonal variance.

Without isolating the seasonality factor, the enterprise slowdown cannot be attributed to the pricing change with certainty. If the drop exceeds historical seasonal baselines, the experiment may have inadvertently altered the perceived value proposition for large accounts or created friction in procurement approval chains. If the drop aligns with historical trends, the pricing change is likely neutral for enterprise, and the volume gain in SMB represents net positive revenue growth.

**Required Actions for CFO Approval**

1.  **Isolate Seasonality:** Immediately compare the current 10% enterprise drop against the same 45-day window in the prior two years. If the current drop is significantly deeper than the historical seasonal average, the pricing change is a likely confounder. If it matches historical trends, the slowdown is external.
2.  **Validate SMB Quality:** Confirm the "low-quality lead" filter hypothesis by calculating the churn rate and support ticket volume for the new SMB cohort compared to the pre-experiment baseline. Higher price must correlate with higher retention or lower support cost to validate the strategic shift.
3.  **Decision Gate:** Do not permanentize the structure until the enterprise variance is explained. If seasonality explains the drop, proceed with the new structure. If the drop exceeds seasonal norms, revert the enterprise pricing component while retaining the SMB adjustment, or extend the experiment duration to capture a full procurement cycle.

**Note:** The 25% volume lift is a strong positive signal, but permanentizing a structure that potentially suppresses high-value deal velocity without ruling out causality risks long-term revenue ceiling compression.
**Note:** Ensure the "total subscription volume" metric is weighted by ARR, not just unit count, to confirm the 15% hike + 25% volume actually resulted in net revenue growth after the enterprise dip.
