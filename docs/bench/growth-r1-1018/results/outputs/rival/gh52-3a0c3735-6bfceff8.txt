# RemoteGear Q2-Q3 Survival Metrics: Board Brief

To navigate the next six months with limited engineering resources, RemoteGear must shift from lagging output metrics (total sales) to leading health indicators that predict retention and profitability. The following three KPIs directly address the 8% return rate ambiguity and the unverified referral hypothesis.

### 1. Net Return Reason Ratio (Defect vs. Expectation)
**Definition:** The ratio of returns categorized as "Product Defect" versus "Mismatched Expectations."
**Implementation:** Modify the return authorization form to require a single mandatory selection: "Item Broken/Defective" or "Not What I Expected." No complex tracking is required; this is a simple database flag on the existing return workflow.
**Interpretation:**
*   **Defect > 50%:** The 8% return rate is a quality control failure. Survival risk is high due to potential reputation damage and unsustainable COGS. Engineering must pause new features to fix manufacturing or assembly flaws immediately.
*   **Expectation > 50%:** The product works, but marketing or product descriptions are overselling capabilities. The fix is copywriting and imagery updates, not engineering rework. This preserves engineering capacity for growth features.

### 2. Verified Referral Rate (VRR)
**Definition:** The percentage of new orders attributed explicitly to an existing customer recommendation.
**Implementation:** Add a single optional field at checkout: "Who referred you?" coupled with a post-purchase email to the buyer asking, "Have you recommended RemoteGear to a colleague? (Yes/No)." Do not build a complex referral portal yet. Manually match names or use simple discount codes shared verbally.
**Interpretation:**
*   **VRR > 15%:** Validates the assumption that quality drives organic growth. You can safely reduce paid acquisition spend to lower burn rate, relying on product-led growth.
*   **VRR < 5%:** The "quality drives referrals" assumption is false. Despite good hardware, the product isn't sparking conversation. You must invest in community building or incentivized sharing programs, as organic survival is unlikely at current volumes.

### 3. Day-30 Active Utilization
**Definition:** The percentage of customers who report still using the desk converter daily after 30 days.
**Implementation:** Automate a single email survey 30 days post-delivery: "Are you still using your RemoteGear desk daily? (Yes/No)."
**Interpretation:**
*   **Utilization > 85%:** Strong product-market fit. Customers who use the product rarely return it and often refer others. Focus engineering on reliability and minor iterative improvements.
*   **Utilization < 70%:** Indicates a "novelty" purchase. Customers try it once and revert to old habits, leading to eventual returns or negative word-of-mouth. This predicts a future spike in churn and requires engineering to focus on usability or ergonomic adjustments that encourage daily habit formation.

### Strategic Synthesis for Profitability
These three metrics form a diagnostic triangle. If **Net Return Reason** points to "Expectations," you fix marketing immediately with zero engineering cost. If **Verified Referral Rate** is low, you know paid ads are your only growth lever, requiring a pivot in cash allocation. If **Day-30 Utilization** is high but returns are high, you have a specific manufacturing defect to target surgically.

By focusing only on these binary data points, your two engineers can avoid building elaborate dashboards and instead focus on the specific product fixes these metrics reveal. This approach minimizes burn while providing the board with clear leading indicators of whether the path to Q4 profitability is driven by product excellence or requires a strategic pivot in positioning.
