# Experiment Evaluation: Gamification Feature Test

## Executive Summary
**Recommendation: Do not launch to all users. Iterate on design.**

While the 18% increase in task creation validates the core hypothesis that gamification drives volume, the 12% drop in session duration and the signal of reduced premium upgrades indicate a critical flaw: the feature encourages "gaming" behavior rather than deep engagement. Launching now risks optimizing for vanity metrics while eroding user value and revenue potential.

## Framework for Interpreting Conflicting Signals

Use a **Value-Weighted Decision Matrix** to evaluate the trade-offs:

| Metric | Change | Business Impact | Weight | Weighted Score |
|--------|--------|-----------------|--------|----------------|
| Tasks Created/User | +18% | High (Core utility) | 30% | +5.4 |
| Session Duration | -12% | High (Engagement/Retention proxy) | 40% | -4.8 |
| Premium Upgrades | Slight Dip | Critical (Revenue) | 30% | -1.5 to -3.0 |
| **Net Score** | | | **100%** | **-0.9 to -2.4** |

**Interpretation:** The negative weighted score indicates that the harms (reduced engagement depth and potential revenue loss) outweigh the benefits (increased task volume). The metric moving in the wrong direction (session time) carries higher strategic weight because it correlates with long-term retention, whereas task volume can be inflated artificially.

## Analysis of Conflicting Signals

1.  **Volume vs. Value:** The divergence between +18% tasks and -12% time suggests users are creating low-effort tasks to trigger badges/streaks, then exiting. This is "metric gaming," not genuine productivity improvement.
2.  **Revenue Risk:** Even a slight dip in premium upgrades is a leading indicator of churn or reduced willingness to pay. If users feel the "game" is sufficient without premium features, or if the gamification trivializes the professional utility, LTV will decline.
3.  **Statistical Caution:** While the upgrade dip may lack statistical significance due to sample size, it acts as a **guardrail metric**. When a guardrail moves negatively, you must assume the worst-case scenario until proven otherwise, especially with revenue.

## Strategic Recommendations

### 1. Immediate Action: Kill the "Launch to All" Plan
Do not roll out the current version. The risk of normalizing shallow usage and depressing ARPU (Average Revenue Per User) is too high.

### 2. Iteration Path: Redesign for Depth
Pivot the gamification mechanics to reward *quality* and *completion*, not just *creation*.
*   **Change Trigger:** Award points/badges for task *completion* or tasks remaining active for a minimum duration, not just creation.
*   **Streak Logic:** Modify streaks to require meaningful interaction (e.g., completing a complex workflow) rather than simple input.
*   **Premium Integration:** Tie advanced gamification features (custom badges, detailed analytics) to premium tiers to reverse the upgrade dip.

### 3. Next Experiment Design
Run a follow-up test (2-3 weeks) with these specific hypotheses:
*   *Hypothesis:* "By rewarding task completion instead of creation, we will maintain task volume (+10%) while recovering session duration (-0%) and stabilizing premium upgrades."
*   *Primary Metric:* Task Completion Rate (not Creation Rate).
*   *Guardrail Metrics:* Session Duration, Premium Upgrade Rate, 7-day Retention.

## Conclusion for Steering Committee
The experiment successfully proved that gamification influences user behavior, but it failed to prove that it influences behavior *profitably*. The current design optimizes for the wrong outcome. We recommend a "Pause and Pivot" strategy: halt the rollout, redesign the reward mechanics to align with deep engagement, and re-test before any broader deployment.
