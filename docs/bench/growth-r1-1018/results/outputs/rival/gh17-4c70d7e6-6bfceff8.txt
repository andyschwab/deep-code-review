# Month-Two Retention Diagnostic Plan for ThriftWise

## 1. Hypothesis Validation Framework
To distinguish between successful stabilization, feature fatigue, and forgetfulness, segment the 45% churned cohort into three behavioral archetypes based on their final 30 days of activity:

*   **The "Graduates" (Stabilization Hypothesis):** Users who hit their budget targets consistently for two months, had zero overspend alerts, and stopped logging in without error messages. *Diagnostic:* Cross-reference churned users with "budget adherence" metrics. If >60% of churners were consistently under budget, the product is working too well, and the value prop must shift from "setup" to "wealth building."
*   **The "Stuck" (Value Gap Hypothesis):** Users who logged in, viewed their dashboard, but never executed a core value action (e.g., categorizing transactions, setting a savings goal) after month one. *Diagnostic:* Analyze the "depth of setup." Compare the feature completion rate of churners vs. retainers. If churners only connected one account and set one budget, the initial setup is a dead end without a "next step" trigger.
*   **The "Drifters" (Forgetfulness Hypothesis):** Users who had active transactions (bank syncs occurred) but did not open the app. *Diagnostic:* Measure "passive utility." If bank syncs succeeded but app opens dropped to zero, the app is becoming a passive utility rather than an active tool. This indicates a failure in re-engagement triggers or notification relevance, not necessarily product value.

## 2. Causation vs. Correlation: The Second Bank Account
To determine if connecting a second account drives retention or simply signals high-intent users:

*   **Timing Analysis:** Plot the exact timestamp of the second account connection relative to the retention cliff.
    *   *Causation Signal:* Users connect the second account *before* day 45 and retain.
    *   *Correlation Signal:* Users who eventually retain connect the second account *after* day 60, or the connection happens simultaneously with other high-intent behaviors (e.g., inviting a partner).
*   **Propensity Matching:** Isolate users with similar "setup depth" (e.g., same number of categories, same initial balance) where one group connected a second bank and the other did not. Compare their Day-60 retention rates. If the delta disappears when controlling for setup depth, the second account is a proxy for engagement, not a driver.
*   **The "Effort" Variable:** Analyze if the churn reduction correlates with the *difficulty* of adding the second account. If users who struggled but succeeded retain better, it's the "sunk cost" effect. If seamless connectors retain better, it's the "data richness" effect.

## 3. Critical Behavioral Patterns to Investigate
Prioritize these specific data queries for the Q3 roadmap discussion:

*   **The "Zero-Event" Window:** Identify the exact number of days between the last meaningful user action (not just a sync) and churn. Is there a universal "silence period" (e.g., 14 days) that predicts churn with >80% accuracy?
*   **Alert Fatigue vs. Alert Absence:** Correlate churn with notification volume. Did churners receive too many "budget update" emails (noise) or too few "milestone reached" alerts (lack of positive reinforcement)?
*   **The "Second Cycle" Shock:** Specifically analyze behavior during the *second* budget reset. Did users manually adjust categories? Did they overspend and ignore alerts? The moment the novelty of the first auto-generated budget wears off is the critical failure point.
*   **Feature Discovery Latency:** Measure the time-to-first-use for advanced features (e.g., reports, export, goal tracking). Do users who discover these features before day 30 have near-zero churn?

## 4. Data Deliverable Structure
Present findings to the Head of Product as a **Retention Risk Matrix**:
*   **X-Axis:** Feature Adoption Depth (Low to High)
*   **Y-Axis:** Financial Health Score (Overspending to Surplus)
*   **Quadrants:** Map churned users to reveal if they cluster in "High Health/Low Adoption" (Graduates) or "Low Health/Low Adoption" (Failed Setup).

This diagnostic isolates whether the roadmap should focus on **new feature development** (for the Stuck), **lifecycle marketing** (for the Drifters), or **value proposition pivoting** (for the Graduates).

*Note: Ensure all analysis excludes users who churned due to technical sync failures to avoid contaminating behavioral insights with engineering bugs.*
*Recommendation: Run this analysis on the January cohort specifically before expanding to February/March to validate the "second cycle" hypothesis.*
