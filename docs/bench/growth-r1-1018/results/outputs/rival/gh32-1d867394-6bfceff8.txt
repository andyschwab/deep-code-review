**Recommendation: Do Not Roll Out Globally. Revert to Control for Legacy Users and Iterate on Variant for New Users.**

Based on the data provided, a global rollout presents an unacceptable risk to FinFlow's revenue stability. While the aggregate 12% conversion lift is attractive, it masks a critical segmentation failure: the variant actively harms your highest-value cohort.

**The Core Conflict: Volume vs. Value**
The experiment succeeded in acquiring new revenue from users with <6 months tenure but failed by degrading the experience for legacy users. Since legacy users constitute 40% of total revenue, a "significant drop" in their engagement and report generation is a leading indicator of churn. In SaaS economics, the Lifetime Value (LTV) lost from churning established customers often outweighs the immediate gain from new conversions, especially when the new conversions come from a less proven cohort.

**Decision Framework**

1.  **Immediate Action (Risk Mitigation):**
    Revert the paywall placement for the legacy segment immediately. Their drop in report generation suggests the new flow creates friction in their core workflow. Protecting the 40% revenue base is the priority. Do not wait for further data on churn; the behavioral signal (reduced usage) is sufficient to act.

2.  **Secondary Action (Optimization):**
    Maintain the variant *only* for users with <6 months tenure. The 12% overall lift implies this group responded very strongly, likely because the new placement aligns better with their "aha!" moment during report generation. Isolate this group to capture the upside without endangering the core business.

3.  **Strategic Next Steps:**
    *   **Qualitative Investigation:** Launch immediate user interviews with legacy users who saw the variant. We need to understand *why* the new placement reduced report generation. Did it feel aggressive? Did it interrupt a saved workflow?
    *   **Cohort Monitoring:** Track the retention of the new converters from the <6-month group over the next 30 days. A high conversion rate followed by rapid churn would indicate the new paywall is "forcing" sales rather than creating value.
    *   **Hybrid Approach:** Investigate if a dynamic paywall is feasible—showing the end-of-flow paywall to new users and the dashboard paywall to legacy users permanently.

**Board Narrative for Sarah**
Frame this not as a failed test, but as a successful discovery of segment-specific needs. The data proves that one size does not fit all. The recommendation is to segment the experience: optimize for acquisition with new users while preserving retention for legacy users. This approach captures the 12% lift potential where it works while safeguarding the 40% revenue base where it doesn't.
