### Interim Analysis Plan for EuroStream Pricing Experiment

**Immediate Recommendation: Do Not Stop or Adjust Yet**
Stopping or adjusting based on four weeks of interim data without statistical validation risks a "peeking error," leading to false conclusions. Your primary task before Monday is to validate data integrity and calculate confidence intervals, not to declare a winner.

#### 1. Validate Observation Windows and Data Quality
Before analyzing lift, ensure the metrics are comparable.
*   **Match Windows:** Confirm that the "4 weeks" covers identical calendar dates for all three cohorts. Ensure you are not dividing full-period spend by partial-period conversions if user behavior lags.
*   **Cohort Integrity:** Verify that users were not accidentally exposed to multiple variants (e.g., a Spanish user traveling to Germany) and that the assignment ratio remains balanced.
*   **External Factors:** Check for country-specific holidays, economic announcements, or site outages between Oct 1–28 that could skew the French ARPU drop independently of price.

#### 2. Statistical Checks to Perform
You must move beyond raw percentage lifts to determine if the results are signal or noise.

*   **Calculate Confidence Intervals (CI):**
    *   **Spain (Conversion):** A 5% observed lift vs. a 20% expected lift is likely statistically insignificant at this sample size. Calculate the 95% CI for the lift. If the interval includes 0% (e.g., -2% to +12%), the result is inconclusive, not a failure.
    *   **France (ARPU):** A 10% drop in ARPU is concerning. Calculate the CI for this delta. If the lower bound of the CI is significantly below 0% (e.g., -15% to -5%), this indicates real revenue erosion. If it spans 0% (e.g., -18% to +2%), it may be variance.
*   **Power Analysis Re-check:**
    *   Re-calculate the required sample size based on the *observed* variance, not your initial assumptions. If the French ARPU variance is higher than expected, you may need more time to detect a true effect.
*   **Segmentation Sanity Check:**
    *   Break down the French ARPU drop by new vs. existing users. If only new users show the drop, the discount may be attracting lower-value customers. If existing users downgraded, that is a critical cannibalization risk.

#### 3. Decision Framework for Monday's Presentation

Present these three scenarios based on your calculations:

| Scenario | Condition | Recommendation |
| :--- | :--- | :--- |
| **A. Statistical Noise** | CIs for both Spain (conversion) and France (ARPU) include 0. | **Extend.** The test is underpowered. Run for 2–4 more weeks to reduce margin of error. |
| **B. Confirmed Harm** | France ARPU CI is entirely negative (e.g., lower bound < -5%). | **Stop French Variant.** Halt the 5% discount in France immediately to prevent revenue loss. Continue Spain/Germany. |
| **C. Confirmed Futility** | Spain Conversion CI upper bound is < 10% (far below your 20% goal). | **Pivot.** The 15% discount is insufficient to drive the hypothesized volume. Consider testing a deeper discount (e.g., 25%) in a new experiment rather than extending this one. |

#### 4. Addressing the "Why"
Do not attempt to diagnose "cultural differences" or "economic factors" yet. These are hypotheses for *future* tests. Currently, you only have data on *price elasticity*.
*   If France shows confirmed harm, the insight is "5% discount erodes ARPU without sufficient volume compensation," not "French users hate discounts."
*   If Spain shows futility, the insight is "15% is below the threshold of perception," not "Spanish users are price insensitive."

**Final Note:** Your recommendation must be argued from the goal of revenue maximization, not operational ease. If the French variant is losing money with high confidence, stopping it is the only valid decision, even if it feels premature.
