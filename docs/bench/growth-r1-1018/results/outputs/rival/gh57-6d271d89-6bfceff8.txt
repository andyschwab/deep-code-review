### Experiment Readout: Customization Engine Rollout Evaluation

**Objective:** Determine if the 15% churn reduction among customizers outweighs the operational risk of a 48-hour fulfillment delay causing cancellations among non-customizing users.

**Current Status:**
*   **Treatment Group:** 2,000 subscribers (20% of 10k base).
*   **Control Group:** 8,000 subscribers (80% of base).
*   **Observation Window:** 1 week post-launch.

#### 1. Primary Success Metric
*   **Net Cohort Churn Rate:** Compare the total churn rate of the Treatment Group vs. the Control Group.
    *   *Calculation:* (Total Churned in Group / Total Users in Group) × 100.
    *   *Decision Threshold:* Launch to 100% only if the Net Cohort Churn in Treatment is statistically lower than Control, accounting for any spike in non-customizer churn.

#### 2. Segment-Specific Analysis (Critical for Diagnosis)
You must split the Treatment Group into two distinct segments to isolate the variable:
*   **Segment A (Adopters):** Users who utilized the customization engine.
    *   *Metric:* Churn rate vs. historical baseline.
    *   *Hypothesis Confirmation:* Verify the reported 15% churn lift holds true.
*   **Segment B (Non-Adopters in Treatment):** Users exposed to the feature but who did *not* customize.
    *   *Metric:* Churn rate vs. the pure Control Group (8,000 users).
    *   *Risk Detection:* If this segment shows higher churn than the Control Group, the 48-hour delay (or UI confusion) is negatively impacting users even when they don't use the feature.

#### 3. Guardrail Metrics (Go/No-Go Triggers)
Monitor these specifically for the **Control Group** and **Segment B** to detect collateral damage from the fulfillment delay:
*   **Support Ticket Volume (Shipping Delays):** Track tickets tagged "Where is my order?" or "Late shipment."
    *   *Trigger:* >10% increase in volume compared to the 4 weeks prior.
*   **CSAT/NPS on Delivery:** Average satisfaction score specifically related to delivery timing.
    *   *Trigger:* Statistically significant drop in scores for non-customizing users.
*   **Fulfillment Error Rate:** Percentage of orders requiring manual intervention or re-shipping due to customization logic errors.
    *   *Trigger:* Any error rate >1% (indicates system instability).
*   **Cancellation Reason Code "Shipping Speed":** Explicit cancellations citing delay.
    *   *Trigger:* >5% increase in this specific reason code among non-customizers.

#### 4. Operational Impact Projection
*   **Average Fulfillment Time:** Compare the mean time-from-close-to-ship for Control vs. Treatment.
    *   *Note:* Ensure the 48-hour delay applies only to custom orders, or if it has created a bottleneck delaying *all* orders.
*   **Warehouse Throughput:** Orders processed per hour during peak customization windows.

#### Recommendation Framework for Friday Decision
*   **GO (Launch to 100%):** If Segment A churn is significantly lower, AND Segment B churn matches Control, AND guardrails are stable.
*   **ITERATE (Hold at 20% or Pause):** If Segment B churn is higher than Control (indicating the delay hurts everyone) OR if support volume spikes unmanageably.
*   **NO-GO (Rollback):** If the net cohort churn (Treatment total) is higher than Control, or if fulfillment errors exceed 1%.

Run a survival analysis on the current 2,000 users segmented by adoption status. Do not aggregate the data; the risk lies entirely in the non-adopters within the treatment group. If non-adopters are churning faster than the control group due to perceived delays, the feature cannot scale without fixing the fulfillment bottleneck first.
