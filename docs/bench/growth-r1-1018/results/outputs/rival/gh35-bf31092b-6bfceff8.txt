# Network Effect Measurement Framework for ConnectPro

To demonstrate non-linear value creation to Series B investors, you must move beyond vanity metrics (total connections) and measure **activation thresholds** and **retention elasticity**. Your hypothesis—that 10 connections drive retention—is a specific "aha moment" claim that requires validation before scaling. Implement the following three-tier measurement strategy:

### 1. Validate the Activation Threshold (The "10 Connection" Hypothesis)
Do not assume 10 is the magic number; prove it. You need to identify the specific inflection point where retention curves diverge.

*   **Metric:** **Cohort Retention by Connection Count Bracket**
*   **Implementation:** Segment users who joined in the same month into brackets based on connections made within their first 14 days: (0–2), (3–5), (6–9), (10–14), (15+).
*   **Analysis:** Plot Day-90 retention for each bracket. If your hypothesis holds, the (10–14) bracket should show a statistically significant jump in retention compared to (6–9).
*   **Investor Narrative:** "Users who reach 10 connections in week 2 have a 3x higher Day-90 retention rate than those with fewer than 5, validating our activation threshold."

### 2. Measure Retention Elasticity (The Non-Linear Proof)
Investors need to see that each *additional* user adds disproportionate value to the whole, not just linear growth.

*   **Metric:** **Marginal Retention Lift per Additional Connection**
*   **Implementation:** Calculate the change in Day-90 retention probability for every single additional connection a user makes, up to 20 connections.
*   **Analysis:** Look for convexity. If the lift in retention from connection 9→10 is significantly higher than 4→5, you have evidence of a network effect tipping point. If the curve flattens after 10, your value prop is linear, not exponential.
*   **Investor Narrative:** "Retention probability increases exponentially up to 12 connections, after which it plateaus, indicating a clear saturation point for initial network density."

### 3. Track Viral Coefficient with Quality Filters
Raw invitation counts are misleading if they don't lead to active connections.

*   **Metric:** **Qualified Viral Coefficient (k)**
*   **Formula:** $k = (\text{Invites Sent per User}) \times (\text{Conversion Rate}) \times (\text{Activations})$
*   **Critical Adjustment:** Define "Activation" strictly as the invitee reaching *their* first 10 connections within 30 days. Do not count a signup as a viral success unless they also become a retained user.
*   **Analysis:** If $k > 1$ using *qualified* activations, your growth is self-sustaining. If $k < 1$ but close, focus on improving the conversion of invitees to activated users.
*   **Investor Narrative:** "Our qualified viral coefficient is 1.2, meaning every 100 activated users generate 120 *new activated users* organically."

### Metrics to Deprioritize for Now
*   **Sub-industry Density:** Too granular for a Series B macro narrative. Use this later for product features, not high-level valuation.
*   **Inbound Message Rate:** This is a *lagging* indicator of engagement, not a driver of the network effect itself. It correlates with retention but doesn't prove the *mechanism* of value creation.

### Immediate Next Steps
1.  **Run a Historical Cohort Analysis:** Use existing data to plot retention vs. early connection counts immediately. Confirm if "10" is actually the right number; it might be 7 or 15.
2.  **Instrument "Connection Milestone" Events:** Ensure your analytics track exactly when a user hits connection counts 5, 10, and 15 to trigger automated engagement campaigns (e.g., "You're 2 connections away from unlocking full value!").
3.  **Build the "Network Effect Curve":** Visualize the marginal retention lift for the board deck. This single chart will do more to prove non-linear value than any table of raw numbers.

*Note: Ensure all retention calculations use closed 90-day windows to avoid partial-period data corruption. Do not mix current month spend/users with incomplete retention data.*
