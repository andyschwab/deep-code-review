### Framework for Churn Model Assessment: Fairness, Effectiveness, and Incrementality

To validate your churn model before automation, execute the following three-phase assessment focusing on fairness segmentation, incremental lift measurement, and cost-benefit analysis.

#### 1. Fairness & Bias Segmentation
Do not rely on aggregate accuracy (78%). You must slice performance metrics (Precision, Recall, False Positive Rate) across these specific dimensions to identify disparate impact:

*   **Demographics:** Age brackets, gender, and location (specifically comparing France vs. Germany to detect regional bias).
*   **Tenure & Loyalty:** Segment by customer lifetime (e.g., <3 months, 3–12 months, >1 year). High false positives here indicate you are penalizing loyal users who are merely exhibiting normal usage dips.
*   **Subscription Tier:** Free vs. Premium vs. Enterprise. Ensure the model isn't flagging price-sensitive tiers as "churn risk" simply because they have lower engagement thresholds.
*   **Usage Patterns:** Heavy users vs. dormant users. Verify the model distinguishes between "about to churn" and "naturally low usage."
*   **Protected Attributes (Proxy Check):** If direct demographic data is limited, test for proxies (e.g., language preference, payment method) that might correlate with protected classes under EU/UK regulations.

**Metric to Watch:** Compare the **False Positive Rate (FPR)** across segments. If loyal users in a specific demographic have a significantly higher FPR, the model is unfairly targeting them for discounts they don't need, eroding margin and potentially annoying the customer.

#### 2. Holdout Group Design for Incremental Impact
To measure true lift (users saved *because* of the intervention) rather than just correlation, you must run a randomized controlled trial (RCT) alongside the model rollout.

*   **Population:** The 15,000 weekly users identified as "High Risk."
*   **Split:** Randomly assign users into four groups:
    1.  **Treatment A (Model + Intervention):** Receives discount offer (90% of high-risk pool).
    2.  **Holdout B (Model + No Intervention):** Identified as high risk but receives *no* offer (10% of high-risk pool). This measures the baseline churn rate of the predicted cohort without interference.
    3.  **Control C (Random + Intervention):** Randomly selected from the *general* population (not flagged by model) receiving the offer. This tests if the offer works on non-churners (waste).
    4.  **Control D (Random + No Intervention):** General population receiving nothing (baseline global churn).
*   **Success Metric:** Calculate **Incremental Lift** = (Churn Rate in Holdout B) – (Churn Rate in Treatment A).
    *   If Churn in B ≈ Churn in A, the model is identifying users who would have stayed anyway (low incremental value), or the offer is ineffective.

#### 3. Cost Analysis: False Positives vs. False Negatives
Define your loss function to optimize the decision threshold beyond simple accuracy.

*   **False Positive (FP) Cost:** You give a discount to a user who would *not* have churned.
    *   *Cost:* Discount amount + potential brand devaluation + annoyance risk.
    *   *Calculation:* $FP\_Count \times Average\_Discount\_Value$.
*   **False Negative (FN) Cost:** You fail to identify a user who *does* churn.
    *   *Cost:* Lost Customer Lifetime Value (LTV) + acquisition cost to replace them.
    *   *Calculation:* $FN\_Count \times (LTV - Retention\_Campaign\_Cost)$.
*   **Optimization:** If $Cost(FN) \gg Cost(FP)$, lower your probability threshold to catch more churners (accepting more FPs). If margins are tight and discounts are expensive, raise the threshold to ensure high precision.

**Reporting for Compliance:**
For the London compliance officer, document the **Disparate Impact Ratio** for each protected segment. If any group's selection rate is less than 80% of the highest group's rate, flag it as potential bias requiring model retraining or threshold adjustment before GDPR-compliant automation.

*Ensure the holdout group is excluded from all other marketing automation during the test window to prevent contamination.*
