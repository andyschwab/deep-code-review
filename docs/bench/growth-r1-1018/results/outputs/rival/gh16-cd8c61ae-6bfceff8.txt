**Executive Summary: Pricing Experiment Analysis (Days 1–45)**

The 45-day experiment reveals a divergent response across market segments, indicating distinct price elasticities that are currently cannibalizing enterprise velocity while expanding SMB volume.

**1. Market Segmentation & Elasticity Signals**
*   **SMB Segment (High Elasticity, Low Friction):** The 25% surge in subscription volume despite a 15% price increase suggests strong inelastic demand or a successful "quality filter" effect. Smaller businesses appear less sensitive to this specific price point, possibly viewing the increase as a signal of higher value, or the friction successfully filtered out non-serious trials, resulting in higher conversion efficiency among remaining leads.
*   **Enterprise Segment (High Sensitivity, High Friction):** The 10% decline in closed deals indicates high price sensitivity or increased friction in the procurement process. Unlike SMBs, enterprise buyers operate on longer cycles with stricter budget approvals. The discount on annual contracts may not have been sufficient to offset the perceived risk of the entry-level price hike, or the timing coincided with a natural procurement lull.

**2. The Volume vs. Velocity Tension**
The core tension lies between **acquisition efficiency** (SMB volume) and **revenue density** (Enterprise value). While total unit volume is up, the drop in enterprise closures threatens overall Annual Recurring Revenue (ARR) growth if the average contract value (ACV) of the new SMB cohort does not compensate for the lost enterprise deals. The assumption that higher entry prices improve sales focus is partially validated by the SMB filter effect, but it is contradicted by the enterprise slowdown, suggesting the pricing structure may be misaligned with large-deal negotiation dynamics.

**3. Seasonality vs. Pricing Impact**
Attributing the enterprise decline solely to seasonality is risky without historical baseline data for this specific 45-day window. However, the simultaneity of the SMB surge and enterprise drop strongly correlates with the pricing change. If seasonality were the primary driver, we would likely see a broad-based slowdown or stability across segments, not a sharp divergence. The data suggests the pricing change is the primary variable, with seasonality potentially acting as an amplifier for enterprise hesitation.

**Recommendation for CFO Decision:**
The experiment confirms that SMB and Enterprise segments respond to price changes as distinct markets. The current structure drives volume but risks stalling high-value growth. Before permanent approval, isolate the enterprise variable: determine if the 10% drop is a temporary delay (deals pushing to next quarter) or a permanent loss (deals lost to competitors). If the latter, the entry-level price increase is creating a ceiling for enterprise expansion that the annual discount cannot currently penetrate.

*Note: Final approval should be contingent on a 30-day lag analysis to confirm if enterprise deals are merely delayed or truly lost.*
