**How to Account for the Control Group Anomaly**

The core issue is that your A/B test relies on the assumption that the control group represents "business as usual." When the control group deviates significantly from historical baselines (–10%), that assumption is broken. This suggests a confounding variable affected the entire population during the test window (early October), such as:
*   **Seasonality:** A post-summer slump in productivity app usage.
*   **External Events:** Industry news or competitor actions affecting user behavior.
*   **Traffic Quality:** A marketing campaign may have driven a surge of low-intent users who were disproportionately assigned to the control group due to randomization variance, or who diluted the control metrics overall.

Because the control group is "broken" as a baseline, you cannot calculate a true lift. The variant group might have also suffered a 10% drop (or more) relative to what *their* historical performance would have been, meaning the tutorial could be neutral or even harmful.
