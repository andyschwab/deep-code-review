### Proposed Activation Definitions for GeoCrowd

Based on your data signals, here are three concrete activation definitions to test, ranging from immediate frequency to deeper engagement.

**Option 1: The "Dual-Action" Threshold**
*   **Definition:** A user is activated when they add their first Point of Interest (POI) **AND** perform one additional action (edit a road segment OR add a second POI) within the first 48 hours.
*   **Pros:** Directly addresses your finding that single POI addition is a weak predictor. It captures the "second step" momentum without requiring the rare road edit immediately. It is easy to implement and measure in your current stack.
*   **Cons:** May significantly lower your raw activation rate initially, which could alarm stakeholders unfamiliar with the metric shift. It delays the "activated" timestamp for users who only do one thing.

**Option 2: The "Editor" Milestone (High Intent)**
*   **Definition:** A user is activated only upon completing their first **road segment edit**.
*   **Pros:** Aligns perfectly with your strongest retention signal. Users reaching this state are highly likely to become long-term community mappers. This defines your "power user" cohort clearly.
*   **Cons:** Extremely low volume (currently ~15% of new sign-ups). Using this as your primary north-star metric might make growth look stagnant even if top-of-funnel health is improving. It ignores the 85% who might convert later or contribute via POIs only.

**Option 3: The "Contribution Score" (Weighted Model)**
*   **Definition:** A user is activated when they reach a **score of 5 points** within 7 days:
    *   Add POI = 1 point
    *   Edit Road Segment = 3 points
    *   Interact with another mapper (comment/review) = 2 points
*   **Pros:** Captures the "combination of actions" hypothesis you mentioned. It allows flexibility in *how* a user activates (diversity vs. volume) and rewards high-value behaviors (editing) more heavily. It is robust against gaming single metrics.
*   **Cons:** Complex to explain to the executive team and harder to track in simple dashboards. Requires defining the scoring weights arbitrarily until validated by historical regression analysis.

### Recommendation for QBR
For your Tuesday presentation, recommend **Option 1 (Dual-Action)** as the primary metric to adopt immediately. It balances statistical significance with predictive power and directly solves the "40% churn after first POI" problem. Propose **Option 3** as a secondary, experimental metric to run in parallel for the next quarter to validate if social interaction or diversity adds predictive value beyond simple repetition.
