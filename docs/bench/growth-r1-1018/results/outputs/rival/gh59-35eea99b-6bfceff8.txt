### Analysis Structure: Search Algorithm Impact Assessment

**1. Isolate the Causal Effect (A/B Test Analysis)**
Leverage your existing 50/50 traffic split to treat this as a live A/B test rather than a before/after comparison. This inherently controls for seasonal trends, as both groups experience the same external factors simultaneously.
*   **Cohort Definition:** Split users into "Control" (old algorithm) and "Treatment" (new algorithm) based on their assigned traffic bucket. Ensure randomization holds by comparing pre-exposure metrics (e.g., historical AOV, device mix) between groups.
*   **Primary Metric Construction:** Move beyond top-line averages. Calculate **Revenue Per Query (RPQ)** for both groups. This combines CTR and AOV into a single north-star metric: $RPQ = (Clicks / Queries) \times AOV$. If RPQ is flat or down despite higher CTR, the algorithm is driving low-value engagement.
*   **Statistical Significance:** With 600k daily queries per group over 14 days, you have sufficient power to detect small changes. Calculate confidence intervals for the difference in RPQ and AOV. Do not rely on point estimates alone.

**2. Diagnose the Mechanism (Segmentation & Funnel)**
The 10% CTR lift and 5% AOV dip suggest a specific behavioral shift. Segment the data to identify *where* value is leaking:
*   **Price Tier Distribution:** Analyze the price distribution of clicked items. Has the new algorithm biased results toward lower-priced items? Compare the mean and median price of clicked items in Control vs. Treatment.
*   **Query Intent Analysis:** Segment by query type (navigational vs. exploratory vs. specific brand). The dip may be isolated to high-intent commercial queries where precision matters more than discovery.
*   **Conversion Rate Check:** Did the conversion rate (Click-to-Order) change?
    *   *Scenario A:* CTR up, CVR flat, AOV down → Users are finding cheaper alternatives faster (potential win for price-sensitive segments).
    *   *Scenario B:* CTR up, CVR down, AOV down → Results are misleading or irrelevant (critical failure).

**3. Assess Temporal Stability (Learning Phase vs. Structural Flaw)**
To determine if this is a temporary learning phase:
*   **Daily Trend Lines:** Plot RPQ and AOV daily for both groups. If the gap is widening or stable, it is structural. If the Treatment group's AOV is creeping upward day-over-day while CTR stabilizes, the model may be learning.
*   **Cohort Aging:** Compare Day 1 vs. Day 14 performance for users exposed to the new algo. Are returning users behaving differently than new exposures?

**4. Customer Satisfaction Proxies**
Since direct satisfaction surveys lag, use behavioral proxies:
*   **Return Rate:** Check if items purchased via the Treatment group have higher return rates.
*   **Search Refinement:** Measure "pogo-sticking" (clicking a result and immediately returning to SERP) or subsequent search refinements. An increase suggests the initial results were misleading.

**Recommendation Framework for VP**
Present a decision matrix based on **Net Revenue Impact**:
*   **Rollout:** If RPQ is statistically neutral or positive, and return rates are stable. The AOV dip is acceptable if volume compensates.
*   **Revert:** If RPQ is significantly negative, or if return rates/refinements indicate poor relevance.
*   **Hybrid/Iterate:** If the impact varies by segment (e.g., great for browsing, poor for branded search), recommend a targeted rollout or parameter tuning before the spring sale.

Do not wait for the spring sale to resolve this; the risk of compounding revenue loss during a high-traffic event is too high. Finalize the analysis within 48 hours using the existing split.

*Note: Ensure data integrity by filtering out bot traffic which often inflates CTR without contributing to revenue. All metrics must use the same observation window for numerator and denominator.*
