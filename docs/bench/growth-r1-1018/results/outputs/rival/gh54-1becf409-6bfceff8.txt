# Investigative Plan: DAU Growth vs. Revenue Stagnation

## Phase 1: Segment the Traffic Surge (Quality Assessment)
Determine if the 18% DAU growth consists of users with low monetization potential.
*   **Action:** Break down the new DAU cohort by acquisition source, geography, and device.
*   **Hypothesis A (Low-Quality Traffic):** The surge is driven by viral, non-premium content (e.g., short clips, user-generated content) attracting users from regions with low purchasing power or via channels that historically yield low LTV.
*   **Hypothesis B (High-Quality Traffic):** The new users match the profile of existing payers, indicating the friction lies post-acquisition.

## Phase 2: Audit the "February Content" Impact
Isolate the behavior of users engaging specifically with the new library added in February.
*   **Action:** Create a cohort of users whose primary viewing activity is the new content. Compare their conversion rate against the legacy content cohort.
*   **Hypothesis C (Value Mismatch):** The new content drives high engagement (time spent) but is perceived as "free-tier sufficient," failing to trigger the desire for premium features (e.g., no exclusives, ads are tolerable, or content doesn't justify the upgrade price).
*   **Hypothesis D (Discovery Friction):** Premium-only content within the new library is not being discovered or is gated too aggressively, causing drop-off before the value proposition is realized.

## Phase 3: Map the Monetization Funnel Friction
Identify where the conversion process breaks for the new 18%.
*   **Action:** Analyze the drop-off rates at each step: *View Content → Click Upgrade Prompt → Land on Pricing Page → Initiate Checkout → Complete Payment*.
*   **Hypothesis E (UX Friction):** A technical bug or UX change in the checkout flow (coinciding with the traffic surge) is preventing completion, specifically on mobile or specific browsers used by the new cohort.
*   **Hypothesis F (Offer Misalignment):** The pricing tiers or promotional offers presented to new users are not compelling compared to the perceived value of the free tier.

## Phase 4: Engagement Depth vs. Breadth Analysis
Challenge the assumption that "more views = more upgrades."
*   **Action:** Correlate conversion rates with *depth* of engagement (completing series, multiple sessions per week) rather than just *breadth* (total DAU).
*   **Hypothesis G (Superficial Engagement):** The new users are "grazers" (single session, low depth) who naturally convert at lower rates regardless of content quality, diluting the overall conversion percentage despite higher total volume.

## Recommended Immediate Next Steps
1.  **Cohort Isolation:** Immediately segment the Feb–April acquisitions by source and content affinity to validate Hypotheses A and C.
2.  **Funnel Audit:** Run a technical audit on the checkout flow for the top 3 devices used by new signups to rule out Hypothesis E.
3.  **Survey Deployment:** Trigger an exit survey on the pricing page for non-converting users to gather qualitative data on price sensitivity vs. content desire.

**Note:** Do not aggregate data across old and new cohorts; the average (4.5%) is masking the specific behavior of the surge.
**Note:** Prepare a "worst-case" narrative for investors assuming Hypothesis A is true, alongside the remediation plan.
