**Recommendation: Implement a Dual-Track Strategy with Immediate Normalization and Targeted Retroactive Correction**

Do not choose exclusively between fixing history or protecting the future; you must do both to satisfy the CDO's requirement for minimal disruption and data integrity. A "future-only" approach leaves Q4 reporting fundamentally flawed, while a full "retroactive rewrite" of three months of global data poses an unacceptable risk of breaking downstream dependencies and delaying the CDO's deadline.

**Phase 1: Immediate Normalization Layer (Future Data)**
Deploy a normalization middleware effective immediately for all incoming data from London, Tokyo, and New York. This layer must enforce a strict UTC standard with mandatory millisecond precision before data enters the central warehouse.
*   **Benefit:** Stops the bleeding instantly. Ensures all new hourly aggregations and inventory algorithms run on clean data starting today.
*   **Risk:** Low. This isolates the fix to the ingestion pipeline without touching historical tables or existing reports.

**Phase 2: Targeted Retroactive Correction (Historical Data)**
Do not attempt to rewrite the entire three-month historical dataset. Instead, execute a targeted correction strategy:
1.  **Isolate Critical Windows:** Identify the specific hourly windows where inventory replenishment algorithms triggered actions. These are the only records where the 4-hour timezone discrepancy likely caused operational errors (e.g., false stockouts or overordering).
2.  **Recalculate Aggregates Only:** For the Q4 reports already sent to stakeholders, issue a "Data Quality Advisory" noting potential variances in hourly granularity. Provide recalculated daily and weekly totals for the affected regions, as these higher-level aggregations often smooth out hourly timezone errors.
3.  **Patch Specific Records:** Only rewrite the raw historical records for the ~15% of entries missing millisecond precision if they fall within the identified critical windows. Leave non-critical historical data as-is but flag it with a metadata tag indicating "lower precision."

**Risk/Benefit Analysis for CDO Approval**

| Approach | Risks | Benefits |
| :--- | :--- | :--- |
| **Full Retroactive Fix** | **High:** High probability of breaking downstream BI tools; requires massive engineering downtime; misses the tomorrow deadline. | Creates a perfect historical record. |
| **Future-Only Fix** | **Critical:** Validates flawed Q4 reports; inventory algorithms continue to react to bad historical patterns; erodes stakeholder trust. | Fastest to implement; zero risk to history. |
| **Dual-Track (Recommended)** | **Moderate:** Requires careful coordination to ensure the normalization layer doesn't conflict with patch scripts. | **Stops future errors immediately;** limits engineering scope to critical historical windows; allows delivery of corrected executive summaries by tomorrow. |

**Action Plan for Tomorrow's Deadline**
1.  **Approve Engineering Sprint:** Allocate resources to build the UTC normalization layer (Priority 1) and the script to recalculate daily/weekly Q4 aggregates for London, Tokyo, and NY (Priority 2).
2.  **Stakeholder Communication:** Prepare a brief note for report recipients stating that hourly data between August 20th and present contains known timezone variances, but corrected daily totals are attached.
3.  **Long-Term Backlog:** Schedule the full historical millisecond precision cleanup for Q1 next year when reporting pressure is lower.

This approach minimizes operational disruption by avoiding a full database rewrite while ensuring the CDO can confidently sign off on corrected Q4 top-line numbers immediately.
