### Executive Summary: Push Notification Timing Experiment

**Recommendation:** Prioritize **workout completion rates** over open rates for the global rollout. Select the **6:00 AM** timing, but reframe the success metric from "engagement" to "actionable conversion."

#### Interpretation of Conflicting Metrics

The divergence between a 12% lift in opens and only a 3% lift in completions reveals a critical distinction between **attention capture** and **behavioral execution**.

1.  **The "Snooze" Hypothesis is Likely Partially True:** The small gap in completion rates suggests that a significant portion of the 7:30 AM group is indeed working out, just later than the notification trigger. If the 6:00 AM notification acts as an immediate alarm for highly motivated users, the 7:30 AM notification may function as a "snooze button" for users who intend to exercise but delay the action. Since your tracking relies on immediate logging, delayed workouts are being undercounted in the 7:30 AM cohort, artificially suppressing their completion metrics.
2.  **Diminishing Returns on Motivation:** The 6:00 AM slot successfully filters for high-intent users (those willing to wake up immediately). However, the fact that a 12% increase in attention yielded only a 3% increase in results indicates that **timing alone cannot manufacture motivation**. You are reaching more people earlier, but not necessarily converting them into *more* workouts; you are simply shifting *when* the motivated users act.
3.  **Value Delivery vs. Vanity Metrics:** Open rates measure the effectiveness of the *headline* and *timing* in grabbing attention. Completion rates measure the product's core value proposition. A strategy optimized for opens risks training users to engage with notifications without completing the core loop, leading to notification fatigue over time.

#### Strategic Implications for Rollout

*   **Primary Metric Shift:** For the global rollout, define success strictly by **workout completion within a 4-hour window** of the notification, rather than immediate logging. This accounts for the "snooze" behavior and provides a fairer comparison of whether the 7:30 AM group eventually converts.
*   **Segmentation Strategy:** The data implies two distinct user personas:
    *   *The Early Riser (6:00 AM):* High motivation, immediate action. Keep this timing for users who historically log out before 8:00 AM.
    *   *The Delayed Starter (7:30 AM):* Moderate motivation, delayed action. This group may benefit from a follow-up nudge or a wider attribution window rather than an earlier alarm which they might ignore or dismiss.
*   **Rollout Decision:** Proceed with the 6:00 AM timing as the default **only if** the 3% completion lift translates to statistically significant revenue or retention gains. If the operational cost of earlier support or server load outweighs a 3% gain, consider a hybrid approach: send at 6:00 AM to high-intent segments and 7:30 AM to the rest.

**Conclusion:** Do not optimize for the 12% open rate lift; it is a vanity metric in this context. The 3% completion lift is the true signal of value. If 6:00 AM drives more actual workouts, however marginally, it is the superior time. However, verify if the 7:30 AM group is logging late before discarding that slot entirely, as shifting them earlier might not increase total volume, only shift the timestamp.

*Note: Ensure your analytics window for "completion" extends at least 2–3 hours post-notification to capture delayed loggers before finalizing the rollout.*
*Next step: Run a qualitative survey with the 7:30 AM non-converters to confirm if they are working out later without logging.*
