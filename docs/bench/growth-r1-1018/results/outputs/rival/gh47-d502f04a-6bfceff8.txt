# FoodAggro Japan Launch: Localized Success Scorecard

## Executive Summary
While Global North markets prioritize Average Order Value (AOV), the Japanese market entry strategy must pivot to **Order Frequency** and **Service Reliability** as primary success indicators. The hypothesis posits that high-density urban living and smaller household sizes drive a "high-frequency, low-basket" behavior pattern. Success in Q1 will be defined by validating this behavioral shift and establishing operational trust, rather than maximizing immediate revenue per transaction.

## Primary Metrics (The "North Stars")

### 1. Orders Per Active User Per Week (OPAU)
*   **Why it matters:** This replaces AOV as the leading indicator of product-market fit. If the hypothesis holds, retention and Lifetime Value (LTV) will be driven by repeat usage, not basket size.
*   **Target:** Validate the assumption of **≥3.0 orders/week** for users in high-density wards (e.g., Shibuya, Minato) by Week 6 of launch.
*   **Benchmark Strategy:** Do not benchmark against the global AOV-driven model. Instead, establish a local baseline in Week 1-2 and measure week-over-week growth in frequency. Success is defined by the *slope* of adoption, not the absolute dollar value.

### 2. On-Time Delivery Rate (<20 Minutes)
*   **Why it matters:** The hypothesis explicitly links frequency to speed. In Japan, reliability is a baseline expectation, not a differentiator. Failure here will suppress frequency regardless of restaurant variety.
*   **Target:** **≥90%** of orders delivered within 20 minutes of placement during peak hours.
*   **Benchmark Strategy:** This is an absolute operational standard, not a relative benchmark. If this metric dips below 85%, frequency targets will likely become unattainable.

## Secondary Metrics (Diagnostic & Trade-off Analysis)

### 3. Fee Sensitivity Ratio
*   **Definition:** Conversion rate drop-off when delivery fees exceed ¥200 vs. ¥0.
*   **Purpose:** To resolve the conflicting survey data regarding fees vs. variety.
*   **Action:** Run localized A/B tests in Q1 offering free delivery vs. expanded restaurant lists to determine the elasticity of demand.

### 4. Restaurant Density Coverage
*   **Definition:** Percentage of user addresses within a 15-minute delivery window of ≥15 active restaurant partners.
*   **Purpose:** Frequency is impossible without proximate supply. This is a lagging operational metric that predicts future frequency potential.

## Implementation & Reporting Cadence

**Week 1-2 (Validation Phase):**
*   Focus exclusively on **On-Time Delivery Rate** and **OPAU**.
*   Ignore AOV fluctuations; expect Japanese AOV to be 40-50% lower than the global $28 average.
*   Report daily on delivery performance to operations; weekly on frequency trends to marketing.

**Week 3-8 (Optimization Phase):**
*   Introduce **Fee Sensitivity** analysis.
*   Begin correlating restaurant density with local frequency rates to identify expansion priorities.

## Strategic Recommendation for the Country GM
Do not attempt to force global AOV targets onto the Japanese launch. Doing so may incentivize marketing tactics (e.g., "Spend ¥3000 get free delivery") that actively suppress the high-frequency behavior required for long-term success in this market. The Q1 goal is **habit formation**, not revenue maximization. If we achieve 3+ orders per week per active user with 90% on-time delivery, the lower basket size will be offset by volume and retention, validating the localized strategy.

*Note: This scorecard assumes the operational capability to staff for 20-minute delivery windows; if logistics cannot support this, the frequency hypothesis must be re-evaluated immediately.*
