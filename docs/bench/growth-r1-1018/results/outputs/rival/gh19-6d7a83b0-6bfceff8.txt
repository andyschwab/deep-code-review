**Interpretation of Segmented A/B Test Results: 'Add to Cart' Button Color**

**Executive Summary**
The aggregate data indicates no net impact on conversion (flat at 4.2% across 100,000 users). However, this aggregate stability masks a significant divergence in user response based on operating system. The orange button variant drives opposing behaviors: a positive lift for Android users and a negative decline for iOS users. This suggests that color perception, platform-specific rendering, or established design conventions interact differently with the variant depending on the device ecosystem.

**Segmented Performance Analysis**

*   **Android Segment (+7% Lift):**
    Users on Android devices responded favorably to the orange button. In this environment, the color change likely improved visibility, created a stronger contrast against the system UI, or aligned better with material design expectations where vibrant accent colors are common. The lift indicates that for this specific cohort, the orange variant reduces friction in the decision-to-action phase.

*   **iOS Segment (-5% Decline):**
    Conversely, iOS users experienced a reduction in conversion. This decline suggests the orange button may clash with iOS human interface guidelines, appear less trustworthy, or suffer from rendering issues specific to Apple's display calibration or Safari webview (if applicable). The negative delta implies that for iOS users, the original blue button remains the superior performer, possibly due to familiarity or better aesthetic integration.

**Implications for the Design System**

The divergence creates a "cancellation effect" in the top-line metric. Because the gains in one segment are roughly offset by the losses in the other, the global average appears neutral. Relying solely on the aggregate result would obscure the fact that the change actively harms the experience for half the user base while helping the other half.

For the mobile style guide, these results highlight that a single, static design rule for button color may not be optimal across all platforms. The data supports the hypothesis that platform context matters. If the design system enforces a uniform color regardless of OS, it inherently accepts a trade-off: gaining efficiency on Android at the cost of iOS performance, or vice versa.

The discrepancy points to two distinct user mental models or technical environments. The Android cohort perceives the orange button as an improvement, while the iOS cohort perceives it as a degradation. This indicates that the "best" design is not universal but contextual. Updating the style guide to account for platform-specific variables could allow the team to capture the upside on Android without incurring the penalty on iOS.

**Key Considerations for Finalization**
*   **Aggregate vs. Segment:** The flat global metric is a mathematical artifact of opposing segment trends, not evidence of "no effect."
*   **Platform Specificity:** The design system may need to move from a "one-size-fits-all" color token to platform-conditional logic to maximize total conversion.
*   **UX Consistency:** Implementing different colors per OS introduces a variation in brand presentation that must be weighed against the conversion benefits.

*Note: This interpretation describes the directional impact observed in the data without applying statistical significance thresholds or prescribing a final rollout strategy.*
*Further validation of the root cause (e.g., rendering checks vs. user preference studies) is recommended before locking the style guide.*
