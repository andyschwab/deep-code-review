# Diagnostic Analysis Plan: Verification-Induced Churn

## Executive Summary
The data indicates a **friction and trust problem**, not a value problem. While verified users show higher retention (validating the feature's long-term value), the 35% drop-off at the verification step suggests the barrier to entry is too high or poorly communicated. The immediate churn spike is likely driven by users abandoning the process before becoming "active" users, rather than existing users leaving due to dissatisfaction.

## 1. Quantitative Segmentation Strategy
Slice your data by these specific dimensions to isolate the root cause:

### A. Funnel Step Drop-off (The "Where")
Break down the 35% drop-off by specific sub-steps:
*   **View Verification Screen → Click "Send Code"**: Indicates **trust/motivation** issues (users don't see the value or fear privacy risks).
*   **Click "Send Code" → Enter Code**: Indicates **delivery/technical** issues (codes not arriving, SMS delays, email spam filters).
*   **Enter Code → Submit**: Indicates **UX/validation** errors (wrong format, code expired, UI confusion).

### B. User Context (The "Who")
*   **Device Type**: Compare mobile vs. desktop. Mobile users may struggle with switching apps to retrieve SMS codes.
*   **Traffic Source**: Do paid ads (high intent) churn less than organic social (lower intent)?
*   **Geography**: Are specific regions experiencing SMS delivery failures or higher privacy skepticism?
*   **Account Type**: B2B vs. B2C. B2B users might have corporate firewalls blocking verification emails.

### C. Time-to-Verify (The "How Long")
*   Correlate **time spent on verification screen** with abandonment.
    *   *Short time (<10s)*: Immediate rejection (trust/motivation).
    *   *Long time (>2 mins)*: Waiting for code (delivery issue) or confusion.

## 2. Qualitative Signals to Investigate
Triangulate the quantitative data with these signals:

*   **Support Ticket Tags**: Filter for "verification," "code not received," "privacy," and "phone number" since Nov 1st. Look for volume spikes and specific error descriptions.
*   **Session Replays**: Watch 20–30 recordings of users dropping off at the verification step.
    *   *Look for*: Rage clicks, copying/pasting codes multiple times, switching tabs/apps and not returning, or immediate exits after reading the copy.
*   **Exit Survey Data**: If you have an exit intent survey on the verification page, analyze the text responses for keywords like "too much info," "later," or "spam."
*   **App Store Reviews**: Check recent reviews for mentions of "verification" or "phone number."

## 3. Hypotheses for Product VP
Present these three distinct hypotheses with recommended next steps:

### Hypothesis 1: The "Trust Deficit" (Primary Suspect)
*   **Premise**: Users do not understand *why* a phone number is required for a shopping account and perceive it as a privacy risk or overreach.
*   **Evidence to Cite**: High drop-off immediately upon viewing the screen (before requesting code); support tickets mentioning "privacy" or "why do you need this?"
*   **Proposed Test**: A/B test the verification copy. Variant A (Current) vs. Variant B (Value-prop focused: "Verify your phone to prevent fraud and recover your account instantly").

### Hypothesis 2: The "Delivery Latency" Friction
*   **Premise**: SMS/Email codes are arriving too slowly (>30 seconds), causing users to abandon the flow assuming it's broken.
*   **Evidence to Cite**: High drop-off between "Send Code" and "Enter Code"; session replays showing users waiting then leaving; geographic clusters of failure.
*   **Proposed Test**: Implement a "Resend Code" button with a countdown timer and display an estimated delivery time ("Code arrives in ~15s").

### Hypothesis 3: The "Mobile Context" Mismatch
*   **Premise**: Mobile users cannot easily switch between the browser/app and their SMS app to copy the code, leading to friction.
*   **Evidence to Cite**: Significantly higher churn rates on mobile devices compared to desktop.
*   **Proposed Test**: Enable "Auto-fill SMS" on iOS/Android or offer an email-only verification option for mobile users as a temporary bridge.

## Recommendation
**Do not roll back yet.** The higher retention of verified users proves the feature works for those who complete it. Instead, **immediately deploy Hypothesis 1 (Copy Test)** to address potential trust issues, as this is the fastest fix with the highest potential impact on the 35% drop-off. Simultaneously, audit SMS delivery logs for latency issues.

*Note: Ensure your analytics events distinguish between "verification_requested" and "verification_completed" to accurately measure the sub-step drop-offs described above.*
*If mobile churn is disproportionately high, consider making phone verification optional for mobile while keeping email mandatory as an interim mitigation.*
