### Recommended North Star Metric: **Weekly Carbon Reduced (Verified)**

Your current metric, "logs per week," measures activity, not outcome. It incentivizes users to open the app frequently, but as you suspected, it does not guarantee environmental impact. A user could log 10 times a week with negligible changes, while another logs once after completing a major challenge (e.g., switching to renewable energy) and achieves significant reduction.

To align with your mission and scale to corporate clients, your North Star must measure **value delivered**, not just effort expended.

**Primary Recommendation:** **Weekly Carbon Reduced (Verified)**
*   **Definition:** The sum of estimated CO2e savings from user actions logged and verified within a 7-day window.
*   **Why it wins:** It directly ties user behavior to your core mission (environmental impact). It naturally balances retention (users must return to log/verify) and depth of engagement (completing high-impact challenges). It scales to B2B by aggregating employee reductions for corporate sustainability reporting.

**Secondary Guardrail Metric:** **4-Week Retention Rate**
*   Monitor this to ensure you aren't optimizing for one-off "big wins" at the expense of long-term habit formation.

### Criteria for Selecting Your North Star

Use this framework to evaluate candidates before your December 1st meeting:

1.  **Mission Alignment:** Does an increase in this metric *guarantee* progress toward your core purpose? (Logs do not; Carbon Reduced does).
2.  **Actionability:** Can your team influence this metric through product changes? (You can design features to prompt high-impact actions; you cannot force users to log without value).
3.  **Measurability:** Can you calculate it reliably with current data? If your carbon calculation logic is weak, this fails.
4.  **Scalability:** Does this metric make sense for both individual consumers and future corporate dashboards? (Corporate clients care about total tonnage reduced, not login frequency).
5.  **Leading Indicator:** Does it predict long-term business success (LTV, referrals) better than lagging indicators like revenue?

### How to Test "Weekly Carbon Reduced" Before Committing

Since you need to validate actionability and measurability by December 1st, run this rapid analysis on your existing 30,000 users:

**1. The Correlation Check (Measurability & Value)**
Pull a cohort of users from 6 months ago. Compare their average "Weekly Carbon Reduced" in Month 1 against their retention in Month 3.
*   *Hypothesis:* Users with higher verified carbon reduction in Month 1 will have higher retention.
*   *Result:* If the correlation is weak, your carbon estimation model may be flawed, or the metric is too noisy. If strong, you have validation.

**2. The Sensitivity Test (Actionability)**
Identify your top 10% of users by "Carbon Reduced." Analyze their behavior path. Did they complete specific challenges? Did they log differently?
*   *Goal:* Determine if product interventions (e.g., highlighting specific challenges) can move the needle on this metric. If the top users achieved results through random chance rather than product guidance, the metric is not yet actionable.

**3. The Data Gap Audit**
Calculate the percentage of logs that *cannot* be converted to a carbon value due to missing data parameters.
*   *Threshold:* If >30% of logs lack sufficient data for calculation, you must improve data collection before adopting this as your North Star, or it will be incomplete.

**4. The B2B Simulation**
Aggregate individual user data into hypothetical "company" groups (e.g., by domain or region). Can you generate a meaningful "Total Carbon Reduced" report for these groups? If the data aggregates cleanly, your scalability requirement is met.

**Immediate Next Step:** Run the Correlation Check immediately. If "Carbon Reduced" correlates strongly with retention, present this data at your all-hands to unify the team around impact over activity.

*Note: Ensure your carbon calculation methodology is documented and defensible, especially for future corporate auditing.*
*If data quality is too low for "Carbon Reduced," use "Challenge Completion Rate" as a temporary proxy for impact.*
