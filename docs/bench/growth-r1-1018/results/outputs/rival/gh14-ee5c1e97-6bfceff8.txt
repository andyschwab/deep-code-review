**Executive Summary: Dashboard Customization Feature Test Analysis**

**Observed Trade-offs**
The four-week experiment reveals a clear divergence between engagement depth and output efficiency. The treatment group (1,200 accounts) demonstrated a **20% increase in time spent**, suggesting the drag-and-drop interface successfully increases interaction density. Conversely, the **8% decline in report exports** indicates a friction point in the core workflow of deriving and distributing insights.

**Interpretation of Diverging Metrics**

1.  **Engagement Quality vs. Quantity**
    The increase in time spent is ambiguous without further segmentation. It represents either:
    *   **Constructive Exploration:** Users are investing time to create high-value, personalized views that will yield better insights long-term.
    *   **Frictional Overhead:** Users are struggling with the new interface or getting distracted by layout mechanics, inflating time-on-site without adding value.
    *   **Novelty Effect:** The lift may be driven by early adopters testing the feature's limits, a behavior that typically decays over 4–6 weeks as the "newness" wears off.

2.  **The Export Decline Signal**
    The 8% drop in exports is a critical signal because exports often correlate with the completion of an analysis cycle and the distribution of value to stakeholders. This decline suggests:
    *   **Workflow Disruption:** The customization step may be interrupting the "analyze → export" flow, adding cognitive load or extra clicks.
    *   **Analysis Paralysis:** Too many layout options may be delaying the decision-making process, preventing users from finalizing reports.
    *   **Shift in Value Perception:** Users might be finding value *within* the live dashboard (reducing the need for static exports), though this requires validation via downstream metrics like sharing links or meeting usage.

**Strategic Implications for Q3 Decision**

*   **Risk of Vanity Metrics:** If the time-spent increase does not correlate with retention, feature adoption depth, or downstream revenue actions, it risks being a vanity metric that masks reduced productivity. Releasing now could optimize for "play" rather than "work."
*   **Fundamental Workflow Shift:** If the export drop is temporary while users learn the new paradigm, the feature could represent a modernization of the platform. However, if exports remain suppressed, the feature may fundamentally misalign with enterprise users' need for efficient reporting.
*   **Segmentation Necessity:** The aggregate data hides whether power users (who drive value) are behaving differently from casual users. A uniform rollout assumes homogeneity that likely doesn't exist.

**Key Unknowns for the CPO**
To distinguish between temporary novelty and fundamental shift, the following distinctions are required before a final go/no-go decision:
*   **Cohort Decay:** Does the time-spent metric stabilize or drop in weeks 5–8?
*   **User Segmentation:** Is the export drop concentrated among power users (high risk) or low-frequency users (lower risk)?
*   **Alternative Value Proxies:** Are users substituting exports with other sharing mechanisms (e.g., direct dashboard links, scheduled emails)?

The data presents a classic tension between engagement depth and workflow efficiency. The decision hinges on whether the organization prioritizes maximizing time-in-product (potential stickiness) or minimizing time-to-insight (potential productivity).

*Note: This analysis interprets the provided trade-offs without prescribing a release strategy. Further segmentation and longitudinal data are required to resolve the ambiguity between novelty and habit formation.*
