### Framework for Selecting the Reporting Module North-Star Metric

To align the engineering roadmap and demonstrate value by December 31, StockFlow should adopt a **"Value-Adjusted Efficiency Ratio"** as the primary success metric, rather than raw speed or volume. This framework prioritizes *outcome* over *output*, ensuring the metric correlates with retention without requiring historical data you do not yet possess.

#### 1. Metric Definition: Value-Adjusted Efficiency Ratio
**Formula:** `(Total Time Saved per Active Account) × (Report Completion Rate)`

*   **Total Time Saved:** The delta between the baseline time-to-generate (current state) and the new module's time-to-generate, aggregated per account.
*   **Report Completion Rate:** The percentage of initiated reports that are successfully generated, viewed, or exported. This filters out "failed" speed gains where users abandon the process due to complexity or errors.

**Why this works:**
*   **Eliminates Vanity:** It ignores the sheer volume of reports (40/month), focusing instead on the efficiency gain per active user.
*   **Captures Value:** Speed only matters if the report is actually used. Multiplying by completion rate ensures you are measuring *usable* speed.
*   **Actionable for Engineering:** It gives devs two clear levers: reduce latency (numerator) and improve UX reliability (denominator).

#### 2. Selection Criteria for the North-Star
When presenting to the VP of Product, validate the metric against these three constraints:
*   **Sensitivity:** Can the metric move within a 2-week sprint? (Raw churn cannot; this ratio can).
*   **Controllability:** Does the engineering team directly influence both variables? (Yes, via performance optimization and UI fixes).
*   **Strategic Alignment:** Does improving this metric logically support the hypothesis that "time saved drives retention"? (Yes, by quantifying the specific time benefit delivered).

#### 3. Implementation Roadmap (Now – Dec 31)
*   **Phase 1 (Baseline & Instrumentation):** Immediately instrument event tracking to capture `report_initiated`, `report_generated`, and `time_elapsed` for every session. Establish the current baseline average.
*   **Phase 2 (Iterative Optimization):** Set a quarterly target to increase the Ratio by 15%. Engineering sprints should focus on features that either reduce generation time or increase successful completions.
*   **Phase 3 (Validation):** By Q4, correlate the account-level Ratio scores with early renewal signals (e.g., support ticket volume regarding reporting, feature adoption depth).

#### 4. Tracking Downstream Churn Effects
Since you lack historical data linking speed to retention, you must construct a **Leading Indicator Cohort** over the next two quarters:

1.  **Segment by Efficiency:** Divide customers into terciles based on their "Value-Adjusted Efficiency Ratio" (High, Medium, Low adopters of the new speed).
2.  **Monitor Leading Indicators:** Instead of waiting for actual churn (which lags), track these proxy metrics monthly:
    *   **Support Ticket Density:** Number of "reporting issue" tickets per 1,000 reports generated.
    *   **Login Frequency:** Do high-efficiency users log in more often?
    *   **Expansion Signals:** Are high-efficiency accounts adding more user seats?
3.  **The Correlation Study:** By Q2, you will have enough data to run a regression analysis: *Does the High Efficiency cohort show statistically lower intent-to-churn signals than the Low Efficiency cohort?* This builds the business case for the next fiscal year.

**Recommendation:** Do not wait for renewal data to prove value. Use the **Value-Adjusted Efficiency Ratio** as your immediate north star to drive product improvements, while simultaneously building the dataset required to prove the retention hypothesis by year-end.

*Note: Ensure all time-tracking events include user-ID and account-ID properties to enable the cohort analysis described above.*
*This framework assumes the engineering team can accurately measure "time elapsed" at the session level without impacting report generation performance.*
