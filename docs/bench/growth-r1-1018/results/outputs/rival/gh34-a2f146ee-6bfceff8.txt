**Recommendation: Do Not Roll Out High-Frequency Defaults**

Based on the data provided, rolling out the "three times daily" frequency as the default setting for all new sign-ups is unsustainable for a long-term subscription business model. While the 15% lift in task completion validates the hypothesis that reminders drive action, the doubling of the unsubscribe rate to 4% within just three weeks signals a critical failure in user retention.

**The Sustainability Analysis**
Your business model relies on Lifetime Value (LTV), which is a function of retention duration. A 4% unsubscribe rate in three weeks implies an annualized churn rate that would likely decimate your cohort before they generate sufficient revenue to cover acquisition costs.
*   **Short-term gain:** 15% more completed tasks.
*   **Long-term pain:** 2x churn acceleration.
In a subscription economy, retaining a user is almost always more valuable than maximizing their activity for a few weeks before they leave. The data suggests that for a significant segment of your audience, the "cure" (reminders) has become more painful than the "disease" (forgetting habits).

**Strategic Middle Ground: Segmentation Over Standardization**
Abandoning the high-frequency approach entirely would be premature, as it clearly works for a subset of users who did not unsubscribe. The error lies in applying a single frequency to the entire population. Instead of a binary "roll out" or "kill" decision, adopt a segmented strategy:

1.  **Default to "Once Daily" or "On-Demand":** Set the default for new sign-ups to the lower-frequency option (likely "once daily" based on the control group stability). This protects the majority of users from notification fatigue while maintaining engagement.
2.  **Make "Three Times Daily" an Opt-In Power Feature:** Position the high-frequency setting as an advanced mode for "power users" or those specifically struggling with consistency. Market it as an intensity booster rather than a standard setting.
3.  **Implement Adaptive Triggers:** If technically feasible, use the first week of user behavior to recommend frequency. If a user consistently misses tasks with "once daily," prompt them to try "three times daily." If they unsubscribe or mute notifications, immediately downgrade the frequency automatically.

The goal is to maximize the number of users who stay subscribed long enough to form habits, not to maximize the number of reminders sent.
