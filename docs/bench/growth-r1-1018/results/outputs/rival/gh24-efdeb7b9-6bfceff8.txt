To holistically evaluate the guest checkout experiment for the marketing director, you must move beyond the aggregate 20% lift and 0% return rate by running the following specific cohort analyses. These will isolate whether the immediate revenue gain compensates for the potential loss in long-term customer lifetime value (LTV).

**1. 30-Day Purchase Frequency Cohort (Behavioral Return vs. Account Return)**
The current 0% return rate likely measures *account logins*, not *repeat purchases*. Guest users cannot "log in," so they appear non-returning even if they buy again using the same email.
*   **Action:** Create a cohort of users who completed a guest checkout between Sept 1–14. Track this cohort by email address (not user ID) for 30 days post-purchase.
*   **Metric:** Measure the percentage of these unique emails that completed a *second* transaction within 30 days, regardless of whether they created an account for the second purchase.
*   **Goal:** Determine the true behavioral retention rate. If guest users are buying again at a rate closer to 10-12% (vs. the 15% baseline), the "0%" figure is a tracking artifact, not a business failure.

**2. Email Validity and Deliverability Audit**
Before projecting future marketing value, verify the quality of the data captured during the experiment.
*   **Action:** Segment the guest checkout emails by validation status (syntax check, domain existence, SMTP ping) and engagement (opens/clicks if any post-purchase emails were sent).
*   **Metric:** Calculate the % of "risky" or "invalid" emails in the guest cohort compared to the registered user cohort.
*   **Goal:** Quantify the addressable audience. If 20% of guest emails are invalid, your potential recovery rate drops proportionally. This validates or invalidates the assumption that you can "recover" these users via email marketing.

**3. LTV Projection by Acquisition Mode (Short-Term vs. Long-Term Value)**
Compare the total value generated per user in the first 30 days, not just the conversion rate.
*   **Action:** Calculate the Average Order Value (AOV) and Total Revenue per Visitor (RPV) for both variants.
    *   *Guest RPV:* (Conversion Rate × AOV) + (Repeat Purchase Rate × Repeat AOV).
    *   *Registered RPV:* (Baseline Conversion × Baseline AOV) + (15% Return Rate × Return AOV).
*   **Metric:** Projected 30-day Revenue Per Visitor.
*   **Goal:** Determine if the 20% immediate lift in conversion generates enough cash flow to offset a potentially lower repeat rate. If guest users have a significantly lower AOV or zero repeat purchases even after the behavioral check above, the immediate lift may be a "hollow win."

**4. The "Account Creation" Conversion Funnel**
Analyze how many guest users eventually convert to registered users.
*   **Action:** Track the guest checkout cohort to see how many utilized a "create password" link in post-purchase emails or created an account during a second purchase.
*   **Metric:** Guest-to-Registered Conversion Rate within 30 days.
*   **Goal:** Assess the potential to migrate high-value guest users into the loyal registered base over time, rather than expecting immediate registration.

**5. Segment-Specific Performance (New vs. Returning Visitors)**
The friction of account creation impacts new and returning visitors differently.
*   **Action:** Split the experiment results by "First Time Visitor" and "Returning Visitor" (based on browser cookies or prior session data).
*   **Metric:** Conversion lift and return rate for each segment separately.
*   **Goal:** Identify if guest checkout should be the default *only* for new visitors, while forcing account creation for returning users who already have a profile. This hybrid approach often maximizes both acquisition and retention.

By executing these five analyses, you will present a data-driven view of whether the 20% conversion lift is sustainable profit or a short-term gain that erodes long-term equity.

*Note: Ensure your analytics tool matches users across sessions by email hash, not just device ID, to accurately track guest repeat purchases.*
