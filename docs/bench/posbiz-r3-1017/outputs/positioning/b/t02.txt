**Choice: Market GreenSpire as a serious supply-chain solution driven by the head chef's margin requirements.**

While the "menu marketing gimmick" generates excitement among marketing teams, the "supply-chain solution" offers the stronger, more scalable wedge for growing from 12 to 100 locations. Your current data indicates that the tangible economic value ($3,000 annual savings, 40% spoilage reduction) is the consistent factor across all 12 existing installations. In the restaurant industry, operational budgets are tightly controlled, and decisions that directly improve the bottom line are easier to justify and replicate than those relying on speculative brand lift.

The "marketing gimmick" path relies on a variable outcome: the ability of a specific restaurant to successfully monetize "table-to-fork" storytelling. This varies wildly based on the restaurant's concept, location, and existing brand equity. A high-end bistro might leverage the visual story effectively, but a high-volume casual spot may see no return on the "photo opportunity," making the purchase hard to justify solely on image. Furthermore, marketing budgets are often the first to be cut during economic downturns, whereas food cost reduction is a perpetual priority.

By positioning GreenSpire as a supply-chain tool, you align the purchase decision with the Head Chef or Operations Director, who feels the pain of spoilage and fluctuating herb costs daily. The alternative they are replacing is not just "local farm delivery," but the financial leakage of waste and the unpredictability of supply. The 40% spoilage reduction is a hard metric that pays for the unit regardless of whether a single diner takes a photo. This creates a universal value proposition that applies to your next 88 targets regardless of their marketing sophistication.

To scale, you must lead with the guaranteed ROI. The "story" becomes a powerful secondary benefit—a "cherry on top"—that helps close the deal, but it should not be the foundation of the wedge. If you lead with the story, you force every prospect to believe they are a marketing genius. If you lead with the margin, you only require them to believe in math.
