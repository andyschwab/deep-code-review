**Recommendation: Lead with "Staff Time-Saver to Prevent Burnout"**

While the $4 million in secured funding is a powerful validation metric, positioning GrantFlow primarily as a "fundraising multiplier" risks misaligning with the immediate emotional reality of your buyer: the overworked development director. In the non-profit arts sector, the barrier to entry is rarely a lack of ambition to win money; it is a lack of capacity to write the applications.

Your data shows a 90% reduction in drafting time (20 hours to 2 hours). This is not just an efficiency gain; it is a survival mechanism for small teams. If you position as a "money maker," you invite skepticism about the quality of the grants won and may sound transactional to mission-driven leaders. If you position as a "time saver," you address the acute pain of burnout, positioning GrantFlow as the tool that allows them to keep their jobs and actually do the artistic work they love. The money won becomes the *proof* that the time saved was well-spent, rather than the sole promise.

**Draft Headline:**
"Stop choosing between your mission and your sanity: The grant-writing assistant that cuts application time from 20 hours to 2, so your team can stop burning out and start creating."

**Note:** Treat the "$4 million" figure as a trust signal in your sub-head or case studies, not the primary hook.
**Note:** Validate this hypothesis by A/B testing this headline against a "multiplier" variant on your waitlist landing page before full rollout.
