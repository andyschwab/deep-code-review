**Primary Segment:** Parents of pediatric patients (ages 4–12) newly fitted with prosthetics who are actively seeking ways to normalize their child's appearance and encourage daily device usage.

**Lead Message:** "The only prosthetic cover designed to be worn every day, turning medical necessity into a source of pride."

**Positioning Statement (Hypothesis to Validate):**
For **parents of young prosthetic users** who **struggle to get their children to wear bulky, stigmatizing medical devices**, **LittleSteps** is a **custom pediatric prosthetic cover** that **transforms the device into a beloved accessory kids actually want to wear**; unlike **standard beige sleeves (MedCover) or basic plastic options (ProsthoCare)** which prioritize long-term material durability but suffer from low daily adherence, **LittleSteps** delivers **90% daily wear rates through personalized, character-themed designs**, accepting an **18-month replacement cycle** as the necessary trade-off for consistent therapeutic use and emotional confidence.

**Why this choice:**
You are not selling "durability" (you lose 5 years vs. 18 months) nor purely "emotion" (which risks medical credibility). You are selling **adherence**. The data shows your product succeeds where others fail: kids actually wear them. Doctors and insurance adjusters care about therapeutic outcomes; a device left in the closet has zero clinical value regardless of its 5-year lifespan. By framing the shorter lifespan as a deliberate feature of a "growth-friendly" system for changing kids rather than a defect, you resolve the tension. The "boring" alternative is durable but unused; your solution is used daily, driving the actual medical benefit.

**Addressing the alternatives:**
*   **Vs. MedCover/ProsthoCare:** The buyer gives up the promise of a 5-year static shell to gain a device their child accepts psychologically. The winning line is: "Durability doesn't matter if it stays in the drawer."
*   **Vs. Pure Emotional Play:** We avoid losing medical credibility by anchoring the "fun" in the hard metric of **90% daily wear**, proving to doctors that the design directly impacts compliance.

**Riskiest Assumption & Test:**
*Assumption:* Insurance adjusters and doctors will accept a higher lifetime cost (replacing every 18 months vs. 5 years) in exchange for verified daily wear data.
*Test:* Present the 90% vs. 40% wear-rate data to three pediatric orthotists and ask if they would prescribe LittleSteps despite the replacement frequency, specifically probing if "daily wear" justifies the recurring cost in their clinical notes.

**Note:** This statement is a hypothesis to be validated with real buyers; do not assert the 90% figure as a universal market truth without broader clinical trials. Trademark clearance for specific character themes must be verified legally before commercial messaging.
