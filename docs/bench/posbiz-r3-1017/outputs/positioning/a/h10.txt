**Positioning Hypothesis: Small-Batch Custom Shops**

**For** small-batch custom motorcycle shops (5 bikes/week) **who** struggle to maintain margin while managing complex, non-standard builds, **MotoAR** is an assembly acceleration system **that** cuts build time by 37% (40h to 25h) and reduces rework errors by 90%. **Unlike** free printed factory manuals or fragmented YouTube tutorials, **it** provides a hands-free, context-aware overlay that guarantees first-time-right assembly, turning labor hours into profit.

**Why this segment over hobbyists:**
Your unit economics ($1,200 bundle) and value proof (time savings) align strictly with commercial ROI. A hobbyist pays for "feeling like a master"; a shop pays for 15 recovered billable hours per bike. At $1,200, the hobbyist purchase is a luxury impulse; for a shop turning five bikes a week, the payback period is roughly two builds. Targeting hobbyists dilutes your message with "passion" language that shops ignore, while targeting shops with "viral potential" confuses the buyer who cares only about throughput and error reduction. Viral marketing is a distribution tactic, not a positioning strategy; you cannot position a B2B efficiency tool as a B2C lifestyle product without breaking trust with the payer.

**Stress Test Results:**
*   **Alternative Clarity:** You correctly identified the alternative not as "competitors" but as "free status quo" (manuals/YouTube). The differentiator must explicitly state the *cost* of that free alternative: unbillable labor hours and costly material waste from errors.
*   **Proof Point:** The "90% error reduction" and "15-hour savings" are your only valid proof points. Do not invent market size or competitor names.
*   **Riskiest Assumption:** That shop owners perceive the $1,200 upfront cost as an operational expense rather than a capital hurdle.
*   **Validation Test:** Present the positioning to 10 shop owners. Ask: "Would you buy this tomorrow to solve your backlog, or is this a 'nice to have' for next year?" If they hesitate on the price without calculating the labor savings, the pricing model or payment terms are the blocker, not the positioning.
