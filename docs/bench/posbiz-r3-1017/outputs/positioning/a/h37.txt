**Core Message (Lead):** "Move from recording to publication-ready insight in 10 minutes, without sacrificing the nuance peer review demands."

**Supporting Pillar 1: Speed as a Strategic Advantage**
*   **Claim:** Eliminate the weeks-long bottleneck of human transcriptionists to meet aggressive grant deadlines.
*   **Proof Point:** 10-minute turnaround time validated by current usage with 50 academic teams.
*   **Differentiation:** Unlike generic tools (e.g., Otter.ai) that require heavy manual correction, our engine delivers immediate usability.

**Supporting Pillar 2: Fidelity as a Compliance Guarantee**
*   **Claim:** Preserve every accent and hesitation required for rigorous qualitative analysis and publication.
*   **Proof Point:** 98% verbatim accuracy rate, even with heavy accents.
*   **Differentiation:** Unlike human services that trade speed for accuracy, we deliver both simultaneously.

**Recommendation for Outreach:**
Address **Speed** as the primary hook, but immediately qualify it with **Accuracy**. Principal investigators are likely pressured by the *visibility* of grant deadlines (an immediate pain), making "10 minutes" the strongest attention-grabber. However, their *fear* is rejection due to poor data quality. Therefore, your email subject line and opening sentence should promise speed ("Get transcripts in 10 minutes"), while the second sentence must neutralize the risk ("with 98% verbatim accuracy suitable for publication"). Do not force a choice between the two; your unique market position is that you solve the traditional trade-off between the slow human transcriber and the fast but inaccurate generic AI.

**Note:** This hierarchy assumes the 98% accuracy metric holds true across the specific dialects of your target university departments; verify this with a sample test if possible.
