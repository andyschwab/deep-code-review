**Stress Test Results**

Your draft successfully identifies the specific alternative you replace (static libraries and freelance hiring) and names the target segment. However, it currently hedges between your two potential leads: "Copyright Safety" and "Creative Discovery." Based on the context you provided—specifically the recent "Daily Dive" takedown and the explicit user fear of lawsuits—leading with "Creative Discovery" is likely a secondary benefit, not the primary hook.

In a market where the consequence of failure is the deletion of a creator's entire show, "safety" is a threshold requirement that often outweighs "uniqueness" until that safety is guaranteed. Your positioning statement should lead with the relief of fear (the pain reliever) as the primary value, using "unique sounds" as the proof that they don't have to sacrifice quality for safety. If you lead with discovery, you compete with free music; if you lead with safety, you compete with the existential threat of their business failing.

**Recommendation:** Commit to "Copyright Safety" as the headline value proposition. Frame "Creative Discovery" as the mechanism that makes safety palatable (i.e., "You don't have to choose between being safe and sounding good"). Test this by running two landing page variants: one headlined "Never Face a Copyright Strike Again" and the other "Find Sounds No One Else Has." Given the "Daily Dive" incident, the fear-based headline will likely yield higher conversion among independent creators who lack legal teams.
