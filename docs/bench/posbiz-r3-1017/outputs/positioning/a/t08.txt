**Primary Narrative: Premium Brand Elevation**

For the skeptical artisan worried about margins, the primary narrative must be **Premium Brand Elevation**, not Environmental Responsibility.

**The Positioning Hypothesis**
*For [artisanal soap makers] who [need to justify higher price points to protect margins], EcoWrap is a [packaging solution] that [elevates product perception to luxury status]; unlike [standard plastic or recycled paper], it [delivers a tactile, high-end finish that customers associate with quality, allowing you to raise prices by more than the 15% material cost increase].*

**Why This Wins Over the "Green" Angle**
Your target buyer is not a non-profit; they are a small business owner where every cent counts. To a skeptic, "Environmental Responsibility" sounds like a cost center—a tax they must pay to be good citizens. If you lead with the 90-day decomposition, you are asking them to absorb a 15% cost increase for a value (saving the planet) that primarily benefits the end consumer, not the maker's bottom line. In this niche, "green" is increasingly a **hygiene factor**: customers expect it, but they rarely pay a *significant* premium for it alone.

Conversely, **Premium Brand Elevation** directly addresses the margin fear. Artisanal soap is an emotional, sensory purchase. The "look and feel" of the wrap is the first physical touchpoint the end-customer has with the brand. If EcoWrap feels luxurious compared to the crinkle of standard plastic or the roughness of recycled paper, it signals that the soap inside is superior. This allows the soap maker to increase their retail price by 20–30%, easily absorbing the 15% material cost and expanding their actual profit per unit.

You are not selling "decomposition"; you are selling **pricing power**. The environmental benefit becomes the "cherry on top"—a secondary validation that reassures the maker they aren't compromising their values—but the *closer* is the profit margin.

**How to Validate This**
Do not guess if "green" is a differentiator. Test it with your current 50 producers:
1.  Ask the 25 who claim "look and feel" drives repeats: "If a cheaper green option looked cheap, would you switch?" (Likely no).
2.  Ask the 25 who claim "customers pay extra for green": "Would you pay 15% more for green packaging if it looked worse than your current plastic?" (Likely no).

The data will likely show that aesthetics drive the willingness to pay, while sustainability merely prevents churn. Lead with the luxury angle to open the wallet; use the eco-stats to close the conscience.

*Note: This positioning assumes your film actually delivers a superior tactile experience compared to recycled paper; if the feel is identical, this strategy fails. Ensure your "luxury" claim is physically verifiable before printing it on the website.*
