**Primary Narrative: Premium Brand Elevation**

For the skeptical artisan worried about margins, the winning message is **Premium Brand Elevation**, not Environmental Responsibility.

**The Positioning Hypothesis**
*For artisanal soap makers who need to justify higher price points to protect thin margins, EcoWrap is a packaging solution that transforms product perception from "homemade" to "luxury boutique"; unlike recycled paper (which can look rustic) or standard plastic (which feels cheap), it delivers a high-end tactile experience that directly supports a 15% price premium, while the 90-day biodegradability serves as a risk-mitigating bonus rather than the primary sales driver.*

**Why This Converts the Skeptic**
Your core friction point is the **15% cost increase**. A skeptical artisan calculates this strictly as a margin erosion unless they see a direct path to revenue recovery.

1.  **Direct ROI vs. Abstract Value:** "Environmental responsibility" is a hygiene factor in your niche; many customers expect it, but few will pay a *significant* premium solely for it if the product looks cheap. If the wrap looks like standard eco-packaging, the artisan cannot raise their soap price. Conversely, "Premium Brand Elevation" directly addresses the margin fear. If the film makes the soap look luxurious, the artisan can raise their retail price by 20–30%, easily absorbing the 15% material cost increase and increasing their absolute profit per unit.
2.  **The "Look and Feel" Driver:** You noted that some makers cite "look and feel" as the driver for repeat purchases. In the luxury goods sector (which artisanal soap aspires to), tactile quality signals value. Recycled paper often signals "rustic" or "budget," limiting price ceiling. Standard plastic signals "mass-market." EcoWrap's specific advantage is bridging the gap: it offers the sustainability of paper with the clarity and sheen of high-end plastic.
3.  **Reframing the "Green" Label:** For this specific buyer, "green" is likely a table stake (a requirement to play), not the differentiator that closes the sale. If you lead with "eco-friendly," you compete on values, which are hard to quantify against a 15% cost hike. If you lead with "luxury finish that justifies higher pricing," you compete on *profit*, which is immediately quantifiable. The 90-day decomposition becomes the "guilt-free" closer that removes the objection to using plastic-like materials, rather than the primary hook.

**Recommendation for Messaging**
Lead your website and sales materials with the visual transformation and price-justification potential. Use headlines like *"The Finish That Justifies Your Price"* or *"Elevate Your Bar from Batch to Boutique."* Place the biodegradability claim as a secondary supporting pillar: *"Luxury you can feel, sustainability you can trust."* This structure assures the artisan that the investment protects their margin first, while aligning with their values second.

**Note:** This positioning assumes your film visually outperforms recycled paper in clarity and texture; if the aesthetic is merely "equal," the premium argument fails.
**Note:** Validate this by A/B testing landing pages: one focusing on "margin protection via luxury" vs. "customer alignment via green values" to measure click-through rates from your target segment.
