**Recommendation: Frame as "Invisible Preservation"**

Do not choose between "pure preservation" and "modern aid." Choosing "modern aid" alienates your purist core who fear losing the soul of the hobby. Choosing "pure preservation" without explaining *how* you achieve it ignores the reality that your tool is modern tech.

The winning strategy is **Invisible Preservation**. Position RetroFix not as a modernization tool, but as the ultimate guardian of originality. Your value is not the AI itself; it is the *prevention of non-original replacements*. By predicting failure, you allow owners to source correct vintage parts in advance rather than being forced to install modern substitutes during an emergency breakdown at a show. The technology is the means; total historical fidelity is the end. This allows you to scale by appealing to the deepest desire of both segments: keeping the car exactly as the manufacturer intended, for as long as possible.

**Positioning Statement (Hypothesis to Validate)**

For **vintage restoration shop owners and preservation purists** who **fear that unexpected breakdowns will force them to compromise a vehicle's historical authenticity with non-original parts**, RetroFix is a **predictive monitoring service** that **identifies impending part failures before they occur**. Unlike **routine manual inspections or reactive repairs after a breakdown**, it **provides the lead time necessary to source correct vintage components, ensuring the car remains 100% original without visible modern interference**.

*Note: This statement assumes that "lead time to source vintage parts" is the primary pain point driving the fear of breakdowns; validate this by asking current hiding-users if "avoiding modern substitute parts" is their main motivation for using the sensors.*
*Note: The 88% accuracy and 70% reduction stats are your internal proof points to support sales conversations, but the market message must focus on the outcome (authenticity), not the algorithm.*
