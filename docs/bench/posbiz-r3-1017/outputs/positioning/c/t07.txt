**Recommendation:** Lead with the **fundraising multiplier** angle.

While saving 18 hours per application is a massive operational win, non-profit arts organizations operate under a "survival constraint": they do not lack effort; they lack capital. Positioning as a "time-saver" risks sounding like a luxury efficiency tool when their primary existential threat is running out of money. By framing GrantFlow as a multiplier that helps small teams win bigger grants, you align directly with their most urgent job-to-be-done: securing the funding that keeps the lights on and the art alive. The time saved is the *mechanism* that allows them to apply to more grants or focus on donor relationships, but the *outcome* they buy is financial sustainability.

**Primary Headline:**
"Turn Your Small Team Into a Major Fundraising Powerhouse: Secure More Grants Without the Burnout."

**Why this wins over the alternative:**
1.  **Economic Reality:** An arts organization can survive with overworked staff for a while; it cannot survive without funding. The $4 million secured by your current users is your strongest proof point.
2.  **The "Luxury" Trap:** Framing time-saving as the primary benefit suggests you are selling "relief." Framing it as a multiplier suggests you are selling "growth." In a resource-scarce sector, growth tools get budget approval; relief tools get deferred.
3.  **Addressing the Fear:** You worried financial gain sounds transactional. However, for non-profits, revenue *is* mission enablement. The transaction isn't cold; it's the fuel for their art.

**How to handle the time-saving proof:**
Do not discard the time metric. Use it as the credible proof that makes the financial promise believable.
*   *Supporting Pillar:* "Reclaim 18 hours per application to focus on strategy and stewardship, not drafting."
*   *Differentiation:* Unlike freelance writers (expensive, slow turnover) or Google Docs templates (manual, error-prone), GrantFlow automates the heavy lifting so your existing team can punch above their weight class.

**Hypothesis to Validate:**
This positioning assumes that your waitlist's primary barrier to entry is the fear of *not winning enough money*, rather than the fear of *staff quitting*. To test this before a full launch, A/B test your waitlist landing page:
*   **Variant A (Multiplier):** "Secure 2x more funding with your current team."
*   **Variant B (Time-Saver):** "Cut grant writing time by 90% and prevent burnout."
Measure click-through rates and sign-up conversions. If Variant A wins, the market is motivated by revenue survival. If Variant B wins, the market is in a crisis of capacity. Given your data ($4M secured vs. hours saved), the revenue narrative is the stronger strategic bet.

*Note: Ensure your "proof points" (the $4M figure and 200 organizations) are prominently displayed near the headline to substantiate the multiplier claim immediately.*
*Note: Avoid naming specific freelance competitors or claiming "cheaper than X" without a direct cost comparison table provided by your users.*
