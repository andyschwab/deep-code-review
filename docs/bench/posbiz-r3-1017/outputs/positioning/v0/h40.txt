### Positioning Hypothesis: Stress Test Results

**Your Draft Approach:** Balancing "participant safety" (mission) and "liability reduction" (financial fear).

**The Failure Point for a Risk-Averse Director:**
The message fails because **balancing dilutes urgency**. A risk-averse director does not buy "safety"; they buy **certainty of outcome** and **transfer of risk**. When you lead with "care for runners," a liability-focused buyer hears "soft benefit" or "marketing fluff." When you try to speak to both simultaneously, you signal that you are not fully committed to solving their specific, terrifying problem: the lawsuit that ends their career.

Specifically, the message resonates poorly because:
1.  **"Safety" is vague; "Liability" is quantifiable.** Saying you "care for runners" is what every aid station claims. Saying you "reduce medical tent visits by 30%" is a metric, but linking it directly to *insurance premiums* or *legal exposure* is the hook you are missing.
2.  **The alternative comparison is weak.** If you position against "traditional monitoring," you are competing on *effort*. If you position against "the cost of a single lawsuit," you are competing on *survival*. A risk-averse director ignores tools that make them feel good; they buy tools that keep them employed.
3.  **The "20-minute warning" is framed as a feature, not a shield.** For a risk-averse buyer, this isn't about hydration; it's about **documented due diligence**. The message fails if it doesn't explicitly state that HydroPatch provides the *data trail* necessary to defend against negligence claims.

**Revised Positioning Hypothesis (To Validate):**
*For [risk-averse race directors] who [fear catastrophic liability incidents and rising insurance costs], HydroPatch is a [risk-mitigation infrastructure] that [provides documented, early-warning data to prevent medical emergencies]; unlike [reactive aid stations or extra staff], it [quantifiably reduces medical tent volume by 30% and creates an auditable safety record to defend against negligence claims].*

**Recommendation:**
Stop balancing. Segment your outreach. For the **risk-averse director**, drop the "care" language entirely in the headline. Lead with the **financial and legal shield**. Use the "care" angle only for the *mission-driven* organizer, but recognize that the "fear" buyer requires a message that sounds like an insurance policy, not a charity.
