**Choice: Position GreenSpire as a supply-chain solution driven by the head chef's need for margin stability and freshness control.**

While the "menu marketing gimmick" angle generates excitement among marketing teams, the "supply-chain solution" offers the stronger, more scalable wedge for growing from 12 to 100 locations. Your current evidence indicates that the actual purchasing drivers in your installed base are economic: clients report saving ~$3,000 annually and reducing spoilage by 40%. These are hard, quantifiable metrics that directly impact the P&L, whereas "photo opportunities" are soft benefits that rarely justify capital expenditure on their own.

In the restaurant industry, the head chef or operations manager typically controls the budget for kitchen equipment and ingredient sourcing. They are evaluated on food cost percentages and waste reduction. A value proposition centered on stabilizing costs and ensuring freshness speaks directly to their primary jobs-to-be-done. Conversely, positioning the product primarily as a marketing tool risks relegating it to a discretionary expense that must compete with advertising budgets, making the sale harder to close and more vulnerable to economic downturns when marketing spend is cut first.

Furthermore, the "supply-chain" narrative is more defensible and scalable. Every restaurant, regardless of its brand prestige, struggles with herb spoilage and volatile supply costs. This is a universal pain point. The "table-to-fork" story, while compelling, is situational; it only resonates with concepts where brand image is the primary differentiator. By leading with the economic wedge, you secure the purchase based on ROI. Once installed, the marketing benefits (the "gimmick") become a realized bonus that the owner can leverage, effectively satisfying both stakeholders without compromising the core sales argument.

To scale to 100 locations, you must target the decision-maker who feels the pain of waste most acutely. That is the chef managing margins, not the owner seeking press coverage. Lead with the $3,000 savings and 40% spoilage reduction as your primary differentiator against traditional deliveries and refrigeration. Use the visual appeal as a secondary closer, not the primary hook.
