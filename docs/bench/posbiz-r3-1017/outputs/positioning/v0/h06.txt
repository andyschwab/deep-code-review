### Positioning Hypothesis to Validate

**Primary Segment:** Airport Facility Directors at major international hubs facing verified, unfillable shifts for night cleaning crews (trigger: >15% vacancy rate in janitorial staff for 3+ months).

**Positioning Statement:**
For [Airport Facility Directors] who [cannot staff sufficient night-shift cleaning crews due to labor shortages], CleanPath is an [autonomous floor scrubbing fleet] that [guarantees terminal coverage without adding headcount]; unlike [traditional ride-on scrubbers or manual crews], it [operates continuously with zero labor dependency, converting a fixed $150k annual wage burden into a one-time $65k capital asset].

**Strategic Recommendation on the "Minefield":**
Lead with the **Labor Shortage** narrative, but frame the robot as a "force multiplier" for existing staff rather than a replacement.
*   **Why Labor over Hygiene?** Hygiene (99% vs 85%) is a "nice-to-have" upgrade; inability to clean the floor at all due to lack of staff is an existential operational failure. Buyers pay to solve existential threats first.
*   **Navigating Unions:** Do not position as "replacing humans." Position as "covering the shifts you cannot hire for." The alternative to CleanPath is not "happy union workers"; it is "dirty terminals and cancelled contracts" because the humans simply do not exist to do the work. This aligns the robot with the union's goal of maintaining service levels despite membership gaps.
*   **The Proof Point Needed:** You must validate the "zero labor dependency" claim. If your units still require a human to monitor them, the "zero" claim is false. If they require *less* labor, quantify the reduction (e.g., "one supervisor manages 5 robots").

**Key Assumptions & Risks to Test:**
*   **[ASSUMED]:** The primary buyer pain is strictly labor availability, not budget capex vs. opex constraints.
*   **[ASSUMED]:** Unions will accept "filling gaps" as a valid use case rather than viewing any automation as a threat.
*   **Riskiest Assumption:** That facility managers perceive the $65k upfront cost as easier to approve than the ongoing $150k wage line item, given airport procurement cycles.
*   **Test:** Conduct 5 sales conversations explicitly asking: "If you had full staffing tomorrow, would you still buy this for the hygiene benefit?" If the answer is "no," your hygiene angle is secondary. Ask union reps: "Would you prefer we cover unfilled shifts with robots or leave terminals partially unclean?"
