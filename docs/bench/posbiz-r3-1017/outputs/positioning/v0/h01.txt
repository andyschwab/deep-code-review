### Positioning Hypothesis to Validate

**Primary Segment:** Parents of pediatric patients (ages 5–12) newly fitted with prosthetics, specifically those whose children have rejected standard devices due to stigma or discomfort.

**Positioning Statement:**
For **parents of children newly fitted with prosthetics** who **struggle with their child refusing to wear bulky, stigmatizing medical devices**, **LittleSteps** is a **custom pediatric prosthetic cover category** that **turns medical necessity into a source of confidence and daily play**. Unlike **standard beige sleeves (e.g., MedCover) or basic plastic options (e.g., ProsthoCare)** that prioritize long-term material durability but suffer from low adherence, **LittleSteps delivers character-themed customization proven to drive 90% daily wear rates**, accepting a **18-month replacement cycle as the cost of ensuring the device is actually used during critical developmental years**.

**Key Trade-off Acknowledged:**
This positioning explicitly frames the shorter lifespan (18 months vs. 5 years) not as a defect, but as the necessary trade-off for the primary benefit: **adherence**. A $350 cover worn daily provides more value than a $120 cover stored in a closet.

**Message House Core Pillars:**
1.  **Emotional Empowerment (Primary):** "From medical burden to favorite accessory." (Proof: 90% daily wear rate vs. 40% industry average).
2.  **Clinical Validity (Secondary):** "Doctors recommend what gets worn." (Proof: Adoption across three Ohio clinics; addresses doctor credibility by linking fun to therapeutic compliance).
3.  **Value Reframe:** "Cost per wear, not cost per unit." (Proof: High abandonment rates of cheaper alternatives render their lower upfront price and longer lifespan irrelevant if the child refuses to wear them).

**Riskiest Assumption to Test:**
That insurance adjusters and doctors will accept the "adherence over durability" argument enough to approve the higher unit cost ($350) despite the frequent replacement need.
*Test Method:* Present the wear-rate data (90% vs 40%) to 5 insurance case managers and ask if they would authorize a replacement every 18 months given the alternative is 60% non-compliance.
