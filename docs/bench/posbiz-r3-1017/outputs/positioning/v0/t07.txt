**Recommendation: Lead with "Fundraising Multiplier" (Financial Gain)**

While time savings are emotionally resonant, positioning GrantFlow primarily as a "staff time-saver" risks framing your product as a luxury or an efficiency tool in a sector where survival is the priority. Non-profits in the arts do not buy software to save hours; they buy it to secure the funding that keeps their doors open. Time saved is a secondary benefit; money won is the primary job-to-be-done.

Your data supports this: $4 million secured is a tangible, high-stakes outcome that directly correlates to organizational survival and mission expansion. "Preventing burnout" is a strong emotional hook, but it is often a budget line item that gets cut when funds are tight. "Winning bigger grants" is a revenue generator that pays for itself. In a resource-constrained environment, the argument that "this tool makes you more money" almost always outperforms "this tool makes your life easier," because the former solves the root cause of the latter (lack of resources).

**Draft Headline:**
"Turn Your Small Team Into a Major Grant Winner: The Automated Assistant That Helped Arts Non-Profits Secure $4M."

**Supporting Sub-headline (to capture the time benefit without leading with it):**
"Reduce application drafting from 20 hours to 2, so you can focus on the art while we help you fund it."

**Next Step for Validation:**
Split-test these two angles on your waitlist landing page. Measure click-through rates and sign-up conversions for the "Money Won" headline versus the "Time Saved" headline. Do not rely on mixed anecdotal feedback; let the market's wallet vote on which pain point is most acute right now.

*Note: This recommendation treats your $4M and time-saving metrics as verified inputs provided by you. Ensure your legal team clears any specific dollar claims in public advertising.*
