**Positioning Hypothesis: Environmental Responsibility as the Primary Driver**

For artisanal soap makers who struggle to justify premium pricing to eco-conscious end-consumers, EcoWrap is a biodegradable packaging solution that validates their brand's sustainability claims; unlike standard plastic or generic recycled paper, it offers a verified 90-day decomposition cycle that directly supports a "green" price premium, turning packaging from a cost center into a margin-expanding asset.

**Why this narrative converts the skeptical artisan:**

You asked to choose between "premium brand elevation" (look/feel) and "environmental responsibility" (values). While aesthetics drive repeat purchases, **environmental responsibility is the stronger primary narrative for acquiring new, skeptical customers worried about margins.** Here is the reasoning based on your inputs:

1.  **The Margin Math:** Your material costs 15% more than traditional polyethylene. A "premium look" is subjective and easily mimicked by high-gloss plastics at a lower cost. However, "biodegradable in 90 days vs. 400 years" is an objective, defensible claim. In the artisanal soap niche, end-consumers are often willing to pay a specific surcharge for verified sustainability. By leading with the environmental angle, you equip the soap maker with the *reason* their end-customer will accept a higher price, directly offsetting your 15% cost increase and protecting the artisan's margin.
2.  **Hygiene Factor vs. Differentiator:** You questioned if "green" is a hygiene factor. In the *mass* market, it might be. But for *artisanal* soap makers, it is a core differentiator. Their entire value proposition often rests on being "natural," "small-batch," and "earth-friendly." Using standard plastic undermines their brand story. Therefore, the "green" label is not just a hygiene factor; it is the **license to operate** in their specific segment. If the packaging contradicts their brand values, the "premium look" becomes irrelevant because the product feels inauthentic.
3.  **Addressing the Skeptic:** A skeptical artisan worries about cost. If you sell "luxury," they compare you to expensive shiny plastics. If you sell "margin protection through brand alignment," you compare yourself to the *loss of sales* from inconsistent branding. The narrative shifts from "This wrap costs more" to "This wrap allows you to charge more because it proves your eco-claims."
