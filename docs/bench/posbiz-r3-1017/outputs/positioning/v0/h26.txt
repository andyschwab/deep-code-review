### Positioning Hypothesis (To Validate)

**For** zero-waste grocery operations directors who struggle with perishable inventory margins and food waste costs,
**ShelfSmart** is a dynamic pricing infrastructure that automatically optimizes revenue for aging stock;
**Unlike** static paper tags or basic LCD labels (e.g., PriceTag Inc.) that only display fixed information,
**It** increases sales of near-expiry items by dynamically adjusting prices based on real-time inventory age.

**Key Differentiator & Proof Point:**
*   **Capability:** Real-time price elasticity based on product shelf-life.
*   **Proof:** Pilot locations achieved a **40% increase in sales of near-expiry items** and a **25% reduction in food waste**.
*   **Cost of Switching:** Managers must shift from manual markdown routines to an automated, data-driven pricing workflow.

**Secondary Message (Sustainability Education):**
*   **Hypothesis:** The carbon footprint score is a *supporting feature* that reinforces the brand mission but should not obscure the primary price signal required for inventory movement.
*   **Status:** `UNVERIFIED` — Needs A/B testing to confirm if displaying carbon scores alongside dynamic prices dilutes the urgency of the price drop.

**Target Segment Recommendation:**
*   **Primary Buyer:** Operations Director (focused on margins/waste reduction).
*   **Rationale:** The purchase decision is likely driven by hard ROI (revenue recovery and waste cost avoidance) rather than brand mission alone. The "education" aspect serves as a tie-breaker for mission-aligned stores but is rarely the sole budget driver.

**Riskiest Assumption to Test:**
*   That store managers will prioritize automated dynamic pricing over static sustainability messaging when the two compete for screen space.
*   **Test:** Run a split test in pilot stores: Group A sees price-only dynamic tags; Group B sees price + carbon score. Measure the sell-through rate of near-expiry items in both groups.
