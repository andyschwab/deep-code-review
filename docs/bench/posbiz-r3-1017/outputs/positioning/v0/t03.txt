**Recommended Primary Messaging Hierarchy: Productivity-Ergonomics**

Prioritize the **productivity-ergonomics** angle for your landing page headlines and ad copy. While health is a powerful personal motivator, the "productivity" narrative uniquely bridges your split audience: it validates the individual engineer's desire for comfort (as a means to focus) while directly addressing the manager's goal of performance. This positioning avoids the "wellness trap" of being perceived as a nice-to-have perk, instead framing StandTall as essential infrastructure for remote engineering teams. By anchoring the value proposition in work output, you unlock enterprise sales without alienating individual buyers who ultimately want to work without pain.

**Supporting Pillars:**

*   **Quantifiable Performance Gain:** Unlike generic wellness apps that offer abstract relaxation, StandTall delivers measurable workflow improvements. Leverage your internal data to claim that users achieve **60% fewer posture-related interruptions**, directly translating to deeper focus states and more sustained coding hours per day.
*   **The Ergonomic Upgrade for Remote Work:** Position the device not as a medical supplement, but as the missing layer in the modern remote stack. Just as engineers demand high-performance hardware, they require physical optimization to maintain peak cognitive load; StandTall is the active counterpart to static investments like Herman Miller chairs, ensuring the body supports the mind throughout the sprint.
*   **Manager-Validated ROI:** Shift the purchase justification from "feeling better" to "performing better." This narrative equips engineering leaders with a concrete tool to reduce burnout-related downtime and improve team velocity, making the device an defensible line-item for enterprise budgets rather than an individual expense report.

Note: Your 60% slouching reduction and 25% pain relief stats are strong proof points but must be framed as drivers of focus, not just health outcomes, to maintain this narrative. Do not mix the "wellness" language into these specific headlines; keep the benefit strictly tied to work capacity.
