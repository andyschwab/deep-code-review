### Break-Even and Deal Analysis for SecureNest

**Current Unit Economics (Standard Price)**
*   **Selling Price:** $600
*   **Variable Costs:** $400 ($200 Hardware + $150 Labor + $50 Overhead/Warranty)
*   **Contribution Margin:** $200 per unit (33.3% margin)
*   **Total Deal Value (500 units):** $300,000
*   **Total Contribution:** $100,000

**Proposed Unit Economics (20% Discount)**
*   **New Selling Price:** $480 ($600 - 20%)
*   **Variable Costs:** $400 (Unchanged)
*   **New Contribution Margin:** $80 per unit (16.7% margin)
*   **Total Deal Value (500 units):** $240,000
*   **Total Contribution:** $40,000

**Break-Even Analysis**
Your cash break-even point (covering only direct hardware, labor, and support costs) is **$400 per unit**.
*   At the proposed price of $480, you are **above** the cash break-even by $80 per unit.
*   However, this $80 margin must cover all corporate overhead (rent, salaries, R&D, sales commissions) and profit. If your fully loaded cost per unit (including allocated corporate overhead) exceeds $480, this deal generates a net loss.
*   Given your history of net losses on similar discounted contracts, your fully loaded overhead allocation likely exceeds $80 per unit.

**Financial Verdict**
**Do not accept the deal as currently structured.** While technically cash-flow positive ($80/unit), the margin erosion is severe (60% reduction in contribution). You are taking on significant long-term liability (warranty support) and operational complexity for a thin sliver of profit that likely disappears once corporate overhead is applied. This replicates the conditions that caused losses last year.

**Restructuring Options to Maintain Profitability**
To secure the contract while protecting margins, propose one of the following counter-offers:

1.  **Tiered Service Reduction (Recommended):**
    Maintain the $480 price point but reduce the variable costs to restore the $200 margin.
    *   *Action:* Remove the "premium" warranty support ($50 value) and shift to a standard 1-year parts-only warranty. Offer labor at a billable hourly rate rather than included installation.
    *   *Result:* Lowers your cost basis to ~$300/unit, restoring a healthy margin even at the discounted price.

2.  **Volume-Based Discounting:**
    Refuse the flat 20% discount. Offer a tiered structure:
    *   First 200 units at $570 (5% discount).
    *   Next 300 units at $540 (10% discount).
    *   *Result:* Blended price ~$552, preserving significantly more margin than the flat $480.

3.  **Subscription Offset:**
    Accept the $480 hardware price only if the HOA signs a mandatory 3-year monitoring/service subscription at a premium rate.
    *   *Result:* Recoups the $120/unit hardware discount through recurring revenue (LTV), shifting the profit center from hardware to services.

**Conclusion**
Walking away is a viable option if the HOA refuses to adjust scope. A deal that barely covers variable costs consumes resources that could be deployed to higher-margin customers. If you must win this reference account, insist on **Option 1**: lower the price only if you lower the service level and cost basis proportionally.

*Note: Ensure any warranty reductions are clearly legally defined to prevent future liability creep. Calculate your specific corporate overhead allocation per unit to confirm the exact loss threshold before final negotiations.*
