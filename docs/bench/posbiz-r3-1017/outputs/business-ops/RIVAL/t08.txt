### Revenue Comparison & Break-Even Analysis

**Assumptions:**
*   **Capacity per week:** 5 headsets × 40 classes × 8 attendees = 1,600 potential slots.
*   **Revenue per slot:** $25.
*   **Total Potential Weekly Revenue:** $40,000.
*   **Monthly Multiplier:** ~4.33 weeks.

#### 1. Projected Monthly Revenue per Studio

| Utilization Rate | Active Slots/Mo | Studio Gross Revenue | **Flat Fee Model** ($150/headset) | **Revenue Share Model** (12%) |
| :--- | :--- | :--- | :--- | :--- |
| **70%** | 4,988 | $124,700 | **$750** | **$14,964** |
| **90%** | 6,396 | $159,900 | **$750** | **$19,188** |

*Note: The Flat Fee yields $750/month regardless of usage. The Revenue Share scales directly with attendance.*

#### 2. Break-Even Utilization Rate
To find where the Revenue Share equals the Flat Fee ($750):
*   Target Monthly Share Revenue: $750
*   Required Studio Gross Revenue: $750 / 0.12 = $6,250
*   Required Weekly Gross Revenue: $6,250 / 4.33 = ~$1,443
*   Required Weekly Slots: $1,443 / $25 = ~58 slots
*   **Break-Even Utilization:** 58 slots / 1,600 total capacity = **3.6%**

**Conclusion:** The Revenue Share model becomes more profitable than the Flat Fee at a mere **3.6% utilization rate**. Given your scenarios of 70% and 90%, the **Revenue Share model is vastly superior** for FitVerse VR, generating ~20x more revenue per studio than the flat fee.

#### 3. Operational Break-Even
To cover your $60,000 monthly operational expenses:
*   **Flat Fee Model:** You need **80 studios** (80 × $750 = $60k).
*   **Revenue Share Model (at 70% util):** You need only **4 studios** (4 × $14,964 = $59,856).

#### 4. Critical Data to Request from Pilot Studios
Your projections assume every attendee pays $25 specifically for a class using your headset. To validate this, request:
1.  **Attribution Rate:** What percentage of attendees are actually using the VR headsets versus traditional equipment during those 40 classes? (If only 20% use VR, your revenue share drops proportionally).
2.  **Pricing Structure Verification:** Is the $25 fee a general gym membership, a specific class add-on, or an unlimited pass? If it's an unlimited pass, you cannot claim 12% of the $25 without a defined allocation method.
3.  **Seasonality Trends:** Weekly attendance fluctuations over the last 6 months to stress-test the "average" against low-season dips.

The flat fee model leaves significant value on the table given the high price point of your hardware and the potential volume of classes. Unless studios refuse to share revenue data, the 12% share is the clear financial winner.

*Ensure contracts define "bookings generated" clearly to avoid disputes over general membership fees vs. specific class revenue.*
