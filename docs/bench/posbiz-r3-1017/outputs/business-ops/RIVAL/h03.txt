**Accounting Treatment Guidance for StarLeap Deferred Revenue**

Based on the facts provided, the $5,000,000 in non-refundable deposits must be classified entirely as **Deferred Revenue (a liability)** on your balance sheet. Under current accounting standards (such as ASC 606 in the US or IFRS 15 globally), revenue can only be recognized when the performance obligation is satisfied. Since the suborbital flight has not occurred and the service has not been delivered, you have not earned this revenue, regardless of the non-refundable nature of the deposits or the cash receipt.

**Classification and Balance Sheet Presentation**
The full $5,000,000 should remain in the liability section. Given the two-year delay, you must assess the split between current and non-current liabilities:
*   **Current Liability:** Only the portion expected to be recognized as revenue within the next 12 months. Given the two-year pushback, this amount is likely zero unless a flight is confidently scheduled within the coming year.
*   **Non-Current Liability:** The vast majority, if not all, of the $5,000,000 should be classified as a non-current liability, reflecting that the service delivery is expected beyond the next operating cycle (12 months).

Do not recognize any of the deposit as revenue to offset your $200,000 monthly operational costs. Recognizing revenue before the flight violates the matching principle and overstates your financial health, creating significant audit and legal risks. Your monthly operational costs ($200,000) must be recorded as expenses on the Income Statement as incurred, which will result in a net loss on your P&L until flights commence. This accurately reflects your current burn rate against zero earned revenue.

**Risk Assessment and Refund Liability**
Although the deposits are "non-refundable," a two-year delay introduces significant legal and reputational risk that could force refunds or lead to litigation. You must evaluate whether a **Refund Liability** needs to be established. If there is a probable chance that customers will demand refunds due to the excessive delay or if contractual terms allow for cancellation under force majeure or excessive delay clauses, you may need to record a liability for potential refunds, reducing the deferred revenue balance accordingly. If the risk of having to return funds is deemed probable and estimable, this must be accrued.

**Required Disclosures**
To remain compliant and transparent with investors, your financial statement footnotes must include:
1.  **Nature of Deferred Revenue:** Explicitly state that the $5,000,000 represents customer deposits for future suborbital flights and explain the revenue recognition policy (revenue recognized only upon flight completion).
2.  **Delay Explanation:** Disclose the technical delays, the revised timeline for the first commercial flight, and the uncertainty associated with the new schedule.
3.  **Risk Factors:** Detail the risks regarding the non-refundable status in light of the delay, including any potential for customer litigation or regulatory scrutiny that could impact the obligation to perform or refund.
4.  **Going Concern Consideration:** If your cash reserves (excluding these restricted deposits) are insufficient to cover the $200,000 monthly burn rate for the foreseeable future without additional financing, a "Going Concern" disclosure may be required to warn investors of potential liquidity issues.

**Summary of Action**
Keep the $5M as a Non-Current Liability. Expense the $200k/month as incurred. Do not recognize revenue until the flight happens. Draft extensive footnotes explaining the delay and associated risks.
