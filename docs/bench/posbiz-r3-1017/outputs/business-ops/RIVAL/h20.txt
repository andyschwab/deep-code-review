### Strategic Analysis: Price Increase vs. Formulation Change

**Recommendation:** Do **not** reduce the dosage by 20%. In the nutrition and health sector, altering the product formulation (shrinkflation) is significantly more damaging to trust and retention than a transparent price increase. Customers subscribe to NutriFit for specific health outcomes; reducing the active ingredient directly threatens the perceived efficacy of the product. If customers feel the product no longer works, churn will spike regardless of the price, destroying Lifetime Value (LTV).

**Option 1: Increase Price to $70/month (+16.7%)**
*   **Financial Impact:** This fully absorbs the 25% COGS increase and restores your margin per unit.
*   **Retention Risk:** With a current 5% monthly churn, a 16.7% price hike in a high-inflation environment may temporarily increase churn to 7–8% as price-sensitive customers leave. However, health supplements often have inelastic demand if results are visible.
*   **LTV Outcome:** Even with slightly higher churn, the increased Average Revenue Per User (ARPU) usually results in a higher overall LTV compared to degrading the product. You retain the "value promise."

**Option 2: Reduce Dosage by 20% (Maintain $60 price)**
*   **Financial Impact:** This maintains the current margin percentage but reduces the total value delivered.
*   **Retention Risk:** High. Once customers realize the efficacy has dropped (e.g., less energy, slower results), voluntary churn could exceed 15–20%. In the age of social media and reviews, "they changed the formula" is a common trigger for mass cancellations and brand reputation damage.
*   **LTV Outcome:** Likely negative. The reduction in subscription lifespan due to perceived ineffectiveness will outweigh the savings from maintaining the price point.

**Implementation Strategy**
If you choose the price increase, do not apply it immediately to all existing subscribers without notice. Consider a "grandfathering" period: announce the new price ($70) for new customers immediately, and give existing subscribers 60 days to lock in their current rate by switching to an annual plan. This generates immediate cash flow and reduces future churn.

### Australian Consumer Law & Legal Requirements

**Notification and Formulation Changes**
Under the *Australian Consumer Law (ACL)*, you must not engage in misleading or deceptive conduct.
1.  **Formulation Changes:** If you reduce the dosage, you are changing the product description. If your marketing claims specific ingredient amounts or health benefits that rely on the original dosage, continuing to make those claims with a reduced product is likely misleading. You must update your labeling and marketing materials immediately to reflect the new composition.
2.  **Price Increases for Subscriptions:** For ongoing subscription services, unilateral price increases can be considered unfair contract terms if the customer cannot easily cancel. You generally cannot force a price hike on an existing fixed-term contract without their consent. For month-to-month subscribers, you can typically raise prices with proper notice, but the terms of your specific service agreement dictate the required notice period (often 30 days).

**Legal Professional Review**
**Yes, you absolutely need a legal professional to review your updated Terms of Service (ToS) before implementing either change.**
*   **Why:** The ACL has recently strengthened penalties for unfair contract terms. A lawyer must ensure your notification process for the price hike complies with your current contract terms to avoid breach of contract claims.
*   **Formulation Specifics:** If you choose the dosage reduction, a lawyer must verify that your new labeling and marketing copy do not violate consumer guarantees regarding fitness for purpose or matching descriptions.

**Note:** Communicating the "why" (rising ingredient costs) transparently to your Australian customer base often yields higher retention than silent changes.
