### Financial Break-Even Analysis

**Current Baseline (Per Location):**
*   **Gross Sales:** $45,000
*   **Food Cost (30%):** -$13,500
*   **Labor Savings:** +$8,000 (Value add, not revenue)
*   **Remaining for Rent/Overhead/Profit:** $31,500 (before fees)

**Model A: 6% Royalty on Gross Sales**
*   **Fee Calculation:** $45,000 × 0.06 = **$2,700/month**
*   **Franchisee Net (pre-other costs):** $28,800
*   **Franchisor Revenue (12 units):** $32,400/month

**Model B: Fixed $2,500 Tech Fee**
*   **Fee Calculation:** **$2,500/month**
*   **Franchisee Net (pre-other costs):** $29,000
*   **Franchisor Revenue (12 units):** $30,000/month

**The Break-Even Point for Franchisees**
To determine when the fixed fee becomes more burdensome than the royalty, we solve for Sales ($S$) where 6% of $S$ equals $2,500.
*   $0.06S = 2,500$
*   $S = 41,667$

**Analysis:**
*   **Above $41,667/mo:** The **6% Royalty** is more expensive for the franchisee. At current sales ($45k), the royalty costs them $200 *more* per month than the fixed fee.
*   **Below $41,667/mo:** The **Fixed Fee** is more expensive. If sales drop to $35,000 during a downturn, the royalty drops to $2,100, while the fixed fee remains $2,500, increasing the franchisee's burden by $400 exactly when they can least afford it.

### Recommendation: Hybrid "Floor-and-Cap" Structure

Adopting a pure fixed fee exposes you to significant legal risk regarding "excessive fees" during downturns, as the fee consumes a growing percentage of shrinking revenue. Conversely, a pure royalty caps your upside and reduces cash flow predictability.

**Recommended Structure:**
Implement a **minimum monthly fee of $2,500 OR 6% of gross sales, whichever is higher.**

1.  **Cash Flow Stability:** This guarantees your $30,000/month baseline (covering your 6-person support team) while allowing revenue to scale if franchisees exceed current performance.
2.  **Franchisee Solvency:** It aligns with the status quo at current sales levels. However, to truly protect retention during downturns, you should add a **hardship clause**: if a franchisee's sales drop below $35,000 for two consecutive months, the fee temporarily reverts to strictly 6% until recovery.
3.  **Legal Defensibility:** This structure is highly defensible. The fixed portion is explicitly tied to "technology maintenance and updates" (a tangible cost), while the variable portion captures value share. The hardship clause demonstrates good faith and reduces the likelihood of successful lawsuits claiming the fee structure forces insolvency during economic contractions.

This approach secures your operational costs immediately while preserving the partnership dynamic necessary for long-term unit growth.

*Note: Current fixed fee ($2,500) yields slightly less revenue than current royalty ($2,700) at today's sales volume.*
*Ensure the "hardship clause" is clearly defined in the franchise agreement to avoid ambiguity.*
