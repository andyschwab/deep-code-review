### Unit Economics Breakdown: CleanBot Autonomous vs. Human Labor

**1. Annual Human Labor Cost (Per Stadium)**
*   **Workforce:** 40 workers/shift. Assuming 3 shifts/day for 365 days = 120 worker-days daily coverage. To cover this continuously, you need ~4.5 workers per shift position including time off, or simply calculate total hours: 40 workers × 24 hours × 365 days = 350,400 labor hours/year.
*   **Hourly Rate:** $18/hour.
*   **Total Annual Labor Cost:** 350,400 hours × $18 = **$6,307,200**.
*   **Cost Per Square Foot (Human):** $6,307,200 / 1,500,000 sq ft = **$4.20/sq ft/year**.

**2. Annual Robot Operational Cost (Per Stadium)**
*   **Fleet Size:** 10 robots.
*   **Hardware CapEx:** 10 × $35,000 = $350,000 (One-time).
*   **Recurring OpEx:** $2,000/month/robot × 10 robots × 12 months = **$240,000/year**.
*   **Cost Per Square Foot (Robot OpEx Only):** $240,000 / 1,500,000 sq ft = **$0.16/sq ft/year**.

**3. Total Cost Comparison & Crossover Point**
To compare accurately, we must amortize the robot CapEx. Assuming a 5-year useful life for the hardware:
*   **Annualized CapEx:** $350,000 / 5 years = $70,000/year.
*   **Total Annual Robot Cost:** $240,000 (OpEx) + $70,000 (CapEx) = **$310,000**.
*   **Total Cost Per Square Foot (Robot):** $310,000 / 1,500,000 sq ft = **$0.21/sq ft/year**.

**Result:** Your robot model costs **$0.21/sq ft** vs. **$4.20/sq ft** for humans. This represents a **95% cost reduction**, far exceeding your 40% target.

**Crossover Point (Payback Period):**
*   **Annual Savings:** $6,307,200 (Human) - $310,000 (Robot Total) = $5,997,200.
*   **Initial Investment:** $350,000.
*   **Payback Time:** $350,000 / ($5,997,200 / 12 months) ≈ **0.7 months (less than 3 weeks)**.
*   *Note: Even if you include 6 months of supervision staff at $50k/year, the payback remains under 2 months.*

**4. Critical Variables to Include in Your Model**
To ensure investor confidence, explicitly model these "hidden" costs that erode the theoretical margin:
*   **Supervision Staff:** Robots are not fully autonomous in complex stadium environments. Include 1–2 floor managers per shift ($50k–$80k/year each) to handle exceptions, stuck units, or public interaction.
*   **Maintenance Downtime:** Assume 10–15% downtime for repairs. If a robot is down, do you need a human backup crew on standby? If yes, cost that partial labor line item.
*   **Battery Replacement Cycles:** Industrial batteries typically last 3–4 years. Factor in a replacement cost of ~$3,000 per unit in Year 4.
*   **Consumables & Wear:** Brushes, squeegees, and filters require monthly replacement. Estimate $200/month/robot.
*   **Insurance & Liability:** Autonomous machinery in public spaces may carry higher liability premiums than standard janitorial bonds.

**Revised Conservative Estimate:**
Even adding $150,000/year for supervision, backup labor, and extra maintenance, your total robot cost rises to ~$460,000/year ($0.31/sq ft). This still yields a **92% cost reduction** and a payback period under 1 month.

*Ensure you clarify whether the $18/hr human rate includes burden (taxes, insurance); if not, human costs are actually 20% higher, widening your advantage further.*
*The "crossover" is immediate; the real metric for investors is scalability and contract stickiness, not just unit cost.*
