### Strategic Pathway for NeuroLink Payer Engagement

**1. Immediate Evidence Requirements for a Single Payer Trial**
To secure coverage for a $15,000 per patient trial under existing Durable Medical Equipment (DME) codes or a temporary workaround, you must pivot from "device functionality" to "clinical utility and cost-offset." A single major insurer will not cover based on potential alone; they require:
*   **Comparative Cost-Benefit Analysis:** Data demonstrating that the $15,000 device cost is lower than the annual cost of current standard care (e.g., full-time caregiver hours, facility stays, or secondary complication treatments).
*   **Functional Outcome Metrics:** Validated scores (e.g., FIM or specific BCI task completion rates) showing the device enables a specific, billable improvement in Activities of Daily Living (ADLs) that reduces other medical expenditures.
*   **Safety Registry Data:** Even without full FDA widespread approval, you need a clean safety record from your controlled trials to satisfy the insurer's medical director regarding liability.

**2. Steps to Engage Payers Pre-Approval**
Do not wait for a new code assignment. Execute this sequence immediately:
*   **Target the Innovation Committee:** Bypass standard formulary reviews. Request a meeting with the "Innovation" or "Emerging Technology" committee of a regional Blue Cross Blue Shield plan (given your Massachusetts base). These groups have budgets for pilot programs that standard departments do not.
*   **Propose a "Coverage with Evidence Development" (CED) Model:** Frame the $15,000 trial not as a permanent price, but as a data-gathering exercise. Offer to provide real-time outcomes data in exchange for temporary coverage. This lowers the insurer's risk.
*   **Secure a Champion Physician:** Identify a key opinion leader (KOL) within the insurer's network who treats paralysis patients. Their clinical endorsement is often the prerequisite for the medical director to approve a non-standard code usage.

**3. Pricing Strategy: Selling at a Loss vs. Precedent Risk**
Offering the device at a loss (or significantly below your target commercial price) to secure the initial pilot is a **high-risk strategy** that requires strict contractual guardrails.
*   **The Risk:** Insurers anchor heavily on historical claims data. If you establish a $15,000 price point now, their algorithms will flag any future attempt to raise the price to $50,000+ as an unjustified spike, leading to automatic denials post-approval.
*   **The Mitigation:** Do not simply discount the invoice. Structure the pilot as a **research grant** or a **limited-term evaluation agreement**. The contract must explicitly state: *"The $15,000 unit price is a promotional rate valid only for the 12-month data collection period and does not establish a prevailing market rate for future commercial coverage."*
*   **Alternative Approach:** Instead of lowering the price, keep the price at your target commercial level but offer a **100% outcomes-based rebate**. If the patient does not achieve specific milestones, the insurer gets a full refund. This protects your price anchor while removing the financial risk for the payer.

**Conclusion**
Your nine-month cash flow necessity dictates pursuing the CED pilot model immediately. However, you must legally isolate this pilot pricing from your future commercial rate. Treat the initial revenue as subsidized market entry funded by the pilot's data value, not as a validation of a low permanent price point.
