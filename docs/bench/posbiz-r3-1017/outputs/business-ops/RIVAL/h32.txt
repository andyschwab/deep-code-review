**Specific Risks Regarding Re-identification and Aggregation**
Your assumption that "public records" equal "safe to sell" is legally flawed in the EU/UK context. Under GDPR, data remains "personal data" even if publicly available if it relates to an identified or identifiable natural person. The specific risk you face is **mosaic effect re-identification**. While a single court document or property register entry might be public, aggregating disparate sources allows for the construction of detailed profiles that reveal sensitive information (e.g., financial status, legal history, location patterns) not intended for public consumption in that combined format.

European regulators and courts (including the CJEU) have established that the *purpose* and *context* of processing matter. Selling aggregated datasets for LLM training is a fundamentally different purpose than the original public record's intent (transparency of legal proceedings or property ownership). This "purpose limitation" violation is a primary trigger for enforcement. There is no safe "threshold of record volume" that exempts you; in fact, processing 50 million records increases your scrutiny. If your aggregation allows a third party to infer sensitive attributes about an individual that were not obvious in the source documents, you are creating new personal data profiles, requiring a lawful basis for processing that "public availability" does not automatically provide.

**Data Broker Registration and Consent**
In the EU, there is no single "data broker" license, but your activity likely classifies you as a data controller or joint controller for the derived dataset. This imposes strict obligations:
1.  **Lawful Basis:** You likely cannot rely on "legitimate interest" for commercial resale of aggregated personal data, especially involving sensitive categories potentially inferable from court records. Explicit consent is often required for secondary commercial uses of public data, which you do not have.
2.  **Transparency:** You must be able to inform data subjects how their data is used. If you cannot identify the subjects to notify them due to the scale of aggregation, you are likely non-compliant.
3.  **UK Specifics:** The UK ICO has explicitly warned against scraping and repurposing public data without a valid lawful basis, treating such activities as high priority for investigation.
