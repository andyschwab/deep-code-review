### Cartridge Pricing Strategy & Break-Even Analysis

To recover the reader subsidy within 12 months while ensuring clinic adoption, you should price the disposable cartridge between **$18 and $22**.

**The Economics**
Your reader subsidy is **$5,300** ($8,500 sale price - $3,200 manufacturing cost). To recover this solely through cartridge margins in 12 months, we analyze a typical clinic performing 50 tests daily (18,250 tests/year).

However, you must account for the **15% annual churn rate**. On average, you will lose half of that year's volume to churn over the period, effectively reducing your realized volume for payback calculations to roughly **16,880 tests** in the first year.

**Break-Even Scenarios (12-Month Recovery)**
*   **At $15/cartridge:** Margin is $11. Annual contribution: ~$185,680. *Result: Subsidy recovered in <2 months. Risk: Leaving massive profit on the table; signals low quality.*
*   **At $18/cartridge:** Margin is $14. Annual contribution: ~$236,320. *Result: Subsidy recovered in ~2.5 months. Strong cash flow.*
*   **At $22/cartridge:** Margin is $18. Annual contribution: ~$303,840. *Result: Subsidy recovered in ~2 months. Maximizes unit economics.*

**Recommendation: Price at $19.50**
This price point recovers the $5,300 subsidy in approximately **75 days** (given churn-adjusted volume). This far exceeds your 12-month target, securing the positive cash flow needed by next quarter. The high margin ($15.50 per unit) creates a buffer against competitor undercutting while funding your $600k runway extension.

**Should you sell the reader at a loss?**
**No.** Your current model already subsidizes the reader significantly (selling at $8,500 vs. $3,200 cost is a gain, but if your "subsidy" logic implies the *market value* is higher than $8,500, you are already discounting). Do not drop the $8,500 price further. A lower upfront price attracts low-quality customers who may churn faster. The $8,500 price acts as a commitment filter; clinics paying this are serious about volume. Your profit engine is the cartridge, not the hardware.

**Communicating Value to Cost-Sensitive Clinics**
Clinic owners focus on *cost-per-diagnosis*, not hardware specs. Frame the narrative around **predictability and speed**:
1.  **The "All-In" Cost:** "At $19.50 per test, your total cost of ownership is predictable. No hidden maintenance fees."
2.  **Throughput Value:** "50 tests/day means you can serve 1,500 patients monthly. Even at $20/test, you generate $30,000 in billing revenue. Our device cost is less than 3% of your potential revenue."
3.  **Risk Reversal:** Offer a **volume rebate**. "If you commit to 15,000 cartridges in Year 1, we credit $500 toward your next reader upgrade." This locks in retention without lowering the sticker price.

**Strategic Note:** Your 12-month recovery goal is too conservative; aim for 3 months to protect against churn risk.
**Cash Flow Tip:** Require annual cartridge pre-purchase contracts for a 5% discount to immediate positive cash flow.
