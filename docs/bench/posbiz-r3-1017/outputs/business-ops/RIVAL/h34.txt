### Regulatory Pathway Trade-offs for ClearSight Lenses

Given your constraint of $1.8M cash and a $140k/month burn rate, you have approximately **12.8 months of runway**. A 24-month delay is fatal. You must prioritize speed to revenue over comprehensive clinical claims.

#### 1. 510(k) Clearance (Narrowed Indication)
**Strategy:** Target "glucose trending and pattern recognition" rather than "non-adjunctive dosing decisions."
*   **Timeline:** 6–10 months total (assuming a predicate device exists for optical glucose sensing or a similar physiological monitor). If no direct predicate exists, this path collapses into De Novo.
*   **Cost:** FDA user fee for small business is approx. **$5,400** (2024 rates). Total development/cleanup costs likely $200k–$400k.
*   **Claims Scope:** Limited. You can market the device as an aid to show glucose direction and trends. You **cannot** claim the device replaces fingersticks for insulin dosing without further data.
*   **Revenue Potential:** Immediate B2B sales to hospitals for monitoring in-patient trends; potential direct-to-consumer (DTC) subscription for data visibility, provided patients understand it is not for dosing.
*   **Risk:** High risk of FDA rejecting the "predicate" argument if optical sensing is deemed too novel compared to existing enzymatic sensors.

#### 2. De Novo Classification
**Strategy:** Acknowledge novelty, request new classification rules for optical glucose monitoring.
*   **Timeline:** 12–18+ months. The statutory clock is 150 days, but real-world interaction cycles often extend this beyond your runway.
*   **Cost:** FDA user fee for small business is approx. **$28,000**. Total costs (clinical data generation, regulatory consulting) often exceed $750k.
*   **Claims Scope:** Moderate to High. Allows you to set the precedent for the category. You might secure broader claims than a constrained 510(k), but likely still restricted from "dosing" without pivotal trials.
*   **Revenue Potential:** Delayed. Unlikely to generate significant revenue within your 12-month survival window.
*   **Risk:** Moderate regulatory risk, but **critical financial risk**. If the review drags to month 15, you run out of cash before approval.

#### Recommendation: The "Trending-First" 510(k)
To survive, you must attempt the **510(k) pathway with a narrowed indication**.
*   **Why:** It is the only path with a mathematical chance of fitting within your 12.8-month runway.
*   **The Pivot:** Explicitly position the device as a "Glucose Trend Monitor" rather than a "Glucose Measurement System." This lowers the bar for clinical evidence required, focusing on correlation and trend accuracy rather than absolute dosing precision.
*   **Immediate Revenue:** Upon clearance, you can charge hospitals for in-patient monitoring suites and offer DTC subscriptions for trend awareness. While you cannot claim "insulin dosing," the value of avoiding hypo/hyperglycemic events via early trend detection is sellable to insurers and health systems.
*   **Contingency:** If the FDA determines no predicate exists during pre-submission (Q-Sub), you must immediately pivot to a "De Novo with 510(k) features" strategy or seek bridge financing, as the standard De Novo timeline exceeds your cash.

**Note:** Engage a regulatory consultant immediately for a "Pre-Submission" meeting with the FDA to confirm predicate viability before spending further on testing. Do not proceed with full clinical trials for dosing claims until revenue from the "trending" version funds the expansion.
