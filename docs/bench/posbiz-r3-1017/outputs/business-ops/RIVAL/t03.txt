### Net Profit Analysis: Lottery vs. First-Come-First-Served

**Financial Baseline**
*   **Revenue Potential:** 2,500 pairs × $220 = $550,000 (assuming 100% sell-through).
*   **Gross Margin per Unit:** $220 - $65 = $155.
*   **Total Gross Profit (Ideal):** $387,500.
*   **Platform Upgrade Cost:** $5,000 (sunk cost for this decision).

**Scenario A: First-Come-First-Served (FCFS)**
*   **Risk:** High traffic (15,000 users) creates a bottleneck. You estimated a $10,000 loss from crashes/fixes.
*   **Hidden Cost:** FCFS heavily favors bots and fastest connections, often alienating genuine fans. If the site slows but doesn't crash, conversion drops as users abandon slow load times.
*   **Estimated Net Impact:** $387,500 (Ideal Profit) - $10,000 (Crash Loss) - $5,000 (Upgrade) = **$372,500**.
*   *Caveat:* This assumes the $10k estimate covers *all* lost sales. If crash duration exceeds expectations or checkout failures spike due to latency, lost revenue could easily exceed $50,000, drastically reducing profit.

**Scenario B: Lottery System**
*   **Risk:** Perceived loss of urgency. However, with only 2,500 pairs for 15,000 interested buyers (16.6% win rate), scarcity is inherent regardless of mechanism.
*   **Benefit:** Eliminates server load spikes. Traffic is distributed over an entry window (e.g., 24 hours), ensuring 100% site stability and a smooth checkout experience for winners.
*   **Brand Equity:** Lotteries are standard in high-demand sneaker culture (e.g., SNKRS app). They are often viewed as "fairer" than FCFS, protecting brand equity among core fans who hate losing to bots.
*   **Estimated Net Impact:** $387,500 (Ideal Profit) - $0 (Crash Loss) - $5,000 (Upgrade) = **$382,500**.

**Recommendation**
**Choose the Lottery System.**
Financially, it secures an additional ~$10,000 in immediate profit by guaranteeing site stability. Strategically, it protects brand equity by ensuring a fair distribution method. The fear of "dampened urgency" is unfounded; the constraint is supply (2,500 units), not the sales mechanism. A lottery converts the "rush" into "anticipation," which often drives higher social sharing and email signups from those who didn't win, lowering future customer acquisition costs.

**Specific Conversion Metrics to Review**
To validate this decision, analyze these three metrics from your last three drops:
1.  **Checkout Abandonment Rate during Peak Minute:** Did users drop off specifically when traffic spiked? If this exceeded 15%, FCFS is too risky.
2.  **Bot vs. Human Traffic Ratio:** What percentage of your "fastest" purchasers were flagged as bots or had suspiciously low dwell times? High bot usage indicates FCFS is leaking revenue to resellers rather than fans.
3.  **Post-Drop Customer Support Ticket Volume:** How many complaints were related to "site errors" or "payment failures" versus "selling out too fast"? High technical error tickets confirm the $10k crash risk is real.

The lottery system maximizes net profit by removing the variable of technical failure while maintaining the scarcity that drives your brand value.
