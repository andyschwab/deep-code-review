### Recommended Pricing Structure: The "Data Buyout" Model

Do not accept the client's current offer of $150/device. It undervalues your asset by $25,000 immediately and sacrifices $100,000 in future potential. Instead, propose a **Full Data Buyout Fee** structured as follows:

**1. The Calculation**
*   **Base Hardware Cost:** $100 per device (covers your immediate costs).
*   **Lost Future Value:** $200 per patient (the aggregate insights you are forfeiting).
*   **Total Fair Value:** $300 per device equivalent.

**2. The Counter-Proposal**
Present two options to the pharma client to give them choice while protecting your valuation:

*   **Option A: Full Buyout (Recommended)**
    *   **Upfront Price:** **$285 per device** (Total: $142,500).
    *   **Terms:** They receive full data ownership. You receive the entire lifetime value upfront.
    *   **Rationale:** Offer a 5% discount ($15/unit) off the full $300 value in exchange for immediate cash flow, which addresses your runway needs without giving away equity in your data assets.

*   **Option B: Hybrid License (If they resist the high upfront cost)**
    *   **Upfront Price:** **$125 per device** (Total: $62,500).
    *   **Data Royalty:** **$150 per patient** payable upon study completion or data handover.
    *   **Terms:** They get exclusive rights to the raw data for the study duration, but you retain the right to aggregate and sell anonymized insights after a 12-month embargo.
    *   **Rationale:** Lowers their initial cash outlay but ensures you capture the lost value if the study succeeds.

**3. Negotiation Script Key Points**
*   "Our standard pricing reflects not just the hardware, but the long-term insights ecosystem. Transferring full ownership removes our ability to monetize the R&D embedded in that data."
*   "The $200 difference isn't a fee; it's the purchase price of our future revenue stream. At $150, you are asking us to subsidize your study by $100,000."
*   "Option A allows you to own the data outright while providing us the capital efficiency we need to support your study timeline."

**Financial Impact Comparison**
*   **Client's Current Offer:** $75,000 total revenue. Net loss of potential value: $100,000.
*   **Your Counter (Option A):** $142,500 total revenue. Captures 95% of total value immediately.
*   **Your Counter (Option B):** $62,500 upfront + $75,000 deferred. Total $137,500.

Given your $75k monthly burn, **Option A** is superior as it nearly doubles your immediate cash injection compared to their offer, extending your runway significantly without relying on future contingent payments.

***

*Ensure the contract explicitly defines "aggregated and anonymized" data if you choose Option B, to prevent loopholes where they claim ownership of derived insights.*
*If they refuse both, walk away; subsidizing a major pharma company's R&D at a 50% discount is unsustainable for a hardware startup.*
