### Executive Summary: SaaS Subscription Drives Higher Valuation

For a Series A raise in the current infrastructure software market, **shifting to a SaaS subscription model will almost certainly generate a higher valuation multiple** than maintaining a perpetual license model. Investors currently prize predictable, recurring revenue (ARR) over lumpy, transactional license fees. While the perpetual model offers immediate cash influx, the SaaS model commands premium multiples (often 8x–12x ARR vs. 2x–4x revenue for perpetual) due to superior cash flow stability and lifetime value visibility.

### Financial Comparison: Perpetual vs. SaaS

**Current State (Perpetual License)**
*   **Revenue Structure:** $600k upfront + $90k/year maintenance (15%).
*   **Cash Flow:** High volatility. You must constantly hunt new "big bag" deals to maintain growth.
*   **Valuation Impact:** Investors discount future maintenance revenue heavily. The business looks like a services firm with product margins, limiting your Series A appeal.
*   **Customer Friction:** Aligns with your customers' preference for ownership but creates a "feast or famine" sales cycle.

**Proposed State (SaaS Subscription)**
*   **Revenue Structure:** $75k/month ($900k/year) per city.
*   **Cash Flow:** Smooth and predictable. This stability de-risks the investment for VCs.
*   **Valuation Impact:** Converts your revenue into high-quality ARR. Even with a transition dip, the *quality* of revenue drives a higher total enterprise value.
*   **Pricing Analysis:** Your proposed $900k/year ACV is a 50% increase over the first-year perpetual equivalent ($690k). This is aggressive but justifiable if you frame it as "continuous innovation" rather than "renting software."

### Transition Modeling: The 60% Conversion Scenario

You asked specifically about the runway impact if only 60% of existing customers convert. Let's model this based on your four current cities.

**Scenario A: Stay Perpetual (Baseline)**
*   Assume you sell 1 new city this year: $600k license + $90k maint (existing) = ~$960k cash inflow.
*   **Runway:** With $180k/mo burn ($2.16M/yr), you are barely covering costs with heavy new sales effort.

**Scenario B: Switch to SaaS (60% Conversion)**
*   **Existing Customers:** 4 cities × 60% conversion = 2.4 cities (round to 2 for conservative modeling).
    *   Revenue: 2 cities × $900k = $1.8M ARR.
    *   *Loss:* 2 cities revert to old maintenance only ($180k total) or churn.
*   **New Sales:** Assume sales velocity slows during transition; 0 new cities Year 1.
*   **Total Year 1 Cash Collection:**
    *   If contracts are signed annually upfront: $1.8M collected.
    *   If monthly: $150k/mo collected initially.
*   **Runway Impact:**
    *   **Monthly Burn:** $180k.
    *   **Monthly Revenue (at 2 cities):** $150k.
    *   **Net Burn:** $30k/month.
    *   **Result:** You become nearly cash-flow neutral immediately with just 2 cities, extending runway indefinitely without new sales.

**The Trade-off:**
In the perpetual model, you need to close **one massive $600k deal** to cover 3+ months of burn. In the SaaS model with 60% conversion, your **existing base covers 83% of your burn**. The stability of the SaaS model drastically reduces your risk of running out of cash, even with lower conversion rates.

### Strategic Recommendation

1.  **Execute the Pivot:** Move to SaaS to secure the Series A multiple. The market does not reward perpetual license models in grid tech anymore.
2.  **Mitigate Customer Pushback:** Do not force the 50% price hike immediately. Offer a "Grandfathered Transition" where existing customers lock in a lower rate (e.g., $50k/month) for 2 years if they sign a 3-year term. This secures ARR for investors while respecting customer budget constraints.
3.  **Model for Investors:** Show the "Net Burn" improvement. Demonstrating that 60% conversion makes you nearly cash-flow positive is a stronger narrative than showing a 100% perpetual collection rate with high volatility.

The SaaS path lowers your immediate cash ceiling but raises your floor and your ceiling simultaneously.

*Note: Ensure your legal team reviews contract termination clauses before proposing the switch to avoid breach of contract claims from municipalities.*
*Consider a hybrid "lease-to-own" option if municipal procurement rules strictly forbid operating expense (OpEx) subscriptions.*
