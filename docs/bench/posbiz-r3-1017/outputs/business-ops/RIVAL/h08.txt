### Recommended Strategy: Hybrid Tiered Pricing with Dynamic Capacity Allocation

Do not choose between raising prices or capping capacity; implement both simultaneously as a unified **Tiered Service Model**. This approach maximizes profitability by capturing the high willingness-to-pay for urgency while protecting your brand reputation through strict quality controls.

**1. Implement a "Rush Premium" Tier ($500/part)**
Proceed with the price increase to $500 for 24-hour delivery. In the aerospace sector, speed is often correlated with critical mission needs, making demand highly inelastic. Your customers are likely paying for risk mitigation and timeline adherence, not just the physical part.
*   **Profit Impact:** At $500, your margin per rush part is $470 ($500 - $80 base cost - $30 overtime). This is a 587% margin increase over your standard $120 margin. Even if you lose 20% of rush customers due to the price hike, the remaining volume generates significantly more profit than the current model.
*   **Competitive Moat:** Competitors charging less likely cannot sustain the overtime labor or quality assurance required for 24-hour aerospace-grade printing. A higher price signals higher reliability, which appeals to your specific demographic.

**2. Enforce a Strict Capacity Cap (10 Parts/Week)**
Immediately cap rush orders at 10 parts per week (approx. 40-43 parts/month). Your current defect rate jump from 1% to 5% is a critical brand risk. In aerospace, a single defective part can ground a project or cause safety failures, destroying your reputation instantly.
*   **Quality Control:** Limiting volume ensures your team can manage the stress of rapid turnaround without compromising inspection protocols.
*   **Scarcity Marketing:** Position the rush tier as "Limited Availability." This validates the $500 price point and prevents the "rush" option from cannibalizing your standard 5-day workflow.

**3. Operational Execution**
*   **Standard Tier:** Maintain $200/5-day service for non-critical needs.
*   **Rush Tier:** $500/24-hour service, capped at 10 units/week.
*   **Overflow Protocol:** If rush demand exceeds 10 units, offer the next available 48-hour slot at a mid-tier price (e.g., $350) or refer excess to competitors. Do not accept more than 10 true 24-hour jobs.

**Financial Projection**
Assuming your current monthly volume is ~1,000 parts with 40% rush (400 parts):
*   **Current State:** 400 rush @ $120 margin + 600 standard @ $120 margin = $120,000 total monthly margin (ignoring defect costs).
*   **Proposed State:** Cap rush at 43 parts @ $470 margin = $20,210. The remaining 357 former rush customers convert to standard tier (or leave). If they convert to standard, you gain 357 @ $120 = $42,840. Plus 600 standard @ $120 = $72,000. Total = ~$135,050.
*   **Result:** You increase monthly margin by ~$15,000 while drastically reducing defect-related liability. If some customers leave entirely, the margin on the retained rush customers is so high that you only need to retain a fraction of them to break even.

**Conclusion**
Adopt the $500 price point immediately to filter for high-value clients and fund the operational rigor required for speed. Simultaneously, enforce the 10-part weekly cap to guarantee zero defects. This positions PrintAero as a premium, reliable partner rather than a commodity shop, securing long-term profitability and trust.

*Note: Monitor defect rates weekly; if they rise above 1% even at the capped volume, reduce the cap further. Communicate the cap clearly to manage expectations.*
