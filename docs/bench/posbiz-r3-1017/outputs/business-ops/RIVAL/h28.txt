### Recommended Pricing Strategy: Hybrid "Complexity Unit" Model

Neither "per citizen" nor "per square mile" alone is the optimal value metric for UrbanTwin. Both fail to capture the true driver of value and cost: **simulation complexity**.

*   **Per Citizen Flaw:** Penalizes high-density cities (e.g., Barcelona's Eixample) where simulating 10,000 people in 1 km² requires exponentially more compute and logic than 10,000 people spread across a rural region. It misaligns price with your server costs and technical difficulty.
*   **Per Square Mile Flaw:** Undervalues dense urban centers. A square mile of Manhattan or Central Barcelona generates vastly more data points (traffic lights, pedestrian flows, energy grids) than a square mile of countryside. You leave money on the table for your highest-value customers.

**The Solution: The "Simulation Node" Metric**
Adopt a hybrid metric that combines population density and area into a single **"Simulation Node"** count. This aligns price with the actual computational load and the value of insights derived from complexity.

**Proposed Structure:**
1.  **Base Unit:** 1 Node = 1,000 citizens OR 1 square mile, whichever is *higher* for a specific zone.
    *   *Rural Zone (Low Density):* Charged by area (sq mile).
    *   *Urban Zone (High Density):* Charged by population (citizens).
2.  **Tiered Packaging:**
    *   **Pilot Tier (€15k/year):** Up to 50 Nodes. Includes basic traffic/energy modeling. Targeted at small municipalities or district-level pilots.
    *   **City Tier (€35k/year):** Up to 300 Nodes. Full emergency response simulation, API access, priority support. Targeted at mid-sized cities (your 250k citizen example).
    *   **Metro Tier (€80k+/year):** Unlimited Nodes within a defined region. Custom integrations, dedicated success manager. Targeted at major capitals or consortiums.

**Why This Wins for European Municipalities:**
*   **Fairness:** A dense city pays more because they get more value (granular data) and cost you more to serve. A sprawling rural province pays for area, not imaginary population density.
*   **Predictability:** Governments hate variable bills. Selling "Nodes" as an annual allowance allows them to budget fixed costs while giving you upside if they expand simulation zones.
*   **Scalability to €3M ARR:** To hit €3M, you need ~85 "City Tier" clients or a mix of 40 Metro and 100 City clients. This is achievable across the EU's thousands of municipalities. The higher price point (€35k+) positions you as enterprise infrastructure, not a disposable tool, increasing retention.

**Implementation Note:**
Frame the metric as "Coverage Units" in sales conversations to avoid technical confusion. Start with a fixed-price pilot for your first 3 customers to validate the Node calculation formula before hardcoding it into contracts.

*Ensure your contract defines "density thresholds" clearly to prevent disputes over how Nodes are calculated for mixed urban/rural regions.*
