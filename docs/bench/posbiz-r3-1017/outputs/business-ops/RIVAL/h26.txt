### Core Determination: Taxable Income vs. Deferred Revenue

For a hardware startup like NeuroPlay, pre-order deposits are generally **not** considered earned taxable income in the year they are collected, provided the product has not been delivered. Instead, they are recorded as **Deferred Revenue** (a liability on your balance sheet).

**1. Financial Accounting (GAAP)**
Under Generally Accepted Accounting Principles, revenue is recognized when performance obligations are met. Since NeuroPlay has not shipped the headsets, you have not fulfilled your obligation.
*   **Action:** Record the $1.8 million as **Cash** (Asset) and **Deferred Revenue** (Liability).
*   **Result:** This $1.8 million does **not** appear on your Income Statement (P&L) as revenue yet. It stays off your taxable income calculation for financial reporting purposes.

**2. Federal Tax Treatment (IRS)**
The IRS generally aligns with the "claim of right" doctrine but offers specific deferral methods for advance payments.
*   **Full Deferral Method:** For goods (like your headset), the IRS typically allows you to defer recognizing income until the year the goods are shipped. Since you are shipping in nine months (likely the next fiscal year), you can likely defer the entire $1.8 million to next year's tax return.
*   **Deferral Method (Rev. Proc. 2004-34):** Even if strict deferral doesn't apply, this procedure often allows businesses to defer advance payments to the next tax year if the goods aren't delivered in the current year.
*   **Result:** You likely owe **$0** in federal income tax on this specific $1.8 million for the current fiscal year. The tax liability triggers in the year of shipment.
