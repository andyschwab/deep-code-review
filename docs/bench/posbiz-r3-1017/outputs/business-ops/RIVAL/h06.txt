### Cash Flow & Strategic Analysis for AutoHaul Series B

**Current Financial Position**
*   **Cash Balance:** $3,000,000
*   **Monthly Burn:** $400,000
*   **Current Runway:** 7.5 months

**Option A: Leasing 10 New Trucks**
*   **Upfront Cost:** $0
*   **New Monthly Cost:** +$20,000 ($2k × 10 trucks)
*   **New Monthly Burn:** $420,000
*   **New Runway:** ~7.1 months
*   **Balance Sheet Impact:** No asset accumulation; liabilities remain operational (OpEx). Cash reserves remain intact at $3M.

**Option B: Buying 10 New Trucks**
*   **Upfront Cost:** $1,000,000 ($100k × 10 trucks)
*   **Monthly Savings:** 30% reduction on operating costs for these units. Assuming the $2k lease represents the full operating cost, buying saves ~$600/truck/month. Total savings: $6,000/month.
*   **New Monthly Burn:** $394,000 ($400k - $6k savings)
*   **New Runway:** ~5.1 months ($2M remaining / $394k burn)
*   **Balance Sheet Impact:** Adds $1M in hard assets (CapEx). Cash reserves drop to $2M.

**Strategic Recommendation: Choose Leasing for Series B Preparation**

For a pre-Series B autonomous trucking network, **leasing is the superior strategy** to secure valuation growth and ensure survival until the round closes.

**1. Runway Preservation is Critical**
Your current runway of 7.5 months is already tight for a Series B timeline, which often takes 6–9 months to close. Buying the trucks immediately cuts your runway to roughly 5 months. If the fundraise slips by even two months, buying puts the company in a "death spiral" where you run out of cash before the check clears. Leasing preserves your $3M war chest, keeping you safely above the 6-month danger zone.

**2. Investor Perception: Cash vs. Assets**
While buying adds assets to the balance sheet, Series B investors in high-growth tech/logic sectors prioritize **cash efficiency and scalability** over asset ownership.
*   **Signaling Risk:** Deploying 33% of your total cash reserves on immediate CapEx signals poor capital allocation discipline. It suggests you are prioritizing long-term unit economics over immediate survival.
*   **Valuation Leverage:** Investors value your ability to scale the network to 200 trucks. Leasing allows you to deploy capital toward growth initiatives (sales, R&D, operations) rather than tying it up in depreciating hardware. You can argue that your capital is better used to prove the *network effect* rather than own the *nodes*.

**3. Flexibility for Expansion**
You plan to grow from 50 to 200 trucks in 18 months. Leasing provides the flexibility to scale up or down based on route density and demand without being burdened by owned inventory. If the market shifts, you are not stuck with $10M in owned trucks; you have an agile cost structure.

**Conclusion**
Buying improves unit economics slightly ($6k/month savings) but creates an existential liquidity risk. Leasing increases burn marginally but secures the runway necessary to reach the Series B close. Present the leasing model to investors as a capital-efficient strategy to maximize network growth per dollar raised, positioning AutoHaul as a scalable tech platform rather than a capital-heavy logistics firm.

*Note: This analysis assumes the $2k lease covers comparable operating costs to the ownership scenario. Ensure legal review of lease terms regarding early termination or buyout options.*
