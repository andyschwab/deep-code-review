**Executive Summary: Direct Sale vs. PPA Model**

Based on your current constraints, the **Direct Sale model** is the superior strategic choice for securing Series A funding and ensuring survival, despite the lower theoretical long-term revenue of the PPA model.

**1. Financial Modeling & IRR Comparison**

*   **Direct Sale Model:**
    *   **Immediate Cash Flow:** +$25,000 profit per unit ($110k price - $85k cost).
    *   **Liquidity:** Instant recycling of capital. With $1.2M cash, you can fund ~14 installations immediately without debt, or leverage debt to scale faster while maintaining a healthy cash buffer.
    *   **Risk:** Zero exposure to maintenance cost overruns or energy production variability.

*   **PPA Model ($0.18/kWh):**
    *   **Revenue Assumption:** Assuming a conservative urban output of 150 MWh/year per turbine, annual revenue is ~$27,000.
    *   **Net Cash Flow:** $27,000 (Revenue) - $4,000 (Est. Maintenance) = $23,000/year.
    *   **Payback Period:** ~3.7 years just to recover the $85k hard cost, ignoring the cost of capital.
    *   **Estimated IRR:** Approximately 18–22% over 15 years *if* maintenance holds at $4k. If maintenance rises to $8k due to access issues, IRR drops to ~12%, and payback extends to 6+ years.
    *   **Capital Trap:** To install just 10 units under this model, you deploy $850k of your $1.2M cash. You effectively become a bank, not an installer.

**2. Balance Sheet Implications**

Choosing the PPA model fundamentally alters your company structure from a **technology/installation firm** to an **asset-holding utility**.

*   **Asset Heavy:** Your balance sheet will be bloated with $85k depreciating assets per unit rather than cash.
*   **Debt Capacity:** You would likely max out your $500k debt capacity immediately to fund growth, increasing interest expenses and covenants risk.
*   **Liability Mismatch:** You are funding 15-year assets with short-term cash and limited debt lines. Any delay in energy production or spike in maintenance creates an immediate liquidity crisis.

**3. Series A Investor Attractiveness**

Your investors' wariness of "asset-heavy businesses" is the deciding factor.

*   **Valuation Multiples:** Software and tech-enabled services (Direct Sale) typically trade at 5x–10x revenue. Asset-heavy utilities/infrastructure often trade at 1x–3x EBITDA due to capital intensity and slower growth.
*   **Scalability:** Direct sales allow you to scale linearly with capital efficiency. The PPA model requires exponential capital to scale linearly.
*   **Risk Profile:** Investors prefer you to de-risk the technology via direct sales first. Owning the assets transfers performance risk (wind variability, maintenance spikes) from the customer to you, which is unattractive for early-stage equity unless you have cheap, permanent capital (which you do not).

**Recommendation**

**Pivot to Direct Sales.** Use the $25k/unit margin to build a war chest for R&D and sales expansion. If customers cannot afford the $110k upfront, consider partnering with a dedicated infrastructure fund to hold the PPAs while you act as the paid installer and maintenance provider. This captures the service revenue without burdening your balance sheet with long-term assets, keeping you "asset-light" and attractive for Series A.

*Note: The PPA model only becomes viable once you have secured cheap, long-term project finance debt specifically ring-fenced for asset ownership, separate from your corporate operating capital.*
