### Demand Forecasting Framework for CellBite's Launch

Since you lack historical data, you must build a **bottom-up adoption model** based on your specific capacity constraints and the behavior of your target niche (high-end EU chefs). Do not rely on broad market reports; instead, use a "Pipeline Conversion" approach tailored to your 2,000 kg/month limit.

**1. The "Chef Pipeline" Conversion Model**
Instead of guessing total market size, calculate demand based on the number of chefs you can actively engage.
*   **Step A: Define the Addressable Pool.** List every high-end restaurant in your target EU cities capable of paying €90/kg. If your target is 500 restaurants, this is your Total Addressable Market (TAM) for the launch phase.
*   **Step B: Estimate Conversion Rates by Phase.** Apply conservative conversion rates typical for luxury B2B food launches:
    *   *Trial Phase (Month 1-2):* Assume 5–10% of contacted chefs will agree to a trial tasting.
    *   *Adoption Phase (Month 3-6):* Of those who trial, assume 40–60% convert to recurring monthly orders.
    *   *Expansion Phase (Month 6-12):* Existing adopters increase volume by 10–20% as they add menu items.
*   **Step C: Calculate Volume per Chef.** High-end restaurants rarely use more than 5–10 kg of a *single* premium protein item per week initially. Assume an average of **20 kg/month per adopting restaurant** for the first quarter.

**2. Scenario Planning for Operational Ranges**
Create three scenarios to guide your raw material and staffing purchases for the next six months.

*   **Conservative Case (Slow Adoption):**
    *   *Assumption:* 5% trial rate, 40% conversion.
    *   *Month 1-3:* 10 restaurants × 20 kg = 200 kg/month.
    *   *Month 4-6:* 25 restaurants × 25 kg = 625 kg/month.
    *   *Implication:* Utilize only 30% of capacity. Focus staffing on flexibility; minimize raw material bulk buying.

*   **Base Case (Moderate Adoption):**
    *   *Assumption:* 10% trial rate, 50% conversion.
    *   *Month 1-3:* 25 restaurants × 20 kg = 500 kg/month.
    *   *Month 4-6:* 60 restaurants × 30 kg = 1,800 kg/month.
    *   *Implication:* Near full capacity utilization by Month 6. Secure supply chains now for the ramp-up.

*   **Aggressive Case (High Demand):**
    *   *Assumption:* 15% trial rate, 60% conversion, rapid menu expansion.
    *   *Month 1-3:* 40 restaurants × 25 kg = 1,000 kg/month.
    *   *Month 4-6:* 100+ restaurants × 35 kg = 3,500+ kg/month (exceeds capacity).
    *   *Implication:* You will hit your 2,000 kg cap by Month 4. Implement a waitlist strategy immediately to manage expectations without losing momentum.

**3. Validation via "Commitment Surveys"**
Replace generic surveys with a **Letter of Intent (LOI)** campaign. Contact 50 target chefs with a specific offer: "Reserve 20 kg/month at €90/kg for Q3."
*   If <5 sign: Your pricing or value proposition needs adjustment before scaling production.
*   If 10–20 sign: You have data for your Base Case.
*   If >25 sign: Prepare for the Aggressive Case and investigate capacity expansion.

**Operational Recommendation:**
For the first six months, staff and purchase materials for the **Base Case peak (1,800 kg)**, but structure supplier contracts with "flexible volume" clauses allowing you to drop to 500 kg without penalty if adoption lags. This protects you from spoilage costs while ensuring you can meet demand if the Aggressive Case materializes.

*Note: At €90/kg with a €40 variable cost, your margin is healthy (€50/kg), allowing some buffer for promotional tasting samples to drive the trial phase.*
*Prioritize securing the top 20 "influencer chefs" in your region; their adoption will drive the remaining 80% of your pipeline through industry peer pressure.*
