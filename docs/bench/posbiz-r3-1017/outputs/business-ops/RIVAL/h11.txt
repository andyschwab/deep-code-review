**Executive Summary: Your current pricing model is unsustainable.**

At $4.99/month with an 8% monthly churn rate, your Customer Lifetime Value (LTV) is approximately $62. Against a Customer Acquisition Cost (CAC) of $35, your LTV:CAC ratio is roughly 1.8:1. While this exceeds the bare minimum 1:1 break-even, it leaves insufficient margin to cover operational overhead, compliance costs, and development, especially with only nine months of runway. The core issue is not just the price point, but the misalignment between your flat fee and the low asset balances ($185 average) of your target demographic.

**Viability of Current Flat Fee Structure**
A flat fee of $4.99 represents an effective annual fee of ~32% on an average balance of $185. This is economically irrational for the user compared to traditional savings accounts or even high-yield options, creating high friction for retention. The 8% churn confirms that users do not perceive $4.99 as fair value for managing such small balances. You are not pricing yourself out of the market due to the absolute dollar amount ($5 is low), but because the *value proposition* feels exploitative relative to the account size.

**Recommended Pivot: Hybrid or Tiered Model**
Do not pivot to a pure percentage-of-assets fee yet. Charging a standard advisory fee (e.g., 0.5% - 1.0%) on $185 yields pennies per month, which will not cover your fixed operational costs per user. Instead, adopt a **Hybrid Model**:

1.  **Low-Cost Entry Tier ($1.99/mo):** Includes basic cash management and automated "gig-income smoothing." This lowers the barrier to entry and reduces churn by aligning better with user cash flow.
2.  **Value-Add Tier ($4.99 - $7.99/mo):** Includes tax withholding automation, quarterly estimated tax filing, and access to human support. This targets the "power users" who derive tangible ROI from tax savings rather than investment growth.
3.  **Optional Asset Fee:** Apply a small percentage fee (e.g., 0.25%) only on balances exceeding a certain threshold (e.g., $1,000), allowing you to capture upside as users grow their wealth without penalizing low balances.

**Regulatory Compliance & Legal Review**
Before scaling, you must engage legal counsel specializing in RIA (Registered Investment Advisor) and fintech regulations. Specific issues include:

*   **Fiduciary Duty & Fee Reasonableness:** The SEC and state regulators require fees to be reasonable relative to services provided. A flat fee that consumes 30%+ of a user's principal could be scrutinized as unreasonable for low-balance accounts, potentially triggering fiduciary breaches.
*   **Disclosure Requirements:** If you shift to a tiered or hybrid model, clear disclosure is required regarding how fees are calculated, especially if they vary by asset level. Ambiguity here can lead to enforcement actions.
*   **State-by-State Licensing:** As a robo-advisor, you may need registration in every state where you have clients. Some states have specific rules about minimum fees or fee structures for retail investors.
*   **Banking Partnership Agreements:** If you hold funds via a partner bank, your fee collection method (direct debit vs. asset deduction) may be constrained by your BaaS (Banking-as-a-Service) agreement.

**Immediate Action Plan**
1.  **Pause Paid Acquisition:** Stop spending $35 CAC until the unit economics are fixed.
2.  **A/B Test Pricing:** Immediately test a $1.99 entry tier against the current $4.99 on new signups to measure churn impact.
3.  **Legal Audit:** Commission a review of your current fee structure's compliance with the Investment Advisers Act of 1940 regarding "reasonable fees" for low-asset accounts.

Your survival depends on lowering the entry price to stabilize churn while upselling high-value tax features, rather than relying on asset growth alone.
