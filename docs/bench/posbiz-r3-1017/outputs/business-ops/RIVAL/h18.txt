**Immediate Recommendation: Do Not Launch Marketing Claims Yet**

You cannot legally market your product as "biodegradable" in the European Union without third-party certification, and attempting to charge a premium based on this unverified claim exposes GreenPoly to severe legal and reputational risks.

**Legal Analysis: The EU Green Claims Directive**
Under current EU regulations, specifically the evolving Green Claims Directive and the Unfair Commercial Practices Directive, environmental claims must be substantiated by scientific evidence and verified by an accredited third party. Self-declared claims like "breaks down in soil within 12 months" without an official eco-label (such as OK Biodegradable SOIL or DIN CERTCO) are considered unsubstantiated.

The EU is actively cracking down on "greenwashing." Making a specific biodegradability claim without certification violates the requirement for clarity and proof. National consumer protection agencies have the authority to impose significant fines, often calculated as a percentage of your turnover, and can order an immediate cease-and-desist of all marketing materials.

**Risk Assessment**
1.  **Regulatory Fines:** Penalties for greenwashing in the EU are escalating. Beyond direct fines, you face the cost of mandatory corrective advertising and potential product recalls if labels are already printed.
2.  **Reputational Damage:** The toy industry is highly sensitive to safety and environmental integrity. If your 10 interested brands discover the claim is unverified, they will likely drop immediately to avoid guilt-by-association. In the B2B sector, trust is your primary currency; losing it before launch could be fatal.
3.  **The "Premium" Trap:** Charging a 30% premium (€5.20/kg vs. €4.00/kg) explicitly ties your value proposition to the environmental claim. If the claim is invalidated, you have no justification for the premium, potentially leading to breach of contract disputes or demands for refunds from early adopters.

**Strategic Path Forward**
You must consult with a **regulatory affairs specialist or legal counsel** specializing in EU environmental law before launching any campaign. Do not rely on internal assessments.

While you wait for certification (the 6-month period):
*   **Pivot Your Messaging:** You may discuss the *technology* factually (e.g., "developing a novel polymer formulation") without making performance claims about biodegradability timelines. Avoid words like "biodegradable," "eco-friendly," or "compostable" in public-facing materials.
*   **Leverage the Samples:** Provide samples to your 10 interested brands under Non-Disclosure Agreements (NDAs) for *technical testing* only. Frame this as a "pilot validation program" rather than a commercial launch. This keeps the dialogue open without triggering consumer protection laws.
*   **Reevaluate the Investment:** The €50,000 certification cost is not an expense; it is the price of entry for your business model. Without it, your product is legally indistinguishable from standard plastic in the eyes of the regulator, regardless of its actual chemical properties.

**Conclusion**
Launching now is a high-risk gamble with low upside. The cost of legal counsel and the certification fee is negligible compared to the potential fines and the permanent loss of credibility in the European market. Secure the certification first, then launch with a defensible, premium-priced product.
