### Pricing Strategy Evaluation for QuantumLeap

**Recommendation: Reject both current options in favor of a Hybrid Credit Model.**

Neither Option A ($50/qubit-hour) nor Option B ($2,000/run) effectively balances your cost recovery needs with researcher incentives.

**Why Option A Fails:**
Billing by qubit-hour creates a **perverse incentive** for your target market. Academic researchers prioritize result verification over speed. If they are billed by the hour, they will artificially slow down algorithms to minimize costs, clogging your queue and reducing total throughput. Furthermore, with job times varying from 10 minutes to 12 hours, revenue becomes unpredictable, making it impossible to guarantee coverage of your $2M hardware investment within your 18-month runway.

**Why Option B Fails:**
A flat $2,000 per successful run is too risky for early adopters. In quantum computing, "success" is probabilistic and often requires hundreds of failed iterations to achieve one valid result. If researchers are billed only on success, you bear 100% of the infrastructure risk for their failed experiments. Conversely, if they interpret this as "$2,000 regardless of attempts," a 10-minute job becomes grossly overpriced compared to a 12-hour job, driving away smaller university labs.

**Proposed Solution: The Pre-Purchased Credit Model**
Adopt a **credit-based system** where researchers purchase blocks of compute credits upfront (e.g., $5,000, $20,000, $50,000 packages).
1.  **Cost Alignment:** Assign credit costs based on actual resource consumption (qubit-hours + error correction overhead). This ensures every minute of hardware usage generates revenue.
2.  **Researcher Incentive:** Researchers control their budget. They can run many cheap, short tests without fear of surprise bills, encouraging experimentation (early adoption).
3.  **Cash Flow:** Upfront credit purchases improve immediate cash flow, critical for your 18-month runway.
4.  **Tiered Discounts:** Offer volume discounts on credit packages to incentivize larger commitments from your 15 pilot universities.

### Regulatory & Compliance Assessment

**Export Control & Technology Transfer:**
Canada regulates quantum technologies under the **Export and Import Permits Act (EIPA)** and aligns closely with the **Wassenaar Arrangement**. Quantum computing hardware and specific encryption-breaking algorithms often fall under "dual-use" goods (civilian and military application).

1.  **Billing/Licensing Restrictions:** There are no specific laws restricting *how* you bill (hourly vs. flat). However, there are strict restrictions on *who* can access the technology. Providing quantum computing resources to researchers in sanctioned jurisdictions or for prohibited end-uses (e.g., weapons development) requires an export permit, even if the service is delivered via the cloud from within Canada.
2.  **International Universities:** Serving international universities triggers "deemed export" considerations if data or control of the algorithm crosses borders to restricted entities. You must screen the end-user, not just the location.

**Compliance Requirements:**
*   **Legal Counsel:** **Mandatory.** You cannot draft your Terms of Service without specialized legal counsel experienced in Canadian export controls and dual-use technology. Standard SaaS terms are insufficient.
*   **Compliance Officer:** **Highly Recommended.** Given the high value and sensitivity of quantum assets, relying solely on external counsel for every transaction is inefficient. An internal compliance officer (or designated senior staff) is needed to implement automated end-user screening and maintain audit trails for the 15 pilot universities and future expansion.
