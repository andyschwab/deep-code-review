**Legal Status of Royalties**
Paying royalties to the estate is **not legally mandatory** under current UK statute in the same way copyright royalties are. The UK does not have a standalone, statutory "personality right" or "publicity right" that automatically survives death and grants estates a perpetual percentage of commercial revenue. Unlike the United States, where states like California and Tennessee have specific statutes protecting the commercial value of a deceased celebrity's likeness and voice, the UK relies on a patchwork of common law torts (such as passing off) and specific intellectual property statutes.

Therefore, the estate's demand for 15% is currently a **negotiation tactic** leveraging their moral position and potential litigation threats, rather than a pre-existing statutory obligation. However, accepting this term without legal counsel is dangerous because signing the contract *creates* the legal obligation voluntarily.

**Intellectual Property Risks & Precedents**
While there is no "voice copyright," you face three distinct legal risks in the UK:

1.  **Passing Off:** If the marketing implies the deceased actor endorsed this new series or was involved in its creation, the estate could sue for "passing off." This requires proving goodwill, misrepresentation, and damage. Using a cloned voice without clear, conspicuous disclosure that the narration is synthetic significantly increases this risk.
2.  **Copyright in the Source Material:** If the voice cloning model was trained on recordings where the estate or a third party holds the copyright (e.g., previous audiobooks or radio archives), the *act of training* the AI or generating the output could constitute copyright infringement. The UK's text-and-data-mining exceptions are narrow and may not cover commercial voice synthesis of protected works.
3.  **Moral Rights & Defamation:** While moral rights generally expire with the author, the estate could argue that the synthetic performance distorts the actor's legacy, potentially leading to reputational damage claims or defamation if the generated content contradicts the actor's known values or previous public statements.

**Precedents**
There are **no direct UK court precedents** specifically ruling on post-mortem AI voice synthesis for commercial audiobooks. The legal landscape is evolving rapidly, with recent high-profile disputes globally (such as the Scarlett Johansson/OpenAI situation in the US) highlighting the volatility of this space. In the UK, courts have historically been reluctant to expand "passing off" to cover mere imitation without explicit misrepresentation, but the introduction of generative AI adds a layer of deception that courts may view differently.
