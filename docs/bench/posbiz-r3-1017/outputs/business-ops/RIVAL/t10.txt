### Revenue Impact Analysis: Flat Fee vs. Usage-Based Model

**Current State (Flat Fee)**
*   **Subscribers:** 1,200
*   **Revenue:** $118,800/month ($99 × 1,200)
*   **Server Costs:** $14,400/month ($12 × 1,200)
*   **Net Margin:** $104,400

**Proposed State (Usage-Based)**
*   **Lost High-Volume Users:** 240 users (Top 10%)
    *   *Revenue Loss:* $23,760/month (240 × $99)
*   **Gained Low-Volume Users:** 400 users
    *   *Usage:* 50 documents/month @ $0.50/doc = $25/user
    *   *Revenue Gain:* $10,000/month (400 × $25)
*   **Remaining High-Volume Users:** 960 users
    *   *Assumption:* These users remain on the flat fee or migrate to a capped plan. If they migrate to pure usage, revenue depends entirely on their volume. For this model, we assume the 240 churn is the only loss of high-volume revenue, and the remaining 960 stay at $99 (hybrid model) or that the "gain" replaces the "loss" in a pure switch scenario.
    *   *Scenario A (Pure Switch, Remaining 960 migrate to usage):* Without knowing the exact document count of the remaining 960, we cannot calculate total revenue precisely. However, if the top 10% generate 40% of volume, the remaining 90% generate 60%. If volume is evenly distributed among the remaining 90%, average usage drops significantly.
    *   *Scenario B (Conservative Estimate - Only counting known changes):*
        *   New Base Revenue (from remaining 960 @ $99): $95,040
        *   Plus New Low-Volume Revenue: $10,000
        *   **Total Projected Revenue:** $105,040
        *   **Net Change:** -$13,760/month (-11.6%)

**Cost Implications**
*   **Current Costs:** $14,400
*   **New Costs:**
    *   Remaining 960 users @ $12: $11,520
    *   New 400 users (50 docs each): Assuming server cost scales with documents, and currently $12 covers an unknown average volume. If the top 10% drive 40% of costs, the cost per document is likely higher for them.
    *   *Risk:* If heavy users leave, your average cost-per-user might drop, but if the $0.50 price point is below your marginal cost per document for heavy users, you lose money on every additional document they process.

**Conclusion**
Based strictly on the numbers provided, **the switch results in an immediate net revenue loss of approximately $13,760 per month**, assuming the remaining users stay on the flat fee. If the remaining 960 users also move to usage-based pricing and their volume is high, the revenue collapse could be significantly worse. The gain from 400 low-volume users ($10k) does not offset the loss of 240 high-volume subscribers ($23.7k). Furthermore, attracting users at $25/month who cost $12/month in server fees leaves a thin $13 margin, vulnerable to any usage spike.

**Recommended Data Segmentation**
Before proceeding, analyze these three specific patterns in your current database:
1.  **Document Distribution Curve:** Plot the number of documents analyzed per user to identify the exact break-even point where $0.50/doc equals $99 (198 documents). Determine how many current users are above vs. below this threshold.
2.  **Volatility Metrics:** Identify users with sporadic high-volume spikes vs. consistent usage. Heavy users with variable months might prefer a hybrid model (base + overage) rather than pure usage.
3.  **Cost-to-Serve Ratio:** Calculate the specific server cost per document for your top 10% vs. bottom 50%. If your cost per document for heavy users exceeds $0.50, the usage model is financially unsustainable regardless of churn.

*Note: Consider a hybrid "Freemium + Overage" or "Base + Usage" model to retain heavy users while capturing low-volume prospects.*
*Do not implement a pure usage switch without validating the marginal cost per document.*
