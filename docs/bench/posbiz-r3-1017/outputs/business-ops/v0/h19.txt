### Market Sizing Framework: Voluntary vs. Mandatory Adoption

To estimate your Total Addressable Market (TAM) without relying on uncertain regulatory mandates, apply a **scenario-based bottom-up analysis**. This method separates known quantities (your inputs) from unknown variables (adoption rates) to create a sensitivity range rather than a single fabricated figure.

**1. Define the Input Variables**
*   **Total Population ($N$):** 5,000 large manufacturing firms in Japan (your provided data).
*   **Average Contract Value ($ACV$):** You must calculate this based on your pricing hypothesis (e.g., glove hardware cost + annual software license per site). *Do not guess this; use your actual unit economics.*
*   **Adoption Rate ($R$):** The variable percentage of firms likely to buy.

**2. The Formulas**
Apply these formulas using your specific $ACV$ and assumed $R$ values:

*   **Conservative Scenario (Voluntary Adoption):**
    $$Revenue_{voluntary} = N \times R_{voluntary} \times ACV$$
    *Methodology:* Estimate $R_{voluntary}$ based on current safety culture leaders. In industrial safety, voluntary adoption of new tech before regulation is often low (e.g., 1%–5%).
    *Application:* If $R_{voluntary} = 2\%$, then $5,000 \times 0.02 = 100$ customers. Multiply by your $ACV$.

*   **Aggressive Scenario (Mandatory Compliance):**
    $$Revenue_{mandatory} = N \times R_{mandatory} \times ACV$$
    *Methodology:* Assume the regulation mandates training for high-risk sectors only, not all 5,000 firms. Estimate the subset of firms in "high-risk" categories (e.g., heavy machinery, electrical) as a percentage of $N$. Then apply a compliance rate (usually high, e.g., 80%–90% of the subset).
    *Application:* If 40% of firms are high-risk ($2,000$) and compliance is 90%, then effective $N = 1,800$. Multiply by your $ACV$.

**3. Decision Aid & Sensitivity**
*   **The Read:** Your revenue potential swings entirely on the definition of "high-risk" industries and the speed of voluntary uptake.
*   **The Flip Input:** The variable that changes your outcome most is not the total number of firms, but the **definition of the mandated subset**. A regulation covering only "electrical utilities" yields a vastly different TAM than one covering "all manufacturing with moving parts."
*   **Rule of Thumb:** If your runway ($800k development cost implies significant capital deployed) requires the "Mandatory" scenario to break even within 18 months, your current risk profile is critical. You must validate if the "Voluntary" scenario alone can sustain operations.
