### Lane A: Unit Economics & Pricing Structure

You are facing a trade-off between immediate cash flow (runway) and long-term unit economics (LTV). Here is the arithmetic applied to your numbers to structure the decision.

**1. The "True Cost" of the Data Clause**
*   **Standard LTV per device:** $100 (upfront) + $200 (future data) = **$300**.
*   **Current Offer LTV per device:** $150 (upfront) + $0 (data) = **$150**.
*   **Value Gap:** You are effectively selling a $300 asset for $150, a **50% discount** on total value.

**2. Runway Impact Analysis**
*   **Monthly Burn:** $75,000.
*   **Contract Volume:** 500 devices.
*   **Cash Injected by Offer:** 500 × $150 = **$75,000**.
*   **Runway Extension:** $75,000 ÷ $75,000/month = **1 month**.
*   *Observation:* This deal buys exactly one month of survival but sacrifices $100,000 in potential future value (500 patients × $200).

**3. Pricing Model Options**
To compensate for the lost data stream, you must restructure the deal so the Net Present Value (NPV) approaches your standard $300/device target, adjusted for risk and time.

*   **Option A: Full Buyout (Upfront Only)**
    *   *Goal:* Capture the full $300 value now.
    *   *Math:* Target Price = $100 (hardware) + $200 (data rights) = **$300/device**.
    *   *Total Ask:* 500 × $300 = $150,000.
    *   *Runway Impact:* 2 months.
    *   *Negotiation Lever:* "Our standard model includes data monetization. To transfer full ownership, the upfront fee must reflect the total lifetime value."

*   **Option B: Hybrid (Reduced Upfront + Royalty)**
    *   *Goal:* Lower their upfront cash hit while retaining upside.
    *   *Structure:* Lower device price (e.g., $125) + Data Royalty.
    *   *Royalty Math:* If they own the data, you need a license fee. If the data is worth $200/patient, propose a royalty of **$X per patient-month** or a **% of study value** that sums to ~$150–$200 over the study life.
    *   *Example:* $125 upfront + $150 royalty (paid quarterly based on data usage milestones).

*   **Option C: Restricted License (Keep Data Rights)**
    *   *Structure:* Charge $100/device. Grant a **limited, non-exclusive license** for *this specific study only*.
    *   *Clause:* "Client owns data *generated*, but Provider retains rights to aggregate, anonymize, and monetize insights derived from the dataset."
    *   *Result:* You keep the $200 future value; they get their study data. This often satisfies pharma clients who need study results but don't necessarily need to own the underlying IP or aggregate market insights.

**Decision Aid**
*   **Break-even Check:** If you accept $150 flat, your effective margin is halved. Only accept if the **probability of closing a $300 deal is <50%** and you strictly need the 1-month runway bridge.
*   **Threshold Rule:** If LTV:CAC drops below 3:1 or payback extends beyond the study duration due to lost data revenue, the unit economics are broken.
*   **Next Step:** Draft a counter-proposal offering **Option C** (Restricted License) first, as it preserves your asset. If they refuse, pivot to **Option A** demanding $250–$300 upfront, explicitly line-iteming the "$200 data buyout fee."

### Lane R: Legal & Compliance Routing

**Regime:** Intellectual Property Law, Data Privacy (HIPAA/GDPR depending on location), and Securities (if this contract is material to fundraising).

**Action Required:** You must route the drafting of "full data ownership" vs. "limited license" clauses to **legal counsel**.
*   **Why:** Defining "ownership" of biometric data involves complex privacy regulations. A clause that seems to sell "data" might inadvertently violate patient consent forms or transfer liability you cannot insure.
*   **Preparation for Counsel:** Bring the client's redlined contract, your standard Terms of Service, and the specific patient consent forms used in your trials. Ask counsel to define the exact scope of "aggregated insights" you can legally retain if the client owns the raw data.
