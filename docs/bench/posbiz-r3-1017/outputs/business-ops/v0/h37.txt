### Lane A: Unit Economics & Pricing Sensitivity Analysis

**1. Current Unit Economics (Baseline)**
*   **Sale Price:** $4,500
*   **COGS:** $1,800
*   **Gross Margin per Unit:** $2,700 ($4,500 - $1,800)
*   **Gross Margin %:** 60%

**2. The Variable: Customer Operational Cost (OpEx)**
You cannot calculate the *customer's* Lifetime Value (LTV) or churn risk without their specific usage data (kWh/month) and local climate data (humidity/temperature affecting run-time).
*   **Formula for Customer Monthly Energy Cost:** `Run Hours × kW Rating × Rate ($/kWh)`
*   **Impact:** If a unit runs 10 hours/day at 1.5 kW:
    *   Winter Cost: 300 hrs × 1.5 kW × $0.12 = **$54/month**
    *   Summer Cost: 300 hrs × 1.5 kW × $0.24 = **$108/month**
    *   *Result:* Customer OpEx doubles. If their budget is fixed, this creates churn risk.

**3. Scenario Modeling: Absorbing vs. Passing Costs**
Since you do not own the customer's electricity bill, "absorbing" the cost implies a subsidy or hardware price adjustment.

*   **Scenario A: Pass-Through (Status Quo)**
    *   Your Margin: $2,700/unit.
    *   Customer Risk: High shock in summer bills.
    *   *Churn Variable:* If price sensitivity > 20% volume drop upon bill shock, revenue declines.

*   **Scenario B: Subsidy (You absorb 50% of summer energy variance)**
    *   *Assumption:* You estimate average summer excess cost per unit is $X. You pay $0.5X.
    *   *New Margin:* $2,700 - ($0.5X × Summer Months).
    *   *Trade-off:* Lower margin per unit vs. potentially higher retention/volume.

*   **Scenario C: Price Adjustment (Raise hardware price to buffer)**
    *   *Goal:* Pre-collect summer energy costs.
    *   *New Price:* $4,500 + Buffer.
    *   *Elasticity Test:* You noted a potential >20% volume drop.
    *   *Break-even Volume Calculation:*
        `Required Volume = Current Revenue / New Price`
        If you raise price by 10% ($450) to cover energy, you can afford a volume drop of only ~9% to maintain total gross profit dollars. If volume drops 20%, total profit collapses.

**4. Decision Aid & Next Steps**
*   **The Flip Input:** The specific **kWh consumption per unit in peak summer**. Without this, the subsidy cost is unknown.
*   **Rule of Thumb:** If LTV:CAC falls below 3:1 or payback period exceeds 12 months due to subsidies, the model is unsustainable.
*   **Immediate Action:** Do not guess the elasticity. Run a **Van Westendorp price sensitivity survey** with your 200 existing customers or leads. Ask: "At what price would this unit be too expensive considering summer electric bills?" Use their responses to define the acceptable price band before altering your pricing structure.
*   **Financial Health Check:** With $550k cash and 200 units sold, ensure your runway accounts for the timing mismatch if you offer rebates/subsidies later in the year.

**Note:** You must instrument actual energy usage data from a sample of deployed units to replace the "unknown" variables in this model.
**Note:** Any structured subsidy plan may have tax or securities implications; consult a CPA or counsel before promising future financial credits to customers.
