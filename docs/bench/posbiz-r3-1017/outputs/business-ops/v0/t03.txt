### Net Profit Analysis: First-Come-First-Served vs. Lottery

To determine the optimal launch method, we must compare the **Expected Net Profit** of each scenario by applying your specific inputs to the risk of site failure and the constraints of inventory.

**Common Inputs:**
*   **Inventory Cap:** 2,500 pairs (Revenue is capped at $2,500 \times \$220 = \$550,000$ regardless of demand).
*   **Variable Cost:** $65/pair.
*   **Gross Margin per Unit:** $\$220 - \$65 = \$155$.
*   **Total Potential Gross Profit (if sold out):** $2,500 \times \$155 = \$387,500$.
*   **Platform Upgrade Cost:** $5,000 (applied to First-Come scenario to mitigate risk).

#### Scenario A: First-Come-First-Served (FCFS)
*Assumption:* You spend the $5,000 upgrade budget. The prompt estimates a residual risk cost of $10,000 (lost sales + fixes) if the site struggles, though the upgrade aims to prevent this. We will model the "Net" based on the probability of success vs. failure.

1.  **Revenue:** Capped at $550,000 (assuming the upgrade prevents total collapse).
2.  **COGS:** $162,500 ($2,500 \times \$65$).
3.  **Launch Costs:** $5,000 (upgrade).
4.  **Risk Adjustment:** If the site crashes despite the upgrade, you lose the estimated $10,000.
    *   *Best Case (Stable):* $\$387,500 \text{ (Gross Profit)} - \$5,000 = \mathbf{\$382,500}$.
    *   *Worst Case (Crash):* If a crash prevents selling all 2,500 units, profit drops by $155 for every unsold pair, plus the $10,000 penalty.

#### Scenario B: Lottery System
*Assumption:* No site crash risk (traffic is asynchronous), but potential "urgency dampening" reduces the conversion rate of the 15,000 interested buyers.

1.  **Revenue:** Capped at $550,000 (if you successfully convert 2,500 winners).
2.  **COGS:** $162,500.
3.  **Launch Costs:** $0 (no upgrade needed).
4.  **Conversion Risk:** If "dampened urgency" means fewer than 2,500 people complete the purchase after winning:
    *   *Formula:* $\text{Net Profit} = (\text{Units Sold} \times \$155)$.
    *   *Break-even on Urgency:* You can afford to sell **33 fewer pairs** in a lottery ($\$5,000 \text{ saved} \div \$155 \text{ margin}$) compared to the FCFS upgrade cost and still come out ahead financially.
    *   *If urgency drops conversion by >1.3%* (relative to the pool needed to fill inventory), FCFS becomes more profitable *provided* the site holds.

#### Decision Framework
The mathematical pivot point is **Site Stability vs. Conversion Drop-off**.
*   Choose **FCFS** if: You are confident the $5,000 upgrade reduces crash probability to near zero. The upside is capturing maximum impulse volume immediately.
*   Choose **Lottery** if: You believe the "urgency dampening" will result in selling fewer than 2,467 pairs (2,500 - 33). However, lotteries often *increase* perceived equity and entry volume, potentially ensuring a full sell-out without technical debt.

**Specific Conversion Metrics to Review from Last Three Drops:**
To finalize this decision, calculate these three figures from your historical data:
1.  **Peak Concurrent User Conversion Rate:** What % of users bought during the highest traffic minute of previous drops? (If this was <5%, FCFS risk is high).
2.  **Cart Abandonment Rate under Load:** Did abandonment spike when site latency exceeded 2 seconds in past launches?
3.  **Waitlist-to-Purchase Ratio:** For any previous lotteries or raffles, what % of "winners" actually completed checkout? (If <85%, the lottery risks leaving inventory unsold).

**Next Step:** If your historical "Cart Abandonment under Load" exceeds 20%, the lottery mathematically safeguards the $387k gross profit potential better than a risky FCFS event, even with the upgrade.
