### Unit Economics Comparison: Robots vs. Human Labor

To compare the cost per square foot, you must calculate the **Total Cost of Ownership (TCO)** for both models over a specific period (e.g., 12 months) and divide by the total square footage cleaned. Below is the arithmetic structure applied to your provided numbers.

#### 1. Human Labor Model (Annual Cost)
*Formula:* `Annual Cost = (Hourly Rate × Workers × Hours/Shift × Shifts/Day × Days/Year)`
*Assumptions required from you:* Hours per shift, shifts per day, operating days per year.
*Example Structure (using 8hr shifts, 1 shift/day, 365 days):*
- Hourly Rate: $18
- Workers: 40
- Annual Hours: 8 × 365 = 2,920
- **Annual Labor Cost** = $18 × 40 × 2,920 = **$2,102,400 per stadium**
- **Cost per Sq Ft** = $2,102,400 ÷ 1,500,000 sq ft = **~$1.40/sq ft/year**

#### 2. Robot Model (Annual Cost)
*Formula:* `Annual Cost = (CapEx Amortization + OpEx) ÷ Sq Ft`
*Inputs provided:*
- CapEx (Initial): $35,000 × 10 units = $350,000
- OpEx (Recurring): $2,000/month × 10 units × 12 months = $240,000
- *Missing Variables (User must supply):* Depreciation schedule (e.g., 3 years), maintenance downtime cost, battery replacement, supervision staff.

*Structure for Year 1 (assuming full CapEx hit in Year 1 for conservative cash flow, or amortized if accounting rules allow):*
- **Scenario A (Full CapEx Year 1):** ($350,000 + $240,000) = $590,000
- **Scenario B (Amortized over 3 years):** ($116,666 + $240,000) = $356,666
- **Cost per Sq Ft (Scenario A)** = $590,000 ÷ 1,500,000 = **~$0.39/sq ft/year**
- **Cost per Sq Ft (Scenario B)** = $356,666 ÷ 1,500,000 = **~$0.24/sq ft/year**

#### 3. Crossover Point & Break-Even Analysis
*Formula:* `Break-Even Months = Total CapEx ÷ (Monthly Labor Cost − Monthly Robot OpEx)`
- Monthly Labor Cost (from above example): $2,102,400 ÷ 12 = $175,200
- Monthly Robot OpEx: $240,000 ÷ 12 = $20,000
- Monthly Savings (before CapEx): $175,200 − $20,000 = $155,200
- **Break-Even Time** = $350,000 (CapEx) ÷ $155,200 = **~2.25 months**

*Decision Aid:* If your actual labor hours are lower or robot OpEx is higher, the break-even extends. At 2.25 months, you exceed the 40% reduction target within Year 1 easily, *provided* no major hidden costs exist.

#### 4. Critical Missing Variables for Accuracy
To finalize this for investors, you must insert real data for:
1.  **Supervision Staff:** Robots rarely run fully autonomous. Add `(Supervisor Hourly Rate × Hours)` to Robot OpEx.
2.  **Maintenance Downtime:** If robots are down 10% of the time, do you pay human backup? Add `(Backup Labor Cost × Downtime %)` to Robot OpEx.
3.  **Battery Replacement:** If batteries last 2 years, add `(Replacement Cost ÷ 24 months)` to monthly OpEx.
