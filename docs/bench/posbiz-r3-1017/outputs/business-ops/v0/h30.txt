**Lane R: Regulation-Dependent Routing**
Your question concerns **revenue recognition**, which is a strict accounting determination governed by regulatory standards (specifically **ASC 606** in the US GAAP regime). Because the answer depends on interpreting contract terms, performance obligations, and transfer of control under these standards, this is a **Lane R** issue. I cannot conclude whether you should recognize revenue now or later; doing so would constitute unauthorized accounting advice.

**The Regime and The Conflict**
Under US GAAP (ASC 606), revenue is recognized when a company satisfies a **performance obligation** by transferring control of a promised good or service to a customer. The core dispute between your auditors likely hinges on defining that "unit of account":
1.  **Over Time (Data Collection):** Is the service the continuous monitoring and data processing? If the client receives and consumes the benefit of the monitoring as you perform it (e.g., they use the data for internal reporting before the certificate exists), revenue *might* be recognized over the six months.
2.  **Point in Time (Certificate Issuance):** Is the service solely the issuance of the tradable certificate? If the data collection has no value to the client without the final certificate, and the certificate is contingent on approval, control may not transfer until the moment of issuance.

**The Contingency Risk**
You noted the certificate approval is "contingent." If the final issuance is uncertain or subject to third-party validation that could fail, recognizing revenue before that uncertainty is resolved creates a high risk of **material misstatement**. If you recognize revenue early to secure a bridge loan, and the certificates are later delayed or rejected, you may be forced to restate earnings. This can trigger loan defaults, damage credibility with investors, and lead to regulatory scrutiny or auditor resignation.

To get a definitive answer from your CPA, prepare the following:
