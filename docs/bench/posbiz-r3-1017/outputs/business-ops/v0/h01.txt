### Lane A Analysis: Pricing Impact vs. Cost Absorption

To decide between passing on the cost or absorbing it, we must apply your specific numbers to two scenarios. The critical variable here is **price elasticity**, evidenced by your recent data: a 5-cent hike caused a 4% churn.

**Current Baseline (Pre-Tax Impact)**
*   **Cost per Unit:** $0.80
*   **Price per Unit:** $1.40
*   **Margin per Unit:** $0.60
*   **Observed Elasticity:** A ~3.6% price increase (5¢/$1.40) resulted in a 4% volume loss. This suggests your customers are highly sensitive (elasticity > 1).

**Scenario 1: Pass Full Cost Increase to Customers**
*   **New Cost:** $0.92
*   **New Price:** $1.52 (+$0.12 increase)
*   **Projected Volume Impact:** Your previous data shows a 5¢ hike caused 4% churn. A 12¢ hike is 2.4 times larger. If sensitivity remains linear, you could face roughly **9.6% churn** (approx. 10%).
*   **New Margin per Unit:** $1.52 - $0.92 = $0.60 (Margin % drops from 43% to 39.5% due to lower volume efficiency, though per-unit margin is stable).
*   **Net Revenue Impact:** If you lose ~10% of your 200+ cafes, your total contribution margin drops significantly despite the higher price.
    *   *Formula:* `Total Margin = (Units × 0.90) × $0.60`
    *   *Result:* You maintain per-unit margin but sell significantly fewer units. Given your customer sensitivity, this risks triggering a cascade of cancellations among the remaining price-sensitive accounts.

**Scenario 2: Absorb Half the Cost Increase**
*   **New Cost:** $0.92
*   **New Price:** $1.46 (+$0.06 increase)
*   **Projected Volume Impact:** A 6¢ hike is slightly larger than your previous 5¢ test. Churn might range between **4.5% and 5%**.
*   **New Margin per Unit:** $1.46 - $0.92 = $0.54.
*   **Financial Strain:** You noted an $8,000/month increase in burn rate.
    *   *Runway Calculation:* `Runway Reduction = Cash Reserves / (Old Burn + $8,000)`. You estimated this shortens runway by 3 months.
*   **Net Revenue Impact:** You retain more volume than Scenario 1, but your margin per unit shrinks by 10% ($0.60 → $0.54).
    *   *Formula:* `Total Margin = (Units × 0.95) × $0.54`

**Decision Aid & Thresholds**
1.  **The Break-Even Volume Question:** To justify Scenario 1 (full pass-through) over Scenario 2, the volume lost must be less than the margin gained. However, your data suggests volume loss will be high.
2.  **Rule of Thumb:** If LTV:CAC is healthy but churn spikes above 5% quarterly, retention usually outweighs immediate margin recovery in B2B relationships.
3.  **The "Flip" Input:** The deciding factor is whether your top 50 accounts have different elasticity than the average. If the top 50 represent >40% of volume and are locked in, Scenario 1 becomes safer. If they are as sensitive as the rest, Scenario 2 preserves cash flow stability.

**Recommended Next Step:**
Before changing prices for all 200+ cafes, **segment your top 50 accounts**. If X (top 50 volume) > 40% of total, consider a tiered approach: absorb costs for the top tier to secure contracts, and test the full increase on the remaining 150 smaller accounts. This isolates the churn risk.
