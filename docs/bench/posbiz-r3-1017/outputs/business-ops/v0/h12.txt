### Lane A: Arithmetic Framework (Apply to Your Numbers)

You can structure the potential write-down using the **Lower of Cost or Net Realizable Value (LCNRV)** principle, common in many jurisdictions for perishable goods. The math below shows how to calculate the exposure; the decision to book it and the specific percentage allowed depend on German regulations.

**1. Current Inventory Exposure**
*   **Total Volume:** 500 liters
*   **Sold:** 30% (150 liters)
*   **Remaining:** 350 liters
*   **Historical Cost:** €2,400/liter
*   **Total Book Value at Risk:** $350 \times €2,400 = €840,000$

**2. Valuation Logic (The Formula)**
Accounting standards typically require inventory to be valued at the lower of its historical cost or its Net Realizable Value (NRV).
$$ \text{Write-Down Amount} = \text{Book Value} - \max(0, \text{Estimated Selling Price} - \text{Costs to Sell}) $$

*   **Scenario A (Sold before expiry):** If you sell at €2,400 or higher, Write-Down = €0.
*   **Scenario B (Discounted sale):** If you must discount to €1,000/liter to move stock, the NRV is €1,000.
    *   $\text{Loss per liter} = €2,400 - €1,000 = €1,400$
    *   $\text{Total Potential Write-Down} = 350 \times €1,400 = €490,000$
*   **Scenario C (Total loss):** If unsold by month 6, NRV = €0.
    *   $\text{Total Write-Down} = €840,000$

**3. Impact on Runway (Sensitivity)**
Your runway is 4 months. A write-down reduces reported assets and equity but does not immediately consume cash. However, it signals a liquidity crisis if the inventory cannot be converted to cash before expiry.
*   **Decision Aid:** If the probability of selling 350L at >€2,400 within 6 months is low, the *economic* value is already impaired. The accounting question is *when* and *how much* of that impairment must be recognized formally under German law.

### Lane R: Regulation & Compliance (Route to Professional)

**Regime Identified:** German Commercial Law (HGB – *Handelsgesetzbuch*) and German Tax Law (EStG – *Einkommensteuergesetz*).

**Required Action:** You must engage a **German certified tax advisor (*Steuerberater*)** or auditor immediately.

**Reasons for Routing:**
1.  **Mandatory Write-Down Rules:** Under HGB (§ 253), strict rules govern when a write-down is mandatory versus optional. Determining if the "expiration risk" constitutes a permanent or temporary impairment requires a legal interpretation of your specific contract terms and market conditions.
2.  **Tax Deductibility:** While a commercial write-down reduces book income, German tax law often has different thresholds for recognizing losses for tax purposes. Claiming a deduction prematurely or incorrectly can trigger audits and penalties.
3.  **Audit Defense:** Since you are facing an upcoming financial audit, the documentation supporting the valuation assumption (e.g., proof of slowed demand, shelf-life testing) must meet specific evidentiary standards accepted by German auditors.

**Next Step:** Do not finalize the audit figures until a *Steuerberater* confirms the allowable write-down percentage and the required documentation for the tax office (*Finanzamt*).
