**Lane R: Route to a Professional**
Your request asks for specific contractual language, financial thresholds, and structural guidance to ensure leases are classified as "operating" rather than "capital" (finance) leases to avoid balance sheet recognition. This is a **Lane R** matter.

1.  **Regime Identification:** In Germany, lease classification is governed primarily by **IFRS 16** (for consolidated financial statements) and **HGB** (German Commercial Code) for local statutory accounts. Under IFRS 16, the distinction between operating and finance leases for *lessees* has largely been eliminated, requiring most leases to be recognized on the balance sheet. For *lessors* (your company), the classification determines whether you recognize the asset or a receivable. Determining whether a contract contains a lease, how to classify it, and the impact on your debt covenants is a binding legal and accounting determination.
2.  **The Risk:** Attempting to structure contracts specifically to achieve a desired accounting outcome ("off-balance-sheet" treatment) without professional validation carries high risk. If the bank or auditors determine the substance of the transaction differs from its form (e.g., if the lease term covers a major part of the economic life or if there is a bargain purchase option), they may reclassify the leases. This could trigger a covenant breach regarding your 2.5 debt-to-equity ratio.
3.  **Action Required:** You must engage a qualified **German CPA (Wirtschaftsprüfer)** or **legal counsel** specializing in commercial leasing and IFRS/HGB compliance. They will review your specific term sheets, residual value assumptions, and ownership clauses to provide a definitive opinion on classification.
