### Lane A: Unit Economics & Pricing Sensitivity

**Current Winter Margin Impact (Status Quo)**
*Formula:* `Margin per lb = Price - Cost`
*   **Standard Month:** $4.50 - $2.80 = **$1.70/lb**
*   **Winter Month (Jan):** $4.50 - $3.90 = **$0.60/lb**
*   **Margin Compression:** Your margin drops by 65% in winter. At 8,000 lbs, monthly contribution falls from $13,600 to $4,800.

**Scenario Analysis: $0.60 Price Increase**
*Assumption:* You successfully implement a new price of $5.10 ($4.50 + $0.60).
*Formula:* `New Margin = New Price - Winter Cost`
*   **New Winter Margin:** $5.10 - $3.90 = **$1.20/lb**
*   **Volume Sensitivity (High Elasticity):** "High elasticity" means a small price increase causes a disproportionately large drop in quantity demanded.
    *   If demand drops by **15%** (conservative high elasticity): Volume = 6,800 lbs.
        *   Total Contribution: 6,800 × $1.20 = **$8,160** (Still higher than the $4,800 status quo).
    *   If demand drops by **40%** (extreme high elasticity): Volume = 4,800 lbs.
        *   Total Contribution: 4,800 × $1.20 = **$5,760** (Marginal gain over status quo).
    *   **Break-even Volume Drop:** To match the current $4,800 contribution with the new margin, volume cannot fall below 4,000 lbs (a 50% drop).

**Decision Aid**
The math suggests a $0.60 increase protects profitability even with significant volume loss, provided demand does not halve. However, because your contracts are fixed until March, this arithmetic applies only to the *next* cycle or if you can negotiate an interim "energy adjustment clause."

**Next Step:** Before approaching buyers, calculate your specific "churn threshold." If you believe a price hike will lose you more than 50% of your volume, the increase destroys value. If you believe loss will be under 40%, the increase is mathematically sound.

### Strategic Inputs for Negotiation (Cost Benchmarks)

To justify a seasonal surcharge or higher base price, you must shift the conversation from your internal costs to market-wide conditions. Procurement officers reject "your costs are high" but accept "the category cost floor has risen."

**Data to Gather:**
1.  **Regional Energy Indexes:** Obtain the specific Commercial Electricity rates for Chicago (ComEd) for Jan/Feb vs. July/Aug. Show the percentage spike applicable to *all* indoor ag, not just you.
2.  **Competitor Production Modes:** Identify if competitors are field-grown (seasonal downtime in winter) or imported (high logistics costs in winter). If local competitors are also indoor, their costs likely spiked similarly. If they are imported, benchmark against rising freight/diesel costs.
3.  **Substitute Price Volatility:** Track the wholesale price of California leafy greens during winter months. Historically, supply shocks in CA (rain/flood) spike national prices. If market rates naturally rise in winter due to scarcity, your surcharge aligns with the market, rather than fighting it.

**Note:** Since your current contracts are fixed-price until March, any price change requires mutual agreement or waits for renewal; forcing a change now could be a breach of contract (Lane R: Legal).
Gather the external energy and freight data first; do not lead the negotiation with your internal margin squeeze.
