### Lane A: Unit Economics & Pricing Arithmetic

Your current flat-fee model shows negative unit economics based on the data provided. Here is the applied math:

**1. Lifetime Value (LTV) Calculation**
*   **Formula:** $LTV = \frac{\text{Monthly Revenue}}{\text{Churn Rate}}$
*   **Inputs:** Revenue = $4.99; Churn = 0.08 (8%)
*   **Result:** $LTV = \frac{4.99}{0.08} = \$62.38$

**2. LTV to CAC Ratio**
*   **Formula:** $\frac{LTV}{CAC}$
*   **Inputs:** LTV = $62.38; CAC = $35.00
*   **Result:** $1.78:1$
*   **Read:** A ratio below 3:1 typically indicates unsustainable growth before operational costs. You recover acquisition costs, but little remains for overhead or profit.

**3. Payback Period**
*   **Formula:** $\frac{CAC}{\text{Monthly Margin}}$ (Assuming near 100% gross margin on software fees)
*   **Result:** $\frac{35}{4.99} \approx 7$ months.
*   **Read:** It takes 7 months to recoup acquisition costs. With an 8% monthly churn, you lose over half your cohort before breaking even on acquisition.

**Pricing Model Sensitivity Analysis**
You asked whether to pivot to percentage-based or tiered pricing. Applying the arithmetic to your average balance ($185):

*   **Percentage-Based Fee:** To match the $4.99 revenue, the fee would need to be $\approx 2.7\%$ per month ($32.4\%$ APR).
    *   *Arithmetic Reality:* A 2.7% monthly fee is likely prohibitively high for users, potentially exceeding regulatory usury limits or market tolerance. At a standard 1% annual fee ($0.08\%$ monthly), revenue drops to $0.15/month, worsening the LTV:CAC ratio to $0.05:1$.
*   **Tiered Pricing (Good-Better-Best):**
    *   If you keep the $4.99 tier but add a lower tier (e.g., $1.99 for balances <$100), you reduce churn among low-balance users but lower LTV further unless volume compensates.
    *   *Decision Aid:* The constraint here is not just the fee structure, but the **average balance size**. With $185 balances, fee-based revenue is mathematically insufficient to cover a $35 CAC unless churn drops drastically or CAC decreases.

**Next Step:** Before changing pricing, instrument a test to lower CAC (e.g., organic referrals) or increase average balance (e.g., features encouraging deposits). If CAC cannot drop below $15 or churn below 3%, the current demographic may not support a standalone fee-based model.

### Lane R: Regulatory & Compliance Routing

Your request highlights specific areas requiring a licensed attorney (securities and consumer finance) before scaling:

1.  **Fee Structure & Usury/UDAAP:** While you calculated that a percentage fee matching your revenue needs would be ~32% APR, stating or charging this may violate state usury laws or federal Unfair, Deceptive, or Abusive Acts or Practices (UDAAP) standards. An attorney must review if your fee constitutes "interest" under state lending laws.
2.  **Investment Adviser Registration:** Charging a fee specifically for managing assets (even via a robo-model) often triggers registration requirements with the SEC or state regulators under the Investment Advisers Act of 1940, regardless of account size. The "robo-advisor" label itself carries specific compliance obligations regarding fiduciary duty.
3.  **Banking Partnerships:** If you hold these balances, you may need a bank charter or a specific partnership agreement with a chartered institution. Holding customer funds without proper licensing creates severe legal liability.

**Action:** Do not implement a percentage fee or scale marketing until counsel confirms your fee structure does not classify you as an unregistered lender or investment adviser requiring specific capital reserves.
