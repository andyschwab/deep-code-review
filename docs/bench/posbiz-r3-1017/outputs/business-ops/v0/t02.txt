### Net Revenue Model & Break-Even Analysis

**Current Baseline (15% Denial Rate)**
*   **Gross Revenue per Visit:** $85
*   **Denial Impact:** 15% of visits yield $0 revenue.
*   **Effective Revenue per Visit:** $85 × (1 - 0.15) = **$72.25**
*   **Operational Cost per Visit:** $30 (fixed, regardless of payment)
*   **Contribution Margin per Visit:** $72.25 - $30 = **$42.25**
*   **Current Monthly Volume:** 12,000 visits / 3 months = 4,000 visits/month
*   **Current Monthly Contribution:** 4,000 × $42.25 = **$169,000**
*   **Current Monthly Burn:** $140,000
*   **Current Monthly Net Cash Flow:** $169,000 - $140,000 = **+$29,000** (Positive)

**Scenario A: Denial Rate Stabilizes at 15%**
*   **Result:** You maintain a positive cash flow of ~$29,000/month. No additional volume is required to maintain current cash flow, as you are already covering burn.

**Scenario B: Denial Rate Creeps to 20%**
*   **Effective Revenue per Visit:** $85 × (1 - 0.20) = **$68.00**
*   **New Contribution Margin per Visit:** $68.00 - $30 = **$38.00**
*   **Target Monthly Contribution:** To maintain the *current* net cash flow of $29,000 against a $140,000 burn, you need total contribution of $169,000.
*   **Required Volume Calculation:**
    *   Formula: `Required Visits = Target Contribution ÷ New Margin`
    *   Calculation: $169,000 ÷ $38.00 ≈ **4,448 visits/month**
*   **Additional Visits Needed:** 4,448 - 4,000 = **448 additional visits/month** (an ~11.2% increase).

**Sensitivity Check (Break-Even Point)**
If denials rise further, the volume required grows non-linearly.
*   **Break-Even Margin:** To simply cover the $140,000 burn at 20% denials: $140,000 ÷ $38.00 = 3,685 visits. You are currently above this, but the buffer shrinks as denials rise.
*   **Critical Threshold:** If denials reach **~29.4%**, your contribution margin drops to $30.01. At this point, each visit barely covers its own cost, making it impossible to cover fixed burn regardless of volume.

### Decision Aid
1.  **Read:** At 20% denials, you must process ~450 extra visits monthly just to stay where you are today; growth requires even more.
2.  **Flip Input:** The variable that flips this answer is the **operational cost**. If you can reduce the $30 fixed cost per visit, the required volume increase drops significantly.
3.  **Rule of Thumb:** If denial rates exceed 25%, volume scaling becomes inefficient; focus must shift entirely to revenue cycle management rather than sales.
4.  **Next Step:** This week, isolate the top 3 denial codes from the last 30 days. If they are administrative (e.g., missing modifiers), train staff; if clinical (medical necessity), review therapist documentation protocols before attempting to drive the 448 extra visits.

### Data Points to Gather for Forecasting
To refine this model, gather the following from your payer contracts and claims data:
1.  **Appeal Success Rates by Payer:** Historical % of denied claims successfully overturned on appeal (this recovers revenue without new volume).
2.  **Contractual Write-offs vs. Denials:** Distinguish between "denied" (fixable) and "contractually adjusted" (pre-negotiated lower rates masquerading as losses).
3.  **Days Sales Outstanding (DSO) by Payer:** High denials often correlate with delayed payments; cash flow timing may be a bigger risk than total revenue loss.
4.  **Specific Denial Reason Codes:** Aggregate data by code (e.g., CO-16, CO-97) to determine if the 15-20% loss is solvable via process change or requires contract renegotiation.
