### Lane A: Stress-Testing Framework (Apply, Don't Direct)

To model the impact of the fee change without fabricating unknown variables, use a **Contribution Margin Sensitivity Analysis**. This isolates how much volume can drop before your net revenue equals your current burn.

**1. Define Your Current Baseline (Quarterly)**
*   **Gross Transaction Volume (GTV):** $3,400,000
*   **Current Platform Fee:** 2% ($68,000)
*   **Your Commission (5% of GTV):** $170,000
*   **Current Net Revenue (Commission - Platform Fee):** $102,000
*   **Estimated Burn:** You have $400k cash and 8 staff. If you assume a typical early-stage burn of $100k/month, your runway is ~4 months. *You must replace this estimate with your actual monthly net burn.*

**2. The Stress Test Formula**
You need to solve for the **Break-Even Volume Drop** under the new tiered fee structure.

$$ \text{New Net Revenue} = (\text{GTV} \times (1 - \text{Drop\%}) \times 5\%) - (\text{GTV} \times (1 - \text{Drop\%}) \times \text{New Tiered Fee\%}) $$

Set *New Net Revenue* equal to your fixed costs (Burn). Since the new fee is tiered, you must calculate this at three points:
*   **Scenario A (Optimistic):** Volume drops 10%, fees stay low tier.
*   **Scenario B (Target Stress):** Volume drops 30% (as requested). Calculate the effective fee % at this lower volume.
*   **Scenario C (Critical):** Volume drops until Net Revenue = $0.
