### Lane A: Cash Flow & Runway Arithmetic

**Current Baseline**
*   **Cash:** $3,000,000
*   **Monthly Burn:** $400,000
*   **Current Runway:** $3,000,000 ÷ $400,000 = **7.5 months**

**Scenario 1: Leasing 10 New Trucks**
*   **Upfront Cost:** $0
*   **Added Monthly Cost:** 10 trucks × $2,000 = $20,000
*   **New Monthly Burn:** $420,000
*   **New Runway:** $3,000,000 ÷ $420,000 ≈ **7.14 months**
*   **Balance Sheet Impact:** No immediate asset addition; liability increases via lease obligations. Cash reserves remain intact at $3M.

**Scenario 2: Buying 10 New Trucks**
*   **Upfront Cost:** 10 trucks × $100,000 = $1,000,000
*   **Remaining Cash:** $2,000,000
*   **Operating Cost Reduction:** The prompt states buying lowers monthly operating cost by 30%.
    *   *Assumption Required:* To calculate the dollar savings, we must know the current monthly operating cost *per truck*. The prompt provides the lease rate ($2,000) but not the current owned-truck operating cost.
    *   *Hypothetical Application:* If we assume the $2,000 lease rate approximates the current fully-loaded monthly cost of operation, a 30% reduction saves $600/truck/month.
    *   **Estimated Savings:** 10 trucks × $600 = $6,000/month.
    *   **New Monthly Burn:** $400,000 - $6,000 = $394,000.
*   **New Runway (Hypothetical):** $2,000,000 ÷ $394,000 ≈ **5.08 months**.
*   **Balance Sheet Impact:** Cash drops to $2M; Property, Plant & Equipment (PP&E) increases by $1M.

**Sensitivity & Decision Thresholds**
*   **The Flip Input:** The viability of buying depends entirely on the *actual* dollar value of the 30% operating reduction.
    *   *Break-even Savings Calculation:* To maintain the same 7.5-month runway after spending $1M, the new burn must be ≤ $266,666 ($2M ÷ 7.5). This requires a burn reduction of $133,334/month, which is impossible with only 10 trucks.
    *   *Realistic Goal:* If the goal is simply to extend runway relative to the *leasing* scenario, buying fails in this specific 10-truck snapshot because the upfront cash loss ($1M) outweighs the modest monthly savings (~$6k) over an 18-month horizon.
*   **Rule of Thumb:** If `Upfront Cost > (Monthly Savings × Months Until Fundraise)`, leasing preserves runway better in the short term. Here: $1,000,000 > ($6,000 × 18).

**Decision Aid**
1.  **Read:** Buying 10 trucks immediately cuts runway from ~7.1 months (lease) to ~5.1 months (buy), assuming the 30% saving is based on a $2k cost basis.
2.  **Formula:** `Runway Impact = (Upfront Cost) - (Monthly Savings × Months to Close)`. If positive, buying reduces cash available for the raise.
3.  **Threshold:** If your Series B close is >6 months away, a 5-month runway (buy scenario) triggers a "distress" signal regardless of asset quality.
4.  **Next Step:** If the fundraise timeline is uncertain, consider leasing these 10 units to preserve the $3M cash signal, or model a phased buy where capital is deployed only after term sheets are signed.

### Lane R: Securities & Fundraising Context

**Regime:** Securities Law (Series B Equity/SAFE Offering).

**Routing Required:**
The question of which approach "better positions us for valuation growth" and how the balance sheet affects investor perception involves securities disclosure and corporate finance strategy.
*   **Securities Offering:** Raising a Series B is a securities offering. How you present liabilities (leases) vs. assets (owned trucks) and cash burn rates in your Private Placement Memorandum (PPM) or pitch deck requires legal and financial professional judgment to avoid misrepresentation.
*   **Action:** Consult with your corporate counsel and a CPA before finalizing the capital allocation strategy for the raise. They must validate whether the lease obligations constitute off-balance-sheet financing that requires specific disclosure under relevant securities regulations.
