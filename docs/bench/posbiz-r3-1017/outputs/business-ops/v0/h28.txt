### Pricing Model Analysis for UrbanTwin

You are choosing between two **value metrics** (the unit you charge for). The right choice aligns your revenue with the customer's perceived value and your cost to serve, without penalizing specific city types.

#### 1. The Arithmetic of Your Current Options
Using your provided figures for a mid-sized city (250k citizens, 80 sq miles):
*   **Per Citizen:** $0.10 × 250,000 = **$25,000/year**
*   **Per Square Mile:** $400 × 80 = **$32,000/year**

**Sensitivity Check (The "Density" Problem):**
*   **High Density Scenario** (e.g., Barcelona center): 500k citizens in 20 sq miles.
    *   Per Citizen: $50,000 (Double revenue)
    *   Per Sq Mile: $8,000 (Quarter revenue)
    *   *Risk:* Per-square-mile drastically undervalues complex, dense centers where simulation utility is highest.
*   **Low Density Scenario** (e.g., suburban sprawl): 50k citizens in 100 sq miles.
    *   Per Citizen: $5,000
    *   Per Sq Mile: $40,000
    *   *Risk:* Per-citizen fails to capture value in sprawling areas; per-square-mile may price you out of rural contracts.

#### 2. Strategic Recommendation: Good-Better-Best Tiers + Hybrid Metric
Single linear metrics (purely per citizen or per mile) often fail in B2G (Business-to-Government) because they don't reflect **complexity**. A dense city center requires more compute and offers higher value (traffic/emergency simulation) than empty rural land.

**Proposed Structure:**
Adopt a **Good-Better-Best** packaging model with a **hybrid value metric** or a **base + usage** model.

*   **Option A: Base Fee + Density Multiplier**
    *   Charge a base fee per square mile (covering infrastructure).
    *   Apply a "complexity multiplier" based on population density or number of active simulation nodes (traffic lights, energy grids).
    *   *Formula:* `Price = (Base Rate × Sq Miles) + (Variable Rate × High-Density Zones)`

*   **Option B: Tiered Outcome Packages (Recommended for EU Municipalities)**
    *   **Tier 1 (Planning):** Fixed price per sq mile up to a cap. Good for rural/suburban zoning.
    *   **Tier 2 (Operations):** Includes traffic/energy simulation. Priced per "Active Simulation Unit" (e.g., per 10k citizens OR per 5 sq miles of urban core, whichever is higher).
    *   **Tier 3 (Emergency/Real-time):** Premium pricing based on critical infrastructure count (hospitals, major intersections).

#### 3. Path to €3M ARR
To reach €3M ARR in 24 months from a €1.2M cost base:
*   **Break-even:** You need ~€100k/month in recurring revenue to cover costs immediately.
*   **Target:** €250k/month average by Month 24.
*   **Unit Economics Check:** If your average contract value (ACV) is €30k (midpoint of your examples), you need **100 cities**.
    *   *Decision Aid:* If sales cycles in EU municipalities are 12–18 months, you must close ~6–8 deals *now* to hit the Year 2 target.
    *   *Threshold:* If your Customer Acquisition Cost (CAC) exceeds €15k (50% of ACV), the model is unsustainable. Track this immediately.

#### 4. Immediate Next Step
Do not set the final price yet. Run a **Van Westendorp Price Sensitivity Survey** with 5–10 pilot city planners. Ask:
1.  At what price is the platform too expensive regardless of density?
2.  At what price is it a bargain?
3.  At what price does the "per citizen" model feel unfair?
4.  At what price does the "per square mile" model feel disconnected from value?

Use their answers to define your "acceptable price band" and select the metric that minimizes friction for your densest, most valuable targets.

*Note: EU public procurement rules often require transparent, non-discriminatory pricing models; ensure your chosen metric can be justified as proportional to service usage.*
