### Lane A: Demand Forecasting Framework (Arithmetic & Structure)

Since you lack historical data, you must construct a **bottom-up adoption model** driven by your specific constraints and estimated conversion rates. Do not guess total market size; instead, calculate demand based on your reachable universe of chefs.

**1. The Funnel Formula**
Apply this structure to your own research numbers:
`Monthly Sales (kg) = (Target Chefs Contacted × Conversion Rate %) × Avg Order Volume (kg/chef)`

*   **Target Chefs Contacted:** The number of high-end restaurants you can physically reach and pitch in Month 1.
*   **Conversion Rate (%):** The percentage of pitched chefs who place an order. *Unknown input: Estimate conservatively (e.g., 2–5%) based on luxury food sales cycles, then validate via survey.*
*   **Avg Order Volume:** Likely capped by your trial offering or their menu needs (e.g., 5kg/week per chef).

**3. Data Acquisition Methods (To fill the "Unknown" slots)**
*   **Van Westendorp Price Sensitivity:** Before launching, survey 20–30 target chefs. Ask four specific questions: At what price is it too expensive? Too cheap? Expensive but worth considering? A bargain? This validates your €90/kg point and estimates purchase intent without fabricating sales figures.
*   **Pilot Cohort:** Do not forecast the whole year immediately. Secure 5–10 "launch partner" chefs with committed pre-orders. Use their actual uptake as the `Conversion Rate` and `Order Volume` inputs for the broader model.

**4. Unit Economics Check**
*   **Contribution Margin:** €90 (Price) - €40 (Variable Cost) = **€50/kg**.
*   **Break-even Volume:** If your fixed monthly costs (staff, rent, utilities) are `F`, then `Break-even kg = F ÷ 50`.
*   **Decision Threshold:** If your Base Case volume is below `Break-even kg`, you must either reduce fixed costs, increase price, or increase the assumed conversion rate before committing to full staffing.
