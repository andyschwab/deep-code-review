### Lane A: Unit Economics & Scalability Analysis

We can apply your provided numbers to compare the two models. The decision rests on your risk tolerance and growth targets, not a single "correct" answer.

**1. Direct-to-Consumer (DTC) Subscription Model**
*   **Formula:** `Annual Revenue = Total Passengers × Conversion Rate × Price Per Trip`
*   **Your Inputs:** 45,000,000 passengers; 3% conversion; $2.99/trip.
*   **Calculation:** 45,000,000 × 0.03 × $2.99 = **$4,036,500** (Top-line potential).
*   **Unit Economics Note:** This model assumes high Customer Acquisition Cost (CAC). If you must spend marketing dollars to find that 3% within the airport, your net margin shrinks.
*   **Scalability:** High upside if you expand to other airports without renegotiating base fees. However, revenue is volatile and depends entirely on user adoption at the point of need.

**2. B2B Licensing Model**
*   **Formula:** `Annual Revenue = Fixed License Fee × Number of Airports`
*   **Your Inputs:** $150,000/year (Changi only).
*   **Calculation:** **$150,000** (Guaranteed).
*   **Unit Economics Note:** CAC is likely lower (one sales cycle per airport), but the ceiling is capped by the contract.
*   **Scalability:** Linear. To match the DTC potential of ~$4M, you would need to sign roughly 27 airports at the same rate.

**Decision Aid & Thresholds**
*   **Break-even Comparison:** The DTC model generates ~27x more revenue *if* your 3% conversion and $2.99 price hold true.
*   **The Flip Input:** The variable that flips the decision is **Conversion Rate**. If conversion drops below ~0.11% (approx. 50,000 users), the DTC revenue ($2.99 × 50k = $149.5k) falls below the license fee.
*   **Rule of Thumb:** With only **6 months of runway** and a team of eight, your monthly burn is likely high. The B2B model offers certainty to extend runway immediately. The DTC model requires time to validate the 3% conversion hypothesis, which you may not have before running out of cash.
*   **Next Step:** If you pursue DTC, consider a "freemium" pilot for 30 days to instrument real conversion data before committing. If you pursue B2B, prepare a sensitivity analysis showing the airport authority how their passenger volume increases your value, justifying a higher tier.

### Lane R: Regulation & Legal Routing

Your questions regarding data privacy, consumer protection, and contract drafting fall under **Lane R**. These depend on specific statutes and legal determinations that cannot be resolved via arithmetic.

**1. Data Privacy & Consumer Protection (Singapore)**
*   **Regime:** The **Personal Data Protection Act (PDPA)** of Singapore governs the collection, use, and disclosure of personal data.
*   **Issue:** Selling passenger movement data or charging for navigation involves "purpose limitation" and "consent" obligations under the PDPA. Whether your specific data flow constitutes a breach or requires specific notification is a legal determination.
*   **Action:** You must route this to a Singapore-qualified data privacy counsel. Do not assume anonymization protects you without legal verification.

**2. Commercial Agreements**
*   **Regime:** Singapore Contract Law and specific aviation/airport bylaws.
*   **Issue:** Drafting licensing agreements or terms of service that limit liability for navigation errors (critical in an airport setting) requires professional legal structuring.
*   **Action:** **Yes, you need a lawyer.** Do not use templates for liability waivers in safety-adjacent applications.

**Summary:** Use the math above to stress-test your cash flow needs against the two revenue models. Immediately engage a Singapore law firm to address the PDPA implications and draft your commercial contracts before signing or launching.
