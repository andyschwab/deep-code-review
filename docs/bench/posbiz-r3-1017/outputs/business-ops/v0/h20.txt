### Lane A: Unit Economics & Pricing Analysis (Arithmetic)

We can model the immediate impact on your contribution margin and the theoretical impact on Lifetime Value (LTV) based on your inputs. We cannot calculate the exact retention impact without your specific price elasticity data, but we can establish the break-even retention rates required for each option to maintain current profit levels.

**Current State (Baseline):**
*   **Price:** $60
*   **COGS:** $25
*   **Contribution Margin (CM):** $35 per unit
*   **Monthly Churn:** 5% (implies an average customer lifespan of 20 months, assuming constant churn: $1 \div 0.05$)
*   **Current LTV (Gross Margin):** $35 \times 20 = $700

**Option 1: Increase Price to $70**
*   **New CM:** $70 - $25 = $45
*   **Required Retention Logic:** To maintain the same total monthly profit from the existing base, you can afford to lose subscribers up to the point where the gain in margin per user offsets the loss of users.
*   **Break-even Churn Calculation:** If $P_{old} \times M_{old} = P_{new} \times M_{new}$ (where M is margin), then $35 \times 5,000 = 45 \times N_{new}$. You need 3,889 subscribers to match current total margin. This implies you could survive a one-time drop of ~22% of your base.
*   **LTV Sensitivity:** If churn remains at 5%, New LTV = $45 \times 20 = $900. However, if price sensitivity causes churn to rise to 8% (lifespan 12.5 months), New LTV = $45 \times 12.5 = $562.50.
*   **Decision Aid:** The "flip" input here is **churn elasticity**. If raising the price increases monthly churn above ~7.8%, your LTV decreases compared to the current state.

**Option 2: Reduce Dosage by 20% (Maintain $60 Price)**
*   **Estimated New COGS:** Assuming linear cost scaling, a 20% reduction in ingredients reduces the $25 COGS by $5 (approx). New COGS ≈ $20.
*   **New CM:** $60 - $20 = $40.
*   **LTV Sensitivity:** If churn holds at 5%, New LTV = $40 \times 20 = $800.
*   **Risk Factor:** This option relies on "shrinkflation" not triggering voluntary churn. If customers perceive lower value and churn rises to 6.5% (lifespan ~15.4 months), New LTV = $40 \times 15.4 = $616.
*   **Decision Aid:** Compare the perceived value loss. If dosage reduction causes churn to exceed ~6.25%, the price increase (Option 1) becomes mathematically superior, provided the price hike doesn't spike churn beyond ~7.8%.

**Next Step:** Before deciding, run a Van Westendorp price sensitivity survey or A/B test the $70 price on a small segment (e.g., new sign-ups only) to observe actual churn behavior before applying it to the full 5,000.

### Lane R: Regulatory & Compliance (Route to Professional)

**Regime Identified:** Australian Consumer Law (ACL) and Contract Law.

**Analysis:**
You asked about notification periods for formulation changes or price hikes and whether you need a legal professional.
1.  **Formulation Changes:** Under the ACL, changing the ingredients or dosage of a product sold under an existing subscription may be considered a "variation of terms" or potentially misleading conduct if the product no longer matches its description or the consumer's reasonable expectations. Specific notification requirements often depend on the terms of your existing contract and whether the change is "material."
2.  **Price Hikes:** Increasing prices for existing subscribers mid-contract is generally not permitted unless your current Terms of Service (ToS) explicitly allow for price variations with specific notice. Even with a clause, the notice period and method must be fair and compliant with the ACL.
3.  **Legal Review:** **Yes, you must engage an Australian legal professional.** Determining whether your current ToS permits these changes, what constitutes a "material" change requiring consent vs. notice, and drafting the compliant notification language is a legal determination.

**Action:** Do not implement either change until a qualified Australian lawyer reviews your current subscription agreements and advises on the specific notice periods and consent mechanisms required under the ACL for your specific contract structure.
