### Lane A: Profitability & Runway Arithmetic (Apply)

Your current unit economics and runway are sensitive to fuel volatility. Below is the applied math using your provided inputs.

**1. Current Baseline (with hedging/stable costs)**
*   **Monthly Volume:** 10,000 doses
*   **Margin per Dose:** $1.20
*   **Total Monthly Contribution:** $12,000 (10,000 × $1.20)
*   **Monthly Burn:** $45,000
*   **Net Monthly Burn:** $33,000 ($45,000 burn − $12,000 contribution)
*   **Runway:** ~6.1 months ($200,000 cash ÷ $33,000 net burn)

**2. Fuel Volatility Sensitivity (Without Hedging)**
Fuel is 40% of operating expenses. Assuming your $45,000 burn includes fixed and variable costs, we must isolate the variable fuel portion to model the impact.
*   *Assumption:* If "Operating Expenses" equals your Burn Rate, then Fuel Cost = 40% of $45,000 = $18,000/month.
*   *Formula:* `New Margin = Current Margin − (Fuel Cost Increase per Dose)`
*   *Fuel Cost per Dose:* $18,000 ÷ 10,000 doses = $1.80/dose.

**Scenario Analysis:**
*   **Scenario A (Fuel +20%):** Fuel cost rises by $0.36/dose ($1.80 × 0.20).
    *   New Margin: $1.20 − $0.36 = $0.84.
    *   New Contribution: $8,400.
    *   New Net Burn: $36,600.
    *   **New Runway:** ~5.5 months.
*   **Scenario B (Fuel +50%):** Fuel cost rises by $0.90/dose.
    *   New Margin: $1.20 − $0.90 = $0.30.
    *   New Contribution: $3,000.
    *   New Net Burn: $42,000.
    *   **New Runway:** ~4.8 months.
*   **Scenario C (Break-even Point):**
    *   To wipe out profitability entirely (Margin = $0), fuel costs must rise by exactly $1.20/dose.
    *   This represents a **67% increase** in your fuel bill ($1.20 ÷ $1.80).

**Decision Aid:**
*   **Read:** At current margins, a 50% fuel spike reduces runway by over a month; a 67% spike eliminates contribution margin entirely.
*   **Flip Input:** The critical variable is the **fuel price increase percentage**. If you expect increases >40%, hedging or price adjustments are mathematically urgent.
*   **Threshold Rule:** If `LTV:CAC` or `Contribution Margin` approaches zero due to variable costs, stop scaling volume until the cost structure is fixed.
*   **Next Step:** If diesel futures indicate >20% volatility, consider testing a "fuel surcharge" clause on 10% of new contracts this week to measure client resistance before renewing hedges.
