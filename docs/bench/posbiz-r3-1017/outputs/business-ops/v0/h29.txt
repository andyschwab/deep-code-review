### Lane A: Unit Economics & Pricing Arithmetic

**1. The Subsidy Recovery Model**
To recover the reader subsidy within 12 months, we calculate the required monthly contribution from cartridges.
*   **Reader Subsidy:** Manufacturing Cost ($3,200) − Sale Price ($8,500) = **−$5,300** (You are currently making a gross profit of $5,300 per reader, not subsidizing it).
    *   *Correction:* Your prompt states you want to "recover the reader subsidy," but your current numbers show the reader is already highly profitable ($8,500 price vs $3,200 cost). If your strategy is to *lower* the reader price to drive adoption (e.g., sell at cost $3,200), the subsidy to recover is **$3,200**.
    *   *Scenario Assumption:* We will assume you lower the reader price to **$3,200** (break-even hardware) to lock in cartridge sales, creating a $3,200 subsidy to recover.

*   **Clinic Volume:** 50 tests/day × 30 days = **1,500 cartridges/month**.
*   **Recovery Target:** $3,200 subsidy ÷ 12 months = **$266.67/month** required contribution.
*   **Required Margin per Cartridge:** $266.67 ÷ 1,500 tests = **$0.18 per cartridge**.

**Break-Even Cartridge Price (Hardware Subsidy Only):**
*   Formula: `Cartridge Price = Manufacturing Cost + Required Margin`
*   Calculation: $4.00 + $0.18 = **$4.18**.
*   *Result:* At **$4.18**, you recover the hardware cost in 12 months. Any price above this contributes to overhead and profit.

**2. Incorporating Churn (15% Annual)**
Churn reduces the total volume sold over the year. A 15% annual churn implies an average monthly retention rate of ~98.7%. Over 12 months, a clinic buys roughly 16,800 tests instead of 18,000 if they stayed perfectly constant (simplified linear approximation for sensitivity: assume ~7.5% volume loss on average over the year).
*   Adjusted Volume Estimate: ~1,440 cartridges/month (average).
*   Revised Required Margin: $266.67 ÷ 1,440 = **$0.19**.
*   **Revised Break-Even Price:** **$4.19**.

**3. Sensitivity Analysis (Decision Aid)**
| Cartridge Price | Margin/Test | Monthly Contribution (1,500 tests) | Months to Recover $3,200 Subsidy |
| :--- | :--- | :--- | :--- |

**One-Line Read:** At a volume of 50 tests/day, pricing the cartridge at **$4.20** recovers a cost-price reader in under a year; raising the price to **$5.00** recovers it in ~2 months, significantly de-risking the churn variable.

**The Flip Input:** The **daily test volume** is the critical variable. If volume drops below **22 tests/day**, even an $8.00 cartridge fails to recover a $3,200 hardware subsidy within 12 months.

**Thresholds (Rules of Thumb):**
*   If LTV:CAC < 3:1, reconsider the subsidy depth.
*   If payback period > 12 months, the hardware discount is too aggressive for your cash flow needs ($600k funding, positive cash flow target next quarter).

### Strategic Note on Upfront Costs
Since your current reader price ($8,500) is high for cash-sensitive clinics, consider a "Rental-to-Own" structure: Charge $0 upfront for the reader, lock in a 24-month contract at **$6.00/cartridge** with a minimum monthly volume commitment. This removes the upfront barrier while securing higher lifetime margin.
