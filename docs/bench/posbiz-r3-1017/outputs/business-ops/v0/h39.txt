### Lane A: Arithmetic & Unit Economics (Apply)

**1. Direct Sale Model (Immediate Cash)**
*   **Formula:** `Gross Profit per Unit = Sale Price − COGS`
*   **Your Inputs:** $110,000 (Price) − $85,000 (Cost) = **$25,000 Gross Profit/unit**.
*   **Cash Impact:** Selling 10 units generates $1.1M revenue, $250k gross profit. This adds immediately to your $1.2M cash reserve.
*   **Decision Aid:** This model maximizes immediate liquidity and minimizes balance sheet risk. The "flip input" here is volume; you need high sales velocity to grow, as you capture no long-term value per unit.

**2. PPA Model (Asset Ownership)**
*   **Formula:** `Annual Net Cash Flow = (kWh Generated × $0.18) − Maintenance Cost`
*   **Unknown Variable:** You must determine `kWh Generated` based on your specific turbine efficiency and urban wind data. Without this, IRR cannot be calculated.
*   **Sensitivity Check:**
    *   If Maintenance = $4,000/yr: Net Flow = (Revenue) − $4,000.
    *   If Maintenance = $8,000/yr (high access difficulty): Net Flow drops by $4,000/yr.
*   **Upfront Capital Requirement:** You must fund the $85,000 cost per unit. For 10 units, that is $850,000.
    *   Your Cash: $1.2M.
    *   Remaining Cash after 10 units: $350,000 (before operating expenses).
    *   Debt Capacity Used: You could leverage the $500k debt to preserve cash, but this introduces interest costs which lower the IRR.
*   **Decision Aid:** The PPA model flips from profitable to loss-making if maintenance exceeds your revenue per turbine. The "flip input" is the **maintenance cost vs. energy yield**. If access costs rise, the 15-year asset becomes a liability.

**3. Investor Attractiveness (Qualitative Application)**
*   **Series A Preference:** Investors wary of "asset-heavy" businesses typically prefer the **Direct Sale** model.
    *   *Reasoning:* Direct sale offers scalable software-like margins (once manufactured) without tying up capital in depreciating hardware on balance sheets.
