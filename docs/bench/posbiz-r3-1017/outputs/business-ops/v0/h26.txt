**Lane R: Regulation-Dependent (Route to Professional)**

Your question regarding whether the $1.8 million in pre-order deposits counts as taxable income in the current fiscal year or can be deferred is a **tax regulation question**. It depends entirely on your entity's accounting method (cash vs. accrual), specific IRS revenue procedures regarding advance payments for goods, and potentially California state tax codes.

**The Regime:** This falls under **US Federal Income Tax (IRS)** regulations concerning "Advance Payments" and **Revenue Recognition** principles (ASC 606 for GAAP, which often influences tax positions, though tax and book treatment can differ).

**Why this cannot be answered with arithmetic:**
Determining if the funds are "earned" upon collection or delivery is a legal determination.
- If you are on a **cash basis**, the IRS generally taxes income when received, though specific deferral elections (such as under Rev. Proc. 2004-34) may allow you to postpone recognition until the next tax year or until delivery, depending on the terms.
- If you are on an **accrual basis**, recognition usually ties to when the performance obligation is satisfied (delivery), but strict rules apply to how deposits are recorded and reported.
- **California State Tax** may have different conformity rules regarding federal deferrals.

A wrong conclusion here creates immediate liability. If the IRS deems this taxable now and you have spent the cash on development, you could face penalties and interest that exceed your remaining runway.

**Action Required:**
You must engage a **CPA or tax attorney** immediately. Do not rely on general guidelines for this specific $1.8M event.

While the *tax* treatment is a legal question, the *accounting* entry typically involves recording the cash as a **Liability (Deferred Revenue)** on the balance sheet, not as Revenue on the income statement, until the product ships. However, **taxable income** does not always match **book income**. Only a professional can confirm if you can legally defer the *tax* payment to match the shipping date.
