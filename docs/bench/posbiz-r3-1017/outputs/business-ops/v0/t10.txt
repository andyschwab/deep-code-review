### Revenue Impact Analysis (Lane A: Arithmetic)

**Current State Baseline**
*   **Revenue:** $118,800/mo (1,200 users × $99).
*   **Variable Cost:** $14,400/mo (1,200 users × $12).
*   **Contribution Margin:** $104,400/mo.

**Proposed Scenario Modeling**
We analyze the impact of losing 240 high-volume users and gaining 400 low-volume users under the new $0.50/document model.

1.  **Lost Revenue (Churn):**
    *   240 users × $99/mo = **$23,760 loss**.
2.  **Gained Revenue (New Users):**
    *   400 users × 50 docs × $0.50/doc = **$10,000 gain**.
3.  **Net Revenue Change:**
    *   $10,000 (gain) − $23,760 (loss) = **−$13,760/mo**.
    *   *Result:* Total monthly revenue drops to ~$105,040.

**Cost & Margin Sensitivity**
While revenue drops, your variable costs also shift.
*   **Cost Savings (Churned Users):** 240 users × $12 = $2,880 saved.
*   **New Costs (New Users):** 400 users × 50 docs. *Assumption:* If server cost is strictly per-user ($12), cost is $4,800. If strictly per-document, we lack the per-doc cost factor. Assuming the $12 is per active user regardless of volume:
    *   New Variable Cost: 400 × $12 = $4,800.
    *   Net Cost Change: $4,800 (new) − $2,880 (saved) = +$1,920 increase.
*   **Net Contribution Impact:**
    *   Revenue Loss ($13,760) − Cost Increase ($1,920) = **−$15,680/mo** in contribution margin.

**Decision Aid**
*   **Read:** Under these specific assumptions, the swap destroys value; you lose ~13% of top-line revenue and ~15% of contribution margin.
*   **Break-Even Formula:** To offset the $23,760 loss from churn, new users must generate equivalent revenue.
    *   `Required Docs per New User = Lost Revenue / (New Users × Price per Doc)`
    *   $23,760 / (400 × $0.50) = **118.8 documents/month**.
    *   *Insight:* Your projected 50 docs/user is less than half the volume needed just to break even on revenue, ignoring cost increases.
*   **Threshold Rule:** If the average new user analyzes <119 documents, this specific swap reduces total revenue.
*   **Next Step:** Before changing pricing, run a "Van Westendorp" style survey or A/B test on a small segment to verify if 400 users will actually sign up at $0.50/doc, and precisely measure the document count of your current top 10% to see if a hybrid cap might retain them.

### Required Data Segmentation

To refine this model, extract the following from your database:
1.  **Document Distribution Histogram:** Plot users (X-axis) vs. documents analyzed/month (Y-axis) to identify the exact cutoff where the "top 10%" begins and their average volume.
2.  **Cost-to-Serve Ratio:** Determine if your $12/user cost is fixed per account or variable per document. If it scales with volume, the margin erosion on heavy users under a flat fee might be worse than calculated, potentially justifying a change despite revenue loss.
3.  **Elasticity Clusters:** Segment current users by "docs per dollar" to identify who is currently over-paying (subsidizing heavy users) and might welcome usage-based pricing.

*Note: This model assumes the $12 cost is per active user; if it scales per document, the financial impact on heavy users currently may be significantly negative.*
*Ensure any pricing change complies with existing subscriber contracts; modifying terms for existing users often requires legal review (Lane R).*
