### Lane A: Unit Economics & Break-Even Analysis
We can apply your numbers to compare the two fee structures. The decision on which maximizes retention or minimizes litigation risk is yours, but the arithmetic below shows the sensitivity.

**Assumptions:**
*   Gross Sales: $45,000/month
*   Food Cost: 30% ($13,500)
*   Labor Savings: $8,000 (treated as a benefit offsetting other labor costs, not revenue)
*   **Model A (Royalty):** 6% of Gross Sales
*   **Model B (Fixed Fee):** $2,500/month

**Monthly Fee Calculation:**
*   **Model A:** $45,000 × 0.06 = **$2,700**
*   **Model B:** **$2,500**
*   *Current Difference:* Model A costs the franchisee $200 more per month at current sales levels.

**Break-Even Sales Point (Where fees are equal):**
To find the sales volume where the 6% royalty equals the $2,500 fixed fee:
$$ \text{Break-Even Sales} = \frac{\text{Fixed Fee}}{\text{Royalty Rate}} = \frac{2,500}{0.06} = \$41,667 $$

**Sensitivity Analysis (Economic Downturn Scenario):**
*   **If Sales Drop to $35,000:**
    *   Model A (6%): $2,100 (Fee decreases automatically)
    *   Model B (Fixed): $2,500 (Fee remains static)
    *   *Impact:* Under Model B, the franchisee pays $400 more than under Model A during a downturn.
*   **If Sales Rise to $55,000:**
    *   Model A (6%): $3,300
    *   Model B (Fixed): $2,500
    *   *Impact:* Under Model B, the franchisee saves $800 compared to Model A.

**Decision Aid:**
At current volumes ($45k), the Fixed Fee is slightly cheaper for franchisees. However, the **$41,667 sales level** is the flip point. Below this, the Fixed Fee becomes a heavier burden relative to revenue, potentially increasing insolvency risk during downturns. A common rule of thumb is that variable costs (royalties) align incentives better during volatility, while fixed costs provide predictability. If your primary goal is franchisee solvency during dips, the variable model mathematically reduces their cash outflow when revenue contracts.

### Lane R: Legal & Litigation Risk (Route to Professional)
Your question regarding which structure is "more defensible if a franchisee fails and sues" and the implications for "franchisee retention rates" involves legal determinations that must be routed to qualified counsel.

*   **Regime:** Franchise Law (FTC Franchise Rule, state-specific registration states like Illinois, Indiana, Michigan, etc., given your Midwest footprint) and Contract Law.
*   **Issue:** Determining whether a fee structure constitutes an "unfair practice," violates implied covenants of good faith, or triggers specific disclosure requirements in the Franchise Disclosure Document (FDD) is a legal conclusion.
*   **Action:** You must consult a franchise attorney to review your FDD Item 6 (Fees) and the specific language in your franchise agreements. An attorney can advise on how courts in your operating states have historically viewed fixed tech fees versus royalties in the context of franchisee failure and litigation defense. Do not rely on arithmetic alone to assess litigation defensibility.
