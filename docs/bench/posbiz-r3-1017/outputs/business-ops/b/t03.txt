### Net Profit Analysis: First-Come-First-Served vs. Lottery

To determine the higher net profit, we must apply your specific numbers to two scenarios. The decision rests on whether the probability of a site crash outweighs the potential loss of impulse conversions in a lottery.

**Common Inputs:**
*   **Inventory:** 2,500 pairs
*   **Price:** $220/pair
*   **Variable Cost:** $65/pair
*   **Contribution Margin:** $155/pair ($220 - $65)
*   **Max Potential Revenue (if all sell):** $550,000
*   **Max Potential Gross Profit:** $387,500 (2,500 × $155)

#### Scenario A: First-Come-First-Served (FCFS)
*   **Risk:** Site crash.
*   **Estimated Cost of Failure:** $10,000 (lost sales + fixes).
*   **Platform Upgrade Cost:** $5,000 (optional mitigation).
*   **Conversion Assumption:** High urgency typically drives higher conversion rates among the 15,000 interested buyers. Let's assume a conversion rate of $C_{fcfs}$.
*   **Formula:**
    $$Net Profit_{FCFS} = (\min(2,500, 15,000 \times C_{fcfs}) \times \$155) - \$5,000 (\text{if upgraded}) - (\$10,000 \times P_{crash})$$
    *(Where $P_{crash}$ is the probability of a crash without the upgrade, or near zero with it.)*

**Application:**
If you **do not** upgrade and the site crashes ($P_{crash} \approx 1$), you lose the $10,000 estimated cost. If the crash prevents *any* sales, profit is -$10,000. If it slows sales but you still sell 2,500 pairs, profit is $377,500 ($387,500 - $10,000).
If you **do** upgrade ($5,000 cost), you mitigate the crash risk. Assuming you sell all 2,500 pairs due to high urgency:
$$Net Profit = \$387,500 - \$5,000 = \$382,500$$

#### Scenario B: Lottery System
*   **Risk:** Dampened urgency leading to lower conversion or drop-off.
*   **Cost:** Likely lower server load, potentially avoiding the $5,000 upgrade and $10,000 crash risk.
*   **Conversion Assumption:** Urgency is lower. Let's assume a conversion rate of $C_{lottery}$.
*   **Formula:**
    $$Net Profit_{Lottery} = (\min(2,500, 15,000 \times C_{lottery}) \times \$155) - \text{Platform Costs}$$

**Application:**
If the lottery reduces the effective pool of buyers such that you sell only 2,000 pairs (due to drop-off):
$$Net Profit = (2,000 \times \$155) = \$310,000$$
*Comparison:* In this specific hypothetical, the FCFS model with an upgrade ($382,500) yields $72,500 more than a lottery with significant drop-off ($310,000). However, if FCFS crashes without an upgrade and sells 0 pairs, the Lottery wins by default.

**Decision Thresholds:**
1.  **Break-even on Upgrade:** The $5,000 upgrade is mathematically justified if it prevents a crash that would cost >$5,000 in lost margin or fixes. Since the estimated crash cost is $10,000, the upgrade has a positive expected value if the crash probability is >50%.
2.  **Conversion Sensitivity:** How much can conversion drop in a lottery before it underperforms FCFS?
    *   FCFS (with upgrade) Net: $382,500.
    *   Lottery Net needed to match: $382,500.
    *   Required Lottery Sales: $382,500 / $155 ≈ 2,468 pairs.
    *   **Insight:** The lottery must maintain a 98.7% sell-through rate (2,468/2,500) to match the upgraded FCFS model. If the lottery causes more than ~32 unsold pairs compared to FCFS, FCFS is more profitable.
