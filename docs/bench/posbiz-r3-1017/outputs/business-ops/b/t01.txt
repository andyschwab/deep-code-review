Below is the unit economics analysis applying your provided numbers and Ravi's estimated duty range (4%–12%) to illustrate potential margin erosion. You must insert the confirmed duty rate once obtained from counsel.

**Inputs:**
*   Selling Price (P): $400
*   Manufacturing Cost: $210
*   Freight Cost: $18
*   Estimated Duty Rate (d): 4% to 12% (Applied to customs value, assumed here as Manufacturing Cost + Freight for conservative estimation, though local rules vary on duty base).

**Formulas:**
1.  `Duty Cost = (Manufacturing Cost + Freight) × d`
2.  `Total Cost of Goods Sold (COGS) = Manufacturing Cost + Freight + Duty Cost`
3.  `Gross Margin % = (Price − Total COGS) ÷ Price`

**Scenario 1: Low-End Duty (4%)**
*   Duty Cost: ($210 + $18) × 0.04 = $9.12
*   Total COGS: $210 + $18 + $9.12 = $237.12
*   Gross Margin: ($400 − $237.12) ÷ $400 = **40.78%**

**Scenario 2: High-End Duty (12%)**
*   Duty Cost: ($210 + $18) × 0.12 = $27.36
*   Total COGS: $210 + $18 + $27.36 = $255.36
*   Gross Margin: ($400 − $255.36) ÷ $400 = **36.16%**

**Analysis vs. Target:**
Your target gross margin is **45%**.
*   At 4% duty, you miss the target by ~4.2 percentage points.
*   At 12% duty, you miss the target by ~8.8 percentage points.
*   **Break-Even Price for 45% Margin:** To achieve a 45% margin at the worst-case duty (12%), the required price is:
    `Required Price = Total COGS (at 12%) ÷ (1 − 0.45)`
    `Required Price = $255.36 ÷ 0.55 ≈ $464.29`
