### Revenue Model Comparison (Per Studio, 5 Headsets)

**Baseline Metrics:**
*   **Classes/Week:** 40
*   **Avg Attendees/Class:** 8
*   **Price/Class:** $25
*   **Potential Weekly Revenue (100% util):** 40 classes × 8 attendees × $25 = $8,000
*   **Potential Monthly Revenue (100% util):** $8,000 × 4.33 weeks ≈ $34,640

**Model A: Flat Licensing Fee**
*   **Formula:** $150 × 5 headsets
*   **Monthly Revenue:** **$750** (Fixed regardless of utilization)

**Model B: 12% Revenue Share**
*   **Formula:** (Potential Monthly Revenue × Utilization Rate) × 12%
*   **At 70% Utilization:** ($34,640 × 0.70) × 0.12 = $24,248 × 0.12 ≈ **$2,910**
*   **At 90% Utilization:** ($34,640 × 0.90) × 0.12 = $31,176 × 0.12 ≈ **$3,741**

**Comparison:**
At both 70% and 90% utilization, the **12% Revenue Share model generates significantly higher revenue** ($2,910–$3,741) compared to the Flat Fee ($750). The flat fee would only be superior if the studio's generated revenue dropped below ~$6,250/month (approx. 18% utilization).

### Break-Even Utilization Analysis

To find where Revenue Share equals Flat Fee:
1.  **Target Revenue:** $750 (Flat Fee amount)
2.  **Required Gross Studio Revenue:** $750 ÷ 0.12 = $6,250
3.  **Required Utilization Rate:** $6,250 ÷ $34,640 (Max Potential) ≈ **18%**

**Result:** The revenue share model becomes more profitable than the flat fee at any utilization rate **above 18%**. Given your scenarios of 70% and 90%, the revenue share is mathematically superior in both cases based on the provided inputs.

### Operational Context (Monthly Expenses: $60,000)
*   **Flat Fee Model:** You need **80 studios** ($60,000 ÷ $750) to break even on operations.
*   **Revenue Share Model (at 70% util):** You need **~21 studios** ($60,000 ÷ $2,910) to break even.
*   **Revenue Share Model (at 90% util):** You need **~16 studios** ($60,000 ÷ $3,741) to break even.

### Data Requests for Pilot Validation

To validate these projections before committing to a model, request the following specific data from pilot studios:
1.  **Actual vs. Capacity Attendance:** Request weekly logs showing actual attendees versus maximum capacity per class to calculate *real* utilization rates, not just estimated percentages.
2.  **Attribution Rate:** Determine what percentage of bookings are actually generated *through* your software versus walk-ins or other channels (since you only share revenue on software-generated bookings).
3.  **Churn/No-Show Rates:** High no-show rates might affect whether studios honor payments on booked-but-empty slots in a revenue share model.
4.  **Seasonality Trends:** Weekly attendance data over at least 8 weeks to identify if the 70-90% range is stable or fluctuates wildly.

**Note:** The hardware margin ($500/unit) is separate from these recurring revenue calculations. Ensure your cash flow covers the $60k ops expenses while waiting for recurring revenue to ramp up.
