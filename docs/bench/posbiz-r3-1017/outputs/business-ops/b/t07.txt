### Lane A: Unit Economics & Pricing Sensitivity

**Current Winter Margin (Status Quo)**
*   **Formula:** `Margin per lb = Price - Cost`
*   **Inputs:** $4.50 (Fixed Price) - $3.90 (Winter Cost) = **$0.60/lb**
*   **Total Monthly Winter Margin:** $0.60 × 8,000 lbs = **$4,800**
*   *Observation:* While not "near zero" in absolute terms, this represents an 87% drop from your standard margin ($1.70/lb), significantly impacting cash flow relative to fixed overheads.

**Scenario: $0.60 Price Increase ($5.10/lb)**
*   **New Margin per lb:** $5.10 - $3.90 = **$1.20/lb**
*   **Volume Sensitivity (High Elasticity Assumption):**
    *   If demand is highly elastic, a price increase causes a proportionally larger drop in volume.
    *   **Break-Even Volume Drop:** To maintain the current $4,800 total margin with the new $1.20/lb margin, you can afford to sell: `$4,800 / $1.20 = 4,000 lbs`.
    *   **Implication:** You could lose up to **50% of your volume** (dropping from 8,000 to 4,000 lbs) and still match your current winter profitability.
    *   **Risk:** If volume drops by more than 50%, total profit decreases. If volume drops by less than 50%, total profit increases.

**Decision Aid**
1.  **Read:** A $0.60 hike doubles your per-unit winter margin, allowing you to sustain profitability even if sales volume is cut in half.
2.  **Flip Input:** The critical variable is your specific **price elasticity coefficient**. If a 13% price hike ($0.60/$4.50) causes a >50% volume loss, the hike reduces total profit.
3.  **Threshold Rule:** If historical data or buyer feedback suggests volume loss will exceed 50%, a blanket hike is dangerous; consider a targeted surcharge instead.
4.  **Next Step:** Before the next cycle, present a "Cost-Pass-Through" model to buyers showing the 40% energy spike, offering a temporary **Winter Energy Surcharge** (e.g., $0.40/lb) rather than a permanent base price hike, which may face lower resistance.

### Competitor Cost Benchmarks for Negotiation

To justify a price adjustment to procurement, do not share your internal margins. Instead, gather data on these external cost drivers to frame the hike as a market-wide necessity:
1.  **Regional Commercial Electricity Rates (kWh):** Specifically winter rates in the Midwest vs. sun-belt competitors (e.g., California, Arizona). If your energy cost per kWh is higher or your usage intensity (kWh/lb) is higher due to climate, this validates the surcharge.
2.  **Heating Degree Days (HDD) Impact:** Data showing the correlation between Chicago's winter temperatures and increased HVAC loads compared to other growing regions.
3.  **Freight/Logistics Fuel Surcharges:** If competitors are shipping from afar, their fuel surcharges might be rising too. Compare your local production advantage against their rising transport costs.
4.  **Certified Sustainable Energy Premiums:** If competitors use renewable credits that cost more, benchmark that premium.

**Note:** Since your current contracts are fixed until March, any price change requires mutual agreement or must wait for renewal; attempting to unilaterally alter fixed contracts is a legal matter (Lane R).
**Note:** "High elasticity" is an assumption; validate this with small-scale tests or buyer interviews before applying to the full 8,000 lbs.
