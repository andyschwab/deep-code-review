### Unit Economics and Pricing Strategy Analysis

**Current Winter Margin Impact (January)**
Using your provided figures, the winter margin compression is severe under fixed contracts.
*   **Formula:** `Contribution Margin = Price − Variable Cost`
*   **Current Winter Reality:** $4.50 (Price) − $3.90 (Winter Cost) = **$0.60/lb**.
*   **Monthly Winter Contribution:** $0.60 × 8,000 lbs = **$4,800**.
*   **Comparison to Normal:** Normal margin is $1.70/lb ($13,600/month). You are losing **$8,800/month** in contribution during winter spikes compared to your baseline.

**Impact of $0.60 Price Increase with High Elasticity**
You asked to analyze a price hike to $5.10/lb assuming "high" demand elasticity.
*   **New Unit Margin:** $5.10 − $3.90 = **$1.20/lb** (doubling your per-unit winter margin).
*   **The Elasticity Risk:** "High elasticity" means a small price increase causes a disproportionate drop in volume.
    *   If elasticity is **-2.0** (a standard proxy for "high" in commodities), a 13.3% price increase ($0.60/$4.50) could reduce volume by ~26.6%.
    *   **Projected Volume:** 8,000 lbs × (1 − 0.266) ≈ **5,872 lbs**.
    *   **New Total Contribution:** 5,872 lbs × $1.20 = **$7,046**.
*   **Result:** Even with a significant volume drop, the total contribution ($7,046) exceeds the current fixed-price contribution ($4,800). However, if elasticity is extreme (e.g., -4.0), volume could drop >50%, reducing total contribution below the current $4,800 baseline.
*   **Decision Threshold:** You break even on the *total* contribution if volume drops no more than **50%**. If you lose more than half your customers, the hike destroys value.

**Strategic Options for Next Cycle**
1.  **Seasonal Surcharge (Energy Pass-Through):** Instead of raising the base price, propose a "Winter Energy Adjustment" clause triggered when utility rates exceed a specific index. This frames the hike as a cost-recovery mechanism rather than a margin grab, which procurement teams often accept more readily if tied to transparent data.
2.  **Good-Better-Best Tiering:** Offer a lower-priced "standard" mix (lower yield crops) and a premium "winter-fresh" tier at $5.10+. This segments buyers by price sensitivity.

**Competitor Cost Benchmarks to Gather**
To justify a hike to procurement, do not share your internal costs. Instead, gather external data points that validate the *market* cost floor:
*   **Regional Energy Rates:** Public utility commercial rates for Illinois industrial users in Q1 vs. Q3.
*   **Competitor Sourcing Mix:** Are competitors shipping from warm climates (lower lighting costs) or also using vertical farms? If they are field-grown, their winter costs may be lower, making a surcharge harder to sell.
*   **Freight Fuel Surcharges:** If local competitors also face high logistics costs, bundle your argument with broader supply chain inflation.

**Next Step:** Before approaching buyers, calculate your specific break-even volume at the $5.10 price point. If your contracts allow, propose a pilot surcharge for January only to test elasticity without locking in a permanent base price increase.
