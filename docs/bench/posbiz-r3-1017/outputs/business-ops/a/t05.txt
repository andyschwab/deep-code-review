### Lane A: Unit Economics & Runway Analysis

**1. Current Break-Even Point (Monthly)**
To cover your fixed monthly burn of $90,000 using only the contribution from converted districts:
*   **Revenue per converted district:** $800/month.
*   **Variable cost assumption:** The $40/month cost applies per *active school*. Without the number of schools per district, we cannot calculate the exact net contribution per district.
*   **Scenario A (Best Case, ignoring variable costs):** If variable costs are negligible or covered by the $800 fee:
    *   Formula: `Break-Even Districts = Monthly Burn ÷ Revenue per District`
    *   Calculation: $90,000 ÷ $800 = **112.5 districts**.
    *   *Reality Check:* You currently have 500 trial districts. At a 4% conversion rate, you expect **20 converting districts** (500 × 0.04).
    *   *Gap:* You are generating ~$16,000/month in revenue against $90,000 in burn. You are covering only ~18% of your costs with current conversion logic.

**2. Path to 18-Month Runway**
*   **Current Runway:** $800,000 cash ÷ ($90,000 burn - $16,000 revenue) = $800,000 ÷ $74,000 ≈ **10.8 months**.
*   **Target Net Burn:** To achieve 18 months, your net monthly burn must be: $800,000 ÷ 18 ≈ **$44,444**.
*   **Required Monthly Revenue:** $90,000 (gross burn) - $44,444 (target net burn) = **$45,556**.
*   **Required Converting Districts (ignoring variable costs):** $45,556 ÷ $800 ≈ **57 districts**.
*   **Required Conversion Rate:** 57 districts ÷ 500 trial districts = **11.4%**.
    *   *Analysis:* You must nearly triple your conversion rate from 4% to 11.4% to hit an 18-month runway without raising capital or cutting fixed costs.
*   **Acquisition Cost (CAC) Impact:** CAC ($1,200) is a sunk cost for the 500 existing districts. Reducing future CAC does not extend the runway on the *current* cash reserve unless it reduces the *future* burn rate by halting new trials. If you stop acquiring new trials immediately, CAC is irrelevant to the 18-month calculation. If you continue trials, you must lower CAC to reduce the monthly cash outflow added to the $90k burn.

**3. Decision Aid & Next Steps**
*   **Read:** You need 57 paying districts (11.4% conversion) to survive 18 months; current trajectory yields 20 districts and ~11 months of runway.
*   **Flip Input:** The critical unknown is **schools per district**. If the average district has >10 schools, the $40/school cost ($400+/district) drastically lowers your margin, requiring even higher conversion.
*   **Threshold Rule:** If you cannot identify a path to 11% conversion within 60 days, the current model is insolvent before 18 months.
*   **Next Step:** Immediately instrument trial data to segment districts by "activation depth." Stop broad acquisition until the conversion model is proven.

**4. Engagement Metrics to Track**
To predict conversion, track leading indicators correlated with value realization:
*   **Teacher Activation Rate:** % of invited teachers who log in and create content within 14 days.
*   **Student Reach:** % of total district student population actively using the platform weekly.
*   **Feature Depth:** Frequency of using core "sticky" features (e.g., grading, analytics) vs. passive viewing.
*   **Admin Engagement:** Number of logins by district administrators (often the economic buyer).

*Note: Variable costs per district must be calculated (Schools/District × $40) to refine the break-even point above. If this number is high, the required conversion rate exceeds 11.4%.*
