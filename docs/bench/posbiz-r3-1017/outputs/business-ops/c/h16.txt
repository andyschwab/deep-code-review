### Lane A: Liquidity Arithmetic & Structure

**The Cash Gap Calculation**
Your constraint is a timing mismatch between cash outflows and inflows.
*   **Total Production Cost:** 200 units × $30,000 = **$6,000,000**.
*   **Current Working Capital:** **$1,500,000**.
*   **Operating Burn During Production:** Assuming a 3-month production/delivery window, burn is 3 × $200,000 = **$600,000**.
*   **Total Cash Needed Before Payment:** $6,000,000 (materials) + $600,000 (burn) = **$6,600,000**.
*   **Projected Deficit:** $6,600,000 (needed) - $1,500,000 (available) = **-$5,100,000**.

Even with zero production time, the net-90 term on a lump-sum delivery creates a deficit. You must cover ~$5.1M in costs before receiving the ~$9M revenue.

**Structural Options (Apply to Your Negotiation)**
1.  **Progress Billings:** Instead of one payment at the end, structure the contract for payments upon milestones (e.g., 30% upon material purchase, 40% upon completion of 100 units, 30% upon final delivery).
    *   *Impact:* If you secure 30% upfront ($2.7M), your immediate cash need drops to $3.9M, reducing the financing gap significantly.
2.  **Invoice Factoring:** Selling the government invoice to a third party for immediate cash (less a fee).
    *   *Math:* If a factor advances 80% of the $9M invoice immediately, you receive $7.2M. This covers the $6.6M need, leaving a $600k buffer, minus factoring fees (typically 1–3% of the face value).
3.  **Supply Chain Financing:** Negotiating net-60 or net-90 terms with your material suppliers to align their payment dates with the government's payment date.

**Decision Aid**
*   **Read:** You cannot fulfill this contract under current terms without external capital or revised billing; the math shows a $5.1M hole.
*   **Flip Input:** The deal works if you can shift **$5.1M** of costs to post-payment dates or secure financing covering that specific gap.
*   **Threshold:** If factoring fees exceed your net margin per unit ($15,000), the deal becomes unprofitable. Ensure fees < $15,000/unit.
*   **Next Step:** Draft a "Progress Payment" clause proposal requesting 30% mobilization funds and present it to the contracting officer as a risk-mitigation measure for timely delivery.

### Lane R: Regulatory Regime & Routing

**Regime Identification**
Your request touches on **Federal Acquisition Regulations (FAR)**, specifically regarding allowable payment structures for government contracts.
*   **Progress Payments:** FAR Part 32 governs contract financing. While progress payments are common, they are not automatic; they require specific clauses (e.g., FAR 52.232-16) and agency approval. Some agencies have strict limitations on advancing funds before delivery.
*   **Assignment of Claims:** The **Assignment of Claims Act** allows contractors to assign payment rights to a bank or factor, but the government must acknowledge this assignment. Specific FAR clauses dictate how this is processed.
*   **Legal Determination Required:** Determining whether this specific agency *permits* progress billings for this contract type, or if there are statutory prohibitions against your proposed structure, is a legal conclusion. Furthermore, negotiating these terms without inadvertently triggering a contract modification that voids the award requires legal expertise.

**Routing Instruction**
You must engage a **government contracts attorney** before submitting any alternative payment proposal.
*   **Why:** An attorney must review the specific solicitation and FAR supplements applicable to this agency to confirm if progress payments are permissible or if you are restricted to "Commercial Item Financing" or invoice factoring only.
*   **Preparation for Counsel:** Bring the draft contract, your cash flow model (above), and a list of proposed milestone percentages. Ask specifically: "Does FAR Part 32 allow progress payments for this specific NAICS code and agency, and what is the risk of requesting a modification to the payment clause?"
