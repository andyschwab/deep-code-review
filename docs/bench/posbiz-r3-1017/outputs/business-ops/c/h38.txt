*   **Regime:** Medicare/Medicaid coverage determinations (National/Local Coverage Determinations), private payer medical policy, and Anti-Kickback/Stark Law implications regarding "loss-leading" pricing.
*   **Action:** You must engage a healthcare regulatory attorney and a reimbursement consultant specializing in neurotechnology. Do not attempt to bill or sign payer contracts based on general information; incorrect billing can trigger fraud investigations and clawbacks.

**Lane A: Arithmetic & Strategic Application (Secondary)**
While the *permission* to bill is Lane R, the *financial impact* of your pricing decision is arithmetic you can model now to inform those legal discussions.

**1. Precedent Price Risk Model**
Setting a trial price ($P_{trial}$) creates an anchor for future negotiations ($P_{final}$). Payers often demand "most favored nation" clauses or reference past prices.
*   **Formula:** $Recovery\_Gap = (P_{final\_target} - P_{trial}) \times Volume_{future}$
*   **Application:** If your target commercial price is $50,000 but you accept $15,000 to secure a pilot, you create a $35,000 gap. If a payer covers 1,000 patients later, the "lost" potential revenue is $35M.
*   **Decision Aid:** Do not view the $15,000 as a discount; view it as a **data purchase**. The payer is buying evidence; you are buying a reference account.
    *   *Condition:* Only accept $15,000 if the contract explicitly states: "This price is for a limited pilot study and does not establish a precedent for commercial rates."
    *   *Flip Input:* If legal counsel confirms you cannot contractually isolate the pilot price, the effective cost of the $15,000 deal is the discounted lifetime value of the account, not just the immediate cash.
