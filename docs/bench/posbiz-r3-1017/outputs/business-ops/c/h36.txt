### Lane A: Unit Economics & Break-Even Analysis

**Assumptions & Inputs:**
*   **Gross Sales:** $45,000/month
*   **Food Cost:** 30% ($13,500)
*   **Labor Savings:** $8,000 (treated as a benefit offsetting other labor costs, not revenue)
*   **Option A (Royalty):** 6% of Gross Sales
*   **Option B (Fixed Fee):** $2,500/month
*   **Unknowns:** Other operating expenses (rent, utilities, remaining labor, marketing). These are required for a true "break-even" (net zero profit) calculation. Without them, we can only calculate the **Fee Indifference Point** (where both models cost the franchisee the same).

**1. Fee Indifference Point Calculation**
At what sales volume do the fees equal each other?
*   Formula: `Royalty Rate × Sales = Fixed Fee`
*   Calculation: `0.06 × Sales = $2,500`
*   **Break-Even Sales Volume:** `$2,500 ÷ 0.06 = $41,667`

**Interpretation:**
*   **Above $41,667 sales:** The 6% royalty costs the franchisee *more* than the fixed fee. (At current $45k sales: Royalty = $2,700 vs. Fixed = $2,500. Fixed fee saves the franchisee $200/mo).
*   **Below $41,667 sales:** The 6% royalty costs the franchisee *less* than the fixed fee. (e.g., at $30k sales: Royalty = $1,800 vs. Fixed = $2,500. Royalty saves the franchisee $700/mo).

**2. Franchisor Revenue Sensitivity**
*   **Current State ($45k sales):**
    *   Royalty Model: $2,700/location × 12 = $32,400/mo total.
    *   Fixed Model: $2,500/location × 12 = $30,000/mo total.
    *   *Immediate Impact:* Switching to fixed fees reduces your monthly revenue by $2,400 ($28,800 annually).
*   **Downturn Scenario ($35k sales):**
    *   Royalty Model: $2,100/location.
    *   Fixed Model: $2,500/location.
    *   *Impact:* Fixed fees provide you stability but increase franchisee burden by $400/location during dips.

**Decision Aid:**
*   **Read:** The fixed fee is only cheaper for franchisees when sales exceed ~$41.7k. Below that, it acts as a higher fixed cost, increasing insolvency risk during downturns.
*   **Flip Input:** If you expect average unit sales to drop below $41,667 consistently, the royalty model preserves franchisee solvency better.
*   **Threshold Rule:** If franchisee failure costs you >$2,400/month in lost future fees or litigation, the royalty model's downside protection may be worth the immediate revenue dip.
*   **Next Step:** Instrument a pilot with 2 locations on the fixed fee for 90 days to measure actual retention sentiment before rolling out system-wide.

### Lane R: Legal & Regulatory Routing

**Regime Identified:** Franchise Law (FTC Franchise Rule, State Registration Laws) and Contract Law regarding "unconscionable terms" or fiduciary duties in franchisor-franchisee relationships.

**Routing Instruction:**
The question "which structure is more defensible if a franchisee fails and sues" is a legal determination, not an arithmetic one.
*   **Obligation:** Franchise agreements are highly regulated. Changing fee structures mid-stream or selecting a model that arguably pushes franchisees into insolvency can trigger claims of bad faith or violations of state franchise relationship laws.
*   **Action Required:** You must consult a **franchise attorney** before altering the fee model.
*   **Preparation for Counsel:** Bring the unit economics sensitivity analysis above, specifically highlighting the sales volume ($41,667) where the fixed fee exceeds the royalty. Ask counsel to review the existing Franchise Disclosure Document (FDD) and franchise agreements to determine if unilateral changes to fee structures are permitted and which model better mitigates liability risk in your specific operating states.
