### Unit Economics and Pricing Analysis

**1. Reader Subsidy Recovery (Break-Even Volume)**
To recover the subsidy on the reader, you must first calculate the loss per unit and the contribution margin per cartridge.

*   **Reader Subsidy (Loss per unit):** $8,500 (Price) - $3,200 (Cost) = **$5,300 loss**.
*   **Cartridge Contribution Margin:** $P_{cartridge}$ - $4.00.
*   **Daily Volume:** 50 tests.
*   **Annual Churn Adjustment:** A 15% annual churn rate implies a monthly retention rate of $(1 - 0.15)^{1/12} \approx 0.987$. Over 12 months, the cumulative volume is not simply $50 \times 365$. Assuming linear decay for simplicity in this projection, the average monthly volume drops roughly 7.5% over the year. A precise integration of exponential decay suggests the effective total tests over 12 months is approximately **15,800 tests** (vs. 18,250 with zero churn).

**Formula for Break-Even Price:**
$$P_{cartridge} = \frac{\text{Reader Subsidy}}{\text{Effective 12-Mo Volume}} + \text{Cartridge Cost}$$
$$P_{cartridge} = \frac{5,300}{15,800} + 4.00 \approx 0.34 + 4.00 = \mathbf{\$4.34}$$

**Analysis:**
At a price of **$4.34**, you recover the reader subsidy in 12 months. Any price above $4.34 generates profit on the cartridge stream after the first year.
*   **At $6.00/cartridge:** Contribution is $2.00. Break-even volume = $5,300 / $2.00 = 2,650 tests. At 50 tests/day, you recover the subsidy in **~53 days**.
*   **At $8.00/cartridge:** Contribution is $4.00. Break-even volume = 1,325 tests. Recovery in **~27 days**.

**2. Cash Flow and Runway Context**
You have $600,000 funding and need positive cash flow next quarter.
*   **Fixed Cost Constraint:** To hit positive cash flow in 3 months, your total operating expenses (OpEx) must be less than your Gross Margin generated in that period.
*   **Gross Margin Projection:** If you sell 10 readers in Q1 (hypothetical adoption), you incur $53,000 in reader losses. You need cartridge sales to cover this plus OpEx.
*   **Decision Aid:** If your OpEx is $150,000/quarter, you need $203,000 in cartridge contribution margin in Q1. At $4.00 margin ($8.00 price), you need ~50,750 cartridge sales in Q1. With 10 clinics doing 50 tests/day, that is 45,000 tests. You are close, but volume risk is high.

**3. Strategic Recommendations**
*   **Pricing Model:** Use **Value-Based Pricing** anchored to the cost of alternative diagnostics. If a competitor's test costs $10, pricing at $8.00 offers immediate savings while securing a 27-day payback on the reader.
*   **Reader Strategy:** Yes, offering the reader at a loss is mathematically sound *if* you can contractually or practically lock in cartridge supply. Without lock-in, the $5,300 subsidy is a sunk cost risk.
*   **Communication:** Frame the value proposition as "Lower Cost Per Test" rather than "Machine Price." Show clinic owners a 12-month Total Cost of Ownership (TCO) comparison: Competitor ($10/test) vs. Your System ($8/test + negligible machine amortization).

**Thresholds & Next Step:**
*   **Rule of Thumb:** If LTV:CAC < 3 or Payback > 12 months, reconsider the subsidy depth. Here, payback is <2 months at $8.00, which is strong.
*   **Next Step:** Run a Van Westendorp price sensitivity survey with 20 target clinic owners asking at what price a cartridge becomes "too expensive" vs. "a bargain." Use the upper bound of the acceptable range to set the final price, ensuring it stays above $4.34.
