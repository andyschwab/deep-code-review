**The Regimes and The Risk**
You are operating in a high-risk cross-border environment involving two distinct securities frameworks:
1.  **United States:** The SEC applies the **Howey Test** to determine if an asset is an "investment contract." The four prongs are: (1) an investment of money, (2) in a common enterprise, (3) with a reasonable expectation of profits, (4) derived from the efforts of others. Your specific concern—the secondary market where users trade for profit—directly targets prongs 3 and 4. If regulators argue that the value appreciation relies on LuxChain's development efforts rather than pure supply/demand utility, the token may be deemed a security.
2.  **European Union:** Under **MiCA (Markets in Crypto-Assets Regulation)**, tokens are categorized as Asset-Referenced, E-Money, or "Other" (utility). However, if a token exhibits characteristics of transferable securities (e.g., traded on secondary markets with profit expectation), it may fall under the stricter **Prospectus Regulation**, requiring a registered prospectus before offer to the public. The fact that you sell the token for $25 and enable secondary trading raises the immediate question of whether it functions as a transferable security in Italy and France.

In the US, this includes potential criminal exposure if willful violation is proven. In the EU, national competent authorities (CONSOB in Italy, AMF in France) have enforcement powers.
