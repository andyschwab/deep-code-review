### Evaluation of Rush Order Strategies for PrintAero

**Current Baseline (Standard Orders):**
*   Price: $200
*   Cost: $80
*   Margin: $120 per part
*   Defect Rate: 1% (Effective yield: 99%)

**Option A: Tiered Pricing Model ($500 Rush Price)**
*   **New Rush Economics:**
    *   Price: $500
    *   Cost: $80 (base) + $30 (overtime) = $110
    *   Gross Margin: $390 per part
    *   Defect Rate Risk: 5% (implied from recent trends if volume is uncapped)
    *   *Effective Margin (accounting for 5% waste):* $390 × 0.95 = **$370.50**
*   **Comparison:** The rush margin ($370.50) is ~3.1x the standard margin ($120).
*   **Sensitivity:** Even if the higher price reduces rush volume by 50%, the remaining units generate significantly more profit per unit than standard work. However, a 5% defect rate in aerospace can destroy brand reputation instantly, regardless of margin. One failed part can lead to liability exceeding years of profit.

**Option B: Capacity Cap (10 Rush Parts/Week)**
*   **Volume Constraint:** 10 parts/week × 4.3 weeks ≈ 43 rush parts/month.
*   **Quality Control:** Limiting volume allows you to dedicate specific resources to these jobs, theoretically returning the defect rate toward the 1% baseline.
*   **Revenue Impact:**
    *   If priced at $500 with 1% defects: Effective Margin ≈ $386 per part.
    *   Total Monthly Rush Margin: 43 × $386 = **$16,598**.
    *   *Opportunity Cost:* You are turning away roughly 357 potential rush orders (40% of 1,000 capacity = 400 total rush demand; 400 - 43 capped = 357 rejected).
    *   Lost Potential Margin (if uncapped and flawless): 357 × $390 = $139,230.
    *   *Reality Check:* You cannot process 400 rush parts/month without quality collapse based on your current data.

**Decision Aid & Thresholds**
1.  **One-Line Read:** Capping volume protects the brand but leaves massive margin on the table; raising prices without volume controls risks catastrophic reputation damage due to defects.
2.  **The Flip Input:** The decision hinges on the **cost of a defect**. If one defect costs >$50,000 in lost contracts/liability, the cap is mandatory. If the market tolerates occasional retries, the price hike is superior.
3.  **Rule of Thumb:** In aerospace, **reputation elasticity > price elasticity**. Customers pay for certainty, not just speed. A 5% failure rate is likely unacceptable regardless of price.
4.  **Recommended Next Step (This Week):** Implement a **hybrid pilot**. Raise the price to $500 immediately *but* strictly cap rush intake at 20 parts/week (double your proposed cap) for 30 days. Monitor if the defect rate drops below 2% under this controlled load. If defects remain >2%, revert to the 10-part cap. If defects drop <1%, gradually increase the cap while maintaining the premium price.

**Conclusion:** Do not choose between "uncapped high price" and "strict low cap." The math favors the high price, but the risk favors the cap. Use the price to suppress demand to a level where your quality control can handle the speed.
