### Unit Economics Analysis: Worst-Case Duty Scenario

**Current Baseline (Excluding Duties)**
*   **Selling Price:** $400.00
*   **Variable Costs:**
    *   Manufacturing: $210.00
    *   Freight: $18.00
    *   *Subtotal:* $228.00
*   **Current Gross Margin:** $172.00 per unit
*   **Current Margin %:** 43.0% ($172 / $400)

*Note: Your baseline margin of 43% is already below your stated target of 45%, even before accounting for customs duties.*

**Scenario Analysis: Worst-Case Duty (12%)**
Customs duties in the EU are typically calculated on the CIF value (Cost of goods + Insurance + Freight). Assuming insurance is negligible or included in freight for this estimate:
*   **Dutiable Value:** $210 (Mfg) + $18 (Freight) = $228.00
*   **Estimated Duty (12%):** $27.36
*   **New Total Variable Cost:** $228.00 + $27.36 = $255.36
*   **New Gross Margin:** $400.00 - $255.36 = $144.64
*   **New Margin %:** 36.2%

**Margin Erosion Impact**
*   **Dollar Impact:** Margin drops by $27.36 per unit.
*   **Total Batch Impact (5,000 units):** $136,800 reduction in total gross profit.
*   **Percentage Point Drop:** From 43.0% down to 36.2% (a 6.8 percentage point erosion).

**Decision Framework**
You asked whether to absorb the cost or adjust the price. Since your baseline (43%) already misses your 45% target, absorbing the duty moves you further away from your financial goals.

1.  **Absorb Cost:** Result is 36.2% margin. This fails your internal hurdle rate immediately.
2.  **Adjust Price to Maintain 45% Margin:**
    *   Target Cost Ratio: 55% (100% - 45%)
    *   Required Price = Total Variable Cost / 0.55
    *   Required Price = $255.36 / 0.55 ≈ **$464.29**
    *   *Price Increase Needed:* ~$64.29 per unit (approx. 16% increase).

**Recommendation Logic**
Do not absorb the full cost if maintaining a 45% margin is a hard constraint; the math shows it is impossible at the current $400 price point. You have three structural options:
*   **Pass-Through:** Increase price to ~$465. Test willingness-to-pay with distributors immediately.
*   **Hybrid:** Split the difference. Raise price by $32 (to $432) and accept a ~39% margin, acknowledging the 45% target is unachievable under these specific cost conditions.
*   **Cost Reduction:** Negotiate manufacturing or freight down by ~$28/unit to restore the original margin at the $400 price.

**Next Step:** Before finalizing POs, present the $464.29 price point to your European distributors to gauge elasticity. If they reject a >15% increase, you must formally lower your margin target or renegotiate the $210 manufacturing cost, as the current unit economics do not support a 45% margin under worst-case duty assumptions.
