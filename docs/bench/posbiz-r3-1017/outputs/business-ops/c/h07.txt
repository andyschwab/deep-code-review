### ROI Model: CalmChat Enterprise Value Narrative

**The Core Equation**
To justify the $50,000 annual contract (1,000 seats × $50), you must demonstrate that CalmChat prevents more than $50,000 in losses. Given the sector's turnover cost ($15,000/person) and absenteeism cost ($200/day), the math relies on preventing a small fraction of these expensive events.

**1. The Turnover Savings Model**
*Formula:* `Savings = (Total Employees × Baseline Turnover Rate × Reduction % × Cost Per Turnover)`
*Application:* If the baseline turnover rate is 15% (150 employees leaving), and CalmChat reduces stress-driven departures by just **2.2%**, you save one full employee departure ($15,000).
*Target Metric:* To break even on the $50k fee, you need to prevent roughly **3.3 turnovers** annually (3.3 × $15,000 = $49,500).
*Narrative Hook:* "CalmChat needs to retain only 4 employees per year to pay for itself. With a 15% stress reduction, retaining 4 out of 150 at-risk employees is a conservative expectation."

**2. The Absenteeism Savings Model**
*Formula:* `Savings = (Total Employees × Avg Sick Days × Reduction % × Daily Cost)`
*Application:* If the average employee takes 5 sick days/year due to stress, that is 5,000 lost days total. At $200/day, the potential loss is $1,000,000.
*Target Metric:* To break even, you need to reduce stress-related absenteeism by just **0.25%** (saving 12.5 days total, or $2,500).
*Refined Narrative Hook:* "If CalmChat reduces stress-related absenteeism by merely half a day per employee across the whole company, the savings ($100,000) double the contract cost."

**3. Combined Sensitivity Analysis**
You do not need to maximize both variables. The decision threshold for the CFO is:
`Break-Even Condition: (Turnovers Prevented × $15,000) + (Days Absenteeism Prevented × $200) > $50,000`

*Scenario A (Conservative):* Prevent 2 turnovers ($30k) + Prevent 100 sick days ($20k) = $50k Savings.
*Scenario B (Aggressive):* Prevent 4 turnovers ($60k) + No absenteeism change = $60k Savings.

**Metrics to Track in the Next Pilot**
To make this calculation undeniable, you must move from "reported stress levels" to behavioral proxies during the pilot:
1.  **Voluntary Exit Interviews:** Tag exits with "burnout/stress" vs. other causes. Compare the % of stress-tagged exits in the CalmChat group vs. a control group.
2.  **Short-Term Disability/Leave Claims:** Track the frequency of mental-health-related leave claims (aggregated/anonymized) between groups.
3.  **Self-Reported "Near-Miss" Retention:** Survey users: "In the last month, did you consider quitting due to stress? Did CalmChat influence that decision?" Use this % as a leading indicator for actual turnover.
4.  **Absenteeism Frequency:** Track the number of unscheduled absences per user in the pilot group vs. historical baselines.

**Decision Aid**
*One-line read:* At $50/seat, CalmChat is profitable for the client if it prevents roughly 0.3% of the workforce from quitting or saves 0.5 sick days per employee annually.
*Flip Input:* The model flips from "loss" to "gain" if the stress-reduction-to-retention conversion rate exceeds 1.5% (i.e., 1.5% of stressed employees stay who otherwise would have left).
*Rule of Thumb:* If LTV (savings) > 3× CAC (contract cost), the deal is safe; here, you only need LTV > 1× Cost to break even.
*Next Step:* Instrument the pilot to capture "intent to quit" surveys monthly; if >5% of users report "stayed because of CalmChat," project that directly against the $15k turnover cost to build the CFO deck.
