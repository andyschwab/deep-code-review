**Pricing Structure Analysis for UrbanTwin**

You are choosing between two value metrics: **Per Citizen** (volume of population served) and **Per Square Mile** (geographic scope). Neither is inherently "correct"; the optimal choice depends on which variable correlates most strongly with your customer's perceived value and your cost-to-serve.

**1. Arithmetic Comparison (Based on Your Inputs)**
*   **Scenario A (Per Citizen):** 250,000 citizens × $0.10 = **$25,000/year**.
*   **Scenario B (Per Square Mile):** 80 sq miles × $400 = **$32,000/year**.
*   **Gap:** The geographic model yields 28% more revenue for this specific mid-sized city.

**2. Sensitivity to Density (The "Fairness" Test)**
Your concern about density is mathematically valid. Let us apply your formulas to two hypothetical cities with the same population but different densities to see how the revenue fluctuates.

*   *Hypothetical City X (Dense):* 250,000 citizens in 40 sq miles.
    *   Per Citizen: $25,000 (Unchanged).
    *   Per Sq Mile: 40 × $400 = $16,000 (Revenue drops 50% vs. the mid-sized example).
*   *Hypothetical City Y (Sprawling):* 250,000 citizens in 160 sq miles.
    *   Per Citizen: $25,000 (Unchanged).
    *   Per Sq Mile: 160 × $400 = $64,000 (Revenue doubles vs. the mid-sized example).

**Observation:** The "Per Citizen" model stabilizes revenue based on the *beneficiary count* (often tied to municipal budget size), while "Per Square Mile" creates high volatility based on geography. If your compute costs scale with the number of agents (citizens/vehicles) simulated, "Per Citizen" aligns costs and revenue. If costs scale with map data storage and rendering area, "Per Square Mile" aligns better.

**3. Strategic Alignment with European Municipalities**
European public procurement often favors predictability.
*   **Per Citizen:** Easier for a city council to justify as a "cost per constituent." It scales naturally if the city grows, but may face resistance in dense historic centers where space is limited but population is high.
*   **Per Square Mile:** Risks undervaluing complex, dense city centers (like Barcelona's Eixample) where simulation complexity is highest per unit of area. It may overvalue rural outskirts with low simulation activity.

**4. Decision Aid & Next Steps**
*   **Read:** The "Per Square Mile" model currently offers higher upside for average density but carries higher risk of underpricing dense, complex urban cores where your software provides the most value (traffic/energy simulation).
*   **Flip Input:** The decision flips if your server costs are driven primarily by the number of simulated entities (citizens/cars) rather than map size. If compute cost per citizen > $0.10, the per-citizen model threatens margins in dense cities.
*   **Threshold Rule:** If LTV:CAC falls below 3:1 under the "Per Citizen" model due to low pricing in dense areas, reconsider the metric.
*   **Recommended Structure (Apply, Don't Direct):** Consider a **Good-Better-Best tiered model** or a **hybrid metric** to capture complexity.
    *   *Option A (Hybrid):* Base fee per sq mile (covers map data) + variable fee per 1,000 active simulated agents (covers compute/complexity).
    *   *Option B (Tiered):* "Small City" (up to 100k citizens), "Medium" (100k–500k), "Large" (500k+), with price anchored to a blend of area and population.
    *   *Action:* Run a **Van Westendorp price sensitivity survey** with 5–10 target municipal CTOs. Ask: "At what price would a per-citizen model feel too expensive given your density?" Use their answers to set the bands, not this arithmetic alone.
