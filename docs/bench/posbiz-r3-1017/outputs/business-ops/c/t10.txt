### Revenue Impact Analysis: Flat Fee vs. Usage-Based Model

**Current State (Baseline)**
*   **Subscribers:** 1,200
*   **Revenue:** $118,800/mo ($99 × 1,200)
*   **Server Costs:** $14,400/mo ($12 × 1,200 active users)
*   **Contribution Margin:** $104,400/mo

**Proposed Scenario (Usage-Based)**
*   **Lost Users:** 240 high-volume subscribers (20% of base).
    *   *Revenue Loss:* $23,760/mo (240 × $99).
*   **Gained Users:** 400 new low-volume subscribers.
    *   *Volume:* 50 documents/user/mo.
    *   *Price:* $0.50/document.
    *   *Revenue Gain:* $10,000/mo (400 users × 50 docs × $0.50).
*   **Remaining Users:** 960 original subscribers.
    *   *Assumption:* To isolate the specific variables you provided, this calculation assumes remaining users stay on the flat fee or their usage revenue nets to zero change for this specific comparison. If *all* users switch to usage-based, the result changes drastically based on the document volume of the remaining 960 users.
*   **New Total Revenue:** $105,040/mo ($118,800 - $23,760 + $10,000).
*   **New Server Costs:**
    *   Remaining Users (960): $11,520 ($12 × 960).
    *   New Users (400): $4,800 ($12 × 400).
    *   *Total Cost:* $16,320/mo.
*   **New Contribution Margin:** $88,720/mo ($105,040 - $16,320).

**Result:**
Under the specific inputs provided (losing 240 high-volume users and gaining 400 low-volume users), the switch results in a **monthly revenue decrease of $13,760** and a **contribution margin decrease of $15,680**. The gain from new price-sensitive users ($10k) does not offset the loss of high-volume subscribers ($23.7k) in this specific model.

**Decision Aid**
The math indicates the proposed swap is negative unless the remaining 960 users generate significantly less than their current $99 equivalent in usage fees, or if the "gained" users volume is underestimated.
*   **Flip Input:** The new users must average **124 documents/month** (instead of 50) to break even on the lost revenue alone ($23,760 loss ÷ 400 users ÷ $0.50).
*   **Rule of Thumb:** If LTV:CAC drops or payback extends due to lower margins, do not scale acquisition until unit economics are fixed.
*   **Next Step:** If your data shows heavy users average >200 documents/month, consider a "Good-Better-Best" tiered model (e.g., $49 for 50 docs, $149 for 250 docs) rather than a pure usage switch, to retain predictable revenue from heavy users while capturing the low-end market.

**Data Segmentation Recommendations**
Before changing pricing, query your database for these three specific patterns:
1.  **Document Distribution Curve:** Plot documents per user to identify the exact cutoff where the top 10% diverges from the median. This validates if the "20% churn" assumption aligns with users who currently pay $99 but would exceed that cost under usage pricing.
2.  **Elasticity Clusters:** Segment users by "documents per dollar" currently implied. Identify users paying $99 for <20 documents (subsidizing heavy users); these are your safest candidates for a usage model migration without churn risk.
3.  **Growth Trajectory:** Analyze month-over-month document growth for the middle 50%. If usage is organic and growing, a hard cap or pure usage model might penalize your most engaged future power users.
