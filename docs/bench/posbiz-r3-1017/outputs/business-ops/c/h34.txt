**Lane R: Regulation-Dependent Route**

Your question concerns the selection of an FDA regulatory pathway (510(k) vs. De Novo vs. PMA), the determination of allowable medical claims (trending vs. dosing), and the timeline for market entry for a Class II/III medical device. These are legal and regulatory determinations that define what is permitted to be sold and under what conditions. Under the asymmetric boundary rules, this falls strictly into **Lane R**. I cannot conclude which pathway applies to your specific sensor technology, estimate the exact clearance time for a novel device, or advise on the "fastest" route to revenue, as an incorrect classification could result in severe enforcement action, product seizure, or invalidation of future approvals.

**Regime and Routing:**
The relevant regime is the **Federal Food, Drug, and Cosmetic Act (FD&C Act)** enforced by the **U.S. Food and Drug Administration (FDA)**, specifically regulations governing Medical Devices (21 CFR Parts 800-898). The classification of your device (Class I, II, or III) depends on the level of control necessary to assure safety and effectiveness, which hinges on whether your sensor is "substantially equivalent" to a predicate device (510(k)) or requires a new classification (De Novo).

**Practical Obligations and Triggers:**
You must engage a **regulatory affairs attorney or a specialized FDA consultant** immediately. The trigger for this engagement is your intent to submit a premarket notification or request. A professional will perform a "predicate search" to determine if a substantially equivalent device exists. If no predicate exists for continuous glucose monitoring via contact lenses, you may be forced into the De Novo pathway (for novel low-to-moderate risk devices) or Premarket Approval (PMA) if the risk is deemed high. Only counsel can interpret the nuance of "trending data" vs. "dosing decisions" to see if it successfully lowers the risk profile enough to avoid a PMA.

**Preparation for Professional Counsel:**
Before your first call with counsel, prepare the following to maximize efficiency and reduce billable hours spent on fact-finding:
1.  **Device Description:** A technical summary of the sensor mechanism, power source, and data transmission method.
2.  **Intended Use Statement:** Your draft language for "trending only" vs. "dosing" claims.
3.  **Risk Analysis:** Any preliminary ISO 14971 risk management files you have generated.
4.  **Predicate Hypothesis:** List any existing cleared glucose monitors (fingerstick, implantable, or optical) you believe are similar, even if the form factor differs.
5.  **Financial Constraints:** Explicitly state your 12-month runway constraint and $1.8M cash position so they can prioritize strategies with the highest probability of clearance within that window.

**Time-Sensitive Item:**
Do not initiate any clinical data collection or marketing materials until the regulatory strategy is defined by counsel. Collecting data under the wrong protocol can render it unusable for your eventual submission, wasting both time and your limited cash. Regarding user fees: while the FDA publishes annual fee schedules for "small businesses" (which require certification), the exact fee and potential waivers depend on your specific submission type and status; your counsel will verify the current fiscal year rates and assist with the small business qualification request.
