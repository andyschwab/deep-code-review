**Lane R: Regulation-Dependent (Route to Professional)**

**Regime Identified:** UK Intellectual Property Law (Copyright, Designs and Patents Act 1988), Common Law Rights (Passing Off), and emerging statutory proposals regarding "performer's property rights" in relation to AI synthesis.

**Analysis & Routing:**
You asked whether paying royalties is legally mandatory and requested an analysis of IP risks and precedents for synthesized voices in the UK. **I cannot answer whether the payment is mandatory, nor can I analyze the specific legal risks or precedents to determine if you are infringing rights.** These are binding legal determinations that depend on the specific facts of the actor's existing contracts, the nature of their "personality rights" (which are not a standalone statutory right in the UK but are protected via Passing Off and false endorsement cases), and the interpretation of how current copyright law applies to post-mortem voice cloning.

In the UK, there is currently no explicit statutory "post-mortem voice right" akin to some US states (like California or Tennessee). However, the estate may have valid claims based on:
1.  **Passing Off:** If the synthesis misleads the public into believing the actor endorsed the work.
2.  **Existing Contracts:** The actor's original recording contracts may assign rights to the estate that extend to derivative works or new technologies.
3.  **Moral Rights:** While generally ending at death, some aspects may persist depending on prior assertions.
4.  **Pending Legislation:** The UK government is actively consulting on changes to IP law specifically regarding AI and performers' rights; the landscape is in flux.

Because the legal status is "unclear" (as you noted) and involves high-stakes liability (15% of revenue on 50 titles at £25 each is a significant exposure), **you must engage a specialized entertainment lawyer with expertise in AI and IP before signing any agreement or proceeding with synthesis.**

**Actionable Preparation for Counsel:**

*   **The Estate's Demand:** The written communication specifying the 15% royalty demand and their legal basis for it.
