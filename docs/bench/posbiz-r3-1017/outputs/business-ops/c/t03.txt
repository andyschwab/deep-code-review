### Net Profit Analysis: First-Come-First-Served vs. Lottery

**Assumptions & Inputs**
*   **Inventory:** 2,500 pairs (Hard Cap).
*   **Price:** $220/pair.
*   **Variable Cost:** $65/pair.
*   **Contribution Margin:** $155/pair ($220 - $65).
*   **Potential Revenue (Max):** $550,000 (2,500 × $220).
*   **Potential Gross Profit (Max):** $387,500 (2,500 × $155).
*   **Site Crash Cost (FCFS Risk):** $10,000 (lost sales + fixes).
*   **Platform Upgrade Cost:** $5,000 (applied to FCFS to mitigate crash risk).
*   **Demand:** 15,000 interested buyers (6x oversubscription).

**Scenario A: First-Come-First-Served (FCFS)**
*   **Mechanism:** High urgency, high technical load.
*   **Revenue Potential:** Captures full $550,000 *if* the site holds. If the site crashes before 2,500 units sell, revenue drops proportionally to downtime.
*   **Cost Structure:**
    *   Base COGS: $162,500 (2,500 × $65).
    *   Crash Penalty: -$10,000 (estimated).
    *   Mitigation Option: Spend $5,000 on upgrade to reduce crash probability.
*   **Net Profit Calculation (Successful Drop):** $387,500 (Max Gross Profit).
*   **Net Profit Calculation (Crash Scenario):** $387,500 - $10,000 = $377,500 (assuming partial recovery or fixed costs only; if sales are lost entirely during crash, profit decreases by $155 per unsold pair).
*   **Risk Profile:** High variance. Profit depends entirely on server throughput during the spike.

**Scenario B: Lottery System**
*   **Mechanism:** Controlled load, reduced urgency/impulse.
*   **Revenue Potential:** Depends on conversion rate of selected winners. If urgency drops and selected winners do not complete checkout, inventory remains unsold.
*   **Cost Structure:**
    *   Base COGS: Scales with actual units sold.
    *   Technical Cost: Negligible (no crash risk).
    *   Platform Upgrade: $0 needed (traffic is smoothed).
*   **Net Profit Calculation:** (Units Sold × $155) - $0 technical penalty.
*   **Risk Profile:** Low technical risk, high behavioral risk. If conversion among winners falls below 100%, you leave money on the table that FCFS would have captured via impulse.

**Decision Logic & Thresholds**
Since inventory is capped at 2,500, **FCFS is mathematically superior** *if and only if* the site can process 2,500 transactions before crashing or timing out.
*   **Break-Even on Upgrade:** Spend the $5,000 upgrade if it prevents >32 units of lost sales ($5,000 ÷ $155 margin). If the crash risk implies losing more than 32 pairs worth of sales, the upgrade pays for itself immediately.
*   **Lottery Viability:** Choose Lottery only if the probability of FCFS selling <2,468 units (due to crash) is high, OR if your historical data shows lottery conversion rates remain near 100% despite lower urgency.

**Required Metrics from Last Three Drops**
To finalize this decision, calculate these three specific metrics from your historical data:
1.  **Peak Checkout Conversion Rate:** The percentage of users who reached the checkout page and completed purchase during the first 10 minutes of previous drops. (Low rate suggests friction/impulse loss).
2.  **Server Error Rate at Peak Load:** The percentage of failed transaction attempts during the highest traffic minute of previous launches. (Directly quantifies the "crash" risk).
3.  **Time-to-Sell-Out:** How many minutes it took to sell available inventory previously. (If <2 minutes, FCFS urgency is critical; if >15 minutes, Lottery may not dampen demand significantly).

**Recommendation:** Given the 6x oversubscription (15k buyers for 2.5k pairs), the demand buffer is massive. Allocate the $5,000 to harden the FCFS infrastructure. The $10,000 crash risk outweighs the theoretical urgency loss of a lottery, provided your historical "Time-to-Sell-Out" is under 5 minutes.

*Note: This analysis applies arithmetic to your provided figures; actual server performance and consumer behavior may vary. This is educational information, not legal, tax, accounting, financial, or investment advice.*
