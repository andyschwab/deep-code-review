**1. Current Unit Economics (Baseline)**

*   **COGS (Hardware):** $1,800
*   **Gross Margin per Unit:** $2,700 (60%)
*   **Cash Runway:** $550,000 / (Monthly Burn). *Note: Monthly burn cannot be calculated without operating expense (OpEx) data (salaries, rent, marketing). With only 200 units sold historically, revenue recognition timing is needed to determine current cash flow status.*

**2. The Variable: Customer Energy Cost Impact**
You cannot calculate the *customer's* operational cost without the unit's energy efficiency (kWh per gallon/liter) and average daily usage volume.
*   **Formula:** `Daily Energy Cost = (Daily Output in Gallons × kWh per Gallon) × Electricity Rate`
*   **Summer Variance:** At $0.24/kWh vs $0.12/kWh, the customer's operating cost doubles.
*   **Churn Risk Trigger:** If the total cost of ownership (TCO) spikes in summer, churn risk increases. To model this, you must estimate the "sticker shock" threshold. If a customer expects $50/month and receives a $100 bill, does that exceed their willingness to pay?

**3. Scenario Modeling: Subsidy vs. Price Adjustment**
Since specific usage data is missing, we apply the logic to your decision variables.

*   **Option A: Absorb 50% of Energy Cost (Subsidy Plan)**
    *   *Mechanism:* You pay the customer $0.06/kWh during summer months (June–Sept, approx. 120 days).
    *   *Impact on Your Margin:* `Subsidy Cost = Daily Usage × 120 days × $0.06`.
    *   *Result:* Your $2,700 margin decreases by the total subsidy amount per unit.
    *   *Break-even Volume Check:* If this subsidy causes a price increase elsewhere to fund it, apply the elasticity test:
        *   Current Contribution: $2,700 × 200 units = $540,000 (gross).
        *   If you raise hardware price to fund subsidies and volume drops >20% (to <160 units), you lose total contribution unless the new margin per unit compensates significantly.

*   **Option B: Pass-Through (No Subsidy)**
    *   *Impact on Your Margin:* $2,700 remains static.
    *   *Risk:* Churn. If churn increases by even 5% annually due to high operating costs, your Customer Lifetime Value (LTV) drops.
    *   *LTV Sensitivity:* `LTV = (Monthly Subscription/Service Fee × Gross Margin %) / Churn Rate`.
        *   If Churn rises from 2% to 4% because of energy bills, LTV is cut in half.
        *   If LTV drops below 3× CAC (Customer Acquisition Cost), the business model becomes unsustainable.

**4. Decision Aid & Next Steps**
*   **The Flip Input:** The decision hinges on **Daily Usage Volume**. Without knowing how many kWh a unit consumes daily, you cannot quantify the subsidy cost or the customer's pain point.
*   **Rule of Thumb:** If the summer energy bill exceeds 15–20% of the customer's expected monthly utility budget, churn risk is high.
