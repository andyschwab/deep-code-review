### Revenue Model Comparison & Break-Even Analysis

**Assumptions & Inputs**
*   **Headsets per studio:** 5
*   **Classes/week:** 40
*   **Price/class:** $25
*   **Avg attendees/session:** 8
*   **Weeks/month:** ~4.33 (52 weeks / 12 months)
*   **Hardware Margin:** $1,200 - $700 = $500 (one-time)
*   **OpEx to cover:** $60,000/month (context only; break-even calculated per studio below).

**1. Projected Monthly Recurring Revenue (MRR) Per Studio**

*Base Potential Revenue (100% Utilization):*
40 classes × 8 attendees × $25 × 4.33 weeks = **$34,640** potential monthly booking revenue per studio.

*Scenario A: 70% Utilization*
*   **Effective Booking Revenue:** $34,640 × 0.70 = $24,248
*   **Model 1 (Flat Fee):** $150 × 5 headsets = **$750**
*   **Model 2 (12% Share):** $24,248 × 0.12 = **$2,910**
*   *Difference:* Revenue share yields +$2,160 more per studio.

*Scenario B: 90% Utilization*
*   **Effective Booking Revenue:** $34,640 × 0.90 = $31,176
*   **Model 1 (Flat Fee):** $150 × 5 headsets = **$750**
*   **Model 2 (12% Share):** $31,176 × 0.12 = **$3,741**
*   *Difference:* Revenue share yields +$2,991 more per studio.

**2. Break-Even Utilization Rate**
To find where Revenue Share > Flat Fee:
*   Formula: `(Base Potential Revenue × Utilization × 12%) > $750`
*   `$34,640 × U × 0.12 > $750`
*   `$4,156.8 × U > $750`
*   `U > 0.18`

**Result:** The revenue share model becomes more profitable than the flat fee at just **18% utilization**. Given your pilot assumptions (70–90%), the revenue share model generates significantly higher income per studio in both cases.

3.  **Churn/Cancellation Rate:** Frequency of last-minute cancellations that might reduce net billable revenue.
4.  **Seasonality Trends:** Monthly variance in class bookings to model cash flow volatility under the share model.

**Decision Aid**
At current assumptions, the 12% share dominates the flat fee immediately (break-even at 18% usage). However, the flat fee offers predictable cash flow to help cover the $60k OpEx, whereas the share model introduces variance. If cash flow stability is critical for immediate survival, the flat fee reduces risk; if maximizing long-term value is the priority and you can withstand variance, the share model is mathematically superior based on these inputs.
