**Lane A: Pricing Model Evaluation (Arithmetic & Incentives)**

Your decision hinges on the variance in job duration (10 minutes to 12 hours) and the alignment with researcher incentives.

**1. The Math on Your Options**
*   **Option A ($50/qubit-hour):**
    *   *Short Job (10 mins = 0.17 hrs):* Revenue = ~$8.50.
    *   *Long Job (12 hrs):* Revenue = $600.
    *   *Incentive:* Researchers are incentivized to optimize code for speed to save money. However, for very short jobs, the revenue may not cover the fixed overhead of job scheduling and queue management.
    *   *Risk:* If "qubit-hour" implies dedicated access, idle time kills your margin. If it implies actual compute time, you need precise metering.
*   **Option B ($2,000 flat/run):**
    *   *Short Job (10 mins):* Revenue = $2,000. (High margin, but risks customer churn if perceived as gouging).
    *   *Long Job (12 hrs):* Revenue = $2,000. (Risk of loss if 12-hour jobs consume disproportionate energy/cooling/maintenance costs).
    *   *Incentive:* Researchers are incentivized to run many small, potentially inefficient jobs since the marginal cost of a "retry" is zero once they pay the flat fee. This could clog your queue.

**2. Break-Even Context**
*   **Capital Recovery:** You have $2M in hardware. To recover this purely through cash flow over your 18-month runway (ignoring operating expenses for this specific calculation):
    *   Monthly target = $2,000,000 / 18 ≈ $111,111/month.
    *   **Option A path:** Requires ~2,222 billable hours/month at $50/hr. With 15 universities, that is ~148 hours/university/month. Given the 10min–12hr variance, achieving consistent utilization is critical.
    *   **Option B path:** Requires ~56 successful runs/month total across all universities. If average job time is low, this is highly profitable; if average job time is high, you risk operating losses per unit.

**3. Decision Aid**
*   **Read:** Option B creates a "moral hazard" where users over-consume resources on long/failed runs, threatening your cost recovery on long jobs. Option A aligns cost with resource consumption but penalizes the iterative nature of academic research (many short tests).
*   **Flip Input:** The deciding factor is your **marginal cost per hour** of operation (energy + cooling + maintenance).
    *   *Formula:* `Profit per Job = Price - (Duration × Marginal Cost/hr)`.
    *   If your marginal cost is high, Option B is dangerous for 12-hour jobs. If your marginal cost is low relative to $50/hr, Option A leaves money on the table for short jobs.
*   **Threshold Rule:** If the coefficient of variation in job times is high (which you stated: 10m to 12h), a pure flat fee or pure time-based model usually fails.
*   **Next Step:** Consider a **hybrid model** (Good-Better-Best): A base "access fee" (covering overhead) + a lower variable rate per qubit-hour (covering marginal cost). Test a "credits" system on 3 universities this week where 1 credit = 1 minute, priced to average $50/hr, but sold in bundles.
