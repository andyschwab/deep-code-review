**Lane A: Arithmetic Application & Sensitivity Analysis**

**Current State Baseline**
*   **Annual Recurring Revenue (ARR) Equivalent:** 4 cities × $250k = $1.0M.
*   **Monthly Burn:** $180k → **Annual Burn:** $2.16M.
*   **Current Runway:** Assuming cash on hand covers the burn, the runway depends on existing cash reserves (not provided). If cash equals one year of burn, runway is ~6 months without new revenue.

**Model Comparison: Perpetual vs. SaaS**

1.  **Perpetual License (Current)**
    *   **New Deal Cash Flow:** $600k upfront + $90k (15% of $600k) Year 1 maintenance = $690k Year 1 cash per city.
    *   **Revenue Recognition:** Only the maintenance portion ($90k) is typically recognized annually; the license fee is recognized immediately but is non-recurring.
    *   **Valuation Context:** Investors often apply lower multiples (e.g., 3x–5x) to non-recurring revenue compared to ARR.

2.  **SaaS Subscription (Proposed)**
    *   **Annual Contract Value (ACV):** $75k/month × 12 = $900k per city.
    *   **Cash Flow:** $900k collected over 12 months ($75k/mo).
    *   **Revenue Recognition:** 100% is recurring ARR.
    *   **Valuation Context:** Infrastructure SaaS often commands higher multiples (e.g., 8x–12x ARR) due to predictability, *if* growth and retention metrics are strong.

**Transition Impact Analysis (60% Conversion Scenario)**
*Assumption:* You convert 60% of existing/pipe customers to SaaS and lose 40% who prefer ownership. We compare one new city acquisition under both models to isolate the unit economics.

*   **Scenario A (Perpetual - 100% close rate hypothetical):**
    *   Year 1 Cash: $690k.
    *   Recognized Recurring: $90k.
*   **Scenario B (SaaS - 60% close rate):**
    *   Effective ACV per *original* lead: $900k × 0.60 = $540k.
    *   Year 1 Cash: $540k (collected monthly).
    *   Recognized Recurring: $540k.

**Runway Sensitivity (The "Valley of Death" Risk)**
Switching to SaaS creates a cash flow dip before ARR scales.
*   **Formula:** `Monthly Net Burn = Gross Burn - (New MRR + Existing MRR)`
*   **Current MRR:** $250k / 12 ≈ $20.8k/month (if treated as annuity) or lumpy perpetual deals.
*   **SaaS MRR (4 cities converted):** 4 × $75k = $300k MRR.
*   **Transition Gap:** If you stop selling perpetual today to build SaaS, and only convert 60% of the pipeline, your immediate cash inflow drops significantly compared to the $600k upfront checks.
*   **Break-even Conversion Rate:** To match the $690k Year 1 cash of the perpetual model, the SaaS model needs: `$690k / $900k = 76.6%` conversion.
    *   *Implication:* At your estimated 60% conversion, Year 1 cash flow is **~22% lower** than the perpetual model ($540k vs $690k). This shortens runway unless you have sufficient capital reserves to bridge the gap until Year 2 when the SaaS base compounds.

**Decision Aid**
*   **Read:** The SaaS model generates higher *valuation multiples* on paper but reduces *immediate cash flow* by ~22% at a 60% conversion rate compared to perpetual licensing.
*   **Flip Input:** The decision flips if your conversion rate exceeds **77%** or if investors demand >8x multiples that outweigh the immediate cash shortfall.
*   **Threshold Rule:** If your current cash runway is <18 months, the cash dip from a low-conversion SaaS transition may jeopardize reaching the Series A milestone.
*   **Next Step:** Model a "Hybrid Pilot" where you offer SaaS to new leads only while maintaining perpetual for existing loyalists, measuring the actual conversion rate and churn over two quarters before a full pivot.
