### Unit Economics Comparison: Robots vs. Human Labor

To present an accurate cost-per-square-foot analysis, you must separate **Capital Expenditure (CapEx)** from **Operating Expenditure (OpEx)** and annualize the CapEx over the expected useful life of the robots. Below is the applied math using your provided numbers, with clear slots for the variables you identified as missing.

#### 1. The Human Labor Baseline (Annual OpEx)
*Formula:* `Annual Cost = (Hourly Rate × Workers × Hours/Year)`
*Assumption:* Stadiums operate 365 days/year. You must define shifts per day (e.g., 1 shift of 8 hours, or 3 shifts of 8 hours).
*Calculation Structure:*
- **Hourly Rate:** $18
- **Workers:** 40 per stadium
- **Hours:** [Insert Hours/Year, e.g., 8 hrs × 365 = 2,920]
- **Total Annual Labor Cost:** $18 × 40 × [Hours] = **$[Result] per stadium**
- **Cost per Sq Ft:** Total Annual Labor Cost ÷ 1,500,000 sq ft.

*Note:* This baseline likely excludes payroll taxes, benefits, and management overhead, which typically add 20–30% to raw hourly wages. Including these strengthens your savings argument.

#### 2. The Robot Model (Annualized Cost)
*Formula:* `Annual Cost = (Annualized CapEx) + (Annual OpEx)`

**A. Capital Expenditure (CapEx)**
- **Unit Cost:** $35,000
- **Quantity:** 10 units
- **Total Upfront:** $350,000 per stadium
- **Annualization:** You must choose a depreciation period (useful life).
  - *Equation:* $350,000 ÷ [Useful Life in Years]
  - *Example (5-year life):* $70,000/year
  - *Example (3-year life):* $116,667/year

**B. Operating Expenditure (OpEx)**
- **Software/Charging:** $2,000/month × 10 units × 12 months = **$240,000/year**
- **Missing Variables (Your Question):** To get the *precise* number, you must add:
  1.  **Maintenance Downtime:** Cost of spare parts + labor for repairs per year.
  2.  **Battery Replacement:** Cost per pack ÷ replacement cycle (years).
  3.  **Supervision Staff:** Hourly rate × number of supervisors needed to monitor the fleet (likely far fewer than 40, but not zero).

*Total Annual Robot Cost:* [Annualized CapEx] + $240,000 + [Maintenance] + [Batteries] + [Supervision]

#### 3. The Crossover Point (Break-Even Analysis)
The crossover occurs when **Cumulative Robot Costs < Cumulative Labor Costs**.
*Equation:*
`Upfront CapEx + (Annual Robot OpEx × Years) < (Annual Labor Cost × Years)`

Rearranged to solve for **Years to Break-Even**:
`Years = Upfront CapEx ÷ (Annual Labor Savings − Annual Robot OpEx)`

*Where:* `Annual Labor Savings = (Human Annual Cost) − (Robot Annual OpEx excluding CapEx)`

If your annual operating savings (Labor minus Robot OpEx) do not exceed the annualized CapEx, you will never break even on a cash-flow basis within the asset's life.

#### 4. Variables Required for Your 40% Reduction Target
To claim a 40% reduction, your Total Annual Robot Cost must be ≤ 60% of the Human Labor Baseline.
- **Critical Input:** If your human labor cost (with benefits) is ~$1.2M/year (est. 3 shifts), your robot total must be under ~$720k.
- **Risk Factors:** If battery replacement is needed every 18 months at $5k/unit, that adds $33k/year. If downtime requires 20% spare inventory, CapEx increases by 20%. These must be modeled explicitly.

**Decision Aid:**
1.  **Read:** Your break-even timeline depends entirely on the "Useful Life" assumption and the hidden labor cost of supervision.
2.  **Flip Input:** The variable that most likely flips your model from "profitable" to "loss-making" is the **number of shifts** the humans currently work. If humans work 24/7 (3 shifts) and robots only work 1 shift, your savings are overstated.
3.  **Threshold:** If `(Annual Labor Cost) < (Robot OpEx + Annualized CapEx)`, the model fails immediately.
