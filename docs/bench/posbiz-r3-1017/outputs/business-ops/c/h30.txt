**The Regime and Why It Applies**
Revenue recognition for services with contingent deliverables (like your verification certificates) falls under **ASC 606 (Revenue from Contracts with Customers)** in the United States. The core determination relies on identifying your "performance obligation." Your auditors are split because the answer hinges on a legal and factual analysis:
1.  Is the obligation satisfied **over time** (as you collect data), allowing proportional revenue recognition?
2.  Is the obligation satisfied **at a point in time** (only when the certificate is issued and transferable), requiring you to defer all revenue until that moment?

The "contingent nature" of the certificate approval is a critical variable. If the certificate is not issued, do you get paid? If the client can terminate without paying for the data collection alone, recognizing revenue early creates a high risk of restatement.

**The Risks of Each Path (Educational Context Only)**
*   **Recognizing Early (Over Time):** If regulators or future auditors determine your performance obligation was not satisfied until the certificate issuance, you would have to restate your financials. This could trigger loan covenants, damage credibility with investors, and potentially raise **securities fraud** concerns if the inflated revenue was used to secure financing under false pretenses.
*   **Recognizing Late (At Issuance):** This is generally the conservative approach. The risk here is primarily operational (showing lower revenue now), but it minimizes the risk of regulatory penalty or loan default due to financial misrepresentation.
