### Demand Forecasting Framework for CellBite (Lane A: Arithmetic & Structure)

Since you lack historical sales data, you must construct a **bottom-up adoption model** driven by your specific constraints and validated assumptions. Do not guess a total market number; instead, calculate demand based on the number of chefs you can physically onboard and their individual consumption rates.

**1. The Core Formula: Adoption Funnel**
Forecast monthly volume ($V_m$) using this structure:
$$V_m = (N_{total} \times R_{contact} \times R_{conversion} \times Q_{avg})$$

Where:
*   $N_{total}$: Total addressable high-end restaurants in your target EU regions.
*   $R_{contact}$: Percentage you can realistically pitch per month (sales capacity).
*   $R_{conversion}$: Percentage of pitched chefs who place an order (your critical unknown).
*   $Q_{avg}$: Average monthly kg ordered per adopting chef.

**2. Estimating the Critical Unknown ($R_{conversion}$)**
Without past data, use a **Van Westendorp-style sensitivity check** or a **Pilot Cohort** to derive this rate rather than guessing:
*   **Method A (Survey):** Ask 20 target chefs four questions: At what price is it too expensive? A bargain? Expensive but considered? Too cheap? Map their "acceptable range" against your €90/kg. If €90 falls in the "too expensive" zone for >50%, adjust your conversion assumption downward.
*   **Method B (Pilot):** Commit to a small batch (e.g., 50kg). Offer it to 10 chefs. If 3 buy, your initial $R_{conversion}$ is 30%. Use this real data point to project the next month.

**3. Unit Economics & Break-Even Analysis**
Apply your numbers to determine the minimum volume required to cover fixed costs (staffing/overhead).
*   **Contribution Margin per kg:** $\text{Price} - \text{Variable Cost} = €90 - €40 = €50$.
*   **Break-Even Volume:** $\text{Monthly Fixed Costs} \div €50$.
    *   *Example:* If fixed costs are €20,000, you must sell **400 kg** just to cover overhead. Anything below this burns cash; anything above contributes to profit.
*   **Spoilage Risk:** If you produce 2,000 kg but sell 1,000 kg, your loss on unsold inventory is the full variable cost (€40/kg) plus disposal fees.

**4. Operational Planning Scenarios (Next 6 Months)**
Run these three scenarios to set staffing and raw material purchases. Do not plan for the "average"; plan for the downside and cap the upside.

| Scenario | Assumed Conversion Rate | Est. Monthly Volume | Action Plan |
| :--- | :--- | :--- | : |
| **Conservative** | 5% of pitched chefs | 400 kg | Produce only to order. Keep staff minimal. Zero spoilage risk. |
| **Base Case** | 15% of pitched chefs | 1,200 kg | Run production at 60% capacity. Build 1-week safety stock. |
| **Optimistic** | 25%+ of pitched chefs | 2,000 kg (Cap) | Max out capacity. Implement waitlist for excess demand. |

**Decision Aid:**
Your break-even point is likely around **400–600 kg** (depending on your actual fixed costs). If your pilot survey suggests you cannot secure enough chefs to hit 600 kg within 3 months, **do not scale staffing yet**; operate as a boutique supplier until the conversion rate is proven.
