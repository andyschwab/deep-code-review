**The Regime:**
This issue is governed by **employment classification law**. In Texas, courts and agencies often apply a "right-to-control" test (similar to the common law test) to determine employment status. Key factors include who controls the details of the work, who provides tools, who sets the schedule, and who has the power to hire/fire. The Department of Labor and the IRS also have their own multi-factor tests. Misclassification can trigger back taxes, penalties, and workers' compensation liabilities.

**Why This Must Be Routed:**
The line between "protecting an asset" (permissible for a lessor) and "controlling the worker" (which suggests employment) is extremely thin and fact-dependent.
*   **Training:** Mandating specific safety training *might* be seen as asset protection, but if it dictates *how* the worker performs their job duties beyond basic equipment operation, it could be evidence of control.
*   **Schedule:** Enforcing a schedule for *maintenance* is likely fine; enforcing a schedule for *when the worker must wear the suit* is likely evidence of employment control.
*   **Monitoring:** Usage monitoring for theft prevention differs from monitoring for productivity enforcement.
