# Q1 2027 Primary Goal: Pre-read for CEO and CFO

**Recommendation:** Make activation the primary Q1 goal. Report ARR alongside it as a guardrail and board metric, not as the goal itself. Confirm at the offsite once the three pulls below are in.

## The decision
| | Revenue (ARR) goal | Activation goal |
|---|---|---|
| For | Board-visible, unambiguous | Earlier lever; the CEO sees it as the bottleneck |
| Against | Lagging; teams can't steer it directly. Current 3%/month growth (~9% per quarter, ~$390K ARR) may arrive with no team action, so a goal could be met by momentum | The 3x conversion gap may be partly selection. The payoff may land after Q1 |
| Main risk | Rewards discounting or short-term pricing moves | A rate that rises without revenue following |

## What the numbers say
- Activation is 29%, and activated users convert about 3x as often. If non-activated conversion is p, blended conversion is 1.58p.
- **Illustration only, with an assumed +5 points (29% to 34%):** if the gap were fully causal, blended conversion would rise about 6%. If half of it were causal, about 3%.
- So the revenue payoff of an activation gain is real but modest per quarter, and only for users who convert within the quarter. It is not a shortcut to the board number.

## Unknowns that decide this
1. **Causality.** The 3x is a correlation. Motivated users both activate and pay more.
2. **Lag.** We don't know how long signup-to-paid takes. If most paid conversions come after 60 days, Q1 ARR will barely register a Q1 activation gain.
3. **Quality.** Activation (publishing a first form) could be gamed by templates or nudges that don't produce lasting use.

## Proposed goal structure
- **Primary:** raise 7-day activation from 29%. Set the target after the baseline pull, not before.
- **Guardrails:** ARR growth stays at or above its own trailing trend, and the paid conversion of activated users doesn't fall.
- **Causal read:** run the activation changes as a controlled test with a holdout. Fix the sample size and duration in advance, name one primary metric and one guardrail, and read it once at the end. A mid-test win is not a result.
- **Revenue linkage:** track each cohort's paid conversion at 30, 60 and 90 days, so the board sees the link building.

## Where the CFO's view wins
If the lag analysis shows most conversions take longer than a quarter, an activation goal can't be validated in Q1 and leadership would be steering by faith. In that case, choose a revenue-linked input such as paid conversions from cohorts that activated, or keep revenue primary with activation as its named driver.

## This week
1. **Lag:** pull the distribution of days from signup to first payment for the last 4 to 6 monthly cohorts. Decision rule: if under about half of paid conversions occur within 60 days, favor the revenue-linked framing.
2. **Selection check:** compare paid conversion of activated vs. non-activated users within the same signup source and first-session behavior segments. If the 3x shrinks sharply within segments, lower the expected revenue payoff.
3. **Test design:** specify one activation experiment with a holdout, its minimum detectable effect, required sample, and duration, and check it fits in Q1.
