**Draft note to Liam**

Subject: Trial-reminder timing test: promising, and how to lock in the win

Liam, the early signal is encouraging. The new reminder timing showed an 11% lift in trial-to-paid conversion after one week, with about 9,800 users per arm. I'd like us to confirm it, so that what we announce holds up when finance or the board checks it.

**How far we can trust it today**
- **The test was designed for four weeks and read at day 7.** Stopping the first time a dashboard shows significance inflates the false-positive rate well above the nominal 5%. A one-week "significant" result is a strong lead, not a confirmed win. I can't say how likely it is to be noise without our baseline conversion rate and the confidence interval on the difference.
- **The October 1-3 discount covers 3 of the 7 days.** Randomization should have spread it across both arms, so it probably didn't bias the comparison. The risk is that the lift appears only when a discount is running, for example if the reminder works because it lands next to a sale. Then the result wouldn't carry over to a normal week. I haven't seen the data, so I can't say whether this happened.
- **Trial length matters.** Users who joined late in the week may not have reached their conversion decision yet. A better reminder time could also pull conversions forward without raising the total.

**Risks of rolling out now**
- We might ship a change with no real effect, then see the lift disappear and have to explain it.
- If the effect is discount-dependent or a timing shift, the quarter's numbers won't show the gain.
- We haven't checked guardrails: notification opt-outs, app uninstalls, refunds, and retention after the first payment.

**Proposed plan (this week)**
1. **Split the existing data by day and arm.** Compare the lift on Oct 1-3 with the lift on the other days. Similar lifts support the result. A lift only on discount days means we shouldn't roll out yet.
2. **Run two sanity checks.** Confirm the arms are the size we intended (a sample-ratio check), and confirm the discount-day users were split evenly across arms.
3. **Report the difference with a confidence interval, plus the guardrail metrics.** If the interval excludes zero by a margin that matters and the guardrails are clean, that's a much stronger story.
4. **Roll out next week with a 5-10% holdout on the old timing, and keep measuring through the original end date (Oct 26 or later).** Liam gets the rollout on his timeline, and the holdout gives us a clean read to confirm the win. The cost is that a small slice misses the benefit for a few weeks.

**What would change the plan:** if the lift holds on non-discount days and the guardrails are clean, we proceed with confidence. If it fades or depends on the discount, we pause and re-test.

*Notes for you:*
- I don't know your trial length or baseline conversion rate. The interval in step 3 and the timing caveat depend on both.
- Whether the lift is relative or absolute should be stated explicitly in the write-up.
