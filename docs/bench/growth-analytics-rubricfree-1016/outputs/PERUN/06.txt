# Tallyhouse launch instrumentation: priority order for Marco

**North Star:** weekly users who send at least one invoice. Retention is the same measure read by signup cohort. Everything below feeds it.

**Build before Nov 6 (about 2 dev-days, my rough split for Marco to rescope):**

1. **Stable pseudonymous user ID, signup timestamp, and source (UTM/referrer/partner tag) saved on the user record at signup.** Launch-day newsletter and partner traffic can't be re-attributed afterward, and without a stable ID nothing else can be followed across sessions.
2. **`invoice_sent` (with `invoice_created`), each with user ID and timestamp.** Sending an invoice is the core value action. These two events give you the North Star, activation, and every retention cohort.
3. **`invoice_paid` (or marked-paid), with user ID and timestamp.** It shows whether users get paid, which is the real value delivered, and it supports a revenue story later. Don't send client names or free text, and decide with Marco whether the amount is needed.
4. **Billing record of truth for Tallyhouse's own revenue.** Check that plan start, change, and cancel timestamps are kept with the user ID and can be exported. If you charge from day one, this is what a January revenue question will rely on. It is probably a check on the billing provider, not new code. I don't know your pricing model, so confirm which case applies.
5. **A login or app-open event.** It separates users who came back from users who merely have an account, and it is cheap once the ID exists.
6. **Verification (half a day, don't skip).** Fire the whole sequence for a test user on staging and confirm it lands in your analytics with the right ID and source. Do this before the launch spike, because bad data on Nov 6 can't be fixed.

**Can wait (add after launch when a question needs it):**
- Detailed onboarding-step events: with 340 beta users and one core action, first-invoice-sent already gives activation.
- Referral tracking (such as a "sent with Tallyhouse" link): useful later, not lost if added in a month.
- A/B testing tooling: too little traffic to read a test soundly.
- Session replay, NPS, feature-level click events, and more page-view detail.
- Cancellation-reason capture: nice to have, can be a plain exit survey later.

**Why this covers the January risk:** items 1 to 5 are the only data you can't reconstruct afterward. Retention and revenue can both be computed later from them. Everything on the wait list can start collecting in December without losing board-relevant history.

**Reading the launch:** your spike-then-trickle assumption is plausible but unverified. Split cohorts by source, so curious one-time visitors from the newsletter don't mask partner-referred users. Judge retention against your own beta baseline and trend, not an outside figure.

**This week:**
1. Email your lead investor or board chair: "Will you want retention and revenue at the January meeting, and as what definitions?" That answer matters more than any guess.
2. Ask Marco whether beta invoice rows have creation and sent timestamps in the database. If they do, you can rebuild beta retention history without new events.
3. Agree one definition of "active" (at least one invoice sent in a calendar week) and write it down before launch.

**Notes:** Keep the user ID pseudonymous, and have whoever owns privacy check consent and retention rules for launch-day visitors. I haven't seen your stack, so the day estimates are placeholders.
