# What to check first, and in what order

**Core arithmetic.** If week-4 retention really held at ~21% on ~1,800 more signups a week, you'd expect roughly 380 more week-4 retained users per weekly cohort (1,800 × 0.21). Whether that is visible depends on how large your total active base is, which I don't have. Flat retention plus more signups means one of three things: the extra signups aren't real people, the extra retained users are too small to show in total active users, or offsetting segments are hiding a change. Your assumption that new users resemble earlier ones is **unverified**, so test it rather than assume it.

## Order of work

1. **Signup definition (Sept 2).** Plot daily signups by method (email vs Google) and see whether the step up starts in mid-August (campaign) or on Sept 2 (login change). Check what event counts as a "signup" (account created vs email verified vs first session). Look for duplicate people, such as the same person with an email account and a Google account.
2. **Reconcile the two dashboards.** Confirm that "week-4 retention" and "active users" use the same definition of active, the same user ID, and the same treatment of Google-login accounts. Multiply cohort size by retention for each cohort and compare it to what active users shows.
3. **Split by acquisition source.** Compare campaign vs organic on weekly signups and on week-4 retention separately. First confirm campaign attribution is tagged reliably, since untagged traffic will blur the split.
4. **Split by method × source.** Flat 21% overall can hide campaign users retaining worse while Google-login users retain better, or the reverse.
5. **Size the effect.** Compare the incremental retained users to total weekly active users to see whether the lift should be visible at all.

## Explanations to rule in or out

| Explanation | What would show it |
|---|---|
| Signup count inflated by the definition change or duplicates | Step change on Sept 2, or a high share of Google signups matching existing emails |
| Campaign users are lower quality, masked by mix | Campaign cohort retention below organic, with another segment offsetting it |
| Retention/active definition excludes or mislabels Google accounts | Google accounts show near-zero activity events |
| Effect is real but too small to see in total actives | Step 5 shows the increment is a small share of the base |
| Cohorts too young | September cohorts after about Sept 18 haven't reached week 4 yet; exclude them |

Only the first, third and fourth can explain why the same data looks flat. The August step-up timing in step 1 tells you whether the Sept 2 question matters at all.
