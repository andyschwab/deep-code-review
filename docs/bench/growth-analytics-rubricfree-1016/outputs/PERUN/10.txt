# Report structure for the October 29 pricing committee

**1. Decision and recommendation (top of page)**
State the three options (keep $15, roll back to $12, try something else), your recommendation, and the main uncertainty in one sentence each.

**2. Test design**
- Starter plan, $12 to $15 per seat per month, random 50/50 split of new visitors, five weeks, ended Oct 3.
- Primary metric: revenue per visitor. Secondary: trial-to-paid conversion by segment.
- Sample: about 11,000 small-business visitors and 2,300 mid-size.
- Say whether the segment split was planned before the test. If not, label it exploratory.

**3. Results table**
Per segment and overall, for both arms: visitors, trials, paid conversions, conversion rate, seats per account, revenue per visitor, and a confidence interval on each difference.

**4. What we can conclude**
- **Small businesses:** conversion fell from 6.8% to 5.9%. My rough hand calculation, assuming about 5,500 visitors per arm, puts this near the edge of significance (p around 0.05). If the denominator is trials rather than visitors, the sample is smaller and the result is weaker. It is probably a real drop, but treat the size as uncertain.
- **Mid-size:** the rise from 4.1% to 4.6% is not distinguishable from noise. With roughly 1,150 per arm, the same rough calculation gives a z-score of about 0.6. Your instinct is right: report it as "no detectable change," not as an improvement.
- **Overall:** revenue per visitor rose about 4%. Report it with an interval. It is the only figure that matters for the decision, and I can't tell if it is significant.

**5. What we can't conclude**
- That mid-size customers tolerate the higher price better. The data doesn't show it.
- Long-term revenue impact. Five weeks can't show churn or retention at the higher price, and a lower-converting but pricier cohort may churn differently.
- Late-test trials may not have had time to convert before Oct 3. Check that trial length fits inside the window.
- Self-reported company size adds misclassification. It likely blurs the segment differences rather than creating them, but it is unchecked.

**6. A reconciliation problem to resolve first**
A 25% price rise with conversion down 13% (small) implies roughly +8% revenue per converting visitor, and mid-size roughly +40%. Both are above the reported overall +4%. Something is missing: other segments, seats per account, discounts, annual plans, or a conversion denominator that isn't visitors. Explain this before the meeting, because a committee member will spot it.

**7. Options and risks**
For each option, state what you'd expect, what it risks, and what evidence would support it. Examples: keep $15 overall, keep $15 only for mid-size, roll back for small, or run a longer follow-up test.

**8. This week**
1. Pull trials, conversions, seats and revenue per arm per segment. Compute intervals for each difference and for revenue per visitor.
2. Check that the arms are balanced (traffic source, device, segment mix) and that the trial window was fully observed.
3. Pull 30-day retention for paying customers in each arm. If $15 customers churn visibly faster, that would favor rolling back.

*Note: the p-values above are my approximations from your stated figures, not computed on your raw data.*
