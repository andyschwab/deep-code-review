# Recommendation: Checkout button test (draft for Dana Whitfield)

**Bottom line:** Don't call this a win yet, and don't drop it. The variant looks slightly better, but the test can't distinguish a real gain from noise. I recommend extending the test to a sample size we set in advance. If the change is cheap and easily reversed, a ship with a holdback (below) is a defensible alternative.

**What we saw**
- Each arm had about 48,200 visitors over 21 days.
- Control converted at 3.10% (roughly 1,494 orders) and the variant at 3.24% (roughly 1,562 orders).
- That is +0.14 percentage points, or about a 4.5% relative lift.

**How confident we should be: not very**
- The dashboard's "87% chance the variant is better" means we lean toward the variant, but it is well short of the 95% we would normally want before calling a win.
- By my rough calculation, the plausible range for the true difference runs from about **-0.08 to +0.36 points** (roughly -3% to +12% relative). Zero, or a small loss, is still inside that range. Please have the dashboard confirm this.
- To reliably detect a gap as small as 0.14 points, we would need roughly 5 times the current traffic per arm, which is about 3 to 4 months in total at current volume. This is my approximation, so please have it checked. If we only care about larger effects, the required sample is much smaller.

**Caveats Dana should know**
1. **We can't say why it moved.** The variant changed size, color and wording at once. Even if it wins, we won't know which change did it.
2. **Mobile vs. desktop is unchecked.** A blended result can hide a gain on one device and a loss on the other. A breakdown after the fact is a hint, not proof, so treat it as a reason to design the next test, not as a verdict.
3. **Two assumptions are unconfirmed.** We believe the traffic split was clean and nothing else on the site changed, but neither is verified. Either one could bias the result.
4. **The test design is unconfirmed.** I don't know whether the 21-day length and the primary metric were fixed before the test started, or whether anyone stopped to peek at results. If not, the 87% is even less trustworthy.
5. **We haven't checked for side effects.** "Pay securely now" could raise payment failures, refunds or cancellations, or lower order value, even while it lifts conversion.

**Options**
- **Keep testing (recommended):** Fix the sample size and end date in advance, name conversion as the primary metric, and add guardrails (payment failures, refund rate, order value). Read it once at the end.
- **Ship with a holdback:** If the change costs almost nothing to build and reverse, roll it out to most traffic and keep 10% on the old button to keep measuring. Treat it as a bet, not a proven win.
- **Drop it:** Not supported. The data lean positive, and a small gain is still possible.

**This week, before Friday, Oct 23**
1. Pull conversion by device (mobile/desktop) for both arms, with counts. If the variant is flat or negative on either device, that shifts me toward extending the test with a device-specific design.
2. Verify the split: check that the assignment ratio matches the intended one and that nothing was deployed on the checkout path between the test's start and end dates. Any imbalance or change means the result needs a rerun.
3. Pull the guardrails (payment failure rate, refund rate, average order value) by arm. A worse guardrail in the variant means don't ship, even if conversion is up.

*Note: I haven't seen the raw data, so the interval and sample-size figures are my rough estimates from the numbers you gave.*
