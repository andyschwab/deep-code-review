**To:** Jane Okafor (CEO), Head of Sales
**From:** Product
**Re:** One company-wide top-line metric, starting January 2027

**Recommendation**
Adopt **Collaborating Workspaces** as the company metric: the number of paying workspaces in which at least three members edited a board in the last 30 days.

**Why this metric**
- **It measures value delivered, not activity or sales.** Seats sold only shows what we sold. Ticket volume mostly shows problems, and it moves for good and bad reasons. Daily active users (about 18,000) counts individuals, so one person using the tool alone counts the same as a team working together.
- **It matches what we sell.** Northwind is a team product. Our working belief is that customers get value when several people work on boards together. Today about 62% of paying workspaces already meet the three-editor bar, so there is room to move and the number is not already maxed out.
- **Every department can move it.** Sales can bring in teams likely to collaborate, product can make shared work easier, and support can help stalled workspaces get going.
- **It is counted in workspaces, so it ties to renewals.** Leadership can read it as "how many of our customers are getting team value."

**Two caveats we must resolve first**
1. **The link to value is an assumption.** We have not confirmed with customer interviews that team use is what customers value. Before January we should interview a dozen or so customers and check that workspaces meeting the bar renew and expand more than those that don't. If they don't, we change the metric.
2. **Large accounts may behave differently.** We have never split usage by account size. A three-editor threshold may be too easy for a 200-seat account and too hard for a 5-seat team. We should split the data by size band before locking the definition. We may need a threshold that scales with seats, for example a share of seats rather than a fixed three.

**Supporting metrics (the levers underneath)**
1. **Share of paying workspaces that are collaborating:** the 62% today, shown by size band. This keeps big and small accounts comparable.
2. **Time to first collaboration:** days from workspace creation until a third member edits a board. It shows whether onboarding gets teams to value fast.
3. **Collaborating workspaces retained after 90 days:** whether teams that reach the bar keep paying. This is the check that the metric predicts what we care about. Only count workspaces that have been active for the full 90 days. Newer ones are still pending and should not be treated as failures.

**What each department keeps**
Seats sold, ticket volume and daily active users stay as department metrics, but they report into this one rather than competing with it.

**Next steps**
- Customer interviews and size-band split: complete by end of November.
- Confirm the final definition and threshold: early December.
- Publish a baseline, then report monthly from January 2027.

**Note:** The 62% figure comes from your current data, but I haven't seen how it breaks down by account size, so treat it as a company-wide average only.
