# Pre-Read: Primary Team Goal for Q1 2027
**For:** Amara Lindqvist (CFO), CEO | **From:** Head of Product | **Decision at:** Nov 20 offsite

## Recommendation
Set **activation** as the primary Q1 goal, with **new-customer revenue** as a named guardrail and a stated Q2 revenue expectation. Rationale: it is the lever product controls most directly, it is measurable within the quarter, and it serves the board's revenue goal. If the group needs one number the board sees, use a combined framing: "Raise 7-day activation from 29% to X%, with a leading-indicator forecast of revenue impact."

## The two options against the stated goals
| | Revenue goal (CFO) | Activation goal (CEO) |
|---|---|---|
| Serves | Board-tracked outcome | Suspected funnel bottleneck |
| Controllability | Low: ARR growth (~3%/month, roughly 42% annualized) is driven by pricing, sales, retention and the existing base, not just product | High: product owns onboarding |
| Feedback speed | Slow, noisy | Within 7 days per cohort |
| Main risk | Product team optimizes things it can't move in one quarter | Gains may not reach revenue, or only after Q1 |
| What it trades away | Funnel fix deferred | Direct accountability to the board metric |

## What we know and don't
- **Known:** ARR $4.2M, ~3%/month growth; activation 29%; activated users convert to paid at ~3x the rate of non-activated users.
- **Unknown, and decisive:**
 1. **How much of the 3x is causal.** Activated users are self-selected: motivated people both activate and pay. The true lift is somewhere between 1x and 3x.
 2. **Lag from activation to revenue.** If trial-to-paid takes more than a few weeks, Q1 activation gains will mostly land in Q2 revenue. Cohorts still inside their conversion window should be treated as pending, not as failures.

## Illustrative sensitivity (assumptions, not forecasts)
Let non-activated conversion be c, so activated is 3c. Blended rate = 0.29(3c) + 0.71c = 1.58c. Raising activation to 35% gives 0.35(3c) + 0.65c = 1.70c, a **7.6% increase** in paid conversion if the 3x were fully causal. If only half the gap is causal (activated = 2c): 1.29c rises to 1.35c, about **4.7%**. Even the optimistic case adds a modest increment to new-customer volume, and new customers are only part of ARR growth. So activation alone will probably not visibly move Q1 ARR. This is the CFO's strongest point, and the plan should say so openly.

## How to de-risk (these need to happen before or early in Q1)
1. **Measure the lag now:** from historical signup cohorts, plot days from signup to first payment. This tells us what share of a Q1 activation gain can convert inside Q1. (Use only cohorts whose observation window has closed.)
2. **Test causality:** run onboarding changes as A/B experiments, holding out a control group, and measure paid conversion, not just activation. A change that raises activation without raising conversion falsifies the bottleneck thesis early.
3. **Pre-agree a decision rule:** e.g., if tested activation lifts show no conversion lift by mid-Q1, shift the goal toward revenue levers.
4. **Report both:** weekly activation by cohort, and paid conversion for matured cohorts, each over matching closed windows.

## Where the CFO's case wins
If the lag analysis shows conversion takes 60+ days, or experiments show a weak causal link, revenue (or paid conversion/retention) becomes the better primary goal. The recommendation above is conditional on those checks, which can be done before Nov 20.

## Asks for the offsite
- Agree on activation as primary, with a target set after the lag analysis.
- Agree that revenue is a guardrail with an explicit review point.
- Commit to the experiment design above.
