**To:** Dana Whitfield | **Re:** Checkout button test, ship / keep testing / drop | **Date:** Oct 23

**Recommendation:** Don't call this a win yet. The result is a small, uncertain lift. Because the change is cheap and the downside looks small, I recommend shipping the green button only if the checks below come back clean, and describing it internally as a low-risk bet rather than a proven improvement. If any check fails, keep the grey button.

**What we saw (21 days, about 48,200 visitors per arm)**
- Control converted at 3.10% and the variant at 3.24%, a gain of 0.14 percentage points, or about 4.5% relative.
- That is roughly 70 extra orders per 48,200 visitors (about 1,562 vs. 1,494).

**How confident we should be: moderately at best**
- The dashboard's 87% means roughly a 1-in-8 chance the variant is not actually better. That is below the 95% bar we normally use to call a winner.
- By my calculation from the rounded figures, the plausible range for the true effect runs from about -0.08 to +0.36 points (roughly -2.5% to +12% relative). It includes "slightly worse" and "meaningfully better."
- The test was sized to detect a lift of about 10% relative. The observed 4.5% is smaller, so this test can't settle it. Confirming a lift that size would take about 100 more days at current traffic (my rough estimate).

**Caveats**
1. **Unverified assumptions.** We believe the traffic split was clean and that nothing else on the site changed, but neither is confirmed. Before Friday, check that the arms are split as intended (a ~50/50 check) and review the site change log for the 21 days.
2. **No device breakdown yet.** We don't know whether the effect holds on both mobile and desktop. A button change can behave very differently across the two. Run this breakdown, but treat it as exploratory, because splitting the data further makes false signals more likely.
3. **Two changes at once.** The variant changed size, color and label. We can't say which one drove any difference.
4. **Only conversion rate is reported.** Check average order value, payment failures and refunds. "Pay securely now" sounds final, and it could affect behavior in ways conversion rate alone won't show.
5. **Duration.** 21 days is three full weeks, which is good for weekly cycles, but it doesn't cover promotions or seasonal shifts.

**Options**

| Option | Serves | Trades away |
|---|---|---|
| Ship (after checks) | Fast, low-cost chance at a small lift | Certainty; we may be shipping a neutral change |
| Keep testing | A defensible answer | About 100 days of traffic on a small expected gain |
| Drop | Simplicity | A possible lift of up to roughly 12% |

If we ship, I suggest keeping a small holdback on the grey button for a few weeks so we can keep measuring. If the device split shows the variant losing on one device, ship only where it helps.

*Note: all figures above are my calculations from the rounded numbers you gave, so please re-run them on the raw counts before sending. I don't know the dashboard's method, so I can't say exactly how its 87% was derived.*
