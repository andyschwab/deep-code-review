**Draft note to Liam**

**Subject: Trial-reminder timing test: where we stand and a path to a bankable win**

Liam, the early result is encouraging, and I think we can turn it into a win that holds up. Here is my read.

**What we know**
After seven days, with about 9,800 users per arm, the new reminder timing showed an 11% lift in trial-to-paid conversion, flagged significant. That is a good sign for the idea.

**How far I'd trust it today: directionally, not yet as a number**
1. **Early stopping inflates results.** The test was planned for four weeks. Significance thresholds assume we look once, at the end. Checking a dashboard daily and stopping when it turns green makes false positives much more likely, and the lifts we catch this way tend to be overstated. The true effect may be positive but smaller than 11%.
2. **Some trials hadn't finished.** Users who started trials late in the week hadn't reached their conversion decision. Those users are still pending, not non-converters. If the arms differ in how many pending users they hold, the lift could be skewed. The clean comparison uses only trials whose window has closed.
3. **The Oct 1-3 discount fell inside our one week.** Random assignment means both arms should have received it equally, so it probably didn't create the lift. But it could still matter in two ways. It lifted baseline conversion, so the effect without a discount may differ. It could also interact with reminder timing, for example if the reminder works better when a deal is live. This week wasn't a normal week.

**Risks of rolling out to everyone now**
- We may ship a smaller effect than we report, and the quarter's numbers could disappoint.
- We lose our control group, so we can't confirm the effect later.
- Unmeasured side effects, such as notification opt-outs or uninstalls, go unwatched.

**What I suggest (about a week of work, not four)**
1. Re-cut the data using only closed-trial cohorts, with a confidence interval, not just a point estimate.
2. Split results by enrollment date: discount days (Oct 1-3) versus the other days. If the lift shows up in both, that's strong evidence.
3. Check that arm sizes match the intended split, and review opt-out and uninstall rates.
4. Roll out to ~90% with a ~10% holdout, and keep measuring. We get the upside immediately, and by quarter-end we can report a lift that has been validated.

If the re-cut shows a lift in both date groups with an interval that excludes zero, I'd back the full rollout and be glad to present it as a confirmed win.

**Notes for you (Remi)**
- I don't have the raw conversion counts, trial length or the dashboard's method, so I can't give a confidence interval or say whether 11% is relative or absolute. Fill those in before sending.
- If the re-cut doesn't show a lift in both date groups, the holdout still lets you resume the test cleanly.
