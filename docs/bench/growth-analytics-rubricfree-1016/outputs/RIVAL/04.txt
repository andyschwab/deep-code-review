# What to check, in order

**Step 0: Mark which cohorts are mature.** Week-four retention needs 28 days of data. As of Oct 16, cohorts after roughly Sept 18 are still pending, so the "flat at 21%" for September covers only part of the month. Exclude pending cohorts from the rate and label them pending. The cohorts that matter are mid-August through mid-September, which are the first campaign cohorts.

**Step 1: Test the Sept 2 definition change.** The lift began in mid-August, two weeks before Google login, so the login change can't explain the whole increase. Compute weekly signups by method (email, Google, other) from the raw account table, using a script rather than a dashboard. Check:
- Mid-Aug to Sept 1 signups: is the lift already there? If so, it's campaign-driven.
- After Sept 2: any step-change in signups or in the share of Google signups.
- Duplicates: the same person creating one email account and one Google account, or Google accounts that don't merge with existing email accounts. Match on normalized email.

**Step 2: Reconcile the counts.** If retention really is 21% for both periods, retained users per week should rise from about 1,090 (5,200 × 21%) to about 1,470 (7,000 × 21%), about 35% more. If active users haven't risen, one of these is true:
- Retention isn't actually flat once you split by source (the blend hides it).
- The retention or active-user definitions differ from each other.
- The new accounts are inflating the denominator and numerator inconsistently.

This is the sharpest framing for Theo's question. Recompute week-four retained counts two ways (from events, and from the cohort table) and chase any mismatch to specific rows.

**Step 3: Split by acquisition source.** Report paid-social-attributed cohorts on their own, not folded into the total. Compare week-four retention, activation (first budget or account link), and paid conversion for paid social vs. everything else, using mature cohorts only. Your assumption that new users resemble old ones is the thing to test here.

**Step 4: Check the rest of the ruling-in list.**

| Explanation | Rules in if | Rules out if |
|---|---|---|
| Paid users are lower intent but retention is masked by the blend | Paid-social week-4 retention is clearly below 21% and others are above | Paid retention is ~21% too |
| Duplicate or non-merged Google accounts | Matching emails across accounts; Google share jumps Sept 2 | Duplicate rate is negligible |
| Bots or low-quality signups | Odd device, geography or zero-event accounts | Event profiles look normal |
| Cannibalized organic signups | Organic fell as paid rose | Organic is steady |
| Seasonality | Prior-year Aug–Sept shows a similar bump | No prior pattern |
| Active-user metric mismatch | Definition differs from the retention event | They use the same event |

**Step 5: Cost per outcome.** Divide campaign spend by week-four retained users, using the same closed weeks for both. Don't divide full-period spend by partial-period retained users.
