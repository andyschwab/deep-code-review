Here is a draft review plan. I haven't seen your system, so treat the specifics as assumptions to check, and I haven't run any tests.

## Main risks

The core issue is that users may act within minutes on a summary that is often the only text they see. That makes these risks most important:

- **Factual errors that move money.** Wrong figures, a reversed direction ("beats" vs "misses"), the wrong ticker or a similarly named company, rumor presented as confirmed fact, and dropped hedges like "according to unnamed sources."
- **Stale or superseded news.** Corrections, retractions, and updates that arrive after a notification has gone out.
- **Manipulation.** Fake press releases or planted text meant to move a price, and instructions hidden in article text that try to steer the model. Treat all provider content as untrusted input.
- **Advice-like language.** Phrases like "good time to buy" may carry regulatory implications depending on your jurisdiction.
- **Licensing.** Summaries may breach the providers' limits on length, derivative use, attribution, or display in push notifications.
- **Operational.** Burst load during market-wide events, provider outages, duplicate or out-of-order notifications, and no fast way to turn the feature off.

## What to test

1. **Accuracy evaluation set.** Build a labeled set of historical breaking stories, including earnings, M&A rumors versus confirmations, halts, guidance changes, corrections, and multi-company stories. Score numeric fidelity, entity and ticker resolution, direction and sentiment, certainty and attribution, and omissions. Define pass thresholds before you look at results, and weight errors by severity (a flipped number is worse than awkward phrasing).
2. **Notification-specific testing.** Push text is short and context-free. Test truncation on small screens, lock-screen display, and whether the summary stays accurate at that length.
3. **Adversarial testing.** Feed articles containing embedded instructions, fabricated releases, ambiguous tickers, and non-English or garbled text. Check that the system declines or flags low-confidence cases rather than guessing.
4. **Guardrails.** Test for advice or prediction language, and check that a low-confidence or failed check suppresses the push and falls back to the headline.
5. **Corrections flow.** When a source is corrected or retracted, confirm the summary updates or is withdrawn, and decide how users who already saw it are told.
6. **License compliance.** Automate checks of output length and attribution against each provider's terms, and have counsel confirm the terms actually cover AI summaries.
7. **Reliability and load.** Replay a high-volume news day, test provider and model-vendor outages and timeouts, and check latency from publication to notification. Confirm the kill switch works without a deploy.
8. **Shadow mode.** Run the feature against the live feed with no user exposure for a couple of weeks. Have humans review a sample, including every high-severity category.
9. **Monitoring and logging.** Log input, output, model version, and prompt version for audit. Track user reports, corrections, and suppression rates.

## Sign-offs

- **Product owner**: scope, launch criteria, user-facing labeling (clear "AI-generated" marking and a link to the source).
- **Engineering lead**: reliability, rollback, and on-call readiness.
- **ML/evaluation owner**: accuracy results against the thresholds. This should be someone other than the person who built the prompts.
- **Legal/compliance**: securities-related regulation (advice, market manipulation, disclosures) in the jurisdictions you serve, plus the data-licensing review.
- **Content licensing/partnerships**: confirmation from both providers, ideally written.
- **Security and privacy**: prompt-injection results, and the model vendor's data-handling terms.
- **Support/operations**: a playbook for user complaints and a process for issuing corrections.
- **Executive sponsor**: accepts any residual risk.

## Suggested eight-week shape

- **Weeks 1–2:** Legal and licensing review starts now, since it has the longest lead time and may change the design. Build the evaluation set and set thresholds.
- **Weeks 3–4:** Fix issues found in evaluation and add guardrails, the corrections flow, and the kill switch. Run adversarial testing.
- **Weeks 5–6:** Shadow mode, load and outage tests, and human review of samples.
- **Week 7:** Go/no-go review with sign-offs. Start an internal or small-percentage rollout.
- **Week 8:** Hold a buffer for fixes and a staged ramp.

With four engineers, push the notification path in last or ship it separately if time runs short. In-app summaries beside tickers are lower risk than push, because users can see more context and the summary is not interrupting them.

## Recommendations

Decide now what happens when the system is uncertain. The safest default is to show the headline and source link rather than a summary. Also consider a gradual rollout starting with in-app only, then push for a small cohort, with a pre-agreed rollback trigger. If the accuracy or legal work isn't done by week seven, slipping the push component is a better outcome than shipping it.
