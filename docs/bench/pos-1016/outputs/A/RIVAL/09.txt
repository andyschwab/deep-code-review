The central idea is to spend human attention where a mistake could hurt someone, and to make the AI's job small enough that most errors can be caught mechanically.

**1. Shrink what the AI is allowed to write**

- Have the AI write only the narrative: what the product is, who it suits, and taste, form, or use. Pull safety-critical content from your data and render it verbatim: dosage, serving size, ingredient and allergen lists, and label warnings (pregnancy, medication interactions, age limits).
- Give the model only the spec sheet and instruct it to use no outside knowledge. If a fact isn't in the source, it shouldn't appear.
- Ban disease and treatment language. No "treats," "cures," "prevents," "detox," "clinically proven," "safe for pregnancy," or named medical conditions. Describe what the product contains and what it is traditionally used for, with a standard disclaimer.
- If a spec sheet lacks warnings or dosage, flag the item for a supplier follow-up. Never let the AI fill the gap.

**2. Automated checks on every page**

Engineers can build these in a few days:
- Compare every number, ingredient, and allergen in the description against the source data, and fail on anything extra or missing.
- Scan for banned phrases and require the disclaimer and the verbatim warning block.
- Run a second model pass that checks the description claim by claim against the spec sheet and lists unsupported claims. Treat this as a filter that routes pages to humans, not as a replacement for them.

Anything that fails goes to the human queue.

**3. Tier the catalog by risk**

- **Tier 1, review 100%:** prenatal and pregnancy-adjacent items, anything for children or teens, herbals with known drug interactions (St. John's wort, ginkgo, kava, and similar), and products touching blood sugar, blood pressure, sleep, mood, weight loss, or hormones. Also include stimulant-heavy sports products. Expect a few hundred items. At roughly five minutes each, that is a manageable number of hours split across the team.
- **Tier 2, review 100%:** any page that failed an automated check.
- **Tier 3, sample:** plain vitamins, minerals, and basic protein powders that pass all checks. Start by reading 10–15%. If you find even one safety-relevant error, widen the sample and fix the cause.

Reviewing is a matching task (does the page agree with the source?), so engineers can do the factual pass and the merchandiser the tone pass. No copy editor is needed.

**4. Use your fifty test pages now**

Have two people review all fifty this week and log every error by type: invented facts, wrong numbers, overclaiming, missing warnings, and tone. This gives you a real error rate, shows which categories need rules, and supplies examples for your banned list and checks. Without it, the sampling rates above are guesses.

**5. Get a one-time claims check**

Supplement marketing is regulated (FDA rules on structure/function versus disease claims, and FTC rules on substantiation). A short session with a regulatory or food-and-drug attorney to approve your claims policy and disclaimer language is far cheaper than reviewing copy. I can't give legal advice, but this is worth doing before launch.

**6. Four-week timeline**

- **Week 1:** Review the fifty pages, tighten the prompt and templates, build the checks, tier the catalog, and hold the claims-policy review.
- **Week 2:** Regenerate in batches, run the checks, and begin Tier 1 review. Send missing-data items to suppliers.
- **Week 3:** Finish Tier 1 and 2 review, sample Tier 3, and publish in waves starting with the lowest-risk items.
- **Week 4:** Keep this as a buffer for fixes, supplier answers, and a final spot-check of what's live.

Set a hard rule now: a page that isn't reviewed or sampled by the sale date doesn't get AI copy. Use the supplier text or a plain factual template instead. Missing a few pages costs far less than one wrong pregnancy or interaction statement.

**7. After launch**

- Add a "report an error" link on product pages, and watch returns and customer emails for health-related complaints.
- Log the prompt version, source data version, and reviewer for each page, so you can find and pull affected pages quickly.
- Regenerate and recheck a page whenever the supplier updates its spec sheet.

This plan limits risk but doesn't eliminate it. Its strength is that the highest-stakes content never depends on the AI getting it right, and your limited review time goes to the pages where errors matter most.
