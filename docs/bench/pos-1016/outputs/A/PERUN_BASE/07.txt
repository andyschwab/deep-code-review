**Bottom line:** I can't tell you this feature is safe to ship, and no review can. What I can give you is a harm inventory, a test plan, and a sign-off structure. The ship decision belongs to your named owner. Because users may trade within minutes, and the summary is often all they see, wrong output here can directly cost them money.

## Harm inventory

- **Hallucination as fact.** Wrong figures, wrong direction ("beats" vs "misses"), or the wrong company or ticker. Affected: any user who acts on it. Worst case: a trade on a false claim.
- **Stale or retracted news.** A summary persists after a correction or retraction.
- **Over-reliance.** The license limits source text, so users can't easily check the summary. Nothing tells them how reliable it is.
- **Deceptive urgency.** Push notifications can read as "act now" and nudge hasty trades.
- **Missing disclosure.** Users can't tell the text is machine-generated.
- **Bias.** Coverage and summary quality may skew toward large caps, English sources, or one provider.
- **Injection and manipulation.** Hostile or planted text in a feed could steer a summary. This is a sweep for the generation pipeline, alongside excess agency.
- **Excess agency.** Any link from a notification to a trade must not execute without explicit confirmation.

## Human gate (the floor)

Nothing in this feature should place or pre-fill a trade without explicit user confirmation. For push notifications, I'd gate by default: a human reviews before a summary pushes, or only items that pass automated grounding checks are pushed, with failures held for review. Your owner may loosen this, but only with measured evidence. Wording should never be imperative.

## Confidence display

Don't show model percentages. Use a few defined tiers, such as Confirmed (multiple providers agree), Single-source, and Unverified. Pair each with text and a colourblind-safe cue, and publish a definition per tier. Keep source reliability separate from claim corroboration. Add a visible "AI-generated" label, a timestamp, and a one-tap path to the full original where the license allows.

## Test plan: one row per harm

Every row needs a probe set with a known-bad positive control and a known-good negative control, sliced by segment. Thresholds are yours to set. I'd propose zero tolerance for any failure that reaches a push, and I won't suggest a "typical" rate.

| Harm | Probe set | Metric | Stop condition |
|---|---|---|---|
| Hallucination | Sampled real articles with planted number and entity traps; earnings and M&A edge cases | Share of summaries with an unsupported claim, checked against source | Any failure in the push path blocks launch |
| Stale/retracted | Replayed correction and retraction sequences | Time to withdraw or flag | Failing replay blocks launch |
| Over-reliance | Usability sessions with part-time investors | Do users notice the tier and label? Do they reach the source? | Owner decides on a UX fix |
| Urgency | Copy review of all push templates | Imperative or urgent phrasing found | Any found: rewrite |
| Disclosure | UI audit across surfaces (ticker, push, lock screen) | Surfaces missing the label | Any missing: fix |
| Bias | Summaries sliced by market cap, sector, provider, language | Quality gap across slices | Gap above owner's threshold |
| Injection/excess agency | Adversarial text in feed items; attempts to trigger actions | Successful steering or action | Any success blocks launch |

Until you run these, every rate is **UNVERIFIED**. Define a probe budget and who reviews failures. Keep a rollback switch tested, and a kill switch for pushes specifically.

## Transparency artifact

Write a model card covering intended use, out-of-scope uses, subgroup performance from the tests above, known failure modes, model version, and owner. Add a short data note on both providers' feeds. Leave unmeasured cells marked UNVERIFIED rather than filled.

## Questions for counsel, not me

Please route these to qualified counsel; I won't assert the answers:

- Whether AI summaries next to tickers could be treated as investment advice or a regulated communication in your jurisdictions.
- Whether and how AI disclosure is legally required.
- Whether your two providers' licenses permit derived summaries and push delivery, which is a contract question.
- Which AI-governance regimes (for example the EU AI Act, ISO/IEC 42001, the NIST AI RMF) apply to you, and any deadlines.

## Sign-off

- **Accountable owner:** one named product lead who makes the ship call.
- **Engineering lead:** pipeline, kill switch, and rollback.
- **Legal/compliance:** the routed questions above.
- **Domain reviewer:** someone with market-data or editorial expertise to grade summary accuracy.
- **Partnerships/licensing:** the provider terms.
- **Support:** an incident path for wrong summaries.

Record the owner for each harm and re-review whenever the model, prompts, or providers change.

## Eight-week shape

With four engineers, build the eval set and the push gate first (weeks 1–3). Run tests and fix (weeks 3–6). Complete the model card and counsel answers (weeks 5–7), then sign-off. A full governance program can wait, but the push gate and the no-invented-metrics rule can't. If counsel's answers or zero-tolerance tests aren't done in time, slip the push notifications and ship in-app summaries only.
