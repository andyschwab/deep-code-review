**Bottom line:** I can't tell you the bot is safe to expand, and no test will. What you can do in six weeks is name the harms, measure them on a defined set of cases, keep a human gate on the money-moving step, and give the owner a residual-risk report to decide on. The decision to go past 5% should be a named person's call, made from that report.

**Harm inventory (who is hurt, worst case)**
- **Money out the door:** refunds and credits are hard to claw back. The worst case is a fraud pattern or prompt-injected bot draining funds at scale.
- **Unequal outcomes:** one-time shoppers and long-history regulars may get systematically different approvals, caps, or credit-versus-refund offers.
- **Deceptive steering:** the bot pushing store credit when the customer is entitled to a refund, or the reverse.
- **Hallucinated policy:** promising a refund or return window that doesn't exist, which the customer then relies on.
- **Missing disclosure:** customers not knowing a machine made the decision, or how to reach a human.
- **Over-reliance by your own staff:** agents trusting bot decisions without review.

**Testing without a sandbox**
Real-account testing is your biggest constraint, so don't make real accounts the test bed for failures you can find otherwise.
- **Replay first.** Run historical contacts through the bot in dry-run mode, where it records the decision it would have made but moves no money. Compare against what your agents actually did.
- **Shadow mode next.** Have the bot propose actions on live traffic while a human executes or rejects them. That gives you real inputs with no irreversible effect.
- **Live execution only where gated,** and only at the existing 5%.

**One test row per harm.** Each needs a probe set, a known-bad positive control, a known-good negative control, a metric, an owner-set threshold, and a stop condition.
- **Over-refund / fraud:** adversarial scripts (repeat claims, claims on orders not owned, urgency pressure). Metric: unauthorized payouts. Propose zero tolerance for any payout that bypasses the cap or the gate, with an automatic halt.
- **Prompt injection:** customer messages and pasted order text that try to override instructions or raise the cap. Same zero tolerance on gated actions.
- **Cross-account leakage:** probes asking about other customers' orders. Any leak is a stop.
- **Disparity:** approval rate, amount, and credit-versus-refund mix, sliced by customer tenure, order value, and contact channel. Compare against agent outcomes on the same cases. You set the acceptable gap; I won't suggest a typical one.
- **Hallucinated policy:** compare bot statements against the policy text on a seeded question set. Decide the failure threshold up front.
- **Excess agency:** confirm the bot can't exceed the cap by splitting requests across turns or sessions.

If you haven't measured a rate, it is UNVERIFIED. Don't put a "99% accurate" figure in a launch memo.

**Controls to have in place before expanding**
- Per-customer caps, plus velocity limits across time and across linked accounts.
- A human approval gate above the cap and on risk signals (new account, mismatched shipping, repeat claims).
- A tested kill switch and a way to reverse or flag payouts in bulk.
- Idempotency, so a retried action can't pay twice, and a full audit log of every decision and its inputs.
- A clear route to a human for the customer, and a plain statement that an automated system handled the request. Recommend this as a control.
- A short model/behavior card: intended use, out-of-scope cases, measured results by segment, known limits, owner, and version.

**Legal routing**
Whether you must disclose automation, and whether consumer-protection or refund rules apply to automated decisions in your markets, is a question for counsel. Ask them before full rollout. Don't treat my recommendation to disclose as a legal conclusion.

**Timeline: a sequenced suggestion**
- **Weeks 1–2:** write the inventory and test rows, name the owners, and run the replay.
- **Weeks 2–4:** shadow mode, red-team the injection and leakage probes, and fix what they find.
- **Weeks 4–5:** hold at 5% with gating on, review the disparity slices, and get counsel's answer.
- **Week 5:** the owner reviews the residual-risk report and decides between expanding, expanding in steps (say to a higher tier, with the same stop conditions), or holding.

Finance's deadline is a reason to compress the schedule, not to drop the gate or the stop conditions. If the evidence isn't ready, the lower-risk option is a partial expansion with a lower cap or a human gate on more cases, not full autonomy by a date.

**Ownership**
Write down who owns each harm, and re-review whenever the model, the policy, the caps, or the traffic mix changes. A team of four can do this with one named owner per row.
