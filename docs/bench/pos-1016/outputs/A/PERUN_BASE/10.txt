**Bottom line:** I can't tell you this is safe or unbiased to roll out, and no test will. What you can do before next month is build a harm inventory, run measurements, gate the riskiest action, and hand the ship decision to named owners with the residual risk written down. The most important finding is that "decline" with a landlord who doesn't read further is effectively an automated housing decision, so treat it as high-stakes.

## Harm inventory (who is affected, worst case)

- **Bias and unfair exclusion:** Applicants with irregular income, recent moves (thin rental history), or housing assistance may be scored down for traits that proxy for protected classes or lawful income sources. Worst case: systematic denial of housing to a group.
- **Hallucination as fact:** The rationale may misread an income document or invent a rental-history problem, and landlords will treat it as authoritative. Worst case: a wrongful decline based on a claim that isn't in the file.
- **Over-reliance:** Landlords follow the output without reading. Nothing in the design currently pushes back on that.
- **No disclosure or recourse:** Applicants never see the recommendation, so they can't correct errors or contest it.
- **Unsafe automation:** A decline is hard to reverse in a tight market, since the unit is gone by the time an error is found.
- **Data leakage and injection:** Uploaded documents and references are untrusted input. They could carry instructions that steer the output, or expose one applicant's data in another's rationale.

## Gates to finish before rollout

1. **Human gate on decline.** Decline should never be a one-click outcome. Require the landlord to open the cited evidence and confirm, and consider renaming it "needs review" until measurements support more. If you can't finish the measurement below in time, the fallback is to ship approve/review only for the large partner.
2. **Rationales must cite the source.** Every claim in a rationale should point to a document and field. Unsupported claims shouldn't render.
3. **Show confidence as defined tiers** (for example Corroborated, Single-source, Unverified), not a percentage, and show when the file is thin so a thin file isn't read as a bad file.
4. **Easy override and correction path,** with overrides logged.
5. **Model card and data card.** Cover intended use, out-of-scope uses, performance by subgroup, known limitations, data provenance, owner, and model version. Any unmeasured cell reads UNVERIFIED.

## Measurement plan: one row per harm

Each row needs a probe set with a known-bad positive control and a known-good negative control, a metric, an owner-set threshold, a stop condition, and a named reviewer. I won't suggest thresholds, so your owners should set them. I'd propose zero tolerance for fabricated claims in a decline rationale.

- **Bias:** Run matched-pair probes that vary only income pattern, move recency, or assistance source, and compare recommendation distributions across slices. Stop condition: a gap above your threshold blocks launch for that slice. Subgroup slicing depends on data you may not collect, so ask counsel before inferring protected attributes.
- **Hallucination:** Take a sample of real beta files and have a human check every rationale claim against the source. Metric: unsupported-claim rate in decline rationales. Include documents with missing or ambiguous fields.
- **Over-reliance:** Mine your beta logs for how often landlords open evidence and how often they override. I haven't seen these numbers, so they're UNVERIFIED until you pull them.
- **Injection and leakage:** Plant adversarial text in documents and references, and test for cross-applicant exposure. Any success is a stop.
- **Excess agency:** Confirm the system can't send notices or change application status on its own.

## Route to counsel (I won't assert the duties)

Ask counsel now, since this is likely the longest-lead item:

- Whether this is a regulated consumer-report or screening decision, and what that means for adverse-action notices and applicant access or dispute rights (FCRA is the regime to ask about).
- Fair housing exposure, including disparate impact, for you and your landlord customers.
- State and local source-of-income protections, which vary by jurisdiction and bear on the housing-assistance applicants.
- Whether any state automated-decision or AI laws apply, and whether applicants must be told an automated tool was used. I'd recommend disclosure as a control regardless of what counsel says is required.
- Contract terms: who is responsible for the decision, and what the partner is allowed to do with a recommendation.

Name the frameworks counsel and your owners may care about, such as the NIST AI RMF and the EU AI Act, without assuming any applies. Whether one binds you is counsel's call.

## Governance for a nine-person team

Write down one owner per harm and what triggers a re-review: a model change, a prompt change, new document types, or a new jurisdiction. Stage the rollout to a subset of the partner's portfolio, with monitoring of recommendation distributions and overrides, before the full portfolio.

## Decision for the owners

If the measurement rows aren't done by next month, the honest options are a narrower rollout (no decline recommendation, or a slower staged rollout) or a delay. The contract pressure is real, but a wrongful-decline pattern at a large portfolio is the outcome that costs far more than the hire. The ship call is yours, and it should be made against written residual risk, not a clean bill of health.
