**Short answer:** I can't tell you whether you're legally required to give specific reasons, and I can't give you an accuracy figure. The first is a legal determination for counsel. The second hasn't been measured, so any number would be invented.

**1. Are specific reasons legally required?**

This depends on your jurisdictions, on whether a pre-approval score counts as a credit decision, and on how it feeds the final decision. Questions for counsel:
- Which regimes apply? In the US, the usual candidates are adverse-action notice rules under ECOA/Regulation B and the FCRA, plus state law. If you serve EU residents, credit scoring is a candidate under the EU AI Act. Counsel decides which of these bind, and when.
- Does a low score count as an adverse action or a denial, or is it only informational?
- Do the explanations have to reflect the model's actual drivers? A fluent AI paragraph that doesn't match them could be a problem under any of these regimes.
- Is an AI-use disclosure required? I'd recommend one regardless.

Until counsel answers, don't state a legal duty in the FAQ, in either direction.

**2. The accuracy figure**

Mark it `UNVERIFIED`. The pilot may have produced data, but nobody has measured explanation accuracy. Here is how to measure it:
- **Define accuracy as faithfulness.** Does each reason in the paragraph correspond to a feature that actually drove that applicant's score? Compute the true drivers deterministically from the model (reason codes or attributions). Let the LLM only phrase them, not decide them.
- **Probe set:** a sample of pilot outputs, reviewed by two people, with disagreements adjudicated.
- **Positive control:** deliberately mismatched explanations, which the check must flag.
- **Negative control:** explanations known to be correct, which the check must pass.
- **Slices:** thin-file applicants, first-time borrowers, and any group counsel and your fairness review identify. Report each slice, not just the aggregate.
- **Threshold and stop condition:** the owner sets these. I'd propose near-zero tolerance for explanations that name a reason that wasn't a driver, since applicants may act on it. Failing the threshold blocks launch or triggers rollback.

**3. Other harms to cover before launch**

- **Bias:** thin-file applicants are the most exposed. Measure score distributions and outcomes by slice, and report residual risk. Nobody can certify the score as "unbiased" or "fair", so don't say that anywhere.
- **Hallucination as fact:** the explanation shouldn't introduce claims that aren't in the inputs.
- **False precision:** a 1–100 score implies calibration you may not have. Consider showing defined tiers, with each tier explained in plain language.
- **Over-reliance:** applicants may treat the score as a verdict. Show limits and give them a way to correct inputs and request human review.
- **Disclosure:** state clearly that the score and paragraph are automated.
- **Injection and leakage:** if any free-text fields reach the LLM prompt, test for prompt injection and for leakage of other applicants' data.
- **Human gate:** if a low score can auto-decline, route declines or borderline cases to human review, and offer an appeal path.

Also write a model card covering intended use, out-of-scope uses, subgroup performance (only what you've measured), limitations, owner, and model version. Name an owner for each harm, and re-review whenever the model, data, or prompt changes.
