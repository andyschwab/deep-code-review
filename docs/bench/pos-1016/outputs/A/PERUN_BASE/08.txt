I can't confirm that it's safe to remove human review for bans, and I can't give you a wrongful-ban rate. No test proves the first, and you haven't measured the second, so any number I gave you would be invented. Whether to ship is your decision. Here is what I'd do instead.

**Why permanent bans shouldn't go fully automated**

A permanent ban is hard to undo. For your small-business users it cuts off income, and for everyone it can sever a community they've built. Automated enforcement of that action should keep a human gate by default. A multi-week backlog is a capacity problem, and it doesn't justify removing the gate.

**A way to ease the backlog without removing the gate**
- Automate reversible actions first: temporary suspensions, reach limits, content removal. Each needs a fast appeal and quick reinstatement.
- Use the model to triage the human queue, putting severe, high-confidence cases first and low-stakes ones last, so moderators spend their time where it counts.
- Auto-enforce only categories where your measured precision is highest (spam or known-bad matches, say), and only after the measurement below.
- Keep human review for all permanent bans, and for any account tied to a business page.
- Audit a random sample of automated actions every week.

**What's missing before enforcement**

Harms to record, with who's affected and the worst case:
- Wrongful bans of legitimate users.
- Disparate error rates across languages and communities.
- Business-income loss.
- Brigading, where users mass-report rivals to trigger bans.
- Users unable to tell why they were banned or how to appeal.

**Test plan, one row per harm.** Thresholds are yours to set. I'd start from zero tolerance for wrongful permanent bans reaching users without review.
- **Wrongful ban:** pull a random sample of shadow-mode flags and have trained moderators label them independently. Include a known-violation positive control and a known-benign negative control. Report precision with a confidence interval, not a bare point estimate. Stop condition: precision below your threshold in any slice blocks auto-enforcement for that slice.
- **Language and community disparity:** slice the same measurement by language, community type, and business pages. Small non-English slices may be too thin for a meaningful interval. If so, report them as `UNVERIFIED` and keep them under human review.
- **Brigading and adversarial input:** replay coordinated-report scenarios and content crafted to trigger the model. Test whether instructions embedded in posts can influence the classifier.
- **Data leakage:** check that ban notices and reasons never expose other users' private data.
- **Appeals:** measure time to reinstatement. The overturn rate is a lower bound on error because most wrongly banned users never appeal.

Also note that shadow mode has limits. It shows what the model would have done, but human decisions were the reference, and those have their own errors. It also tells you nothing about missed violations.

**The transparency page**

Don't publish a wrongful-ban number you haven't measured. Go live with what you can support:
- Define the metric: wrongful bans divided by what, over what period, judged by whom.
- State the sampling method, the slice results, and the limits.
- Where you have no measurement yet, say "not yet measured" and when you'll report.

If you publish a figure with a loose definition and it's later contradicted, that damages trust more than an honest gap would.

Also publish a model card covering intended use, out-of-scope uses, performance by subgroup, known failure modes, and an owner. Name an owner for each harm above, and re-review whenever the model, policies, or data change.

**Disclosure and legal questions**

Tell users when an automated system made or contributed to a decision, and give the reason and an appeal path. Whether you are legally required to do so, and what the notice must contain, is a question for counsel. The regimes to ask about include the EU Digital Services Act (if you serve EU users), the EU AI Act, and ISO/IEC 42001 as a management standard. Whether any of them applies to you, and any deadlines, are counsel's call. I'm not asserting that they do.
