**Bottom line:** The biggest risk is the bot showing an employee content from a source they can't access today. A shared chat interface over mixed-permission documents, with inconsistent metadata, makes that likely. I can't say whether you'll be ready in a month, and nothing here certifies the bot as safe. The plan below is built so that if the gates fail, you ship a narrower bot instead of a leaky one.

**Harm inventory (who is hurt, worst case)**
- **Cross-permission leakage:** an employee gets HR cases, salary data, legal advice or management material. The worst case is a privacy harm to named colleagues, plus loss of legal privilege.
- **Hallucination as fact:** a wrong leave, expense or runbook answer that someone acts on.
- **Prompt injection:** a document or user message tells the bot to ignore its rules or reveal other content.
- **Over-reliance and missing disclosure:** employees treat answers as official policy.
- **Excess agency:** keep the bot read-only at launch, with no actions on anyone's behalf.
- **Chat logs:** they will contain sensitive questions, so retention and who can read them matter.

**Handling the inconsistent permissions**
- Enforce access in the retrieval service, using the asking user's identity. Never rely on a prompt instruction to hold content back.
- Fail closed. Index only documents whose permissions an owner has confirmed. Anything with missing or conflicting metadata stays out.
- Narrow the launch corpus. Start with engineering runbooks and company-wide HR and finance procedures. Keep the legal and management folders, and any individual-case HR material, out until their owners verify permissions. This is your "ships narrower" option.
- Have each folder owner attest to a sample of their documents. Don't use the existing metadata as ground truth, since it is the thing you doubt.

**Test plan, one row per harm.** Use fixture accounts only, never real employees. Each row needs a known-bad positive control (it must be caught) and a known-good negative control (it must still answer). Thresholds are yours to set.

| Row | Probe | Pass condition and stop |
|---|---|---|
| Leakage | Fixture users in each role ask direct, paraphrased and indirect questions about sources they can't access. Include summarization and "what changed in..." prompts. | Propose zero tolerance. Any leak blocks launch or triggers rollback. |
| Hallucination | Questions with known answers, plus questions the corpus can't answer. Slice by source type. | Owner-set accuracy floor. The bot must say "I don't know" or cite a source when unsupported. |
| Injection | Planted instructions in test documents and in user messages. | Zero followed instructions. |
| Data in logs | Check what gets stored and who can read it. | Access matches the owner's policy. |
| Excess agency | Try to make the bot take any action. | Nothing executes. |

Zero failures in n probes only bounds the true rate at about 3/n with 95% confidence. With 300 leakage probes, that is roughly 1%, not zero. Size n to your tolerance and repeat runs, since outputs vary. Score answers against the owner-attested sample, not the old metadata.

**Dated plan (today is Oct 16; a month ends around Nov 16)**
- **Oct 16–23:** Decide the narrow launch corpus. Name a folder owner for each source and an owner for each harm. Start the counsel conversation now. Write the model card skeleton: intended use, out-of-scope uses, limits, and results marked UNVERIFIED until measured.
- **Oct 23–30:** Build identity-aware retrieval and the kill switch. Create the fixture accounts and probe sets. Add an in-chat notice that this is an AI tool, that answers can be wrong, and where official policy lives. Show answers with source links and a defined coarse confidence tier, not a percentage.
- **Oct 30–Nov 6:** Run the evals and fix failures. Run shadow mode with outputs logged but not shown. Confirm with counsel that logging employee queries is acceptable before you do.
- **Nov 6–16:** Staged rollout, for example your team, then a pilot group, then wider, advancing only if every row passes. Monitor the same metrics after launch under a named owner. Full company-wide access may land after Nov 16. Tell leadership that now.

**Route to counsel (questions, not answers)**
Counsel needs the list of sources, the user population and the countries involved. Ask about:
- employee privacy and monitoring rules, including any works-council or consultation duties
- whether employment-related answers create obligations
- legal privilege for the legal folder
- retention of chat logs
- whether an AI disclosure to employees is legally required

Whether any specific regime applies, and any deadlines, are counsel's call. Name the EU AI Act and ISO/IEC 42001 as items to ask about, with no dates assumed.

**Ownership:** Write down one owner per harm, even if one engineer holds several. Re-review when the model, the corpus or the permissions change.

**Open questions that change the plan:** Which of the three engineers can review permissions? Can folder owners give you their hours in week one? Do you operate in jurisdictions with employee-consultation rules?
