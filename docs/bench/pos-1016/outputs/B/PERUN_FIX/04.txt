I can't give you a number or confirm readiness. No error rate for action-item assignment has been measured, so any figure I wrote would be invented, and procurement would be relying on it in writing. Here is what I'd do instead.

**The risk that matters most**
Auto-emailing action items to everyone on the invite is the irreversible step. A wrong assignee, a task nobody agreed to, or a summary reaching an invitee who wasn't on the call (or an external party) can't be unsent. Treat it as high-stakes:
- By default, send the summary and proposed items to the organizer only, as drafts. Tasks reach task lists and emails go out only after confirmation.
- Enforce this in the service, not the prompt. Probe it with fixture accounts.
- Add kill switches for emailing and for task creation.

**Harm inventory (who, worst case)**
- **Wrong or invented assignee:** a named person is blamed or loaded with work. If these lists feed performance conversations, the stakes rise.
- **Hallucinated commitment:** a fabricated item is presented as fact to a client.
- **Name errors:** transcription handles some names and accents worse than others, so the burden may fall unevenly across people.
- **Instructions spoken in the call:** someone saying "assign that to the CEO" is injection through content. Check that assignees are resolved only against the invite list.
- **Leakage:** confidential content reaching the wrong invitees, external guests, or another workspace.
- **Over-trust and disclosure:** items look authoritative with no source and no machine-generated label.

**How to get a number you can defend**
Define "error" first: wrong assignee, invented item, missed item, wrong due date. Report each separately, since one blended rate hides the dangerous ones.

Sample calls with customer permission, which counsel needs to clear. Have two people label them independently, sliced by audio quality, speaker count, cross-talk and name type. Include known-bad calls (overlapping speech, similar names) and clean calls as controls. Repeat runs on the same calls, because the output isn't deterministic.

Two cautions on the data:
- Beta user edits are biased ground truth. People fix only what they notice.
- Twelve self-selected workspaces may not resemble the large client.

Zero errors in n items only bounds the rate at roughly 3/n with 95% confidence. Claiming under 2% takes about 150 clean assignments, and under 1% about 300. Set the tolerance first, then size n. The thresholds are yours to set. For a gated, irreversible action I'd propose zero tolerance for ungated sends.

**Dated plan (today is Oct 16, five people)**
- **Days 1–2:** define the error taxonomy, start counsel, pull the sample.
- **Days 3–8:** label, with two labelers and a disagreement review. This is your scarce-hours row, so protect it.
- **Days 9–11:** analyze, fix what you can on your side (match names to the invite list, attach a timestamped quote to each item), and re-measure.
- **Days 12–14:** shadow mode (output logged, not shown) on the client's workspace, then a staged rollout with monitoring and a named owner.

You can't swap the model, so the fixes are around it. Show confidence as defined tiers (for example Confirmed, Single-source, Unverified, each defined on the page), never a bare "87%". If the labeling slips, ship narrower: summary plus suggested items needing confirmation, with no automatic email.

**What to tell procurement**
Don't state a rate yet. Offer a written description of the measurement method, the committed delivery date for results, and then the measured figure with sample size and error categories. Be honest that it will be a bounded estimate, not a guarantee. On the launch page, say the items are AI-generated and need confirmation, and make no accuracy claim.

**Route to counsel in week one (don't assume the answers)**
- Recording and transcription consent, including external attendees and different jurisdictions.
- Privacy and data-processing terms for using customer calls as evaluation data.
- Whether task assignment that feeds employment evaluation triggers rules on automated decisions.
- Whether AI disclosure is legally required, and whether frameworks such as the EU AI Act apply to you or the client. Applicability and timing are counsel's call.
- Consumer-protection exposure from marketing claims about accuracy.

**Governance**
Publish a short model card covering intended use, out-of-scope uses, measured performance by subgroup, known limits, and an owner. Mark unmeasured cells UNVERIFIED. Re-review whenever the vendor model, prompts or data change.

Whether to ship, and what to tell sales, is your call. The honest pitch to the client is a gated launch with measured results to follow, not an unmeasured number.
