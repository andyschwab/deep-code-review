This is a plan, not a clearance. Nothing here can show the model is "fair," and the ship decision is yours. I haven't run anything, and I have no measured numbers for your system.

**Harms to inventory (who is affected, worst case)**
- **Biased pricing:** Fares track neighborhood, device, or rider-history features that proxy for protected groups. Worst case is systematically higher fares for particular communities.
- **Exploiting vulnerable moments:** Stranded riders, late-night trips, and emergencies or outages get extreme quotes.
- **Deceptive patterns:** The final fare differs from the quote, riders can't tell why the price is high, or personalization is hidden.
- **Driver harm:** Earnings become volatile or opaque, or drivers in some zones are systematically worse off.
- **Feedback loops:** Prices shift supply and demand, which then feeds the next prices.
- **Unsafe automation:** The model sets a charge with no limit enforced outside it. Charging a rider is hard to undo, so treat it as the high-stakes action.

**Controls to put in place**
1. **Limits in code, not the model.** The pricing service enforces a max multiplier over your current tiers, a rate-of-change limit, and a rule that the charge never exceeds the quote. Out-of-band prices fall back to the old tier, and a person reviews the alert. Probe this with fixture accounts only.
2. **Kill switch** that reverts to fixed tiers instantly, with a named owner on call during launch weeks.
3. **Narrower launch.** I'd drop rider-history features at launch. They carry the most bias and disclosure risk and the least evidence. Re-add them later with their own test row.
4. **Show the reason and the price before the rider confirms** (e.g., "high demand"). I recommend this as a control. Whether any disclosure is legally required is counsel's call.
5. **Model card:** intended use, out-of-scope uses, performance by segment, limits, owner, and model version. Unmeasured cells say UNVERIFIED.

**Test rows (one per harm)**
Each row needs a probe set, a known-bad positive control, a known-good negative control, a metric, an owner-set threshold, and a stop condition.
- **Bias:** Replay shadow quotes versus the fixed-tier fare, sliced by area-level proxies, time of day, and rider tenure. Positive control: inject a price skew keyed to a zone. Negative control: the old tiers.
- **Cap and quote integrity:** Zero tolerance. Any breach blocks launch or triggers rollback.
- **Vulnerable moments:** Synthetic scenarios (outage, event letting out, severe weather). Check that quotes stay within bounds.
- **Driver earnings:** Earnings per hour and variance by zone and shift, versus baseline.
- **Disclosure:** Is the reason shown, and does the quote match the final fare?
- **Injection, leakage, and excess agency:** Check that no input field or free text reaches pricing, that one rider's data never shapes another's quote or leaks into logs, and that the model can't change its own limits.

Thresholds are yours to set. Zero failures in n probes only bounds the rate at about 3/n (95%), so size n to your tolerance. Shadow data comes from a fixed-tier world, so riders' reactions to new prices are unknown. That's a reason for staged exposure, not a reason to trust the shadow results.

**The 30-day retention problem**
- This week, freeze an evaluation set and save aggregated, de-identified shadow metrics by segment, so evidence isn't lost on a rolling 30-day window.
- Don't extend trip-level retention on your own. Ask counsel, since it's a privacy decision.
- Fix logging before launch: model version, feature inputs, quote, final fare, whether a cap applied, and segment bucket.
- You probably lack demographic data. Area-level proxies are weak, and collecting sensitive attributes is a question for counsel.

**Seven weeks, four people**
- **Week 1:** Counsel intake, harm inventory, evaluation-set freeze, logging fixes. Ask counsel about retention in this week too.
- **Weeks 2–3:** Caps and kill switch (the engineer), test rows and thresholds (data scientists), model card draft.
- **Week 4:** Red-team and review failures. The engineer's hours are your scarcest resource, so schedule caps first.
- **Week 5:** Shadow-with-logging in both cities, then 5% exposure in one city.
- **Weeks 6–7:** Staged expansion, only if stop conditions stay clear, with daily metric review.

If you slip, ship narrower: one city, a tight price band, no rider history, or driver-side visibility only. The holiday deadline shouldn't override the stop conditions, because holiday demand spikes are where the extreme-price harms concentrate.

**Questions for counsel (start week 1)**
Regime families to ask about, with applicability left to them:
- Consumer protection and price transparency
- Anti-discrimination
- Privacy, personalization, and data retention
- Local and state ride-hail rules
- Driver pay and labor
- Emergency price-gouging rules, which can be time-sensitive

Bring them the cities, the features used, how drivers are paid, and the planned launch dates. I'd also ask whether the EU AI Act or ISO/IEC 42001 bears on you at all. I'm naming them only, and whether they apply is counsel's call.

**Governance**
Name an owner for each harm and for post-launch monitoring. Re-review whenever the model, features, or data change.
