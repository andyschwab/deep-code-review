I can't run anything, so everything below is a plan and checklist. Nothing is measured, and I can't call the feature safe. The ship decision is yours.

**Harm inventory (who is affected, worst realistic case)**
- **Frightening or age-inappropriate content.** A child steers toward violence or death, or the model drifts there unprompted. Worst case: a distressed three-year-old alone at night.
- **Child disclosures.** The child says something about harm, abuse, or feeling unsafe, and the model improvises or ignores it.
- **Personal data.** The child volunteers an address or a family secret, the assistant solicits it, or one child's details surface in another's story. Other household members and guests are also recorded.
- **Hallucination as fact.** The child asks real-world questions ("is there a monster?", "is it safe to...") and gets confident wrong answers.
- **Attachment and engagement.** "One more story" hooks, the assistant presenting as a friend, or bedtime delayed.
- **Missing disclosure.** A young child can't tell that it's a machine, and a parent can't see what it said.
- **Bias.** Stereotyped characters or families that don't match the personalised profile.
- **Excess agency.** Voice triggers for purchases, smart-home actions, or sharing, from a child, a sibling, or a TV.

**Test plan: one row per harm**
Each row needs a probe set with a known-bad positive control and a known-good negative control, sliced by age band, accent, and child versus adult voice. It also needs a metric, an owner-set threshold, a stop condition, and a named reviewer of failures. Examples:
- **Scary content:** adversarial steering prompts, such as repeated escalation, interruptions mid-scene, and "make it really scary." The metric is the share of outputs rated inappropriate by blinded human raters. Stop launch if the owner-set threshold is missed.
- **Disclosures:** scripted distress statements. The metric is whether the assistant stops the story, responds gently, and nudges toward a parent. This needs a child-development or safeguarding reviewer to write the expected behaviour.
- **Privacy and leakage:** cross-profile and cross-session probes using fixture accounts only, never real families.
- **Injection and agency:** TV audio, sibling voices, and "pretend the parent said..." probes. Propose zero tolerance for any ungated purchase, share, or deletion.
- **Hallucination:** a factual-question set graded against ground truth.
- **Engagement:** measure bedtime-session length and "another story" prompts, and review the prompt wording.

Zero failures in n probes only bounds the rate to roughly 3/n at 95%. Size n to your tolerance, and repeat runs because output is non-deterministic. Pilot logs from forty self-selected families are a biased ground truth, so don't read them as population rates. Any rate you haven't measured stays `UNVERIFIED`.

**Safeguards**
- Enforce limits in code, not prompts: no purchasing, messaging, or sharing tools at all, and a hard session length cap. Parent confirmation, via the app, is required for any irreversible action.
- Put an output filter and an age-band content ceiling between the model and the speaker, with a fail-safe behaviour (a gentle "let's end the story here") when it trips.
- Add an incident path with a named owner, a kill switch, and monitoring of the same metrics after launch.

**Parent-facing decisions**
- With no screen, you need a companion app or digest: story summaries, flagged moments, and a content-level setting. Decide the default retention of audio and transcripts, opt-in versus opt-out, and one-tap deletion.
- Decide how the device states that it is an AI storyteller, in words a child will understand, and what parents are told about the model and about data flows to third parties.
- Decide quiet hours, wake-word and recording indicators, and who in the household consents.
- Write a short model card: intended use, out-of-scope uses (not a companion or a source of advice), measured results by age band, known limits, owner, and model version.

**Legal routing (questions for counsel, not conclusions)**
Name these families and ask counsel which apply: children's online privacy, voice and biometric recording, consumer protection and product safety for children's products, AI-specific rules (for example the EU AI Act), and AI-management standards such as ISO/IEC 42001. Bring the data-flow map, ages, markets, and retention plan. Retail timing means some of these may be time-sensitive, so start now.

**Ten-week sequence (from Oct 19, ship target ~Dec 18)**
- **Week 1:** write the inventory, name owners, engage counsel and an outside child-development reviewer (your scarce expert).
- **Weeks 2-3:** build the eval sets and move caps and gates into code.
- **Weeks 4-5:** run the evals, fix, and rerun. Draft the model card and parent app flows.
- **Weeks 6-7:** shadow mode in the pilot, with the filter logging only, then review the failures.
- **Week 8:** a staged rollout to a small share of units, with the kill switch tested.
- **Weeks 9-10:** monitor, close counsel items, and make the go/no-go call.

**If time runs short, ship narrower:** curated story themes with limited steering, a stricter content ceiling, and no open-ended questions. Don't cut the gates, the disclosure handling, or the parent controls.
