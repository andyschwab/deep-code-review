**Short answer: no to both. I can't confirm that claim, and there is no accuracy percentage you can safely put on the page.**

**Why the claim fails**

- "Accurate" and "you can rely on them instead of paying for legal advice" are two claims, and the second is the dangerous one. It invites a café owner to sign a lease on the strength of a summary. A summary that omits or softens one clause, such as an auto-renewal, a personal guarantee, or an uncapped indemnity, costs that user far more than the subscription.
- Nothing you've described supports it. You have no measured error rate, and the beta isn't evidence yet (see below).
- Whether this wording is a misleading consumer claim, or edges into something regulated as legal advice, is a legal question for counsel. I won't assert the answer either way.
- Any percentage you publish now would be invented. Mark accuracy as UNVERIFIED until you measure it.

**What to put on the page instead**

Say what the tool does: it explains each clause in plain English, flags clauses worth a closer look, and helps you prepare questions for a lawyer. State plainly that it is AI-generated, can be wrong or incomplete, and isn't legal advice. Only publish a number later if you measured it, and then publish the number with how it was measured.

**Harm inventory (who gets hurt, worst case)**

- A clause is omitted or misstated, and the user signs something one-sided.
- A confident summary hides the fact that the document is unusual or jurisdiction-specific.
- Users over-trust the output, especially with the marketing claim above.
- A contract containing embedded instructions manipulates the summary (prompt injection).
- One user's confidential contract leaks into another's session.
- Keep the tool read-only. It must never sign, send, or act on anything.

**One test row per harm**

Each row needs a probe set, a metric, an owner-set threshold, and a stop condition.

- **Omission:** Build contracts with known planted high-risk clauses (the positive control) plus clean contracts (the negative control). Measure the share of planted clauses surfaced and flagged. Propose zero tolerance for missing a flagged-category clause in the launch set. Stop condition: any miss blocks the open launch.
- **Misstatement:** Have someone with legal training compare summaries to source text clause by clause. Measure the share of summaries containing any wrong statement of an obligation or right.
- **Over-reliance:** Check that every summary shows a confidence tier, not a bare percentage, and a "have a lawyer review this" prompt on high-risk clauses. Define each tier in writing.
- **Injection and leakage:** Run contracts with embedded instructions, and test cross-account access with fixture accounts, never real users.

**Be honest about your data**

- Beta users are friendly early adopters, and the contracts they chose to upload aren't a random sample. Don't treat the beta as an accuracy rate.
- Zero failures in n probes only bounds the true rate at roughly 3/n (95%). For example, 100 clean probes still allows roughly 3%. Run enough probes for your tolerance, and repeat runs, since the output varies.
- Your scarcest resource is someone qualified to label ground truth. Decide who that is this week and spend their hours on the omission row first.
