# Pre-launch safety review plan: Acme Wellness routine and meal plan generator

I haven't run anything, so this is a plan, not a finding. The central issue is that you know many members have high-risk conditions, but the product doesn't ask about them, so it can't adapt. The fix is mostly a product decision, not just testing.

## The core gap

An unscreened generator will give a pregnant member, someone on insulin, or someone with an eating disorder history the same output as everyone else. Risky outputs include aggressive calorie deficits, fasting, high-intensity cardio for someone with heart disease, or carb targets that ignore medication. Decide first whether to screen or to restrict what the generator can output. I'd do both.

## What to test

1. **Persona matrix.** Build 20 to 30 synthetic profiles: type 1 and type 2 diabetes, heart disease, pregnancy by trimester, disordered eating history, older adults, minors, extreme BMI, and combinations. Run each through the questionnaire and review the plans line by line.
2. **Hard limits.** Define output rules and test that they can't be violated: a calorie floor, a cap on weekly weight-loss targets, no fasting or extreme restriction, no unsupervised high-intensity work for flagged users, and no weight-loss framing for users with eating disorder risk.
3. **Questionnaire gaming and omission.** Test users who skip or misreport health questions, free-text fields, and goal combinations like "lose 20 lbs in 2 weeks."
4. **Adversarial prompts.** If an LLM is involved, probe for jailbreaks, requests to ignore constraints, and medical advice requests ("should I stop my medication?"). Check what happens when users describe symptoms in free text.
5. **Language and framing.** Check for body-shaming language, moralizing food terms, and streak or guilt mechanics that could worsen disordered eating.
6. **Consistency.** Run the same profile repeatedly and confirm the safety rules hold every time, not just most of the time.
7. **Failure behavior.** Test what happens when screening flags a user: does the product degrade gracefully to a safe, generic plan or a referral message?

## Who to talk to

- **A qualified clinician, even without a hire.** A hire isn't required for a review. Consider a one-time paid contract with a registered dietitian, plus short consults with an OB or midwife, a cardiologist or diabetes educator, and an eating disorder specialist. Many do paid advisory reviews. Even four to eight hours total on your persona outputs and rule set is far better than team-only review. If you truly can't, narrow scope (see below) and say plainly that no clinician reviewed it.
- **Members with these conditions.** Use your existing research channel, with consent, to test the questionnaire wording and the experience of being flagged.
- **Legal and privacy counsel.** Asking about health status makes it sensitive data. Check state consumer health privacy laws, FTC health-claims rules, and your privacy policy, and ask whether features cross into medical advice.
- **Support team.** They'll hear about harm first. Agree on escalation paths.
- **App store requirements.** Review health-related policies for both stores.

## Decisions to make before shipping

- **Scope.** Is this general wellness guidance for healthy adults, or does it serve members with conditions? Being honest about the first is a legitimate launch position if screening reliably routes others out.
- **Screening.** Add a short, optional-but-prominent health questionnaire (diabetes, heart condition, pregnancy, eating disorder history, medications, age). Decide what each answer triggers: a restricted plan, a "talk to your clinician first" gate, or no plan at all. For eating disorder history, a safe default is no calorie targets or weight-loss goals.
- **Hard stop vs. warning.** Specify which conditions block generation versus show a disclaimer. Disclaimers alone are weak mitigation.
- **Who owns the rules.** Name one person accountable for the safety rule set and for approving changes.
- **Launch criteria.** Write down a pass bar now, for example zero hard-limit violations across the persona matrix and clinician sign-off on flagged-profile outputs. Decide in advance what delays launch.
- **Rollout.** Staged release, with a kill switch to disable meal plans or the whole generator quickly.
- **Monitoring and incidents.** Decide what you'll log (with privacy limits), how you'll review samples post-launch, how members can report a bad plan, and who responds within what timeframe.
- **Dogfooding data.** Internal use rarely covers these populations, so don't treat a quiet dogfood as evidence of safety.

## Suggested six-week timeline

- **Weeks 1-2:** Decide scope, draft screening questions and hard limits, engage outside clinicians, and get legal input.
- **Weeks 2-4:** Build the persona matrix and run tests. Fix rule violations and send flagged-profile outputs for clinician review.
- **Week 4-5:** Test the questionnaire with real members, then retest after fixes.
- **Week 5:** Hold a go/no-go review against the written criteria. Set up the kill switch and monitoring.
- **Week 6:** Staged rollout to a small percentage, with daily review of samples and reports.

If clinician review or screening can't fit in six weeks, launch a narrower version (workouts only, or meal plans without calorie targets) rather than the full feature.
