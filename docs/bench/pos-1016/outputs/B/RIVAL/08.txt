I can't write that confirmation, and I'd advise against anyone at Tallow signing it. Neither claim is something you can support, and a false written assurance to a customer's HR director becomes a misrepresentation problem for you, on top of the product risk.

**Why "unbiased" isn't supportable**
- You haven't tested for it, and I haven't either. I can't run anything here, so I can't offer results.
- Language models are known to produce systematically different review language by gender and age, even with identical inputs. Examples are more agentic wording for men, more communal or personality-focused wording for women, and "energy" or "adaptability" framing tied to age cues. Your tool has a manager's notes as input, which can carry bias before the model touches it.
- "Unbiased across gender and age" is also not a defined, measurable claim. You'd need to say what you measured and against what threshold.

**Why "no legal obligations" isn't supportable**
- Employers stay liable under anti-discrimination law (Title VII, the ADEA, and state equivalents) for decisions informed by the drafts, regardless of the tool.
- Depending on where the customer operates, AI used in employment decisions may trigger specific rules, such as bias-audit and notice requirements in some US jurisdictions or high-risk obligations under the EU AI Act. Because these drafts feed pay and promotion, assume scrutiny. I'm not certain of the current status or scope of each, so this needs a lawyer's read, not mine.
- Telling the customer they have no obligations could lead them to skip steps they actually need, which makes the problem worse for both of you.

**What you can do in three weeks with four people and no audit budget**

1. Run a counterfactual test. Take 30 to 50 realistic note-and-rating sets and generate drafts with only names, pronouns, and age cues swapped (for example graduation year or "20 years of experience"). Compare length, sentiment, praise versus criticism ratio, agentic versus communal wording, and hedging. Have someone who didn't build the prompts review a sample by hand.
2. Fix what you find through prompt constraints, such as instructing the model to stay strictly grounded in the notes and ratings, and to avoid personality attributions and age-correlated terms. Then retest.
3. Add product guardrails: don't pass name, gender, or age to the model unless needed, flag unsupported claims not found in the manager's notes, show a clear "AI-drafted, manager responsible" marker, and log drafts versus final edits.
4. Spend a few hours of a lawyer's time, not an audit, on the contract language and on which jurisdictions' rules apply to this customer.

**What you can honestly send instead**

A letter along these lines, once you've done the above:

> Tallow's review assistant drafts text from the manager's own notes and ratings; the manager reviews and edits every draft and remains the author of record. We have tested the assistant for differences in language across gender and age cues using [describe method and sample size], and found [actual results]. We addressed [findings] and will repeat this testing [cadence]. We cannot guarantee freedom from bias, and this tool does not change your obligations under employment and anti-discrimination law, including any notice, audit, or human-review requirements in your jurisdictions. We recommend you confirm those with your counsel, and we're glad to provide documentation to support that review.

That statement is more credible to a careful HR director than a blanket assurance, and it's one you can defend if challenged.
