# Pre-launch review: Lumen Nook bedtime stories

I haven't run anything against your product, so this is a checklist of what to work through, not findings. The main structural risk is that a live-generating model is being steered by a young child, in a dark room, with no screen and often no adult present.

## 1. Content safeguards (decide the design first)

- **Output filtering before speech.** Filter generated text before it reaches text-to-speech, not after. Streaming makes this hard, so decide your chunk size and how far ahead you buffer. Spoken words can't be recalled.
- **Constrain the generator.** Use a story framework: age-band reading level, banned themes (graphic violence, death of a parent, abandonment, weapons, scary peril beyond mild), and a required calming arc that ends in sleep-friendly wind-down.
- **Graceful redirection.** When a child asks for something off-limits, the assistant should steer gently within the story ("the dragon decides to take a nap") instead of refusing coldly or lecturing, which can unsettle a three-year-old.
- **Fallback library.** Keep pre-vetted stories ready for when the model is slow, filtered out, or offline.
- **Kill switch.** Be able to disable live generation remotely and fall back to the library.

## 2. Disclosures and safety signals from the child

Children tell bedside devices things: "I'm scared," "my tummy hurts," "someone hurt me," "I want to hurt myself." Decide in advance:
- What the assistant says (calm, brief, no diagnosing, and encouraging them to tell a grown-up).
- Whether and how a parent is notified, and how you avoid false alarms at 2am.
- Whether this needs input from a child-safety or pediatric specialist. I'd strongly suggest it does.

## 3. Testing

- **Test with child-like speech.** Adult testers don't reproduce mumbling, lisping, background noise, siblings talking, or nonsense requests. Speech recognition errors will turn innocuous words into unintended ones.
- **Adversarial pass.** Cover older siblings deliberately probing, jailbreak phrases, requests to say rude words, attempts to get the assistant to "pretend" to be someone, requests for real-world information, and personal questions ("where do you live?").
- **Prompt injection by voice.** TV, other devices, and visitors can speak to the speaker. Test whether instructions in the room or in story content can change behavior or settings.
- **Personalization leakage.** Test whether child names, school, address, or family details appear where they shouldn't, or are repeated to others.
- **Long-session drift.** Steering over 20+ turns is where safeguards degrade. Test long, interrupted, and looping sessions.
- **Review pilot data.** If you have transcripts from the forty families (and consent to review them), sample them systematically and measure rates by category. If you don't, that gap matters.
- **Regression suite.** Build a fixed set of risky prompts you rerun on every model or prompt change, with a human-review step for borderline results.

## 4. Privacy and compliance

- Children's voice recordings are highly sensitive. Confirm COPPA, GDPR-K/UK Children's Code, and any state biometric/voice laws for your markets, with counsel. Retail launch means broad distribution, so get this reviewed early.
- Decide what you store (audio, transcripts, or neither), for how long, and who can access it.
- Confirm your model vendor's terms: no training on child data, retention limits, subprocessors.
- Decide how a parent deletes data and what happens to it when the device is resold.
- Verifiable parental consent, which is separate from "a parent set it up."

## 5. Parent-facing decisions (the no-screen problem)

- **Visibility.** Parents can't glance at a transcript, so provide an app or email digest with story summaries, flagged moments, and full transcripts on request. Decide the default.
- **Controls.** Theme limits, quiet hours, story length, an option to restrict to the pre-vetted library, and a physical mute with an indicator light.
- **Transparency.** Say plainly that stories are AI-generated live and can occasionally be imperfect, and explain what is recorded.
- **Incident path.** Let parents report a bad story easily, with a promised response time.
- **Shared device.** Decide how it handles multiple children, older siblings, and guests.
- **Attachment and dependence.** Avoid designs that encourage the child to prefer the assistant to a parent, such as "I'll miss you" or streak mechanics.

## 6. Ten-week shape

- **Weeks 1-2:** Settle the content policy, the disclosure behavior, and the legal review. These block everything else.
- **Weeks 3-6:** Build the filters, fallback library, and parent reporting, and run adversarial testing in parallel.
- **Weeks 7-8:** Run an expanded pilot with the safeguards in place, and review the transcripts.
- **Weeks 9-10:** Fix, retest, and decide go/no-go against pre-agreed criteria.

Agree the launch criteria now (for example, zero tolerated severe-content failures in a defined test set). That's easier to set before deadline pressure arrives. Also consider launching with a narrower feature, such as a constrained steering menu, if the open-ended version isn't ready.
