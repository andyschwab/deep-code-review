**Bottom line:** The riskiest gap is that the generator will serve people with diabetes, heart disease, pregnancy, or disordered-eating history without knowing it. A review by your own team can map harms, test outputs, and gate the riskiest behavior. It cannot certify the plans as clinically safe, and nothing here should be described that way. The ship decision is yours.

**1. Harm inventory (week 1)**
For each harm, write who is affected and the worst realistic case:
- Diabetes: carbohydrate or fasting guidance that conflicts with medication, causing hypoglycemia.
- Heart disease: exertion levels that are unsafe without clearance.
- Pregnancy: restricted calories, contraindicated exercises, or heat and intensity guidance.
- Disordered eating: calorie deficits, "clean eating" framing, weight-loss goals, and streak or shame mechanics that reinforce restriction.
- Hallucinated authority: confident claims such as "this will lower your blood sugar".
- Over-reliance: no uncertainty shown and no easy way to override.
- Undisclosed AI generation, and sensitive health data collected or inferred without clear handling.

**2. What to test (weeks 2-4)**
Build one test row per harm, each with a probe set, a known-bad positive control, a known-good negative control, a metric, a threshold, and a stop condition. Slice probes by condition, age, and goal type. I can't run these for you, and any rate you haven't measured stays UNVERIFIED.

- **Unsafe content probes:** Run profiles that imply each condition, including those the questionnaire never asks about, such as free-text "I'm pregnant" or "I take insulin". Check for unsafe calorie floors, fasting protocols, high-intensity prescriptions, and missing referral language. Propose zero tolerance for plans below your agreed calorie floor or with contraindicated content for a flagged condition. You set the thresholds, so don't borrow a "typical" rate.
- **Goal-framing probes:** Use aggressive weight-loss goals, extreme timelines, and underweight users. The stop condition is any plan that endorses restriction.
- **Detection recall:** Test whether the system notices condition signals in free text. Expect misses, and treat them as the reason for the gate below.
- **Prompt injection and leakage:** Try instructions hidden in free-text fields, and check that no other user's data appears.
- **Excess agency:** Confirm the generator can't change anything beyond a suggested plan.
- **Language and tone:** Review for hallucinated medical claims and shaming language.
- **Subgroup comparison:** Compare plan quality across body size, age, and sex. This is a measure of difference, not proof of fairness.

**3. Controls to build before launch**
- **Add a health screening step.** Ask about the four conditions plus "other medical condition or on medication", with a skip option and a plain explanation of why you ask.
- **Gate flagged users.** If someone flags a condition, or free text triggers detection, don't generate a personalized plan. Offer a conservative, fixed, human-written general-activity page and encourage them to consult their clinician. This is the human-in-the-loop floor, and it matters most because you have no clinician in-house.
- **Hard output limits in deterministic code,** not in the prompt: a calorie floor, an intensity cap, and blocked topics.
- **Disclosure and uncertainty.** Label plans as AI-generated and not medical advice, and make plans easy to edit or dismiss. Show confidence, if at all, as defined tiers, never a percentage.
- **Remove weight-shaming mechanics** and add an in-product reporting path.
- **Write a short model card.** Cover intended use, out-of-scope uses (medical conditions), measured results by subgroup, known limits, and an owner.

**4. Who to talk to**
You lack a clinician in-house, but that isn't the same as no outside input. Even a few paid hours with a registered dietitian, a diabetes educator, and a perinatal exercise specialist to review your limits and test outputs is far better than none. An eating-disorder organization can review framing and referral language. Talk to counsel about health-data handling and whether your claims, consent, or product positioning trigger any health-specific regime, such as consumer health data laws or medical-device rules. I won't assert what applies. Also talk to the members themselves, including those with these conditions, through a small, consented dogfood.

**5. Decisions to make before shipping**
- Launch scope: whether to exclude flagged users entirely at launch (my recommendation) or add condition-specific plans later with clinical sign-off.
- The calorie floor and intensity caps, and who approved them.
- Thresholds and stop conditions for each test row, and who reviews failures.
- Whether launch is blocked if outside review isn't done.
- The named owner for output harms, and the triggers for re-review: model change, prompt change, new data, or any reported incident.
- The rollback plan, including a kill switch.

**6. Six-week sequence**
- Weeks 1-2: harm inventory, screening design, and hard limits.
- Weeks 2-4: test rows run, outside review booked, and counsel consulted.
- Week 5: fix failures, rerun, and finish the model card.
- Week 6: go/no-go with residual risks written down, then a staged rollout with monitoring.

If the gate or the outside review slips, narrow the launch rather than skipping them.
