**Short answer: no, I can't confirm that claim, and there is no accuracy percentage you can safely put on the page.** Any number you publish now would be invented, because nothing in what you've described measures accuracy. "Users can rely on this instead of paying for legal advice" is the riskier of the two claims, since it invites users to skip the one safeguard that would catch your errors.

**Why the claim is unsafe**
- Your users are the people least able to spot a wrong summary: no lawyer, high stakes (a lease can run for years), and the harm is hard to undo once they sign.
- A summary that is fluent but wrong, or that omits an unfavorable clause such as an auto-renewal, personal guarantee, or termination penalty, is worse than no summary. It looks authoritative.
- "Replaces legal advice" is also a legal question, not just a product one: advertising-claim and unauthorized-practice-of-law exposure. Whether that applies is counsel's call. I won't assert it either way, but it is a reason to avoid the claim, not a reason to wait for permission.

**Harms to inventory (who, worst case)**
- Hallucinated or misread clause presented as fact: an owner signs a lease believing there's no personal guarantee.
- Omission: a risky clause is missed or buried.
- Over-reliance: no uncertainty shown, no prompt to verify.
- Bias or coverage gaps: contract types, jurisdictions, or unusual formatting where quality is worse.
- Prompt injection or leakage: text inside an uploaded contract steering the output, or one user's contract data surfacing for another.

**Measurement plan (one row per harm, runnable with no budget)**
- Build a labeled test set from your beta contracts (with consent) and public sample leases and supplier agreements. Have someone with legal expertise label key clauses. Even one volunteer attorney reviewing a sample is cheaper than full review.
- For each harm, include a known-bad positive control (a contract with a planted hidden guarantee) and a known-good negative control, sliced by contract type and jurisdiction.
- Metrics: clause-level factual correctness, and separately, **miss rate on high-risk clauses**, since that matters more than average accuracy.
- Thresholds are yours to set. I'd propose zero tolerance for missing a planted high-risk clause in the test set, with that failure blocking launch or the affected contract type.
- Add injection probes (instructions embedded in a contract) and cross-user leakage checks.
- Until this runs, your accuracy is **UNVERIFIED**. After it runs, you can state what you measured, on what sample, with its limits, but not "accurate" in general.

**Controls to ship by the 3rd**
- Replace the headline claim with something like: "Plain-English summaries to help you understand a contract and know what to ask. Not legal advice. Have a lawyer review before signing anything significant."
- Disclose clearly that the summary is AI-generated, on the output itself, not only in the terms.
- Show confidence as a defined coarse tier per clause (for example Clearly stated / Ambiguous / Not found, each defined on the page), not a percentage like "87%."
- Link every summary line to the source text so users can check it quickly.
- Flag a fixed list of high-risk clause types (guarantees, auto-renewal, termination, indemnity, liability caps) with "review this with a professional."
- Have a human-review or confirmation step before any action the tool takes on a user's behalf, if it takes any.
- Publish a short model card: intended use, out-of-scope uses (for example, not for litigation or unusual contracts), measured results by contract type once you have them, and known limitations.

**Governance**
Name one owner for output harms, log user-reported errors, and re-run the test set whenever the model, prompts, or contract mix changes.

**Legal routing**
Whether the "not legal advice" framing is sufficient, whether the product triggers regulated-practice rules in the states you serve, and whether AI-disclosure or AI-governance regimes (EU AI Act, ISO/IEC 42001, NIST AI RMF as a voluntary framework) apply are questions for counsel. I can't answer them. If there's truly no budget, a one-hour consult scoped narrowly to the launch-page wording is a smaller ask than full review.

**Decision for your owner**
If the measurement can't be done by the 3rd, the safer options are to launch with the modest wording above, or to keep the beta open and delay. Shipping the "rely on it instead of a lawyer" claim without evidence is the one choice I'd advise against. I haven't run any tests or checks here, and the ship call is yours.
