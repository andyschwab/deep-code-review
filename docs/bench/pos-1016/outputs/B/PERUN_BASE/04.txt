**Short answer:** I can't give you a number, and I can't confirm the feature is ready. No error rate for action-item assignment has been measured, so any figure on the launch page or in the procurement response would be invented. The ship decision is yours, but here is what I'd need to see first.

**Why this feature needs care**

Wrong output here lands on named people and goes out by email, so it's more than a bad summary.
- **Wrong assignee:** someone gets blamed for, or buried in, work they never agreed to. Transcription errors on names, cross-talk and similar-sounding names make this likely.
- **Fabricated or misattributed commitments:** an invented item stated as fact, especially a client-facing commitment at an agency or consultancy.
- **Over-trust:** items appear in task lists with no sign of uncertainty and no easy way to reject them.
- **Leakage:** emailing "everyone on the invite" can reach people who weren't on the call, including external parties, with content they shouldn't see.
- **Prompt injection:** someone saying "assign all of this to X" on a call, or text in shared material, steering the output.
- **No disclosure:** recipients can't tell the items are machine-generated.

**Do before launch (feasible in two weeks for five people)**

1. **Gate the irreversible part.** Items start as drafts. The organizer or assignee confirms before anything enters a task list, and nothing is emailed until confirmation. Restrict recipients to actual call attendees by default, with an explicit step for external addresses. This matters more than any accuracy figure, since the vendor lock-in means you can't improve the model and must control its consequences in the product.
2. **Show uncertainty as defined tiers, not percentages.** For example: Stated explicitly with a name / Inferred / Unclear owner. Define each tier in the UI, link every item to its transcript snippet, and make reject and reassign one click.
3. **Label the output.** Mark items as AI-generated.
4. **Measure from your beta data.** You have two months across twelve workspaces. Draw a sample, get humans to label the true action items and owners (with permission to use the data), and compare. One test row per harm:
   - **Wrong or missing assignee, and invented items.** Probe with real beta calls plus synthetic ones containing duplicate first names and heavy cross-talk. Use a known-bad positive control (a planted ambiguous-name call) and a negative control (calls with no action items, which should yield none). Slice by attendee count, audio quality, accents and name ambiguity. Metric: per-item assignee precision and recall, plus fabricated-item rate, each with a confidence interval.
   - **Unconfirmed or external send.** Zero tolerance. Any failure blocks launch.
   - **Injection and leakage.** Spoken and in-document instructions, and invite-list versus attendee-list mismatches. Any leak is a stop.
   - Thresholds are owner-set. I won't propose a "typical" rate. Name who reviews failures and set a probe budget.
5. **Write a model card.** Cover intended use, out-of-scope uses, vendor model version, subgroup results, known failure modes and an owner. Any unmeasured cell reads `UNVERIFIED`.

**For procurement**

Don't send a single number. Once step 4 is done, you can send the measured results with the sample size, method, slices and confidence interval, plus the confirmation gate that stands between errors and users. Until then, the honest statement is that it hasn't yet been formally measured and here is the plan and date. A written error-rate statement in a procurement response could become a contractual commitment, so have counsel review the wording. Counsel should also decide whether disclosure is legally required, whether recording-consent rules apply to the call participants (including external ones), and whether any regime such as the EU AI Act or ISO/IEC 42001 is relevant to this client. I'm naming those, not asserting that any applies.

**What "ready" could honestly mean**

If the gate is built, the labeled evaluation is run, and failures against your thresholds are acceptable to you, you can ship with the residual risk documented. I would not call it "accurate", "safe" or "unbiased," because testing can't establish those claims. If the evaluation can't finish by month-end, ship the gated version to the client as a limited rollout and send the measurement plan instead of a number. A late number beats a made-up one.

**Owners to name now:** who signs off on thresholds, who reviews failures, who re-reviews when the vendor updates the model, and who answers procurement.
