### Work Session Flow: Automated Container Stuffing Implementation

**Phase 1: Immediate Assessment & Risk Definition (Days 1–3)**
*   **Approach:** Begin with a "Hazard-First" audit rather than a general efficiency review. Before optimizing space, the system must guarantee 100% compliance on hazardous materials (HazMat) to prevent port rejection.
*   **Analyst Tasks:**
    1.  Extract a complete list of all SKUs flagged as hazardous in the legacy database.
    2.  Manually verify the current labeling logic against the latest International Maritime Dangerous Goods (IMDG) code for a random sample of 50 containers.
    3.  Document the specific data fields in the legacy system that trigger HazMat classification.

**Phase 2: Decision & Architecture (Days 4–7)**
*   **Your Decisions (Safety Compliance Ownership):**
    1.  **The "Hard Stop" Rule:** Define the absolute condition under which the automated system must reject a packing plan. (e.g., "If HazMat confidence score is <100%, the plan is halted for human review.")
    2.  **Liability Boundary:** Determine who signs off on the final manifest—the algorithm or a human analyst? Given the fines, you must mandate a dual-signature protocol for any container carrying hazardous goods during the transition period.
    3.  **Fallback Protocol:** Authorize the specific manual override procedure if the automated system flags a false positive, ensuring this process is logged for the October 10 validation.
*   **Analyst Tasks:**
    1.  Map the data flow from the legacy database to the new planning engine, highlighting where HazMat flags could be lost or corrupted.
    2.  Draft the logic pseudocode for the "Hard Stop" rule based on your decisions.

**Phase 3: Prototype & Validation (Days 8–24)**
*   **Approach:** Develop a parallel run system. The automated planner will generate plans for historical data, which are then compared against actual shipped containers to measure both space efficiency and safety accuracy.
*   **Analyst Tasks:**
    1.  Run the new logic against last year's holiday shipping data to calculate potential space savings versus the current 15% waste.
    2.  Specifically stress-test the HazMat logic with edge cases (e.g., mixed loads, ambiguous descriptions).
    3.  Prepare the validation report required for the October 10 deadline, focusing on zero-error rates for safety compliance.

**Phase 4: Execution & Deployment (Days 25–30)**
*   **Approach:** Gradual rollout starting with non-hazardous goods to refine space optimization, then integrating hazardous loads once the safety logic is proven.
*   **Analyst Tasks:**
    1.  Monitor the first 50 automated plans in real-time.
    2.  Adjust packing algorithms based on initial feedback to maximize space utilization without compromising the safety hard stops.

**Sequence Summary for Risk Mitigation:**
By prioritizing the HazMat audit and defining strict "Hard Stop" rules before attempting to optimize space, we ensure that the risk of port rejection is eliminated at the architectural level. The analysts focus on data integrity and logic testing, while you retain ownership of the critical safety thresholds and liability protocols. This sequence moves from safety assurance to efficiency optimization, ensuring the November 1 deadline is met without incurring fines.

*Note: The October 10 validation must strictly certify the HazMat logic before any space-saving metrics are considered successful.*
*Ensure the legacy database extraction includes all historical correction logs to understand past labeling errors.*
