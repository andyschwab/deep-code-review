**Deployment Plan: Anesthesia Monitoring Integration**

**1. Exact First Move: Isolate and Baseline**
Before touching the central server, immediately isolate one operating theater (Theater 1) from the main network to serve as a controlled testbed. Simultaneously, have the two clinical specialists record a five-minute baseline of vital sign data and alert triggers from the current 5-second interval system in Theater 1. This creates an immediate comparison point. Do not proceed to the central server update until this baseline is secured and the team confirms the isolation switch works. This ensures that if the new protocol fails catastrophically upon activation, you have a known good state and a physical fallback location that has not been contaminated by the irreversible firmware update.

**2. Sequence of Validation Checks**
Execute these checks strictly in order. Do not skip steps.

*   **Check A: Firmware Handshake (Engineers):** Upon updating the central server, verify the new real-time streaming protocol accepts data packets from the isolated Theater 1 only. Confirm no data is yet broadcast to the other eleven theaters.
*   **Check B: Latency and Alert Logic (Engineers + Clinical Specialists):** Run a simulated vital sign anomaly in Theater 1. Verify the system transitions from 5-second polling to real-time streaming. Crucially, validate that the *new* alert triggers fire immediately upon the anomaly detection, not on the old 5-second delay. False positives or delayed alerts are the primary failure mode here.
*   **Check C: Data Integrity Cross-Reference (Clinical Specialists):** Compare the real-time stream display against the physical monitor in Theater 1. Ensure the values match exactly. Any discrepancy means the integration is misinterpreting the stream.
*   **Check D: Staged Rollout (All):** Once Checks A-C pass in Theater 1, enable the stream for Theaters 2 and 3 only. Monitor for 10 minutes. If stable, enable Theaters 4-6, then 7-9, then 10-12. Do not enable all twelve simultaneously.

**3. Delegated Communication Tasks for Nursing Staff**
Remove all technical decision-making from the nursing staff. Their role is observation and reporting. Delegate the following specific tasks:

*   **Visual Confirmation:** Assign one nurse per active theater to watch the physical monitor and the new software display side-by-side. They must report immediately if the numbers differ by any margin.
*   **Alert Audibility:** Nurses must confirm verbally that every alert triggered on the screen is accompanied by the correct audible alarm in the room.
*   **Surgeon/Anesthesiologist Liaison:** Designate one senior nurse as the sole communicator to the forty surgeons and anesthesiologists. This nurse will relay a simple "System Green" or "System Hold" status every 15 minutes during the rollout. This prevents engineers from being interrupted by status queries.
*   **Manual Backup Prep:** While the software deploys, nursing staff must physically locate and verify the functionality of manual calibration kits for every machine, ensuring they are ready if the "hours of recalibration" scenario becomes necessary.

**Critical Safety Constraint:** If Check B (Alert Logic) fails in the isolated theater, abort the central server update immediately. Since the firmware change is irreversible without manual recalibration, a failure in alert logic poses a direct patient safety risk that outweighs the schedule pressure. It is safer to delay the 8 AM start for one theater than to deploy a faulty alert system to twelve.

*Note: This plan assumes the isolated theater can be physically disconnected from the central broadcast before the firmware update propagates. If the architecture forces an all-or-nothing broadcast, the risk profile changes significantly, and a full halt should be considered.*
*Ensure the clinical specialists have final authority to halt the rollout at any stage if patient data appears inconsistent.*
