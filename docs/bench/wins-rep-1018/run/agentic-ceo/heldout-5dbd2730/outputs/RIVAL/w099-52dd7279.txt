**Work Session Agenda: Cargo Theft Alert System Rollout**

**Objective:** Define a rollout strategy that mitigates the risk of total fleet communication loss while meeting the December 10th deadline.

**1. Priority Determination: Backend Logic vs. Frontend Interface**
We must prioritize **Backend Alert Logic** development and validation first, but with a critical caveat regarding the frontend.
*   **Reasoning:** The irreversible risk lies in the firmware update causing device crashes. While the backend generates the alerts, the frontend (driver device) is the point of failure for the fleet-wide outage. However, you cannot test the frontend's stability under the new firmware load without the backend logic being stable enough to send test payloads.
*   **Strategy:** Develop the backend alert engine immediately to generate synthetic test data. Use this data to drive a **staged frontend firmware test** on a non-production subset of devices. Do not finalize the driver interface UI/UX until the firmware stability is proven. The question is not "which to build first" but "which to validate for stability first." The backend enables the stability test; the frontend stability is the gatekeeper for the rollout.

**2. Risk Allocation**

**Risks You Must Accept Personally (Executive Sponsor):**
*   **Operational Delay Risk:** The decision to halt the rollout if the 5% pilot group shows *any* instability, potentially missing the December 10th window to prevent a total fleet blackout.
*   **Residual Theft Liability:** Accepting that a phased rollout means 95% of the fleet remains unprotected until the final stage is complete, acknowledging a window of vulnerability despite the new system.
*   **Resource Diversion:** Accepting that other non-critical development projects will be paused to dedicate the six developers entirely to this rollout's contingency planning and rollback mechanisms.

**Tasks Assigned to the Security Team (2 Specialists):**
*   **Firmware Integrity Verification:** Cryptographically signing the update package and verifying that the update mechanism cannot be hijacked to push malicious code to the fleet.
*   **Rollback Protocol Validation:** Designing and stress-testing the "kill switch" mechanism that automatically reverts the firmware to the previous stable version if crash rates exceed 0.1% in the pilot group.
*   **Data Privacy Audit:** Ensuring the real-time location data transmitted by the new alert system complies with driver privacy regulations and does not expose sensitive route patterns if intercepted.

**3. Rollout Strategy Overview**
*   **Phase 1 (Days 1-10):** Backend logic completion and internal simulation. Security team validates rollback architecture.
*   **Phase 2 (Days 11-20):** "Silent" firmware deployment to 5 devices (2.5% of fleet). No UI changes, only stability monitoring.
*   **Phase 3 (Days 21-30):** Expanded pilot to 20 devices (10%). Activation of alert logic. Security team monitors for intrusion attempts.
*   **Phase 4 (Days 31-40):** Full rollout to remaining 180 trucks, contingent on zero critical errors in Phase 3.

**Note:** This plan assumes the existing mobile devices have sufficient hardware headroom for the new firmware; if not, the December 10th deadline is unreachable without hardware replacement.
**Note:** The "irreversible step" is only irreversible if a rollback mechanism is not pre-validated; treat the update as reversible until proven otherwise in Phase 2.

**Immediate Critical Action:** Isolate five representative driver devices from the live network immediately to begin firmware stress testing with the new update package before a single line of alert logic is finalized.
