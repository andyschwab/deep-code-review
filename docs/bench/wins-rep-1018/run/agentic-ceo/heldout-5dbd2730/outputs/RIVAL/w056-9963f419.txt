### Patient Transport Scheduling System Rollout Plan

**Rollout Sequence Strategy**
Begin the rollout with **Building 4 (Main Emergency & Trauma Center)**. As the highest-volume hub for stretcher and wheelchair requests, it provides the most rigorous stress test for the new routing logic. Successfully stabilizing the system here validates capacity for the remaining eleven buildings. Follow a "hub-and-spoke" model: stabilize Building 4 by noon, then cascade to the three largest satellite clinics (Buildings 1, 7, and 9) by end-of-day tomorrow. Complete the remaining eight low-volume administrative buildings by Monday afternoon to meet the Tuesday deadline.

**Delegated Tasks for Floor Managers**
Assign the following specific responsibilities to floor managers immediately:
1.  **Staff Grouping & Rotation:** Divide the 200 staff members into groups of 15. Schedule continuous 45-minute training blocks in the three available rooms, prioritizing dispatch coordinators and charge nurses first. Managers must maintain a "shadow roster" of trained staff to ensure every shift has at least one super-user present.
2.  **Hybrid Workflow Enforcement:** Until the main switch is flipped, managers must enforce a dual-log protocol. Staff must enter data into the new system while keeping a paper backup log. Managers are responsible for auditing 10% of these logs hourly to ensure data integrity before the cutover.
3.  **Hardware & Access Verification:** Managers must physically verify that every tablet and kiosk in their zone is logged in and has active credentials. Any access failure must be escalated to the IT helpdesk immediately, not during the launch window.

**Critical Go/No-Go Decisions (Reserved for Lead)**
You must personally retain authority over these three irreversible checkpoints:
1.  **The 06:00 AM System Health Check:** Only you can authorize the start of training based on real-time server latency and database connectivity reports. If latency exceeds 2 seconds, delay the start.
2.  **The "Main Switch" Authorization:** Only you can give the command to disable the old phone-based dispatch line. This decision requires confirmation that Building 4 has processed at least 50 successful live requests without critical error.
3.  **The Contingency Trigger:** If critical patient transport delays exceed 15 minutes post-cutover, only you can authorize the emergency reversion protocol to temporarily reactivate the phone lines while the system is debugged.

**Immediate Actions (Next 60 Minutes)**
1.  **Secure Training Logistics:** Confirm with facilities that the three training rooms are equipped with projectors and Wi-Fi boosters, and publish the mandatory staff rotation schedule to all department heads.
2.  **Establish Command Channel:** Create a dedicated, high-priority communication channel (e.g., specific Slack channel or radio frequency) connecting you, the three lead floor managers, and the IT lead for real-time status updates.
3.  **Validate Credential Bulk-Upload:** Perform a spot-check audit of 20 random staff accounts across different roles to ensure permissions are correctly mapped before the morning rush.

Note: Ensure all contingency contact numbers for the legacy phone system are printed and distributed to managers before the switch is flipped.
Do not attempt any system configuration changes after 05:00 AM to allow for server stabilization.
