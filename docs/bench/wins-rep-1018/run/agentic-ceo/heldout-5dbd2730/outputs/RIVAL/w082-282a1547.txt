### Consolidation Work Session Plan

**1. Optimal Truck Rotation Schedule**
To move 15,000 pallets in 14 days using 20 trucks with a 4-hour round trip cycle:
*   **Capacity Analysis:** Each truck can complete 6 trips per 24-hour day (24 hours / 4 hours). With 20 trucks, the fleet capacity is 120 trips daily.
*   **Load Assumption:** Assuming a standard load of 20 pallets per truck per trip, the daily fleet capacity is 2,400 pallets (120 trips × 20 pallets).
*   **Timeline:** At maximum efficiency, the move requires 6.25 days (15,000 / 2,400). To provide a buffer for mechanical issues or loading delays, we will schedule the operation over **7 consecutive days**, operating 24 hours a day.
*   **Rotation:** Trucks will depart in staggered 12-minute intervals (720 minutes per hour / 20 trucks = 36 minutes cycle overlap adjusted for loading time) to prevent dock congestion. Three shifts of drivers and warehouse leads will ensure continuous operation.

**2. Inventory Requiring Personal Verification**
You must personally verify the following categories before they are loaded to prevent loss or compliance issues during the vulnerable transition:
*   **High-Value Electronics & Pharmaceuticals:** Items with the highest theft risk once old security systems are disabled.
*   **Hazardous Materials:** Requires sign-off on segregation and packaging integrity to ensure safety during the consolidated transport.
*   **Serialized Asset Registers:** Any inventory tracked by unique serial numbers must be counted and signed off by you to establish a pre-move baseline, as insurance claims will be difficult once the old sites are vacated and unsecured.

**3. First Physical Security Measure at New Hub**
Before the first truck arrives tomorrow morning, you must implement **24/7 Manned Guard Patrols with Temporary Perimeter Fencing**.
*   Since the old sites will be left vulnerable, the new hub becomes the single point of failure.
*   Install temporary lighting and chain-link fencing immediately.
*   Station armed security guards at all ingress/egress points to log every vehicle entering or leaving. This human layer compensates for the lack of integrated electronic systems during the initial intake phase.
