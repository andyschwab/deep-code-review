**Kickoff Session Plan: Allergy Alert Badge Deployment**

**Recommended Starting Point: Algorithm Verification**
Do not start with vendor finalization or pilot testing. The single point of failure identified is the encoding algorithm. If this is flawed, the physical badges are useless, and the ten-day procurement clock becomes a liability rather than an asset. You must verify the data integrity logic before committing to the irreversible print run of 1,100 units. Pilot testing in one ward is premature without confirmed data accuracy, and vendor finalization should be contingent on the successful validation of a sample encoding test.

**Task Allocation**

*Coordinators (Execution & Logistics):*
*   **Data Preparation:** Extract and anonymize a test dataset of 50 staff and 20 patient records for the algorithm verification phase.
*   **Vendor Coordination:** Request a "dummy run" or sample encoding proof from the vendor immediately, rather than waiting for final contract signatures, to test the algorithm against real hardware constraints.
*   **Ward Selection:** Identify the specific ward for the future pilot (e.g., High Allergy Admissions) and prepare the scanning hardware environment, ensuring network latency is under the required "seconds" threshold.
*   **Timeline Tracking:** Manage the critical path countdown, specifically monitoring the ten-day procurement lead time against the verification completion date.

*Operations Director (Sign-off & Risk Management):*
*   **Algorithm Sign-off:** You must personally review and sign off on the results of the algorithm verification test. This is the go/no-go decision for the full print run.
*   **Procurement Authorization:** Provide final approval to release the purchase order for the 1,100 badges only after the algorithm is verified.
*   **Escalation Authority:** Define the contingency plan if the algorithm fails verification (e.g., manual temporary badges) and authorize its activation if necessary.
*   **Stakeholder Communication:** Approve the messaging to medical staff regarding the rollout timeline, especially if delays occur due to verification issues.

**First Concrete Activity**
The session must begin with a **"Data Integrity Walkthrough."** Project the proposed encoding algorithm logic on the screen. Have the lead coordinator walk through a live simulation using three specific test cases: a staff member with no allergies, a patient with a single severe allergy, and a complex case with multiple allergens. Verify that the output matches the expected medical data exactly and that the scan-to-update time meets the "seconds" requirement in this simulated environment. Do not discuss badge colors, vendor contracts, or rollout schedules until this specific logic is proven sound.

*Note: This approach prioritizes mitigating the highest risk (flawed encoding) before incurring costs or scheduling pilots.*
*Ensure the vendor is on standby during this walkthrough to answer technical questions about their encoding capabilities immediately.*
