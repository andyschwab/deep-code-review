**Optimal Session Sequence and Immediate Action Plan**

**1. First Specialist Step: Risk-Mitigated Migration Planning**
The absolute first step is a **collaborative migration design session** led by your most senior technical coordinator, with your direct oversight. Do not begin execution or data validation yet. Because the legacy database cannot handle simultaneous writes and the risk of irreversible data loss is high, the team must first architect a "write-lock" protocol. This plan must define a precise moment where all seven coordinators stop entering new data into the legacy system, creating a static snapshot for migration. Without this frozen state defined first, any attempt to migrate live data across four driver locations will result in conflicting writes and lost units.

**2. Decision Matrix: Leader vs. Delegated**

**Decisions You Must Make Personally:**
*   **The Cutover Window:** You must authorize the specific 30-to-60-minute window where data entry halts network-wide. This impacts hospital relations and requires your authority to enforce.
*   **Risk Acceptance:** You must formally approve the final migration checklist, accepting the residual risk that the snapshot might miss donations occurring minutes before the lock.
*   **Communication Authority:** You must be the sole signatory on the final announcement to hospitals regarding updated availability to ensure message consistency during the crisis.

**Decisions to Delegate to Coordinators:**
*   **Protocol Drafting:** Delegate the drafting of the step-by-step migration script to the senior technical coordinator.
*   **Driver Coordination:** Assign a lead coordinator to synchronize the four logistics drivers, ensuring they are physically or digitally paused during the cutover window.
*   **Validation Logic:** Task two coordinators with creating the cross-verification method (e.g., manual ledger vs. database count) to be used immediately after the switch.

**3. First Concrete Actions (Next 60 Minutes)**

1.  **Convene the "Lock-Down" Briefing:** Immediately gather all seven coordinators and four drivers (via conference call if remote). Declare the intent to migrate and establish the rule: *No new data entries are permitted in the legacy system until the "All Clear" is given.*
2.  **Execute a Dry-Run on Non-Critical Data:** Before touching the O-negative inventory, have the technical team practice the migration sequence on a non-critical blood type or a dummy dataset within the legacy database to verify the write-lock mechanism holds.
3.  **Establish the Manual Fallback:** Distribute physical or digital temporary logs to all eleven staff members. Instruct them that any donation or transfer occurring during the migration window must be recorded here first, to be entered into the new system only after validation is complete.
4.  **Snapshot the Current State:** Once the dry-run succeeds, initiate the formal write-lock on the O-negative records. Export the current count of twelve units to a secure, read-only file. This is your baseline for validation.

**Note:** Do not announce updated availability to hospitals until the post-migration validation confirms the twelve units plus any pending arrivals are accurately reflected in the new protocol.
**Note:** If the dry-run fails even once, delay the cutover and revert to manual tracking until the script is perfected; speed cannot compromise data integrity.
