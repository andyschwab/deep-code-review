### Work Session Strategy: Tax Engine Rebuild & Cutover

**Phase 1: Parallel Logic Construction & Shadow Validation (Weeks 1–6)**
Divide the ten engineers into two squads. Squad A builds the new engine module-by-module for the 12 countries. Squad B builds a "Shadow Harness" that routes live production traffic to *both* the old and new engines simultaneously without returning the new results to users.
*   **Goal:** Achieve 100% bitwise equality between old and new outputs for historical data, then validate divergence against new regulations using legal test cases.

**Phase 2: Stress Testing & Penalty Simulation (Weeks 7–9)**
Scale the Shadow Harness to ingest double the current load (10,000 calc/min) to simulate the holiday rush. Introduce "Chaos Financials": inject edge-case enterprise data designed to trigger the specific automatic penalties you fear.
*   **Goal:** Prove system stability under 2x load and zero false-positive penalty triggers.

**Phase 3: The Canary Cutover (Weeks 10–11)**
Do not switch all traffic at once. Route 0.1% of live traffic (lowest risk clients) to the new engine for actual billing. Monitor for 48 hours. If stable, increase to 1%, then 5%, then 25%.
*   **Goal:** Limit blast radius. If a cent-level error occurs, it affects only a tiny fraction of clients and can be manually reversed before compounding.

**Phase 4: Full Cutover & Rollback Readiness (Week 12)**
Complete the migration to 100% traffic. Maintain the old engine in a "hot standby" mode for 7 days post-cutover to allow instant reversion if latent issues appear.

### Critical Go/No-Go Decisions for the Founder

1.  **The Divergence Threshold Decision:** After Phase 1, the Shadow Harness will show differences. You must define the tolerance. Is *any* deviation a No-Go, or only deviations >$0.01? Given your penalty constraints, the rule must be: **Any unexplained cent-level divergence is a No-Go.** You must halt and audit until every cent is accounted for by regulatory changes, not bugs.
2.  **The Canary Expansion Decision:** Between 1% and 25% traffic, you will face pressure to speed up. If the error rate is 0.00% but latency spikes, do you proceed? **Decision Rule:** If latency impacts the user experience or timeout thresholds, it is a No-Go. Stability under load is as critical as calculation accuracy.
3.  **The Hard Freeze Decision:** Two weeks before Dec 31st, you must decide whether to proceed with the cutover or delay to the next fiscal year. If the system is not at 100% traffic with zero errors by Dec 15th, the default decision must be **No-Go**. Rushing the final 15% creates the exact risk of irreversible penalties you aim to avoid.

### Immediate First Actions to Validate Logic Safely

1.  **Construct the "Golden Dataset":** Immediately aggregate 12 months of historical transaction logs covering all 12 countries, specifically isolating the largest enterprise clients. This is your baseline truth.
2.  **Build the Shadow Comparator:** Task two senior engineers to build a standalone service that takes the Golden Dataset, runs it through the *current* live engine and a prototype of the *new* logic, and outputs a diff report. Do not touch the live routing yet; this is an offline validation tool.
3.  **Define the "Kill Switch" Protocol:** Draft the exact technical steps to revert traffic to the old engine in under 60 seconds. Test this mechanism manually on a staging environment today. You cannot proceed with confidence until you know exactly how to abort.

*Note: This strategy prioritizes risk containment over speed, acknowledging that a single calculation error carries existential financial weight.*
*Ensure legal teams validate the "expected divergences" caused by new regulations separately from engineering bugs.*
