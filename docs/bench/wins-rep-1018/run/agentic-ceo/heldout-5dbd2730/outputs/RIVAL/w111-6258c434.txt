### Glacia Retail Return Automation Work Session Structure

**Phase 1: Risk-Calibrated Planning (Weeks 1-2)**
Focus on defining boundaries before writing logic. Map the legacy database's write limits against the projected 40% surge volume to establish a hard throughput ceiling. Design the rule engine architecture with a "circuit breaker" mechanism that automatically reverts to manual review if transaction rates approach the crash threshold.

**Phase 2: Controlled Build & Simulation (Weeks 3-4)**
Develop the rule engine in a sandbox environment that mirrors the legacy database constraints. Do not connect to live payment gateways yet. Instead, simulate the post-holiday surge using historical data to verify that the auto-approval logic for items under $50 does not trigger excessive writes or approve known fraud patterns.

**Phase 3: Staged Testing & Rollout (Weeks 5-6)**
Execute a "shadow mode" test where the engine processes real returns but only recommends actions without executing them. Compare these recommendations against manual team decisions. Once accuracy is confirmed, enable auto-approval for a small subset (e.g., 5% of traffic) and gradually increase volume while monitoring fraud rates and database load in real-time.

### Critical Decisions Required

You must explicitly decide on the **Fraud Risk Threshold Strategy**. Specifically:
1.  **The Dollar Limit:** Is $50 the absolute hard cap, or should it be lower (e.g., $25) for the first two weeks of the surge to minimize exposure while the system stabilizes?
2.  **The Velocity Cap:** What is the maximum number of auto-approvals allowed per minute before the system forces a manual review, regardless of item value, to protect the legacy database?
3.  **The Fraud Tolerance:** What is the acceptable dollar amount of fraud loss per day? (e.g., "We accept up to $500/day in fraud to save $4,000/day in labor"). This number dictates how aggressive the auto-approval rules can be.

### First Actionable Task

**Conduct a "Load & Loss" Audit.**
Have your lead developer and finance officer jointly calculate the exact maximum transactions-per-minute the legacy database can sustain without crashing. Simultaneously, have the fraud team quantify the total dollar value of fraudulent returns under $50 from last year's surge. These two numbers form the non-negotiable constraints for your rule engine; without them, any logic built is speculative and dangerous.

*Note: Ensure the "circuit breaker" logic is prioritized over feature completeness to prevent system collapse.*
*Note: Delay full automation until the shadow mode confirms zero database errors over a 48-hour simulated surge.*
