### Pre-Launch Work Session Structure: CareVisit Wound Care Tool

**1. Order of Testing and Deployment**
To ensure offline functionality and privacy, execute steps in this strict sequence:
*   **Step A: Offline-First Functional Test.** Verify on a device with zero connectivity that nurses can capture, store locally, and queue photos for upload. Confirm no data leaves the device until a connection is established.
*   **Step B: Private Bucket Validation.** Deploy the cloud storage bucket with "Block All Public Access" enabled at the account level. Attempt to access a test image via a public URL; the request must fail.
*   **Step C: Authenticated Upload Simulation.** Simulate a nurse coming online. Verify that only authenticated service accounts can write to the bucket and that files are encrypted at rest.
*   **Step D: Staged Rollout.** Do not enable all 40 nurses simultaneously. Activate 5 nurses first at 8:00 AM. Monitor logs for 30 minutes. If zero errors occur, enable the remaining 35.

**2. Critical Configuration Decision: Data Retention**
You must decide on the **local device cache retention policy** before launch.
*   **The Decision:** Set the local auto-delete trigger to "Immediately upon successful server acknowledgment" rather than a time-based delay (e.g., 24 hours).
*   **The Reasoning:** Since nurses work in poor signal areas, photos may sit on devices for hours. If a device is lost or stolen before syncing, a time-based delay keeps high-risk patient photos vulnerable on the device longer than necessary. Immediate deletion upon verified upload minimizes the window of exposure on edge devices. Do not retain local copies once the cloud confirms receipt.

**3. First Actionable Item for the Development Lead**
**Task:** Generate and sign the Infrastructure-as-Code (IaC) script that enforces the "Block All Public Access" setting on the cloud bucket, ensuring this setting is immutable by any user other than the root account.
*   **Why:** Manual console clicks are prone to human error during high-pressure launches. The lead must produce a script that programmatically guarantees the bucket cannot be made public, even if a junior developer misconfigures permissions later. This script must be reviewed and committed before any data migration begins.

**Path to Ensuring Patient Privacy**
Privacy is secured by treating the cloud bucket as a "black hole" where data can only enter via authenticated pipes and never exit publicly. By enforcing the "Block All Public Access" flag at the infrastructure level, you remove the possibility of accidental public exposure during the switch. The offline-first architecture ensures that data is never transmitted insecurely over weak cellular networks; it remains encrypted on the device until a stable, secure handshake occurs. The combination of immediate local deletion post-upload and immutable private bucket settings creates a chain of custody where patient photos are either encrypted on a specific nurse's device or encrypted in a private cloud silo, with no intermediate state where they are publicly accessible.
