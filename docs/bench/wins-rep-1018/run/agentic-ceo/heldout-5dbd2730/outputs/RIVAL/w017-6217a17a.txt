### Strategy Session Agenda: Patient Consent Migration

**1. Data Batch Processing Order**
Do not process records chronologically or randomly. Adopt a **Risk-Stratified Phasing** approach to minimize liability exposure:
*   **Phase 1 (The Canary Batch):** Select 50 records representing the full decade span (5 records per year) and varying consent types (surgical, general, research). This tests temporal data integrity without mass exposure.
*   **Phase 2 (High-Liability Cohort):** Process records associated with ongoing litigation, high-risk procedures, or minors who have since reached adulthood. These carry the highest legal stakes if hash verification fails.
*   **Phase 3 (Standard Historical Bulk):** Migrate the remaining 24,950 records in chunks of 5,000, pausing for legal sign-off between each chunk.
*   **Phase 4 (Exception Handling):** Address any records that failed hashing or validation in previous phases manually.

**2. Legal Thresholds for Personal Verification**
Before authorizing the irreversible hash generation, you must personally verify and document the following:
*   **Chain of Custody Continuity:** Confirm that the metadata logged on the blockchain creates an unbroken legal link between the original signed document and the new hash, satisfying evidentiary standards for "best evidence" rules in your jurisdiction.
*   **Statutory Retention Compliance:** Verify that the blockchain ledger structure meets the specific retention period requirements (e.g., 10 years post-treatment or until age 21 for minors) mandated by the new privacy laws, ensuring the ledger itself is legally recognized as a valid repository.
*   **Irrevocability Acknowledgement:** Sign off on a formal risk assessment stating that the local deletion post-hash complies with the "right to be forgotten" exceptions where medical records must be preserved, ensuring the one-way nature of the script does not violate conflicting health information statutes.

**3. First Technical Action (Non-Production)**
Execute a **"Shadow Ledger Dry Run"** using a sanitized clone of the production database.
*   Create a private, permissioned test blockchain instance that mirrors the proposed production architecture.
*   Run the migration script against the *Phase 1 (Canary)* dataset within this isolated environment.
*   **Critical Step:** Do not delete the original signatures in this test. Instead, configure the script to log the "deletion command" to a separate audit file without executing it.
*   Manually compare the generated blockchain hashes against the original local signatures to mathematically prove the hashing algorithm is flawless and reversible only in the sense of verification (input matches hash), ensuring no data corruption occurs before enabling the actual deletion function.
