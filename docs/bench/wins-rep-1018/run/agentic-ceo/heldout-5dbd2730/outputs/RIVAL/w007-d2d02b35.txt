### Emergency Triage Algorithm Rollout: Work Session Plan

**1. Specialist Groups to Engage First**
Do not start with engineers. Given the resistance from hospital administrators and the critical nature of patient safety, the following groups must lead the initial session:
*   **Clinical Operations & Risk Management:** To validate the algorithm against current triage protocols and define "failure" states that would endanger patients.
*   **Legal & Regulatory Compliance:** To map the specific state mandate requirements to the approval workflow and identify any liability gaps before technical discussion.
*   **Hospital Administrator Liaisons:** To address resistance early by framing the rollout as a compliance necessity rather than a technical upgrade, securing their buy-in for the timeline.

*Only after these groups align should the 12 engineers join to discuss feasibility.*

**2. Critical Decisions You Must Make Personally**
Before the team proceeds, you must resolve these three strategic blockers:
*   **The "Stop-The-Line" Authority:** Define explicitly who has the unilateral power to halt the rollout mid-deployment if live patient data shows anomalies. This cannot be a committee decision during the switch-over.
*   **Risk Acceptance Threshold:** Determine the maximum acceptable delay in patient intake (e.g., 30 seconds vs. 5 minutes) that triggers an automatic revert to the legacy system. You must sign off on this metric.
*   **Budget Allocation for Contingency:** With a $200,000 cap, decide now how much is reserved strictly for emergency rollback resources and overtime support versus the actual deployment labor. Do not let this be consumed by initial setup costs.

**3. The Very First Concrete Action**
**Conduct a "Regulatory Gate" Workshop immediately.**
Do not write code or plan server migration yet. The first action is a 4-hour session with Legal, Risk, and Administrator Liaisons to produce a signed **Compliance Sign-off Matrix**. This document must list every state mandate requirement and the specific evidence needed to prove the new algorithm meets it.
*   *Why:* If technical feasibility is high but regulatory approval is missing, the project fails. This matrix becomes the prerequisite checklist; no code deployment strategy will be discussed until every item in this matrix has an assigned owner and a verification method.

**Session Structure Overview**
*   **Hour 0-4:** Regulatory Gate Workshop (Non-technical stakeholders only). Output: Compliance Sign-off Matrix.
*   **Hour 5-8:** Feasibility & Risk Review (Engineers + Risk Management). Engineers review the Matrix to confirm technical ability to meet those specific evidence requirements.
*   **Day 2 Morning:** Administrator Alignment. Present the Matrix and Feasibility report to resistant administrators to demonstrate safety and compliance, reducing friction.
*   **Day 2 Afternoon:** Deployment Strategy. Only now do engineers draft the switch-over plan, bounded by the risks and constraints defined in the morning.

This approach ensures you do not waste engineering hours on a deployment strategy that might be rejected by regulators or hospital leadership. It prioritizes the "human" and "legal" switches before the technical one.

*Note: Ensure the "Stop-The-Line" authority is communicated to all shift leads before Friday. Keep the contingency budget separate from the main deployment fund.*
