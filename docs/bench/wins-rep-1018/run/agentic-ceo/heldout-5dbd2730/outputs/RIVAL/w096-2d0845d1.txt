### Emergency Reverse Logistics Plan Structure

**Priority Order of Execution**

1.  **Negotiate Contracts with External Partners (Immediate Priority)**
    Your current capacity (500 units) covers only 10% of the projected volume (5,000 units). Without external sorting facilities, the system collapses regardless of internal space or software. Since contracting is the irreversible step with long-term financial liability, it requires the most time for legal review and negotiation. This must start immediately to meet the seven-day operational deadline.

2.  **Audit Current Warehouse Space (Parallel Activity)**
    While legal teams review external contracts, your six logistics coordinators must instantly audit existing space. This determines the exact "gap" volume needed from third parties. Knowing whether you can stretch to 800 units internally versus staying at 500 directly impacts the volume clauses you negotiate in step one. Do not wait for this audit to finish before starting contract talks, but use preliminary data to inform initial offers.

3.  **Build Customer Return Portal (Secondary Priority)**
    The portal is essential for flow management but useless without a physical location to send the goods. You can deploy a minimal viable product (MVP) landing page with a static email address or phone line for intake within 48 hours. A full-featured automated portal can be iterated on after the physical network is secured. If goods arrive before the portal is perfect, manual logging is an acceptable temporary bottleneck; if goods arrive with nowhere to go, the recall fails.

**Critical Financial Decisions for Leadership**

You must personally authorize the trade-off between **long-term liability and short-term survival**.
*   **The Commitment:** You are deciding whether to sign minimum volume fees for a full year based on a crisis that may last only three months.
*   **The Risk:** If you under-contract to save money, you risk breaching service levels during the peak return window, leading to brand damage and potential regulatory fines that exceed the cost of unused capacity.
*   **The Decision:** You must approve paying for "idle capacity" post-crisis as an insurance premium. Do not attempt to negotiate "pay-per-use" only models at this stage; the urgency requires you to accept fixed costs to guarantee immediate availability. You must also decide on the budget ceiling for expedited legal and setup fees to ensure the seven-day deadline is met, potentially overriding standard procurement cooling-off periods.

**Single Most Critical Task**

**Initiate term sheet negotiations with three pre-vetted third-party logistics (3PL) providers immediately.**

Do not wait for the final warehouse audit or the portal design. Your legal and procurement leads must be in meetings with potential partners within the hour to secure tentative capacity agreements. The seven-day clock is ticking primarily on the legal signing process, not the physical setup. If you do not have a signed letter of intent by day four, you will miss the Tuesday deadline.
