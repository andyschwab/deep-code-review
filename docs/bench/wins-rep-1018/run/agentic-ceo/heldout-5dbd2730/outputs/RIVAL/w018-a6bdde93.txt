### Structured Work Session Plan

**1. Sequence of Load Testing**
Execute the following three-phase testing sequence immediately to validate the two-second latency constraint:
*   **Phase A: Baseline Latency Stress Test.** Simulate 500 concurrent synthetic video streams with zero clinical content to measure pure infrastructure overhead. Verify that the AI moderator handshake and initial packet processing stay under 1.2 seconds (leaving 0.8s buffer for content analysis).
*   **Phase B: Content-Heavy Load Test.** Ramp up to 500 concurrent sessions using varied, complex motion patterns typical of physical therapy exercises. This tests the AI inference time under realistic worst-case scenarios. Monitor for any stream exceeding the 1.9-second mark.
*   **Phase C: Failure Mode & Recovery Drill.** Intentionally induce latency spikes above two seconds to confirm the system terminates calls cleanly without corrupting session logs or blocking reconnection attempts for the next day. Verify that the "no pause" constraint holds during these induced failures.

**2. Decision Resting Solely With You**
The exclusive decision you must make is defining the **clinical risk threshold for the fallback procedure**. Since the system automatically terminates calls exceeding two seconds and reconnection is impossible until the next day, you must determine the acceptable protocol for patients mid-exercise when a drop occurs. Specifically, you must decide whether the clinical coordinators should immediately attempt to contact these patients via standard phone lines to guide them manually for the remainder of the session, or if the protocol dictates an immediate stop to the treatment with no same-day alternative. The engineering team can build the trigger, but only you can authorize the clinical safety net that activates upon a system-enforced termination.

**3. First Configuration Change**
The very first configuration change required on the servers is to **disable all non-essential logging and telemetry aggregation for the video stream path**. Standard debug logs, detailed metric sampling, and verbose error reporting introduce I/O wait times and CPU contention that will jeopardize the two-second deadline. Configure the servers to log only critical failure events (binary pass/fail status) to a separate, asynchronous disk stream, ensuring the main processing thread dedicates 100% of its resources to the AI verification pipeline.
