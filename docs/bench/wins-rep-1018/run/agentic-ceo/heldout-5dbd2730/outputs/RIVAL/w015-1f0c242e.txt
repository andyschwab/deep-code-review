**Work Session Structure: Zero-Disruption Wi-Fi Replacement**

**Phase 1: Isolation and Baseline (Hours 0–1)**
*   **Action:** Physically segment the network. Create a parallel, isolated test environment mirroring the production VLANs but disconnected from live medical devices.
*   **Goal:** Validate the new controller's firmware handshake with legacy device simulators before touching live hardware.
*   **Sign-off Required:** **YES.** You must personally verify that the test environment successfully negotiates connections with representative legacy device models before any physical work begins in the ICU.

**Phase 2: Staged Rollout – Non-Critical Zones (Hours 1–4)**
*   **Scope:** Administrative offices, waiting rooms, and cafeterias.
*   **Action:** Swap access points (APs) zone by zone. Monitor connection stability and latency.
*   **Constraint:** No work in clinical areas yet. This phase validates the physical installation process and basic connectivity without risking patient care.

**Phase 3: Critical Care Preparation (Hours 4–5)**
*   **Action:** Biomedical engineers pair with network technicians. Perform bedside verification of every IoT device in the ICU and ER. Document current signal strength and connection status as a "pre-flight" baseline.
*   **Protocol:** Establish a "Rollback Trigger." If any device shows packet loss >0.1% during the swap, the team immediately reverts the specific AP to the old hardware.

**Phase 4: Critical Care Execution (Hours 5–7)**
*   **Scope:** ICU, ER, and Operating Theaters.
*   **Action:** Swap APs one at a time per wing. After each swap, biomedical engineers must confirm every affected device reconnects automatically within 30 seconds.
*   **Safety Net:** The old network core remains powered and cabled but logically disabled, ready for immediate physical reconnection if the new controller fails.

**Phase 5: Validation and Handover (Hours 7–8)**
*   **Action:** Full network stress test with simulated load. Verify all 800+ IoT devices are online.
*   **Final Sign-off:** **YES.** You must sign off on the "All-Clear" report confirming 100% device connectivity before the 7 AM shift change.

**Order of Operations Summary:**
1.  Build/Validate Isolated Test Environment.
2.  Deploy to Non-Critical Zones.
3.  Baseline Critical Care Devices.
4.  Deploy to Critical Care Zones (One-by-One).
5.  Final System Verification.

**Required Sign-Off Points:**
1.  **Pre-Execution:** Approval of the isolated test results confirming legacy device compatibility.
2.  **Post-Execution:** Final approval confirming 100% IoT device connectivity before the morning shift.

**First Physical Action:**
The team must immediately **physically disconnect the uplink cable between the current production core switch and the new controller hardware**, then connect the new controller to an isolated switch populated with a representative sample of legacy device simulators (or spare legacy devices). Do not connect the new controller to the live network carrying medical traffic until the firmware handshake is proven safe in this air-gapped environment.

***

*Note: Ensure biomedical engineers hold veto power during Phase 4; if they flag an anomaly, work stops immediately.*
*Time buffer: The 8-hour window allows for a 30-minute contingency per critical zone swap.*
