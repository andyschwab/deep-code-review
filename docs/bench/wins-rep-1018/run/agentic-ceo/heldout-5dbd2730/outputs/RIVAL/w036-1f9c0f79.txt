**Execution Plan: RFID System Switch**

**Strategy: Staggered Calibration with Non-Sterile Pilot**
Do not calibrate all machines simultaneously. You must test the readers on non-sterile linens first. Calibrating all four washers at once creates a single point of failure; if the frequency is incorrect, you risk losing the entire weekend's inventory of surgical gowns. Instead, reprogram and test Washer #1 exclusively using a controlled batch of non-sterile linens (e.g., standard sheets or towels). Only after confirming 100% scan accuracy on the conveyor for this pilot batch should you proceed to calibrate the remaining three washers. This isolates risk and ensures the frequency calibration is verified against your specific linen mix before exposing critical surgical assets to the new system.

**Contingency Plan for Authorization**
You must authorize the **"Immediate Reversion Protocol."** If the new tags fail to scan or the calibration drifts during the pilot or subsequent cycles, the crew must immediately halt the introduction of new tags and revert the reader software to the previous backup version. Crucially, any linens processed during the failed window must be manually segregated and logged by hand (pen and paper) rather than released into the general sterile supply chain. These items will require 100% manual recount and verification before they can be cleared for surgical use on Monday. Do not attempt to "fix forward" while the line is moving; stop, revert, and manually track the affected batch.

**Pre-Arrival Maintenance Checks**
Before the vendor technician unpacks their tools, the maintenance crew must perform these three checks:
1.  **Network Connectivity Verification:** Confirm that all four industrial washer controllers have stable IP connectivity to the central tracking server and that no firewalls are blocking the specific ports required for the RFID middleware.
2.  **Power Cycle and Diagnostic Logs:** Perform a hard power cycle on all four RFID readers to clear temporary cache errors, then extract and print the last 24 hours of error logs to identify any pre-existing hardware faults or antenna degradation.
3.  **Physical Antenna Inspection:** Visually inspect the antenna arrays on the conveyor entry and exit points for physical damage, loose cabling, or accumulation of lint/debris that could interfere with signal propagation, cleaning them if necessary.

**Schedule Note:** With the technician arriving at 11 PM and a 6-hour window, aim to complete the Washer #1 pilot by 1:30 AM to leave 4.5 hours for the remaining three units and final validation.

**Risk Note:** The 10-minute total downtime limit applies per machine or cumulatively; clarify this with the vendor immediately upon arrival to ensure the reprogramming script meets the constraint.
