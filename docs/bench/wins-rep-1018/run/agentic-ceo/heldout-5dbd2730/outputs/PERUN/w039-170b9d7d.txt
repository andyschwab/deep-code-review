**Decision: Execute a Single-Site Pilot on Monday, not a parallel rollout.**

The risk of bricking devices (requiring physical replacement) outweighs the speed benefit of parallelism. With only three engineers and a four-day window, a failure rate above 1.5% in a parallel rollout would exhaust your team's capacity to recover, causing total project failure. A single-site pilot (20–30 units) on Monday validates the firmware update process and the local technicians' capability before committing the remaining 170+ units.

**Workflow Breakdown & Delegation Matrix**

**Phase 1: Site Assessment & Prep (Monday AM)**
*   **Engineers:** Verify network connectivity at the pilot site and stage the firmware on a local test bench.
*   **Local Technicians:** Physically inspect the first 30 autoclaves for sensor mounting compatibility and clear access paths.
*   **Your Sign-off Required:** Approval to proceed to flashing after the first successful bench test.

**Phase 2: Pilot Installation & Firmware Flash (Monday PM – Tuesday)**
*   **Engineers:** Perform the firmware update on the first 5 units personally. Monitor for bricking. If successful, supervise local technicians flashing the next 25.
*   **Local Technicians:** Mount hardware sensors and run cabling under engineer supervision.
*   **Your Sign-off Required:** Authorization to scale to the remaining five sites only after 100% success rate on the pilot batch is confirmed and data is flowing to the dashboard.

**Phase 3: Parallel Rollout (Wednesday – Thursday)**
*   **Engineers:** Split into pairs to oversee two sites each, acting as escalation points for firmware issues.
*   **Local Technicians:** Execute sensor mounting and firmware flashing on the remaining units using the validated procedure.
*   **Engineers Only:** Any device that fails the firmware update (bricked unit) must be handled by an engineer immediately to swap hardware.

**Phase 4: Final Validation (Thursday PM)**
*   **Engineers:** Verify data integrity across all six sites on the central dashboard.
*   **Local Technicians:** Clean up installation sites and log serial numbers.
*   **Your Sign-off Required:** Final project acceptance and certification submission.

**Specific First Action: Secure Replacement Supply Chain**

Contact your hardware vendor immediately to **reserve 10 spare autoclave control boards (or full units if the board is not field-swappable) for overnight delivery to the pilot site.** Do not wait for the pilot to fail; place the reservation now with a "hold for release" instruction. This ensures that if the firmware bricks a unit on Monday, the replacement part is on-site by Tuesday morning, preventing a multi-month certification delay. Confirm the vendor's emergency shipping cutoff time today.

*Note: If the vendor cannot guarantee 24-hour delivery, you must physically transport spares from your central inventory to the pilot site before Monday morning.*
*Do not begin flashing any production unit until the spare parts are physically verified on-site.*
