**Decision: HALT. Do not proceed with the migration plan as stated.**

The single number driving this call is **0%**: there is zero margin for error in a one-way hash operation that destroys original legal evidence before regulatory validation. The plan to "permanently remove" signatures before the audit creates an unacceptable liability floor; if the hash generation has a flaw, the hospital loses legal proof of consent forever. No budget or team size mitigates the risk of irreversible data loss prior to external sign-off.

**Figures:**
*   **Records:** 25,000 historical records (10-year span).
*   **Budget:** $50,000 cap for external auditing tools.
*   **Team:** 7 experts (4 legal tech, 3 DBAs).
*   **Deadline:** January 1st (firm).
*   **Risk:** 100% loss of legal standing if hash logic fails before audit.

**Corrected Strategy & Immediate Actions:**

1.  **Legal Thresholds to Verify Personally (Before Any Code Runs):**
    *   **Regulatory Acceptance:** Obtain written confirmation from the specific regulators that a blockchain ledger hash satisfies the new law's "original signature" requirement. Do not assume equivalence.
    *   **Destruction Policy:** Verify if the law *requires* the deletion of local originals immediately upon hashing, or if a "read-only archive" period is permitted until the audit concludes. If deletion is not explicitly mandated *before* audit, suspend the deletion step.
    *   **Liability Transfer:** Confirm if the blockchain provider accepts liability for hash collisions or chain failures in a legal context.

2.  **Order of Data Batches (Once Legal Clearance is Secured):**
    *   **Batch 1 (Control Group):** 100 records from varied years (e.g., 2014, 2018, 2023) to test format compatibility.
    *   **Batch 2 (High-Risk):** 500 records involving minors or sensitive procedures, requiring extra legal scrutiny.
    *   **Batch 3 (Volume Test):** 5,000 random records to stress-test throughput and cost.
    *   **Batch 4 (Full Migration):** Remaining ~19,400 records, executed only after Batches 1–3 are legally validated.

3.  **First Technical Action (Non-Production):**
    *   **Execute a "Dry-Run" with Reversibility:** Create a cloned, isolated sandbox of 100 records. Run the hashing script but **disable the deletion function**. Verify the hash output against a known-good validator and attempt to legally challenge the hash in a mock audit. Only after the mock audit passes should you enable the deletion function for the next test cycle.

**Halt Trigger:** If regulatory written confirmation is not received by **December 1st**, pause the technical migration and escalate to legal counsel for an emergency compliance waiver or extension request.

**Fallback Trigger:** If the "Dry-Run" reveals any hash mismatch or format error in the first 100 records, **immediately freeze all development** and allocate $25,000 of the budget to a third-party cryptographic audit before rewriting the script.

**Note:** The current plan conflates "technical completion" with "legal compliance." Legal proof must survive the transition; destroying the source before the audit violates the principle of evidence preservation.
**Note:** Re-basing the assumption that deletion must happen before the audit reduces the risk of permanent data loss from catastrophic to manageable.
