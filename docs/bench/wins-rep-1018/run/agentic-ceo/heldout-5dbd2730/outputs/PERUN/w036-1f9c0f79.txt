**Decision:** Test readers on non-sterile linens first; do not calibrate all machines simultaneously. Calibrating all four washers at once creates a single point of failure that risks total system blindness across the entire 8,000-pound daily volume. A phased approach isolates risk: if the frequency calibration fails on the first unit, you lose only that machine's throughput rather than the entire sterile supply chain.

**Execution Plan:**
1.  **Phase 1 (11:00 PM – 12:30 AM):** Reprogram and calibrate Washer #1 only. Run a controlled batch of 50 non-sterile linens (e.g., standard sheets) through the conveyor. Verify scan rates match the baseline before proceeding.
2.  **Phase 2 (12:30 AM – 2:30 AM):** If Phase 1 succeeds, replicate the exact configuration to Washers #2, #3, and #4 sequentially.
3.  **Phase 3 (2:30 AM – 4:00 AM):** Run a full mixed-load stress test on all four units to ensure conveyor synchronization.
4.  **Buffer (4:00 AM – 5:00 AM):** Reserved for rollback or minor tuning before the 6-hour window closes.

**Contingency Plan (Requires Your Authorization):**
If the system fails mid-cycle or scan accuracy drops below 99% during Phase 1, **immediately halt reprogramming on remaining units and revert Washer #1 to the legacy firmware.** Authorize the maintenance crew to switch to "Manual Batch Logging" for the remainder of the weekend. This involves pausing the automated RFID tracking and having staff manually record batch IDs and weights on paper logs tied to time stamps. This ensures traceability for surgical gowns even without real-time RFID data, preventing a sterile supply shortage by Monday. Do not attempt to "fix" the new system during peak flow; prioritize data integrity via manual fallback.

**Pre-Arrival Checks (Maintenance Crew):**
Before the technician unpacks tools, the crew must perform these three checks:
1.  **Power Stability Verification:** Confirm all four industrial washer controllers are receiving stable voltage within the manufacturer's specified tolerance (+/- 5%) to prevent reader corruption during reprogramming.
2.  **Legacy Baseline Capture:** Run a diagnostic scan on the current RFID system for all four washers and save the raw frequency calibration logs. This provides the exact "rollback point" if the new tags fail.
3.  **Conveyor Sensor Clearance:** Physically inspect the scanner apertures on the conveyors for lint, detergent residue, or physical obstruction that could interfere with the new tag frequency, cleaning them if necessary.

**Note:** The 10-minute total downtime limit applies per washer cycle interruption, not the entire 6-hour window; schedule reboots during natural load transitions. Ensure the vendor technician brings the legacy firmware image on a local drive, not reliant on network download.
