**Decision:** Proceed with a **Phased Canary Deployment** starting immediately, capping simultaneous firmware updates at **four vehicles** (strictly below the five-vehicle failure threshold) until validation is complete. Do not attempt a mass rollout.

**Figures:** 18 days remaining; 200 trucks total; 30% paper-log exposure; Max concurrent update limit = 4 trucks (derived from "fail > 5 bricks engines" constraint).

**Deployment Sequence**
1.  **Hour 0–4 (Sandbox & Safety Gate):** Isolate one non-critical truck. Perform the firmware update and digital log integration in a disconnected environment. Verify the engine control unit (ECU) does not brick and data writes locally.
2.  **Day 1–3 (Canary Group):** Select 5 diverse trucks (different routes/ages). Update them **sequentially** (one at a time), not simultaneously. Monitor for 24 hours per truck. If any brick, halt immediately.
3.  **Day 4–10 (Regional Waves):** Roll out to 20 trucks per day in geographic clusters. Updates occur only when trucks are docked for >2 hours. Maintain the 4-truck concurrent limit strictly.
4.  **Day 11–15 (Paper Log Elimination):** Force digital-only input on the updated fleet. Address driver resistance and hardware glitches.
5.  **Day 16–17 (Audit Dry-Run):** Generate sample reports from the new system to verify federal compliance formatting.
6.  **Day 18 (Contingency Buffer):** Reserve for emergency rollbacks or final patching before the October 30th audit.

**Authorization Matrix**
*   **Requires Your Direct Authorization:**
    *   Approving the "Stop Work" trigger if >1 vehicle bricks during the Canary Group.
    *   Authorizing the temporary use of third-party backup logging services if the primary system fails integration tests.
    *   Signing off on the final "Digital Only" mandate date for drivers.
*   **Field Supervisors Handle:**
    *   Scheduling specific trucks for updates based on dock availability.
    *   Managing driver training on the new digital interface.
    *   Executing the physical firmware update process within the 4-truck safety limit.
    *   Collecting and digitizing the remaining 30% of paper logs during the transition.

**Single Most Important Action (First Hour)**
**Establish the "Hard Brake" Protocol and Update Cap.** You must formally issue a written directive stating that **no more than four ECUs will be flashed simultaneously** under any circumstance, and define the exact communication channel (e.g., a dedicated radio frequency or instant message group) that any supervisor must use to declare a "Brick Event," which instantly halts all further updates across the entire fleet. Without this hard constraint enforced before the first tool connects, a coordinated batch update could inadvertently exceed the five-vehicle failure limit and immobilize your core capacity.

**Note:** The 30% paper log usage represents a data continuity risk; ensure supervisors have a manual fallback process for those specific routes until the digital system is proven stable on those specific vehicles.
**Note:** This plan assumes the firmware file itself is not corrupted; verify the file checksum against the vendor's hash before Hour 1.
