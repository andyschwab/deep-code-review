**Decision:** Halt all build and deployment activities immediately. The single most critical action for the first ten minutes is to **draft and sign a "No-Execute" covenant** that technically and procedurally locks the deployment pipeline until three specific conditions are met: (1) a verified rollback script exists, (2) the safety lead has signed off on the logic review, and (3) a maintenance window is officially confirmed by floor management.

**Session Structure & Sequence**

1.  **Define the "Kill Switch" (Minutes 0–15):** Before writing code, define the exact technical command that aborts the flash mid-process and the manual procedure to isolate robots if the network command fails. Document this as the primary safety artifact.
2.  **Rollback Safeguard Planning (Minutes 15–45):** Design the recovery protocol. Since bricking is permanent, your "rollback" is actually a "replacement workflow." Calculate the time and parts needed to swap a failed robot. If this exceeds acceptable downtime, the update cannot proceed.
3.  **Logic Review with Safety Lead (Hour 1–2):** Present the firmware diff and the kill switch protocol to the safety lead. No code is merged until this review is signed. This is a hard gate.
4.  **Build the Update Package (Hour 2–3):** Only after the safety sign-off do engineers build the binary. The build environment must be isolated to prevent accidental pushing.
5.  **Announce Maintenance Window (Hour 3+):** Communicate with floor managers. Provide the exact start time, duration, and the "go/no-go" criteria. Do not announce until the package is built and the rollback is verified.
6.  **Execution (Scheduled Window):** Deploy during the approved off-peak window with the full team present.

**Immediate First Step (First 10 Minutes)**
Convene the five engineers and the session lead. Do not open the IDE. Instead, open a shared document titled "DEPLOYMENT LOCK." Write down the following statement: *"No firmware binary shall be flashed to any robot until the Safety Lead signs off on Section 3 and the Floor Manager confirms the maintenance window in writing."* Have every engineer type their name below this statement. This creates a psychological and procedural break between "preparation" and "execution," preventing the common error of testing the update on live hardware prematurely.

**Note:** Given the irreversible risk of bricking, treat this as a high-stakes surgical operation, not a standard software release. If the rollback plan cannot guarantee robot replacement within the audit timeline, the update must be deferred regardless of the throughput lag.
