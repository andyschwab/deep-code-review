**Decision:** Do not attempt a full infrastructure replacement tonight. The constraint of "zero disruption" combined with a "complete controller reboot" that risks bricking legacy life-support devices creates an unacceptable failure mode that cannot be mitigated within an eight-hour window. The correct order of operations is to **abort the cutover**, execute a non-disruptive audit, and reschedule for a phased migration.

**Figures:** 800 IoT devices vs. 0 allowable failures; 8 hours available vs. unknown time-to-recover bricked devices (potentially days); 1 central controller reboot vs. 800 unique firmware handshake dependencies. The mismatch between the single point of failure (controller) and the zero-failure tolerance dictates a halt.

**Order of Operations:**
1.  **Immediate Halt:** Stop all planned hardware swapping.
2.  **Non-Disruptive Audit:** Use the existing network to map every MAC address to its specific device type and location without sending new handshake commands.
3.  **Vendor Verification:** Contact the biomedical device manufacturers to confirm if their specific firmware versions support the new controller's handshake protocol. This cannot be assumed.
4.  **Phased Pilot:** Schedule a future window to replace *one* access point in a non-critical zone to validate the handshake, not the entire fleet.
5.  **Parallel Run:** Plan for a future date where old and new controllers run simultaneously, migrating devices VLAN-by-VLAN, rather than a "rip and replace" reboot.

You must personally sign off on the **Vendor Verification Matrix** before any hardware is touched.
