**Decision:** Execute a "Parallel Shadow Run" strategy where the new engine processes live traffic alongside the legacy system for 14 days before the irreversible cutover. Do not delete legacy mapping tables until the new engine matches 99.9% adjudication accuracy against the legacy baseline for 72 consecutive hours.

**Figures:** 5,000 daily claims × 14 days shadow = 70,000 validation transactions; 4-hour downtime window allows for 20,000 claims buffer (4 hours × 5,000/24); Q4 end (Dec 31) minus 14-day shadow minus 7-day validation = Go/No-Go decision deadline of December 10th.

**Delegation to Data Team:**
Assign the data analysts exclusive ownership of the "Shadow Comparison" phase. Their specific deliverables are:
1.  Build the dual-write connector that sends live claims to both the legacy system and the new engine without affecting production latency.
2.  Generate daily discrepancy reports highlighting any claim where the new engine's ruling (pay/deny/amount) differs from the legacy ruling.
3.  Validate the historical mapping tables by running a sample of past year's claims through the new engine to ensure no regression in historical logic before deletion is authorized.

**Risks Requiring Your Direct Approval:**
1.  **The Deletion Authorization:** You must personally sign off on the command to delete legacy mapping tables. This is the single point of no return. Require a written certificate from the data team lead confirming the 72-hour accuracy threshold was met.
2.  **The Fallback Trigger:** Define the exact error rate that forces an immediate rollback to the legacy system during the 4-hour cutover. If the new engine fails to process 95% of claims correctly within the first hour of live switching, you must authorize the reversion plan immediately.
3.  **Compliance Sign-off:** Before the shadow run begins, you must approve the data privacy impact assessment to ensure dual-processing does not violate patient data residency rules.

**First Action Item:**
Commission a "Data Lineage Audit" immediately. Before writing any connector code, have the data team map every field in the current legacy mapping tables to the new engine's schema. Identify any fields in the legacy system that have no counterpart in the new engine. This audit determines if the new engine can actually reconstruct historical rulings if needed, or if an archive strategy is required before deletion.

**Halt Trigger:** If the Data Lineage Audit reveals more than 5% of historical claim attributes cannot be mapped to the new schema, halt the migration plan. The risk of losing reconstructible history is too high.

**Fallback Trigger:** If the shadow run shows a discrepancy rate higher than 0.1% after 7 days, revert to the legacy system for the quarter-end and delay the cutover to Q1.
