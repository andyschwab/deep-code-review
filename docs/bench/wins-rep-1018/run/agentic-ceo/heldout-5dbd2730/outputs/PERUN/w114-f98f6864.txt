### Planning Session Flow & Order of Operations

**Phase 1: Data Pipeline & Bridging (Weeks 1–6)**
1.  **Build the 24-Hour Bridge:** Engineer a middleware layer that ingests the single daily legacy export and normalizes it for the new platform. This is your single point of failure; prioritize robustness over features.
2.  **Historical Replay:** Load the last 90 days of historical data into the new platform to verify slot calculation logic against known outcomes.
3.  **Shadow Mode Launch:** Run the new platform in "read-only" parallel with the legacy system. It ingests the daily export and calculates schedules, but results are not published. Compare outputs daily.

**Phase 2: Validation & Dry Run (Weeks 7–9)**
4.  **Discrepancy Analysis:** Investigate any divergence between legacy and new platform slot calculations. Zero tolerance for unexplained variance.
5.  **Full Dry Run:** Simulate the cutover process end-to-end using a snapshot of current data. Measure the time required to switch and, critically, the time required to roll back.
6.  **Freeze Protocol:** Define the exact timestamp (T-minus 72 hours) when no further changes are permitted in the legacy system to ensure the final export is clean.

**Phase 3: Cutover (Week 10, prior to August 10)**
7.  **Final Export & Import:** Execute the final data migration.
8.  **Go/No-Go Gate:** If the dry run metrics were met and shadow mode showed 100% calculation alignment, authorize cutover.
9.  **Live Monitoring:** Watch the first 24 hours of live scheduling intensely.

### Critical Decisions Required: Rollback Strategy

You must decide and document the **Rollback Trigger Threshold** now. Do not wait for the crisis to define failure.
*   **The Trigger:** Define the specific error rate (e.g., >0.1% slot miscalculation) or latency spike that immediately aborts the cutover.
*   **The Mechanism:** Since you cannot easily roll back live shipments, your rollback strategy is **not** switching software back; it is **reverting to a manual contingency plan**. You must decide: If the new platform fails, do we revert to the legacy read-only view and dispatch manually, or do we halt all non-essential scheduling until fixed? Given the "hundreds of confirmed shipments," the default must be a pre-printed, manual fallback schedule generated from the last good legacy export.
