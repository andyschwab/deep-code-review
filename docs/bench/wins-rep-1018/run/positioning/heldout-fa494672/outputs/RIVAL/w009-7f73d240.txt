### Recommendation: Target Pediatrician Referrals First

**Primary Segment to Dominate:** Parents of children with diagnosed food allergies who are currently under the active care of pediatric allergists in Tokyo (specifically starting with clinics like Sakura Children's Clinic).

**Specific Behavior Being Replaced:** You are not just replacing "notebook logs"; you are replacing the **fragmented, anxiety-driven communication loop** between parent and doctor. Currently, parents manually track symptoms in notebooks or generic apps, then struggle to summarize months of data into a coherent story during a 10-minute consultation. Your app replaces this with a clinical-grade report generated automatically from the symptom logger, turning a stressful guesswork session into a data-driven diagnosis review.

**Differentiation from Generic Apps (e.g., WellBaby):**
Generic health apps like WellBaby are designed for *general wellness* and broad milestone tracking. They lack the specificity required for high-stakes allergy management.
*   **Trust & Validation:** Unlike WellBaby, which offers unverified tips, AllergyTrack JP provides data validated by the child's own doctor. The differentiation is not just "features," but **clinical endorsement**. A generic app says, "You logged a rash." AllergyTrack JP says, "Dr. Tanaka has reviewed this log and confirmed this reaction pattern."
*   **Outcome Specificity:** Generic apps aim for "awareness." AllergyTrack JP aims for **ER avoidance**. Your data proves a 40% reduction in ER visits compared to paper diaries; generic apps cannot claim this because they are not integrated into the clinical workflow that prevents escalation.
*   **Workflow Integration:** Parents tolerate generic apps because they are "better than nothing," but they abandon them when the data isn't useful to the doctor. AllergyTrack JP differs by closing the loop: the output is formatted specifically for the pediatrician's review, making the app indispensable for the medical visit itself.

**Strategic Rationale:**
Given your 12-month runway and the high-trust nature of pediatric health in Japan, a direct-to-parent social media strategy is too slow and expensive to overcome the initial trust barrier. Parents are unlikely to switch from a tolerated habit (notebooks) to a new app for a life-or-death condition without professional validation.

By partnering with pediatricians like Dr. Tanaka, you leverage their existing trust capital. The doctor becomes the sales channel, instantly solving the "trust" doubt. While this feels slower initially, it creates a defensible moat: once a doctor integrates your reports into their workflow, they will not recommend a competitor. This "wedge" strategy allows you to dominate a specific, high-value niche (clinic-referred families) before expanding to the broader market. Your 40% ER reduction statistic is your strongest asset, but it only resonates when framed as a clinical outcome endorsed by a medical professional, not a marketing claim on Instagram.

*Note: Secure a formal pilot agreement with Sakura Children's Clinic immediately to validate the referral workflow before scaling to other practices.*
*Focus your limited runway on making the doctor's experience seamless; if the doctor loves the generated reports, the parent adoption will follow automatically.*
