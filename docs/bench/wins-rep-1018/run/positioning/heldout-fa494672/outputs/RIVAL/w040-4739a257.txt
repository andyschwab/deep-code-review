### Comparative Analysis: AndesSkin Positioning Strategies

#### Option A: Public Health Solution (Ministry of Health)
*   **Primary Stakeholder:** The Minister of Health and regional directors responsible for rural equity metrics.
*   **Primary Economic Argument:** **Cost Avoidance & Equity Compliance.** You must argue that early detection in remote villages prevents late-stage cancer treatments, which are exponentially more expensive for the state. The value proposition is reducing the six-hour travel burden and fulfilling constitutional mandates for universal health access, positioning AndesSkin as the only viable mechanism to reach the 800+ unconnected villagers without building physical infrastructure.
*   **Biggest Risk:** **Procurement Bureaucracy & Payment Cycles.** Selling to the government often involves tender processes lasting 12–18 months with delayed payments. For a team of eight, a single delayed contract renewal or budget freeze could cause immediate cash flow collapse before the next cycle opens.

#### Option B: Urban Efficiency Tool (Private Clinics)
*   **Primary Stakeholder:** Clinic Owners and Medical Directors at private urban practices facing overflow.
*   **Primary Economic Argument:** **Throughput & Revenue Capture.** You must argue that AndesSkin allows clinics to triage non-urgent cases remotely, freeing up specialist hours for high-revenue procedures. By reducing diagnosis time from three weeks to 48 hours, clinics can increase patient volume and prevent churn to competitors, directly translating to higher monthly revenue.
*   **Biggest Risk:** **Market Saturation & Feature Commoditization.** Private clinics may view tele-dermatology as a feature they can replicate using existing WhatsApp workflows or purchase from established global SaaS vendors. Unlike the government, they have no mandate to serve the remote poor, so your unique "Andean village" data and mission may be irrelevant to their bottom line.

#### Strategic Recommendation: The Rural Public Health Angle
You asked specifically for the biggest risk of choosing the **Rural Public Health** angle over the Urban Private angle.

**The Risk:** **Mission-Market Mismatch.**
By positioning solely as a public health solution for remote equity, you risk becoming dependent on donor funding or government grants that are politically volatile. If the political administration changes or international aid shifts focus, your entire revenue model evaporates. Furthermore, this positioning may make it difficult to pivot later to the private sector; private clinics may perceive a "government tool" as slow, non-compliant with private insurance standards, or lacking the premium features they expect. You risk building a product optimized for low-resource constraints that fails to meet the speed and integration demands of high-end urban clinics, effectively locking you out of the more liquid private market.

**Note:** Given your team size of eight, the long sales cycles of the public sector pose an existential threat unless you have bridge funding secured.
Consider a hybrid pilot where urban private revenue subsidizes the rural public mission, rather than choosing one exclusively.
