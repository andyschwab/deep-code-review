### Strategic Recommendation: Target School Districts First

You should position FitKids Slovakia as a **curriculum supplement for teachers**. While parents control the wallets, they are currently blocked by the "screen time" objection. Schools, however, already mandate movement time; your app solves their specific problem of low engagement during those mandatory minutes. By securing school adoption, you bypass the parent's initial skepticism. Once children love the app at school, they will demand it at home, turning the "family bonding" angle into an upsell strategy rather than your primary entry point. Selling directly to parents now forces you to fight the "screen time" battle alone; selling to schools lets you frame the app as "active learning," neutralizing that objection.

***

### Positioning Statement

**Value Proposition**
**For** elementary school teachers and physical education coordinators
**that need** to maximize active movement during limited class time without relying on boring, repetitive drills
**FitKids Slovakia**
**is a** gamified movement curriculum platform
**that** turns standard exercise routines into collaborative group challenges, doubling student participation rates while keeping devices as tools for activity rather than passive consumption.

**Differentiation Statement**
**Unlike** traditional physical education classes that engage students for only 45 minutes a week, or generic fitness apps that fail to retain children under 12 due to lack of social context
**FitKids Slovakia**
**provides** a structured, teacher-led competitive environment that leverages peer motivation to sustain high-energy movement, proving that screen-based tools can drive physical output rather than sedentary behavior.

***

*Note: This positioning reframes the tablet from a "distraction" to a "coach," directly addressing the parent's screen time concern by validating usage through educational institutions.*
*Focus your immediate sales efforts on pilot programs in schools to generate the case studies needed to later convince parents for home subscriptions.*
