**Recommendation: Pivot to Selling Directly to Insurance Providers**

To scale faster and neutralize the liability barrier blocking your deals with Clínica Alemana and Universidad Católica, you must shift your primary customer from OB-GYN clinics to insurance providers (Isapres and FONASA administrators).

**Why the "Educate Doctors" Path Will Fail**
Attempting to double down on educating doctors to accept legal risk is a strategic dead end in the current Chilean context. Your analysis confirms that doctors are not ignoring paper forms due to ignorance; they are ignoring them as a deliberate legal defense strategy. Paper forms create plausible deniability ("I didn't see the score"), whereas your digital tool creates an undeniable record of knowledge ("The software flagged this, and I saw it").

No amount of education will convince a physician to voluntarily assume higher personal liability when the current low-tech alternative offers them legal safety, even at the cost of patient outcomes. As long as the doctor is the buyer, the sale hinges on their personal risk tolerance, which is currently zero. This path requires changing deep-seated cultural and legal behaviors, a process that takes years, not quarters. You will remain stuck in legal review limbo while patient harm continues.

**Why the Insurance Provider Path Scales Faster**
Selling to insurance providers aligns incentives and removes the liability bottleneck from the individual physician.

1.  **Shifts the Risk Bearer:** Insurance providers are financially motivated to reduce long-term costs associated with severe postpartum depression cases (hospitalizations, chronic care, litigation settlements). They view the 40% miss rate of paper forms as a financial leak. Unlike individual doctors, insurers have the capital and legal structure to absorb and manage risk. They can purchase your tool as a population health mandate.
2.  **Changes the Legal Dynamic:** When an insurer mandates the use of MamaMind, the liability framework shifts. The usage becomes a standard of care dictated by the payer, not a discretionary choice by the doctor. The insurer can provide indemnification or legal coverage for clinics using the mandated tool, effectively shielding the individual OB-GYN. The "fear of being sued" is replaced by "compliance with insurer requirements."
3.  **Accelerates Adoption:** Instead of selling to 45 individual clinics one by one, convincing one major insurer (or the two hospital chains acting as payers) allows you to deploy across their entire network instantly. The decision moves from the exam room to the boardroom, where the 90% sensitivity metric is viewed as a cost-saving asset rather than a legal threat.

**Strategic Execution**
Reframe your value proposition: MamaMind is no longer just a screening tool for doctors; it is a risk-mitigation asset for insurers. Your pitch to Clínica Alemana and Universidad Católica should bypass their legal teams' fear by proposing a pilot where the insurance arm sponsors the deployment. Offer a contractual agreement where the insurer assumes the liability risk in exchange for the data and cost reductions your 90% sensitivity provides.

By making the insurer the customer, you turn the doctor from a terrified decision-maker into a compliant user of a mandated system. This is the only path that resolves the specific legal paralysis you are facing while unlocking rapid scale across the Chilean healthcare system.

*Note: Ensure your contracts explicitly define the insurer's assumption of liability to protect your own company.*
