Your draft statement, "WoundWatch replaces unnecessary clinic visits with accurate home monitoring," fails because it is too generic and triggers immediate defensive reflexes in your key stakeholders. By leading with "replaces visits," you alienate surgeons worried about revenue loss before establishing clinical value. Furthermore, "accurate home monitoring" is a feature claim, not a benefit outcome; it doesn't tell the buyer *why* accuracy matters (e.g., preventing sepsis, reducing anxiety, or saving time). Finally, it ignores the true primary competitor: unsecured messaging apps like WhatsApp, which are free and already embedded in the workflow.

Here is a revised Geoffrey Moore-style positioning statement that addresses these gaps:

**Value Proposition**
*   **For** post-operative patients and surgical teams at high-volume centers
*   **That need** to detect early signs of cellulitis without compromising data privacy or requiring physical travel
*   **WoundWatch**
*   **Is a** medical-grade remote wound monitoring system
*   **That** triages infection risks instantly via encrypted analysis, ensuring only critical cases require clinic visits

**Differentiation Statement**
*   **Unlike** informal photo sharing via unsecured messaging apps or reactive in-person inspections
*   **WoundWatch**
*   **Provides** standardized, clinically validated image capture and automated risk stratification that integrates securely into hospital workflows

### Analysis of Your Specific Questions

**1. Who exactly is the buyer?**
In this B2B2C model, the **patient** is the user, but the **surgical center or hospital administration** is the economic buyer. They bear the cost of no-shows, readmissions due to late infection detection, and potential liability from data breaches (WhatsApp). The surgeon is the *influencer*; if they feel threatened, they will block adoption. Your positioning must appeal to the hospital's desire for efficiency and risk mitigation while reassuring the surgeon that this tool enhances their practice rather than replacing it.

**2. How do you differ from the status quo (WhatsApp)?**
The status quo relies on variable lighting, non-standard angles, and zero data security. Your differentiation is not just "analysis" (which you want to protect), but **standardization and compliance**.
*   **Status Quo:** "Dr. Muller guesses based on a blurry, dark photo sent at 8 PM on an unsecured line."
*   **WoundWatch:** "The system guarantees a medically lit, focused image analyzed by a compliant algorithm, flagging only the 15% of cases that actually need attention."
You are selling **triage certainty**, not just image taking.

**3. Refining the "Replacement" Fear**
Do not position this as replacing the surgeon or the visit entirely. Position it as replacing **uncertainty** and **low-value interactions**. You are replacing the "anxiety-induced phone call" and the "routine check-up where nothing is wrong." This protects the surgeon's billable hours for complex cases while eliminating the administrative burden of reviewing hundreds of safe wounds.

**Note:** Ensure your pilot data from the 85 patients specifically highlights the reduction in false-positive alarms compared to WhatsApp to prove the "triage" claim.
Focus your sales pitch to hospital admins on "liability reduction" regarding patient data, as this is a stronger lever than convenience.
