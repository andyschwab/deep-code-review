**Stress Test of Your "Cost-Saving" Pitch**
Your current pitch fails because it asks the CFO to bet on *future* savings (reduced claims) against *current* certain costs, while competing against a "free" alternative they already pay for via taxes. CFOs discount future insurance savings heavily because the lag time is long, the attribution is fuzzy, and the savings often benefit the insurer more than the company depending on the plan structure. Furthermore, framing yourself against "free gyms" invites a feature comparison you lose on price. The gym argument is a trap; you cannot win a cost battle against a government subsidy.

**The Sharper Angle: "Liability Conversion"**
Stop selling "savings" and start selling "asset recovery." Your pitch must reframe the existing gym subsidy not as a competitor, but as a **sunk cost with zero ROI**. The narrative should be: "You are already paying for wellness via taxes and subsidies, but 80% of that capital is wasted on unused memberships that do not move metabolic needles. PreventCorp is not an *add-on* cost; it is the *activation layer* that makes your existing wellness spend actually reduce liability."

Shift the metric from "money saved" to "risk mitigated per dollar spent." A CFO understands that a 0.8% drop in HbA1c across 3,500 employees is a quantifiable reduction in future balance sheet liability, whereas a gym membership is an indefinite expense with no outcome guarantee. Position HealthHub and gyms as "monitoring tools" and PreventCorp as the "intervention engine." You are not selling a gym alternative; you are selling the only solution that closes the gap between *having* a wellness budget and *achieving* wellness results.
