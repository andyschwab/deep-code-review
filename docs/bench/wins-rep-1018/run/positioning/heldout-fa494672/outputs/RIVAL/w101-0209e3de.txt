### Strategic Recommendation: Choose "Simplicity Tool"

You should position RailLink Europe as a **simplicity tool that saves administrative time**, not as an aggregator competing on network width.

**Reasoning:**
1.  **Defensibility:** You cannot win a "widest network" battle against incumbents like DB Cargo or SNCF Connect. They own the infrastructure; you are a layer on top. Claiming superior coverage invites skepticism (which your users already expressed) and sets you up for failure if a specific lane is missing.
2.  **Proven Value:** Your data shows a 40% increase in bookings driven by reducing clicks from eight to two. This is a tangible, proven outcome. Your customers are already voting with their wallets for speed, not coverage.
3.  **Target Alignment:** Small freight forwarders are resource-constrained. Their primary pain is not a lack of options (the market is fragmented but full); their pain is the *cost* of accessing those options (4 hours of manual emailing). Solving the "time tax" is more urgent than solving "network breadth."

### The Specific Friction Being Eliminated

The specific friction RailLink eliminates is **context-switching and data re-entry across fragmented carrier silos.**

Currently, a small forwarder faces a disjointed workflow:
1.  **Fragmented Discovery:** They must leave their workspace to visit multiple carrier portals (DB, SNCF, etc.) individually to check availability, as no single view exists.
2.  **Manual Reconciliation:** They must manually compare rates and schedules across these disparate systems, often copying data into spreadsheets.
3.  **Redundant Entry:** Once a choice is made, they must re-enter identical shipment details (weight, dimensions, origin/destination) into the chosen carrier's specific portal or draft lengthy emails to brokers.

This process forces the forwarder to act as a human API, manually transporting data between incompatible systems. RailLink eliminates this by providing a **unified interface** that normalizes the input once and distributes it instantly, turning a 4-hour administrative burden into a 2-click transaction. The value is not just "booking a train"; it is the removal of the administrative overhead required to navigate a fragmented market.
