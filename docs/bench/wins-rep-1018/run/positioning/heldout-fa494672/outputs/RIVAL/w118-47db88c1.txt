**Positioning Statement for SwiftCollect**

**Value Proposition**
**For** large e-commerce shippers and retailers in Nigeria (e.g., Jumia, Konga)
**that need** to drastically reduce the cost and complexity of reverse logistics in rural regions without building their own infrastructure
**SwiftCollect**
**is a** distributed last-mile returns network
**that** lowers return processing costs by 40% compared to proprietary networks while delivering 3x faster turnaround than national postal services.

**Differentiation Statement**
**Unlike** building proprietary drop-off centers or relying on slow national postal services
**SwiftCollect**
**provides** immediate access to an existing, incentivized network of 200+ local agents that scales with volume, eliminating the millions in upfront capital expenditure required for physical infrastructure.

***

**Strategic Recommendation:**
You must position SwiftCollect as **infrastructure for retailers**, not a livelihood engine. Investors funding logistics rounds buy unit economics, scale, and defensibility; they view "social impact" as a positive byproduct, not the primary value driver. Your data proves shippers care about cost and speed: if you prioritize speed over agent payout, retention collapses, meaning your *product* (the agent network) breaks. Therefore, the "livelihood" aspect is your **operational moat** (how you keep the network alive cheaply), but the **value proposition** must remain focused on solving the shipper's expensive returns problem. Positioning as a "livelihood engine" confuses buyers who simply want cheaper returns; positioning as "low-cost infrastructure" explains exactly why you are cheaper than them building it themselves.
