**Positioning Statement**

**For** hospital administrators and oncology department heads
**that need** to prove financial ROI from remote patient monitoring while preventing avoidable acute episodes
**OncoTrack Slovenia**
**is a** tele-oncology outcomes platform
**that** converts scattered clinical improvements into verified departmental savings and reduces ER visits by 35%
**Unlike** standard weekly phone check-ins that miss 60% of acute episodes and offer no cost-tracking visibility
**OncoTrack Slovenia** provides real-time symptom interception with automated financial attribution that validates operational efficiency.

**Strategic Recommendation: The Operational Efficiency Engine**

You should position OncoTrack as an **operational efficiency engine**, not just a clinical safety net. While "patient safety" is the emotional hook, hospital administrators cannot approve budgets based on vague safety claims when the status quo (phone calls) appears "free." Your primary barrier is not clinical efficacy—you have proven that with the 35% ER reduction—but financial measurability.

By framing the product as an efficiency engine, you directly address the administrator's inability to measure ROI. Phone protocols are not actually free; they consume significant, untracked nurse labor and result in costly ER leaks. Your platform captures the data necessary to quantify these hidden costs. A "safety net" position implies an insurance policy (nice to have); an "efficiency engine" position implies a profit center (must have).

**Stress Test of Message Hierarchy**

1.  **"Guarantee patient safety" (Weakest):** This is table stakes, not a differentiator. Administrators assume all approved tools meet safety standards. Furthermore, "guarantee" is a dangerous legal claim in healthcare. This message fails to displace the status quo because phone calls also claim to ensure safety, despite missing 60% of episodes. It does not solve the measurement gap.
2.  **"Reduce ER chaos" (Stronger):** This highlights the outcome (35% reduction) and appeals to the pain of overcrowding. However, "chaos" is an emotional descriptor that lacks the precision required for a CFO. While better than safety, it still focuses on the *clinical* result rather than the *business* mechanism, leaving the ROI question partially unanswered.
3.  **"Save nursing hours" (Strongest - The Winner):** This is the only message that directly attacks the economic model of the status quo. Weekly phone calls are labor-intensive and inefficient. By positioning OncoTrack as a tool that reclaims nurse time while simultaneously preventing expensive ER visits, you provide the two levers administrators need to calculate ROI: reduced labor cost + reduced acute care cost. This message transforms the conversation from "Can we afford this?" to "Can we afford not to track these savings?"

**Conclusion:** The path that clearly replaces reactive phone calls is **"Save nursing hours"** (operational efficiency). It exposes the hidden labor cost of the current "free" phone protocol and pairs it with your hard data on ER reductions to create an undeniable financial argument.
