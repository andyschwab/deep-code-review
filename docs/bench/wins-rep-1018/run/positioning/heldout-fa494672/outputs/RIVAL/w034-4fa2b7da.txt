### Strategic Recommendation: Focus on the Clinical Patient Segment

**Recommendation:** PulseGuard SA should abandon the amateur sports market and focus exclusively on becoming a prescribed medical device for patients with known arrhythmia conditions.

**Analysis of Options:**
*   **Consumer Sports Market (Abandon):** While attractive for volume, this segment creates significant liability risks without regulatory clearance as a diagnostic tool. Athletes seek "reassurance," which is subjective, whereas your device provides clinical data. Competing against smartwatches here is a race to the bottom on price and brand hype, where tech giants dominate. Furthermore, marketing "medical-grade" data to consumers often triggers regulatory scrutiny if not carefully framed as "wellness only," diluting your core value.
*   **Clinical Patient Segment (Focus):** This aligns with your current deployment (1,250 units) and core competency. Cardiologists and clinics like Heart Institute Cape Town need diagnostic certainty, not just data. Your 40% cost advantage over traditional Holter monitors is a decisive factor for insurance providers and clinic budgets. The "14-day continuous recording" is a specific clinical benefit that addresses the "intermittent event" problem better than standard 24-48 hour Holters. By focusing here, you move from a confusing dual-message to a clear value proposition: a cost-effective, prescribed diagnostic tool that improves detection rates.

**Conclusion:** The clinical path offers a defensible moat through medical partnerships, insurance reimbursement codes, and regulatory clearance. It solves a critical, high-stakes problem (diagnostic certainty) rather than a nice-to-have (training safety).
