### Strategic Recommendation
**Choose the "Urban Working Class" segment.**

In the Guyanese context, the rural population's primary barrier is not just the cost of care, but a lack of digital infrastructure and a deep-seated reliance on immediate, tangible solutions (the $15 cream). Trying to shift rural behavior from "home remedy" to "remote monitoring" requires educating the market on telemedicine itself, which is expensive and slow.

Conversely, the urban working class in Georgetown and Linden already values time over money. Their pain point is acute: missing a day's wage to wait three weeks for a $45 consultation is a genuine economic threat. Your 88% retention rate proves you have already solved the trust issue for this group. Positioning as a "cost-effective alternative" for rural families dilutes your value; you are not cheaper than a $15 cream. You are *more effective* than a cream and *faster* than a clinic. Win the urban market first where the economics of time make your service a no-brainer, then expand.

**Differentiation Statement**

**DermiLink**
