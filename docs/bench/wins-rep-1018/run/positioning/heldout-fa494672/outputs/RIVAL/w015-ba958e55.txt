### Comparative Analysis: Parents vs. Speech Therapists

**Option A: Targeting Anxious Parents (Direct-to-Consumer)**
*   **Pros:** Direct access to the 600 potential families in Sweden; immediate resolution of the "boring flashcard" pain point; ability to leverage the "4x longer practice" metric as a direct emotional win for parents worried about their child's progress; no need to convince a third-party gatekeeper.
*   **Cons:** High susceptibility to the "gamification distracts from technique" objection without a therapist's seal of approval; parents may lack the expertise to verify articulation quality, leading to potential plateauing or frustration; marketing costs to reach scattered individuals are higher per acquisition than institutional sales.

**Option B: Targeting Speech Therapists (B2B2C)**
*   **Pros:** Therapists are the trusted authority who can validate that SpeechPlay maintains correct technique while increasing engagement; a single therapist adoption can onboard multiple patients instantly; positions the app as a professional "homework tool" rather than just a game, directly countering the skepticism about gamification; solves the therapist's pain point of patients not practicing between expensive (1,000 SEK/hr) sessions.
*   **Cons:** Longer sales cycle due to professional skepticism; requires proving clinical efficacy over simple engagement; therapists may be resistant to changing established workflows or recommending specific commercial apps.

### Recommendation

**Primary Audience: Speech Therapists**

**Justification:**
You should target speech therapists as your primary entry point. While parents feel the immediate pain of boring flashcards, they are not the experts who can validate your solution against the critical objection that "gamification distracts from correct technique." In the medical and therapeutic domain, trust is the currency of adoption. If you target parents directly, every sale will be fragile, constantly undermined by the fear that the child is playing but not learning correctly.

By positioning SpeechPlay as a **professional homework tool for therapists**, you turn your biggest weakness (the gamification concern) into your strongest asset. When a therapist prescribes the app, they implicitly certify that the game mechanics do not compromise technique. This endorsement neutralizes the skepticism of both the parent and the therapist community. Furthermore, given the high cost of private sessions (1,000 SEK/hr), therapists are highly motivated to find tools that ensure their patients practice effectively between visits to maximize the return on investment for the family. Your data showing "4x longer practice" is not just a fun stat for parents; it is a clinical efficiency argument for therapists. Securing the therapist as the champion allows you to scale through their existing patient rosters, converting your 200 active families into a case study that convinces the broader Swedish therapeutic community, rather than fighting a fragmented battle for individual parent attention.

*Note: Ensure your messaging to therapists highlights "clinically validated engagement" rather than just "fun."*
*Focus your roadmap on therapist dashboards that allow them to monitor technique accuracy, not just time spent.*
