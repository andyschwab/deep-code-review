### Recommended Strategic Focus
**Target the Small Fleet Owner (10–20 trucks) exclusively for the next year.**

While your current base is solo owner-operators, your 20% churn rate when they attempt to grow indicates your product fails at the exact moment of highest customer lifetime value expansion. The "solo driver" market is fragmented and price-sensitive, often reverting to free spreadsheets. In contrast, small fleet owners managing 10–20 trucks have hit the ceiling of what spreadsheets can handle but find legacy tools like FleetMaster too complex and expensive. This is your "chasm" crossing point. By positioning for the growing fleet, you solve the retention issue (giving them a home as they scale) and attack a more defensible market segment where efficiency directly impacts margin across multiple assets.

**Core Message Focus:** Highlight **fleet-wide efficiency**. Solo drivers care about individual earnings, but fleet owners care about asset utilization and administrative overhead. Your value proposition must shift from "helping one driver make more money" to "helping one manager control ten trucks without drowning in paperwork."

### Geoffrey Moore-Style Positioning Statement

**Value Proposition**
**For** small drayage fleet owners in the Long Beach port complex managing 10 to 20 trucks,
**that need** to digitize dispatch operations without the bloated complexity and cost of legacy enterprise software,
**PortFlow**
**is a** specialized dispatch platform for container drayage,
**that** centralizes booking and tracking to eliminate spreadsheet chaos while scaling seamlessly as the fleet grows.

**Differentiation Statement**
**Unlike** manual spreadsheets that break down beyond five trucks or legacy systems like FleetMaster that require heavy IT overhead,
**PortFlow**
**provides** a lightweight, port-specific workflow that delivers enterprise-level visibility with the simplicity independent operators trust.

***

**Note:** This positioning explicitly names the "10–20 truck" segment to force specificity, avoiding the trap of trying to be everything to everyone. It frames spreadsheets and FleetMaster as the two extremes you sit between, validating the customer's current frustration.
