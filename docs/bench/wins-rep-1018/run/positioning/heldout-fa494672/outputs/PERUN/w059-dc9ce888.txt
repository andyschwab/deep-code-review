**Positioning Hypothesis (to validate with buyers)**

For **CFS patients in Liechtenstein** who **need to manage limited energy without triggering crashes**, **PaceRight** is a **self-management pacing tool** that **replaces harmful "push-through" tracking with safe, data-driven activity balancing**; unlike **generic fitness trackers (e.g., Fitbit) or unanalyzable paper diaries**, it **uses a unique pacing algorithm to prevent overexertion while generating clear pattern reports patients can optionally share with clinicians**.

**Core Message:** Stop guessing your limits. Start pacing them.
**Headline:** The first activity tracker designed specifically *not* to make you push harder.

**Why this direction?**
We commit to the **self-management tool** position. The clinical route requires validation studies you stated you cannot afford yet; attempting to position as a "medical device" now would be an unverified claim that risks regulatory issues and credibility. By positioning as a patient-owned tool that *generates reliable data*, you solve the immediate patient pain (harmful pushing) and create a bottom-up pull where patients bring credible reports to doctors, rather than waiting for a top-down prescription you cannot yet support.

**Addressing your alternatives:**
*   **Vs. Generic Fitness Trackers:** Buyers give up the danger of being encouraged to "beat yesterday's steps." Our line: "Fitness trackers measure how much you did; PaceRight measures how much you *can* do safely."
*   **Vs. Paper Diaries:** Buyers give up the frustration of writing data they can never analyze. Our line: "Turn your daily symptoms into actionable patterns instantly."
*   **Vs. Wellness Apps:** Buyers give up vague "mindfulness" advice. Our line: "Built specifically for the physiology of Chronic Fatigue Syndrome, not general fitness."

**Riskiest Assumption & Test:**
*Assumption:* Patients are willing to pay for a self-management tool without a doctor's explicit recommendation.
*Test:* Run a landing page campaign targeting Liechtenstein CFS support groups offering a 14-day trial. Measure conversion rate against a control message that mentions "doctor recommended" (even if currently just a future feature) to see if the lack of clinical endorsement blocks sales.
