**Positioning Hypothesis to Validate**

**For** risk-averse HR Directors at major Colombian enterprises (e.g., Bancolombia, Grupo Éxito) **who** need to reduce absenteeism and turnover without triggering reputational stigma, **NuevaVida** is a **confidential productivity recovery partner** **that** restores high-performing employees to full capacity through proven peer support; **unlike** generic EAPs (e.g., Sura) or internal counseling that often fail to sustain deep recovery, **it** delivers verified long-term sobriety (850 successful cases) under a strict "health asset" framework that isolates the solution from public brand association.

**Core Message (The Reframe)**
"Do not sell 'addiction support.' Sell 'retention of trained talent.'"
*Current Risk:* Your language likely highlights "addiction" and "recovery," forcing the HR Director to visualize a drug problem inside their company. This triggers fear of reputational damage.
*Proposed Pivot:* Frame the service exclusively as **"High-Risk Employee Retention & Productivity Restoration."** The problem is not "drugs"; the problem is "losing invested human capital to preventable health crises."

**Message House & Proof Points**
1.  **Pillar: Confidentiality as a Shield, Not a Secret.**
    *   *Claim:* The program is structurally invisible to the public and the broader workforce; only the specific participant and HR know.
    *   *Proof Needed:* [USER TO SUPPLY] Specific protocol details on how data is siloed so the company name never appears in marketing or group settings. Without this, the fear remains valid.

    *   *Claim:* Generic EAPs offer short-term talk therapy; NuevaVida offers sustained behavioral change.
    *   *Proof:* 850 individuals maintained sobriety over 2 years. (Use this number as the anchor of reliability, not the nature of the illness).
3.  **Pillar: Economic Asset, Not Liability.**
    *   *Claim:* Replacing a senior banker or retail manager costs far more than the 50M COP budget.

**Addressing the Alternatives**
*   **Vs. Generic EAPs (Sura):** They offer breadth (many issues, shallow help). You offer depth (one critical issue, permanent resolution). *Buyer gives up:* The false comfort of a "check-the-box" vendor for actual retention of specific high-value staff.
*   **Vs. Internal Counseling:** Internal staff lack anonymity and specialized peer dynamics. *Buyer gives up:* The illusion of control for a solution that actually works outside the office walls.

**Riskiest Assumption & Test**
*   *Assumption:* HR Directors believe they can completely decouple the service from the company brand in the eyes of employees.
*   *Test:* Present the "invisible protocol" to 3 HR targets. If they ask, "What if employees talk?", the positioning fails. You must prove the operational silence is stronger than the stigma.
