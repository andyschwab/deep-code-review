### Value Proposition Stress Test: GeneRare

**Current Argument Diagnosis**
Your current pitch fails because it asks insurers to compare **line-item costs** ($600 vs. $150) rather than **total cost of care**. Insurers operate on short-term budget cycles; a 4x immediate price increase triggers an automatic "no," regardless of long-term logic. You are selling a "luxury" because you haven't quantified the "tax" they are currently paying for the status quo.

**The Missing Frame: Cost of Delay (CoD)**
To shift the conversation, you must stop selling a "test" and start selling "odyssey termination." The argument must move from *unit price* to *net savings per member per year (PMPY)*.

**Revised Hypothesis for Payers**
*For [Insurance Payers] managing [members with undiagnosed rare conditions], GeneRare is not a diagnostic test but a [Cost Containment Tool] that [eliminates the 4.5-year diagnostic odyssey]; unlike [standard blood panels], it [prevents $30,000 in downstream waste within 14 days], delivering a net savings of ~$29,400 per confirmed case.*

**Critical Gaps in Your Evidence**
To convince a financial decision-maker, your current data ($30k waste, 4.5 years) is too aggregate. You are missing the specific math they require to approve a formulary change:

1.  **The "Hit Rate" Variable:** Insurers will ask: "If we cover this for 1,000 symptomatic patients, how many actually have a rare disease?" If your yield is low, the $600 cost applies to many negatives, diluting the savings. You need data on your **Positive Predictive Value (PPV)** in your specific referral population.
2.  **Attributable Savings Breakdown:** The "$30,000" figure is likely an industry average. You need a breakdown of *what* that money spends on (e.g., MRIs, specialist consults, ineffective medications) and evidence that *your* test specifically prevents those exact line items. General claims are dismissed; line-item offsets are approved.
3.  **Time-to-Savings Horizon:** Insurers discount future savings. You must demonstrate that the $30k avoidance happens *within their fiscal year* or provide a model showing cash-flow impact quarter-by-quarter.
4.  **Member Retention/Churn Data:** For commercial payers, the value isn't just medical cost reduction; it's retaining a member who might otherwise leave due to frustration with care gaps. This is often an overlooked lever.

**Strategic Pivot**
Stop leading with "$600 for a kit." Lead with: *"We reduce the cost of undiagnosed rare disease episodes by 95%."*

Frame the $600 not as a cost, but as an **investment cap**. The alternative isn't a $150 test; the alternative is a $30,000 leak. Your pricing should be presented as an insurance policy against that leak. Until you provide a localized model showing the *net* impact on their specific book of business (factoring in your specific hit rate), you will remain categorized as a luxury add-on.

**Note:** Do not claim the $30k savings applies to every patient; segment your pitch to "high-probability symptomatic cohorts" to protect your PPV math.
**Note:** Prepare a one-page "Budget Impact Model" template for their actuaries to fill in; make the math easy for them to validate internally.
