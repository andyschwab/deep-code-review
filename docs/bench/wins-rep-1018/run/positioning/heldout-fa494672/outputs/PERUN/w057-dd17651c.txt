Surgeons do not fear reduced billable hours as much as they fear missing an infection due to poor image quality or unsecured data. "Accurate home monitoring" is vague; it does not explain *why* your accuracy surpasses a smartphone photo, nor does it address the surgeon's legal exposure when using WhatsApp. Furthermore, "replacing visits" sounds like revenue theft to a skeptical surgeon, whereas "triaging visits" sounds like efficiency.

**The Buyer**
The primary buyer is not the patient; it is the **Surgical Center Administrator** or **Head of Department**. They hold the budget for post-op protocols and bear the liability for readmissions. The patient is the user, but the surgeon is the economic buyer who must endorse the workflow. If the surgeon feels threatened, the administrator cannot buy. You must position this as a tool that protects the surgeon's license and optimizes their schedule, not one that eliminates their income.

**Differentiation vs. Status Quo (Without Revealing IP)**
You differ from informal photo sharing not by claiming a "better algorithm" (which invites skepticism), but by guaranteeing **input integrity** and **workflow compliance**.
1.  **Standardized Capture:** WhatsApp photos vary wildly in lighting and angle, leading to diagnostic error. Your device enforces medical-grade lighting and fixed distance, ensuring the image itself is diagnostically usable before any analysis occurs.
2.  **Compliance by Design:** Informal apps violate GDPR/HIPAA. Your system is encrypted and audit-ready out of the box, removing the legal risk for the hospital.
3.  **Actionable Triage:** A photo on WhatsApp is just data; it requires the surgeon's cognitive load to interpret. Your system outputs a risk status, allowing the care team to ignore low-risk cases confidently and focus only on potential cellulitis.
