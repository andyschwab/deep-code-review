**Recommendation: Position as the Operational Efficiency Engine.**

While "clinical safety" is your emotional hook, "operational efficiency" is the only angle that directly solves the buyer's stated barrier: the inability to measure ROI. Hospital administrators cannot buy a "safety net" if they cannot quantify the savings against a free alternative (phone calls). You must reframe the narrative so that the *clinical* outcome (fewer ER visits) becomes the *financial* proof point for operational efficiency.

**Committed Positioning Statement**
For **hospital administrators** who **struggle to justify telehealth spend against zero-cost phone protocols**, OncoTrack Slovenia is an **operational efficiency engine** that **converts scattered clinical wins into measurable departmental savings**; unlike **reactive weekly phone check-ins** that miss acute episodes, it **captures 35% fewer ER visits as hard currency to offset nursing labor costs**.

**Message Hierarchy Stress Test**
Your current draft fragments the value. Here is the corrected hierarchy to replace the status quo:

1.  **Primary Headline (The Hook):** "Turn Invisible Savings into Measurable ROI."
    *   *Why:* This attacks the specific objection (measurement gaps) immediately. It promises to solve the administrator's problem, not just the patient's.
2.  **Supporting Pillar 1 (The Proof):** "Capture the 35% ER Reduction Phone Calls Miss."
    *   *Replacement Logic:* Explicitly names the alternative (phone calls) and quantifies their failure rate (missing 60% of acute episodes leads to the 35% ER stat). This moves the argument from "safety" (abstract) to "avoided cost" (concrete).
3.  **Supporting Pillar 2 (The Mechanism):** "Free Nurse Time by Automating Acute Detection."
    *   *Replacement Logic:* Reframes "saving nursing hours" from a generic benefit to a direct result of replacing manual, reactive phone checks with automated monitoring.
4.  **Supporting Pillar 3 (The Validation):** "Backed by 90% Patient Adoption."
    *   *Replacement Logic:* Uses satisfaction not as a "nice to have," but as proof of implementation risk reduction. If patients don't use it, the ROI is zero. High adoption guarantees the efficiency gains are real.

**Why This Replaces the Status Quo**
The status quo (weekly phone calls) sells itself on "zero extra cost." To win, you must prove that "free" is actually expensive because it leaks revenue through unprevented ER visits.
*   **Against "Guarantee patient safety":** Safety is expected; it doesn't unlock budget. Efficiency unlocks budget.
*   **Against "Save nursing hours":** Alone, this feels like a minor tweak. Paired with ER reduction, it becomes a systemic fix.
*   **Against "Reduce ER chaos":** "Chaos" is emotional; "ER visits" are line-item costs.

**The Riskiest Assumption & Test**
*Assumption:* Administrators can attribute specific ER avoidance savings to your platform across scattered departments.
*Test:* Run a pilot with one department head where you manually aggregate the ER cost avoidance data for them. If they cannot approve the pilot even with the data pre-calculated, the barrier is not measurement, but budget authority.
