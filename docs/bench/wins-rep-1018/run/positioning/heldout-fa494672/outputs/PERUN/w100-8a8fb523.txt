**Positioning Hypothesis to Validate**

**Segment:** Yard managers at Brazilian soy export cooperatives facing peak harvest volatility, currently relying on physical whiteboards and Excel due to distrust of complex digital tools.

**Positioning Statement:**
For **yard managers at soy export cooperatives** who **struggle to track container stacking volatility during peak harvest using physical whiteboards**, **SoyVision** is a **visual yard management system** that **reduces re-handling moves by 30% and increases loading speed by 15%**; unlike **generic warehouse tools (e.g., StockPro, LogiTrack) or manual whiteboards**, it **offers a zero-training digital interface designed specifically for the dusty, high-pressure reality of seasonal soy exports**.

**Core Message (The "Call"):**
"The digital whiteboard that thinks."

**Why this works:**
This framing wins because it respects the user's current workflow (the whiteboard) rather than demanding a behavioral overhaul. It directly addresses the skepticism of "high-tech optimization engines" by promising familiarity ("digital whiteboard") while subtly introducing the value add ("that thinks"—implying the 30% efficiency gain without requiring complex training).

**Addressing Alternatives:**
*   **Vs. Physical Whiteboards:** The buyer gives up the comfort of a tangible, simple tool. *Winning Line:* "Keep the simplicity of the whiteboard, but let the system do the counting and optimizing so you don't have to."
*   **Vs. Generic Warehouse Tools (StockPro/LogiTrack):** The buyer gives up broad features they don't need. *Winning Line:* "Generic tools force you to adapt to their logic; SoyVision adapts to the specific chaos of soy harvest season."

**Riskiest Assumption & Test:**
*Assumption:* Managers believe the tool truly requires "zero training" and fits the "dusty yard" context.
*Test:* Conduct a 15-minute live demo on a tablet in an actual yard environment (not a conference room) with a skeptical manager. If they cannot navigate the core stacking view without instruction, the "zero training" claim fails.

*   30% reduction in container re-handling moves.
