### Comparative Analysis: AndesSkin Positioning Strategies

**Option A: Public Health Solution (Ministry of Health)**
*   **Primary Stakeholder:** Ministry of Health (MINSA) leadership and regional health directors responsible for rural equity mandates.
*   **Primary Economic Argument:** **Cost avoidance and equity compliance.** The argument centers on eliminating the logistical cost of patient travel (six-hour journeys) and preventing late-stage cancer treatment costs by catching lesions early (evidenced by your 35 missed cases now identified). The value is measured in public health outcomes and adherence to constitutional mandates for universal access, not direct revenue per transaction.
*   **Biggest Risk:** **Procurement inertia and budget cyclicality.** Selling to the state often involves multi-year tender processes, shifting political priorities, and payment delays that can starve an eight-person startup of cash flow before a contract is fully executed. Your pilot data proves clinical value, but it does not prove the government's ability to pay quickly or navigate its own bureaucracy to adopt you.

**Option B: Urban Efficiency Tool (Private Clinics)**
*   **Primary Stakeholder:** Owners and Medical Directors of private urban clinics facing overflow and waiting list attrition.
*   **Primary Economic Argument:** **Revenue capture and throughput efficiency.** The argument is that every patient turned away due to wait times is lost revenue. By reducing diagnosis time from three weeks to 48 hours, clinics can increase patient volume, reduce administrative burden on specialists, and monetize overflow they currently cannot serve. The value is immediate, measurable cash flow improvement.
*   **Biggest Risk:** **Market saturation and feature commoditization.** Private clinics may view your solution as a "nice-to-have" rather than a critical necessity if their current waitlists aren't causing significant financial bleed. Additionally, they may attempt to replicate basic triage using existing low-cost tools (like structured WhatsApp workflows) rather than paying for a dedicated platform, forcing you into a price war.

**Strategic Recommendation**
Given your constraint of an eight-person team and the need for immediate viability, **position as the Urban Efficiency Tool (Option B)** first.

While the rural public health mission is your moral core, the private urban market offers a faster feedback loop and a clearer path to revenue survival. Your pilot data regarding the reduction from three weeks to 48 hours is a direct economic lever for private clinics losing patients to wait times. Use the revenue and refined product from the urban private sector to subsidize and de-risk the eventual expansion into the public sector. Attempting to sell to the Ministry of Health as your primary buyer now exposes you to existential cash-flow risk before you have achieved product-market fit in a paying segment.

**Note:** This recommendation assumes your current pilot data in Lima is sufficient to demonstrate the 48-hour turnaround to private buyers.
**Note:** Do not abandon the rural mission; treat the private sector as the engine that funds the public impact.
