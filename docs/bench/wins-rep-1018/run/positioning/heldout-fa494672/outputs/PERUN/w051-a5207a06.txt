**Recommendation: Target School Districts First**

Commit to positioning FitKids Slovakia as a **curriculum supplement for teachers** rather than a direct-to-parent family tool. While parents control wallets, they currently view the product through the lens of "screen time anxiety," creating high friction for direct sales. Schools, conversely, already love the group challenges and solve your distribution problem by providing the devices (tablets) and the captive audience. The 50% higher engagement in schools validates that the product works best in a social, guided environment. The budget constraint is a sales hurdle to overcome, not a signal to pivot to a segment (parents) that is currently skeptical of the core value proposition. Sell to schools to prove the outcome; use that traction to later ease parent concerns for home expansion.

**Positioning Statement (Hypothesis to Validate)**

*For* **primary school teachers in Slovakia** *who* **struggle to keep students physically active and engaged during limited physical education hours**, *FitKids Slovakia* **is a** **game-based curriculum supplement** *that* **transforms sedentary screen time into high-energy group movement challenges**. *Unlike* **standard physical education classes that only reach children for 45 minutes a week or generic fitness apps that bore children under 12**, *it* **leverages existing school tablets to drive 50% higher engagement through social competition without increasing harmful screen dependency, because it replaces passive viewing with active, teacher-led gameplay.**

**Why This Wins Against Alternatives**
*   **Vs. Standard PE Classes:** Teachers give up the limitation of time and space. You win by extending activity into any classroom moment using devices they already possess, turning "screen time" into "active time."
*   **Vs. Generic Fitness Apps:** Parents and teachers give up the risk of children quitting due to boredom. You win by embedding social competition and group challenges that generic apps lack, ensuring sustained participation.

**Riskiest Assumption & Test**
*   **Assumption:** School administrators will allocate budget for software despite tight finances if it demonstrably boosts physical activity metrics.
*   **Test:** Run a pilot with three schools offering a free 3-month trial in exchange for a case study on activity minutes gained. Attempt to convert one pilot school to a paid contract before scaling sales efforts. If none convert despite high usage, the budget barrier is insurmountable, and you must revisit the parent channel with a "school-approved" endorsement strategy.

**Note:** This statement is a hypothesis based on your provided metrics; validate the "budget willingness" assumption before hiring sales staff. Do not claim "screen time reduction" as a proven fact until you have longitudinal data from the pilot.
