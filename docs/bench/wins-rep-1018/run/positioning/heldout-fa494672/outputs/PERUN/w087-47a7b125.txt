Your draft claim that you "replace the need for human analysts entirely" is likely too aggressive and counterproductive. It directly attacks the buyer's existing trust in human expertise rather than addressing the specific gap your data fills. When a buyer loses a deal because they cannot distinguish algorithmic predictions from "gut feelings," the solution is not to dismiss the feeling but to upgrade the data feeding it.

*For US importers who need to reroute shipments in real-time, PortSight is a congestion prediction API that provides 15-minute updates; unlike legacy consulting reports that rely on historical quarterly data, it empowers logistics teams to validate human intuition with live evidence, preventing costly delays before they happen.*

*   **Pillar 3 (The Outcome):** Move from reactive explanation to proactive decision-making.
    *   *Proof:* 8 logistics companies currently rely on this data for critical routing decisions.

**Strategic Analysis: Replacement vs. Supplement**
Positioning as a "supplement" is safer but may undervalue your speed. Positioning as a "replacement" triggers defensiveness regarding human expertise. The winning frame is **"Augmentation."**

The doubt you face regarding algorithms versus human expertise stems from the fear that automation removes accountability. Veteran consultants sell "judgment," not just data. If you claim to replace them, you force the buyer to choose between their trusted advisor and your black box. Instead, frame PortSight as the tool that makes the human analyst *more* accurate. The narrative should be: "Your team's experience is vital, but it is blind without live data. We provide the eyes; your team provides the judgment."

This approach answers the retailer's confusion. The difference between your algorithm and a consultant's gut feeling is not that one is "better" at thinking, but that one is operating on data from 90 days ago while the other operates on data from 15 minutes ago. You are not selling a replacement for the brain; you are selling a replacement for the outdated information the brain was previously forced to use.
