**Recommendation: Target "Anxious First-Time Parents in Tokyo" via Direct-to-Parent Social Media.**

Do not wait for a scaled pediatrician referral program; your 12-month runway requires immediate user acquisition that only direct marketing can provide. While doctor endorsements build long-term trust, relying on them as your *primary* growth channel now is a bottleneck you cannot afford. Instead, use your hard data to build trust directly with parents who are already actively searching for solutions.

**The Segment:**
First-time parents in Tokyo (ages 28–35) managing a newly diagnosed food allergy for their infant (6–18 months). Their trigger event is the first severe reaction or a confusing ER visit that left them feeling unprepared. They are digitally native, heavily active on LINE and Instagram, and currently overwhelmed by conflicting advice.

**The Behavior We Replace:**
We are replacing the "panic-driven paper notebook" and fragmented photo gallery. Currently, these parents scribble symptoms on whatever paper is handy during a reaction and try to correlate foods later from memory. This manual routine is error-prone during high-stress moments and fails to provide the clear patterns needed to prevent future ER visits.

**Differentiation vs. Generic Alternatives:**
Unlike generic health apps like WellBaby, which treat allergy logging as just one minor feature among hundreds, AllergyTrack JP is a specialized emergency prevention tool.
*   **Generic Apps:** Offer broad tracking but lack specific pediatric allergy logic. They do not highlight the correlation between specific ingredients and delayed reactions effectively.
*   **AllergyTrack JP:** Provides a dedicated, streamlined interface for rapid symptom entry during a crisis and automatically generates the specific reports doctors need.
*   **The Proof Point:** Families using our symptom logger visit the ER 40% less often than those using paper diaries. This is not a vague promise of "better health"; it is a measurable reduction in trauma and medical costs.

**Why This Wins Over the Doctor-Referral Strategy Now:**
Your doubt about trust without a doctor's endorsement is valid, but your data solves this. You do not need Dr. Tanaka's personal introduction to prove value; you need to broadcast the "40% fewer ER visits" statistic directly to anxious parents. They trust outcomes that protect their children. By targeting parents directly, you capture demand *now* while simultaneously building the user volume that will make your app attractive to pediatricians later. A doctor is more likely to recommend an app that 500 of their patients are already using than one they have to discover themselves.

**Positioning Statement (Hypothesis to Validate):**
*For first-time Tokyo parents managing a new infant food allergy who fear another ER visit, AllergyTrack JP is a specialized pediatric allergy monitor that reduces emergency room visits by 40% compared to paper diaries; unlike generic health apps like WellBaby, it provides crisis-optimized logging and automated doctor-ready reports specifically designed for Japanese food allergens.*

**Riskiest Assumption & Test:**
*Assumption:* Parents will trust a standalone app's data over a doctor's verbal recommendation enough to change their behavior immediately.
*Test:* Run a targeted Instagram/LINE ad campaign focusing solely on the "40% fewer ER visits" claim with a link to a landing page. Measure the click-through rate and sign-up conversion against a control ad featuring a "Recommended by Pediatricians" badge (even if the program isn't fully scaled yet). If the data-driven ad converts higher, your hypothesis that outcomes drive trust is confirmed.

**Note:** This strategy assumes your current 3,200 users can provide testimonials to support the social proof needed for ads.
**Note:** Ensure all marketing claims regarding the 40% reduction are backed by your internal data logs to avoid regulatory issues in Japan's health advertising sector.
