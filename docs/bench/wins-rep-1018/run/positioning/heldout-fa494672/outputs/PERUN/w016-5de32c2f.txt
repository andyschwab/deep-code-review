The argument collapses because it fights a price war against a zero-cost alternative without proving immediate, tangible cash flow impact.

**Sharper Angle: The "Productivity Arbitrage" Hypothesis**

Stop selling "healthcare cost reduction" and start selling "revenue protection via productivity." Your differentiator is not that you are cheaper than the gym; it is that the gym is failing to move the needle on metabolic health (20% utilization for actual improvement), while you deliver a proven 0.8% HbA1c reduction.

**Positioning Statement (Hypothesis to Validate):**
"For Singaporean CFOs who [need to justify wellness spend amidst budget tightening], PreventCorp is a [productivity recovery engine] that [converts low-utilization gym subsidies into measurable workforce output]; unlike [government-subsidized gyms or generic trackers like HealthHub], we [guarantee metabolic engagement that reduces sick days and restores energy within six months, proven by a 0.8% average HbA1c drop]."

**Why this wins against the alternatives:**
*   **Vs. Free Gyms:** You are not competing with their price; you are exposing their inefficiency. The argument shifts from "Why pay for PreventCorp?" to "Why accept 20% utilization on the free asset you already have?" You frame the free gym as a wasted opportunity cost, not a competitor.
