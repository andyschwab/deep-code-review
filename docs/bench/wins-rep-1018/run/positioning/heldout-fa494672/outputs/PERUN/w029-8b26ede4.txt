**Positioning Hypothesis: The "Clinical Co-Pilot" Strategy**

**Decision:** Do not market directly to patients yet. Your initial buyer for validation is **neurologists and epilepsy clinic directors** (specifically at centers like Helsinki University Hospital), positioning the device as a **clinical decision-support tool** rather than a consumer independence gadget.

**Reasoning:** Direct-to-patient marketing fails your current metrics. A user seeking "independence" views three false alarms a week as a broken promise, leading to churn and reputational damage. However, a neurologist views those same alerts as **high-value data points** within a controlled monitoring period. Clinicians are trained to interpret signal noise; patients are not. By shifting the buyer to the clinic, you transform your weakness (false positives) into a manageable clinical variable, while your strength (15-minute prediction) becomes a powerful tool for medication adjustment and safety planning.

**Positioning Statement (Hypothesis to Validate)**
*For* **epileptologists and clinic directors managing drug-resistant cases** *who* **struggle to correlate seizure triggers with medication efficacy due to lack of pre-ictal data**, *SeizureGuard* **is a** **clinical-grade predictive monitoring system** *that* **provides a 15-minute pre-seizure warning window to enable proactive intervention and precise therapy tuning**. *Unlike* **current post-event detection wearables (e.g., Empatica) or manual patient diaries**, *it* **identifies patterns before onset with 82% accuracy, allowing clinicians to distinguish true pre-ictal states from noise through professional oversight rather than relying on patient self-reporting.**

**Core Message House**
*   **Headline:** Turn Uncertainty into Actionable Clinical Data.
*   **Pillar 1: The 15-Minute Advantage.**
    *   *Claim:* Provides a critical window for intervention that post-event detectors miss.

*   **Pillar 2: Professional-Grade Interpretation.**
    *   *Claim:* Designed for clinical oversight, not unsupervised consumer use.
    *   *Proof:* Protocol where clinicians review alert logs to filter false positives, turning the "3 false alarms/week" into a calibration metric rather than a user failure.
*   **Pillar 3: Therapy Optimization.**

**Addressing the False Alarms**
Do not hide the 3 false alarms/week. Frame them explicitly in your pitch to clinicians: *"Our system casts a wide net to ensure no true pre-ictal state is missed. In a home setting, this requires user education; in a clinical setting, your team validates the signals, rapidly refining the algorithm for long-term deployment."* This honesty builds trust with medical buyers who expect trade-offs in sensitivity vs. specificity.

**Next Validation Step**
Pitch a 3-month pilot to Helsinki University Hospital where the "customer" is the hospital department, not the patient. The success metric is not patient retention, but **clinician adoption of the data in treatment plans**. If neurologists find the 15-minute window useful despite the noise, you have product-market fit for the clinical channel. Only after the algorithm improves (via this clinical data) should you expand to direct-to-patient sales.
