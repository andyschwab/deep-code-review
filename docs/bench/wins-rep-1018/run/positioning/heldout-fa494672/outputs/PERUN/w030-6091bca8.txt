**Recommendation: Pivot to selling directly to insurance providers.**

Do not attempt to "educate doctors to accept the risk." In the Chilean medical liability context, fear of negligence suits is a structural barrier, not an information gap. No amount of training will convince an OB-GYN to adopt a tool that increases their perceived legal exposure when a safer, albeit less effective, alternative (paper forms) exists. Trying to force this adoption will stall your sales cycle indefinitely, regardless of interest from Clínica Alemana or Universidad Católica.

**The Winning Positioning Hypothesis**
*For [Chilean health insurance providers] who [need to reduce long-term maternal health costs and liability exposure], MamaMind is a [risk-management platform] that [identifies 90% of high-risk postpartum cases early]; unlike [current paper-based screening which misses 40% of cases], it [shifts liability from individual doctors to the insurer's proactive care protocol, creating a documented standard of care].*

**Why this wins against the alternative:**
1.  **Liability Reframe:** When selling to doctors, your tool is a "liability trigger." When selling to insurers, your tool is a "liability shield." Insurers can mandate the use of MamaMind as part of their covered standard of care. If a doctor uses the mandated tool and a tragedy occurs, the liability shifts toward the system's protocol rather than individual negligence for "ignoring" a risk. You are not asking doctors to take a risk; you are giving insurers a way to enforce safety.
2.  **Economic Alignment:** Insurers bear the long-term cost of untreated postpartum depression (chronic care, infant complications). They have a direct financial incentive to improve the 40% miss rate of paper forms. Doctors only see the immediate risk of a lawsuit.
3.  **Speed to Scale:** A contract with one major insurer can mandate adoption across hundreds of clinics instantly, bypassing the need to convince individual legal teams at each hospital chain.

**Headline for your pitch:** "Turn Postpartum Screening from a Legal Risk into a Mandated Standard of Care."

**Riskiest Assumption & Test:**
*Assumption:* Chilean insurers are willing to assume the centralized liability risk in exchange for lower long-term payouts and are legally permitted to mandate specific diagnostic tools to providers.
*Test:* Do not build new features. Secure a Letter of Intent from one legal counsel representing a major insurer (e.g., Banmédica or Colmena) stating that mandating MamaMind would legally protect their affiliated doctors more than the current paper status quo. If they cannot articulate this legal shield, the pivot fails.

**Note:** This strategy relies on the hypothesis that insurers can legally mandate tools; verify this specific regulatory nuance with a Chilean health law expert before pitching. Do not claim the 90% sensitivity or 40% miss rate as absolute facts in legal contracts without third-party clinical validation.
