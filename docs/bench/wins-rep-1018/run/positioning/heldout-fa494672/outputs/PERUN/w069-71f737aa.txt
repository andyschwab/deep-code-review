**Core Message & Headline**
*Headline:* "Cut Referral Delays by 14 Days: The Digital Tracker for Trinidad's Pediatricians."
*Supporting Proof:* Currently used by 80 pediatricians to monitor 300 children, detecting risks 2 weeks earlier than paper-based methods.

**Why This Focus Wins**
Your draft positioning fails because it attempts to solve two distinct jobs with one message: **clinical diagnosis** (a high-stakes, evidence-based job for doctors) and **parental reassurance** (an emotional, comfort-based job for families). When compared to your alternatives, this duality dilutes your value:
1.  **Vs. Paper Checklists:** Doctors choose paper because it is simple and familiar. If you add "parental reassurance" features, you increase complexity without solving the doctor's primary pain point: missing the early intervention window. Your 2-week early detection metric is a clinical efficiency gain, not a parenting comfort feature.
2.  **Vs. Consumer Apps (BabyGrowth):** Parents choose these for user-friendliness. You cannot win on "friendliness" against dedicated consumer apps while trying to maintain clinical validation.

By committing to the **pediatrician** as the primary buyer, you directly attack the **manual paper checklist**. The specific thing the buyer gives up by staying with paper is **time**—specifically, the 14-day delay that often determines whether a child receives critical therapy during their most plastic developmental window. The "reassurance" for parents becomes a downstream outcome of the doctor's efficient referral, not the product's primary promise.

**Riskiest Assumption & Test**
*Assumption:* Pediatricians value the 2-week time gain enough to change their workflow from paper to digital, despite potential resistance to new technology.
*Test:* Present the revised headline to 10 non-user pediatricians in government clinics. Ask: "Would switching to this system save you enough time or prevent enough missed diagnoses to justify learning a new tool?" If fewer than 4 say "yes" immediately, the perceived value of the 2-week gain is insufficient to overcome workflow inertia.
