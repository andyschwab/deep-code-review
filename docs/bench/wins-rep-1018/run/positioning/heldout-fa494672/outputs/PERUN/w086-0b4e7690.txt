**Decision:** Target **mid-sized firms worried about product loss** first. Large export houses often have entrenched compliance workflows and slower procurement cycles; mid-sized firms feel the direct financial pain of spoiled goods immediately and can authorize a pivot faster to stop bleeding revenue.

**Core Positioning Hypothesis**
*For mid-sized pharmaceutical exporters who lose revenue to heat spoilage, CoolTrack is a revenue protection system that prevents cargo loss through real-time intervention; unlike manual logs or basic data loggers (FreezeCheck, LogItEasy) that only record failure after it happens, CoolTrack alerts you while the shipment is still salvageable.*

**Addressing the Skeptical Procurement Manager**
*To the manager seeing only the $200 vs. $20 price tag:*
"You are comparing the cost of a thermometer to the cost of a fire alarm. The $20 manual log is cheap, but it only confirms the fire happened after your warehouse burned down. The $200 CoolTrack unit is the alarm that wakes you up while the fire is small enough to put out. If one saved shipment covers the cost of 50 sensors, the 'expensive' option is actually the only fiscally responsible choice."

**Riskiest Assumption & Test**
*Assumption:* Mid-sized exporters have the operational flexibility to act on real-time alerts (e.g., they can actually reroute or fix a cooler mid-transit).
*Test:* Interview 5 current clients who received an alert. Ask: "What specific action did you take because of the SMS, and what was the monetary value of the goods saved by that action?" If they received alerts but took no action, the value proposition shifts from "prevention" to "liability evidence," requiring a message pivot.
