**Recommendation: Target Anxious Parents Seeking Home Support**

Based on the facts provided, SpeechPlay should immediately focus its messaging and roadmap on **parents** rather than therapists. While therapists hold clinical authority, parents control the daily volume of practice, which is your product's primary differentiator.

**Comparative Analysis**

*   **Targeting Therapists (Homework Tool):**
    *   *Pros:* Provides clinical validation; could unlock bulk referrals if adopted as a standard prescription.
    *   *Cons:* You face an active objection regarding "distraction from correct technique." Overcoming this requires clinical studies you likely do not have yet. Furthermore, therapists are gatekeepers with limited bandwidth; convincing them to change their workflow is a slow sales cycle. If they believe gamification compromises technique, they will block adoption regardless of engagement metrics.
*   **Targeting Parents (Home Support):**
    *   *Pros:* Parents are directly motivated by the pain points you solve: the high cost of private sessions (>1,000 SEK/hour) and scheduling difficulties. Your data proves you solve the "boredom" problem of flashcards, delivering **4x more practice time**. For a parent, more practice directly correlates to better outcomes for their child. The decision maker (parent) and the beneficiary (child) are aligned in the home environment.
    *   *Cons:* Parents may lack clinical confidence to judge "technique," potentially requiring in-app safeguards or simple progress reports to reassure them.

**Justification for Primary Audience**

Parents are the superior entry point for scaling because your core value proposition—**increased practice frequency through engagement**—is a direct response to their specific constraints (cost and time) and the failures of current alternatives (boring flashcards).

Therapists currently view your differentiator (gamification) as a risk. Parents view it as the solution. By targeting parents, you bypass the clinical objection hurdle initially and let the **result** (4x practice volume) build the case for efficacy. As your user base grows from 200 to a larger cohort, aggregated data showing improved articulation outcomes will eventually serve as the evidence needed to win over skeptical therapists later. Trying to convince therapists first stalls your growth on an objection you cannot yet refute with clinical trials, whereas parents can be convinced immediately by the logic of "more practice = better progress" at a fraction of the cost of private sessions.

**Positioning Statement (Hypothesis to Validate)**
*For anxious parents of child stroke survivors in Sweden who struggle to maintain consistent therapy at home due to cost and scheduling, SpeechPlay is a gamified speech therapy app that turns daily exercises into engaging play; unlike boring flashcard kits or expensive private sessions, it delivers 4x more practice time per week, accelerating progress without the 1,000 SEK/hour price tag.*

**Next Step:** Validate this by A/B testing landing pages or ads targeting parents with the "4x practice" claim versus those targeting therapists with "clinical homework support," measuring conversion rates among your existing 200 active families' referral sources.

*Note: This recommendation assumes the "4x longer practice" metric correlates positively with clinical improvement; if excessive practice reinforces bad technique, the therapist objection becomes critical.*
*Ensure any claim regarding "improved articulation" is backed by your internal data before public marketing to avoid regulatory scrutiny in the health sector.*
