### Comparative Viability Analysis: Kiosks vs. Tablet-Based Check-In

**Executive Summary**
Based on the time-motion study and operational constraints at CityCare Clinics, the decision hinges on balancing immediate patient flow efficiency against long-term maintenance liability. While kiosks offer a proven 4-minute reduction in wait times, the assumption regarding older patient preference for physical buttons introduces a usability risk that tablets mitigate through staff assistance. The critical unknown remains the 3-year Total Cost of Ownership (TCO), specifically driven by hardware durability and software deployment across twelve sites.

**Option A: Standalone Kiosks**
*   **Patient Flow Efficiency:** High. Data confirms a 4-minute average wait time reduction. This creates immediate capacity gains across all twelve locations.
*   **Usability & Demographics:** Relies on the unvalidated assumption that older patients prefer physical buttons. If this assumption fails, adoption rates may drop, negating flow benefits. Privacy is inherent to the design.
*   **Maintenance & TCO Risks:**
    *   **Hardware:** Dedicated units are prone to specific wear points (screen breakage, card reader jams). Repairing twelve distinct physical units often requires specialized vendor visits, increasing downtime and cost.
    *   **Software:** Updates must be pushed to twelve isolated devices. If connectivity varies by site, update consistency becomes a management burden.
*   **Viability Verdict:** High operational reward if the demographic assumption holds true; high financial risk if breakage rates exceed industry averages.

**Option B: Tablet-Based App with Staff Assistance**
*   **Patient Flow Efficiency:** Moderate. Wait times may not decrease as sharply as with kiosks due to the reliance on staff availability. However, staff assistance can prevent errors that cause downstream delays.
*   **Usability & Demographics:** Low risk. Staff can adapt the interaction for older patients, rendering the "physical button" preference irrelevant. This ensures higher successful check-in rates regardless of tech literacy.
*   **Maintenance & TCO Risks:**
    *   **Hardware:** Tablets are portable and susceptible to drops/theft, but replacement is generally faster and cheaper than repairing fixed kiosk mechanisms. Shared usage means fewer total devices are needed than one-per-station kiosks.
    *   **Software:** Centralized Mobile Device Management (MDM) allows simultaneous updates across all sites, reducing IT overhead.
*   **Viability Verdict:** Lower efficiency ceiling but significantly reduced execution risk and potentially lower long-term support costs due to centralized management.

**Critical Unknown: The Maintenance Cost Variable**
The decision cannot be finalized without quantifying the 3-year maintenance delta.
*   **Kiosk Risk Profile:** High fixed cost per incident. A broken screen often renders the unit useless until a technician arrives.
*   **Tablet Risk Profile:** High frequency risk (drops) but low resolution cost (swap and go).
*   **Recommendation for Finance Director:** Before December 31, request vendor-specific Service Level Agreement (SLA) quotes for both scenarios. Specifically, model the cost of:
    1.  15% annual screen breakage rate for kiosks vs. 25% for tablets.
    2.  On-site technician visits for kiosks vs. overnight shipping replacements for tablets.

**Final Recommendation**
If the primary goal is maximizing patient throughput and the budget allows for a contingency fund for repairs, **Kiosks** are the superior choice, provided a pilot validates the older demographic's comfort level. If budget certainty and risk mitigation are paramount, **Tablets** offer a more flexible, manageable TCO profile, sacrificing some speed for operational resilience.

Given the fiscal year deadline, proceed with **Tablets** if the maintenance cost data cannot be secured within two weeks, as the risk of unforeseen kiosk repair bills poses a greater threat to the 3-year budget than the known variable of staff-assisted check-in times.

*Note: Validate the "older patient preference" assumption with a 2-day pilot before full procurement.*
*Ensure software licensing models for tablets account for concurrent vs. per-device pricing.*
