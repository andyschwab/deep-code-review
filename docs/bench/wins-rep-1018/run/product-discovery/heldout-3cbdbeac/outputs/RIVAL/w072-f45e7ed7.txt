# Risk Assessment Report: VoiceGuide Beta Launch Readiness

**Date:** Current
**To:** Accessibility Advisory Council, Lead Developer
**Subject:** Critical Risk Evaluation Regarding NLP Accuracy and Error Rates

### Executive Summary
While the beta test demonstrated a strong task completion rate of 85% among 30 participants, critical risks regarding Natural Language Processing (NLP) accuracy and error recovery for visually impaired users suggest the product is **not yet safe for a September launch**. The current error profile poses a significant barrier to accessibility that contradicts the core value proposition of independent navigation.

### Critical Risk Findings

**1. Unacceptable Error Recovery Burden**
The most severe finding is the rate of **3 false commands per session**. For sighted users, a false command is a minor annoyance; they can visually identify the error and correct it instantly. For visually impaired users who cannot see the screen, every false command represents a complete loss of context and orientation.
*   **Risk Level:** High
*   **Impact:** Users cannot independently verify or correct mistakes without restarting tasks or seeking external help, effectively breaking the "independent navigation" promise. An error rate requiring frequent external intervention renders the tool unusable for its primary demographic.

**2. Unvalidated Assumption: Dialect Handling**
The team assumes NLP accuracy has improved sufficiently to handle dialect variations without manual tuning. This assumption is **untested and high-risk**.
*   **Risk Level:** High
*   **Impact:** If the system fails to recognize diverse dialects, it systematically excludes specific demographic groups, creating an equity issue and potential legal liability under accessibility standards (e.g., ADA, Section 508). Relying on "improved accuracy" without empirical data from diverse dialect speakers is a strategic gamble.

**3. Medical Jargon Confusion**
Five users reported confusion when encountering complex medical jargon. In a hospital context, misunderstanding terms like "oncology," "triage," or specific department names can lead to patients navigating to incorrect sections, missing appointments, or experiencing heightened anxiety.
*   **Risk Level:** Medium-High
*   **Impact:** Reduced trust in the system. If users cannot rely on the assistant to understand or explain critical health-related terms, they will abandon the tool for safer, albeit less convenient, alternatives (e.g., calling the front desk).

### Recommendation

**Do not proceed with the September launch.**

The current state of VoiceGuide fails the "safe threshold" for accessibility. An assistive technology that introduces confusion and requires visual correction mechanisms for non-visual users increases cognitive load and frustration rather than reducing it.

### Required Actions Before Re-evaluation

1.  **Immediate Halt on Launch Planning:** Shift focus from go-to-market activities to critical remediation.
2.  **Targeted Dialect Testing:** Conduct a specific research sprint recruiting participants with diverse dialects to validate or invalidate the NLP assumption. Manual tuning may be required if off-the-shelf NLP fails.
3.  **Error Rate Reduction:** The engineering team must prioritize reducing the false command rate from 3 per session to near-zero. Implement a "confirmation step" for critical actions if immediate NLP improvements are not feasible, though this may impact speed.
4.  **Jargon Dictionary Integration:** Develop and test a specialized medical terminology layer to handle complex hospital vocabulary.
5.  **Second Beta Cycle:** Run a follow-up beta with the same metrics, specifically measuring "independent error recovery" and "dialect success rates."

### Conclusion
Launching with known confusion triggers and a high false-command rate exposes the organization to reputational damage and fails the ethical obligation to the visually impaired community. A delayed launch with a robust, inclusive product is preferable to a premature launch of a flawed tool.

***

*This assessment is based solely on the beta metrics and assumptions provided in the request.*
*Further quantitative data on specific dialect failures is required to quantify the scope of the NLP risk.*
