### Risk Evaluation: Static Translations vs. Live Multilingual System
**Context:** SafeHaven Refugee Health Initiative (Athens Camp)
**Decision Point:** Immediate build of dynamic translation system vs. reliance on static documents and volunteers.

#### 1. Value Risk (Patient Outcomes & Care Quality)
*   **Static Approach Risk:** High risk of continued intake failure. The 45% struggle rate suggests that language barriers are not the only issue; if literacy in native scripts is low, static documents are useless regardless of translation accuracy. This leads to incomplete medical histories, misdiagnosis, and delayed care.
*   **Live System Risk:** If the system relies on text input rather than voice, it may fail to address the root cause (low literacy). A poorly implemented live system could create a false sense of security, leading staff to bypass human verification of critical symptoms.

#### 2. Usability Risk (Adoption & Accessibility)
*   **Static Approach Risk:** Assumes patients can read their native language. Without data on literacy rates, static forms may exclude the most vulnerable (e.g., elderly, uneducated populations). It also places a heavy cognitive load on patients trying to match complex medical terms.
*   **Live System Risk:** High complexity for first-time users. If the interface is not intuitive or requires smartphone ownership/data plans which refugees may lack, adoption will be near zero. Real-time systems also introduce latency; if the tool is slow during a triage emergency, staff will abandon it immediately.

#### 3. Viability Risk (Operational Sustainability & Cost)
*   **Static Approach Risk:** Hidden operational costs. Relying on volunteers for the "gaps" creates a bottleneck. Volunteer availability fluctuates, leading to inconsistent care quality. If static forms fail, the cost of re-doing intakes or treating errors later far exceeds the cost of prevention.
*   **Live System Risk:** High upfront development and maintenance costs. Dynamic systems require ongoing server costs, API fees for translation engines, and technical support. If the grant funding is one-time, the program may collapse post-June 30 when recurring costs hit. There is also the risk of vendor lock-in or API rate limits during peak intake times.

#### 4. Feasibility Risk (Technical & Resource Execution)
*   **Static Approach Risk:** Low technical feasibility risk, but high logistical risk. Printing and updating dozens of document versions for twelve languages is error-prone. Version control becomes a nightmare; using an outdated form could have legal implications.
*   **Live System Risk:** Integration challenges with existing Electronic Health Records (EHR). Building a secure, HIPAA/GDPR-compliant voice interface within the timeline is aggressive. If the internet connection in the camp is unstable, a cloud-based live system will fail completely, whereas static paper forms do not.

#### 5. Ethics Risk (Equity & Safety)
*   **Static Approach Risk:** Systemic exclusion. If the 45% who struggle are disproportionately from specific linguistic or low-literacy groups, static forms perpetuate health inequities. Providing a solution known to be insufficient for a significant portion of the population raises ethical concerns regarding duty of care.
*   **Live System Risk:** Data privacy and machine translation errors. Automated translation of medical terms can be dangerously inaccurate (e.g., confusing "no pain" with "pain"). Relying on algorithms for life-critical data without human oversight poses a direct safety risk to patients.

#### 6. Go-to-Market Risk (Deployment & Timing)
*   **Static Approach Risk:** Immediate deployment is possible, but effectiveness is unproven. If the June 30 review shows no improvement in the 45% metric, the program risks losing future funding due to perceived inaction.
*   **Live System Risk:** Time-to-value is long. Building, testing, and training staff on a new system before June 30 may result in a rushed, buggy launch that damages credibility with the grant committee more than a delayed, polished version would.

#### 7. Strategy & Objectives Risk (Long-term Vision)
*   **Static Approach Risk:** Misalignment with scalability goals. Static solutions do not scale well if the camp expands or languages shift. It treats the symptom (language) but not the disease (communication accessibility).
*   **Live System Risk:** Over-engineering. Building a complex custom solution when off-the-shelf voice translation tools exist might waste resources that could be better spent on hiring dedicated interpreters or improving internet infrastructure.

#### 8. Team Risk (Capacity & Morale)
*   **Static Approach Risk:** Volunteer burnout. Asking volunteers to constantly bridge the gap for failed static forms leads to fatigue and high turnover, destabilizing the intake process.
*   **Live System Risk:** Technical debt and distraction. The core team (PM, engineers) may get bogged down building the translation feature, neglecting other critical health initiatives. If the system fails, team morale and trust in leadership will plummet.

### Recommendation for Program Directors
Do not assume static documents are sufficient given the unknown literacy rates. The highest risk is **Value/Ethics**: excluding patients due to illiteracy. Before committing to a full dynamic build, validate the literacy assumption immediately. If literacy is low, a **voice-first** approach (even via simple existing tools like WhatsApp voice notes interpreted by volunteers) is safer than static text. A custom live software build is only justified if voice interaction is proven necessary AND infrastructure supports it.
