### Comparative Analysis: Video Library vs. Interactive Checklist

**Option A: Searchable Video Library (20 High-Quality Videos)**
*   **Potential Impact:** Addresses the assumption that patients prefer visual learning. If valid, could significantly reduce cognitive load for complex procedures and lower call center volume regarding "how-to" questions.
*   **Resource Requirements:** High. Requires scriptwriting, medical review, professional filming/editing, hosting infrastructure, and ongoing maintenance for medical accuracy.
*   **Key Risks:**
    *   **Usability Risk:** Adults over 65 may struggle with video navigation, search interfaces, or bandwidth limitations, leading to frustration rather than clarity.
    *   **Viability Risk:** High production costs may not yield a proportional reduction in readmission rates if the core issue is information retention, not format.
    *   **Adoption Risk:** Patients may not know the library exists or find it difficult to access post-discharge without direct guidance.
*   **Evidence Gaps:** No data confirms the >65 demographic prefers video over text. Unknown if current confusion stems from lack of visual aid or lack of interactive reinforcement.

**Option B: Expanded Interactive Recovery Checklist**
*   **Potential Impact:** Builds on the pilot's 15% ticket reduction. Interactivity (e.g., checking off tasks, simple progress tracking) may improve adherence and retention better than passive consumption (video or PDF).
*   **Resource Requirements:** Moderate. Requires engineering time to convert static PDF logic into an interactive web/mobile module, plus content updates. Lower ongoing maintenance than video.
*   **Key Risks:**
    *   **Feasibility Risk:** Integration with existing patient portal architecture may be complex or slow.
    *   **Value Risk:** If the 15% drop was due to the novelty of receiving *any* checklist rather than the format itself, expansion may yield diminishing returns.
    *   **Engagement Risk:** Patients may ignore the tool if it feels like "homework" rather than support.
*   **Evidence Gaps:** Unclear if the pilot's success scales to different surgery types. Unknown if "interactivity" specifically drives the improvement or simply the structured nature of the list.

**Critical Unknowns for Decision Makers**
1.  **Learning Preference Data:** Do Northwind patients >65 actually retain information better via video, or do they prefer skimmable text/checklists?
2.  **Root Cause of Confusion:** Are support tickets driven by patients not *seeing* the steps (visual gap) or not *remembering/executing* them (process gap)?
3.  **Cost-Benefit Ratio:** Does the marginal gain in readmission reduction from video justify the 5x–10x higher production cost compared to software development?

**Recommended Next Steps for Validation**
*   Conduct rapid A/B testing with 50–100 patients: Compare a single high-quality video against an interactive checklist module for one common procedure.
*   Measure specific outcomes: Time spent on content, support ticket volume related to that procedure, and self-reported confidence scores.
*   Interview recent post-surgery patients to understand *why* they were confused, rather than assuming the solution format.

This analysis isolates the assumptions regarding format preference and cost-efficiency to inform the Q3 roadmap decision.

*Note: This comparison highlights evidence gaps; a small-scale experiment is required before committing full engineering resources.*
*Final decision should weigh the speed of checklist iteration against the potential high-ceiling impact of video if the demographic assumption holds true.*
