### Customer Evidence vs. Gaps Evaluation: RehabRemote Home Exercise Device

**Current Customer Evidence**
*   **Adherence Validation:** Pilot data from 50 patients confirms high engagement (90% adherence) with the current sensor-only prototype. This validates the core value proposition of consistency.
*   **Clinical Gap Identified:** Therapists reported missing subtle form errors in 20% of sessions using sensors alone. This indicates a clear usability and value limitation in the current configuration regarding injury prevention and correction quality.
*   **Unvalidated Assumption:** The hypothesis that video feedback will reduce injury rates by 15% remains theoretical. No pilot data currently links video analysis to actual injury reduction outcomes.

**Critical Evidence Gaps**
*   **Privacy Acceptance:** There is zero evidence regarding patient willingness to accept cameras in private living spaces. The assumption that clinical benefit outweighs privacy intrusion is untested.
*   **Behavioral Impact:** It is unknown if the introduction of cameras will negatively impact the current 90% adherence rate due to privacy discomfort or "surveillance fatigue."
*   **Regulatory Specifics:** While a filing deadline exists, specific regulatory precedents for home-based video monitoring in this specific therapeutic context have not been cited in the current brief.

**Risk Evaluation by Path**

**Path A: Proceed with Video Capabilities**
*   **Value Risk:** High. If patients refuse installation due to privacy concerns, the feature provides zero clinical value while increasing hardware costs.
*   **Usability Risk:** High. Introducing cameras may decrease the proven 90% adherence rate if users feel uncomfortable being recorded in their homes.
*   **Viability/Legal Risk:** Critical. Handling biometric/video data introduces significant liability, data security requirements, and potential regulatory delays that could jeopardize the November 30 filing deadline.
*   **Ethics Risk:** Potential violation of patient trust if consent processes are not robust, leading to reputational damage.

**Path B: Launch Sensor-Only MVP**
*   **Value Risk:** Moderate. The product launches with a known deficit: therapists cannot correct 20% of subtle form errors, potentially limiting clinical efficacy claims and long-term retention if injuries occur.
*   **Market Risk:** Competitors with video solutions could claim superior safety outcomes, positioning RehabRemote as a "basic" tier option.
*   **Mitigation:** This path secures the regulatory deadline and validates the business model on adherence first, deferring the privacy complexity.

**Recommendation for Advisory Board**
Do not proceed with full video integration before the November 30 deadline without immediate, targeted validation. The risk of regulatory delay and market rejection due to privacy concerns outweighs the theoretical 15% injury reduction benefit at this stage.

**Immediate Next Steps Required:**
1.  Conduct rapid "Mom Test" style interviews with 10–15 pilot patients specifically asking about past behaviors regarding home cameras and privacy boundaries (not hypothetical willingness).
2.  Run a concierge test where therapists manually review user-uploaded videos (optional, user-initiated) to validate if video actually correlates to the hypothesized injury reduction before hard-coding it into the device.

Launch the sensor-only MVP to meet the regulatory deadline, treating video as a secondary, opt-in feature pending further discovery.

*Note: This evaluation relies strictly on the pilot data and assumptions provided; no external market research was conducted.*
*Legal counsel must review specific regional data privacy laws before any video prototype is distributed.*
