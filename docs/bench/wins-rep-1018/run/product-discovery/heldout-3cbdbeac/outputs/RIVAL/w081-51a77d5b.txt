**Recommendation: Do Not Greenlight Full Development Yet**

The qualitative data from five exit interviews is **insufficient** to justify immediate full-scale development. While the pain point (stockouts due to missing alerts) appears acute, acting on this alone violates core discovery principles. You are currently conflating "what customers say they missed" with "what they will actually use and pay for," and you have not validated whether a simple threshold warning solves the root cause or if the issue lies elsewhere (e.g., ignored alerts, poor integration, or operational delays).

**Immediate Next Steps (Sprint Planning):**

Instead of building the full feature, allocate the first sprint to **risk reduction experiments** that cost less than $2,000 and take under two weeks:

1.  **Validate the Solution Type (Simple vs. Predictive):**
    *   **Action:** Contact 10–15 *current* at-risk customers (not just churned ones). Show them two low-fidelity mockups: one with basic static threshold alerts and one with predictive "running out soon" alerts.
    *   **Question:** Ask about their *past* behavior: "Tell me about the last time you ran out of stock. What data did you look at? Did you have any warnings? Why didn't they work?"
    *   **Goal:** Determine if the failure was a lack of data (needs alerts) or a lack of action (needs better workflow/integration).

2.  **Test Willingness to Adopt (Value Risk):**
    *   **Action:** Create a "fake door" test or a concierge prototype. Manually monitor inventory for 3 pilot customers and send them SMS/Email alerts when stock hits their defined threshold.
    *   **Metric:** Measure if they actually adjust orders based on your manual alert. If they ignore the manual alert, they will ignore the automated one.
    *   **Goal:** Prove that alerts drive behavior change before writing code.

3.  **Refine the Churn Assumption:**
    *   **Action:** Analyze usage logs of the churned clients. Did they configure *any* notifications previously? Did they log in frequently?
    *   **Goal:** Confirm that "missing alerts" was the primary driver of churn and not a post-hoc rationalization for a deeper issue like pricing or poor support.

**Decision Criteria for Greenlighting:**
Only proceed to the $15,000 build if:
*   Pilot customers demonstrate a measurable change in ordering behavior based on manual alerts.
*   At least 70% of interviewed current customers explicitly prefer one alert type (simple vs. predictive) and can articulate why the other would fail.
*   You confirm that the cost of building the feature is lower than the projected revenue retention of the 20% churn reduction.

**Note:** Prioritizing based solely on exit interviews risks building a feature that addresses a symptom rather than the cause. Use the sprint to validate the *mechanism* of the solution, not just the existence of the problem.
