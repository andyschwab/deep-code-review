# Strategic Decision Framework: Cash-on-Delivery vs. Driver Safety

## 1. Problem Definition
**Core Conflict:** Balancing immediate driver safety (15% turnover increase, 10 recent incidents) against potential revenue loss from customers unable to switch from cash to digital payments in high-risk Mexico City zones.

**Objective:** Determine if the risk of retaining cash-on-delivery (COD) outweighs the risk of losing customers by mandating digital payments.

## 2. Critical Assumptions to Validate
Before making a final decision, the following assumptions must be tested immediately:
*   **Assumption A (Customer Capability):** "Customers in high-risk zones cannot or will not use digital payments."
    *   *Risk:* If false, we lose revenue unnecessarily by keeping COD.
    *   *Test:* Survey 50 recent COD customers in affected zones; offer a small discount for pre-payment in a pilot group.
*   **Assumption B (Safety Impact):** "Removing cash from drivers will significantly reduce incidents and turnover."
    *   *Risk:* If criminals target drivers regardless of cash (e.g., for the device or coercion), the feature change won't solve the safety crisis.
    *   *Test:* Analyze incident reports: Was cash the primary motivator in all 10 cases?
*   **Assumption C (Financial Viability):** "The cost of turnover and incident management exceeds the revenue from non-digital customers."
    *   *Risk:* If the margin on COD orders is low but the safety cost is high, retaining them is net-negative.

## 3. Decision Matrix: Trade-off Analysis

| Scenario | Action | Pros | Cons | Recommendation Trigger |
| :--- | :--- | :--- | :--- :--- |
| **High Safety Risk / Low Digital Adoption** | **Hybrid Model** | Maintains revenue; limits cash exposure. | Complex ops; drivers still carry some risk. | If >40% of customers cannot digitize immediately. |
| **High Safety Risk / High Digital Adoption** | **Mandate Digital** | Maximizes safety; reduces turnover; lowers insurance costs. | Short-term revenue dip; customer friction. | If >60% of customers can switch within 2 weeks. |
| **Moderate Safety Risk / Low Digital Adoption** | **Optimize COD** | Keeps revenue stable; minimal customer churn. | Does not solve root cause; turnover continues. | If incidents are not primarily cash-motivated. |

## 4. Recommended Immediate Actions (Next 48 Hours)
1.  **Pilot "Cash-Lite" Zones:** Select the top 2 highest-risk neighborhoods. Mandate digital payment for new orders only. Offer a 10% discount incentive for existing customers to switch.
2.  **Driver Feedback Loop:** Interview 5 drivers who experienced incidents. Confirm if removing cash would have prevented the event.
3.  **Cost-Benefit Calculation:**
    *   *Cost of Status Quo:* (15% turnover cost × hiring/training) + (Incident legal/medical costs) + (Reputation damage).
    *   *Cost of Change:* (Estimated % revenue loss from COD customers) × (Average Order Value).
    *   *Decision Rule:* If *Cost of Status Quo* > *Cost of Change*, proceed with digital mandate.

## 5. Final Recommendation Structure for Executives
*   **The Safety Imperative:** Present the 10 incidents and 15% turnover as an existential threat to operations in these zones.
*   **The Data Gap:** Acknowledge uncertainty regarding customer payment flexibility but propose the 48-hour pilot to close it.
*   **The Proposal:** Recommend a phased elimination of COD in high-risk zones, starting with the pilot, rather than an immediate city-wide ban. This balances safety urgency with revenue protection.

***

*Note: This framework prioritizes human safety as the leading indicator for long-term viability, assuming that no revenue stream is sustainable without a secure workforce.*
