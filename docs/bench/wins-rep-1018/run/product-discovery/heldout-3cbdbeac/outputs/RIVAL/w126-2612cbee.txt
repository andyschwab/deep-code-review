**Risk Assessment & Recommendation: Ice Road Status Feature**

**To:** VP of Operations, IT Infrastructure Lead
**From:** Product Management
**Date:** October 24, 2023
**Subject:** Evaluation of Proposed Ice Road Status Dashboard

**Executive Summary**
Two recent incident reports regarding impassable ice roads do **not** provide sufficient justification to invest in building a new live dashboard feature at this time. The core problem has not been validated: we do not know if the delays were caused by a *lack of information* or by managers *ignoring existing weather bulletins*. Building a solution based on the assumption that "more real-time data equals fewer delays" carries high viability and usability risks.

**Key Risks & Assumptions**

1.  **Value Risk (High):** We assume managers need *more* data. However, if they are already receiving weather bulletins but failing to act on them due to operational pressure or workflow disconnects, a new dashboard will not solve the problem. The issue may be behavioral, not informational.
2.  **Feasibility Risk (Medium-High):** The assumption that satellite and sensor data can be transmitted with low enough latency from remote sites to be "actionable in real-time" is unproven. If latency exceeds the window for decision-making (e.g., before dispatch), the feature provides false security.
3.  **Usability Risk:** Introducing a new tool adds cognitive load. If the data is not perfectly reliable or timely, managers may lose trust in the system entirely, reverting to informal networks or guesswork.
4.  **Strategic Risk:** Allocating scarce technical resources to build this before November 5th without validating the root cause could divert engineering capacity from higher-impact initiatives.

**Root Cause Ambiguity**
The two calls indicate a symptom (delays), not the cause. Before investing in infrastructure, we must answer:
*   Did the managers check the existing bulletins before dispatching?
*   If yes, why did they proceed despite the data?
*   If no, why was the existing channel ignored or inaccessible?
*   Would a 15-minute data latency actually change their decision, given the travel times to these remote sites?

**Recommendation**
**Do not proceed with development** for the Q4 cycle. Instead, allocate immediate low-effort resources to **discovery**:

1.  **Conduct Targeted Interviews:** Speak with the two site managers who called, plus 3–5 others. Ask specifically about their decision-making process during the incidents. "Walk me through the last time you dispatched machinery. What information did you look at? Who did you talk to?"
2.  **Audit Current Usage:** Analyze logs or ask managers directly about the usage rates of current weather bulletins. Are they being opened? Read? Acted upon?
3.  **Prototype the Latency Constraint:** Before building the full dashboard, simulate the expected data latency with a manual process (e.g., SMS updates with known delays) to see if "near real-time" data actually alters dispatch decisions.

**Next Steps for November 5th Review**
By the quarterly review, we should present findings from the interviews and the latency simulation. If discovery confirms that *lack of timely data* is the sole blocker, we can then build a business case for the dashboard with validated confidence. If the issue is workflow or adherence, the solution lies in process change or training, not software development.

**Decision:** Pause build. Invest in discovery.

*Note: This assessment relies solely on the two reported incidents; broader patterns may exist but are currently unverified.*
