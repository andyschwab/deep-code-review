### Viability Analysis: JIT Delivery Coordination Feature

**Executive Summary**
The proposed feature targets a critical pain point: five assembly line stoppages last month costing approximately **22,500 EUR** (5 incidents × 4,500 EUR). If your assumption holds that 80% of these delays stem from communication gaps regarding shift changes, the potential recoverable value is roughly **18,000 EUR per month**. However, the viability of this solution hinges entirely on a single external constraint: client willingness to share real-time production data.

**1. Value & Viability Assessment**
*   **Potential Upside:** Eliminating communication-based delays could reduce stoppages by ~4 incidents monthly, saving ~18,000 EUR. Over a year, this represents **216,000 EUR** in avoided costs, not including intangible benefits like strengthened client relationships and contract security.
*   **The Critical Blocker:** Your hypothesis relies on clients sharing real-time shift schedules. In the automotive sector, production data is often highly guarded due to competitive sensitivity and cybersecurity policies. If clients refuse data sharing due to security protocols, the feature's core value proposition collapses, regardless of engineering quality.
*   **Viability Verdict:** The financial upside justifies the investment *only if* data access is granted. Without a confirmed pathway to receive client schedule data, the feature carries extreme viability risk.

**2. Risk Analysis by Category**
*   **Value Risk:** High. If the root cause is actually trucking capacity or internal warehouse bottlenecks (the remaining 20% of your assumption), better communication will not solve the stoppages. The feature might solve a non-existent problem.
*   **Usability/Adoption Risk:** Critical. Even if clients agree in principle, their logistics teams may lack the bandwidth or authorized systems to push real-time updates. If the integration requires manual entry by client staff, adoption will likely fail.
*   **Feasibility & Integration Risk:** Moderate to High. While building the portal feature is internally controllable, integrating with diverse client ERPs or security gateways varies by plant. Each of the three major plants may have different IT security standards, potentially requiring three unique integration approaches.
*   **Strategic Risk:** If competitors offer similar coordination but have already secured data-sharing agreements, Bohemia risks being locked out. Conversely, pushing too hard for data access could strain current relationships if clients perceive it as a security overreach.

**3. Recommendation for Q3 Budget Allocation**
Do not allocate the full engineering budget to building the feature yet. The risk of building a solution that clients cannot or will not use is too high.

**Proposed Path Forward:**
1.  **Validate the Data Access Assumption First:** Before writing code, conduct high-level discussions with the logistics directors at the three Prague plants. Specifically ask: "What are your security policies regarding sharing real-time shift schedules with suppliers?" and "Would you be open to a pilot data feed?"
2.  **Test the Root Cause:** Verify the "80% communication gap" assumption. Review the logs of the last five stoppages. Was there truly a missed message about a shift change, or was the message received but ignored due to lack of trucks?
3.  **Conditional Investment:**
    *   **If** clients confirm a pathway for data sharing AND logs confirm communication is the primary failure point: Proceed with full development. The ROI is clear.
    *   **If** clients deny data access: Pivot to a lower-fidelity solution (e.g., standardized manual check-in protocols via existing channels) or investigate trucking capacity issues instead.

**Conclusion**
The financial incentive is strong, but the solution is externally dependent. De-risk the initiative by validating client data policies immediately. Only commit significant engineering resources once you have a verbal or written indication that data sharing is permissible.

*Note: This analysis assumes the 4,500 EUR delay cost is accurate and consistent across all incidents. Verify this figure with your finance team before finalizing ROI projections.*
