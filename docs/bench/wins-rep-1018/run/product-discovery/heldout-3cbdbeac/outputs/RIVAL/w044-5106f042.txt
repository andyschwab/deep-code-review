# Decision Brief: Offline Mode vs. Compliance Delay

**Recommendation:** Do not greenlight the full offline mode project at this time. Proceed with a lightweight caching strategy and targeted discovery to validate the scope of the connectivity problem before committing to three months of development.

### Executive Summary
The proposal to build a robust offline mode with automatic sync carries significant risk: a three-month delay to a critical compliance update. Current evidence suggests the problem scope (total connectivity loss) is much smaller than the proposed solution assumes. The cost of delaying compliance likely outweighs the unverified benefit of a full offline architecture for the majority of the user base.

### Evidence Analysis
*   **Problem Scope:** Out of 50 data loss tickets logged last month, only 5 (10%) explicitly cited a total lack of internet. The remaining 40 incidents likely stem from spotty signal, timeouts, or UI issues, which a full offline mode may not fully address or might be over-engineered to solve.
*   **User Distribution Unknown:** We lack data on how many of the 400 active nurses operate primarily in zero-connectivity zones versus areas with intermittent signal. Building for the "zero-connectivity" edge case may not serve the 90% of users experiencing only minor disruptions.
*   **Assumption Validation:** The Product Lead's estimate of a 90% reduction in data loss assumes that *all* data loss is caused by lack of connectivity. The ticket data contradicts this, suggesting other factors are at play.
*   **Opportunity Cost:** Delaying the compliance update introduces regulatory risk and potential penalties, which is a tangible, immediate cost. The benefit of the offline mode is currently theoretical and based on incomplete data.

### Strategic Options

**Option A: Full Offline Mode (Not Recommended Now)**
*   **Effort:** 3+ months (High Technical Debt).
*   **Impact:** Potentially solves 10% of current tickets; delays compliance.
*   **Risk:** High. Builds a complex system based on unvalidated assumptions about user environments.

**Option B: Lightweight Caching + Discovery (Recommended)**
*   **Effort:** 2–3 weeks.
*   **Impact:** Mitigates issues related to spotty signal (likely the cause of the 40 other tickets) without major architectural changes. Keeps compliance update on track.
*   **Action Plan:**
    1.  Implement simple request caching/retry logic to handle transient network failures.
    2.  Launch immediate targeted research: Survey the 400 nurses to map connectivity zones and interview the 5 users who reported total outages.
    3.  Re-evaluate the need for full offline sync once we have concrete data on the frequency and duration of zero-connectivity events.

### Conclusion
The current data does not justify the massive investment and delay required for a full offline mode. The discrepancy between the perceived problem (total data loss due to no signal) and the actual evidence (only 10% of tickets cite this) indicates a need for deeper discovery. We should prioritize the compliance update, implement a low-effort caching fix for immediate relief, and gather the necessary data to make an informed decision on offline architecture in the next quarter.

*Note: This approach aligns with minimizing waste by testing assumptions before building complex features.*
