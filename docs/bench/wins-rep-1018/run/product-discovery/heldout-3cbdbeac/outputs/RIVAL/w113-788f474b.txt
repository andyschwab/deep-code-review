**Executive Summary: Do Not Proceed to Build Phase Yet**

The four rejection emails constitute valid evidence of a critical **problem** (inconsistent ripeness causing waste), but they do **not** constitute sufficient evidence to justify the **solution** (€45,000 spectral imaging system) or proceed to the build phase.

**Analysis of Current Evidence**
*   **Problem Validation (Strong):** The loss of 3 tons of produce and rejection by key partners confirms a severe Value and Viability risk. The current assumption—that color charts guarantee shelf stability—is proven false.
*   **Solution Validation (Weak):** You have not validated that spectral imaging is the *correct* fix, nor that it is the *most efficient* one. The rejection emails diagnose the symptom (bad fruit arrival) but do not prove the root cause is undetectable at packing. The issue could stem from temperature fluctuations during transit, improper packing density, or delays in logistics, none of which spectral imaging at the packing station would solve.
*   **Financial Risk:** Investing €45,000 based solely on the problem statement is a high-risk gamble. If the root cause is transit temperature rather than initial maturity, the hardware will yield zero ROI, exacerbating your financial strain during seasonal fluctuations.

**Recommended Next Steps Before Capital Allocation**
Before presenting to your investment advisory group, you must de-risk the solution assumption with low-cost discovery:

1.  **Root Cause Verification:** Conduct a "post-mortem" on the rejected shipments. Correlate the rejected fruit with specific shipping containers, routes, and temperature logs. Determine if the fruit was over-ripe *before* loading or if it ripened prematurely *during* transit.
2.  **Alternative Solution Testing:** Before buying hardware, test cheaper proxies. Can manual Brix (sugar) testing or firmness gauges on a sample basis predict the failures better than color charts? If manual testing solves the prediction gap, the €45,000 automation is unnecessary.
3.  **Customer Willingness to Pay:** Ask the rejecting supermarkets: "If we guarantee <2% variance in ripeness using new technology, would you commit to a longer contract or higher volume?" If they won't commit to improved terms, the ROI calculation fails regardless of the technology's accuracy.

**Conclusion for the Advisory Group**
Advise the group to allocate funds for a **2-week discovery sprint** (logistics audit and manual testing) rather than the hardware build. The evidence supports solving the waste problem immediately, but it does not support spectral imaging as the specific solution yet. Proceeding to build now violates the principle of validating the solution before scaling investment.
