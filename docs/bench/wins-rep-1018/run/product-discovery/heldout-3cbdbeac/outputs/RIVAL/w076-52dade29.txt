### Critical Questions for Parents and Administrators

Before finalizing the data policy or building integration connectors, MindTrack must validate assumptions regarding consent, trust, and actual workflow value. The following questions are designed to uncover past behaviors and specific concerns rather than hypothetical opinions.

#### For Parents (Focus: Consent & Trust)
*Goal: Determine if privacy fears will block adoption and what specific safeguards are required for consent.*

1.  "Walk me through the last time you had to sign a permission slip or consent form for your child's data to be shared with a third party. What was your immediate reaction, and what specific information did you look for before signing?"
2.  "Tell me about a recent news story regarding school data or privacy that concerned you. What specifically about that story made you hesitate or lose trust?"
3.  "Describe a situation where you decided *not* to share information about your child's health or behavior with a school official. What was the deciding factor?"
4.  "If a tool could automatically send your child's mood trends to their counselor, what would need to be true for you to feel comfortable enabling that today? What specific controls would you need to see?"
5.  "Have you ever revoked permission for a school app or service to access your child's data? What happened, and why did you make that choice?"

#### For School Administrators & Counselors (Focus: Value & Workflow)
*Goal: Validate if automatic integration actually increases intervention rates or creates liability noise.*

1.  "Walk me through the last time a student's mental health trend triggered an intervention. How did you find out about the trend, and what steps did you take immediately after?"
2.  "Tell me about a time when receiving too much data or too many alerts from a school system slowed down your ability to help a student. What happened?"
3.  "Describe your current process for reviewing external mental health reports (like the 10% currently shared manually). What is the most frustrating part of that manual process?"
4.  "If you had automatic access to daily mood logs for all students tomorrow, what is the first thing you would do with that data? Who else in the district would need to approve that access?"
5.  "What is the hardest part about explaining data privacy practices to parents during enrollment? Can you give me an example of a question parents asked recently that was difficult to answer?"

#### Strategic Decision Matrix
Use the answers above to populate this decision framework before July 1:

| Risk Area | Critical Validation Threshold | Go/No-Go Signal |
| :--- | :--- | :--- |
| **Parental Consent** | >70% of parents indicate they would consent *if* specific controls (e.g., opt-in per semester, view-only access) are present. | **Go:** Build connectors with granular consent features.<br>**No-Go:** Remain standalone; focus on manual report optimization. |
| **Counselor Capacity** | Counselors confirm they have time to review automated trends without reducing time spent in direct student sessions. | **Go:** Prioritize dashboard integration.<br>**No-Go:** Risk of alert fatigue; delay integration. |
| **Liability & Trust** | Administrators can articulate a clear protocol for data breaches that satisfies current parent concerns. | **Go:** Proceed with pilot.<br>**No-Go:** Pause until legal/privacy framework is strengthened. |

**Note:** Do not ask "Would you use this?" as it yields false positives. Focus entirely on the specific past instances and concrete conditions described in the questions above.
**Deadline Alert:** Synthesize findings by mid-June to allow three weeks for legal review before the July 1 policy deadline.
