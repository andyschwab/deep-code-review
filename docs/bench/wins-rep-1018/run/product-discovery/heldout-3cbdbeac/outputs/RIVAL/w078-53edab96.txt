### Evaluation: SeptiSense Deployment Readiness

**Recommendation: NO-GO for Full Active Deployment**
Current evidence indicates a critical failure in **Usability** and **Value** risks. Proceeding to active clinical decision support (CDS) with 15 false alarms per day poses an unacceptable risk of alert fatigue, which directly contradicts the goal of saving lives.

**Critical Analysis of Current Evidence**
Your assumption that clinicians will tolerate high false-positive rates in exchange for potential lives saved is unsupported by behavioral data and contradicts established human factors research in healthcare.
*   **The Alert Fatigue Threshold:** In high-acuity environments, alarm rates exceeding 10–15 per bed per day typically trigger "crying wolf" syndrome. At 15 false alarms *daily* (aggregate or per unit), staff will inevitably begin ignoring alerts. If clinicians ignore the system, the True Positive rate becomes irrelevant because the intervention never occurs.
*   **Retrospective vs. Live Discrepancy:** The drop from 92% accuracy in historical data to high false positives in live shadow mode suggests a data drift issue or that the model is picking up on noise present in live feeds that was cleaned in historical datasets. This is a **Feasibility** and **Viability** risk.
*   **Unvalidated Assumption:** You have no evidence that "5 lives saved per quarter" outweighs the operational cost of investigating 450 false alarms monthly (15 × 30). Without quantifying the labor cost and trust erosion, the value proposition is incomplete.

**Required Thresholds for Safety and Trust**
To proceed to active deployment, SeptiSense must meet the following rigorous thresholds before August 15:

1.  **False Positive Rate (FPR):** Must be reduced to **<2 false alarms per day per unit** (or <1 per 100 patient-days). This is the industry standard for actionable CDS to prevent alarm fatigue.
2.  **Positive Predictive Value (PPV):** Must exceed **40–50%**. Clinicians generally reject tools where more than half the alerts are false.
3.  **Workflow Integration:** Evidence that the alert can be acknowledged and cleared in **<30 seconds**. If investigation takes longer, the burden on staff is too high.
4.  **Trust Metric:** Qualitative data from at least 10 frontline nurses and 5 physicians stating they would "act immediately" on an alert without second-guessing the system.

**Immediate Next Steps (The 3-Month Tuning Path)**
Do not deploy actively. Instead, propose a **"Controlled Intervention Pilot"** for the next 90 days:
*   **Scope:** Limit active alerts to a single pilot unit with a dedicated "AI Champion" clinician who validates every alert before it reaches the wider team.
*   **Goal:** Use this period to retrain the model on the specific noise profiles found in City General's live data to drive down the FPR.
*   **Success Criteria:** Only move to hospital-wide active deployment once the pilot unit sustains <2 false alarms/day for two consecutive weeks while maintaining sensitivity.

**Conclusion**
Moving to full deployment now risks patient safety via alert fatigue and could permanently damage SeptiSense's reputation with hospital partners. The data supports a delay for targeted tuning, not a go/no-go launch.
