### Executive Assessment: Social Determinants of Health (SDOH) Data Feature

**Financial Upside vs. Operational Friction**
The potential to unlock **$150,000 in annual grant funding** presents a clear financial incentive aligned with Community First Health's mission. However, this upside is contingent on data quality and volume. With **65% patient willingness** to share sensitive housing and food security information, the addressable market for this data is significant but not universal. The critical bottleneck is not patient reluctance, but **operational capacity**.

**Operational Impact Analysis**
Your current intake workflow already exceeds industry standards by **8 minutes**. Introducing sensitive SDOH questions without workflow optimization poses three specific risks:
1.  **Throughput Degradation:** Adding even 2–3 minutes per patient to discuss housing/food security could extend wait times further, reducing daily patient volume and potentially offsetting the grant revenue through lost visit capacity.
2.  **Staff Reliability & Trust:** Front-desk staff are trained for administrative intake, not social work screening. Asking sensitive questions without proper training or privacy safeguards risks compromising patient trust. If patients feel judged or unsafe, the 65% willingness rate could plummet, jeopardizing the data quality required for the grant.
3.  **Data Integrity:** "Reliable collection" is the unknown variable. Inconsistent data entry due to staff discomfort or time pressure may render the data unusable for grant reporting, nullifying the $150k upside.

**Strategic Recommendation for the Board**
Do not commit to full engineering development yet. The risk of building a feature that captures low-quality data while breaking clinic throughput is too high. Instead, approve a **low-fidelity operational pilot** before writing code:

*   **Test the Workflow:** Run a 2-week manual pilot where designated staff (not necessarily front desk) attempt to collect this data using a script. Measure the actual time added per patient and the completion rate.
*   **Validate Staff Capacity:** Assess if front-desk staff can collect this data without compromising trust. If they cannot, the solution requires hiring care coordinators or changing the intake model, not just software.
*   **Confirm Grant Requirements:** Verify exactly what data granularity the grant requires. Does 65% participation meet the threshold, or is 90% needed?

**Decision Framework**
*   **Go:** If the manual pilot shows data can be collected in <2 additional minutes with >80% completion rates and no patient complaints.
*   **No-Go/Pivot:** If the pilot slows intake by >5 minutes or staff report significant discomfort. In this case, the $150k grant may cost more in lost operational efficiency than it is worth, or requires a different collection strategy (e.g., digital pre-visit surveys) before engineering builds a clinic-side tool.

**Conclusion**
The mission alignment is strong, but the operational friction is a tangible threat to both clinic efficiency and data validity. The Board should authorize a **process experiment** to de-risk the staff/patient interaction before authorizing engineering resources.

***

*Note: This assessment assumes the $150k grant is guaranteed if data is collected; verify if there are performance clauses. Ensure the manual pilot includes a feedback loop for patients to report discomfort anonymously.*
