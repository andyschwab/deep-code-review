### Decision Analysis: IoT Sensor Integration vs. Manual Protocol Improvement

**Executive Summary**
Danube GrainLogistics faces recurring financial loss (48,000 RON in two months) and reputational damage due to inventory discrepancies. The core decision balances the high potential accuracy of IoT sensors against their unproven reliability in high-dust environments, versus the low-cost but error-prone nature of current manual entry.

#### Option A: Integrate IoT Occupancy Sensors
**Benefits**
*   **Accuracy Potential:** Eliminates human error during shift handovers, theoretically addressing the assumed 90% root cause of discrepancies.
*   **Real-Time Visibility:** Provides continuous data rather than point-in-time snapshots, preventing sales contracts based on stale data.
*   **ROI Projection:** If sensors reduce errors by even 50%, the system could pay for itself within months by avoiding the current run-rate of ~24,000 RON/month in penalties.

**Risks & Overhead**
*   **Environmental Failure:** High-dust conditions in Galati silos pose a critical risk. Standard optical or ultrasonic sensors may fail or drift without frequent cleaning, creating a "false sense of security."
*   **Maintenance Burden:** Requires a new maintenance protocol (cleaning schedules, calibration) and specialized technical skills currently absent in the operations team.
*   **Capital Expenditure:** Significant upfront cost for hardware and integration across twelve silos with no guaranteed performance in this specific environment.

#### Option B: Improve Manual Protocols
**Benefits**
*   **Low Capital Cost:** No hardware investment required; changes are procedural.
*   **Immediate Implementation:** New checklists or dual-verification steps can be enacted immediately for the next shift cycle.
*   **Known Variables:** Relies on existing staff and familiar processes, avoiding technical integration risks.

**Risks & Overhead**
*   **Persistent Human Error:** Even with improved protocols, fatigue and distraction during handovers cannot be fully eliminated. The 90% error assumption suggests process tweaks may only yield marginal gains.
*   **Operational Friction:** Adding verification steps increases the time required for shift handovers, potentially causing delays in loading/unloading operations.
*   **Scalability Issues:** As trade volume increases, the likelihood of manual error typically rises, whereas automation scales linearly.

#### Critical Decision Factors
1.  **Validation of the 90% Assumption:** Before committing to either path, you must verify if human error is truly the primary culprit. If sensor hardware fails due to dust, the discrepancy rate could remain unchanged despite the investment.
2.  **Hardware Suitability:** The decision hinges entirely on whether industrial-grade, dust-proof sensors (e.g., radar-based or heavy-duty mechanical) are available and proven in similar grain logistics environments.
3.  **Cost of Inaction:** Continuing with manual processes guarantees further losses of ~12,000 RON per incident. The board must weigh this certain loss against the risk of sensor failure.

#### Recommendation
Do not proceed with a full rollout to all twelve silos immediately. The risk of hardware failure in high-dust environments is too high to validate based solely on the assumption that human error is the cause.

**Proposed Path Forward:**
1.  **Pilot Program:** Install sensors in **one** high-volume silo for 30 days.
2.  **Parallel Tracking:** Run the improved manual protocol alongside the sensor data in the pilot silo to compare accuracy directly.
3.  **Go/No-Go Criteria:** If the sensor maintains >98% accuracy without excessive maintenance during the pilot, approve capital expenditure for the remaining eleven silos. If dust interferes, revert to enhanced manual protocols with dual-signoff requirements.

This approach minimizes capital risk while generating the empirical data needed to confirm the root cause of the inventory discrepancies.

*Note: Ensure the pilot includes a strict maintenance log to quantify the actual labor overhead of keeping sensors clean in Galati's specific dust conditions.*
