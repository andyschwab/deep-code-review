### Feasibility Assessment: Automated Crew Scheduling Tool

**Executive Summary**
Building a fully automated scheduling tool with hard-enforced rest periods presents critical **Viability** and **Feasibility** risks under current conditions. While the technical construction is achievable, the operational and legal constraints suggest that a "hard enforcement" model may violate existing union agreements and threaten fleet utilization. A hybrid approach (decision support with human override) is recommended over full automation for the immediate term.

**1. Legal & Contractual Risk (High Severity)**
*   **Assumption:** The January 2023 union agreements permit algorithmic control over shift assignments.
*   **Risk Analysis:** If the current contracts do not explicitly codify "algorithmic assignment" or "automated enforcement," implementing a system that removes human dispatcher discretion likely constitutes a unilateral change in working conditions. This could trigger immediate grievances, work stoppages, or legal challenges from the union liaison.
*   **Constraint:** "Hard enforcement" (preventing a shift from being assigned if rest rules aren't met) may conflict with emergency operational clauses often found in maritime contracts, where captains retain ultimate authority for safety at sea.

**2. Operational Risk (High Severity)**
*   **Assumption:** Strict automated rest periods will not significantly impact the 88% fleet utilization rate.
*   **Risk Analysis:** With vessels already operating at high intensity (12+ consecutive days), introducing rigid, non-negotiable rest windows without increasing headcount will create scheduling gaps. An automated system rejecting shifts to enforce rest will directly lower utilization.
*   **Impact:** A drop from 88% utilization could disrupt the Athens-Crete frequency, leading to revenue loss and potential breach of cargo/passenger service level agreements. The current manual process likely relies on "gray area" flexibility that an automated system would eliminate.

**3. Technical Feasibility vs. Build Strategy**
*   **Build Internal Tool:** Technically feasible to encode rest rules (e.g., STCW regulations). However, encoding the *nuance* of union exceptions and emergency overrides is complex and prone to error.
*   **Adapt Manual Processes:** Lower immediate risk. Enhancing the current manual process with a "compliance checker" (a tool that flags violations but allows human override with justification) mitigates the risk of union backlash while still addressing fatigue.

**Recommendation**
Do not proceed with a "hard enforcement" automated tool at this stage. The risk of violating the January 2023 union agreement and crashing fleet utilization is too high.

**Proposed Path Forward:**
1.  **Legal Review First:** Before writing code, the union liaison must confirm if the 2023 agreement allows for automated enforcement or if a contract amendment is required.
2.  **Pilot a "Decision Support" System:** Build a tool that *recommends* schedules and *flags* fatigue risks but requires a human manager to finalize assignments. This maintains operational flexibility and respects current contractual hierarchies.
3.  **Quantify the Trade-off:** Run a simulation using the last three months of data to calculate exactly how much utilization would drop if strict rest periods were enforced. Present this data to the safety committee alongside the legal assessment.

**Next Steps for Safety Committee Presentation**
*   Present the "Decision Support" prototype rather than the "Auto-Enforcement" concept.
*   Highlight that safety improvements must be balanced with contractual compliance and fleet viability.
*   Request a joint working group (Ops, Union, Safety) to define acceptable parameters for automation before further technical investment.

*Note: This assessment relies on the stated assumption that union agreements may not cover algorithmic control; immediate legal validation is the critical path item.*
*Do not demo a fully automated system to the union liaison until the contract review is complete.*
