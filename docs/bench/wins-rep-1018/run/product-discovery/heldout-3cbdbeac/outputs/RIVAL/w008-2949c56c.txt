### Risk Assessment: Surgical Scheduling Algorithm Pilot

**Executive Summary**
Proceeding with only three London teaching hospitals presents a **high strategic risk** for generalizing findings to the broader UK market. While the sample size (120 rooms) may offer statistical power for *internal* efficiency metrics, it lacks **external validity** due to selection bias. The pilot will validate feasibility and usability in a specific context but cannot confirm value or viability for non-teaching hospitals without additional evidence.

#### 1. Key Risks of the Current Scope

*   **Selection Bias (Strategy & Objectives Risk):** Teaching hospitals typically have higher case complexity, specialized staffing ratios (residents/fellows), and different administrative layers compared to district general hospitals. An algorithm optimized for the fluid, resource-rich environment of a teaching hospital may fail in settings with rigid staffing or lower specialist availability.
*   **Go-to-Market Misalignment:** If the algorithm relies on specific workflows unique to academic centers (e.g., flexible block scheduling for research), the value proposition will not translate to community hospitals focused purely on throughput and cost containment.
*   **Regulatory & Ethics Exposure:** Regulatory bodies (like the MHRA) require evidence that a medical device is safe and effective across the intended population. Demonstrating efficacy only in a "best-case" scenario (top-tier teaching hospitals) may delay clearance or require costly post-market studies to prove generalizability.
*   **False Positives (Value Risk):** You may observe efficiency gains driven by the hospitals' existing culture of innovation and abundant staff rather than the algorithm itself. This creates a "halo effect" that masks potential usability issues in less resourced environments.

#### 2. Sufficiency of Sample Size
*   **Statistical Significance vs. Representativeness:** A pool of 120 rooms over six weeks is likely sufficient to detect *if* the algorithm works *in these specific hospitals* (internal validity). It is **insufficient** to prove *where* it works (external validity).
*   **Timeframe Constraint:** A six-week window may not capture seasonal variations, staff turnover, or exceptional events (e.g., flu season spikes) that impact scheduling logic.

#### 3. Additional Evidence Required for Generalization

To confidently generalize findings to non-teaching hospitals before seeking regulatory clearance, you must gather the following:

*   **Comparative Workflow Analysis:** Conduct a rapid ethnographic study comparing the staffing models, decision-making hierarchies, and turnover protocols of your pilot sites against 2–3 representative district general hospitals. Identify specific variables where they diverge.
*   **Simulation Modeling:** Use historical data from non-teaching hospitals to run "dry-run" simulations of your algorithm. This tests feasibility and predicted outcomes without live deployment, highlighting where the logic might break due to different constraints (e.g., fewer anesthetists).
*   **Qualitative Validation:** Interview scheduling leads and theater managers in non-teaching hospitals to validate your assumptions about their pain points. Ask: "Would this solution solve your specific bottlenecks, or does it assume resources you don't have?"
*   **Stratified Pilot Expansion (Recommended):** If timeline permits, add one non-teaching hospital to the pilot cohort, even with a smaller room count (e.g., 10 rooms). This provides a direct control group to measure variance in performance.

#### Recommendation
**Conditional Go.** Proceed with the pilot to validate core technology and safety (Feasibility/Usability), but explicitly frame the results as "Proof of Concept in Academic Settings." Do **not** claim broad market efficacy yet.

**Immediate Next Steps:**
1.  Launch the 120-room pilot to gather baseline efficiency data.
2.  Simultaneously execute the comparative workflow analysis and simulation modeling using non-teaching hospital data.
3.  Present the preliminary efficacy report with a clear caveat: "Further validation required for non-academic settings prior to full regulatory submission."

*Note: This approach satisfies the 45-day reporting deadline while mitigating the risk of overclaiming efficacy to investors and regulators.*
*Ensure your preliminary report explicitly separates "observed efficiency" from "generalizable value."*
