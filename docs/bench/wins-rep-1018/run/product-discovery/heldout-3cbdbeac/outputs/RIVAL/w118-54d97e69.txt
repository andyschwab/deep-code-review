**Recommendation: Do Not Proceed with Immediate Build; Conduct Root Cause Analysis First**

The current data from four insurance claims totaling 12,000 EUR is insufficient to justify an immediate investment in automated vibration sensors. While the financial loss and reputational damage are significant, the evidence does not yet validate the core assumption that rough handling during transit is the primary cause of breakage. Implementing sensors now risks solving the wrong problem.

**Analysis of Current Evidence**
Your hypothesis rests on the belief that transit vibration is the culprit. However, your current protocol relies on visual inspections by packers, which you suspect is the weak link. This introduces a critical ambiguity:
1.  **Packing Phase Failure:** If ceramics are poorly packed (e.g., insufficient cushioning, improper stacking) due to human error during the visual inspection, they may break under normal transit conditions. Sensors would detect vibration, but the root cause would remain the packing quality, not the carrier's handling.
2.  **Transit Phase Failure:** If packing is adequate but carriers are dropping or throwing crates, sensors would correctly identify these events.

With only four data points, there is no statistical pattern to distinguish between these two scenarios. Investing in sensors without this distinction could lead to a situation where you have perfect data on transit vibrations but see no reduction in breakage because the actual defect lies in the packing process. Furthermore, insurance providers are unlikely to lower premiums based on sensor data alone if the underlying cause (poor packing) remains unaddressed. They require proof of risk *mitigation*, not just risk *monitoring*.

**Required Next Steps**
Before rebuilding the protocol with new technology, you must isolate the failure point:
*   **Immediate Low-Cost Test:** Implement a "pack-out audit" for the next 50 shipments. Have a supervisor verify the packing quality of every crate before it leaves the warehouse and document it with photos. If breakage still occurs on perfectly packed crates, the evidence shifts toward transit issues.
*   **Targeted Sensor Pilot:** Instead of a full rollout, instrument only 5–10 crates with temporary sensors. Correlate any shock events directly with the specific packing audit records for those crates.
*   **Insurance Dialogue:** Present the plan for root cause analysis to your adjuster. Argue that premium reduction will follow a verified decrease in claim frequency, which requires fixing the actual root cause first, whatever it may be.

**Conclusion**
The four claims confirm a problem exists, but they do not diagnose the cause. Proceeding directly to sensor implementation is a high-risk assumption that ignores the potential failure of your current human-dependent packing protocol. A deeper root cause analysis is mandatory to ensure any technological investment actually reduces breakage and satisfies insurance requirements.

*Note: This approach prevents capital waste on sensors if the issue is purely procedural. Focus on validating the packing process before monitoring the transit environment.*
