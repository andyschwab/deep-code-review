**Recommendation: Do Not Greenlight Full Development Yet**

The current evidence of five warranty claims is **insufficient** to justify the immediate engineering investment in a dedicated serial number tracking system. While the problem is real, the root cause remains unverified, and building a complex tracking module based on an unproven assumption ("labeling error at the partner") violates core product discovery principles. You risk solving the wrong problem or over-engineering a solution for a statistical anomaly.

**Immediate Action Plan (Next 7 Days)**

Instead of building software, execute these three low-effort, high-learning steps to validate the root cause before committing roadmap space:

1.  **Forensic Serial Analysis (Zero Engineering Cost)**
    Manually audit the serial numbers of the five returned "refurbished" units against your production logs.
    *   *Goal:* Determine if these units share a common batch, production date, or specific line at the assembly partner. If they are random, the issue may be systemic; if clustered, it isolates the failure to a specific window or machine.
    *   *Decision Point:* If no pattern emerges, a tracking system may not prevent recurrence.

2.  **Partner Process Walkthrough (Direct Observation)**
    Request a virtual or in-person walk-through of the assembly partner's labeling and packaging station. Specifically observe the hand-off point where new units are boxed versus where refurbished returns are processed.
    *   *Goal:* Verify the "labeling error" assumption. Look for physical proximity of new vs. refurbished stock or manual labeling steps prone to human error.
    *   *Decision Point:* If the partner already has barcode scanning but skips it, the solution is a process contract change, not your custom software.

3.  **Enhanced Inbound Spot-Check (Process Test)**
    Temporarily modify your current inbound inspection to scan and log the serial number of *every* unit in one incoming batch (approx. 400 units) rather than just checking batch totals.
    *   *Goal:* Test if the error is occurring before the units reach you (partner side) or if your current "batch total" logic is masking individual swaps.
    *   *Decision Point:* If discrepancies are found here, you have confirmed the leak exists pre-fulfillment.

**Criteria to Greenlight Development**

Proceed with building the tracking module **only if**:
*   The forensic analysis confirms a recurring pattern that manual checks cannot catch.
*   The partner admits their process lacks digital traceability and cannot guarantee fix without your system.
*   The spot-check reveals more than 1% error rate in a single batch (projecting >12 defective units/month), making the financial cost of returns exceed the engineering cost of the tool.

**Conclusion for Co-Founders**
Delay the roadmap commitment until the root cause is empirically proven. Allocate 2–3 days for the manual audit and partner interview. If those steps confirm a systemic traceability gap that costs more than the build, then prioritize the tracking module. Otherwise, you may solve this with a revised SLA or simple process tweak.

*Note: Five incidents represent a 0.7% failure rate over six weeks; ensure this exceeds your acceptable quality threshold before scaling solutions.*
*Focus on validating the "why" before automating the "how."*
