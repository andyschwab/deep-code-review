### Strategic Analysis: Consumer vs. B2B Pivot in Tokyo

**Current Evidence Assessment**
The data indicates a critical failure in the B2C model (0% conversion from 15,000 views) and a weak signal of potential in the B2B model (two unsolicited inquiries). However, this evidence is **insufficient** to justify a full strategic pivot or a complete market exit. A full pivot based solely on two inquiries risks swapping a known failure for an unvalidated hypothesis, potentially burning the remaining $200,000 runway on a false positive.

**Critical Evidence Gaps Preventing a Go/No-Go Decision**

Before the board can approve a Q3 budget shift, the following four gaps must be closed to distinguish between a viable B2B opportunity and anecdotal noise:

1.  **Validation of Willingness to Pay (Viability Risk)**
    *   *Gap:* An inquiry is not a commitment. We do not know if the HR directors have budget authority, if the price point matches their expectations, or if they are merely gathering information for a future quarter.
    *   *Required Data:* Signed Letters of Intent (LOIs) or pre-orders from at least 3–5 corporate prospects. We need proof that they will pay *now*, not just that they are interested.

2.  **Market Size and Channel Feasibility (Go-to-Market Risk)**
    *   *Gap:* Two customers do not constitute a market. We lack data on the total addressable market for corporate wellness in Tokyo, the length of the B2B sales cycle, and the cost of acquiring these customers compared to our runway.
    *   *Required Data:* A mapped sales funnel showing how many leads are needed to close one deal, the average contract value, and whether our current team can execute long-cycle B2B sales without new hires.

3.  **Root Cause of B2C Failure (Value Risk)**
    *   *Gap:* "Does not resonate" is too vague. We do not know if the failure is due to cultural mismatch, pricing, lack of trust in foreign apps, or specific feature gaps. Without this, we cannot confirm if the *technology* is flawed or just the *positioning*.
    *   *Required Data:* Specific behavioral data from the 15,000 visitors. Did they drop off at pricing, sign-up, or feature explanation? We need to rule out fixable localization issues before declaring the consumer segment dead.

4.  **Product Fit for B2B Context (Usability/Feasibility Risk)**
    *   *Gap:* A consumer app rarely works out-of-the-box for enterprises. We do not know if the product lacks essential B2B features (admin dashboards, privacy compliance, bulk onboarding) that would require months of engineering time we may not have.
    *   *Required Data:* A gap analysis of required enterprise features vs. current capabilities, estimated with engineering effort hours.

**Recommendation for the Board**
Do not approve a full pivot or a total exit yet. Instead, allocate a small portion of the runway (e.g., $20,000) for a **four-week validation sprint**.
*   **Goal:** Secure 3 paid B2B pilots or definitively prove the sales cycle is too long for our runway.
*   **Condition:** If pilots are not secured in 30 days, execute an immediate market exit to preserve remaining capital for other regions.

This approach minimizes downside risk while testing the only remaining upside opportunity with actual money on the table.

*Note: This analysis assumes the two HR inquiries are genuine leads; if they were casual comments, the B2B case collapses immediately.*
*Ensure the engineering lead estimates the "admin dashboard" build time before the meeting, as this is likely the largest hidden cost.*
