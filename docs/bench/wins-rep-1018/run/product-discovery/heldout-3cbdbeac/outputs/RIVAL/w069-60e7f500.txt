# Executive Summary: Population Health Dashboard for PopHealth Mini

## Potential Value Proposition
The proposed dashboard aims to transform PopHealth Mini from a transactional record-keeping tool into a strategic care management partner for independent practices. By aggregating chronic disease metrics, the module promises to:
*   **Proactive Care Enablement:** Shift physicians from reactive treatment to proactive population management, potentially improving patient outcomes and quality-of-care ratings (e.g., MIPS/HEDIS scores).
*   **Differentiation:** Distinguish PopHealth Mini from basic EHR competitors by offering analytics typically reserved for larger health systems with dedicated data teams.
*   **Retention Anchor:** If the data becomes integral to weekly workflow (currently 15 mins/week), it could increase switching costs and reduce the 4% annual churn.

## Critical Risks & Assumptions
Despite the pilot's usage data, significant risks threaten the viability of this initiative:

**1. Value Realization Gap (High Risk)**
While doctors spend 15 minutes reviewing data, only 2 of 12 physicians requested further features. This suggests the current output may be "nice to have" rather than "need to have." If the dashboard provides visibility without actionable workflows, it adds cognitive load without solving a core problem. The assumption that "lack of admin staff" prevents action is unverified; the real barrier may be that the insights do not translate into billable actions or time savings.

**2. Monetization & Willingness to Pay (High Risk)**
The core unknown is whether practices will pay an additional $99/month ($1,188/year). For a practice with <5 doctors, this is a non-trivial line item. Given the 4% churn rate, introducing a paid module that fails to demonstrate immediate ROI could accelerate churn rather than prevent it. There is a risk that current users view this as a feature that should be included in the base price.

**3. Implementation & Usability (Medium Risk)**
Small practices lack data analysts. If the dashboard requires manual data entry, complex filtering, or interpretation beyond a quick glance, adoption will stall. The 15-minute weekly review time must be scrutinized: is this time spent gaining insights, or struggling to find them?

**4. Strategic Distraction (Medium Risk)**
Allocating Q3 resources to build a complex analytics engine may divert focus from core stability or other high-churn drivers. If the feature does not move the needle on retention, the opportunity cost is significant.

## Recommendation for Board Consideration
Before committing Q3 development resources, we must validate the **willingness to pay** and the **actionability of insights**. The current pilot indicates usage but not value creation. We recommend pausing full-scale development to run a "fake door" test or a concierge pilot where we manually generate reports for 5 non-pilot clinics and attempt to sell the $99/month tier. This will confirm demand before engineering costs are incurred.

***

*Note: The low feature request rate (2/12) in the pilot is a leading indicator that the current iteration may not solve a painful enough problem to justify a price increase.*
