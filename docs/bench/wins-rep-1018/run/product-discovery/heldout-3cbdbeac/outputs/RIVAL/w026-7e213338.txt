**Recommendation: Prioritize the Automated Code Suggestions Module**

For the Q1 roadmap, MedBill Solutions should commit its single major delivery slot to the **Automated Code Suggestions** module. While patient satisfaction is a critical strategic goal, the data indicates that automating billing accuracy is the most effective lever to address both immediate financial leakage and the root causes of patient dissatisfaction within your constrained timeline.

**Analysis of Trade-offs**

*   **Immediate Cost Recovery vs. Speculative Gains:**
    The assumption that automation will recover 80% of lost labor hours is grounded in observable behavior: staff currently spend 15 hours weekly on manual corrections. This represents a tangible, recurring cost of $4,000/month. Validating this efficiency gain provides immediate ROI and frees up staff capacity. In contrast, the impact of a dedicated survey module on satisfaction scores is indirect and speculative. Surveys measure sentiment; they do not resolve the operational friction causing the sentiment. Investing the quarter's only major deliverable in a measurement tool (surveys) rather than a resolution tool (automation) delays the actual fix for the patients' complaints.

*   **Root Cause vs. Symptom Management:**
    Patient comments citing "poor communication" in a billing context often stem from delayed invoices, confusing line items, or follow-up calls regarding errors—all downstream effects of manual coding mistakes. By prioritizing code suggestions, you attack the source of these errors. Reducing billing errors naturally reduces the need for corrective communication, thereby improving the patient experience organically. A dedicated survey module would only highlight the dissatisfaction without fixing the underlying billing inaccuracies driving it.

*   **Risk Profile:**
    The risk of failing to meet board targets for patient satisfaction remains high if you build the survey module but leave the error-prone manual process intact. However, the risk associated with the automation module is lower because even if the 80% efficiency target is not fully met, any reduction in manual coding directly lowers costs and likely reduces error rates. The "unknown variable" regarding the correlation between billing accuracy and satisfaction scores is best tested by implementing the fix first and observing the metric shift, rather than building new infrastructure to track the decline.

**Strategic Conclusion**

Choose the path that solves the core operational defect. Automating code suggestions addresses the $48,000 annual labor waste and simultaneously targets the primary driver of patient frustration. This approach maximizes the value of your single development slot by delivering a functional cure rather than a diagnostic tool. You can validate the impact on satisfaction scores post-launch using existing feedback channels, deferring the dedicated survey module until a future quarter when development capacity allows.

*Note: Ensure your Q1 success metrics explicitly track both labor hours saved and the trend in patient satisfaction comments related to billing clarity to validate the indirect benefit.*
