### Viability Risk Analysis: FitPulse Hospital Integration

**Critical Unknowns Requiring Resolution**

1.  **Hospital Technical Compatibility (High Risk/High Impact)**
    *   **Unknown:** Whether regional hospitals can ingest data via your proposed API standard without custom middleware.
    *   **Risk:** If custom middleware is required, the six-week engineering estimate is invalid. Costs could triple, and timelines could extend by months, destroying project viability.
    *   **Evidence Needed:** Technical specification documents from 2–3 target hospital IT departments confirming supported ingestion protocols (e.g., HL7, FHIR) and their stance on third-party API integrations.

2.  **Compliance Audit Duration & Scope (High Risk/High Impact)**
    *   **Unknown:** The specific timeline and requirements of the compliance audit under the March 15 regulations.
    *   **Risk:** An audit adding "months" to the timeline delays ROI and may miss strategic windows. Failure to pass results in legal liability and blocked launch.
    *   **Evidence Needed:** A preliminary scoping document from a healthcare compliance specialist or legal counsel detailing the exact steps, data points, and estimated duration for auditing a new third-party data flow.

3.  **Actual Consent Conversion vs. Survey Intent (Medium Risk/Medium Impact)**
    *   **Unknown:** The gap between the 40% survey opt-in rate and actual user behavior when faced with a real consent friction point.
    *   **Risk:** If actual opt-in is significantly lower (e.g., <15%), the data volume shared may be too low to provide value to hospitals, undermining the partnership's core value proposition.
    *   **Evidence Needed:** Results from a low-fidelity prototype test or a landing page experiment measuring actual click-through rates on a realistic consent modal, rather than survey responses.

4.  **Hospital Willingness to Partner (Medium Risk/High Impact)**
    *   **Unknown:** Whether hospitals have the budget, strategic interest, or bandwidth to onboard a new fitness data source.
    *   **Risk:** Even if technically feasible, hospitals may reject the integration due to internal priorities, liability concerns, or lack of resources to act on the data.
    *   **Evidence Needed:** Letters of Intent (LOI) or recorded discovery interviews with hospital decision-makers (CMIOs or IT Directors) explicitly stating their interest and resource availability for this specific integration.

**Specific Evidence Gathering Plan for Hospital Partners**

To validate the technical readiness assumption, execute the following immediately:

*   **Request Technical Interviews:** Schedule 30-minute calls with IT Directors at three pilot hospitals. Do not pitch; ask: "What are your current supported standards for inbound patient-generated data?" and "Describe your last third-party integration. Did it require custom build work on your end?"
*   **Sandbox Access Request:** Ask one friendly partner if you can run a non-production data push to their test environment using your proposed API standard to verify immediate compatibility.
*   **Middleware Cost Estimation:** If they indicate custom work is needed, ask for their vendor list or internal rate cards to estimate the true cost and time impact on *their* side, which often becomes a blocker for *your* project.

**Recommendation:** Do not commit engineering resources to the full build until the Technical Compatibility and Compliance Audit unknowns are resolved. These are binary gates; if either fails, the project cannot proceed.

*Note: Present the "Technical Compatibility" finding as the primary go/no-go decision factor for the board.*
*Ensure legal reviews all outreach scripts to hospitals to avoid premature data sharing discussions.*
