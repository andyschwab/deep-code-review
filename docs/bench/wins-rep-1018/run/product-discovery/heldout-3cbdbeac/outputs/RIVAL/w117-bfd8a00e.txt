### Executive Assessment: Rush Delivery Network Viability

**Current Evidence Status: Insufficient for Full Infrastructure Investment**

While the financial stakes (€200,000 lost revenue) are significant, the current data set of five incidents over six weeks is statistically weak and highly susceptible to event-driven anomalies. The proximity to the Grand Prix introduces a major confounding variable; luxury yacht activity and part failure rates often spike during major events, making this period unrepresentative of baseline demand.

**Risk Analysis of Core Assumptions**

1.  **Demand Consistency (High Risk):** The assumption that these five requests represent a consistent market pattern is unproven. If this was an anomaly, building a dedicated fleet results in severe overcapacity and fixed cost burdens during standard weeks.
2.  **Regulatory Feasibility (Critical Blocker):** Your uncertainty regarding commercial speedboat regulations in Monaco harbor during peak season is a "showstopper" risk. If regulatory bodies deny operating permits for commercial rush vessels in high-traffic zones, the entire business model collapses regardless of demand.
3.  **Revenue Capture (Moderate Risk):** Assuming a dedicated fleet captures 90% of lost revenue ignores operational realities such as boat maintenance, crew availability, and weather constraints which may lower actual service levels.

**Strategic Recommendation: The "Concierge" Test**

Do not greenlight the capital expenditure for a dedicated fleet yet. Instead, execute a low-fidelity experiment to validate demand and regulatory paths simultaneously:

*   **Action:** For the next 4–6 weeks, manually charter existing third-party water taxis or private speedboats on a per-request basis to fulfill rush orders.
*   **Objective:** This validates if customers will pay the premium for rush delivery without you owning the assets. It also forces immediate engagement with harbor masters to understand permitting requirements for commercial charters versus owned fleets.
*   **Success Metric:** Secure at least 3 additional rush requests outside of event weeks and obtain a preliminary regulatory opinion on commercial operations.

**Conclusion**

The cost of being wrong about the regulatory environment or demand frequency far outweighs the opportunity cost of waiting another month for data. Proceed with a manual, variable-cost pilot immediately. Only commit to fixed infrastructure (buying boats, hiring dedicated crews) once you have confirmed regulatory clearance and observed consistent demand outside of the Grand Prix window.

***

*This approach shifts the risk from capital expenditure to operational learning, satisfying investor caution while addressing the operations director's urgency.*
*Delaying the full build prevents potential stranded assets if the Grand Prix effect skews the data.*
