### Strategic Recommendation: Proceed with Upfront Localization

**Decision:** Invest the $75,000 and eight weeks of development time to launch with a fully French-localized interface. Do not launch an English-only version first.

**Rationale:**
Your current assumption—that language is a strict gatekeeper—is supported by the market data indicating 85% of business decisions in the region are conducted in French. In the B2B mid-sized retail sector, inventory management is an operational core function, not a peripheral tool. Users in this segment rarely compromise on usability or language for a lower price point because the cognitive load and training costs of using a non-native tool outweigh software savings.

Launching English-only presents three critical failures:
1.  **Invalid Signal:** If adoption is low, you will not know if it was due to price, features, or language. You cannot "test" language preference when the market norm is 85% French; the experiment is biased from the start.
2.  **Competitive Disadvantage:** With a French-native competitor launching in four months, an eight-week localization push allows you to enter the market simultaneously or slightly ahead. Launching English-first cedes the "local partner" narrative to the competitor immediately.
3.  **Brand Perception:** Entering Quebec with an English-only SaaS signals a lack of commitment to the region. For mid-sized retailers, trust and local support are primary buying criteria.

**Action Plan:**
*   **Immediate:** Allocate budget and resources for the eight-week localization sprint.
*   **Scope:** Ensure full UI translation, French customer support documentation, and billing in CAD/French.
*   **Go-to-Market:** Position the launch as "Built for Quebec Retailers" to differentiate from generic North American tools before the competitor arrives.

Delaying localization based on the hope that price can override language barriers is a high-risk gamble with a low probability of success in this specific cultural and linguistic context.

*Note: This recommendation assumes the 85% French decision-making statistic applies specifically to software procurement in the retail sector.*
*If budget constraints are absolute, consider a manual concierge pilot with 3-5 friendly French-speaking retailers before full build, rather than a public English launch.*
