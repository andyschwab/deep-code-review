### Critical Signals Needed to Validate Market Demand

Based on your interviews, you have strong evidence of a **problem** (missed appointments, sibling stress) but zero evidence of a **solution demand** (willingness to adopt or pay). To satisfy investors and avoid building a feature nobody uses, you must validate the following four signals before February development:

**1. Evidence of Active Solution-Seeking (Value Risk)**
You noted that no one offered money. This is a critical gap. You need to determine if families are actively trying to solve this now.
*   **Signal to find:** Have participants tried to solve this already? Did they buy a shared calendar subscription, hire a care manager, or create complex spreadsheets?
*   **Why it matters:** If they haven't tried to solve it, the pain may not be acute enough to drive adoption. People rarely pay for problems they are tolerating.

**2. The "Switching Cost" Threshold (Usability & Adoption Risk)**
You suspect families will stick to text threads. You need to prove why they would leave that workflow.
*   **Signal to find:** What specific friction in text/paper causes a measurable negative outcome (e.g., a medical error, a heated family argument)?
*   **Why it matters:** "Stress" is often not enough to change behavior. The new tool must be 10x better than the status quo to overcome the inertia of existing habits.

**3. Willingness to Commit (Viability Risk)**
Since no one offered money verbally, you need to test if they will commit resources in a low-fidelity test.
*   **Signal to find:** Will they pre-order, join a waitlist with an email, or commit to a pilot program where they agree to stop using text threads for two weeks?
*   **Why it matters:** Opinions ("This sounds cool") are noise; commitments are data. Investors need to see that users are willing to change their behavior, not just agree that the problem exists.

**4. The Decision-Maker vs. User Split (Go-to-Market Risk)**
In family care, the person suffering the stress (the coordinator) is not always the person controlling the budget or convincing the siblings to join.
*   **Signal to find:** Who actually has the authority to mandate a new tool for the whole family? Is it the primary caregiver, or does it require consensus?
*   **Why it matters:** If adoption requires convincing three resistant siblings, your churn risk is massive. You need to identify the single point of entry that unlocks the network.

### Recommended Next Steps for Validation

Do not write code yet. Run these two lightweight experiments to generate the signals above:

1.  **The "Fake Door" Test:** Create a simple landing page describing the shared calendar with a "Join Pilot" button. Drive traffic from your existing interview list or relevant forums. Measure the click-through rate. If <5% click, the value proposition is unclear.
2.  **The "Concierge" Pilot:** Recruit 5 families from your interviews. Offer to manually coordinate their calendar for two weeks using existing tools (Google Calendar + You as the moderator) for a small fee ($20). If they refuse to pay or engage, a software app will not solve the underlying coordination dynamic.
