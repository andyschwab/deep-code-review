### Assessment of Scheduler Logic Flaw

**Conclusion:** The current evidence is **insufficient** to confirm a flaw in the scheduler logic or to justify an immediate patch. While the symptoms (90-minute waits, crop rot) are critical, the root cause remains unverified between internal algorithmic failure and external constraints. Proceeding with a code patch based solely on two manager complaints risks solving the wrong problem while wasting the narrow window before January 15th.

**Evidence Analysis:**
1.  **Symptom Validity:** The reported 90-minute average wait time and subsequent crop rotting confirm a severe operational bottleneck exists at the intake gates. This requires immediate mitigation regardless of the cause.
2.  **Causal Ambiguity:**
    *   **Hypothesis A (Internal):** The algorithm fails to account for variable unloading speeds during peak hours.
    *   **Hypothesis B (External):** Factors such as road construction or traffic incidents are delaying arrivals, creating artificial peaks that overwhelm even a correctly functioning scheduler.
    *   **Current Status:** There is no data provided to distinguish between these two scenarios. The managers' complaints identify the *location* of the pain (intake gates) but not the *source* of the disruption.
3.  **Sample Size Limitation:** Complaints from only two mill managers out of the likely broader network covered by 200 daily trips may indicate an isolated incident rather than a systemic logic failure. If the issue were purely algorithmic across the board, one would expect widespread reports unless the specific mills mentioned have unique characteristics not modeled in the system.

**Recommendation for Operations Director and Vendor:**
Do not authorize a code patch or new feature build yet. Instead, execute a rapid 48-hour diagnostic:
*   **Correlate Timestamps:** Compare actual truck arrival times against scheduled slots for the affected mills. If trucks arrive in clusters significantly earlier or later than scheduled due to external delays, the scheduler logic is likely functioning correctly, and the issue is upstream (traffic/roads).
*   **Measure Unloading Variance:** Manually record actual unloading times per truck during peak hours for the complaining mills. If these times deviate wildly from the static variables currently in the algorithm, Hypothesis A is confirmed.
*   **Check External Logs:** Verify local traffic or construction reports for the specific routes leading to the two affected mills during the complaint periods.

**Decision Path:**
*   If arrival times match schedules but unloading times vary significantly: **Patch the logic** to include dynamic unloading variables.
*   If arrival times are highly erratic due to external delays: **Reject the patch**; instead, implement a manual override protocol or communicate with logistics partners to smooth inflow. Building a "dynamic queuing feature" is premature without confirming the system can predict the external variables causing the queue.

The debate between customizing the current module versus building a new feature cannot be resolved until the source of the 90-minute delay is empirically isolated.
