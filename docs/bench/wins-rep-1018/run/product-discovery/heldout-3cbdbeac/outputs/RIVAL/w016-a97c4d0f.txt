### Risk Evaluation: Native Excel Export vs. Data Flexibility

**The Core Risk: Solving the Wrong Problem**
Committing 24 developer-weeks to a native Excel export based on the assumption that "Excel format" is the blocker carries significant **Value Risk**. You are assuming the *file format* is the issue, when the root cause may be *data structure* or *workflow integration*.

If the customers actually need specific pivot tables, custom macros, or dynamic data connections that a static `.xlsx` file cannot provide, building this feature will fail to secure renewals despite the heavy investment. Conversely, if their only true blocker is the inability to easily manipulate CSV data, a simpler solution (e.g., a better CSV schema or a lightweight middleware connector) could solve the problem at 10% of the cost.

**Specific Risks of Proceeding Without Validation:**
1.  **False Security:** You may deliver the feature by August 30, only to find the customers still churn because the *content* or *usability* of the export didn't match their internal reporting workflows.
2.  **Opportunity Cost:** Tying up five developers for six weeks prevents work on other high-value retention features or technical debt reduction that might address the actual "data flexibility" complaints more effectively.
3.  **Maintenance Burden:** Native Excel generation often requires ongoing maintenance to support new report fields, formatting changes, and version compatibility, creating long-term viability drag for a feature built in panic mode.
4.  **Precedent Setting:** Delivering a custom, expensive feature for five accounts sets an expectation for bespoke development that may not scale to your wider customer base.

**Recommended Discovery Questions**
Do not ask "Do you want Excel?" (they will say yes). Instead, investigate their past behavior and specific workflow constraints to uncover the real job-to-be-done.

*   **Workflow Context:** "Walk me through the exact steps you take immediately after downloading the current CSV. What specific actions do you perform in Excel before the report is ready for your stakeholders?"
*   **Pain Point Specificity:** "You mentioned the current workaround is difficult. Can you describe the last time you tried to generate this quarterly report? Where specifically did the process break down or cause frustration?"
*   **Format vs. Function:** "If we provided a file that opened directly in Excel but lacked some of the native formatting you expect, would that solve your problem? Or are there specific calculations/macros you need to run that require a native file structure?"
*   **Alternative Solutions:** "Have you tried using any third-party tools or scripts to convert the CSVs? If so, why did those not work for your team?"
*   **Consequence of Failure:** "If this report isn't in the exact format you need by September, what is the specific outcome? Does it stop the renewal entirely, or does it just create manual work for your team?"

**Strategic Recommendation**
Before committing to the full build, propose a **Concierge Test**: Manually generate a native Excel file for one of the at-risk customers using their exact data and deliver it within 48 hours. Observe if they use it successfully and if it resolves their renewal hesitation. If the manual file works, the value hypothesis is validated; if they still have issues, the problem lies elsewhere, saving you six weeks of wasted development.

*Note: Prioritize interviewing the two accounts threatening to switch immediately, as their timeline drives the critical path.*
*Ensure the Sales VP understands that "building fast" is less important than "building the right thing" to stop churn.*
