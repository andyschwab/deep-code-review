# Evaluation: Dynamic Routing vs. Human Adoption for KiwiMilk Collective

## Executive Summary
The proposal to develop a custom dynamic routing algorithm addresses the symptom (late arrivals) but ignores the primary risk identified: driver adoption. With a current 12% late arrival rate costing $8,000/month, the financial incentive to act is clear. However, assuming a technical solution will automatically yield 98% on-time performance is a high-risk assumption given the decade-long reliance on static routes by experienced drivers. The "human factor" is not merely a variable; it is the critical dependency. Proceeding with a full custom build before validating driver willingness to adopt new instructions is likely to result in high development costs and low operational ROI.

## Risk Analysis of Current Assumptions

### 1. The Value Assumption (High Risk)
**Assumption:** Dynamic routing will improve on-time performance to 98%.
**Reality Check:** Algorithms optimize for mathematical efficiency, not human behavior. If drivers ignore or override the system due to distrust or habit, the theoretical 98% target will not be realized. The 12% failure rate may stem from non-traffic issues (e.g., farm gate access, pump failures, loading delays) which dynamic routing cannot solve. Without verifying the root causes of the last four urgent calls, the solution may miss the mark entirely.

### 2. The Usability & Adoption Assumption (Critical Risk)
**Assumption:** Drivers will adopt new directional instructions without significant resistance or error.
**Reality Check:** Drivers with ten years of static route knowledge possess tacit knowledge (shortcuts, road conditions, farmer preferences) that an initial algorithm will lack. Forcing a "black box" solution on experts often triggers resistance. If the new directions conflict with their experience even once, trust erodes, leading to manual overrides and a reversion to old habits.

### 3. The Feasibility & Viability Assumption
**Assumption:** A custom build is the most viable path.
**Reality Check:** Building a custom algorithm integrating real-time traffic and weather data requires significant ongoing maintenance and data infrastructure. Given the specific constraint of the 4:00 AM – 6:00 AM window, traffic variability in rural Waikato may be negligible compared to weather or farm-side delays. The cost of custom development may outweigh the $96,000 annual penalty savings, especially if adoption fails.

## Strategic Recommendation

**Do not proceed with a full custom technical build at this stage.** The risk of building a sophisticated tool that drivers refuse to use is too high. Instead, adopt a phased discovery approach:

1.  **Root Cause Validation (Week 1-2):** Before coding, interview the drivers involved in the recent late arrivals and the four complaining cooperatives. Determine if the delays were actually caused by traffic/weather (solvable by routing) or by farm-side bottlenecks (unsolvable by routing).
2.  **Low-Fidelity Experiment (Week 3-4):** Instead of a custom build, use existing off-the-shelf navigation tools (e.g., Waze/Google Maps integrated into a simple dispatch tablet) for a pilot group of 3 drivers. Measure if *they* choose to follow the dynamic suggestions when presented alongside their usual route.
3.  **Co-Design Phase:** If the pilot shows promise, involve senior drivers in designing the interface. Their input on what data they trust (e.g., "show me the rain radar" vs. "just tell me the turn") will determine adoption success.

## Conclusion
The problem is likely socio-technical, not purely technical. Investing in a custom algorithm now assumes the solution is code, when the evidence suggests the barrier is behavioral. Validate the root causes of delays and driver openness to change with low-cost experiments before committing to a build. A focus on driver training and co-designed tools offers a higher probability of achieving the 98% target than a standalone software project.

*Note: Immediate engagement with the driver union or representative group is recommended to frame this as a support tool rather than a monitoring mechanism.*
*Financial exposure remains $8k/month until the root cause of the specific Waikato delays is empirically verified.*
