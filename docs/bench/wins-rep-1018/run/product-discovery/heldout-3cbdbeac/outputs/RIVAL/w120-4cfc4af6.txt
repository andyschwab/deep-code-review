### Executive Assessment: Urgency of Digital Artifact Tracker Initiative

**Recommendation:** Proceed immediately with a **limited-scope pilot** of the digital tracker, but do not commit to a full build or permanent wireless installation until specific administrative concerns are addressed. The current evidence supports urgent *action*, but not necessarily the specific *solution* you have assumed.

**Analysis of Evidence Severity**
The safety concerns presented constitute a **critical risk signal**, though they currently lack the statistical volume to guarantee board approval for a full-scale technological overhaul on their own.
*   **Severity (High):** The bypassing of environmental controls for delicate frescoes represents a catastrophic failure mode. Even one incident could result in irreversible cultural loss.
*   **Frequency (Low but Escalating):** Two near-misses in one month from three leading conservationists indicate a systemic process failure rather than isolated human error. In high-stakes conservation, "near-miss" frequency is often a leading indicator of an impending major incident.
*   **Systemic Latency:** A four-hour verification lag in a paper-based system is incompatible with the real-time sensitivity of fresco conservation. This latency is a confirmed vulnerability that digital solutions can objectively resolve.

**Risk Assessment of the Proposed Solution**
Your assumption that a "digital tracker" is the immediate answer contains a hidden **Viability Risk**: the potential rejection by the conservative administration regarding wireless devices.
*   If you build a full wireless system now, you risk creating a sunk cost that the administration will reject, wasting resources and delaying safety improvements further.
*   The "unknown variable" of a formal halt is a **Strategic Risk**. If movements are halted, the museum loses revenue and public access. Preventing this halt requires a solution that satisfies both the conservators' safety needs and the administration's preservation mandates.

**Strategic Path Forward**
Do not frame this as a choice between "build now" or "wait for alignment." Instead, propose a **Feasibility & Ethics Experiment**:

1.  **Immediate Action (Days 1-7):** Propose a **non-wireless or low-emission pilot** (e.g., passive RFID or localized Bluetooth Low Energy with shielded gateways) for a single, low-risk movement corridor. This directly addresses the conservators' safety concerns without triggering the administration's fear of widespread wireless interference.
2.  **Stakeholder Alignment (Parallel Track):** Use the data from the two near-miss incidents to quantify the potential cost of a halt versus the cost of the pilot. Present this to the board not as a request for a new system, but as a **risk mitigation test**.
3.  **Decision Gate:** If the pilot proves it eliminates the 4-hour lag without triggering environmental alarms, use this validated evidence to secure approval for broader implementation before the month-end deadline.

**Conclusion**
The safety concerns are severe enough to justify immediate *investigation and prototyping*, but not yet sufficient to bypass the administration's authority for a full build. The evidence supports launching a targeted experiment to prove the solution's safety and efficacy, thereby de-risking the decision for the board.
