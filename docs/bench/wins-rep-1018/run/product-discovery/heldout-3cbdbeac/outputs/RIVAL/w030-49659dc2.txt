### Strategic Recommendation: Conditional Acceptance with Architectural Guardrails

**Decision:** Accept the custom work for St. Jude's Memorial, but strictly condition the delivery on a "Configurable Theme Engine" pilot that isolates customization from the core codebase. Do not build hard-coded exceptions.

**Rationale & Risk Analysis**
The immediate risk of losing 30% of revenue outweighs the theoretical risk of technical debt, provided the implementation strategy changes. The assumption that a configurable engine allows 70% reuse is viable only if we treat this not as a "custom feature" but as the inaugural test of our multi-tenant architecture.

However, the fear regarding the two-week release cycle is valid. Hard-coding specific flows for St. Jude's will inevitably create branching logic that slows down future deployments. To mitigate this, the engineering team must enforce a strict separation of concerns:
1.  **Core vs. Configuration:** The core intake flow remains unchanged in the main branch. St. Jude's specific branding and field variations must be driven entirely by configuration files or a new abstraction layer, not conditional `if/else` statements within the core logic.
2.  **The "Tax" on Velocity:** Acknowledge that the first release following this implementation may slip from two weeks to three weeks as the team validates the new engine. This is an investment cost, not a permanent slowdown. If the engine is built correctly, subsequent customizations should require *less* time, eventually accelerating velocity for future enterprise clients.

**Action Plan**

1.  **Negotiate Scope with St. Jude's (Account Executive Lead):**
    *   Confirm that the "unique patient intake flow" is defined by data fields and workflow order, not fundamental logic changes.
    *   Secure agreement that future updates to the core platform may occasionally require a brief validation window for their custom configuration, protecting our release cadence.

2.  **Architectural Sprint (VP of Product & Engineering Lead):**
    *   Dedicate the next sprint solely to building the "Theme Engine" prototype.
    *   **Success Metric:** The engine must allow St. Jude's branding/flow changes without modifying a single line of core component code. If this cannot be achieved, the assumption of 70% reuse is false, and we must revisit the commercial terms.

3.  **Timeline Adjustment:**
    *   Target a soft launch for St. Jude's by May 15 to allow a two-week buffer for configuration tuning before their June 1 hard deadline.
    *   Pause non-essential feature work for other clients during the engine build to protect the team's focus.

**Conclusion**
Rejecting the request guarantees immediate financial loss. Accepting it via hard-coding guarantees long-term stagnation. The only viable path is to use this demand as the catalyst to build the scalable configuration layer the product needs anyway. We accept the short-term velocity hit to build the engine, framing it internally as a strategic infrastructure upgrade rather than a one-off client concession.

*Note: If the engineering team determines the intake flow logic requires fundamental architectural changes beyond theming, immediately escalate to renegotiate the June 1 deadline.*
