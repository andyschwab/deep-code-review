### Executive Assessment: Sensor Pilot Justification

**Recommendation:** Proceed immediately with a targeted pilot program, but do not yet commit to full fleet integration. The current evidence of €22,000 in losses is sufficient to justify a low-cost experiment, but insufficient to validate the business case for a permanent 8% cost increase across all shipments.

**Rationale Analysis**
The two recent disputes highlight a critical blind spot in your current quality assurance model: the assumption of stability during transit. Relying on warehouse spot checks fails to account for environmental fluctuations over the five-day journey to Germany. While these incidents prove a problem exists, they do not yet prove that real-time sensors are the correct solution or that the market will absorb the associated costs.

Moving directly to full integration based on two data points carries significant risk. If the root cause is isolated to specific routes, seasons, or carrier handling practices rather than a systemic issue, applying an 8% freight surcharge to every shipment could erode margins unnecessarily or make your bids uncompetitive. Furthermore, without data proving that clients value this transparency enough to pay for it (or that it prevents future disputes), the viability of the solution remains untested.

**Proposed Pilot Strategy**
To de-risk this decision before the steering committee meeting, structure the pilot as follows:

1.  **Scope:** Equip only 5–10 containers on the specific routes serving the disputing German buyers or similar climatic zones.
2.  **Duration:** Run for 4–6 weeks to capture varied weather conditions.
3.  **Cost Mitigation:** Negotiate a short-term rental agreement for the sensors rather than a purchase, and absorb the 8% cost internally for this pilot phase as a research expense.
4.  **Success Metrics:**
    *   **Correlation:** Does sensor data confirm moisture spikes during transit that correlate with warehouse exit data?
    *   **Prevention:** Can the data trigger mid-transit interventions (e.g., adjusting ventilation if possible, or rerouting)?
    *   **Client Validation:** Present the pilot data to the disputing clients. Ask explicitly: "If we provide this continuous data log with every shipment, does it justify a price adjustment, or does it simply resolve disputes faster?"

**Decision Framework for the Committee**
Present the pilot not as a final solution, but as the necessary step to answer three unknowns:
*   **Feasibility:** Do the sensors survive the rigors of timber transport?
*   **Value:** Does the data actually identify the root cause (transit vs. loading)?
*   **Viability:** Will the reduction in dispute losses exceed the 8% operational cost increase?

If the pilot confirms that transit conditions are the culprit and clients agree that the data prevents future financial loss, you will have the empirical evidence needed to roll out the technology broadly. If the data shows moisture levels remain stable despite the disputes, you save the company from implementing an expensive solution to a non-existent transit problem, prompting an investigation into loading procedures or contractual definitions instead.

Two disputes are a warning signal, not a business case. A pilot converts that signal into actionable data.

*Note: Ensure the pilot includes a clause with the sensor vendor for data ownership and ease of return.*
