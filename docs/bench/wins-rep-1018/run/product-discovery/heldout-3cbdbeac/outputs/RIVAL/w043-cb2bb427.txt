### Recommendation: Do Not Build Payment Infrastructure Yet

Based on the evidence provided, you should **wait** and conduct further validation before building payment infrastructure or committing to a monetization strategy for the board meeting. Proceeding now carries an unacceptably high risk of building a feature that fails to generate the projected revenue.

**Analysis of Current Evidence**

Your core assumption is that 3% of your user base (450 users) will convert to a paid tier. However, your recent survey data directly contradicts this:
*   **Survey Conversion Rate:** Only 12 out of 200 respondents (6%) said they would "definitely" pay.
*   **The "Say-Do" Gap:** In product discovery, stated intent ("I would pay") is notoriously unreliable and almost always overestimates actual behavior. If only 6% *say* they will pay, the actual conversion rate is likely significantly lower than 3%, potentially rendering the business model unsustainable.
*   **Competitive Context:** You identified a critical risk: Swedish consumers have access to high-quality, free government health resources. This creates a high barrier to entry for paid content unless your value proposition is distinctly superior or solves a specific pain point that free resources do not.

**Risk Assessment**

Building payment infrastructure is a **Viability** and **Feasibility** investment. If the **Value** risk (willingness to pay) is not resolved, this investment is wasted.
*   **High Risk:** The gap between your 3% target and the <6% stated intent (which will likely drop in reality) suggests the idea may not be viable.
*   **High Impact:** If you build the system and no one buys, you burn additional cash on development and maintenance, worsening your unsustainable burn rate.

**Recommended Next Steps for the Board Meeting**

Instead of asking the board to approve development, propose a **2-week validation sprint** to test willingness to pay with real behavior, not opinions.

1.  **Fake Door Test:** Create a landing page or a modal within your existing platform describing the "Premium Plan" at 99 SEK with a "Subscribe Now" button.
    *   When users click the button, show a message: "We are launching this soon! Enter your email to get notified and lock in a founding member rate."
    *   **Metric:** Measure the click-through rate (CTR) on the button. This measures *intent to act*, which is stronger than survey responses.
2.  **Pre-Sale Experiment:** For a small segment (e.g., 1,000 engaged users), offer the ability to pre-pay for the first month at a discount.
    *   **Metric:** Actual credit card transactions. This is the only true validation of willingness to pay.
3.  **Qualitative Interviews:** Interview the 12 users who said "yes" in the survey. Ask them about the last time they paid for health content and what specifically made them pay. Uncover the "Job to Be Done" that free government sites aren't fulfilling.

**Board Decision Framework**

Present this to the board:
*   **If** the Fake Door CTR is >2% **or** you secure 50+ pre-sales, **then** we proceed to build payment infrastructure.
*   **If** results are below these thresholds, we pivot the content strategy or explore alternative revenue models (e.g., B2B partnerships, corporate wellness) before spending more on B2C infrastructure.

This approach minimizes financial risk while demonstrating a disciplined, data-driven strategy to the board.

*Note: The survey result of 6% "definite" interest is a leading indicator that your 3% conversion goal is optimistic given the typical drop-off from intent to action.*
*Focus your board presentation on the plan to validate real money movement, not on hypothetical revenue projections.*
