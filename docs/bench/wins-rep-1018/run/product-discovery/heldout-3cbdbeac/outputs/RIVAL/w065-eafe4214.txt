### Market Readiness vs. Delay Cost Analysis: Radiant Scan AI Tool

**Executive Summary for Clinical Advisory Board**
The decision to proceed with the beta release hinges on distinguishing between "general utility" and "niche reliability." While 92% accuracy on common fractures suggests strong general value, the 78% accuracy on rare pediatric conditions presents a critical liability and trust barrier. The assumption that "higher accuracy equals adoption" requires validation; radiologists may adopt a tool for triage at 92% but reject it for diagnostic confirmation if niche failure rates are perceived as unpredictable.

**1. Market Readiness Threshold Analysis**
*   **The Trust Baseline:** In radiology, trust is binary for specific use cases. For common fractures, 92% accuracy likely exceeds the threshold for a "second opinion" tool, offering significant efficiency gains. However, for rare pediatric conditions, 78% accuracy implies a 22% failure rate. In a clinical setting, this is often unacceptable for decision support, potentially leading to false negatives that carry severe legal and ethical consequences.
*   **Adoption Driver:** Hospital adoption is likely driven by the tool's ability to reduce workload on common cases without introducing unacceptable risk in niche areas. If the tool cannot reliably flag when it is uncertain about a rare condition, the 78% metric is a liability, not just a performance gap. Radiologists may distrust the entire system if they perceive it as "guessing" on complex cases.

**2. Delay Cost of Waiting (Until August 30th)**
*   **Opportunity Cost:** Delaying the pilot with three major hospital systems postpones real-world validation and revenue generation. It also cedes first-mover advantage to competitors who may launch with similar "good enough" general capabilities.
*   **Data Ingestion Risk:** Ingesting 5,000 new scans is not a guaranteed fix. While projected to boost accuracy to 88%, this still leaves a 12% error rate. The delay assumes this specific data will solve the generalization problem without introducing bias or overfitting.
*   **Stakeholder Momentum:** Delaying may signal insecurity to the hospital partners, potentially jeopardizing the pilot agreements. Conversely, launching with known gaps without a mitigation strategy could damage long-term credibility.

**3. Liability Implications**
*   **Current State (78% on rare cases):** Launching now exposes Radiant Scan to significant liability if a rare pediatric condition is missed. Without explicit guardrails (e.g., the tool refusing to render a verdict on low-confidence inputs), the company could be held negligent for providing a "second opinion" that is statistically unreliable for specific demographics.
*   **Mitigation Strategy:** Liability is less about the raw accuracy number and more about how the uncertainty is communicated. If the tool clearly states, "Low confidence: Human review required" for rare conditions, the liability shifts back to the radiologist, framing the AI as a triage assistant rather than a diagnostic authority.

**Recommendation**
Do not delay the entire beta for the August 30th data ingestion. Instead, proceed with the pilot under a **strictly scoped deployment**:
1.  **Enable** the tool for common fractures immediately (92% accuracy).
2.  **Disable** or flag low-confidence outputs for rare pediatric conditions until further validation.
3.  **Use the pilot** to test the specific assumption: Do radiologists trust the 92% accuracy enough to integrate it into workflow, and does the "hand-off" for rare cases maintain that trust?

This approach balances market entry with liability management, turning the pilot into a live experiment to validate the adoption threshold rather than guessing it in isolation.

*Note: This analysis assumes the tool can technically differentiate confidence levels between common and rare cases. Legal counsel must review specific disclaimer language before pilot launch.*
