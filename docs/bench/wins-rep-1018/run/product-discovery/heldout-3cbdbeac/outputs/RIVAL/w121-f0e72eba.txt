# Recommendation Report: Q3 Roadmap Decision
**To:** Co-founders, Lead Engineer
**From:** Founder, Gibraltar Fuel Logistics
**Date:** October 26, 2023
**Subject:** Evaluation of Real-Time Monitoring vs. Scheduling Algorithms

### Executive Summary
The evidence from four recent client calls indicates a critical pain point (operational halts due to fuel shortages) but **does not yet strongly support** committing the full $50,000 budget to building a real-time tank level monitoring system. While the problem is validated, the root cause (manual error vs. supply chain) and the technical feasibility on legacy infrastructure remain unproven assumptions. Proceeding directly to full build carries high risk of solving the wrong problem or delivering an incompatible solution.

### Analysis of Current Evidence
1.  **Problem Validation (Strong):** Four independent reports of operational halts confirm high urgency and value potential. The cost of downtime for marina clients likely exceeds the cost of a prevention system.
2.  **Root Cause Assumption (Unverified):** The hypothesis that "manual estimation errors" are the primary driver is untested. If shortages are actually caused by supplier delays or theft, real-time monitoring will provide visibility but not prevent the outage.
3.  **Feasibility Risk (High):** The assumption that sensors can retrofit older tanks without full infrastructure replacement is a critical technical unknown. If retrofitting requires expensive tank modifications, the solution becomes viably impossible for the target market.

### Strategic Recommendation
**Do not greenlight the full build yet.** Instead, allocate a small portion of the Q3 budget ($5,000–$8,000) to a two-week discovery sprint to de-risk the top assumptions before committing to the $50,000 development cycle.

#### Immediate Next Steps (Discovery Sprint)
1.  **Validate Root Cause (Value Risk):**
    *   Conduct "Mom Test" interviews with the four complaining managers. Ask specifically about the last three shortage events: "Walk me through exactly how you estimated levels that week," and "What happened with the supplier delivery schedule?"
    *   *Goal:* Confirm if >70% of shortages stem from estimation errors. If not, pivot to scheduling algorithms or supplier management features.

2.  **Test Technical Feasibility (Feasibility Risk):**
    *   The Lead Engineer must perform a physical audit of one representative "older tank" at a partner marina.
    *   *Goal:* Determine if non-invasive sensors exist that integrate with current telemetry. If retrofitting costs exceed $2,000 per tank, the unit economics fail.

3.  **Evaluate Alternative (Scheduling Algorithms):**
    *   If root causes are mixed, simulate whether better scheduling buffers could have prevented the last four outages without new hardware. This may be a lower-cost, higher-speed solution.

### Decision Matrix for Full Build
Proceed with the $50,000 real-time monitoring build **only if**:
*   Interviews confirm manual estimation is the primary cause (>70% of incidents).
*   Engineering audit confirms retrofitting is technically feasible and costs <15% of a new tank installation.
*   Clients express willingness to pay a premium that covers hardware + subscription within 12 months.

If either condition fails, redirect funds to developing advanced scheduling algorithms or supplier integration tools.

### Conclusion
The four calls validate the *pain*, not the *solution*. Investing $50,000 now is premature. A targeted discovery phase will ensure we solve the actual root cause with a technically viable product, protecting our capital and reputation.
