### Channel Comparison: SMS vs. Mobile App for Rural Kenya

**1. Reach Analysis**
*   **SMS Approach:** Leverages the existing infrastructure of 85% of your target demographic (feature phones). It requires no data plan, bypassing the $2/month data constraint. Reach is theoretically near-universal among your interviewed cohort.
*   **Mobile App Approach:** Immediately excludes 85% of the target audience due to hardware incompatibility. For the remaining 15% with smartphones, high data costs create a significant barrier to consistent usage, likely resulting in low adoption and sporadic engagement.
*   **Verdict:** SMS offers superior reach by an order of magnitude.

**2. Reliability & Logistical Gaps**
*   **SMS Reliability:** While generally robust, SMS delivery is not guaranteed. The critical unknown is network congestion and power instability during the April rainy season. Heavy rains can damage infrastructure or cause power outages affecting cell towers in remote areas, leading to delayed or failed message delivery.
*   **App Reliability:** Dependent on stable 3G/4G data signals, which are often the first to degrade in poor weather or remote locations. Even if the app is installed, the cost of loading content may prevent users from opening it during data-saving modes.
*   **Identified Logistical Gaps:**
    *   **Seasonal Latency:** No data exists on SMS delivery success rates specifically during heavy rains in your target clinics.
    *   **Carrier Variance:** Different mobile network operators (MNOs) may have varying tower resilience in specific remote zones; a "one-size-fits-all" SMS strategy might fail if the dominant local carrier has weak rural coverage.
    *   **Power Dependency:** If patients cannot charge phones due to rain-related power outages, neither channel works, but SMS requires less battery to receive than an app does to sync.

**3. Proposed Two-Week Validation Test**
To validate SMS superiority and quantify delivery risks before the rainy season peaks, run a **"Delivery & Response Audit"**:

*   **Objective:** Measure actual SMS delivery rates and user response latency in target zones under current conditions.
*   **Sample:** Select 50 patients across 3 distinct remote clinics (representing different MNOs).
*   **Procedure:**
    1.  Send 10 standardized SMS messages over 14 days (mix of reminders and simple reply requests).
    2.  Track delivery reports from the SMS gateway provider (success/fail/pendings).
    3.  Conduct brief exit interviews to confirm receipt and timing ("Did you get the message about X? When?").
*   **Success Metric:** >90% confirmed delivery rate and <2 hour average delay.
*   **Cost Estimate:** Well under $500 (leaving the majority of your $15k pilot budget for execution), utilizing existing staff for interviews.

If delivery drops below 85% or delays exceed 24 hours consistently, you must investigate multi-carrier routing or voice-call backups before full deployment.

*Note: Ensure your SMS gateway provider supports local Kenyan routes directly to avoid international routing delays.*
