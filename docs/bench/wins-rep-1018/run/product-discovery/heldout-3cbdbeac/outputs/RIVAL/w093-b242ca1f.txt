### Technical Feasibility & Cost Evaluation: Real-Time Truck Tracking

**Executive Summary**
Implementing a standard real-time GPS dashboard will **not** eliminate communication gaps for trucks operating in deep shafts due to inherent signal blockage. To meet the 60-day deployment target and prevent further $50,000 stoppages, a hybrid architecture combining surface GPS with underground proximity sensors is required.

**1. The Signal Loss Challenge (Feasibility Analysis)**
*   **The Problem:** GPS signals cannot penetrate rock or deep earth. In open-pit areas, standard GPS works. Once trucks enter decline shafts or underground tunnels, connectivity drops to zero.
*   **The Risk:** Relying solely on GPS creates a "black hole" in your data exactly where production stoppages are occurring, giving a false sense of security.
*   **Viability of Alternatives:**
    *   **Mesh Networks (LoRaWAN):** Highly viable. Trucks act as nodes, bouncing signals to one another until reaching a surface gateway. Low power, long-range, works in tunnels.
    *   **Leaky Feeder Cables:** Existing communication cables in mines can often be retrofitted to carry data signals, providing continuous coverage but requiring higher installation effort.
    *   **Wi-Fi 6/Industrial RFID:** Viable for specific choke points (loading/unloading zones) but less effective for continuous tracking along long haul routes.

**2. Proposed Hybrid Architecture**
To achieve full visibility within 60 days, deploy a two-tier system:
*   **Surface/Decline Zone:** Standard cellular/GPS trackers (Low cost, easy install).
*   **Underground Zone:** Install LoRaWAN gateways at shaft heads and equip trucks with dual-mode tags (GPS + LoRa). As trucks descend, they automatically switch to the mesh network.

**3. Cost vs. Implementation Timeline (60-Day Target)**

| Component | Estimated Effort | Cost Driver | Feasibility (60 Days) |
| :--- | :--- | :--- | :--- |
| **Dashboard Software** | 1-2 Weeks | SaaS subscription or custom build | **High** (Off-the-shelf mining logistics platforms exist) |
| **Surface Hardware** | 1 Week | GPS units + SIM cards | **High** (Plug-and-play) |
| **Underground Network** | 4-6 Weeks | Gateway installation, cabling, safety certs | **Medium** (Requires mine safety approval & shutdown windows) |
| **Integration** | 2 Weeks | API connection to existing dispatch | **High** |

**4. Strategic Recommendation**
*   **Do not wait for perfect accuracy.** A system that shows "Last known location: Shaft Entry" is infinitely better than total blindness.
*   **Phase 1 (Days 1-30):** Deploy surface GPS and install gateways at the top of the most critical shafts causing stoppages. This captures entry/exit times and surface bottlenecks immediately.
*   **Phase 2 (Days 31-60):** Expand the mesh network deeper into the active zones as safety permits.
*   **ROI Justification:** With two stoppages costing $100,000 total in one month, even a partial system that prevents one incident pays for the initial hardware deployment.

**Conclusion**
The assumption that "GPS alone" solves the problem is false. However, the broader assumption that "real-time visibility is achievable" is true if you adopt a hybrid mesh approach. The technical feasibility is high provided you engage mine safety officers immediately for underground hardware certification. The cost of implementation is negligible compared to the recurring $50,000 loss per incident.
