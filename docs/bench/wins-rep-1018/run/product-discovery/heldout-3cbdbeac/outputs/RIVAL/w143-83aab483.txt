**Recommendation: Prioritize Process Training and Manual Data Logging Before Building a Digital Tracker**

Based on the pattern of four consecutive failures citing "inconsistent curing status," the evidence suggests a fundamental breakdown in the physical curing process or handling protocols, not necessarily a lack of data visibility. Investing immediately in a complex digital tracker carries high risk and may exacerbate the very bottleneck you fear: slowing down loading processes.

**Reasoning:**

1.  **Root Cause Ambiguity:** Your assumption states that *visibility* ensures compliance. However, visibility does not equal control. If the tobacco is already curing inconsistently before it reaches the loading dock, a tracker will only tell you faster that the product is ruined. It diagnoses the symptom but does not cure the disease. The failures likely stem from inconsistent farm-level practices, improper stacking during transport, or lack of standardized curing times, none of which are solved solely by monitoring.
2.  **Operational Risk:** You explicitly identified the risk that digital complexity could slow loading. In a logistics environment where speed is critical, adding hardware installation, sensor calibration, and data entry steps at the farm or port without first streamlining the underlying workflow is a recipe for reduced throughput. If the tracker delays shipments, you risk missing delivery windows, creating a new category of buyer dissatisfaction alongside quality issues.
3.  **Cost Efficiency:** With $40,000 already lost, capital preservation is vital. Developing, deploying, and maintaining a custom IoT solution requires significant upfront investment and technical overhead. Conversely, process training and manual logging (using simple hygrometers and checklists) are low-cost, immediate interventions.
4.  **Validation Strategy:** Before automating, you must prove that humans can consistently achieve the correct curing status when following a strict protocol. If trained staff using manual tools cannot maintain quality, a digital tool will not fix the behavioral or environmental gaps.

**Proposed Action Plan:**

*   **Phase 1 (Immediate - 4 Weeks):** Implement rigorous process training for farm handlers and loaders focused on standardized curing metrics. Introduce mandatory manual logging of humidity/temperature using inexpensive analog devices at key handover points.
*   **Phase 2 (Validation):** Run one shipment cycle with these manual controls. If rejection rates drop to zero, the issue was process discipline, not data visibility. Do not build the tracker; scale the training.
*   **Phase 3 (Conditional Investment):** Only if manual logging proves too labor-intensive or prone to human error *after* processes are stabilized should you invest in the digital tracker to automate data collection and reduce friction.

**Conclusion:**
Do not build the tracker yet. The pattern of failure indicates a process execution gap, not an information gap. Fix the physical workflow first; automate only after the process is proven stable. This approach protects your cash flow, avoids operational drag, and addresses the root cause of the buyer rejections.

*Note: This strategy shifts capital from unproven technology to immediate operational correction, directly addressing the $40k loss driver.*
*Investors should view this as a de-risking measure that validates the need for technology before committing development funds.*
