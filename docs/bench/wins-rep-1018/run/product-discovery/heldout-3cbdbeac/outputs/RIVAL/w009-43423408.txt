# Decision Brief: Human Oversight for Mental Health Chatbot Launch

**Recommendation:** Delay public launch to implement a hybrid human oversight model. Proceeding with current automated safeguards presents unacceptable legal liability and ethical risk given the confirmed failure rate in high-stakes scenarios.

### Executive Summary
The closed beta data reveals a critical gap in safety protocols. While the volume of flagged conversations (12 out of an estimated ~15,000+ interactions, assuming ~30 chats/user) appears low, the **16.7% failure rate** (2 inadequate responses out of 12 high-risk flags) in life-safety scenarios is statistically significant for a mental health product. The inability to measure false negatives (missed crises) creates an unknown but potentially catastrophic risk exposure that automated systems alone cannot mitigate.

### Key Findings from Beta
*   **Confirmed Failure Mode:** In 2 documented instances, the bot failed to provide adequate support during self-harm disclosures. In a regulated health context, even a single failure can result in severe reputational damage, regulatory scrutiny, and legal action.
*   **The "Unknown Unknown":** We currently have zero visibility into false negatives. Without human review of a random sample of non-flagged conversations, we cannot validate the sensitivity of our detection algorithm. It is highly probable that users in crisis were missed entirely, creating a silent liability.
*   **Economic vs. Ethical Trade-off:** The assumption that human oversight will "destroy unit economics" is untested. While a full 24/7 team is costly, the cost of a single lawsuit or a public relations crisis involving user harm far exceeds the operational expense of a targeted moderation team.

### Risk Assessment
*   **Legal Liability:** Launching with known inadequacies in self-harm response protocols exposes the company to negligence claims. Legal counsel will likely advise against scaling until the failure rate in high-risk categories is near zero.
*   **Reputational Risk:** A public incident involving the chatbot failing a user in crisis could permanently brand the product as unsafe, destroying market trust before it is established.
*   **Strategic Risk:** Delaying launch by 2-3 weeks to build a Minimum Viable Oversight (MVO) team is preferable to a forced shutdown post-launch due to a safety incident.

### Proposed Path Forward: The Hybrid MVO Model
Instead of a full 24/7 internal team immediately, which may strain unit economics, adopt a phased approach:

1.  **Immediate Action (Days 1-7):** Partner with an existing third-party crisis intervention service (e.g., specialized BPOs or non-profit partnerships) to handle flagged interactions during off-hours. This reduces hiring overhead while ensuring 24/7 coverage.
2.  **Validation Sprint (Days 8-20):** Implement human review on a **random 5-10% sample** of non-flagged conversations to estimate the false negative rate. This data is essential for long-term risk modeling.
3.  **Revised Launch Criteria:** Define success not by feature completeness, but by safety metrics (e.g., "100% of high-risk flags reviewed by humans within 2 minutes; false negative rate <0.1%").

### Conclusion
The current beta feedback **does not** provide enough justification to proceed with automated safeguards alone; rather, it provides definitive evidence that they are insufficient for a public launch. The presence of *any* inadequate response to self-harm mandates human intervention.

We recommend delaying the launch by approximately 10-14 days to secure third-party oversight partners and establish the sampling protocol. This delay protects the company from existential liability and aligns with the ethical obligations of a mental health product. The marketing campaign should be adjusted to reflect a "safety-first" rollout, which can be framed positively to stakeholders as a commitment to user well-being.

*Note: This brief assumes the 500 users represent the target demographic accurately; if the beta group was unusually tech-literate or low-risk, the false negative rate in the general population could be higher.*
