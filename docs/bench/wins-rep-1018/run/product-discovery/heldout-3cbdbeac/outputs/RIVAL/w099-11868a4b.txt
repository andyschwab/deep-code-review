### Risk Analysis: In-House QR Code Tracking for Bursa Textile Logistics

**Core Assumption Under Review**
*Assumption:* A custom QR-code system will reduce identification errors by 90% and remain reliable in >75% humidity without frequent failure.

**Critical Pitfalls of an In-House Build**

**1. Feasibility Risk: Environmental Degradation (High Risk)**
*   **The Problem:** Standard paper or synthetic QR labels often delaminate, fade, or become unreadable when exposed to sustained humidity >75%, especially in warehouses with temperature fluctuations.
*   **The Consequence:** If labels degrade within weeks, scanning failure rates will spike. Staff will revert to manual checks to meet production deadlines, rendering the 90% error reduction target impossible and wasting the development investment.
*   **Unknown Variable:** Can your team source or engineer industrial-grade, humidity-resistant labels (e.g., polyester with aggressive adhesive) at a cost that fits your margin? Off-the-shelf solutions may fail; custom material science is likely outside your core competency.

**2. Usability Risk: Operational Friction at Scale**
*   **The Problem:** Handling 4,000 rolls weekly means roughly 570 scans per day (assuming a 6-day week). In a humid environment, workers may wear gloves or deal with wet hands.
*   **The Consequence:** If the scanning hardware or software isn't ruggedized for these specific conditions, the time saved by automation could be lost to troubleshooting failed scans. If a scan fails, does the process halt? A 5-second delay per roll adds up to 47 minutes of daily friction, potentially increasing the "manual check" burden rather than reducing the current 20% staff allocation.

**3. Viability Risk: Maintenance and Total Cost of Ownership**
*   **The Problem:** An in-house solution requires ongoing maintenance, server costs, and hardware replacement.
*   **The Consequence:** With six formal complaints already causing 36-hour delays, the cost of failure is high. If the system fails due to humidity, the reputational damage with your twelve major factories could outweigh the efficiency gains. Building custom means you own the liability for every unreadable label.

**4. Value Risk: Solving the Wrong Problem**
*   **The Problem:** You assume misidentification is the sole cause of missing rolls.
*   **The Consequence:** If rolls are going missing due to theft, loading errors *after* scanning, or data entry mistakes unrelated to the label itself, a QR system will not solve the root cause. You might build a perfect tracking system for a process break that happens elsewhere.

**Recommended Next Steps Before Building**
Do not commit to a full custom build yet. The humidity variable is a "showstopper" risk that must be validated physically, not theoretically.
*   **Test the Label, Not the Software:** Before writing code, expose three different types of industrial QR labels to your warehouse conditions for 30 days. Scan them daily to measure readability decay.
*   **Pilot the Process:** Run a manual pilot using ruggedized handheld scanners and pre-printed durable tags on 500 rolls for one week. Measure if the *process* of scanning slows down operations in high humidity.
*   **Explore Alternatives:** Consider if RFID (which can be encased in plastic and doesn't require line-of-sight) might be more suitable for humid, high-volume textile environments, even if the upfront cost is higher.

**Conclusion**
The evidence currently suggests high risk in a custom in-house build due to the unverified durability of labels in your specific environmental constraints. Validate the physical medium first; the software is secondary.
