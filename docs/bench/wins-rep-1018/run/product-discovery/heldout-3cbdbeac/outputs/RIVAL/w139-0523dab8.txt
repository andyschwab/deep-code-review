### Decision Recommendation: No-Go on Building the Full Scheduler Module

Based strictly on the evidence provided, there is **insufficient justification** to allocate three developer-months to build a dedicated factory pickup scheduler. The current data points to a symptom (missed windows due to visibility gaps) but fails to validate the proposed solution (digital locking of slots) or the willingness of the key constraint holders (factories) to adopt it.

#### Evidence Strength Analysis

**1. The Problem is Real, The Solution is Unproven**
*   **Evidence:** Four delay calls in one month, specifically citing "lack of real-time visibility."
*   **Assessment:** Strong evidence exists that visibility is a pain point for *brands*. However, there is zero evidence that a "digital lock-in" is the required fix. Brands may simply need a status update SMS, a shared spreadsheet, or a manual confirmation call from your team. Assuming a software module is the only fix is a leap not supported by the four calls.

**2. Critical Unknown: Factory Friction (High Risk)**
*   **Evidence:** None. You have 15 factories onboarding, but no data on their administrative capacity or resistance to digital scheduling.
*   **Risk:** If factories view "locking in times digitally" as an administrative burden, they will bypass it, rendering the tool useless. In logistics, the party performing the physical action (pickup) often dictates the process. If they resist, the "coordination gap" widens rather than closes. Building before testing this creates high usability and adoption risk.

**3. Critical Unknown: Brand Priority Depth**
*   **Evidence:** Brands complained about missed windows.
*   **Gap:** You do not know if brands care about *how* the visibility is achieved (the interface) or only the *outcome* (on-time delivery). They might prefer you hire a coordinator to manually chase factories rather than forcing factories into a new software workflow. Investing in a feature they don't value over a service they do is a viability risk.

#### Risk vs. Cost Evaluation

*   **Cost:** 3 developer-months is a massive burn for a startup with only 15 onboarded factories. This represents a significant opportunity cost; those resources could be used to manually solve the problem for all 15 factories for a year.
*   **Risk Category:** This is primarily a **Usability** (will factories use it?) and **Value** (will brands pay for/prefers this specific method?) risk.
*   **Conclusion:** The assumption that "digital locking eliminates breakdowns" is unvalidated. The assumption that "factories will comply" is untested. Proceeding to build is betting the quarter on two unproven hypotheses.

#### Recommended Immediate Actions (Before Building)

Do not build the module. Instead, run a "Concierge Test" for the next 4 weeks:
1.  **Manual Simulation:** Have your team manually call factories to "lock in" times and digitally relay this to brands via a simple email or WhatsApp update.
2.  **Measure Resistance:** Track how many factories refuse or fail to provide a time when asked manually. This validates the friction risk without code.
3.  **Validate Brand Preference:** Ask the next brand that complains: "If we guaranteed you a confirmed pickup time via email by 5 PM the day before, would that solve your issue, or do you need a self-service portal?"

Only if the manual process works smoothly *and* brands explicitly demand a self-service tool should you consider building the module.

---
*The current evidence supports solving the visibility problem, but not via this specific technical solution.*
*Prioritize validating factory compliance manually before writing code.*
