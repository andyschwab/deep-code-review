### Executive Summary: Assembly Video Initiative Evaluation

**Current Cost Baseline**
Based on last month's data, assembly friction generated direct costs of **180 EUR** (10 calls × 18 EUR) plus the shipping costs for three returned units. While this monthly figure appears low, annualized without intervention, this specific friction point could cost approximately **2,160 EUR** in labor alone, excluding return shipping and the lost margin on returned inventory. If these ten calls represent a recurring pattern rather than an anomaly, the cumulative cost of returns and support erodes profitability on these specific SKU lines.

**Assumption Analysis & Risks**
Your primary hypothesis is that video content will reduce support calls by 60%. However, two critical unvalidated assumptions threaten this outcome:
1.  **Format Preference:** You assume the older demographic prefers video over text. If this group finds video difficult to navigate or prefers static diagrams, adoption will be near zero, and call volumes will remain unchanged.
2.  **Connectivity Constraints:** You assume customers have sufficient bandwidth to stream content. If buffering occurs during assembly, frustration may increase, potentially driving *more* support calls rather than fewer.

**Strategic Recommendation**
Do not approve the full production budget for twenty products immediately. The risk of building unused assets for a demographic that may not consume them is high. Instead, adopt a phased validation approach:

1.  **Pilot Test (Low Cost):** Select the **single** wardrobe model with the highest return rate. Produce one high-quality instruction video for this item only.
2.  **Channel Validation:** Before hosting, survey the next five customers who call about assembly issues. Ask: "Would a video link help you right now, and can you watch it on your current connection?" This validates both preference and bandwidth instantly.
3.  **Measure Behavior:** Distribute the video link to customers purchasing or calling about the pilot model. Track if support calls for that specific model drop compared to the previous month.

**Financial Implication**
If the pilot proves successful (achieving even a 40% reduction in calls for that model), the ROI justifies scaling to the top twenty products. If the pilot fails to gain traction, you save the production costs for the remaining nineteen videos and must explore alternative solutions, such as simplified printed diagrams or a dedicated phone support line for assembly.

**Conclusion**
The problem (assembly friction) is real and costly over time. However, the proposed solution (video) relies on unproven behavioral assumptions regarding your specific demographic. Validate the medium with a single-product pilot before committing capital to a full library.
