### Assessment: Evidence Sufficiency for Automation Layer Rebuild

**Verdict:** The current evidence is **insufficient** to justify a full rebuild of the automation layer. While the trend is critical, the data does not yet isolate the root cause, creating a high risk of solving the wrong problem.

**Analysis of Current Evidence**
The ten formal audit complaints and the 48-hour resolution time confirm a severe **Value Risk** (compliance failure) and **Viability Risk** (regulatory exposure). However, these symptoms do not validate the assumption that the *engine* is flawed. The evidence points to two distinct, untested hypotheses:
1.  **Flawed Templates:** The logic within the current engine cannot handle specific regulatory nuances.
2.  **Inconsistent Data Inputs:** Client onboarding forms are providing dirty or non-standardized data that the current engine correctly rejects or mishandles.

Investing in a new engine based solely on error counts assumes Hypothesis 1 is true. If Hypothesis 2 is the actual driver, a new engine will simply ingest bad data faster, failing to reduce audit findings while incurring massive technical debt and cost.

**Critical Data Gaps**
To satisfy the Risk Management Committee and regulators, you must close the following gaps before prioritizing a rebuild:
*   **Root Cause Attribution:** There is no breakdown of the 10 errors by category (e.g., logic error vs. data formatting error). Without this, you cannot determine if the fault lies in the generator or the source.
*   **Input Audit Status:** You explicitly noted that client onboarding forms have "not yet been fully audited." This is the highest probability failure point in cross-border logistics, where client data variability is high.
*   **Error Trend Context:** While the error rate is "trending upward," there is no correlation provided against volume spikes, new client types, or recent regulatory changes.

**Recommended Immediate Actions (Before Rebuild Decision)**
Do not commit to a rebuild yet. Instead, execute a rapid diagnostic sprint (3–5 days) to generate the necessary evidence:
1.  **Forensic Error Analysis:** Manually review the specific data fields and template logic for all 10 failed documents. Tag each failure as "Template Logic Defect" or "Input Data Anomaly."
2.  **Sample Input Audit:** Randomly sample 50 recent client onboarding forms to check for schema deviations against the template requirements.
3.  **Pivot Decision:**
    *   If >70% of errors are **Input Data Anomalies**: Prioritize fixing the onboarding intake validation and client education. Do not rebuild the engine.
    *   If >70% of errors are **Template Logic Defects**: Only then is a rebuild (or significant refactor) justified.

**Conclusion for the Committee**
The evidence supports prioritizing a **root cause investigation**, not a development rebuild. Presenting a plan to "audit the inputs and categorize the last 10 failures" demonstrates rigorous risk management to regulators. Presenting a plan to "rebuild the engine" without this data exposes the firm to the risk of spending budget on a solution that does not fix the compliance gap.

***

*Focus the remediation plan on the diagnostic sprint results; regulators value targeted fixes over broad technological overhauls.*
*Delay any engineering resource allocation until the input vs. template split is quantified.*
