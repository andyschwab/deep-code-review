### Critical Unknowns & Evidence Analysis: Oxygen Supply Chain Tracker

**Current Evidence Summary**
The only confirmed evidence is that two clinics experienced critical oxygen stockouts lasting over six hours due to delayed manual log updates. While this indicates a visibility gap, the root cause of the stockout itself remains unproven. It is unclear if the clinics ran out of oxygen because headquarters didn't know they were low (visibility failure) or because transportation could not reach them in time regardless of notification (logistics failure). The known constraint is that northern region connectivity fails approximately three days per week, which directly contradicts the core assumption that staff can update inventory daily.

**Critical Unknowns to Resolve Before Development**

1.  **Root Cause of Stockouts (Visibility vs. Logistics)**
    *   *Unknown:* Did the two recent stockouts occur because the central team was unaware of low levels, or did they occur because no transport capacity was available even if the need was known?
    *   *Risk:* If the bottleneck is transportation capacity or road access, a real-time tracker will provide accurate data on impending stockouts but will not prevent them.

2.  **Viability of Daily Updates in Low-Connectivity Zones**
    *   *Unknown:* Can clinic staff reliably sync data within a 12-hour window given three days of weekly connectivity failure?
    *   *Risk:* If data is consistently 12+ hours old, the "real-time" tracker offers no advantage over current manual logs, rendering the investment ineffective for preventing rapid stockouts.

3.  **Operational Behavior During Outages**
    *   *Unknown:* What specific workarounds do clinic staff currently use when connectivity fails? Do they have alternative communication channels (e.g., SMS, radio, phone calls) for emergencies?
    *   *Risk:* Building a digital solution may ignore robust, existing analog protocols that are already functioning during outages.

4.  **Value Threshold of Data Latency**
    *   *Unknown:* What is the maximum acceptable data age to trigger a successful replenishment before a stockout occurs?
    *   *Risk:* If the lead time for delivery is 24 hours, data that is 12 hours old is useless. If lead time is 48 hours, it might be sufficient. This threshold defines the product's viability.

5.  **Staff Capacity and Incentive**
    *   *Unknown:* Do clinic staff have the time and motivation to prioritize digital updates over patient care, especially when connectivity is poor and the process is frustrating?
    *   *Risk:* Low adoption rates due to friction could result in incomplete data, leading to false confidence in the system.

**Recommendation for Pilot Design**
Before committing to full development, the August 1st pilot must test these unknowns rather than just deploying software. The pilot should include a control group using existing communication methods to compare stockout rates against sites using the tracker. Additionally, the pilot must simulate connectivity outages to measure actual data latency and its impact on decision-making speed. If the data proves that transportation logistics, not visibility, is the primary constraint, resources should be redirected to fleet management or regional stockpiling rather than software development.

*Note: This analysis highlights that solving the wrong problem (visibility) while ignoring the likely constraint (logistics/connectivity) poses a high risk of project failure.*
