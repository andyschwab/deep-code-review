### Executive Summary: Medication Interaction Checker Proposal

**Recommendation:** Proceed with immediate discovery sprints to validate feasibility and value, but **do not yet commit full engineering budget** or pivot the entire roadmap. The current evidence indicates high potential impact but contains critical unvalidated assumptions regarding data feasibility and actual error reduction.

#### 1. Evidence Strength Analysis
*   **Strong Signal (Value):** 12 out of 15 pharmacists (80%) explicitly requesting this feature is a strong qualitative indicator of *perceived* need. In the healthcare domain, expert users (pharmacists) are high-value proxies for patient safety needs.
*   **Weak Signal (Outcome Validation):** The assumption that the feature will reduce errors by **40% in Q1** is currently unsubstantiated. User *requests* do not guarantee *outcome improvements*. This metric is a hypothesis, not a fact.
*   **Critical Gap (Feasibility):** The uncertainty regarding database licenses and data access costs represents a "showstopper" risk. If the data cannot be accessed legally or affordably, the feature cannot exist regardless of demand.

#### 2. Risk Breakdown (Based on 8-Category Framework)

| Risk Category | Status | Critical Assumption to Validate |
| :--- | :--- | :--- |
| **Value** | 🟡 Moderate | Will an automated checker actually change pharmacist behavior enough to cut errors by 40%, or will they override it due to alert fatigue? |
| **Feasibility** | 🔴 High | Can we access the required drug interaction data within our current licensing agreements? What is the marginal cost per query? |
| **Viability** | 🟡 Moderate | Does the cost of additional data licenses erode the margin enough to make the feature financially unsustainable? |
| **Usability** | 🟡 Moderate | Can we present warnings without disrupting the pharmacist's workflow, causing them to ignore critical alerts? |
| **Ethics/Legal** | 🟠 High | If the system misses an interaction (false negative), what is our liability? Does "automated" imply a guarantee of safety we cannot legally make? |
| **Go-to-Market** | 🟢 Low | Pharmacists are already asking for this; adoption barriers appear low if the tool works. |
| **Strategy** | 🟠 Moderate | Does this pivot distract from the core wellness tracking roadmap enough to lose market share in that segment? |
| **Team** | 🟢 Low | Assuming the team has the capability to integrate complex medical data sets. |

#### 3. Strategic Recommendation for the Board

**Do not approve the full engineering build yet.** Instead, approve a **2-week "Feasibility & Value" Sprint** with the following objectives:

1.  **Legal/Data Audit (Immediate):** Confirm with legal and vendor management if current licenses cover automated interaction checking. Get a hard cost estimate for any required upgrades. *If costs are prohibitive, kill the project immediately.*
2.  **Prototype Test (Wizard of Oz):** Before building backend logic, create a frontend mockup. Have pharmacists test it with simulated data to see if they trust the alerts and if it actually fits their workflow.
3.  **Refine the Success Metric:** Replace the arbitrary "40% reduction" goal with a leading indicator, such as "Pharmacists acknowledge and act on 90% of high-severity alerts during testing."

**Conclusion:** The customer pain is real and urgent (near-miss incidents), justifying high priority. However, the **Feasibility risk (data licensing)** and the **Value risk (actual error reduction vs. alert fatigue)** are too high to bet the entire engineering budget on today. Allocate a small, targeted budget to resolve these two unknowns before committing to a full roadmap pivot.

*Note: This analysis relies solely on the incident reports and interview counts provided; no external market data was consulted.*
