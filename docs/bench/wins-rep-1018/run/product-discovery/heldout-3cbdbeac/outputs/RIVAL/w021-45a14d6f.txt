# Decision Memo: MVP Interface Prioritization for ChronicCare Plus

**To:** Advisory Board
**From:** Founder, ChronicCare Plus
**Date:** October 26, 2023
**Subject:** Recommendation for March 2025 MVP Launch Strategy

### Executive Summary
Based on validated data from 40 patients and 12 endocrinologists, I recommend prioritizing the **Patient-Facing Mobile Interface** for the March 2025 MVP. While the doctor-facing dashboard offers a clearer path to immediate hospital contracts, the core value proposition relies on reducing physician workload by solving patient non-adherence first. Without patient engagement, the doctor dashboard lacks the data necessary to reduce their current overwhelm.

### Evidence Analysis
Our recent discovery phase highlighted two critical friction points:
1.  **Patient Behavior:** 85% of patients forget medication schedules. This is the root cause of poor outcomes and increased clinical interventions.
2.  **Provider Pain Point:** 90% of doctors feel overwhelmed by daily patient messages, often reacting to crises caused by missed medications.

### Trade-Off Analysis

**Option A: Doctor-Facing Dashboard First**
*   *Pros:* Directly addresses the "overwhelmed" sentiment; likely accelerates initial hospital contract signing as administrators see immediate utility for staff.
*   *Cons:* Risks creating an "empty dashboard." If patients continue to forget medications (85% rate), they will generate the same volume of crisis messages. The dashboard would merely organize chaos rather than prevent it. Furthermore, without a dedicated patient tool, retention may suffer, causing hospitals to churn once the novelty wears off.

**Option B: Patient-Facing Mobile Interface First (Recommended)**
*   *Pros:* Directly attacks the root cause (forgetfulness). If successful, this reduces the frequency of crisis events, naturally lowering the volume of messages doctors receive. This validates the long-term hypothesis that empowered patients reduce doctor workload. It builds the necessary habit loop for patient retention.
*   *Cons:* Slower initial sales cycle with hospital administrators who cannot "see" the product in their workflow immediately. Requires a narrative shift to sell "prevention" rather than "management."

### Strategic Rationale
The assumption that "empowering patients reduces doctor workload" cannot be validated if we build the doctor tool first. If we build the dashboard while patients remain disengaged, we confirm neither the reduction in workload nor the efficacy of the platform.

By launching the patient interface, we directly test the 85% forgetfulness metric. Even without direct physician oversight in month one, a tool that successfully reminds patients to take medication delivers immediate value. Success here creates the "quiet" doctors crave, making the future dashboard sale significantly stronger because it will display tangible improvements in patient adherence rather than just message logs.

### Recommendation
Allocate the $250,000 budget to develop a robust, standalone **Patient-Facing Mobile Interface**.

**Next Steps for Advisory Board:**
1.  Refine the sales narrative for hospital administrators to focus on "upstream prevention" metrics rather than "downstream management" tools.
2.  Design a lightweight, automated reporting feature (email/PDF) for doctors as a bridge solution, delaying the full dashboard build until patient engagement metrics are proven.
3.  Initiate a pilot program measuring medication adherence rates over the first 90 days post-launch.

This approach secures long-term viability by ensuring the platform solves the fundamental behavioral problem before layering on complex provider workflows.

*Note: This strategy assumes the mobile solution can function effectively with minimal initial clinician input. A lightweight feedback loop for doctors should be maintained manually during the pilot.*
