Based on the three complaint emails and your current operational context, the evidence **does not yet justify** the full build cost of a real-time port congestion alert system. While the pain point (24-hour delays) is severe and financially critical for your Series A valuation, the current data fails to distinguish between an information problem (solvable by software) and an operational problem (requiring physical intervention).

The complaints cite "unexpected congestion," which aligns with your hypothesis that weekly static data is insufficient. If the root cause were purely information latency, a real-time system would allow exporters to reroute trucks or adjust arrival times, theoretically smoothing the surge. However, the emails do not confirm that the recipients lacked visibility; they may have known about the congestion but had no alternative actions to take. If the delays stem from labor strikes or equipment failures—as you suspected—a real-time alert would only inform customers of bad news faster without reducing the 24-hour bottleneck. In this scenario, the feature addresses a symptom (lack of awareness) rather than the root cause (lack of capacity), offering low value despite high technical cost.

Furthermore, relying on these three emails introduces a significant selection bias. Shipping lines are motivated to blame external factors like "congestion" to justify their own scheduling failures or to pressure port authorities. Without corroborating data showing that earlier notification would have changed behavior (e.g., "If we had known at 8 AM, we would have diverted to Port B"), you cannot validate the **Value** risk. You are currently assuming that "information equals action," which is unproven.

For your co-founder and lead investor, the recommendation is to pause the full build. Instead, frame the current situation as a validated problem (delays are happening) but an unvalidated solution (real-time alerts will fix them). Pitching a feature based on the assumption that information latency is the sole culprit risks building a "nice-to-have" dashboard that does not move the needle on vessel turnaround time. The investor will likely question whether you are solving a coordination failure or merely digitizing a physical bottleneck.

To proceed responsibly before November 30th, you must first prove that the delay is actionable. If the port is physically closed due to a strike, real-time data adds zero operational value. If the port is open but overwhelmed by surges, then your hypothesis holds. Until you isolate the specific cause of these three incidents, committing resources to a real-time architecture is a high-risk gamble that could dilute your Series A narrative rather than strengthen it.

Note: Focus your immediate discovery on determining if earlier knowledge would have allowed any of the three shipping lines to avoid the delay entirely.
