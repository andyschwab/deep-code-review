### Risk Assessment: Legacy Reporting Module Sunset

**Executive Summary**
Deprecating the legacy reporting module by December 1 carries a high risk of immediate revenue loss and significant reputational damage if the assumption of "minimal friction" is incorrect. The current financial logic ($60k annual revenue vs. $60k annual maintenance cost) suggests a break-even scenario, ignoring the potential cost of churn and brand erosion.

**1. Revenue Impact Analysis**
*   **Direct Loss:** If migration fails and both clients churn, DataStream loses **$40,000 in Annual Recurring Revenue (ARR)** immediately.
*   **Net Financial Reality:** While saving $60,000 annually in maintenance costs, the net gain in Year 1 is effectively zero if churn occurs. However, in SaaS, the Lifetime Value (LTV) of these accounts likely exceeds the one-year maintenance savings. Losing them permanently reduces the company's valuation multiple.
*   **Upsell Blockage:** If these clients leave due to forced migration friction, they are unlikely to adopt the new analytics dashboard, eliminating future expansion revenue from these accounts.

**2. Reputation & Strategic Impact**
*   **Trust Erosion:** Forcing a migration that breaks a client's compliance workflow signals that DataStream prioritizes internal efficiency over customer success. In B2B data sectors, news of compliance failures travels fast.
*   **Churn Signal:** If these two accounts leave publicly or share their negative experience, it may trigger anxiety among the other 198 customers regarding future deprecations, potentially increasing overall churn rates.
*   **Sales Friction:** Future enterprise deals often require references or case studies. Losing existing clients due to a botched sunset creates a negative narrative for the sales team.

**3. Critical Unknowns**
*   **Compliance Dependencies:** We do not know if the legacy tool contains unique fields required for regulatory reporting (e.g., specific audit trails, data granularity) that the new dashboard lacks.
*   **Workflow Integration:** The non-responsive client may have the legacy report hard-coded into an automated internal workflow. Breaking this could cause operational failure on their end, leading to immediate escalation.

**4. Immediate Validation Step: The "Compliance Audit" Call**
To validate the assumption that migration is low-friction, execute the following step within 48 hours:

*   **Action:** Bypass email. The Product Manager or Head of Customer Success must place a direct phone call to the primary technical contact at both accounts.
*   **Script Focus:** Do not pitch the new tool. Ask specifically about the *past* and *requirements*:
    *   "We are auditing our legacy reports to ensure no compliance data is lost in our upcoming platform updates. Can you walk us through the last time you exported a report for [Specific Regulation/Audit]? Which specific fields were mandatory for that submission?"
    *   "If a specific field from the old report were missing in a new system, would that block your ability to operate, or is it just inconvenient?"
*   **Success Metric:** A clear "Yes/No" on whether unique legacy fields are legally or operationally mandatory.
*   **Contingency:** If either client confirms hard dependencies, the December 1 deadline must be paused for that account until the new dashboard is patched to include those specific fields.

**Recommendation**
Do not proceed with the hard cutoff on December 1 until the phone calls are completed. The cost of a 30-day delay in engineering time is negligible compared to the certainty of losing $40k ARR and damaging brand trust if a compliance gap exists.

*Note: The non-responsive client requires immediate executive escalation via phone; silence often indicates internal friction or dissatisfaction.*
*Financial models should factor in LTV, not just the current year's maintenance savings, before approving deprecation.*
