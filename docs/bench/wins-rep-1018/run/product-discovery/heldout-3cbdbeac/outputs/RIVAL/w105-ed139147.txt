### Critical Unknowns and Assumption Assessment: Valley Rose Essentials

**Core Assumption Validation**
Your working assumption—that rejections stem solely from inadequate monitoring during the four-hour Sofia airport transfer—is **high-risk and currently unvalidated**. Building a temperature logging module addresses *visibility*, not necessarily *compliance* or *root cause*. If the temperature fluctuations occurred before arrival at Sofia (e.g., during loading in Kazanlak) or if the buyers reject based on the *occurrence* of fluctuation regardless of data proof, a new logging module will not prevent financial loss. Furthermore, if buyers legally require third-party certificates rather than proprietary digital logs, the solution fails the viability test entirely.

**Critical Unknowns (Risk Categories)**

1.  **Value & Usability (Buyer Acceptance)**
    *   *Unknown:* Will Paris/Grasse buyers accept proprietary digital logs as legally binding proof to override contract clauses, or do they mandate independent third-party certification?
    *   *Unknown:* Is the rejection driven by the *temperature fluctuation itself* (product damage) or the *lack of data* (administrative breach)? If the oil is chemically altered by heat, data logs won't fix the quality issue.

2.  **Feasibility & Root Cause (Supply Chain Reality)**
    *   *Unknown:* Did the temperature excursions actually happen in the four-hour Sofia window, or during the initial transport from Kazanlak?
    *   *Unknown:* Are current shipping partners willing to integrate a new proprietary module, or do they already use standardized systems (e.g., Cool Chain data loggers) that you are not accessing?

3.  **Viability (ROI & Cost)**
    *   *Unknown:* What is the development and maintenance cost of the module versus the cost of purchasing off-the-shelf IoT sensors or third-party audit services?
    *   *Unknown:* If the module prevents only one type of rejection but not others, does the ROI justify the build? (Current exposure: 30,000 EUR lost).

4.  **Go-to-Market & Strategy**
    *   *Unknown:* Are competitors solving this with simple third-party certificates? If so, building custom software may be a strategic over-engineering error.
    *   *Unknown:* Could this be a pretext for buyers to renegotiate prices or switch suppliers, using "temperature fluctuation" as a convenient contractual exit?

**Recommended Next Steps for Validation**
Before funding development, you must validate the **legal admissibility** of digital logs with your buyers and confirm the **exact location/timing** of the temperature spikes. Do not build software until you confirm that (A) the buyers will accept your data as proof, and (B) the problem is actually a lack of data rather than a failure of insulation or handling.

**Immediate Action Plan**
1.  **Interview Buyers:** Ask specifically: "If we provide real-time digital temperature logs from our own devices, will you accept this as proof of compliance in lieu of a third-party certificate?"
2.  **Audit the Chain:** Run a pilot shipment with a cheap, independent third-party data logger to pinpoint exactly *where* and *when* the temperature shifts occur.
3.  **Cost-Benefit Analysis:** Compare the cost of building a custom module against buying existing enterprise cold-chain monitoring services.
