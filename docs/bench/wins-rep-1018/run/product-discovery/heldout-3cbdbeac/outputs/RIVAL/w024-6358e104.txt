### Decision Framework: Build vs. Validate for TrialMatch Recruitment Module

**Recommendation: Do Not Proceed with Full Build Immediately**
The current evidence represents strong *interest* but unvalidated *commitment*. Investing $280,000 and four months of total engineering capacity based on verbal requests without Letters of Intent (LOI) violates core discovery principles. The risk of building a feature that fails to close the deal is too high given the "all-in" resource requirement.

**1. Evidence Strength Assessment**
*   **Strong Signals:** Three major institutions requested the feature; potential value is clear ($4M pipeline); current pain is quantified (20 hours/study).
*   **Critical Gaps:** No written commitment (LOI); unknown dependency hierarchy (do they need compliance tools first?); unproven conversion logic (will this specific feature trigger a multi-year signature?).
*   **Verdict:** The feedback validates the *problem* (recruitment is slow) but not the *solution's ability to drive revenue*.

**2. Risk Analysis (Impact × Confidence)**
*   **Value Risk (High):** If the feature reduces time by only 30% instead of 60%, or if hospitals prioritize compliance over speed, the $4M pipeline may not materialize.
*   **Viability Risk (Critical):** Spending 100% of engineering capacity for 4 months creates opportunity cost. If the deal falls through, the company has no bandwidth for other revenue-generating work.
*   **Confidence Level:** Low (<40%). You are assuming the feature is the sole deciding factor for contract signing without data.

**3. Proposed Action Plan: The "Pre-Sale" Experiment**
Instead of building the full module, execute a low-fidelity test to secure commitment before writing code.

*   **Step A: Secure Conditional LOIs (Week 1-2)**
    *   Return to the three hospitals with a specific proposal: "We are prioritizing this feature for Q3. If we deliver a beta that reduces recruitment time by 50%, will you sign a 2-year contract amendment?"
    *   **Success Metric:** 2 out of 3 hospitals sign a non-binding LOI contingent on feature delivery.
    *   **Failure Signal:** Hesitation, requests for "more features first," or refusal to put terms in writing.

*   **Step B: Concierge MVP (Week 3-8)**
    *   Do not automate yet. Manually perform the recruitment matching for *one* pilot study using internal scripts or manual labor, but present the output as if it came from the new system.
    *   Measure the actual time savings and gather feedback on the *result*, not the tool.
    *   **Goal:** Validate the "60% reduction" assumption with real data before automating.

*   **Step C: Phased Build (Month 3-4)**
    *   If LOIs are signed and the Concierge MVP proves the time savings, build only the core matching algorithm. Delay secondary features (reporting, integrations) until the first contract is executed.

**4. Final Decision Matrix**
| Scenario | Action |
| :--- | :--- |
| **Hospitals sign LOIs + Concierge proves 60% savings** | **Proceed with Build.** Risk is mitigated by committed revenue. |
| **Hospitals refuse LOIs but claim "high interest"** | **Stop.** Interest without financial commitment is noise. Re-negotiate scope. |
| **Hospitals say "We need compliance tools first"** | **Pivot.** Shift engineering focus to compliance; recruitment is a secondary blocker. |

**Next Steps for CEO/Head of Sales:** Pause development scheduling. Draft the conditional LOI immediately and present it to the hospital stakeholders as the gateway to unlocking the feature.

*Note: This approach shifts the risk from "building hope" to "validating revenue."*
*If the hospitals walk away from an LOI conversation, you saved $280k and 4 months of runway.*
