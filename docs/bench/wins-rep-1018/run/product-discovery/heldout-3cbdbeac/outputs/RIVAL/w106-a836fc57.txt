### Strategic Evaluation: Marina Booking Integration

**Recommendation:** Proceed with a **feasibility-first discovery phase** before committing to full development. Do not build the integration yet. The primary blocker is not your team's ability to code, but the external dependency on marina API access. Building without confirmed access guarantees failure.

#### 1. Validity of Efficiency Assumptions
Your assumption that automation will reduce scheduling conflicts by **75%** is plausible but unverified.
*   **The Risk:** This assumes 100% data accuracy from marina systems and immediate synchronization. If marina systems update slowly (e.g., batch processing overnight) or if manual overrides occur at the marina desk that aren't digitized, your system will still show conflicts.
*   **The Reality Check:** Manual reconciliation currently takes 10 hours/week. Even if you only reduce errors by 40% but save 8 hours of labor, the ROI is positive given the €25,000 loss last season. However, expecting a 75% reduction in *conflicts* specifically relies entirely on the marinas' data hygiene, which you cannot control.
*   **Action:** Validate this by auditing the *cause* of the last season's 25,000 EUR in claims. Were they caused by timing lags, human entry error, or system outages? If the root cause is human error at the marina level, your integration won't fix it.

#### 2. Dependency Risks (Critical Path)
The project faces a **High-Impact, High-Risk** dependency on external legacy systems.
*   **API Availability:** You identified uncertainty regarding API access for the five major marinas in Split and Dubrovnik. Legacy marina systems often lack public APIs entirely.
*   **The "No-API" Scenario:** If APIs are unavailable, your engineering team would need to build fragile scrapers or rely on manual CSV uploads, which reintroduces the very errors you aim to solve. This shifts the risk from "integration complexity" to "data accessibility."
*   **Political/Commercial Barriers:** Even if technically possible, marinas may charge prohibitive fees for data access or refuse integration to maintain their own leverage over charter operators.

#### 3. Strategic Path Forward
Do not wait for industry standards to shift; the €25,000 loss indicates the cost of inaction is too high. However, do not build the full product yet.

**Immediate Next Steps (2-Week Sprint):**
1.  **Technical Probe:** Have your lead engineer contact the IT managers of the 5 target marinas immediately. Ask specifically: "Do you have an API? If not, can you export real-time data via SFTP/CSV?" Get a yes/no answer in writing.
2.  **Value Validation:** Present a mockup of the automated flow to the three funding charter companies. Confirm they are willing to fund the project *even if* the error reduction is only 40% but saves 8+ hours of admin time weekly.
3.  **Pilot Agreement:** Secure a signed letter of intent from one marina and one charter operator to pilot a low-fidelity version (e.g., semi-automated data transfer) before scaling.

**Decision Matrix:**
*   **If >3 marinas have APIs:** Proceed to MVP build. The risk is manageable.
*   **If 0-1 marinas have APIs:** Pause development. Invest in lobbying the marina association for a standard or explore a "manual-entry portal" for marina staff instead of direct system integration.
*   **If Marinas demand high fees:** Re-calculate ROI. If the fee exceeds the €25,000 risk exposure, the business case fails.

**Conclusion:** The efficiency gain is likely real, but the technical dependency is the showstopper. Validate the *existence* of data access before writing a single line of integration code.

*Note: This evaluation assumes the €25,000 loss figure is accurate and attributable to scheduling conflicts. Verify this baseline before presenting to founders.*
