### Executive Summary: Insufficient Evidence for Development

The three incident reports regarding fuel budget overruns do **not** provide sufficient customer evidence to justify building a real-time inventory map feature. While the data confirms a financial problem (18% overruns), it fails to validate your specific hypothesis that "lack of visibility" is the root cause.

Approving this feature based on current information violates the core principle of product discovery: distinguishing between a known problem and an unverified solution. The reports indicate *what* happened (cost overruns) but not *why* it happened.

### Critical Gaps in Current Evidence

Your assumption rests on a binary choice: either captains lack visibility, or they have poor planning habits. However, the three reports do not rule out several other high-probability causes that a software feature cannot fix:

1.  **Operational Constraints:** Captains may be forced into spot purchases due to unexpected itinerary changes mandated by clients, regardless of inventory visibility.
2.  **Contract Limitations:** The "fixed contract rates" may have exclusions (e.g., specific locations, minimum volumes, or time-of-day restrictions) that force captains onto the spot market even when they plan correctly.
3.  **Process Friction:** Captains might see the inventory but find the ordering portal too slow or complex to use while managing vessel operations, leading them to call suppliers directly at higher rates.
4.  **Data Latency:** The issue might not be "real-time" visibility, but rather inaccurate data in the current system, causing captains to distrust the portal entirely.

Building a real-time map addresses only one of these potential root causes. If the true driver is client-mandated itinerary changes or contract exclusions, the new feature will incur engineering costs without reducing the 18% overrun.

### Required Next Steps Before Q1 Budget Approval

To satisfy the CFO's requirement for "proven demand" and guide the engineering team effectively, you must validate the root cause before December 5th. Do not commit budget to development yet. Instead, execute the following low-effort discovery actions:

*   **Conduct Targeted Interviews:** Speak directly with the three captains who submitted reports. Ask specific questions about their last emergency top-up:
    *   "Walk me through the exact moment you realized you needed fuel. What did you check first?"
    *   "Did you look at the portal? If yes, what did you see? If no, why not?"
    *   "What prevented you from using the fixed-rate contract in that specific instance?"
*   **Analyze Usage Logs:** Review the digital portal logs for those three vessels during the incident windows. Did they log in? Did they view inventory pages? Did they abandon a transaction?
*   **Shadow a Refuel Event:** If possible, observe a captain managing a fuel stop to see their decision-making process in real time.

### Recommendation

**Defer the decision to build.** Present the CFO with a plan to spend the next two weeks validating the root cause through the steps above. If interviews confirm that captains consistently fail to purchase because they cannot *see* fuel availability, then the business case for the map feature becomes strong. If the data reveals the issue is behavioral, contractual, or operational, the engineering team can avoid building a feature that solves the wrong problem.

Three data points confirm a symptom; they do not diagnose the disease. Validate the diagnosis before prescribing the cure.
