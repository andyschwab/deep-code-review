### Validation Plan: Inventory Alert System Trust & Value

**Objective:** Determine if the automated alert system delivers net time savings by overcoming the "trust gap" with internal logistics teams at MedCore and HealthPlus before committing engineering resources.

**1. Quantify the "Trust Tax" (Qualitative Discovery)**
Before building, validate the assumption that manual verification negates time savings. Conduct targeted interviews with the specific logistics managers at MedCore and HealthPlus who currently handle antibiotic stockouts.
*   **Ask about past behavior:** "Walk me through the last time you received an automated inventory warning from a vendor. What specific steps did you take to verify it before acting?"
*   **Identify friction:** "What was the hardest part about trusting that data? Have you ever acted on an alert that turned out to be wrong? What were the consequences?"
*   **Goal:** Uncover the specific conditions required for them to bypass manual verification. If they require three layers of approval regardless of the source, the alert system may not reduce the 12-hour delay.

**2. Test Willingness to Delegate (Low-Fidelity Experiment)**
Do not build the algorithm yet. Simulate the output to test the "trust" variable directly.
*   **Method:** Manually generate 5–10 hypothetical "3-day depletion" alerts based on historical data for a single pilot unit within one hospital chain.
*   **Action:** Present these to the procurement head and ask: "If you received this today, would you authorize the reorder immediately, or what specific check would you perform first?"
*   **Success Metric:** If they cannot articulate a path to acting without manual verification, the risk is too high. If they agree to act on 4 out of 5 simulated alerts, the trust barrier is lower than assumed.

**3. Validate the 80% Resolution Assumption (Data Sanity Check)**
Your assumption is that alerts solve 80% of cases. Verify if the root cause of the 40 incidents was actually *late detection* or *supply chain inability*.
*   **Analysis:** Review the 40 incidents from last month. How many occurred because the hospital didn't know they were running low versus knowing but being unable to get stock due to external shortages or budget freezes?
*   **Risk:** If 50% of stockouts were due to supplier unavailability rather than late ordering, an alert system solves far less than 80% of the problem, drastically reducing the Value risk.

**4. Define the "Minimum Trust" Threshold**
Work with the engineering lead to estimate the effort required to achieve "explainable AI" features (e.g., showing the calculation behind the alert) versus a simple notification.
*   **Decision Point:** If logistics teams demand full transparency to trust the alert, does the development effort still fit within the sprint capacity? If the cost to build "trust features" exceeds the value of saving 12 hours on only the preventable incidents, the project may fail the Viability test.

**Recommendation for Steering Committee:**
Do not commit to full build yet. Allocate 3–5 days for the "Simulated Alert" test with one logistics manager. If they refuse to act on the simulated data without extensive verification, pivot to solving the trust interface first or reject the feature. If they act, proceed to a limited technical spike.

*Note: Focus strictly on the "trust" variable as it is the single point of failure for your time-saving hypothesis.*
*Ensure the pilot includes only one hospital unit to limit exposure if the algorithm proves inaccurate.*
