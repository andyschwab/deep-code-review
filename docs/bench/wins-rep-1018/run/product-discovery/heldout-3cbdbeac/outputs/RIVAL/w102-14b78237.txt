# Technical Viability Analysis: Inline Digital Scales for Cork Weight Verification

## Executive Summary
The proposal to install inline digital scales at Lusocork Transport's loading docks aims to resolve high-value weight disputes ($15,000 contested) and reduce manual bottlenecks (10 minutes/truck). However, the specific environmental condition of 40mg/m³ cork dust presents a critical feasibility risk that may negate the benefits of automation if not mitigated.

## Problem Context
- **Current Pain Point:** 1,200 kg of unaccounted product and three formal disputes from EU buyers in one month.
- **Operational Bottleneck:** Manual weighing adds 10 minutes per truck, constraining peak season throughput.
- **Proposed Solution:** Inline digital scales providing immutable point-of-origin data.
- **Critical Constraint:** Ambient cork dust density averages 40mg/m³.

## Feasibility Assessment: The Dust Interference Risk
Standard industrial digital scales (load cells) are highly sensitive to particulate accumulation. In an environment with 40mg/m³ of cork dust, three specific failure modes are probable:

1.  **Mechanical Binding:** Dust accumulation in the gap between the scale platform and the frame can cause "hanging" or binding, leading to significant reading errors (drift) rather than minor fluctuations.
2.  **Tare Drift:** Continuous dust settling on the scale deck when empty will shift the zero point. Without frequent manual cleaning, the system will accumulate error over every weighing cycle.
3.  **Sensor Contamination:** Dust infiltration into load cell housings can corrode connections or alter strain gauge performance over time.

**Verdict:** Off-the-shelf inline scales without specific environmental hardening are **not viable** in the current dust conditions. They risk introducing *more* variance than the current manual process, potentially worsening dispute resolution with unreliable digital data.

## Recommended Path Forward

### Option A: Mitigated Build (Recommended for Testing)
Proceed with a pilot only if the following engineering controls are implemented:
- **IP69K Rating:** Scales must be fully sealed against high-pressure washdown and dust ingress.
- **Positive Pressure Purge:** Install an air-knife system or positive pressure enclosure around the load cells to actively blow dust away from sensitive gaps.
- **Auto-Tare Logic:** Software must include frequent, automated re-zeroing cycles triggered by time or truck absence to compensate for minor settling.
- **Protective Bellows:** Install flexible, dust-proof bellows around the platform perimeter.

### Option B: Process Hybrid (Immediate Interim Step)
Before capital expenditure on hardware:
- Retain manual weighing but digitize the *recording* process immediately.
- Use ruggedized tablets with photo-capture of the analog scale reading at the moment of weighing.
- This creates an immutable digital trail (timestamped photos) without exposing sensitive electronics to dust, addressing the dispute root cause (lack of proof) without the technical risk.

## Decision Criteria for Plant Manager & QA Lead
1.  **Can we secure IP69K-rated equipment within budget?** If no, reject the inline build.
2.  **Is maintenance bandwidth available?** Dust mitigation requires daily cleaning schedules; if operations cannot commit to this, the system will fail within weeks.
3.  **Does the pilot data show <0.5% variance?** Run a 48-hour test with a protected scale. If dust causes drift exceeding 0.5% of bale weight, the technology is not ready for this environment.

## Conclusion
The hypothesis that inline scales will eliminate disputes is valid *only if* the dust interference is engineered out. The evidence suggests that a standard installation will fail. We recommend a **Hybrid Approach**: immediately digitize manual records to stop the bleeding on disputes, while running a strictly controlled, hardened pilot of inline scales to validate long-term viability.

---
*Note: This analysis assumes standard industrial load cell sensitivity; specific vendor tolerances for 40mg/m³ particulate matter must be verified during procurement.*
*Immediate action should focus on creating immutable records via the current manual process to address the active $15k disputes.*
