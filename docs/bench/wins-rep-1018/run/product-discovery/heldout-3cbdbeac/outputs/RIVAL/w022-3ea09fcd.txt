### Executive Summary: Voice Interface Viability Assessment

**Recommendation:** Proceed with a targeted feasibility experiment immediately; do not commit to full development yet. The potential value is high, but the technical risk in the specific operating room (OR) environment is the single point of failure.

**1. Value Proposition & Market Signal**
The qualitative data from 18 surgeons at Mass General and Brigham indicates strong **Value Risk** mitigation.
*   **Pain Point:** 83% (15/18) explicitly identified typing between procedures as a frustration.
*   **Quantifiable Benefit:** Estimated savings of 12 minutes per surgery. Assuming an average of 3 surgeries per day per surgeon, this recovers 36 minutes daily. Across a typical department, this translates to significant capacity recovery or reduced burnout.
*   **Adoption Driver:** The primary barrier is not desire, but utility. Surgeons will not adopt a tool that requires correction time.

**2. Critical Risk Analysis**
Your core assumption—that adoption hinges on >95% accuracy in noisy environments—is the **Feasibility Risk** anchor.
*   **The Gap:** Off-the-shelf models often struggle with OR-specific noise (suction, alarms, overlapping dialogue) and medical terminology. If accuracy drops to 85%, the time spent correcting errors may exceed the 12-minute savings, resulting in net negative value.
*   **Financial Exposure:** A $320,000 investment with a six-month timeline is substantial. If the error rate proves unmanageable post-development, the entire investment is sunk with zero adoption.
*   **Timeline Constraint:** With a hard deadline of November 30 for the board presentation, a six-month full build is impossible if starting today. The board expects a *prototype* demonstrating viability, not a finished product.

**3. Strategic Path Forward**
To justify the capital expenditure to the CFO and satisfy the VP of Engineering's technical concerns, you must validate the accuracy assumption before writing production code.

**Proposed Action Plan:**
1.  **Run a "Wizard of Oz" or Limited Tech Spike (2 Weeks):**
    *   Record audio from 5 live surgeries (with consent) to capture actual noise profiles.
    *   Test top 3 off-the-shelf voice engines against these recordings specifically for medical terminology and noise cancellation.
    *   **Success Metric:** Achieve >95% transcription accuracy on the test set without manual tuning.
2.  **Prototype for the Board (November 30):**
    *   If the tech spike succeeds: Build a narrow-scope prototype handling only 3-5 common commands to demonstrate the *potential* workflow.
    *   If the tech spike fails: Present the data to the board showing *why* the risk is too high, saving the $320k. This is still a successful outcome as it prevents wasted capital.

**Conclusion**
The time savings justify the investment *only if* the technical hurdle is cleared. The current uncertainty makes a full build irresponsible. Use the remaining time before November 30 to de-risk the accuracy assumption through rapid testing rather than full development.
