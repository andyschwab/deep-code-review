### Executive Brief: MedFlow SA Vaccine Module Decision

**Recommendation:** Proceed with a **low-fidelity prototype** immediately, but defer full engineering resource allocation until the December tender release. Do not pull critical resources from the core inventory system yet.

#### 1. The Opportunity (Building Early)
*   **Validated Pain Point:** Interviews with 14 logistics coordinators confirm a critical problem: manual logs cause a 20% spoilage rate. This is a high-value problem worth solving regardless of the specific tender outcome.
*   **First-Mover Advantage:** With the RFP hint released in October and a Q2 2025 deadline, a six-week prototype positions MedFlow to demonstrate capability during the tender evaluation phase, potentially influencing the final requirements.
*   **Risk Mitigation:** Building a prototype now validates the **Feasibility** (can we integrate sensors?) and **Usability** (will rural coordinators use real-time alerts?) before the budget is locked.

#### 2. The Risks of Building Early
*   **Viability Risk (Budget Mismatch):** Your primary assumption is that the tender mandates real-time alerts. However, if the budget only covers software (using existing cheap loggers) rather than new hardware sensors, a hardware-heavy prototype becomes wasted effort.
*   **Opportunity Cost:** Diverting engineering capacity from the core inventory system creates immediate delivery delays. If the vaccine module does not win the tender, you have incurred technical debt and delayed core product roadmap items for no return.
*   **Strategy Risk:** Government tenders often change scope between the "hint" and final release. Building to the October hint may result in a solution that doesn't match the December requirements.

#### 3. The Risks of Waiting
*   **Time-to-Market:** If the tender requires a live pilot or proof of concept by Q1 2025, waiting until December to start building (6 weeks dev + testing) pushes you to February 2025, missing the Q2 deployment window.
*   **Competitive Entry:** Competitors who start prototyping now may secure pilot sites in Gauteng or Eastern Cape, creating a barrier to entry before the tender is officially awarded.

#### 4. Strategic Path Forward
Adopt a **"Hybrid Discovery"** approach to balance risk and speed:

1.  **Immediate Action (Weeks 1-2):** Conduct a **concierge test**. Manually simulate real-time alerts for 2 of the 14 interviewed coordinators using existing low-cost data loggers and a simple dashboard. This tests the **Value** and **Usability** assumptions without writing complex code or buying hardware.
2.  **Prototype Scope (Weeks 3-6):** Build a **software-only** prototype that ingests data from generic Bluetooth sensors. Avoid custom hardware development. This addresses the risk that the budget may not cover proprietary sensors.
3.  **Resource Allocation:** Assign only 1 engineer and 1 designer to this track. Do not disrupt the core inventory team.
4.  **Decision Gate (December):** Upon tender release, evaluate the budget specifics.
    *   *If hardware is funded:* Scale the team and integrate specific sensors.
    *   *If software only:* You already have a compatible prototype ready to deploy.
    *   *If the tender is cancelled/delayed:* You have validated the 20% spoilage problem and can pivot the solution to private sector clinics with minimal loss.

**Conclusion:** The evidence supports the *problem* (spoilage), but the *solution constraints* (hardware budget) remain unknown. A lightweight prototype de-risks the technical approach without jeopardizing core commitments, keeping MedFlow agile for the December decision.

*Note: Ensure the concierge test includes rural connectivity scenarios, as this is often the hidden failure point in South African rural deployments.*
