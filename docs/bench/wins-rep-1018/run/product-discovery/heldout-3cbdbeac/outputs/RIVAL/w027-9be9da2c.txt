### Viability Evaluation: Dynamic Same-Day Matching for HomeHelp

**Executive Summary**
The hypothesis that dynamic, same-day matching will increase worker utilization from 60% to 85% is strongly supported by user preference data (87.5% of interviewed aides prefer short-notice shifts). However, the **viability** of this feature is critically threatened by infrastructure risks specific to Manila's monsoon season. While the *value* proposition is clear, the *feasibility* and *reliability* required to achieve the 85% target are currently unproven under worst-case connectivity scenarios.

**Evidence Gaps: Infrastructure Stability**
Your current data identifies a risk (3-day outage last November) but lacks the granularity required to engineer a resilient solution. The following specific gaps prevent a definitive go/no-go decision:

1.  **Frequency vs. Anomaly:** It is unknown if the three-day outage in November was a singular extreme event or part of a recurring pattern during monsoon months. Without historical uptime data specific to the target neighborhoods during rainy seasons, we cannot calculate the expected availability window for real-time matching.
2.  **Granular Connectivity Loss:** We do not know if "outage" means a total loss of all cellular data or merely a degradation of speed. Real-time matching might fail on 4G but succeed on 2G/SMS-based protocols. The current evidence treats connectivity as binary (on/off) rather than a spectrum of bandwidth availability.
3.  **User Mitigation Behaviors:** There is no data on how the 20% of affected users currently cope with outages. Do they switch to competitor apps, rely on SMS, travel to Wi-Fi zones, or simply stop working? If they have established offline workarounds, a purely online real-time system may fail to displace existing behaviors.
4.  **Device Heterogeneity:** The evaluation lacks data on the specific device models and OS versions used by the most vulnerable 20%. Older devices may struggle to reconnect quickly after a drop, increasing the "time-to-match" and negating the efficiency gains of same-day scheduling.

**Impact on Utilization Goals**
The proposed jump to 85% utilization assumes near-perfect matching efficiency. Infrastructure instability directly erodes this target through two mechanisms:

*   **Matching Latency:** If real-time matching fails due to connection drops, the time required to secure a shift increases. If an aide spends 30 minutes trying to connect rather than working, their effective utilization drops, potentially keeping the aggregate metric closer to the 60% baseline.
*   **Exclusion of the Vulnerable 20%:** If the system requires stable real-time data and 20% of the workforce cannot guarantee this during monsoons, the theoretical maximum utilization for the *entire* pool drops mathematically. Even if the connected 80% reach 100% utilization, the network average caps at 80% unless the disconnected segment is served via an alternative channel (e.g., SMS booking or voice calls).

**Recommendation for Next Steps**
Before committing engineering resources to a real-time architecture, the team must validate connectivity resilience. Conduct a "connectivity audit" survey with the 40 interviewed aides focusing specifically on monsoon-month data reliability and device types. Simultaneously, prototype a low-bandwidth fallback mechanism (such as SMS-based shift claims) to ensure the 20% at risk are not excluded, which is essential to hitting the 85% aggregate utilization target.

*Note: This evaluation relies solely on the provided interview count and outage anecdote; no external weather or telecom data was accessed.*
*Immediate action should focus on quantifying the "20% at risk" rather than refining the matching algorithm.*
