# Feasibility Brief: Parts Tracking Module Assessment

**To:** Operations Director, Head of Software Development
**From:** Product Manager, Isle of Man Racing Logistics
**Date:** September 12, 2024
**Subject:** Evaluation of Proposed Barcode Tracking Module Based on Q3 Incident Reports

### Executive Summary
The three formal incident reports regarding lost shipments during the last racing season highlight a critical operational failure affecting pit stop preparations. However, these reports alone **do not constitute sufficient evidence** to greenlight the development of a dedicated barcode tracking module. While the symptoms (lost parts) are clear, the root cause remains unverified. Proceeding directly to development risks building a solution that addresses a visibility problem when the actual issue may be procedural non-compliance or training gaps.

### Problem Analysis
The current hypothesis assumes that "lack of visibility" is the primary driver of lost parts. The proposed solution is a barcode-based system integrated into the existing mobile app.

**Critical Gaps in Current Evidence:**
1.  **Root Cause Ambiguity:** The reports confirm parts were lost, but they do not explain *how* they were lost. Was the part never logged? Was it logged but placed in the wrong zone? Was it scanned but the scanner failed? Or was the procedure simply ignored due to time pressure?
2.  **Operational Feasibility Unknown:** The chaotic nature of a race weekend introduces a significant variable. We currently have no data on whether staff can realistically scan every component without creating bottlenecks in pit preparation. If the scanning process adds even 30 seconds per critical component, the solution may cause more delays than the lost parts themselves.
3.  **Alternative Explanations:** The possibility that inadequate staff training on *current* procedures is the root cause has not been ruled out. If staff are not following existing manual logs, adding a digital layer may not solve the behavioral issue.

### Recommendation: Do Not Greenlight Development Yet
We cannot validate the assumption that a barcode system will eliminate losses without first confirming that staff have the capacity and discipline to use it in a live race environment. Building the feature now assumes the solution fits the context, which is our highest risk.

### Proposed Next Steps (Before September 20th Planning Meeting)
To make an informed decision for the winter development cycle, we must execute the following low-effort discovery activities immediately:

1.  **Contextual Inquiry (Days 1-3):** Interview the three team managers who filed reports. Focus on the specific moments the loss was discovered. Ask: "Walk me through the last time a part went missing. What was happening around you? Who was involved? What was the standard procedure supposed to be?"
2.  **Shadowing Observation (Next Race/Simulation):** Observe the pit crew during a high-pressure setup. Measure the time taken for current inventory checks. Note where shortcuts are taken.
3.  **Paper Prototype Test:** Before coding, simulate the barcode process using printed QR codes and a generic scanner app during a practice session. Measure the time impact and observe if staff skip steps when under pressure.

### Decision Criteria for September 20th
We will be ready to greenlight development only if:
*   Observations confirm that parts are lost due to lack of real-time location data, not procedural ignorance.
*   The simulated scanning process adds less than 5% to the total setup time.
*   Staff indicate willingness to adopt the new workflow over current habits.

If these conditions are not met, the investment should shift to staff training and process refinement rather than software development.

***

*This brief prioritizes validating the operational context before committing engineering resources. Immediate field observation is required to replace assumptions with behavioral data.*
