### Recommendation: Pause Full Rollout; Execute Targeted Noise Experiment

**Decision:** Do not proceed with a limited rollout yet. The current evidence indicates a critical **Viability** and **Value** risk that could render the product unusable in its primary context (high-acuity trauma). However, do not fully pause all development. Instead, immediately reallocate the two requested data scientists to a focused, two-week experiment to validate the noise threshold before the funding review.

**Rationale Based on Current Evidence:**
1.  **Context Mismatch:** The pilot success (40% speed increase) was achieved in "quiet" conditions, which do not represent the core use case of an ER during codes or traumas.
2.  **Confirmed Failure Signal:** The 12 instances of manual correction during busy periods are qualitative proof that the system fails under load. In emergency medicine, transcription errors are not just inconveniences; they are patient safety risks. If accuracy drops below 80%, the time spent correcting errors may negate the 40% speed gain, resulting in net negative value.
3.  **Data Gap:** You explicitly lack data on error rates above 70 decibels. Making a go/no-go decision on a rollout without this data is reckless.

**Proposed Action Plan (2-Week Sprint):**

**Objective:** Quantify the relationship between decibel levels and transcription accuracy to determine if the current algorithm is salvageable or requires a fundamental rebuild.

**Experiment Design:**
*   **Setup:** Simulate ER noise environments (70–90+ decibels) using recorded trauma bay audio overlaid with standard dictation samples.
*   **Task:** Run the current prototype against 50 distinct medical dictation samples at varying noise levels.
*   **Metric:** Measure Word Error Rate (WER) specifically for medical terminology at >70dB.
*   **Success Threshold:** Accuracy must remain above 85% at 75dB to consider a limited rollout viable.

**Resource Allocation:**
Assign the two additional data scientists exclusively to this experiment. Their goal is not to "fix" the algorithm yet, but to produce the definitive data set required for the funding review.

**Outcomes & Next Steps:**
*   **If Accuracy > 85% at 75dB:** Proceed with a limited rollout restricted to lower-acuity zones while continuing optimization.
*   **If Accuracy < 85% at 75dB:** Halt rollout plans. Present the data to stakeholders to justify a pivot toward advanced noise cancellation R&D or a "human-in-the-loop" hybrid model before seeking further funding.

**Note to Head of Engineering:**
Allocating resources now to *validate* the risk is cheaper than rolling out a flawed product that compromises patient safety. The funding review requires data, not assumptions.
