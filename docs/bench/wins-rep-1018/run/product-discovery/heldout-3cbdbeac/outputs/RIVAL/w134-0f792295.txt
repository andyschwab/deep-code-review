**Decision Memo: River Transport Scheduler Development vs. Adoption Mitigation**

**To:** VP of Operations, Marowijne Timber Corp
**From:** Product Management
**Date:** October 26, 2023
**Subject:** Q3 Engineering Budget Allocation – River Transport Scheduler

**Executive Summary**
We face a critical bottleneck: five formal complaints this quarter regarding three-day average delays caused by unpredictable water levels and boat availability. While an automated scheduler promises a 40% reduction in delays, our primary risk is not technical feasibility but user adoption among independent boat captains. If captains revert to phone calls, the investment yields zero ROI. This memo evaluates immediate development against a "training-first" approach.

**Option A: Immediate Development (Build Now)**
*Pros:*
*   **Speed to Value:** If adoption succeeds, we immediately address the 3-day delay average, potentially recovering significant revenue and logger trust within Q3.
*   **Data Accumulation:** Early launch provides real-world river depth and usage data to refine the optimization algorithm sooner.
*   **Competitive Signal:** Demonstrates to independent loggers that Marowijne is modernizing operations, potentially securing loyalty.

*Cons:*
*   **High Waste Risk:** If captains reject the digital interface, engineering hours and budget are sunk costs with no operational improvement.
*   **Operational Friction:** A partially adopted system creates a hybrid workflow (some digital, some phone), increasing coordination complexity for dispatchers.
*   **Reputation Damage:** Launching a tool that fails to deliver due to non-adoption may erode confidence in future tech initiatives.

**Option B: Invest in Driver Training & Validation First (Delay Build)**
*Pros:*
*   **Risk Mitigation:** Validates the core assumption (captain adoption) before writing code. We can test willingness to use digital tools via low-fidelity prototypes (e.g., WhatsApp-based scheduling trials).
*   **Cost Efficiency:** Redirects Q3 engineering funds to change management, ensuring the "human layer" is ready before the "tech layer" arrives.
*   **Higher Success Rate:** Addresses the root cause of potential failure (behavioral resistance) rather than just the symptom (delays).

*Cons:*
*   **Delayed Relief:** The 3-day average delay persists through Q3, risking further complaints from independent loggers.
*   **Opportunity Cost:** Competitors or alternative transport methods could gain favor while we delay optimization.
*   **Training Limitations:** Training alone cannot solve the physical constraint of unpredictable water levels; it only prepares for the tool.

**Recommendation**
**Do not proceed with full-scale development in Q3.** The risk of building a solution that users reject is too high given the specific dependency on independent captains.

Instead, allocate the Q3 engineering budget to a **four-week "Concierge Pilot"**:
1.  Manually simulate the scheduler using existing data for a small group of 5 captains.
2.  Require these captains to receive assignments via a simple digital channel (SMS/WhatsApp) instead of phone calls.
3.  Measure adherence rates. If >80% comply without reverting to calls, greenlight full development in Q4. If not, invest in targeted incentives or interface redesign before coding.

This approach protects the engineering budget from waste while actively addressing the delay issue through a validated learning loop.

***

*Note: This recommendation prioritizes validating the adoption assumption over immediate feature delivery to prevent sunk costs.*
*Action required: Approval to reallocate Q3 engineering hours to the Concierge Pilot design.*
