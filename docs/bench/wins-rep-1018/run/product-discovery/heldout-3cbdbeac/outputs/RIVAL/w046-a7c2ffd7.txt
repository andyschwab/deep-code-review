### Executive Summary: Genetic Counselor Integration Assessment

**Current Evidence Status:** Insufficient to justify full build ($40k/6 weeks) before November 1st launch.
**Primary Gap:** Critical unknowns regarding patient preference (chat vs. video) and willingness to pay create high viability risk. The assumption that this specific feature will cut support tickets by 50% is unvalidated.

#### Risk Assessment

**1. Viability & Financial Risk (High)**
*   **Unknown Revenue Model:** There is no data on whether patients will pay an extra fee. If the service must be free to ensure safety, the $40k build cost plus ongoing counselor operational costs must be absorbed entirely by GeneView, impacting unit economics.
*   **Utilization Uncertainty:** Without knowing if users prefer asynchronous messaging or immediate video, building the wrong modality could result in low adoption, failing to reduce the current 30 weekly inquiries.

**2. Usability & Operational Risk (Medium-High)**
*   **Modality Mismatch:** If patients prefer quick text answers but the team builds a video scheduling system (or vice versa), friction will increase, potentially worsening confusion rather than alleviating it.
*   **Scalability:** If the feature launches successfully but demand exceeds the capacity of available certified counselors, wait times could increase patient anxiety and liability exposure.

**3. Liability & Ethical Risk (Critical for Advisory Board)**
*   **False Sense of Security:** Integrating counselors might imply that *all* results require interpretation, potentially causing panic for low-risk results that previously went unquestioned.
*   **Scope of Guidance:** Without strict protocols, there is a risk of counselors providing medical advice beyond "interpretation," increasing legal liability. Conversely, if the chat is too restricted, it fails to solve the confusion problem identified in beta testing.

#### Benefit Assessment

**1. Patient Safety & Clarity (High Potential)**
*   Direct access to certified professionals addresses the root cause of the 20% confusion rate observed in beta testing.
*   Reduces the likelihood of patients making drastic life decisions based on misinterpreted risk percentages.

**2. Support Efficiency (Conditional)**
*   If the 50% reduction assumption holds, weekly inquiries could drop from 30 to 15, freeing up support staff for technical rather than clinical issues.
*   Deflects complex clinical questions from general support agents who are not qualified to answer them.

**3. Trust & Brand Differentiation (High)**
*   Offering direct expert access positions GeneView as a safety-first leader, potentially increasing conversion rates and customer lifetime value despite the added cost.

#### Recommendation for Clinical Advisory Board

Do not approve the full $40k build immediately. The evidence supports the *problem* (confusion creates liability) but not the specific *solution* (live chat integration) or its *format*.

**Proposed Path Forward:**
1.  **Conduct a "Concierge" Test (1 Week):** Manually route the current 30 weekly inquiries to a single available counselor via email or phone. Measure resolution time, patient satisfaction, and whether the interaction prevents follow-up tickets.
2.  **Validate Modality & Willingness to Pay:** Survey the 10 confused beta users and a segment of the waiting list. Ask: "Would you prefer a text response within 2 hours or a 15-min video call?" and "Would you pay $X for this service?"
3.  **Decision Gate:** If the manual test shows a clear path to reducing liability and users confirm a preferred modality, proceed with the targeted build. If users are unwilling to pay and the manual process is too costly, explore static educational interventions (e.g., improved result visualizations) before launching live features.

This approach mitigates the risk of building an unused or unpopular feature while directly addressing the board's liability concerns through immediate, low-cost validation.
