# Recommendation: Connected Inhaler OS Strategy

**Executive Summary**
Do not enforce a strict minimum OS requirement (iOS 15/Android 12) at launch. Instead, implement a **graceful degradation strategy** with a fallback mode for older operating systems. Enforcing a hard cutoff risks alienating up to 20% of our target market immediately upon launch, contradicting the assumption that 80% of asthma patients upgrade within two years. Given the clinical nature of our product, accessibility must outweigh data granularity.

**Risk Analysis**
1.  **Market Access Risk**: Your assumption that 80% of users have modern devices is unvalidated for our specific waitlist. If the actual figure is lower (e.g., 60-70%), a hard cutoff excludes a significant portion of early adopters who signed up in January. In the asthma community, device longevity often correlates with lower socioeconomic status, a key demographic for respiratory issues. Excluding them creates an equity gap and reduces our total addressable market.
2.  **Clinical Safety & Trust**: A "connection drop" rate of 12% on older systems is problematic, but a total refusal to connect is worse. If a patient cannot log a dose because their phone is "too old," they may lose trust in the therapy entirely. A fallback mode that reduces data frequency (e.g., storing doses locally on the inhaler and syncing when a stable connection is briefly established, or manual entry prompts) ensures the core job-to-be-done (tracking medication) remains possible.
3.  **Data Integrity vs. Continuity**: While high-frequency data is ideal for engineering optimization, low-frequency or delayed data is still valuable for clinical adherence tracking. Losing the user entirely yields zero data.

**Proposed Strategy: Tiered Connectivity**
*   **Tier 1 (iOS 15+/Android 12+)**: Full real-time Bluetooth Low Energy (BLE) streaming, instant dose confirmation, and cloud sync.
*   **Tier 2 (Older OS)**: "Store-and-Forward" fallback mode.
    *   The inhaler stores dose timestamps locally.
    *   The app attempts background sync less frequently to conserve battery and reduce connection overhead.
    *   Users receive a clear, non-alarming notification: "Connection unstable. Your doses are saved locally and will sync when your phone is ready."
    *   Provide a manual override for users to confirm doses if auto-sync fails repeatedly.

**Immediate Next Steps (Before Tuesday)**
1.  **Validate the Assumption**: Send a urgent, one-question SMS survey to the 200 waitlist users: "What model of phone are you currently using?" This will provide the exact OS breakdown before the steering committee.
2.  **Define Fallback Metrics**: Work with engineering to define the minimum viable data frequency required for clinical validity in Tier 2.
3.  **Draft Communication**: Prepare transparency messaging for the clinical advisory board explaining that while data resolution varies by device, *adherence tracking* remains 100% functional across all supported devices.

**Conclusion**
Building a fallback mode requires slightly more initial engineering effort but secures market entry, protects patient trust, and validates your upgrade assumption with real data rather than guesses. A hard cutoff is a binary failure point; a fallback is a manageable trade-off.
