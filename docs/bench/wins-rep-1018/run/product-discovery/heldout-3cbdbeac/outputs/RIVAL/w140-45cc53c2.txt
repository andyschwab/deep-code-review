### Critical Risks and Missing Data Analysis: Sensor Pilot vs. Driver Training

**Executive Summary**
The proposal to install $40 temperature/pH sensors aims to reduce rejection rates by 50% following two formal complaints from SweetNorth Chocolatiers. However, before allocating the Q1 innovation budget, the steering committee must address significant gaps regarding operational feasibility, financial viability, and the root cause of the fermentation inconsistencies.

#### 1. Value & Usability Risks (The "Will it Work?" Gap)
*   **Root Cause Ambiguity:** We assume inconsistent fermentation is caused by unmonitored temperature/pH levels. We lack data confirming that these specific variables are the primary drivers of the October/November failures versus other factors (e.g., bean variety, turning frequency, or harvest timing).
*   **Technical Capacity:** There is no evidence that community cooperatives possess the technical literacy or infrastructure (charging, connectivity, calibration) to maintain these devices. If sensors fail or provide inaccurate data due to poor maintenance, the system creates false security rather than risk mitigation.
*   **Actionability:** Even if a sensor flags a "risky" batch, do field teams have a defined protocol to salvage it? Without a remediation process, detection alone does not prevent rejection.

#### 2. Viability & Feasibility Risks (The "Can we Afford/Sustain it?" Gap)
*   **Margin Erosion:** At $40 per unit, the upfront hardware cost may be prohibitive for smaller shipments with thin margins. We lack a total cost of ownership (TCO) model including replacement rates, data subscriptions, and labor hours for data review.
*   **Scalability:** We do not know if the solution scales across all cooperatives or if it requires a bespoke setup for each site, introducing logistical complexity that outweighs the benefits.
*   **Alternative ROI:** We have no comparative data on the cost-effectiveness of the alternative: driver training. If human error in transport or handling is a contributing factor, training might offer a higher ROI at a lower capital cost.

#### 3. Go-to-Market & Strategy Risks
*   **Adoption Friction:** We assume farmers will consistently use the technology. There is a risk of low adoption if the devices are perceived as surveillance tools rather than helpful aids, leading to data gaps.
*   **Timing:** With a decision deadline of January 15, we risk rushing a pilot without a proper baseline, potentially wasting Q1 resources on a solution that doesn't address the core issue.

#### Critical Missing Data Points
To make an informed decision by January 15, the following data must be gathered immediately:

1.  **Historical Correlation Data:** Analyze the specific temperature/pH logs (if manually recorded) or environmental conditions of the rejected October/November batches versus accepted ones to confirm causality.
2.  **Cooperative Capability Audit:** Conduct rapid site visits to assess current tech usage, power availability, and staff willingness to adopt new hardware.
3.  **Remediation Protocols:** Define exactly what actions a field team can take if a sensor alerts them mid-fermentation.
4.  **Cost-Benefit Comparison:** Model the cost of the sensor pilot (hardware + maintenance + labor) against the estimated cost of a comprehensive driver training program, projected against potential savings from reduced rejections.
5.  **Rejection Root Cause Breakdown:** Interview SweetNorth's quality team to understand if "inconsistent fermentation" is their only concern or if transport damage (driver-related) is also a hidden factor.

**Recommendation**
Do not commit the full Q1 budget to hardware yet. Allocate a small portion of funds to validate the root cause and technical capacity within the next two weeks. If the data confirms sensors are the silver bullet, proceed with a limited pilot; otherwise, pivot to driver training or process improvements.

*Note: This analysis assumes the $40 unit cost is accurate and excludes potential bulk discounts. Immediate validation of the root cause is the highest priority before any capital expenditure.*
