### Business Case Evaluation: Temperature Excursion Alert System

**Executive Summary**
The current evidence strongly justifies the development of a temperature excursion alert system. With an 8% rejection rate costing $22,000 annually, the financial exposure is significant relative to your fleet size of twelve vans. The primary assumption—that immediate alerts enable corrective action—is logically sound, but the business case hinges on executing a low-cost hardware integration to ensure viability.

**Financial Impact Analysis**
*   **Current Losses:** $22,000/year in lost revenue and penalties.
*   **Break-Even Threshold:** Any solution costing less than $22,000 annually (approx. $1,830/month) to implement and maintain yields a positive ROI if it eliminates rejections.
*   **Scale Context:** For a fleet of only twelve vans, per-unit hardware costs are manageable. Even a premium solution at $100/month per van totals $14,400/year, leaving a net savings of $7,600 while preserving client relationships.

**Pros of Building the Alert System**
1.  **Direct Revenue Protection:** Preventing even half of the current excursions saves $11,000 annually, likely covering the entire cost of the system.
2.  **Client Retention:** Five rejections in one month indicate an imminent risk of losing major hospital contracts. An alert system demonstrates proactive quality control, which is critical for renewing trust with procurement officers.
3.  **Operational Agility:** Instant notifications allow drivers to adjust thermostat settings, check door seals, or reroute to a facility before the cargo temperature becomes irreversible, turning a total loss into a minor delay.
4.  **Data for Liability:** Detailed temperature logs provide defensible data in case of disputes, proving whether an excursion was due to equipment failure or external factors.

**Cons and Risks**
1.  **Hardware Integration Complexity:** Retrofitting twelve older vans may require custom wiring or proprietary gateways, increasing upfront engineering time and cost.
2.  **Driver Response Latency:** The system assumes drivers can and will act immediately. If a driver is unable to stop safely or lacks the authority to make route changes, the alert provides visibility but no solution.
3.  **False Positives:** Poorly calibrated sensors could trigger unnecessary stops, delaying deliveries and eroding driver trust in the system.
4.  **Opportunity Cost:** Engineering resources spent building a custom solution might be better spent on core logistics software if off-the-shelf IoT trackers exist.

**Recommendation**
Proceed with the alert system, but **do not build a fully custom hardware solution from scratch**. The risk of high integration costs outweighs the benefit of custom code for a twelve-van fleet.

**Strategic Next Steps:**
1.  **Validate the "Corrective Action" Assumption:** Before coding, interview three drivers. Ask: "If you received an alert right now while driving, exactly what steps could you take?" If they cannot list specific actions, the technology alone will not solve the problem; you may need revised SOPs or insulated contingency packaging.
2.  **Buy vs. Build Analysis:** Investigate off-the-shelf cellular IoT temperature loggers (e.g., SensoScientific, Controlant) that offer API integrations. Integrating an existing device via API is significantly cheaper and faster than building custom hardware firmware.
3.  **Pilot Program:** Equip only three vans with the chosen solution for 30 days. Measure the reduction in excursions and the speed of driver response. If the pilot prevents just two rejections, the business case is proven for the full fleet.

**Conclusion for Lenders**
The $22,000 annual bleed represents a clear, quantifiable risk. Investing in an alert mechanism is a defensive necessity to secure existing contracts. By leveraging existing IoT hardware rather than custom engineering, MedTrans Costa Rica can mitigate the excursion risk at a fraction of the potential loss, securing both revenue and lender confidence.

*Note: Ensure the selected hardware complies with Costa Rican medical transport regulations regarding data logging frequency.*
*Immediate driver training on "alert response protocols" is required to maximize the system's effectiveness.*
