### Decision Evaluation: AI Forecasting Tool Viability

**Current Status:** Critical Risk Identified
Three live demonstrations resulted in total rejection by senior procurement officers. The core friction is not accuracy, but **trust and control**. Users explicitly prefer a 6-hour weekly manual spreadsheet process over the automated "black-box" solution because they understand and control the spreadsheet logic.

**Key Decision Factors**

1.  **The Control vs. Efficiency Trade-off**
    The data indicates that for this persona (senior procurement officers), *perceived control* currently outweighs *time efficiency*. They are willing to invest six hours weekly to maintain certainty. An algorithm that saves time but removes visibility is viewed as a liability, not an asset. If the tool cannot replicate the "feel" of control found in spreadsheets, adoption will remain zero regardless of algorithmic superiority.

2.  **Validity of the Interface Hypothesis**
    Your assumption that this is purely an interface design issue is plausible but unproven. The officers' citation of "lack of trust in black-box predictions" suggests the problem may be fundamental to how AI forecasts are generated, not just how they are displayed. If the underlying logic cannot be translated into human-readable reasoning (e.g., "Forecast increased due to seasonal trend X + supplier delay Y"), better UI design alone will fail.

3.  **The "Explainability" Variable**
    The unknown variable is whether **explainable AI (XAI)** features bridge the trust gap. If the system can provide the specific "why" behind every number, mimicking the transparent logic of a spreadsheet formula, it may validate the hybrid model. If the AI cannot generate clear, defensible reasons for its predictions, the "black box" label is accurate, and the product fundamentally mismatches user needs.

**Strategic Recommendations**

*   **Do Not Abandon Yet, But Pivot Immediately:** Abandoning the feature entirely is premature without testing the "explainability" hypothesis. However, continuing with the current "automated suggestion" model is guaranteed to fail based on current evidence.
*   **Adopt a Hybrid Human-in-the-Loop Model:** Shift the product vision from "automation" to "augmentation." The tool should not replace the spreadsheet but enhance it.
    *   *Action:* Redesign the workflow so the AI acts as a co-pilot that pre-fills data or highlights anomalies, requiring explicit human approval before any change is finalized. This restores the "control" users crave.
*   **Immediate Validation Experiment:** Before the VP review on the 15th, conduct a rapid prototype test.
    *   *Test:* Present the same forecast data to the officers, but include a plain-English explanation for each prediction (e.g., "We predict a 10% rise because Competitor A raised prices last week").
    *   *Success Metric:* Willingness to accept the suggestion *with* the explanation attached. If they still reject it, the issue is likely deep-seated resistance to AI, not just a lack of transparency.

**Conclusion for Leadership**
The project is viable only if we pivot from a "black-box automation" strategy to a "transparent, hybrid decision-support" strategy. The engineering lead must assess if the current model can output human-readable reasoning. If the technology cannot explain its logic, the feature should be deprecated in favor of tools that integrate directly into existing spreadsheet workflows rather than attempting to replace them.
