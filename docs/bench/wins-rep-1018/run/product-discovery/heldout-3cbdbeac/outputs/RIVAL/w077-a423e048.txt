# Strategic Overview: National Scale Expansion Risks & Requirements

**Executive Summary**
While the central region pilot demonstrated a 94% reduction in reporting time (3 days to 4 hours), expanding to 5,000 users nationally by December presents critical gaps in evidence regarding regional variability and infrastructure capacity. The assumption that central region workflows will translate perfectly to northern districts is unvalidated and represents the highest strategic risk to the grant renewal.

## Critical Evidence Gaps: Regional Differences

**1. Connectivity & Real-Time Sync Viability**
*   **Current Assumption:** Real-time sync functions effectively in northern districts as it did in the central region.
*   **Evidence Gap:** No data exists on network latency, uptime, or bandwidth availability in the northern districts. If connectivity is intermittent, the real-time sync feature may cause data loss or app crashes, rendering the tool unusable.
*   **Risk:** High. Failure here invalidates the core value proposition (speed of reporting).

**2. Workflow Transferability**
*   **Current Assumption:** Operational workflows (user journeys, data entry patterns) are identical across regions.
*   **Evidence Gap:** Northern districts may have different health protocols, languages, or community structures that alter how workers interact with the app. The central pilot did not test these variables.
*   **Risk:** Medium-High. Misaligned workflows lead to low adoption and inaccurate data.

**3. Infrastructure Scalability**
*   **Current State:** System supports 500 concurrent users.
*   **Requirement:** Support 5,000 users (10x increase) by December.
*   **Evidence Gap:** Unknown performance degradation curves under load. We lack stress test results for 1,000+ concurrent connections, specifically regarding database locking and API response times during peak reporting hours.

## Operational Risks

*   **Technical Failure:** Attempting a full rollout without addressing the connectivity gap could result in a system-wide failure in the north, damaging trust with local health officials and jeopardizing the grant.
*   **Data Integrity:** If sync fails, workers may revert to paper, creating data silos and delaying the very reporting speeds the grant aims to sustain.
*   **Support Overload:** A 10x user increase requires a proportional increase in support capacity. Current team structure is validated only for 200 users.

## Recommended Path Forward

**Option A: Full National Rollout (High Risk)**
Proceeding directly to 5,000 users assumes the connectivity and workflow assumptions are true. Given the lack of evidence, this threatens the October grant review if early failures occur.

**Option B: Targeted Northern Pilot (Recommended)**
Execute a focused 2-month pilot with 500 users in the northern districts specifically to:
1.  **Validate Connectivity:** Test offline-first capabilities and sync reliability in low-bandwidth environments.
2.  **Adapt Workflows:** Identify and adjust for regional operational differences.
3.  **Stress Test Infrastructure:** Gradually ramp up load to identify breaking points before the national deadline.

**Decision Criteria for Grant Committee**
Commit funds to **Option B** to de-risk the expansion. Use the findings to build a robust technical architecture (likely shifting to offline-first synchronization) and a validated operational playbook before the December national deadline. This approach protects the grant renewal by demonstrating due diligence and adaptive planning rather than gambling on unproven assumptions.

*Note: Immediate engineering assessment required to estimate costs for offline-first architecture modifications.*
*Grant review presentation should highlight the "Targeted Pilot" as a strategic mitigation step, not a delay.*
