### Trade-off Analysis: Full Automation vs. Hybrid Model for Pre-Authorization

**Current State Baseline**
*   **Volume:** 5,000 requests/week.
*   **Current TAT:** 48 hours.
*   **Prototype Capability:** Automates 70% (3,500 requests) with 92% accuracy.
*   **Projected Savings:** $200,000/year (administrative costs).
*   **Constraint:** Mandatory human review for all automated denials (Legal requirement).

#### Option A: Full Automation with Universal Legal Safeguards
*Model: Algorithm decides all; humans review every automated denial.*

*   **Workflow:** The system processes 100% of requests. Based on a 92% accuracy rate, we assume an 8% error rate or "denial" rate requiring review. If the 70% automation rate implies the system makes a definitive decision on 3,500 cases, and historical denial rates apply, a significant portion of those 3,500 cases may trigger the mandatory review.
*   **Efficiency Impact:** If the "mandatory review" applies to *every* automated denial regardless of confidence, the bottleneck shifts from initial processing to the review queue. If the 8% inaccuracy results in false denials that must be reviewed, the speed gain is preserved only if the review is faster than the original 48-hour manual process. However, if legal counsel interprets "mandatory review" as a full re-evaluation, the TAT for those cases may revert to near 48 hours, eroding the average TAT improvement.
*   **Legal Risk:** Low. Strict adherence to the "human-in-the-loop" for denials satisfies current counsel concerns and mitigates immediate regulatory friction.
*   **Financial Impact:** Savings may drop below the projected $200k if the review layer requires senior staff time equivalent to manual processing.

#### Option B: Hybrid Model (Human-in-the-Loop for Edge Cases Only)
*Model: Algorithm handles clear approvals; humans handle complex cases and all denials.*

*   **Workflow:**
    *   **Clear Approvals (High Confidence):** Fully automated. No human touch.
    *   **Edge Cases/Denials:** Routed immediately to humans before a decision is communicated.
*   **Efficiency Impact:** This maximizes speed for the majority of "clean" claims. By filtering out the 70% of cases the AI is confident about, the human team focuses only on the remaining 30% plus the specific edge cases within the 70%. This prevents the review queue from clogging with simple approvals.
*   **Legal Risk:** Moderate to High. Regulators may argue that an automated denial *without* prior human review violates emerging standards, even if a human reviews it later upon appeal. This model relies on the definition of "decision." If the system *recommends* a denial but a human *makes* the final call before notification, it may satisfy legal requirements while maintaining speed.
*   **Financial Impact:** Likely preserves most of the $200k savings because human hours are concentrated only on complex work, not routine reviews.

#### Critical Unknowns & Recommendations
The core tension is the definition of "automated denial."
1.  **If "Automated Denial" = Final Decision sent to customer:** Option A is non-compliant if regulations require pre-decision human review.
2.  **If "Automated Denial" = System Recommendation:** Option B is viable if humans review the recommendation *before* the customer is notified.

**Recommendation for November Release:**
Adopt a **Modified Hybrid Model**. Configure the prototype to treat all potential denials as "recommendations" rather than final decisions. Route 100% of potential denials to a human queue for final sign-off *before* communication. This satisfies the legal mandate for human oversight on denials while retaining full automation for approvals (likely the bulk of the 70%).

**Immediate Action Required:**
Clarify with Legal whether "review" means pre-decision (before customer notification) or post-decision (appeals). If pre-decision is required, the "Full Automation" model is technically impossible for denials, making the Hybrid approach the only compliant path that retains efficiency gains.

*Note: The 92% accuracy metric must be broken down by "approval accuracy" vs. "denial accuracy" to precisely calculate the review queue volume.*
