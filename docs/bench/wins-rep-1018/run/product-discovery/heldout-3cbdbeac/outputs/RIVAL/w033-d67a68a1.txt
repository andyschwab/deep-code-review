### Recommendation: Prioritize the Patient Wait-Time Estimator

Based on the metrics provided, **building the Patient Wait-Time Estimator is the higher priority** for Q3. While internal efficiency is valuable, the current 45-minute wait time is actively destroying your business stability by causing a 20% drop in return visits. Fixing the "leak" in customer retention takes precedence over optimizing overhead costs.

**Strategic Rationale**
1.  **Revenue Protection vs. Cost Reduction:** A 20% loss in return visits represents a direct hit to top-line revenue that likely far exceeds the $15,000 monthly savings from the scheduling optimizer. Retaining existing patients is generally more cost-effective than acquiring new ones. If patients leave due to wait times, no amount of internal scheduling efficiency will bring them back.
2.  **Immediate Value Perception:** Even if the estimator does not physically shorten the wait, it manages patient expectations and reduces perceived wait time anxiety. This directly addresses the friction point causing the 20% churn.
3.  **Infrastructure Risk Mitigation:** Your concern about internet infrastructure crashing is a valid feasibility risk, but it is solvable without abandoning the feature. You can launch a "low-tech" version (e.g., manual updates every 30 minutes via SMS or a static web page) to validate the value before investing in complex real-time APIs.

**Proposed Q3 Execution Plan**
*   **Week 1-2 (MVP Launch):** Deploy a simplified wait-time display. Instead of real-time streaming, have front-desk staff update a simple digital sign or webpage every 30 minutes. This bypasses heavy infrastructure requirements while testing if transparency improves patient sentiment.
*   **Week 3-4 (Measurement):** Track two metrics: (1) Patient abandonment rate (people leaving before being seen) and (2) Net Promoter Score (NPS) regarding wait times.
*   **Parallel Track:** Assign a single engineer to audit the network infrastructure. If the MVP succeeds, plan the robust real-time integration for Q4.

**Why Not the Scheduling Optimizer Now?**
The $15,000 monthly saving is an *assumption* based on optimized shifts. However, if the patient volume continues to drop by 20% due to poor wait experiences, the clinic may not have enough staff to schedule, rendering the optimizer irrelevant. Solve the demand-side crisis first; optimize the supply side once the patient flow stabilizes.

**Decision Summary**
*   **Primary Goal:** Stop the 20% churn immediately.
*   **Selected Tool:** Patient Wait-Time Estimator (MVP version).
*   **Deferred Tool:** Staff Scheduling Optimizer (Move to Q4).

*Note: Ensure the operations director communicates to staff that the estimator is a transparency tool, not a promise of instant service, to manage expectations.*
*If the MVP shows no improvement in patient sentiment after two weeks, reconsider the scheduling optimizer as a means to actually reduce physical wait times.*
