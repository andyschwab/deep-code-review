### Strategic Comparison: Automated Chatbot vs. Live Nurse Video

**Current State Baseline**
*   **Friction Point:** 30% abandonment rate during a 10-minute sign-up process.
*   **User Sentiment:** Split demand—60% prioritize speed; 40% require human interaction.

#### Option A: Automated Chatbot for Initial Triage
*Focus: Scalability & Speed*

*   **Value Proposition:** Directly addresses the 60% of users demanding speed. Potential to reduce sign-up time from 10 minutes to under 2 minutes, theoretically recovering a significant portion of the 30% abandonment rate.
*   **Operational Impact:** High scalability with low marginal cost per interaction. Handles 80% of common queries automatically, freeing human resources for complex cases.
*   **Critical Risk (Trust & Safety):** The primary assumption is that users will trust algorithmic advice for severe symptoms without human verification. If trust is low, users may ignore critical triage advice or abandon the app post-triage, creating a liability risk.
*   **Best For:** Reducing friction for low-acuity cases and capturing the "speed" segment of the market.

#### Option B: Live Nurse Video Call Feature
*Focus: Patient Safety & Trust*

*   **Value Proposition:** Directly addresses the 40% of users insisting on human interaction. Eliminates the "black box" anxiety of AI diagnosis, likely increasing adherence to medical advice for severe symptoms.
*   **Operational Impact:** Low scalability and high variable cost. Requires immediate hiring of nursing staff. Does not inherently solve the 10-minute sign-up friction unless the video call *replaces* the sign-up form entirely.
*   **Critical Risk (Viability):** High operational cost may make the unit economics unsustainable if used for common, low-acuity queries. Risk of long wait times if demand outstrips nursing supply, which could ironically increase abandonment.
*   **Best For:** High-acuity cases, building brand trust, and serving the "safety-first" user segment.

### Recommendation for the Chief Medical Officer

**Do not choose one exclusively; deploy a Hybrid Triage Model.**

The data indicates a binary split in user needs that neither option solves alone. A chatbot-only approach risks patient safety and alienates 40% of the base. A video-only approach fails to solve the scalability crisis and ignores the 60% who want speed.

**Proposed Path Forward:**
1.  **Implement the Chatbot as a "Smart Gatekeeper":** Deploy the AI to handle the initial intake and reduce the 10-minute sign-up to 2 minutes for *all* users. This directly attacks the 30% abandonment metric.
2.  **Dynamic Routing Based on Acuity:**
    *   **Low/Medium Acuity:** The chatbot provides immediate advice and self-care instructions (satisfying the 60% speed seekers).
    *   **High/Severe Acuity:** The chatbot instantly flags the case and routes the user to a **Live Nurse Video Call** (satisfying the 40% safety seekers and mitigating the trust risk).
3.  **Immediate Next Step:** Before hiring full nursing staff or finalizing AI logic, run a concierge test. Manually simulate the "chatbot" logic with a small user group to verify if reducing sign-up time actually lowers abandonment, and test the hand-off protocol to nurses for severe cases to validate the safety workflow.

This approach maximizes scalability while maintaining a human safety net, directly addressing both the abandonment metric and the CMO's safety concerns.

*Note: The 60/40 survey split suggests market segmentation rather than a unanimous preference; the solution must serve both.*
*Ensure legal reviews the liability framework for the AI-to-human handoff protocol before development.*
