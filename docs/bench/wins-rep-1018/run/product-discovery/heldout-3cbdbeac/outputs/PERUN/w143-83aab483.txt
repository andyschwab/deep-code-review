**Recommendation: Do not build the digital tracker yet. Pause capital allocation for software development and immediately execute a low-fidelity "Riskiest Assumption Test" to validate the root cause and operational impact.**

The current evidence pattern is insufficient to justify building a complex digital solution. While the $40,000 loss and reputation damage are critical, they confirm a *symptom* (rejection), not the *root cause* (digital invisibility vs. human error vs. equipment failure). Your stated assumption—that real-time visibility ensures compliance—remains untested. Building now risks solving the wrong problem or creating a tool that farmers and loaders reject due to friction, negating any quality gains.

**Immediate Action Plan (Next 14 Days):**

1.  **Isolate the Variable (The "Concierge" Test):**
    Before writing code, manually simulate the data capture. Equip the next shipment with standalone, off-the-shelf USB temperature/humidity loggers (cost: <$50). Do not integrate them into a dashboard.
    *   **Hypothesis:** If the loggers show perfect curing conditions but the buyer still rejects the load, the problem is *not* visibility; it is likely handling damage post-curing, buyer specification drift, or equipment calibration errors. A digital tracker would have failed to prevent this loss.
    *   **Decision Rule:** If manual logs reveal significant environmental deviations *during transit*, only then does the "visibility" hypothesis hold water.

2.  **Quantify the Operational Drag (The "Friction" Test):**
    Your fear of slowed loading processes is a valid riskiest assumption.
    *   **Test:** Have the loading team use a simple paper checklist or a basic stopwatch to record the time added by manual logger placement and retrieval for one full cycle.
    *   **Metric:** Calculate the "Time-per-Truck" delta.
    *   **Kill Threshold:** If the manual process adds >15 minutes per truck (extrapolating to digital friction), the operational cost may outweigh the quality benefit unless the rejection rate is 100% preventable.

3.  **Process Training Baseline:**
    Simultaneously, audit the last four failures against standard operating procedures (SOPs). Were the SOPs followed and ignored, or were they unknown?
    *   If SOPs were ignored, **process training** is the primary fix, not software. Software cannot fix a culture that ignores manual checks; it will only digitize the negligence.
    *   If SOPs were followed and failures still occurred, the process is broken, and *then* technology (the tracker) becomes the necessary intervention.

**Investment Verdict:**
*   **Build the Tracker IF:** Manual loggers prove environmental deviation is the sole cause of rejection AND the manual time-cost is negligible.
*   **Invest in Training/Process IF:** Manual logs show good conditions (indicating other failure points) or if the team failed to follow existing manual protocols.
*   **Do Nothing/Re-evaluate IF:** The manual test reveals that buyer specifications are shifting or unrelated to curing (e.g., physical damage).

**Conclusion for Investors:**
Capital is currently best deployed on *validation*, not *construction*. We will spend <$200 on manual loggers and 20 hours of labor to de-risk a potential $100k+ software build. We will return with a go/no-go decision based on empirical data from the next shipment cycle, ensuring that any subsequent technology investment directly targets a proven bottleneck rather than an assumed one.
