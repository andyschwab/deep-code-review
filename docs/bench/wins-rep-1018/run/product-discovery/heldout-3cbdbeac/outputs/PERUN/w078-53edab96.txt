Your current evidence is insufficient to support a go/no-go decision for active clinical decision support. **The Riskiest Assumption Gate**
*   **Assumption:** Clinicians will maintain trust and respond to alerts despite a false positive rate of ~15/day, provided the true positive yield is ≥5 lives/quarter.
*   **Status:** Untested. High Impact × High Uncertainty.

**Required Test Protocol: The "Alert Fatigue" Disconfirmation**
You must define a numeric kill threshold before running this test. Based on your goal of saving 5 lives/quarter (~1.25 lives/month), you cannot afford an alert system that is ignored.

*   **Test Design:** A limited "active" pilot with a small cohort (e.g., one ward or 5–10 specific clinicians) where alerts are visible but require a mandatory one-click acknowledgment ("False Alarm" vs. "Action Taken").
*   **Metric:** Alert Acknowledgment Rate and Time-to-Response for True Positives.
*   **Proposed Kill Threshold (Adjust based on your math):** If the acknowledgment rate drops below 80% within the first 48 hours, OR if the time-to-response for verified true positives increases by >20% compared to baseline protocols, **kill the current deployment strategy**.
    *   *Basis:* If staff ignore 1 in 5 alerts, the probability of missing a sepsis case (the very thing you aim to prevent) becomes unacceptably high, negating the "5 lives saved" benefit.
*   **Sample Size:** Minimum 72 hours of continuous live exposure for the pilot group.
*   **Deadline:** Complete this test by August 10 to allow time for analysis before the CIO deadline.

**Evidence Gaps & Non-Goals for This Stage**

*   **Non-Goal:** Improving model accuracy or adding features. The bottleneck is not the algorithm's retrospective score; it is the human-system interaction in a high-noise environment. Do not tune the model further until the alert frequency is behaviorally validated.
