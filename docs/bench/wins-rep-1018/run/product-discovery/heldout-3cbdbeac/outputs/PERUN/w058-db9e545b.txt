### Assessment: Biometric Login Accessibility Risks vs. Security Benefits

**Current State & Proposed Change**
Oakwood Medical Group proposes adding biometric login to reduce password-related support tickets (currently 30% of volume) and enhance perceived security for 15,000+ patients. The current system relies on username/password with zero recorded breaches.

**Critical Knowledge Gaps (Must Resolve Before Decision)**
The decision cannot be made safely until the following specific data points are gathered. Currently, these are unknown assumptions:

1.  **Device Capability by Demographic:** You estimate 40% of users are older adults, but you have **no data** on what percentage of this specific cohort possesses biometric-capable devices (smartphones with fingerprint/face ID or laptops with sensors).
    *   *Action Required:* Survey a representative sample of the 40% older demographic or analyze existing device telemetry (if available) to determine the exact % capable of biometrics.
2.  **Desktop vs. Mobile Split:** You noted a risk of locking out desktop users. You do not know the **percentage of total logins** occurring on desktops without biometric sensors versus mobile devices.
    *   *Action Required:* Analyze server logs to split login attempts by device type (mobile vs. desktop) and OS/browser capabilities.
3.  **Fallback Behavior:** It is unknown how many users who *have* biometric hardware prefer using passwords due to privacy concerns or habit.
    *   *Action Required:* If a pilot is run, measure the adoption rate of biometrics vs. password retention among capable users.

**Risk Analysis**

*   **Accessibility Risk (High):** If biometrics become the primary or default flow without a seamless fallback, you risk disenfranchising the 40% older demographic if their device adoption is low. Furthermore, if desktop usage is high, a "biometric-first" design could increase support tickets rather than reduce them, as users struggle to find the password login option.
*   **Security Benefit (Uncertain/Low Immediate Gain):** Your audits show **zero breaches**. While biometrics reduce phishing risks, the current threat vector (forgotten passwords) is a usability issue, not a confirmed security failure. The security benefit is theoretical until a breach occurs, whereas the accessibility risk is immediate and tangible.
*   **Support Cost Impact:** While biometrics may reduce "forgotten password" tickets, they may introduce new tickets related to "biometric setup failures," "sensor not recognized," or "fallback access issues." Without knowing device capability, the net impact on support volume is unpredictable.

**Recommendation for Compliance & Advisory Council**

Do not approve a full build yet. The risk of excluding a major patient segment outweighs the theoretical security gain given zero current breaches.

**Proposed Next Steps (Before Roadmap Finalization):**
1.  **Run a Device Capability Survey:** Immediately deploy a non-intrusive survey to the 40% older demographic and a random sample of the rest to quantify biometric hardware availability.
2.  **Analyze Login Telemetry:** Determine the exact split of mobile vs. desktop logins.
3.  **Define a "No-Lockout" Requirement:** Mandate that any biometric feature must include an equally prominent, easy-to-find password fallback. If the design cannot guarantee this without confusing users, the feature should not ship.
4.  **Pilot Test:** If hardware data looks promising, run a concierge test with 50 older patients to observe actual setup success rates before coding.

**Verdict:** The project is currently **blocked by missing evidence** regarding user device capabilities. Proceeding without this data violates accessibility best practices and risks increasing support costs.

***

*Note: This assessment relies strictly on the figures provided; no external market data on biometric adoption was invented. The "zero breaches" metric suggests security is not the urgent driver; usability is.*
