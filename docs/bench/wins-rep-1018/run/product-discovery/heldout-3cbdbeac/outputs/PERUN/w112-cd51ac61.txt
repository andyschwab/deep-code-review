### Decision: Do Not Build Yet – Run the Riskiest-Assumption Gate First

The three delay incidents provide strong evidence of a **problem** (costly delays due to documentation), but they do not yet validate the **solution** (automated validation tool) or prove that automation is feasible given the regulatory complexity. Building the tool now risks creating high-maintenance software that fails to address the root cause if the "incomplete documentation" stems from unpredictable human error or ambiguous regulations rather than simple checkable omissions.

**1. The Riskiest Assumption**
The critical untested assumption is: *"The causes of documentation incompleteness are rule-based and consistent enough across five countries to be automated without excessive maintenance overhead."*
If the delays are caused by subjective customs officer interpretations or frequently changing informal requirements, an automated validator will generate false confidence and still result in delays.

**2. The Cheapest Disconfirming Test**
Before writing code, you must determine if the errors are automatable.
*   **Test:** Conduct a "Root Cause Audit" on the last 10–20 delayed shipments (including the three cited) and 10 successful ones.
*   **Method:** Manually categorize every missing or rejected document field into: (A) Simple Data Entry Error, (B) Missing Static Document, (C) Ambiguous Regulation Interpretation, or (D) Last-minute Regulation Change.
*   **Metric:** Percentage of delays caused by categories A and B (automatable) vs. C and D (human/judgment reliant).
*   **Numeric Kill Threshold:** If fewer than **70%** of the delay root causes fall into categories A or B, **do not build the automated validator**. Instead, pivot to a process intervention (e.g., enhanced checklists or targeted training), as the maintenance overhead for automating edge cases will exceed the value.
*   **Sample Size:** 20 incidents (10 failed, 10 successful controls).
*   **Deadline:** 5 business days.

**3. Required Evidence Structure (Empty Slots)**
You currently have only three data points. To present a robust case to leadership, fill these slots with the audit results:
*   **Total Cost of Delay (Annualized):** [Insert calculated annual loss based on frequency, not just the €15k sample].
*   **Automatable Error Rate:** [Insert % from Root Cause Audit].
*   **Regulatory Volatility Score:** [Insert count of regulation changes in the last 12 months for the 5 countries].
*   **Manual Effort Baseline:** [Insert hours spent per shipment on current manual prep].

**Conclusion**
The current data justifies **investigating the cause**, not building the tool. Present the leadership team with the plan to run the Root Cause Audit. If the audit shows >70% of errors are rule-based, *then* the business case for automation is supported. If not, you have saved the company the cost of building a fragile tool that cannot solve the problem.

**Note:** This approach shifts the request from "building a feature" to "validating a business hypothesis," which is more appropriate for senior leadership review.
**Note:** Do not estimate the "maintenance overhead" yet; let the ratio of ambiguous errors (Category C/D) discovered in the audit dictate that projection.
