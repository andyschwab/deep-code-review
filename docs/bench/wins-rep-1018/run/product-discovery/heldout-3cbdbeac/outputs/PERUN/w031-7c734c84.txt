### Executive Summary: Do Not Pivot Yet
The current evidence is **insufficient** to justify an immediate pivot or engineering budget allocation. While the problem (medication errors) is critical and the user *stated* demand is high, the validation relies entirely on hypothetical intent ("asked for") rather than observed behavior, and a fatal viability gap (data licensing) remains unquantified. Proceeding now risks building an expensive feature that violates license agreements or fails to change actual patient outcomes.

### Evidence Breakdown & Risk Analysis

**1. Desirability Risk (High Uncertainty)**
*   **Current Evidence:** 12 of 15 pharmacists *said* they want an automated checker.
*   **The Gap:** This is stated intent, not behavioral proof. In healthcare, professionals often request "safety features" in interviews because it is the socially correct answer, not because they will integrate it into their workflow or pay for it.
*   **Missing Data:** We do not know if pharmacists currently manually check interactions (and how often), what workarounds they use, or if they have ever switched vendors specifically for this feature.
*   **Required Test:** Before building, observe 5 pharmacists during actual prescription verification shifts. Count how many potential interactions they catch manually vs. miss. Do not ask them what they want; watch what they do.

**2. Viability Risk (Critical Blocker)**
*   **Current Evidence:** None. You explicitly stated uncertainty regarding database licenses.
*   **The Gap:** If the current license prohibits automated interaction checking or requires a prohibitive fee, the feature is impossible or unprofitable regardless of demand.
*   **Required Test:** Immediate legal/licensing review. Obtain a written cost estimate for the required data API access.
*   **Kill Threshold:** If the data cost exceeds [X]% of the projected revenue from this feature, or if the license forbids automated use, the project must stop before a single line of code is written.

**3. Impact Assumption (Unverified)**
*   **Current Claim:** "Reduce medication errors by at least 40%."
*   **The Gap:** This is a fabricated projection with no baseline. Without knowing the current error rate in your specific user base, a 40% reduction is a guess.
*   **Required Metric:** Establish the baseline error rate from the near-miss logs. A 40% reduction of an unknown number is meaningless.

### Recommendation: The "Cheapest Test" Gate
Do not allocate the full engineering budget yet. Execute the following two steps before the board meeting:

1.  **Viability Gate (24-48 hours):** Confirm license permissions and exact data costs. If the answer is "no" or "too expensive," the decision is made: do not build.
2.  **Desirability Gate (1 week):** Run a "concierge test." Select 3 partner pharmacies. When they process prescriptions, have your team manually run interactions using a separate subscribed database and fax/email alerts to the pharmacist. Measure:
    *   Do they act on the alert?
    *   Does it slow them down or speed them up?
    *   Would they pay for this manual service?

**Decision Rule:** Only pivot resources if (A) the license allows it at a viable cost, AND (B) the concierge test shows pharmacists actively relying on the alerts to prevent errors. If either fails, stay on the wellness tracking roadmap.
