### Strategic Recommendation: Do Not Prioritize Full Build Yet

**Verdict:** Hold "Family Mode" off the Q3 roadmap for full engineering commitment. The current data indicates a *behavior* (shared device usage) but not a *need* (desire for distinct profiles). Building role-based permissions is a high-cost solution to a problem that might just be "convenient credential sharing."

**Missing Behavioral Data**
To distinguish between a "workaround" and a "genuine product need," you must observe evidence of **friction** or **active workarounds** in the current flow. Specifically, you are missing:
1.  **Sequential vs. Simultaneous Usage:** Are users logging in/out rapidly (suggesting distinct intent) or staying logged in while handing the device over (suggesting shared access)?
2.  **Error States:** Are there increased rates of "incorrect password" attempts or accidental transaction deletions that correlate with these multi-user sessions?
3.  **Feature Avoidance:** Are these shared-device sessions shorter or lacking specific actions (e.g., viewing sensitive debt details) compared to single-user sessions?

Without this, you cannot confirm if users *want* separation or are just tolerating the current state.

**Proposed Low-Effort Test: The "Concierge" Permission Gate**
Before building role-based logic, run a **Fake-Door/Concierge test** within the existing app interface to measure demand for *separation* specifically.

*   **The Setup:** For the Mexico City cohort only, add a non-functional UI toggle on the login screen or settings labeled: *"Switch to Family View"* or *"Create Child Profile."*
*   **The Action:** When clicked, do not build the feature. Instead, show a modal: *"We are building dedicated family profiles! Would you like to be notified when ready, or join a 10-minute interview to help design it?"*
*   **The Metric:** Click-through rate (CTR) on the toggle.
    *   **Kill Threshold:** If <5% of the shared-device users click this toggle, the demand for distinct profiles is likely too low to justify the build. The current behavior is likely just convenience.
    *   **Go Signal:** If >15% click, you have validated the *desire* for separation.
*   **Follow-up:** Immediately interview the users who clicked. Ask: *"What specifically were you trying to do that you couldn't?"* This validates the *specific* permissions needed (e.g., hiding credit card numbers vs. restricting transfers).

**Next Steps**
1.  Deploy the UI toggle to the Mexico City segment immediately (1–2 days dev time).
2.  Run for 10 days or until 200 clicks are recorded.
3.  If the threshold is met, prioritize the feature for Q4 based on interview insights. If not, mark "Family Mode" as a non-goal for Q3 and investigate other drivers for the usage spike.

*Note: Do not rely on the pending survey results alone; stated intent ("I want family features") often diverges from actual behavior (clicking the button).*
*This approach prevents building complex permission infrastructure for a problem that may not exist.*
