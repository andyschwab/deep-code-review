### Risk Analysis: Cold Storage Booking Module vs. Fleet Expansion

**Decision Context:** Allocate Q3 budget to software (booking tool) or hardware (trucking fleet) by next Friday.

#### 1. The Riskiest Assumption Gate
**Assumption:** Farmers have reliable daily internet access to use a web-based booking interface two weeks in advance.
**Status:** **Untested.** Building the software before validating this is the highest risk. If this assumption is false, the software budget yields zero value.

**Required Cheap Test (Run before coding):**
*   **Action:** Conduct a "connectivity audit" with the 8 complaining cooperatives immediately.
*   **Method:** Do not ask "Do you have internet?" (hypothetical). Ask: "Show me the last time you used WhatsApp or a browser on the farm to solve a problem." Or, attempt to send a test link to their known numbers and measure response time/completion rate over 48 hours.
*   **Kill Threshold:** If fewer than 6 of the 8 cooperatives can reliably load a webpage or complete a digital form within 24 hours without assistance, **drop the web-based booking idea**. Pivot to USSD/SMS or phone-based partnerships immediately.

#### 2. Comparative Risk Breakdown

| Feature | Building Web Booking Tool | Partnering with Phone Networks / Expanding Fleet |
| :--- | :--- | :--- |
| **Primary Risk** | **Adoption Failure.** If connectivity is sporadic, the tool sits unused while crops spoil. The 15% loss continues despite the "solution." | **Operational Complexity.** Managing phone bookings manually or via USSD requires staff time; expanding fleet requires capital maintenance and driver management. |
| **Evidence Needed** | Real-world connectivity data from the 8 cooperatives (see Test above). | Cost-benefit analysis of manual coordination vs. software dev hours; marginal revenue of extra trucking capacity. |
| **Time to Value** | High latency. Requires build, test, deploy, and user training. | Immediate. Phone networks exist today; trucks move produce today. |
| **Failure Mode** | You build a perfect tool for a constraint (internet) that doesn't exist. | You scale operations but hit a coordination bottleneck later. |

#### 3. Recommendation & Next Steps

**Do not allocate the full Q3 budget to the software module yet.** The evidence provided (complaints about storage) validates the *problem* (spoilage), but explicitly invalidates the *solution* (web tool) until connectivity is proven.

**Immediate Action Plan (Before Friday):**
1.  **Run the Connectivity Test:** Contact the 8 cooperatives. Verify actual device usage and signal strength on-site.
2.  **Decision Rule:**
    *   **If Connectivity is Low:** Reallocate software budget to **partnering with existing phone-based networks** (USSD/SMS/Call centers) or hire a coordinator to manage bookings manually. This solves the spoilage issue immediately without relying on unproven infrastructure.
    *   **If Connectivity is High:** Proceed with a "Concierge MVP." Do not build the full automated module. Use a simple spreadsheet backend and have your team manually match bookings via phone/WhatsApp for two weeks. If this reduces spoilage, *then* automate with the Q3 budget.
3.  **Fleet Expansion Consideration:** Only choose fleet expansion if the bottleneck is confirmed to be *transport capacity* rather than *storage coordination*. The current data points to storage access, not truck availability. Expanding the fleet without solving the storage booking bottleneck may just move spoiled tomatoes faster.

**Verdict:** The risk of building the wrong interface (web) outweighs the benefit of speed. Validate connectivity first. If uncertain, default to the low-tech, high-reliability channel (phone/partner) to stop the 15% loss immediately.

*Note: This analysis relies on the "Riskiest Assumption" framework; no market size or user quotes were fabricated.*
*Ensure the connectivity test results are documented before the Friday meeting.*
