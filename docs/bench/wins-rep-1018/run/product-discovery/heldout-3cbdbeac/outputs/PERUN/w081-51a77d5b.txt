**Recommendation: Do not greenlight full development yet. Run a cheap disconfirmation test first.**

Your current evidence (five exit interviews) identifies a strong *stated* need, but it fails the "Riskiest-Assumption Gate" for two reasons:
1.  **Stated vs. Actual Behavior:** Exit interviews capture what users *say* caused churn, not necessarily the root cause. They may cite missing alerts because it is a concrete, fixable complaint, while the real driver might be pricing, support, or broader usability issues.
2.  **Solution Bias:** You are assuming the solution is an "alert system." The interviews suggest the *problem* is stockouts, but you have not validated whether a simple threshold warning solves it better than a manual check, a spreadsheet, or a different workflow. Building a complex system before validating the specific mechanism risks spending your $15k budget on the wrong fix.

**Immediate Action Plan (Cost < $500, Time < 1 Week):**
Before assigning lead developer sprint time, execute a **Concierge/Wizard-of-Oz test** to validate the *behavioral* value of alerts.

*   **The Test:** Manually simulate the alert for 3–5 current at-risk clients (not churned ones). When their inventory hits a low threshold in your database, have a team member personally email or SMS them: "Hi [Name], looks like you're low on [Item]. Want us to reserve stock?"
*   **The Metric:** Track the **conversion rate** of these manual alerts into confirmed orders or explicit "thank you/this saved me" responses.
*   **The Kill Threshold:** If fewer than **2 out of 10** manual alerts result in a positive behavioral signal (order placed or explicit validation) within two weeks, **do not build the automated feature**. This indicates the perceived value does not match the stated pain, or the timing/format is wrong.
*   **Decision Rule:**
    *   **Pass:** High engagement → Greenlight basic threshold alerts (MVP) for the sprint.
    *   **Fail:** Low engagement → Re-interview to understand why the alert didn't trigger action; pivot to a different solution or deprioritize.

**Sprint Planning Instruction for Lead Developer:**
*   **Current Sprint:** Do not allocate core dev hours to building the alert engine. Instead, allocate 4–8 hours to support the manual concierge test (data extraction for the team to send manual messages).
*   **Next Sprint (Conditional):** If the test passes the kill threshold, prioritize building the *simplest* threshold-based notification (email/SMS) only. Defer "predictive" or complex logic until basic thresholds prove retention value.

**Non-Goals for This Stage:**
*   Predictive analytics or AI-driven forecasting.
*   Complex dashboard visualizations for inventory trends.
*   Integration with external supplier systems.

**Summary of Evidence Status:**
*   **Problem Validation:** Moderate (5 churned clients is a signal, but small sample).
*   **Solution Validation:** None (No behavioral data yet).
*   **Verdict:** **UNVERIFIED**. Proceed to experiment, not build.
