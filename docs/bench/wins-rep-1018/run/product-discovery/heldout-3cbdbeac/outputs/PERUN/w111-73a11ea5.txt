**Recommendation: Do not greenlight full engineering development yet.**

The current evidence (five incidents) is insufficient to justify building a dedicated serial number tracking system. While the problem is real, the root cause is unverified, and the proposed solution is a high-cost infrastructure build based on an untested assumption.

**1. The Riskiest Assumption Gate**
Your critical assumption is: *"The mix-up originates at the third-party assembly partner's labeling process."*
This assumption currently has **high uncertainty** because your inbound inspection only checks batch totals. You have no data proving the units arrived mislabeled versus being mislabeled or swapped internally after arrival. Building a complex tracking system before validating this is building the wrong thing faster.

**2. The Cheap Test (Required Before Build)**
Before writing code for a tracking module, you must run a **manual forensic trace** on the next 50–100 units or the next specific warranty return.
*   **Action:** Manually record the serial number at three specific hand-off points: (A) Exit from assembly partner (request this data log from them), (B) Arrival at your inbound dock, and (C) Outbound shipment to the customer.
*   **Metric:** Identify exactly *where* the serial number record diverges from the physical unit reality.
*   **Kill Threshold:** If the discrepancy appears at point (B) or later, the assembly partner is likely innocent, and a factory-level tracking integration will not solve the problem. If the discrepancy exists at (A), the partner is the source.

**3. Evidence Evaluation**
*   **Volume:** 5 incidents out of ~6,000 units shipped (approx. 0.08%) is a signal, but it is statistically low for a systemic "refurbished vs. new" process failure without further pattern analysis. It could be an isolated batch error or a single fulfillment center glitch.
*   **Data Gap:** You lack the "granular visibility" you mentioned. A software system cannot fix a data gap that exists in the physical process; it only records what is scanned. If the physical scan logic is flawed, the software will just faithfully record the error.

**4. Immediate Next Steps for the Roadmap**
Instead of committing engineering resources to a full build:
1.  **Pause the build decision** until the manual forensic trace (above) is complete.
2.  **Engage the assembly partner immediately** to request their outbound serial number logs for the specific batches associated with the five claims. Compare these against your inbound batch totals.
3.  **Refine the scope:** If the trace confirms the partner is at fault, the solution may be a contractual requirement for them to provide a digital manifest, not a custom-built internal tracking module. If the error is internal, the solution may be a process change in inbound inspection, not a new software platform.

**Verdict:** The evidence warrants **investigation**, not **construction**. Re-evaluate the build decision only after the manual trace identifies the specific failure point.
