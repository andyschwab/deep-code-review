### Comparative Assessment: Pathway Editor vs. Analytics Dashboard

**1. Evidence Status & Stakeholder Alignment**
*   **Pathway Editor (Physician Focus):**
    *   **Supporting Evidence:** Direct qualitative data exists (60% of physicians report overwhelm). This addresses a stated "push" force in the user base.
    *   **Strategic Value:** Targets user retention at the point of daily friction. If physicians reject the tool due to admin burden, they may advocate for competitor EHRs during renewal.
    *   **Gap:** No behavioral data confirms that reducing admin time translates to the specific data quality required for population health metrics.
*   **Analytics Dashboard (Administrator Focus):**
    *   **Supporting Evidence:** Direct demand signal from hospital leadership ("demanding better metrics"). This aligns with the economic buyer's renewal criteria.
    *   **Strategic Value:** Directly addresses the criteria likely used by contract negotiators in December.
    *   **Gap:** Risk of building a "nice-to-have" if the underlying clinical data remains fragmented or incomplete due to physician workflow bottlenecks.

**2. Validity of Current Assumptions**
*   **Assumption:** "Improving physician satisfaction will indirectly boost data quality for analytics."
*   **Verdict:** **Unverified / High Risk.**
    *   This is the **riskiest assumption** in the current strategy. While logically plausible, there is no evidence in the provided facts linking "less overwhelm" to "better outcome reporting" specifically for Meridian's 85 hospitals.
    *   Physicians might use saved time for patient care rather than data entry, leaving the analytics dashboard starved of the structured data it needs to function.
    *   Proceeding with the Dashboard based solely on this assumption risks delivering a high-visibility tool that fails to show value due to poor input data.

**3. Strategic Conflict & Renewal Context**
*   **The Tension:** The choice is between satisfying the **daily user** (physician) to prevent churn from below, versus satisfying the **economic buyer** (administrator) to secure contract signatures from above.
*   **Renewal Implication (December):**
    *   Administrators hold the signing authority. A missing dashboard may be a direct contract blocker.
    *   However, if physician sentiment is critically low (60% overwhelmed), administrators may face internal pressure to switch systems regardless of analytics capabilities.
*   **Current Evidence Gap:** There is no data quantifying how much "admin overwhelm" influences the specific renewal decisions of the three major contracts up for negotiation.

**4. Required Validation Before Resource Allocation**
To resolve the conflict without guessing, the following evidence is missing and must be gathered before a final build decision:
*   **Test the Link:** Does reducing admin tasks actually improve the specific data fields needed for the population health metrics? (e.g., via a concierge test or audit of current data entry patterns).
*   **Renewal Driver Analysis:** For the three specific contracts up for renewal, is the primary threat physician revolt or lack of leadership metrics?
*   **Willingness to Pay/Stay:** Will administrators renew *without* the dashboard if physician workflow improves? Will physicians stay *without* workflow tools if the dashboard is delivered?

**Summary of Status**
*   **Pathway Editor:** Strong problem validation, weak linkage to revenue/outcome metrics.
*   **Analytics Dashboard:** Strong buyer demand, weak foundation validation (data quality risk).
*   **Decision State:** **UNVERIFIED.** Building either feature carries significant risk due to the unproven link between physician workflow and data quality, and the undefined weighting of physician vs. administrator influence on the upcoming renewals.
