# Decision Memo: Patient Communication Upgrade Path

**To:** VP of Engineering, Chief Compliance Officer
**From:** Product Discovery Lead
**Date:** [Current Date]
**Subject:** Recommendation on Patient-Provider Communication Strategy

### Executive Summary (BLUF)
**Recommendation:** Proceed immediately with the **Third-Party Secure Email Gateway** integration. Defer the custom portal build.
**Rationale:** The gateway resolves the active compliance risk within eight weeks at 22% of the custom build's upfront cost. The custom portal relies on an untested assumption regarding patient preference and an unverified technical constraint (SSO compatibility) that threatens the timeline.

### Evidence & Risk Analysis

**1. The Compliance Risk is Active and Immediate**
*   **Fact:** Two minor compliance incidents occurred this quarter using standard email.
*   **Implication:** The status quo is unacceptable. The primary objective is risk mitigation, not feature richness.
*   **Comparison:**
    *   *Gateway:* Live in 8 weeks. Risk window closed quickly.
    *   *Custom Portal:* Live in 6 months (approx. 24 weeks). The organization remains exposed to compliance incidents for an additional 16 weeks compared to the gateway option.

**2. The Riskiest Assumption Gate**
*   **Assumption:** "70% of our elderly patient demographic prefers email interfaces over logging into new portals."
*   **Status:** **Untested.** This is a hypothesis, not evidence.
*   **Risk:** If this assumption is false, the $180,000 custom build solves a problem users do not have (portal aversion) while failing to address the actual friction.
*   **Required Test Before Build:** Do not spend $180k until this is validated. However, since the *gateway* solution utilizes the existing email interface patients already use, it hedges against this assumption being wrong while still securing the data.

**3. The Technical Unknown (SSO Integration)**
*   **Unknown:** Whether the existing Identity Provider (IdP) supports the portal's SSO requirements without delay.
*   **Impact:** If the IdP requires significant customization, the 6-month timeline for the custom portal will slip, increasing costs and extending the compliance risk window.
*   **Mitigation:** The third-party gateway likely relies on standard protocols (SMTP/TLS) already in use, presenting a lower integration risk than a full custom SSO implementation.

### Long-Term Maintenance vs. Immediate Safety

| Feature | Option A: Custom Portal | Option B: Secure Gateway |
| :--- | :--- | :--- |
| **Time to Safety** | 6 Months (High Risk) | 8 Weeks (Low Risk) |
| **Upfront Cost** | $180,000 | ~$3,300 (prorated for 2 months) |
| **Recurring Cost** | Staff maintenance (dev/ops) | $40,000/year |
| **Compliance Burden** | Internal team owns audit/logs | Vendor owns core compliance |
| **Patient Friction** | New login required (Unverified preference) | Familiar email flow |

**Maintenance Reality:**
Building custom software creates a permanent maintenance liability (security patches, uptime, feature requests). For a non-differentiating utility like secure messaging, owning the infrastructure often costs more long-term than licensing a specialized vendor who spreads compliance costs across many clients.

### Decision Framework Application

1.  **Stop Building on Untested Assumptions:** We cannot justify a $180k build based on an unverified belief about elderly user preferences.
2.  **Cheapest Disconfirming Test:** The gateway *is* the test. It allows us to secure communications immediately using the interface patients currently use. If adoption drops or complaints rise, we have only spent a fraction of the custom build cost.
3.  **Non-Goals for This Stage:**
    *   *Not* building a branded patient experience (yet).
    *   *Not* solving for "portal fatigue" until we have data proving it exists.
    *   *Not* delaying compliance fixes to perfect the architecture.

### Recommended Next Steps

1.  **Approve Gateway Integration:** Allocate budget for the $40k/year license. Target go-live in 8 weeks.
2.  **Validate SSO Independently:** Task a senior engineer with a 2-day spike to confirm IdP compatibility for a *future* portal, but do not let this block the gateway decision.
3.  **Gather Real Evidence:** Once the gateway is live, track patient support tickets and adoption rates. Use this real data—not assumptions—to decide if a custom portal is ever warranted.

***

*Note: This memo structures the known facts and identifies the untested assumption; it does not fabricate patient feedback or market data. The "70% preference" figure must be validated via user research before any future custom build is scoped.*
