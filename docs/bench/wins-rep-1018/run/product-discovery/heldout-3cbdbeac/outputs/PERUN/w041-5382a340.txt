### Risk Profile Assessment: MedScribe AI Billing Pilot

**Current Status: UNVERIFIED / HIGH RISK**
Based on the data provided, proceeding to a pilot involving real billing codes without resolving the financial penalty variable is inadvisable. The current evidence indicates a critical failure mode in high-stakes scenarios that cannot be mitigated by process assumptions alone.

**1. Data Interpretation**
*   **General Performance:** 94% accuracy is insufficient for billing automation where the cost of error exceeds the value of speed.
*   **Critical Failure Rate:** 3 errors in 50 complex cardiology cases represents a **6% critical failure rate** in your highest-risk domain. In medical billing, a 6% error rate on complex claims likely triggers audits, fines, or legal exposure that outweighs the efficiency gains of automation.
*   **The "Human Reviewer" Assumption:** You assume a human reviewer checking *only* complex cases ensures compliance. This is a **riskiest assumption** that has not been tested. It relies on the reviewer perfectly identifying which cases are "complex" and catching the specific errors the AI makes. If the AI misclassifies a complex case as simple, or if the reviewer suffers from automation bias (trusting the AI output), the safety net fails.

**2. The Missing Variable: Financial Penalty Exposure**
You explicitly stated you do not know the financial penalty for a single incorrect code. **This is a routed unknown.**
*   **Action Required:** This number cannot be estimated by an AI or guessed. It must be sourced immediately from your Compliance Team or legal counsel specializing in healthcare reimbursement (e.g., False Claims Act liabilities, payer-specific clawback policies, audit triggers).
*   **Decision Rule:** Until the cost of one error ($X) is known, you cannot calculate if the efficiency gain covers the risk. If $X is high (which is typical in healthcare), a 6% failure rate may make the product financially non-viable regardless of speed.

**3. Required Validation Steps (Before August 10)**
Do not proceed to the pilot with real patient data until the following "cheap tests" are completed:

*   **Test A: The "Complex Case" Filter Validation**
    *   *Assumption:* Humans can reliably identify which cases need review.
    *   *Test:* Run a blind test where reviewers must flag complex cases without AI help. Measure the miss rate. If they miss >0% of the cases where the AI fails, the workflow is broken.
    *   *Kill Threshold:* If the human filter misses even one of the 3 known failure cases in a sample of 50, the "human oversight" strategy is invalid.

*   **Test B: Wizard-of-Oz Billing Simulation**
    *   *Assumption:* The hybrid workflow is efficient and compliant.
    *   *Test:* Simulate the billing process using the 50 complex cases. Have a human reviewer apply the proposed workflow. Submit these as dummy claims to a internal compliance audit (not real insurance).
    *   *Metric:* Count total errors slipping through AND measure the time cost of the review.
    *   *Kill Threshold:* If *any* incorrect code slips through to the "final" stage in this simulation, delay the pilot.

*   **Test C: Penalty Quantification (Immediate)**
    *   *Action:* Obtain the specific dollar value and regulatory consequence of a single coding error in cardiology from Compliance.
    *   *Outcome:* Use this number to calculate the "break-even" accuracy rate required. (e.g., If one error costs $50k, can you afford a 1% chance of it happening?)

**Recommendation**
**Delay the August 10 pilot for real patient data.** The 6% critical error rate in complex cases combined with an unknown penalty exposure creates an unacceptable liability. Execute Tests A, B, and C immediately. If Test B results in zero slipped errors and the penalty exposure is quantified as manageable relative to the efficiency gain, *then* reconsider the pilot scope. Do not build billing functionality on an untested assumption of human oversight.

*Note: This assessment structures your internal data; it does not constitute legal or compliance advice. The financial penalty figure must be supplied by your qualified compliance officer.*
