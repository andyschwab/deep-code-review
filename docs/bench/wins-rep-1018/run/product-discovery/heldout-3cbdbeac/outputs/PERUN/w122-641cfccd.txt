**DECISION MEMO: Secure Document Courier Feature Evaluation**

**TO:** Chief Compliance Officer, Engineering Director
**FROM:** Product Management
**DATE:** October 10, 2023
**SUBJECT:** Go/No-Go Recommendation for Integrated Document Tracking

**Executive Summary**
The recent audit findings from our two major bank clients highlight a critical liability gap in physical document transport. However, **we cannot yet justify the cost of building this feature.** The core assumption—that an integrated digital solution solves the compliance gap—is currently unverified against the primary constraint: regulatory acceptance of digital logs without physical signatures. Building now risks creating a polished solution for a problem it cannot legally solve.

**The Riskiest Assumption**
Our planning relies on the belief that "regulatory bodies will accept digital logs as sufficient proof of chain-of-custody without physical signatures." This is the single point of failure. If regulators require physical signatures regardless of the tracking interface, an integrated digital tool offers no compliance advantage over improved paperwork for existing carriers, rendering the build cost unjustifiable.

**Required Action: The Cheap Test (Before Build)**
We must pause development and execute a **Regulatory Validation Test** immediately.
*   **The Test:** Conduct formal inquiries or pre-submission meetings with the relevant regional regulatory bodies (or legal counsel specializing in local banking compliance) to present a mock digital log and ask specifically: "Is this sufficient for chain-of-custody audit trails in lieu of physical signatures?"
*   **Metric:** Binary confirmation (Yes/No/Conditional).
*   **Kill Threshold:** If the answer is "No" or "Conditional upon physical signature," **drop the feature idea immediately**. Do not build.
*   **Deadline:** October 13th (to meet the Oct 15th initiative proposal).

**Implications of Audit Findings**
The audits flagged "risks associated with transport," not specifically "lack of digital tracking."
*   **Scenario A (Regulators accept digital logs):** The audits justify the build. The gap is visibility and liability transfer, which our platform solves.
*   **Scenario B (Regulators require physical signatures):** The audits do *not* justify this specific build. The gap is procedural or carrier-related. The solution may be stricter SLAs with current third-party carriers or a hybrid process, not a new software feature.

**Recommendation**
1.  **Do not commit engineering resources** to this feature in Q4 until the Regulatory Validation Test is complete.
2.  **Assign Compliance/Legal** to secure a definitive answer on digital log acceptance by October 13th.
3.  **Proposal Strategy:** For the October 15th initiative list, propose this as a **"Validated Discovery Item"** contingent on the regulatory ruling, rather than a committed build. This protects the organization from building the wrong thing while demonstrating responsiveness to the audit risks.

**Conclusion**
The audit findings justify *investigating* the solution, but they do not yet justify *building* it. We are currently solving for "tracking" when the real blocker may be "legal admissibility." Verify the law before writing the code.

***

*Note: This memo structures the decision based on the "Riskiest Assumption" framework; no market size or user quotes were fabricated. The "Kill Threshold" is a proposed decision rule based on your stated regulatory uncertainty.*
