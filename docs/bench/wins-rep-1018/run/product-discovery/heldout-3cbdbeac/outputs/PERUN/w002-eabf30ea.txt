### Evaluation: Inventory Alert System Opportunity

**Current Evidence Status:** Strong problem signal; unvalidated solution trust.
The data confirms a high-cost problem: 40 incidents/month across two key accounts, costing ~12 hours each. However, your assumption that "automated alerts solve 80% of cases" rests entirely on an untested behavioral variable: **trust in the algorithm without manual verification.** If logistics teams verify every alert manually, the time savings vanish, and the value proposition collapses.

**Recommendation:** Do not commit full development resources yet. The riskiest assumption is **behavioral adoption**, not technical feasibility. You must validate whether users will *act* on the alert before building the automation engine.

#### Required Validation Steps (Before Sprint Commitment)

Execute these three low-effort tests to convert your assumption into evidence:

**1. The "Fake-Door" Trust Test (Behavioral Evidence)**
*   **Goal:** Measure if users act on alerts without verification.
*   **Method:** Manually generate the "3-day depletion" data for the next 2 weeks. Send the alerts via email/Slack as if the system were live, but include a simple "Confirm Order" button or reply instruction.
*   **Metric:** % of alerts where the user initiates an order *without* calling/emailing your support team to double-check the number first.
*   **Kill Threshold:** If fewer than **50%** of alerts result in direct action (or if >30% trigger a "verification call"), the trust gap is too wide for an automated MVP. Pivot to a "recommendation only" interface or delay build.

**2. The "Mom Test" Logistics Interview (Root Cause)**
*   **Goal:** Understand *why* they might not trust the algorithm.
*   **Method:** Interview 2–3 logistics managers from MedCore/HealthPlus. Ask about the *last* time they faced a stockout.
    *   *Ask:* "Walk me through exactly how you decided to reorder last time."
    *   *Ask:* "What specific data points made you doubt your supplier's previous recommendations?"
    *   *Avoid:* "Would you trust our new algorithm?" (They will lie to be polite).
*   **Output:** A list of specific "trust blockers" (e.g., "We don't trust your consumption data latency," or "Our finance team requires a second signature").

**3. Concierge Pilot (Value Verification)**
*   **Goal:** Confirm the 12-hour savings claim is realizable.
*   **Method:** Select 5 critical antibiotic lines. Manually monitor stock and call the procurement head 3 days before projected depletion with the exact order quantity. Track the time from your call to their confirmation.
*   **Metric:** Actual hours saved compared to their baseline 12-hour delay.
*   **Decision Rule:** If the manual process doesn't save at least **8 hours** (validating the 80% hypothesis), the automated version won't either.
