### Decision: Do Not Redesign Yet; Run Targeted Validation First

A complete redesign is a high-cost solution based on low-confidence evidence. Five detailed reviews and fifteen tickets indicate a real problem for *some* users, but they do not prove the issue is widespread enough to impact the 3% retention drop across 8,000 users. The "silent majority" may navigate successfully, or the drop may stem from unrelated factors (e.g., pricing, content gaps, or technical performance).

Proceed with the following two-step validation plan before committing design resources:

#### 1. Quantitative Triage (Immediate, Low Effort)
Determine the scope of the friction without building new features.
*   **Funnel Drop-off Analysis:** Isolate the specific path "Login → Lab Results." Calculate the drop-off rate at each step compared to the previous quarter. If users are clicking through successfully but complaining about "too many taps," the drop-off rate will remain stable even if satisfaction drops.
*   **Session Replay Sampling:** Watch 20–30 random session recordings of users attempting to access lab results. Look for "rage clicks," back-tracking, or long dwell times on navigation menus. This confirms if the behavior matches the complaints without needing new code.
*   **In-App Micro-Survey:** Trigger a one-question survey *only* after a user successfully views a lab result: "How easy was it to find this result?" (Scale: Very Easy to Very Hard). Target 200 responses. This quantifies sentiment among successful users, not just those who failed.

#### 2. Qualitative Verification (High Confidence)
Verify if the complaint is about *navigation* or *accessibility/expectations*.
*   **Targeted Interviews (5 Users):** Recruit 5 users who recently opened a support ticket regarding lab results and 5 who successfully viewed results but rated the ease-of-use low in the micro-survey.
    *   **Ask:** "Walk me through the last time you looked for a specific result. What were you trying to do?" (Observe, do not guide).
    *   **Goal:** Distinguish between "too many taps" (efficiency) vs. "I couldn't find the button" (findability) vs. "I have vision issues and the contrast is poor" (accessibility). A redesign solves efficiency; an accessibility fix solves the latter. A findability issue might only require a label change, not a full overhaul.

#### Go/No-Go Criteria for Redesign
*   **Proceed to Redesign IF:** Funnel analysis shows a >15% increase in drop-off at the navigation step OR session replays show >40% of users hesitating/back-tracking OR interviews reveal fundamental findability failures across both complainants and silent users.
*   **Do Not Redesign (Iterate Instead) IF:** Drop-off rates are stable, session replays show smooth navigation, or interviews reveal the issue is isolated to specific accessibility needs or misunderstood labels. In this case, implement specific fixes (e.g., larger buttons, clearer labels) rather than a full rebuild.

**Recommendation:** Allocate 3–5 days to gather this data. If the problem is widespread, you will have the exact metrics to justify the redesign. If it is isolated, you save weeks of development time by solving the root cause directly.

*Note: The 3% retention drop requires separate correlation analysis; do not assume it is caused solely by portal navigation until the funnel data confirms a spike in churn immediately following failed lab result attempts.*
*Do not interview users about "what they would like"; observe what they actually do, as stated preferences often differ from actual behavior.*
