**Assessment: Do Not Build Yet – Evidence Insufficient for Urgent Initiative**

The current evidence does not support an immediate build of a digital tracker. While the safety concerns are severe, the assumption that a digital tracker is the correct solution remains untested, and the primary constraint (administrative approval for wireless devices) is unresolved. Proceeding to build now risks creating a solution that cannot be deployed or that solves the wrong problem.

**1. The Riskiest Assumption Gate**
Your critical assumption is: *"A digital tracker with wireless capabilities is the only viable way to eliminate the 4-hour lag and prevent safety breaches."*
*   **Status:** Untested and currently threatened by known constraints.
*   **The Conflict:** You cite "wireless devices" as the likely solution, yet you explicitly state the administration may ban them near artifacts. Building a wireless system before confirming its permissibility is a waste of resources.
*   **Required Test Before Build:** Run a **concierge test** (manual simulation) immediately.
    *   *Action:* For the next 48 hours, assign a human runner with a clipboard or a non-wireless handheld device (if allowed) to shadow every movement, logging data in real-time and verifying it instantly.
    *   *Metric:* Does this manual process reduce the verification lag from 4 hours to <15 minutes?
    *   *Kill Threshold:* If the manual process fails to reduce lag significantly due to human error or workflow bottlenecks (not technology), then a software build alone will not solve the problem. If the manual process works but is too labor-intensive, *then* you have evidence for automation.

**2. Evidence Gap: Severity vs. Frequency**
*   **Current Data:** 3 concerns, 2 near-miss incidents in one month.
*   **Analysis:** While "near-misses" are high-severity, the frequency (2 in a month) must be contextualized against the total volume of movements. Without the denominator (total moves per month), the statistical risk is unknown.
*   **Missing Evidence:** You need the **total number of movements** last month to calculate the failure rate. If there were 2,000 moves, the failure rate is 0.1%. If there were 10, the rate is 20%. The board will require this rate to justify emergency spending.

**3. The Political Constraint (The Real Blocker)**
The "conservative administration" is a known stakeholder with veto power.
*   **Fact:** They may ban wireless devices.
*   **Implication:** Building a wireless tracker now is premature.
*   **Immediate Action:** Before any code is written, you must secure a **feasibility ruling** on the technology stack. Present three options to the administration:
    1.  Wireless (Wi-Fi/Bluetooth) – High risk of rejection.
    2.  Wired/Offline-first devices (data synced only outside storage) – Lower risk.
    3.  Purely procedural change (no new hardware).
    *   *Decision Rule:* Do not write a single line of code until the administration signs off on *which* technology class is permissible.

**Recommendation: The Next 7 Days**
Do not start the build. Instead, execute this validation loop:
1.  **Run the Concierge Test:** Manually simulate real-time logging for 48 hours to prove the *process* works, regardless of the tool.
2.  **Quantify the Risk:** Calculate the near-miss rate (Incidents / Total Movements).
3.  **Secure Technology Approval:** Get a definitive "Yes/No" on wireless vs. offline hardware from the administration.

**Verdict:** The evidence supports an urgent *investigation* and *process redesign*, but it does not yet support an urgent *software build*. Build only after the concierge test proves the workflow reduces lag and the administration approves the specific hardware required to automate it.

*Note: The "end of month" deadline for a formal halt is a political trigger, not a technical one; use the concierge test results to argue for a temporary procedural fix if the tech ban stalls the digital solution.*
