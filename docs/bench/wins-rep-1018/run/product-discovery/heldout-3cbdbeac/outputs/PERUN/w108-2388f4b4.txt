**Decision Recommendation: NO-GO on full system overhaul at this time.**

**Reasoning:** Your proposal relies on a critical untested assumption: that automating digital tags ensures 100% compliance. This assumes inspectors can and will scan digital tags. If inspectors lack the hardware, software, or mandate to accept *only* digital records, your automated system fails the primary requirement, leaving you liable for the 50,000 EUR fines despite the technical upgrade. Building a complex dynamic tagging system before verifying the inspector's actual workflow is high-risk over-engineering.

**Immediate Action Plan (Riskiest Assumption Gate):**
Before writing code or overhauling the system, you must disconfirm the riskiest assumption: *"Inspectors are equipped and authorized to rely solely on digital hazardous material tags."*

**The Cheap Test:**
Do not build the automation yet. Instead, run a "Concierge Test" with your next scheduled shipment or a mock audit simulation.
1.  **Action:** Manually prepare a shipment with the proposed *digital-only* tag (no paper backup) and present it to a friendly safety inspector or simulate the inspection with your current compliance officer acting as the inspector using their actual field tools.
2.  **Metric:** Can the inspector successfully validate the hazard class using *only* the digital tag within the standard inspection timeframe?
3.  **Kill Threshold:** If the inspector asks for paper documentation, hesitates for more than 2 minutes, or cannot access the data on their device, **STOP** the overhaul. The solution must include a hybrid (paper + digital) or paper-first approach until the ecosystem matures.

**Required Evidence to Proceed:**
You currently have zero evidence regarding inspector capabilities. You need answers to these specific questions before any build decision:
*   **Inspector Capability:** Do the Bratislava inspection teams currently possess scanners/tablets capable of reading your specific digital format on the road?
*   **Regulatory Text:** Does the new EU directive explicitly state that *digital-only* manifests are legally sufficient, or is it silent on the medium, implying paper is still the default?
*   **Current Gap:** Of the three warnings issued last week, was the failure due to missing *data fields* (which automation solves) or missing *format/medium* (which automation might worsen if it removes paper)?

**Non-Goals for This Stage:**
*   **Full Automation:** Do not automate the tagging logic until the medium (digital vs. paper) is confirmed.
*   **System Overhaul:** Do not replace the existing labeling infrastructure yet.
*   **Assuming Compliance:** Do not treat "automated" as synonymous with "compliant."

**Next Steps for Compliance Officer & VP:**
1.  **Pause** the development budget for the dynamic tagging overhaul.
2.  **Assign** a team member to physically visit the inspection depot or call the regulatory body to confirm: "Is a digital-only manifest legally and practically acceptable for lithium-ion transport today?"
3.  **Re-evaluate** the build scope only after receiving a definitive "Yes, digital-only is accepted and scanned routinely" or "No, you must provide paper alongside digital."

If the answer is "No" or "Unsure," the immediate solution is a manual process update to ensure paper documentation includes the missing details, not a software overhaul.
