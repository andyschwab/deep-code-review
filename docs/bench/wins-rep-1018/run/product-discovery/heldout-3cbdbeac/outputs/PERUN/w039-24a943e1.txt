### Risk Evaluation: Static Translations vs. Live Multilingual System

**Decision Context:** The choice between static translated documents and a dynamic live system hinges on two unverified assumptions: (1) that the target population has high literacy in their native scripts, and (2) that text-based communication suffices for complex medical intake. Current data shows 45% of patients struggle with intake due to language, but does not distinguish between *language* barriers and *literacy* barriers.

#### Riskiest Assumption Gate
The critical assumption driving the "static only" approach is: **"Patients can read and comprehend medical instructions in their native written language."**
*   **Impact:** High. If false, static documents fail 100% of illiterate or low-literacy patients, leaving the 45% struggle rate unchanged or worsening it due to false confidence.
*   **Uncertainty:** Critical. Literacy rates in native scripts for this specific displaced population are unknown.
*   **Cheapest Test:** Conduct a "paper prototype" test with 10 patients from the top 3 language groups. Hand them a translated form and ask them to identify three specific fields (e.g., "Where do you write your birth date?").
*   **Kill Threshold:** If >30% of tested patients cannot correctly identify the fields without verbal aid, the static-only approach is rejected. Proceed to voice/live support design.

#### Key Risks of Sticking with Static Translations

1.  **The Literacy Blind Spot (Desirability Risk)**
    *   **Risk:** Static documents assume reading capability. Many refugee populations may have oral traditions, low formal education, or trauma affecting literacy. If literacy is low, static translations are operationally useless, wasting volunteer time on distribution rather than care.
    *   **Evidence Needed:** Direct observation of patients attempting to fill out current translated forms. Do they ask for help immediately? Do they leave fields blank?

2.  **Complexity Mismatch (Usability Risk)**
    *   **Risk:** Medical intake involves nuance (symptoms, history) that static forms often oversimplify. Without real-time clarification, patients may provide inaccurate data, leading to misdiagnosis or delayed care. Static text cannot answer "What does this word mean?"
    *   **Evidence Needed:** Error rates in completed forms. How many forms require follow-up clarification by a doctor?

3.  **Volunteer Bottleneck (Viability Risk)**
    *   **Risk:** Relying on volunteers to bridge the gap when static forms fail creates an unpredictable workflow. If 45% struggle, and static docs don't fix it, volunteers become triage agents rather than facilitators. A live system (even simple voice notes) could async-match patients to specific language volunteers more efficiently.
    *   **Evidence Needed:** Time-tracking: How long does a volunteer spend helping one patient vs. how many patients could a live system route simultaneously?

#### Key Risks of Building a Live System Prematurely

1.  **Over-Engineering (Feasibility Risk)**
    *   **Risk:** Building a complex real-time translation app before validating the need for *voice* interaction wastes limited dev resources. If the issue is purely vocabulary (solved by a glossary) rather than literacy, a simple app is overkill.
    *   **Mitigation:** Start with a "Wizard of Oz" test: Use existing messaging tools (WhatsApp/Signal) where volunteers manually reply to voice notes, simulating a live system without building software.

2.  **Adoption Friction**
    *   **Risk:** Introducing new technology to a vulnerable population requires trust and device access. If patients lack smartphones or data, a live app fails regardless of utility.
    *   **Evidence Needed:** Device ownership survey within the camp.

#### Recommendation for Program Directors
Do not commit to a full build yet. The 45% struggle rate confirms a problem, but not the solution.
1.  **Run the Literacy Test immediately** (3 days max).
2.  **If literacy is low:** Pivot to voice-based intake (live system required).
3.  **If literacy is high but comprehension is low:** Optimize static forms with visual aids before building software.
4.  **Funding Narrative:** Frame the June 30 request as "Validating the Channel." You are not asking for money to build a guess; you are asking for resources to instrument the *correct* solution based on the literacy test results. This demonstrates fiscal responsibility and evidence-based design.

*Note: Do not estimate literacy rates; the test above is mandatory before any architectural decision. The "Kill Threshold" ensures you do not build a dynamic system if static visuals suffice, nor stick to paper if illiteracy is the root cause.*
