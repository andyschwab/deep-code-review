# Strategic Overview: National Scale Expansion Risks & Evidence Gaps

**Decision Context**
GlobalAid Tech faces a go/no-go decision for a national rollout (5,000 users) by December, contingent on an October grant review. While the central region pilot (200 users) demonstrated a 94% reduction in reporting time (3 days → 4 hours), critical evidence gaps regarding the northern districts prevent a validated recommendation for immediate full-scale expansion.

**Critical Evidence Gap: The Riskiest Assumption**
Your current plan relies on a single untested assumption: *The workflow and technical performance validated in the central region will translate perfectly to the northern districts.*

This is the **riskiest assumption** because:
1.  **High Impact:** If false, the core value proposition (real-time sync) fails, rendering the tool useless for the target demographic.
2.  **High Uncertainty:** You explicitly noted unknown network connectivity issues in the north. Real-time sync is highly sensitive to latency and packet loss, which vary significantly by region.

**Operational & Technical Risks**
*   **Connectivity Failure Mode:** If northern network infrastructure cannot support real-time sync, the app may time out or lose data, increasing worker frustration and reverting reporting times to pre-app levels (or worse, due to failed attempts).
*   **Infrastructure Ceiling:** Current infrastructure supports 500 concurrent users. A national rollout to 5,000 users requires a 10x capacity increase. Without load testing against northern usage patterns (which may differ due to connectivity-driven retry loops), the system risks total outage during peak reporting windows.
*   **Workflow Mismatch:** Northern health officials may have different reporting cadences or data validation needs not captured in the central pilot.

**Recommended Path: The Cheap Test Before Build**
Do not commit to a full national rollout or expensive infrastructure scaling yet. The evidence is insufficient. Instead, execute a **targeted northern district pilot** as a disconfirmation test.

**Test Design:**
*   **Objective:** Disconfirm the assumption that real-time sync works in the north.
*   **Sample:** 50 health workers in the most connectivity-constrained northern district.
*   **Duration:** 4 weeks (conclude before October grant review).
*   **Metric:** Successful sync rate (%) and average reporting time.
*   **Kill Threshold:** If successful sync rate < 80% OR average reporting time > 12 hours, **do not proceed** with the real-time sync architecture for the national rollout. Pivot to an offline-first/store-and-forward model.

**Strategic Recommendation for Grant Committee**
Present two scenarios to the committee:
1.  **Conditional Rollout:** Commit funds *only* upon passing the northern pilot test. Allocate 20% of the budget to the test and 80% to scaling, released contingent on results.
2.  **Phased Approach:** Explicitly fund the northern pilot as "Phase 2," delaying the national infrastructure spend until connectivity constraints are quantified.

**Non-Goals for This Stage**
*   **Full Infrastructure Scale-Up:** Do not build for 5,000 concurrent users until the northern connectivity model is proven.
*   **Feature Parity:** Do not assume the central region feature set is final; the north may require offline capabilities that were unnecessary in the central pilot.

**Conclusion**
The central pilot proved *desirability* and *viability* in one context. It has not proven *feasibility* in the target expansion context. The grant committee should fund a evidence-based validation step (the northern pilot) rather than a blind infrastructure expansion.
