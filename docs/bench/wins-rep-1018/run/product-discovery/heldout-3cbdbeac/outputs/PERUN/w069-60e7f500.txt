**Board Summary: Population Health Dashboard Proposal**

**Current Evidence**
*   **Usage:** Pilot data (3 clinics) shows low engagement: 15 mins/week average review time; only 2 of 12 physicians requested features.
*   **Assumption:** Small practices lack admin staff to act on insights.
*   **Unknowns:** Willingness to pay $99/mo; impact on the 4% annual churn rate.

**Decision Status: UNVERIFIED**
We cannot currently determine if this feature drives retention or adds complexity. The existing pilot data suggests low desirability, but we have not tested the specific value proposition against a payment trigger. Building the full module now risks gold-plating an unvalidated assumption.

**Riskiest Assumption & Required Test**
*   **Assumption:** Practices will pay $99/mo because the dashboard reduces their administrative burden or improves patient outcomes enough to justify the cost.
*   **Cheapest Test:** A "fake-door" or concierge test before Q3 development.
    *   **Action:** Offer the dashboard as a paid beta ($99/mo) to the existing 12 physicians and 50 similar prospects. Do not build the automation yet; offer to generate the reports manually or via a limited prototype.
    *   **Metric:** Conversion rate to paid beta.
    *   **Kill Threshold:** If fewer than **3 out of 60** (5%) prospects commit to paying, **do not build**. The development cost cannot be justified by the projected revenue.
    *   **Deadline:** 2 weeks.

**Potential Value Proposition (Hypothesis Only)**
*   *If* the test passes: The dashboard transforms raw data into actionable alerts that replace manual chart reviews, saving >15 mins/week per doctor and directly linking to revenue (e.g., closing care gaps).
*   *Current Reality:* The 15-minute usage without feature requests suggests the current data presentation is passive, not actionable.

**Risks**
1.  **Complexity Trap:** Adding a dashboard increases UI clutter for users who already show low engagement, potentially increasing churn rather than reducing it.
2.  **Staffing Mismatch:** If the assumption holds (no admin staff), doctors may see the data but lack the workflow bandwidth to act, rendering the insights worthless regardless of accuracy.
3.  **False Positive:** Mistaking "interest" in a demo for willingness to pay. Only actual credit card commits count as validation.

**Recommendation**
**Do not allocate Q3 development resources yet.** Run the paid beta test immediately.
*   **Go:** If ≥5% convert to paid beta.
*   **No-Go:** If <5% convert. Pivot to investigating *why* the current 15-minute review yields no action (e.g., is the data irrelevant, or is the workflow broken?) before building more features.

**Non-Goals for Q3**
*   Building automated data pipelines or polished visualizations.
*   Expanding the feature set beyond the single highest-value metric identified in the test.

***

*Note: This evaluation relies strictly on the provided pilot metrics and explicitly refuses to fabricate market size or willingness-to-pay figures. The "Kill Threshold" is a proposed decision rule based on standard SaaS conversion baselines, adjustable by your unit economics.*
