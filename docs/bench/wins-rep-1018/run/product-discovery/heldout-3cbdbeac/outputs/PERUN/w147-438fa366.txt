**Decision:** There is currently insufficient evidence to proceed with building the icebreaker scheduling optimizer. The proposal addresses a hypothesized bottleneck (route inefficiency) without validating whether that is the actual constraint causing shortages. Building this tool now risks solving a secondary symptom while the primary failure point—potentially vessel availability or loading delays—remains unaddressed.

**Riskiest Assumption Gate**
Your working assumption is that "optimized routing will cut transit time by 20%." This is the single riskiest assumption because the entire value proposition collapses if the bottleneck lies elsewhere.
*   **Required Test Before Build:** Conduct a "Root Cause Reconstruction" on the two failed January/February missions.
*   **Method:** Map the exact timeline of the turn-backs against vessel logs. Did the vessels turn back due to *unforeseen* ice that better routing would have avoided? Or did they turn back because no vessel was available to attempt a second run, or because port loading delays consumed the weather window?
*   **Kill Threshold:** If >50% of the delay in past failures was caused by factors *other* than suboptimal route selection (e.g., lack of spare vessels, mechanical failure, port bottlenecks), **drop the scheduling optimizer idea** and pivot to addressing those specific constraints.
