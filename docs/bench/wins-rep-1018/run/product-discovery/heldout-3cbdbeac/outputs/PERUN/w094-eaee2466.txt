### Decision Framework: Dynamic Pricing Risk vs. Revenue Impact

**Current Evidence Status:**
*   **Validated Problem:** 10 rider departures confirmed due to unpredictable earnings in rain.
*   **Unvalidated Assumption:** A 25% rate increase will retain riders without triggering corporate churn.
*   **Unknown Variable:** Corporate client price elasticity and contract flexibility regarding weather surcharges.

**Riskiest Assumption Gate**
Before implementing a system-wide dynamic pricing engine, you must validate the **Corporate Acceptance Assumption**. If key accounts churn, the revenue gain from retained riders is negated.

**Proposed Cheap Test (Pre-Build):**
Do not build the automated pricing feature yet. Run a **Concierge Test** with your top 5 corporate accounts.
*   **Action:** Manually propose a "Winter Weather Surcharge" addendum to their current contracts for the upcoming season. Frame it as a guarantee of service availability during storms.
*   **Metric:** Signed acceptance or verbal commitment from account holders.
*   **Numeric Kill Threshold:** If **fewer than 3 of 5** key accounts agree to the surcharge (or a negotiated variant), **do not build** the automated dynamic pricing system. Instead, pivot to a flat-rate "Winter Premium" model or absorb the cost as a retention expense.
*   **Deadline:** Complete these 5 conversations before next Tuesday's strategy meeting.

**Revenue Impact Analysis Structure (Blank Inputs)**
To present a balanced view to the CEO, populate the following variables with your internal data. Do not guess these numbers.

| Variable | How to Obtain | Input Slot |
| :--- | :--- | :--- |
| **A: Rainy Week Volume** | Query database for avg. deliveries during rain events last year. | `[Insert #]` |
| **B: Current Rider Churn Cost** | Calculate cost to recruit/train a replacement rider (based on the 10 lost). | `[Insert €]` |
| **C: Projected Retention Gain** | Estimate % of churn prevented by 25% hike (requires rider interview, not assumption). | `[Insert %]` |

**Calculation Logic:**
*   **Potential Upside:** `(A × 25% rate) + (Retained Riders × Lifetime Value)`
*   **Potential Downside:** `(D × E)`
*   **Decision Rule:** Proceed only if **Upside > Downside** based on *real* test results, not estimates.

**Non-Goals for This Stage**
*   **Building automated weather-integration APIs:** This is a non-goal until the manual concierge test proves clients will pay.
*   **Rolling out to all clients simultaneously:** A non-goal until the pilot with top 5 accounts succeeds.
*   **Assuming rider retention:** A non-goal to assume the 25% fixes the issue without asking the 10 departed riders if that specific amount would have changed their decision.

**Immediate Next Steps for Tuesday's Meeting**
1.  **Conduct the 5 client conversations** using the concierge script.
2.  **Interview 3 of the 10 departed riders** to confirm if 25% dynamic pricing specifically addresses their "unpredictability" concern, or if they need guaranteed minimums instead.
3.  **Present the Kill Threshold result:** Either "We have 4/5 clients aligned, proceed to build" or "We failed the threshold, here is the alternative flat-rate proposal."

**Note:** Do not present a theoretical revenue model to the CEO; present the results of the client conversations as the primary decision driver.
**Note:** If client feedback suggests "flat rate with higher baseline" over "dynamic surge," update the proposal before the meeting.
