### Executive Summary: Risk Assessment & Validation Plan

**Current State:** Five key accounts ($2.4M ARR) demand native Excel export by August 30. Two threaten churn.
**Proposed Build:** 4 developers × 6 weeks = ~24 developer-weeks of effort.
**Critical Assumption:** Delivering native Excel export guarantees renewal.
**Risk Status:** **UNVERIFIED.** Committing to this build without validation risks wasting 24 developer-weeks on a solution that may not address the root cause of churn (data flexibility vs. file format).

#### 1. The Riskiest Assumption Gate
The single riskiest assumption is that **"File format (.xlsx) is the blocker, not data structure or workflow."**
*   **Impact:** High ($2.4M ARR at risk).
*   **Uncertainty:** High (You explicitly stated you do not know if they need complex formatting or just a usable CSV).
*   **Cheap Test Required:** Before allocating engineering resources, you must disconfirm the idea that a better CSV or a concierge workaround solves the renewal threat.
*   **Kill Threshold:** If fewer than 3 of the 5 customers confirm that *specifically* the `.xlsx` extension (and not column formatting, formulas, or data grouping) is the contractual blocker, the full build scope must be paused in favor of a targeted data-flexibility fix.

#### 2. Recommended Discovery Questions (The "Mom Test" Approach)
Do not ask "Do you want Excel?" (They will say yes). Ask about past behavior and specific workflow failures to isolate the real job-to-be-done.

**Ask these specific questions to the champions at the 5 accounts:**
1.  "Walk me through the last time you tried to use the current CSV for a quarterly report. Exactly what step failed or required manual fix?"
2.  "When you open the CSV, what is the very first thing you have to change before you can send it to your boss? (e.g., splitting columns, fixing dates, adding formulas?)"
3.  "Have you ever lost a deal or delayed a report specifically because the file was a CSV and not an XLSX? Can you describe that incident?"
4.  "If we provided a CSV that automatically grouped the data exactly how you need it, would you still require the native Excel file? Why or why not?"
5.  "What competitor feature specifically are you comparing us to? Is it the file type, or the ability to pivot the data inside that file?"

#### 3. Trade-off Analysis for VP of Sales

| Option | Effort | Risk | Potential Outcome |
| :--- | :--- | :--- | : |
| **A. Full Native Build** | High (24 dev-weeks) | **High:** May solve the wrong problem. If the issue is data grouping, an Excel file with bad data still fails. | Secures renewals *only if* format is the sole blocker. High opportunity cost (delays other fixes). |
| **B. Targeted Data Fix** | Medium | **Medium:** Customers may still reject non-native files due to policy. | Solves the actual workflow pain (formatting/grouping). Faster to ship. May still lose clients who strictly mandate `.xlsx`. |
| **C. Concierge/Workaround** | Low | **Low:** Manual effort for 5 clients only. | Buys time to validate. If clients accept a manually formatted Excel file sent via email, the *automation* is not the renewal blocker. |

**Recommendation:** Do not commit the full team yet. Run the interviews above immediately. If the feedback reveals the pain is "manipulating data" rather than "file extension," propose Option B (Data Flexibility) as the primary retention lever, potentially delivering a "good enough" Excel file manually (Option C) for the two at-risk clients by August 30 while building the robust solution.

**Note:** A "very disappointed" survey or retention cohort analysis cannot be performed yet as no new feature has shipped; validation must come from the proposed interviews.
**Note:** The August 30 deadline requires a decision on the build path within 5 days to allow any development time.
