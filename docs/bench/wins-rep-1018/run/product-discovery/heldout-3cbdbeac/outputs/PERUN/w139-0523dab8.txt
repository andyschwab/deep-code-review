**Decision: NO-GO on building the scheduler module at this time.**

**Reasoning: The Riskiest Assumption Gate is not cleared.**
Your current evidence identifies a *symptom* (brands complaining about delays/visibility) but validates neither the *cause* (lack of digital locking) nor the *solution* (a scheduler feature). You are proposing a three-developer-month investment based on stated intent from one side of the marketplace (brands) while holding zero behavioral evidence from the other side (factories) who must adopt the tool.

**Evidence Analysis vs. Unknowns**
1.  **The Brand Signal (Weak Validation):** The four delay calls confirm that brands are unhappy with missed windows. However, they do not confirm that a "scheduling interface" is the fix. Brands may simply care about the final delivery date; their demand for "visibility" might be satisfied by a weekly SMS update or a manual spreadsheet sent by you, rather than a software feature forcing factories to act. You have not tested if they would *pay* for visibility or just complain about delays.
2.  **The Factory Risk (Untested):** Your assumption that factories will "lock in times digitally" is the single point of failure. If factories resist this administrative step (high friction), the system breaks, and the delays continue. You have zero evidence regarding their willingness to change their current workflow.
3.  **The Cost of Error:** Building now risks creating a "ghost town" app where brands see empty slots because factories refuse to use it. This wastes three developer months and delays solving the actual root cause, which might be operational (e.g., your team not following up) rather than technological.

**Required Next Steps (Cheapest Tests)**
Before writing code, you must disconfirm the riskiest assumption: *Factories will reliably commit to digital pickup slots.*

*   **Test A (Concierge/Wizard-of-Oz):** Do not build the module. For the next 10 shipments, manually call the factories to get times, lock them in a shared Google Sheet, and email the link to the brands.
    *   *Metric:* Does this manual process stop the delay calls?
    *   *Kill Threshold:* If you still receive >1 complaint about visibility in the next 30 days despite manual coordination, the problem is not the tool; it is the operational process. Do not build.
*   **Test B (Fake Door for Factories):** Ask your 15 onboarding factories to reply to a WhatsApp message with their next available slot. Track the response time and accuracy.
    *   *Metric:* % of factories providing a specific time within 2 hours.
    *   *Kill Threshold:* If <60% comply without a software interface, a new app will not fix the behavior; it will likely worsen adoption.

**Conclusion for Investor/Co-founder**
We are halting the 3-month build. The evidence supports a *communication problem*, not necessarily a *software gap*. We will run a 2-week manual concierge test to prove that structured data (even manual) solves the brand complaints and that factories will provide that data. Only if both metrics pass do we allocate budget to automate the workflow.
