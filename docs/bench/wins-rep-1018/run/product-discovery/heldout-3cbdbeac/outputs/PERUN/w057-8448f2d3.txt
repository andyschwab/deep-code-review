### Validation Plan: FamilyLink Caregiver Coordination

**1. Current Evidence Status**
*   **Problem Validated:** 75% (15/20) of interviews confirm missed appointments due to scheduling confusion; 60% (12/20) confirm sibling coordination stress.
*   **Solution Assumption (Unverified):** That a shared calendar is the correct mechanism to solve this.
*   **Willingness to Pay (Unverified):** No monetary commitment observed; current behavior relies on free tools (text threads, paper).
*   **Adoption Risk (High):** Users may resist migrating from established, zero-cost habits.

**2. Immediate Next Step: The Riskiest-Assumption Gate**
Do not begin development in February. The riskiest assumption is not "can we build it?" but "will families switch from free text threads to a dedicated app?"
*   **Assumption:** Families value centralized coordination enough to change their current workflow.
*   **Cheapest Test:** A "Concierge" or "Fake-Door" test.
    *   *Action:* Create a simple landing page or manual service offering the "Shared Caregiver Calendar."
    *   *Metric:* Conversion rate from visitor to active user (or pre-order).
    *   **Kill Threshold:** If fewer than **5 out of 50** targeted families sign up or commit to a trial after seeing the specific value proposition, **do not build**. This indicates the pain is not strong enough to overcome habit.
    *   *Sample Size:* 50 qualified leads (families currently managing elderly care).
    *   *Deadline:* 2 weeks.

**3. Required Signals to Confirm Direction**
To satisfy investors and validate market demand, you must gather the following behavioral evidence before writing code:

*   **Signal A: Behavioral Commitment (Not Intent)**
    *   *Current Gap:* Interviews measure stated interest, not action.
    *   *Required Data:* Users must perform an action that costs them something (time, data entry, or money).
    *   *How to Obtain:* Run the test above. Measure how many users actually upload a schedule or invite a sibling during a trial, rather than just saying "yes" in an interview.

*   **Signal B: Willingness to Pay (WTP)**
    *   *Current Gap:* No money discussed.
    *   *Required Data:* A specific price point where conversion occurs.
    *   *How to Obtain:* In the test, present three tiers (e.g., Free, $4.99/mo, $9.99/mo). If 0% select paid tiers, the business model is unproven. If they won't pay $5, they likely won't sustain a startup.

*   **Signal C: Retention Proxy (Early Cohort)**
    *   *Current Gap:* Investors need retention evidence; you have none.
    *   *Required Data:* Evidence that users return after the initial setup.
    *   *How to Obtain:* Track the "Day 7 Return Rate" of your test group. If they set it up once and never return, the "shared calendar" is not solving the daily stress. A flattening retention curve in this small test is the only valid predictor of future retention.

**4. Non-Goals for This Stage**
*   **Building the full app:** Do not engineer sync logic, mobile apps, or polished UI until the Kill Threshold is passed.
*   **Solving all caregiver needs:** Focus strictly on the "missed appointment" loop. Ignore medication tracking or financial management for now.
*   **Scaling marketing:** Do not spend ad budget until the concierge test proves organic adoption.

**5. Decision Rule**
*   **GO:** If >10% of test users actively coordinate a real appointment and at least one pays.
*   **NO-GO / PIVOT:** If users sign up but do not invite siblings, or if they refuse to pay any amount. The problem may be emotional (guilt) rather than logistical, requiring a different solution than a calendar.

*Note: This plan relies on your team executing the test and reporting real numbers; no market size or persona data has been invented here. Fill the "Kill Threshold" with your specific unit economics before running the test.*
