**Executive Summary: SDoH Data Collection Initiative**

**Decision Status:** **Do Not Build Yet.** Proceed immediately to a low-cost operational test to validate the riskiest assumption before committing engineering resources.

**1. The Riskiest Assumption**
The initiative depends on a single unproven belief: *Front-desk staff can reliably collect sensitive housing and food security data without degrading trust or increasing intake time beyond the current +8-minute deficit.*
If this assumption is false, the feature creates operational friction without unlocking the $150,000 grant, resulting in net loss.

**2. Required Validation Test (Pre-Build)**
Before writing code, run a **Concierge Test** within 5 business days:
*   **Method:** Have staff manually ask the two specific questions to the next 50 patients during standard intake, recording the exact time added and any patient refusal or visible discomfort.
*   **Success Metric (Go):** Average time increase < 2 minutes per patient; refusal rate < 10% of the willing 65%; zero reported trust incidents.
*   **Kill Threshold (No-Go):** If time increases > 3 minutes per patient OR refusal rates exceed 15% in practice, **drop the current workflow approach**. The cost of collection exceeds the value of the data.

**3. Operational Impact vs. Financial Upside**

| Dimension | Current Evidence | Unknown / To Be Validated |
| :--- | :--- | :--- |
| **Financial Upside** | **$150,000/year** (Grant funding). | Realizable only if data quality/quantity meets grantor specs (verify grant requirements). |
| **Patient Willingness** | **65%** stated willingness in pilot survey. | Does stated intent match actual behavior when rushed at the front desk? |
| **Operational Cost** | Current workflow is already **+8 mins** vs. industry standard. | Will this add further delay? If yes, does it cause bottlenecks or no-shows? |
| **Trust Risk** | N/A | High. Sensitive questions asked by non-clinical staff may damage community rapport. |

**4. Recommendation for the Board**
*   **Approve the Experiment, Not the Build:** Allocate staff time for the 5-day manual test. Do not approve engineering sprint capacity yet.
*   **Mission Alignment:** Acknowledge that while tracking Social Determinants of Health (SDoH) aligns with the mission, *how* we collect it determines success. A broken workflow that alienates patients undermines the mission.
*   **Next Steps:** If the manual test passes the Success Metrics above, proceed to build an automated intake feature. If it fails, pivot to alternative collection methods (e.g., private digital kiosks, post-visit SMS) before attempting to integrate into the front-desk workflow.

**Non-Goals for Q3 (If Test Fails)**
*   Building complex database fields for SDoH into the EHR.
*   Training front-desk staff on a permanent script that slows throughput.

***

*Note: This assessment relies on the "Riskiest Assumption Gate" principle; building software before validating the human workflow is the primary failure mode to avoid. The $150k figure is treated as a potential upside contingent on successful data capture, not a guaranteed return.*
