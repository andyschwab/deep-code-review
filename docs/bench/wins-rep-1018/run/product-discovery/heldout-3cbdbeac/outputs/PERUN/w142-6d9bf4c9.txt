**Decision: Do Not Greenlight Build Yet. Status: UNVERIFIED.**

The current evidence (complaints and wait-time metrics) validates the *problem* (14h vs. 9h regional average) but fails to validate the *solution* or the *adoption risk*. Building the optimizer now violates the riskiest-assumption gate because the critical uncertainty—whether lines will trust and adopt an automated system over manual relationships—remains untested. An algorithmic solution built on unverified adoption assumptions is a high-risk waste of engineering resources.

**Critical Uncertainties & Required Tests (Before Coding)**

You must resolve the following three uncertainties using cheap, behavioral tests. Do not proceed to development until these pass.

**1. Adoption Trust (The Riskiest Assumption)**
*   **Assumption:** Shipping lines will trust an algorithmic recommendation over their established manual relationships with your staff.
*   **Cheapest Test:** "Concierge" Simulation. Do not build the optimizer. Manually select 5 vessels from next week's queue. Use historical data offline to calculate what the "optimal" arrival time *would* have been. Present this manually to the three complaining lines as a "pilot recommendation" from a human planner, explicitly stating it was derived via a new method.
*   **Metric:** Adoption Rate (Did they change their schedule based on your manual recommendation?).
*   **Kill Threshold:** If fewer than 2 of the 3 lines adjust their schedule based on the manual recommendation, **drop the automated build**. The issue is likely relationship/trust, not algorithmic efficiency.
*   **Deadline:** 48 hours (before the steering committee meeting, run a dry-run; execute early next week).

**2. Problem Specificity**
*   **Assumption:** The 14-hour wait is caused by inefficient *slot allocation* rather than external factors (tides, labor shortages, crane breakdowns) that an optimizer cannot fix.
*   **Cheapest Test:** Root-Cause Audit. Analyze the last 20 delayed vessels. Categorize the delay cause: (A) Allocation conflict, (B) External constraint (tide/weather), (C) Operational failure (equipment/labor).
*   **Metric:** Percentage of delays attributable to (A).
*   **Kill Threshold:** If <50% of delays are caused by allocation conflicts, **pivot**. An allocation optimizer will not solve external or operational bottlenecks.
*   **Data Source:** Terminal operation logs (internal).

**3. Value of Prediction**
*   **Assumption:** Historical traffic data is a reliable predictor for future tidal windows and congestion patterns.
*   **Cheapest Test:** Back-testing. Take last quarter's data. Apply your proposed logic manually to see if it *would* have reduced the wait time for those specific vessels.
*   **Metric:** Simulated reduction in wait time.
*   **Kill Threshold:** If the simulated reduction is <10% (half your target), **refine the model** before building. The 20% target may be mathematically impossible given the variance in traffic.

**Immediate Next Steps for VP/Steering Committee**
1.  **Reject the build request** in its current form.
2.  **Approve the Concierge Test** (Uncertainty #1) as the immediate Q3 milestone.
3.  **Define Success:** "We will build the optimizer ONLY IF the manual concierge test achieves >66% adoption among complaining lines."

**Note:** The 20% reduction target is a hypothesis, not a fact; the back-test (Uncertainty #3) must validate this number before it becomes a KPI.
**Note:** Do not survey the lines on "would you use this?"; their stated intent is unreliable. Measure only their actual schedule changes in the concierge test.
