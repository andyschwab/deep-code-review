**Decision: Do not build full localization yet. Run a cheap disconfirmation test first.**

Your current understanding relies on an untested assumption: that price can overcome language barriers. Spending $75,000 and eight weeks before validating this is a violation of the riskiest-assumption gate. You are building a solution to a problem (language rejection) that you have not yet proven exists or is absolute.

**The Riskiest Assumption**
*Assumption:* Mid-sized retailers in Montreal/Quebec City will adopt an English-only inventory tool if the price is significantly lower than local competitors.
*Impact:* High (determines entire go-to-market strategy).
*Uncertainty:* High (no behavioral data exists yet).

**The Cheap Test (Pre-Build)**
Do not write code. Do not translate the interface. Instead, run a **fake-door or concierge test** targeting the specific hypothesis that price trumps language.

1.  **Create a Landing Page:** Build a simple page in **French** describing your inventory tool.
    *   *Variant A:* "Powerful Inventory Management (Interface in English) – 40% cheaper than competitors."
    *   *Variant B:* "Powerful Inventory Management (Interface in French) – Standard Price." (Use this only if you can mock the UI or offer a manual concierge service for the demo).
    *   *Better Approach for Speed:* Create a single French page stating the tool is "Coming Soon to Quebec." Include a clear disclaimer: "Early access available now: Full English interface, 50% discount. Join waitlist?"
2.  **Drive Targeted Traffic:** Spend $500–$1,000 on ads targeting inventory managers in Montreal/Quebec City.
3.  **Measure Behavior:** Track click-through rates and, crucially, **sign-up/conversion rates** for the "English + Discount" offer versus a baseline expectation.

**Numeric Kill Threshold**
Before running the test, agree on this rule:
*   **Go (Build Localization):** If fewer than **2%** of visitors who click the ad sign up for the "English + Discount" waitlist. This indicates language is a strict gatekeeper that price cannot fix. You must localize before entry.
*   **Pivot/Test Further:** If **>5%** sign up, language may be flexible. You might launch with English + discount to gather early users while localizing in parallel, or run a deeper interview series with those sign-ups to confirm they would actually *pay* and *use* it daily.
*   **Drop/Re-evaluate:** If traffic costs are prohibitive or click-through is near zero, the channel or value prop itself may be the issue, not just language.

**Non-Goals for This Stage**
*   **Full UI Translation:** Do not translate the entire application yet. This is the $75k cost you are trying to avoid risking prematurely.
*   **Legal/Contract Localization:** Do not engage legal teams for French contracts until the test proves willingness to engage.
*   **Sales Outreach:** Do not have sales reps pitch the English tool manually yet; let the ad data speak first to remove bias.

**Next Steps**
1.  Define the exact discount percentage and the "kill" conversion rate (e.g., 2%) today.
2.  Launch the French landing page ad campaign within 3 days.
3.  Run for 7–10 days or until 500+ qualified clicks are received.
4.  Make the $75k build/no-build decision based strictly on the conversion data against your pre-set threshold.

*Note: This approach validates whether language is a hard blocker using real market behavior rather than opinions. If the competitor launches in four months, you will have your answer in two weeks, leaving six weeks to either localize properly or pivot.*
