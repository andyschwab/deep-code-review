**Decision: Do not build the custom routing algorithm yet.**

The riskiest assumption is not technical feasibility, but **driver adoption and execution accuracy**. Your hypothesis states that dynamic software will improve on-time performance to 98%. However, if drivers with ten years of static route experience ignore, misinterpret, or actively resist new directional instructions, the software's theoretical optimization will fail in practice. Building a complex algorithm before validating human behavior creates a high risk of delivering a technically perfect solution that operationally fails.

**Required Action: Run the Cheapest Disconfirmation Test First**
Before writing code, you must test whether drivers will follow dynamic instructions under real conditions.

*   **The Test:** Conduct a "Wizard-of-Oz" simulation for 5 days with 2 volunteer drivers.
    *   Do not build software. Have a dispatcher manually send updated route instructions via SMS/radio based on simulated traffic/weather data.
    *   Instruct drivers to follow these new directions exactly, overriding their habitual routes.
*   **Metric:** Percentage of dynamic instructions followed correctly without deviation or delay caused by confusion.
*   **Proposed Kill Threshold:** If **fewer than 85%** of instructions are followed correctly, or if average stop times increase by >10% due to driver confusion, **drop the custom build idea**.
    *   *Basis:* With a current 12% late rate, you need a massive behavioral shift to reach 98% on-time. If drivers cannot follow manual updates 85% of the time, an algorithm will not fix the human friction.
*   **Sample Size:** 2 drivers, 5 days (approx. 40–50 route decisions).
*   **Deadline:** Complete within 7 days.
