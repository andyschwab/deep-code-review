### Strategic Evaluation: Marina Booking Integration

**Recommendation: Do not build the full integration yet.** Proceed immediately to a **Riskiest-Assumption Gate** focused on API feasibility. The value of the solution is high, but the technical viability is currently unproven. Building the automation before confirming data access risks delivering a polished tool that connects to nothing.

#### 1. The Riskiest Assumption: Technical Viability
Your team assumes the five major marinas in Split and Dubrovnik will provide API access to their legacy systems. This is the single point of failure. If these systems are closed or require expensive custom middleware, the projected 75% conflict reduction cannot be achieved via direct integration.

**Required Test (Cheapest Disconfirmation):**
Before writing integration code, execute a **feasibility probe**:
*   **Action:** Contact the IT directors of the five target marinas with a specific technical questionnaire regarding their current booking software vendors, available export formats (CSV, XML, JSON), and willingness to enable API endpoints.
*   **Metric:** Number of marinas confirming accessible, documented data interfaces.
*   **Kill Threshold:** If **fewer than 3 of the 5** marinas confirm viable data access within 14 days, **drop the direct integration plan**. Pivot to a manual-concierge MVP or wait for industry standards to shift.
*   **Rationale:** You cannot automate what you cannot access. A "no" from the majority invalidates the core technical approach.

#### 2. Validity of Efficiency Assumptions
Your assumption that automation will reduce scheduling conflicts by **75%** is plausible but unverified.
*   **Current State:** 10 hours/week manual reconciliation; €25,000/season in error costs.
*   **Validation Gap:** You have not yet isolated whether errors stem from *data latency* (solved by integration) or *human process failure* (which may persist even with automation).
*   **Next Step:** Run a **Wizard-of-Oz test** with one willing charter operator. Manually simulate the "automated" flow using the data you can currently extract (even if manual). Measure if the *process* of having unified data reduces their specific conflict rate before building the pipe.

#### 3. Dependency Risks
*   **Legacy System Fragmentation:** The Dalmatian coast likely operates on disparate, non-standardized legacy systems. Relying on five different vendors to cooperate creates a high coordination risk.
*   **Maintenance Debt:** If APIs are unstable or undocumented, your team will inherit high ongoing maintenance costs, potentially exceeding the current 10-hour/week manual cost.
*   **Adoption Friction:** Marina operators may resist changing their workflows or exposing data, creating a political barrier distinct from the technical one.

#### Decision Path for Stakeholders
1.  **Immediate Action:** Assign a technical lead to conduct the **feasibility probe** with the 5 marinas. Cost: <20 hours.
2.  **Go/No-Go Decision:**
    *   **GO:** If ≥3 marinas confirm access. Proceed to a limited MVP with one charter partner.
    *   **NO-GO:** If <3 marinas confirm access. Do not fund the build. Re-evaluate in 6 months or explore industry-wide standardization initiatives.

**Non-Goal for This Stage:** Building the full-scale, production-ready integration pipeline. This is premature until the API access assumption is validated.

---
*Note: This evaluation relies on the "Riskiest-Assumption Gate" principle; do not proceed to development until the feasibility probe returns data.*
*The €25,000 error cost provides a clear ROI ceiling; ensure the cost of the feasibility probe and subsequent MVP remains a small fraction of this loss.*
