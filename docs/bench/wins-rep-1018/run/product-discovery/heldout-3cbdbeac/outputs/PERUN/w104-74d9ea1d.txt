### Decision Analysis: IoT Sensor Integration vs. Manual Protocol Improvement

**Current State Evidence**
*   **Incident Rate:** 4 formal complaints in 2 months (12 silos).
*   **Financial Impact:** ~48,000 RON direct loss (penalties + trust erosion) in 60 days; annualized risk exceeds 280,000 RON if frequency holds.
*   **Primary Hypothesis:** Human error during shift handovers causes ≥90% of discrepancies.
*   **Critical Unknown:** Sensor hardware reliability in high-dust Galati silo environments.

#### Option A: Integrate IoT Occupancy Sensors
**Potential Benefits**
*   **Accuracy:** Eliminates shift-handover transcription errors, directly addressing the hypothesized 90% root cause.
*   **Real-time Visibility:** Provides continuous data rather than point-in-time snapshots, potentially preventing contract failures before they occur.
*   **Audit Trail:** Automated logs remove ambiguity regarding when inventory levels changed.

**Key Risks & Costs**
*   **Environmental Failure (High Risk):** High-dust environments may obscure optical/laser sensors or clog mechanical units, leading to *systematic* automated errors that are harder to detect than manual typos.
*   **Maintenance Overhead:** Shifts cost from "training humans" to "calibrating/cleaning hardware." If dust requires weekly cleaning, uptime may drop below current manual reliability.
*   **Capital Expenditure:** Upfront hardware/installation costs plus ongoing subscription/maintenance fees.

#### Option B: Improve Manual Protocols
**Potential Benefits**
*   **Low Capital Cost:** No new hardware investment; relies on process redesign (e.g., dual-verification at handover, digital checklists with mandatory timestamps).
*   **Immediate Deployment:** Can be implemented before the board meeting next week.
*   **Resilience:** Humans can contextualize anomalies (e.g., "sensor blocked by dust") that automated systems might misreport as empty/full.

**Key Risks & Costs**
*   **Residual Error Rate:** Even with improved protocols, human fatigue and distraction remain. If the 90% hypothesis is correct, protocols may only reduce errors by 50–70%, leaving significant financial exposure.
*   **Compliance Drift:** Strict protocols often degrade over time without constant enforcement, reverting to previous error rates within months.

#### The Riskiest Assumption Gate
You are assuming **human error is the primary cause (≥90%)** and that **sensors will function reliably in high dust**. Both are unproven.
*   **If the assumption is wrong:** If 40% of errors stem from delayed manual entry (not typos) or mechanical flow issues, sensors won't solve the core problem, and you incur high CAPEX.
*   **If the hardware fails:** You replace human error with sensor drift, potentially increasing discrepancies while adding maintenance costs.

#### Recommended Next Step (Before Capital Approval)
Do not approve full integration yet. Execute a **Cheap Experiment** to validate the environmental risk:
1.  **Pilot Test:** Install *one* industrial-grade sensor in the dustiest silo for 14 days.
2.  **Metric:** Compare sensor readings against a "gold standard" manual measurement taken twice daily.
3.  **Kill Threshold:** If sensor deviation exceeds 2% more than twice in 14 days due to dust interference, **drop the sensor option** for now.
4.  **Parallel Action:** Implement a strict "dual-signoff" digital log for shift handovers immediately to capture baseline error reduction without CAPEX.

**Decision Rule for the Board:**
*   **Approve Sensors ONLY IF:** The 14-day pilot shows <2% deviation in high dust AND the dual-signoff protocol fails to reduce complaints by at least 50% in the same period.
*   **Approve Protocol Upgrade ONLY IF:** The pilot confirms sensors fail in dust OR the dual-signoff trial eliminates the financial bleed.

***

*Note: This analysis relies strictly on the provided incident data and hypotheses; actual sensor specifications and dust density metrics must be sourced from vendors and site engineers before finalizing the pilot design.*
*The financial annualization is a projection based on the current 2-month run rate to illustrate potential exposure, not a guaranteed future cost.*
