**Recommendation: Do not build payment infrastructure yet.**

Your current evidence indicates a high risk of failure if you proceed immediately. The "cheap test" (your survey of 200 users) has already produced a signal that contradicts your core assumption.

**1. The Evidence vs. The Assumption**
*   **Your Assumption:** 3% conversion rate (450 subscribers).
*   **Your Data:** 12 out of 200 respondents (6%) said they would *definitely* pay.
*   **The Reality Check:** In product discovery, stated intent ("I would pay") almost always overstates actual behavior ("I paid") by a factor of 2x to 5x, especially for health content where free government alternatives exist. If only 6% *say* they will definitely pay, your actual conversion rate will likely be 1–3%, putting your revenue projection at severe risk. Furthermore, "definitely" is a high bar; if only 6% hit that bar, the pool of "maybe" users is your only buffer, and they rarely convert without heavy friction.

**2. The Riskiest Assumption Gate**
Your riskiest assumption is not "Can we build a payment wall?" but **"Will Swedish users pay for wellness content when free, trusted government options exist?"**
You have not yet validated this with *behavioral* evidence. A survey asks for hypothetical future behavior. You need evidence of current behavior or a low-fidelity test of actual payment.

**3. The Required Next Step (The Cheap Test)**
Before writing a single line of payment infrastructure code, run a **Fake Door test** or a **Concierge test**:
*   **Action:** Place a "Subscribe for 99 SEK" button on your site for the exclusive content.
*   **Mechanism:** When clicked, show a message: "Coming soon! Enter your email to be notified at launch." (Do not charge yet).
*   **Metric:** Measure the click-through rate (CTR) on that button against your 15,000 monthly users.
*   **Kill Threshold:** If fewer than 1.5% of users click the button (225 clicks), do not build the full infrastructure. The market signal is too weak to justify the burn rate risk.

**4. Board Decision Framework**
For Tuesday's meeting, present the following options:
*   **Option A (High Risk):** Build now. *Probability of missing the 45,000 SEK target is high based on the 6% survey ceiling.*
*   **Option B (Recommended):** Pause infrastructure build. Allocate 1 week to run the Fake Door test on the existing 15,000 users.
    *   *If CTR > 1.5%:* Proceed to build.
    *   *If CTR < 1.5%:* Pivot the offer (e.g., bundle with corporate wellness, change pricing) or cut marketing spend to extend runway while you find a viable model.

**Conclusion:** The survey data suggests your 3% conversion goal is optimistic. Building payment infrastructure now is building on an untested assumption. Run the behavioral test first to avoid wasting engineering cycles on a feature that may not generate the required revenue.

*Note: Do not rely on the "12 out of 200" number as a green light; treat it as a warning sign that requires behavioral validation.*
