### Evidence Evaluation: RehabRemote Home Exercise Monitor

**Current Evidence Status**
*   **Validated Strength:** Sensor-only data drives high adherence (90% in 50-patient pilot).
*   **Validated Gap:** Sensors miss subtle form errors in 20% of sessions, creating a potential safety blind spot.
*   **Unverified Assumption:** Video analysis will reduce injury rates by ≥15%. *Status: No evidence provided.*
*   **Critical Unknown:** Patient willingness to accept in-home video recording. *Status: No evidence provided.*

**Risk Assessment by Path**

**Path A: Add Camera for Video Analysis**
*   **Adoption Risk (High):** If patients reject in-home recording due to privacy concerns, adherence may drop significantly below the validated 90% baseline, rendering the device useless regardless of efficacy.
*   **Regulatory/Legal Risk (High):** Introducing video data before the Nov 30 filing introduces complex data privacy compliance requirements (e.g., HIPAA/GDPR specific to video) that may delay submission if not fully architected and vetted immediately.
*   **Efficacy Risk (Medium):** The assumption that video reduces injury by 15% is unproven. If video detects errors but patients ignore the feedback or feel surveilled, injury rates may not improve.

**Path B: Launch Sensor-Only MVP**
*   **Clinical Safety Risk (Medium):** The known 20% error detection gap remains. If "subtle form errors" correlate directly with injury, launching without a mitigation strategy exposes patients to harm and the company to liability.
*   **Market Differentiation Risk (Low/Medium):** Competitors with video capabilities may claim superior safety profiles, though this depends on whether their users actually tolerate the cameras.
*   **Regulatory Risk (Low):** Sticking to the tested sensor configuration aligns with the current pilot data, offering a clearer path to the Nov 30 deadline.

**Required Immediate Actions (Before Decision)**
You cannot decide based on the current brief. The following evidence slots are empty and must be filled via rapid research before the advisory board meeting:
1.  **Privacy Acceptance Metric:** Conduct 5–10 "Mom Test" style interviews with pilot patients asking about *past* behaviors with home cameras (e.g., security cams, baby monitors) to gauge true tolerance, not hypothetical agreement.
2.  **Injury Correlation Data:** Review the 20% of sessions with missed errors. Did those specific instances lead to reported pain or injury? If missed errors did not cause harm, the 15% reduction assumption is likely inflated.
3.  **Kill Threshold Definition:** Define the minimum privacy acceptance rate required to proceed with video (e.g., "If <70% of interviewed patients agree to a camera test, we drop video for MVP").

**Recommendation**
Do not commit to video capabilities yet. The risk of destroying the validated 90% adherence rate with an untested privacy intrusion is the riskiest assumption. Proceed with a **Sensor-Only MVP** for the Nov 30 filing to secure the regulatory timeline, while running a parallel, opt-in concierge test with a small subset of patients to validate the video hypothesis for a post-MVP update.

Note: The 15% injury reduction figure must be treated as a hypothesis to be tested, not a fact for the filing.
Legal must review data architecture implications of video before any code is written.
