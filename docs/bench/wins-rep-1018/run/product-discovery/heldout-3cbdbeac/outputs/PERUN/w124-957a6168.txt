**Feasibility Brief: Parts Tracking Module Assessment**

**Decision Status:** **DO NOT GREENLIGHT DEVELOPMENT YET.**
The three incident reports confirm a *problem* (lost parts), but they do not validate the *solution* (barcode scanning) or the *root cause* (lack of visibility vs. training/process). Building now risks solving the wrong problem or creating a solution too slow for race weekends.

**1. Evidence Gap Analysis**
*   **Current Evidence:** Three qualitative reports confirm pain and impact (delays). This justifies *investigation*, not *build*.
*   **Missing Evidence:**
    *   **Root Cause:** We do not know if parts are lost due to lack of tracking, or because staff skip existing steps due to time pressure.
    *   **Solution Viability:** The assumption that staff can scan every part in a chaotic environment is untested. If scanning adds >5 seconds per part, adoption will fail, and the tool will be ignored.
    *   **Alternative Explanations:** Inadequate training or unclear handover protocols could be the true culprit.

**2. Riskiest Assumption & Required Test**
*   **Assumption:** "Staff can and will scan every part during a race weekend without slowing down pit preparations."
*   **Cheapest Test (Concierge/Wizard-of-Oz):** Do not build software yet.
    *   **Action:** At the next test day or simulation, assign one person to manually log every part movement using a simple clipboard or a generic notes app, timing each entry.
    *   **Metric:** Time added per part vs. operational tolerance.
    *   **Kill Threshold:** If the manual process adds more than 3 seconds per part or causes any delay in a simulated pit stop, **drop the barcode idea** and pivot to process redesign or RFID (passive scanning).
    *   **Deadline:** Must be completed before the September 20th planning meeting.

**3. Immediate Next Steps (Before Sept 20)**
*   **Conduct "Mom Test" Interviews:** Speak to the three reporting managers and two mechanics. Ask: "Walk me through the last time a part went missing. What were you doing right before? What existing procedure did you try to follow?" (Focus on past behavior, not opinions on barcodes).
*   **Run the Manual Simulation:** Execute the test above to get behavioral data on scanning friction.
*   **Audit Current Process:** Verify if current tracking procedures exist and if training gaps are the actual blocker.

**Recommendation to Directors:**
Allocate 1 week to run the manual simulation and interviews. If the data shows scanning is feasible and visibility is the gap, we greenlight the MVP. If the data shows process failure or scanning friction, we pivot to training or a different technology. Building the module now is a high-risk bet on an unproven assumption.

**Note:** This brief structures the evidence gap; it does not invent market data or user quotes.
**Note:** The "Kill Threshold" (3 seconds) is a proposed starting value based on typical pit-stop margins; adjust based on your specific operational tolerances.
