### Decision Framework: JIT Coordination Feature Viability

**Current Status:** **UNVERIFIED**. Do not allocate Q3 engineering budget to build this feature yet. The core assumption (80% of delays stem from communication gaps) and the primary constraint (client willingness to share data) are untested hypotheses. Building now risks solving the wrong problem or building something clients will not enable.

#### 1. The Riskiest-Assumption Gate
Before writing code, you must validate the single point of failure: **Will clients share real-time schedule data?**
If the answer is "no" due to security policies, the feature provides zero value regardless of engineering quality.

**Required Test:** The "Data-Sharing Concierge"
*   **Action:** Do not build the portal feature. Instead, manually approach your three plant contacts with a specific proposal: "We propose a pilot where we receive your shift-change schedule 24 hours in advance via [secure method they suggest] to prevent the 4,500 EUR stoppage cost. Can we trial this for two weeks?"
*   **Metric:** Number of plants agreeing to the data-sharing pilot.
*   **Kill Threshold:** If **0 out of 3** plants agree to share data within 10 days, **drop the feature idea immediately**. The barrier is policy, not technology.
*   **Secondary Assumption Test:** If they agree, track the root cause of the next 5 delays.
    *   **Kill Threshold:** If fewer than **3 out of 5** subsequent delays are actually caused by shift-change miscommunication (contradicting your 80% assumption), **narrow the scope**. The problem may be trucking capacity or internal dispatch, requiring a different solution.

#### 2. Economic Logic (Pre-Build)
*   **Potential Upside:** 5 stoppages/month × 4,500 EUR = **22,500 EUR/month** at risk.
*   **Break-Even Requirement:** If the feature costs $X$ to build and maintain, it must prevent enough stoppages to cover $X$.
*   **Decision Rule:** Only proceed if the "Data-Sharing Concierge" test proves clients will participate AND the root cause analysis confirms communication is the dominant driver.

#### 3. Implementation Complexity vs. Value
*   **High Complexity / Low Value Scenario:** Clients refuse data sharing due to security. Result: Wasted engineering budget.
*   **Low Complexity / High Value Scenario:** Clients agree to a simple manual exchange (email/EDI) initially. Result: Validate value before automating.
*   **Recommendation:** Start with the manual concierge process. If it stops the line halts, *then* automate it into the portal. This defers engineering cost until value is proven.

#### 4. Non-Goals for Q3
*   **Not Building:** A fully automated real-time integration with client ERPs. (Too complex before validating data access).
*   **Not Solving:** Trucking capacity issues. (If the test reveals delays are due to lack of trucks, not timing, this feature is irrelevant).
