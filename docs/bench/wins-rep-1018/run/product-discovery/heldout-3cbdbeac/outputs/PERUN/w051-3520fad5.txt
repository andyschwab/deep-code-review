### Decision Framework: Automation vs. Hybrid Review

**Status:** **UNVERIFIED.** You cannot decide on the feature scope yet because the critical variable (the time cost of the mandatory human review) is unknown. Building either model now violates the **Riskiest-Assumption Gate**.

#### 1. The Riskiest Assumption
*   **Assumption:** "Adding a human review step for edge cases will not erase the efficiency gains ($200k savings) created by automating 70% of decisions."
*   **Why it is risky:** If the legal requirement forces a review of *every* automated denial (or even a complex subset), and that review takes >15 minutes per case, your net savings could drop to zero or become negative due to liability exposure.
*   **Impact:** High (determines viability).
*   **Uncertainty:** High (regulatory interpretation and actual review duration are unknown).

#### 2. The Required Test (Before November Scope Lock)
Do not build the full integration. Run a **Concierge/Wizard-of-Oz Test** on a small sample of live data.

*   **The Setup:** Take a random sample of 50 recent pre-authorization requests that your prototype would have auto-decided.
*   **The Action:** Have your legal/compliance team manually apply the "mandatory human review" rule to these 50 cases exactly as they interpret the regulation.
*   **The Metric:** Measure the **average time added per case** and the **percentage of cases requiring full re-work** vs. simple sign-off.
*   **The Kill Threshold (Decision Rule):**
    *   *Calculate your break-even time:* To save $200k/year on 5,000 weekly requests, you need to save roughly **$0.77 per request**. If a human reviewer costs $50/hour, they can spend no more than **~55 seconds** per reviewed case to maintain profitability.
    *   *Rule:* If the average review time exceeds **2 minutes** (providing a safety buffer), **DROP** the full automation plan. Pivot to a "Decision Support" tool (humans make the final call, AI just highlights data) instead of an "Auto-Decision" tool.

#### 3. Trade-Off Analysis (To be filled after the test)

| Feature Scope | Potential Upside | Critical Downside | Go/No-Go Condition |
| :--- | :--- | :--- | :--- |
| **Full Automation + Review Layer** | Max speed if review is fast; highest theoretical savings. | Legal risk if review is superficial; savings vanish if review is slow. | **GO** only if test shows avg. review time < [Insert Break-Even Time]. |
| **Hybrid Model (Complex Cases Only)** | Balances risk; humans focus on high-value/edge decisions. | Defining "complex" is subjective; may still trigger broad regulatory scrutiny. | **GO** if regulators define "edge case" narrowly in the test simulation. |
| **Decision Support Only** | Lowest legal risk; full human accountability. | Lowest efficiency gain; may not meet the $200k savings target. | **Default** if the kill threshold is triggered. |

#### 4. Immediate Non-Goals (Scope Defense)
*   **Do not build** the automated denial pipeline until the review time metric is proven.
*   **Do not attempt** to predict the next legislative session's outcome; route this to Legal for a definitive "current state" interpretation only.
*   **Do not optimize** for the 92% accuracy rate yet; accuracy is irrelevant if the process is too slow to be profitable under the new rules.

**Next Step:** Run the 50-case manual simulation within 48 hours. Bring the *measured* average review time to the CLO and VP of Operations. Without this number, any scope decision is a guess.
