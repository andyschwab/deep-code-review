### Board Evaluation: Cold Chain Monitoring Viability

**Verdict:** Do not build the proprietary dashboard yet. The current evidence indicates you are attempting to solve a *symptom* (lack of data) before confirming the *root cause* (where the temperature excursion actually occurs). Building software now risks capital expenditure on a solution that logistics partners may block and that may not prevent future rejections.

**1. The Riskiest Assumption Gate**
Your core assumption is that "installing sensors reduces spoilage by 15%." This relies on two unproven sub-assumptions:
*   **Feasibility:** Logistics partners will allow third-party hardware without contract renegotiation.
*   **Causality:** The temperature excursions happen *during* the 18-day transit, not during pre-loading staging or port handover.

If the failure occurs while goods sit on the dock in Can Tho before the container is sealed, ocean sensors provide zero value. If partners refuse installation, the project halts immediately.

**2. Required Evidence (Currently Missing)**
To satisfy the board, you must replace "average transit times" with specific failure point data. The current $45,000 loss proves a problem exists, but not *where* it exists.
*   **Root Cause Data:** You need temperature logs from the *last* failed shipments. Without these, you cannot prove the excursion happened at sea versus on land.
*   **Partner Constraint Data:** A definitive "Yes/No" from Ho Chi Minh City logistics partners regarding third-party hardware installation.

**3. Recommended Path: The "Concierge" Test**
Before writing code for a dashboard or buying bulk sensors, run this cheap behavioral test:
*   **Action:** Select *one* upcoming high-value shipment. Negotiate a single-instance exception with your logistics partner to install *one* standalone data logger (not a full real-time system) for this specific trip.
*   **Metric:** Retrieve the logger upon arrival.
*   **Kill Threshold:** If the logger shows normal temperatures during transit but the buyer still rejects (indicating the break happened pre-transit), or if the partner refuses the single exception, **drop the proprietary dashboard plan**. Pivot to negotiating better staging protocols or changing logistics providers instead.

**4. Strategic Non-Goals for This Stage**
*   **Not Building:** A real-time dashboard, mobile apps, or cloud infrastructure. These are useless without the data pipe, which is currently blocked by unknown constraints.
*   **Not Solving:** General "visibility." The only goal is preventing the specific temperature excursion that caused the $45k loss.

**Decision Rule for the Board:**
Approve capital expenditure *only* after the single-shipment test confirms: (A) The excursion happens during the monitored window, AND (B) Partners permit the hardware. Until then, the risk of building the wrong thing is 100%.
