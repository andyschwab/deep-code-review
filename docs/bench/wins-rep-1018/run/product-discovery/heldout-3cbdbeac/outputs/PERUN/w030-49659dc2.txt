**Recommendation: Negotiate a "Pilot Customization" Compromise; Do Not Build the Theme Engine Yet.**

**Decision:** Reject the immediate construction of a configurable theme engine. Instead, propose a hard-coded, time-boxed implementation specifically for St. Jude's as a "Pilot Customization," contingent on a contract extension or premium fee.

**Reasoning & Evidence Structure:**
Your current plan relies on a critical untested assumption: that a theme engine will allow 70% component reuse without degrading your two-week release cycle. Building a generalized engine before validating this specific client's needs violates the **Riskiest-Assumption Gate**. You are proposing to build complex infrastructure (the engine) to solve a single data point (one client's request). If the assumption is wrong, you incur the debt of the custom code *plus* the debt of an unused or flawed engine.

**Strategic Action Plan:**

1.  **Isolate the Risk (The "Non-Goal" Defense):**
    Declare the "Configurable Theme Engine" a **non-goal** for the June 1 deadline. The goal is solely "St. Jude's Go-Live." Building for future hypothetical clients now introduces the very technical debt you fear. Hard-code the branding and intake flow for St. Jude's only. This isolates the technical debt to a single branch or feature flag, protecting the core release cycle for other clients.

2.  **Design the Cheap Test (Behavioral Evidence):**
    Before committing to a full engine, run a **Concierge Test** for the intake flow. Manually configure the specific fields St. Jude's needs using existing low-code tools or database tweaks if possible, or hard-code them.
    *   **Metric:** Measure the *actual* engineering hours required to maintain this hard-coded version over two sprint cycles.
    *   **Kill Threshold:** If maintaining this single custom instance slows the core release cycle by >20% (e.g., shipping takes >3 weeks instead of 2), the hypothesis that "we can scale this" is false. Do not build the engine; instead, use this data to negotiate a higher price for a dedicated instance or push back on scope.

3.  **Commercial Leverage (The Switch Interview):**
    St. Jude's threat to churn indicates high "push" forces, but you must verify the "pull." Is their demand for *branding* or *workflow*?
    *   **Action:** The Account Executive must clarify if the "unique intake flow" is a regulatory necessity or a preference.
    *   **Negotiation:** Offer the hard-coded solution by June 1 in exchange for a 12-month contract extension and a 20% "custom development" premium. This monetizes the debt immediately. If they refuse and churn, the revenue loss is painful, but building a potentially flawed theme engine that slows all future development is an existential threat to the remaining 70% of your revenue base.

**Verdict on Product-Market Fit (PMF) & Velocity:**
Your PMF signal (standardized, fast releases) is currently strong ("every two weeks without fail"). Introducing a generalized engine based on a single client's demand is a premature pivot. Treat St. Jude's as a **paid pilot** for customization, not the architect of your future roadmap. Only build the theme engine if you secure signed commitments from *two additional* clients for similar customizations, validating the market need beyond one threat.

**Immediate Next Steps:**
1.  **VP of Product:** Authorize a hard-coded spike for St. Jude's; forbid generic engine work until the pilot proves maintainability.
2.  **Account Executive:** Present the "Pilot Customization" offer with the premium fee and contract extension today.

*Note: This approach converts an untested architectural assumption into a measured, paid experiment.*
*Do not proceed with engine construction until the hard-coded pilot validates the maintenance cost.*
