### Recommendation: Enforce Minimum OS Requirements with a Targeted Waitlist Validation Sprint

**Decision:** Proceed with a strict minimum requirement (iOS 15 / Android 12) for the initial launch. Do not build a fallback mode that reduces data frequency at this stage.

**Rationale:**
1.  **Clinical Integrity vs. Feature Degradation:** A "fallback mode" that reduces data frequency compromises the core value proposition of connected inhalers: accurate adherence tracking. Incomplete data creates false negatives in adherence reporting, which poses a clinical risk and erodes trust with the advisory board. It is safer to exclude users with incompatible devices than to provide them with unreliable clinical data.
2.  **Evidence of Instability:** Your beta data confirms the engineering constraint. A 12% connection drop rate on older OS versions is unacceptable for a medical device. If 12% of sessions fail on tested devices, a software fallback is unlikely to resolve the underlying Bluetooth stack incompatibilities without significant re-architecture.
3.  **Untested Assumption Risk:** Your assumption that "80% of target patients upgrade within two years" is currently unvalidated against your specific waitlist. Building a complex fallback feature based on an unverified market assumption violates the riskiest-assumption gate. You would be spending engineering cycles to support a segment that may not exist in your specific user base.

**Immediate Action Plan (Before Tuesday's Meeting):**
You cannot make a final go/no-go decision on the *market impact* without knowing the OS breakdown of your 200 waitlist users. The assumption is the riskiest variable.

*   **The Test:** Send a mandatory, 30-second survey to all 200 waitlist users immediately.
    *   *Question:* "What specific phone model and operating system version are you currently using?"
    *   *Incentive:* Offer early access priority for completion.
*   **The Kill Threshold:** If **>25%** of the responded waitlist uses unsupported OS versions, the launch strategy must pivot to a phased rollout (excluding those users) rather than building a fallback. If **<10%** are unsupported, proceed with strict enforcement and communicate clearly to the excluded minority.
*   **Deliverable for CTO/Board:** Present the beta failure rate (12%) as the justification for the technical constraint. Present the *plan* to validate the waitlist OS breakdown as the mitigation for market risk. Do not present the 80% assumption as fact.

**Non-Goal for This Stage:**
Building a "reduced frequency" fallback mode. This is a scope expansion that attempts to solve a hardware/OS compatibility issue with software logic, likely resulting in a product that works poorly for everyone rather than well for the majority. Revisit this only if the waitlist survey reveals a critical mass of unsupported users *and* clinical advisors approve lower-fidelity data as safe.

***

**Note:** The 80% upgrade assumption must be treated as a hypothesis until the waitlist survey returns real data; do not cite it as a fact to the board.
**Note:** If the survey response rate is low (<40%), treat the unknown segment as high-risk and plan for a longer exclusion period.
