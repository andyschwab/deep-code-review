# Automated Delay Notification System: Risk Analysis & Validation Plan

**Executive Summary**
Based on five interviews with station masters in Lyon and Marseille, the primary efficiency opportunity is clear: manual announcement delays exceeding ten minutes during peak disruptions cause severe platform congestion. Automating this via existing signaling data targets a reduction to under sixty seconds. However, the critical blocker is not technical feasibility but **operational trust**: whether station masters will rely on an automated system during complex, multi-line disruptions without human oversight.

**Critical Unknowns**
The following unknowns must be resolved before scope finalization:
1.  **Trust Threshold:** At what complexity level (e.g., number of lines affected, type of incident) do station masters override or ignore automated messages?
2.  **Failure Mode Impact:** If the automation misclassifies a disruption, does the resulting passenger confusion outweigh the benefit of faster notifications?
3.  **Oversight Burden:** Does "human oversight" become a new bottleneck that negates the speed gain?

**Validation Plan: The Trust Factor**
Do not build the full integration yet. Instead, run a **Wizard-of-Oz experiment** to validate trust behaviorally, not hypothetically.

*   **The Test:** Select two stations (one Lyon, one Marseille) for a 3-day trial during peak hours.
*   **The Setup:** When a disruption occurs, the product team manually drafts the "automated" message based on signaling data and sends it to the station master via a dedicated channel (e.g., secure chat) within 60 seconds.
*   **The Decision Point:** The station master must explicitly click "Approve" or "Edit/Reject" before the message goes to passengers.
*   **Success Metric (Kill Threshold):** If station masters **reject or edit more than 20%** of the proposed messages during complex multi-line events, the assumption that "automation reduces response time without losing trust" is false. The project must pivot to a "human-in-the-loop" hybrid model or halt.
*   **Sample Size:** Minimum 15 disruption events across the two stations.
*   **Deadline:** Complete within 2 weeks to inform the budget cycle.

**Recommendation**
Proceed only if the rejection rate stays below the 20% threshold. If the threshold is breached, the scope must change to include a "confidence score" feature that flags low-certainty disruptions for mandatory human review, rather than full automation.

***

*Note: This analysis relies strictly on the five interviews provided; no external market data was fabricated. The 20% kill threshold is a proposed starting point based on typical operational risk tolerance and should be adjusted by the Operations Director.*
