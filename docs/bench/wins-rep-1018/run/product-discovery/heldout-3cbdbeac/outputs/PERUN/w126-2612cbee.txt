**Subject:** Risk Assessment: Ice Road Status Feature Investment

**Executive Summary**
The two recent delay reports from site managers indicate a critical operational failure but **do not** provide sufficient justification to invest in building the proposed ice road status feature. Proceeding directly to development based on these anecdotes carries a high risk of building a solution to a problem we have not yet diagnosed: lack of information versus non-compliance with existing bulletins.

**Risk Assessment: The Core Unknown**
Our primary assumption is that managers lack real-time data. However, an equally plausible hypothesis is that managers are ignoring or unable to access the existing weather bulletins we already provide.
*   **Risk of Building the Wrong Thing:** If we build a live dashboard integrating satellite and sensor data, but the root cause is behavioral (managers skipping bulletins) or procedural (no mandate to check before dispatch), the new tool will suffer zero adoption. The latency concern you raised is secondary; even perfect real-time data is useless if the workflow doesn't require checking it.
*   **Cost of Failure:** Developing a live data pipeline for remote sites requires significant IT infrastructure investment. Wasting these resources on an unvalidated assumption jeopardizes our ability to fund other critical Q4 initiatives.

**Recommendation: The "Cheapest Test" First**
Before allocating technical resources for the November 5th review, we must validate the root cause. I recommend **halting all feature development** immediately and executing the following low-cost validation steps:

1.  **Conduct "Mom Test" Interviews (Owner: Product):** Interview the two managers who called, plus three others. Do not ask "Would you use a dashboard?" Instead, ask: "Walk me through the exact steps you took last Tuesday before dispatching the heavy machinery." "Show me the last weather bulletin you received." "What specifically made you decide to dispatch despite the thaw risk?"
    *   *Goal:* Determine if they saw the existing bulletins and chose to ignore them, or if they genuinely had no data.
2.  **Audit Current Usage (Owner: IT/Ops):** Analyze logs or distribution lists for the current weather bulletins. Are they being opened? At what time relative to dispatch?
    *   *Goal:* Quantify the gap between information availability and decision-making.

**Decision Rule for November 5th Review**
*   **Go (Build Feature):** Only if interviews confirm managers *tried* to find data, *could not*, and explicitly state that a live dashboard would have changed their specific decision last week.
*   **No-Go (Process Fix):** If interviews reveal they ignored existing bulletins, the solution is a process change (e.g., mandatory check-in protocol), not new software.
*   **Pivot:** If latency is confirmed as a blocker for *any* data type, we must solve the connectivity issue before visualizing data.

**Conclusion**
We cannot justify the investment based on two delay calls. The evidence suggests a knowledge gap in *why* the delays happened, not necessarily a technology gap. We must close this knowledge gap before writing code.

***

*Note: This assessment relies on the principle that behavioral evidence (what managers actually did) outweighs stated needs (what they say they want).*
