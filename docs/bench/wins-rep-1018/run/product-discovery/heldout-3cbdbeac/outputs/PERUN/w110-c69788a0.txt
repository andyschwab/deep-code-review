**Assessment: Do Not Proceed to Pilot Yet**

The current evidence (two disputes totaling €22,000) is **insufficient** to justify the investment in real-time sensor integration. The data confirms a financial loss but fails to validate the root cause or the economic viability of the proposed solution.

**1. Root Cause Unverified (The "Stable Conditions" Assumption)**
Your current process assumes stability during transit. The disputes prove the *result* (high moisture at arrival) but not the *cause*.
*   **Missing Evidence:** You have no data proving the timber left the warehouse within limits. If the spot checks are flawed or the timber was loaded wet, sensors in transit will only confirm your own upstream failure, not transport fluctuations.
*   **Risk:** Investing in transit sensors without verifying the "exit condition" may solve the wrong problem.

**2. Economic Viability Unknown (The 8% Cost Hurdle)**
The proposed solution adds an 8% freight cost. You have zero evidence that clients will accept this or that it prevents future losses exceeding that cost.
*   **Missing Evidence:** No client has agreed to pay the premium. No calculation exists showing that the cost of sensors is lower than the expected reduction in dispute losses.
*   **Risk:** You may build a system that proves the timber got wet, but which clients refuse to fund, leaving you with higher costs and the same disputes.

**Required Actions Before Pilot (The Cheap Tests)**

Do not build or rent sensors yet. Run these two low-cost tests to generate the missing evidence:

**Test A: The "Exit-Load" Verification (Feasibility/Root Cause)**
*   **Action:** For the next 5 shipments, perform rigorous moisture checks *immediately before sealing* the container and document them with time-stamped photos shared with the client.
*   **Metric:** Compare pre-load data vs. arrival dispute data.
*   **Kill Threshold:** If >20% of shipments show high moisture *before* loading, **drop the sensor idea**. The problem is internal warehousing, not transit. Fix the loading process first.

**Test B: The "Value-Prop" Conversation (Viability)**
*   **Action:** Contact the two disputing clients and three other major accounts. Present the data: "We can guarantee moisture levels with continuous monitoring, but it adds 8% to freight. Would you sign a contract paying this premium to eliminate moisture disputes?"
*   **Metric:** Number of verbal or written commitments to pay the premium.
*   **Kill Threshold:** If 0 out of 5 clients agree to discuss paying the premium, **drop the sensor idea**. The market does not value this specific solution enough to cover its cost.

**Decision Rule for the Committee**
*   **Go to Pilot:** Only if Test A confirms timber leaves dry (proving transit is the culprit) AND Test B secures at least one client willing to share or absorb the 8% cost.
*   **No Go:** If either test fails. The loss of €22,000 is a signal to investigate, not a mandate to spend on unproven technology.

**Summary for Tomorrow's Meeting**
The disputes highlight a risk, but they do not validate the solution. Approving a pilot now is betting on two unproven assumptions: that the warehouse process is perfect, and that clients will pay for data. Run the two tests above within 14 days. If they pass, *then* authorize a limited sensor pilot on one route.
