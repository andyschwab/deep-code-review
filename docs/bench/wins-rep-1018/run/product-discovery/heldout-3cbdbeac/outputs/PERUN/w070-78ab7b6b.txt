# Strategic Briefing: Apex Regional White-Label Proposal

**Decision Context**
Apex Regional proposes a $250,000 two-year contract to white-label our scheduling platform across 40 clinics. This requires removing our branding. The core strategic tension is between immediate revenue validation and the potential erosion of long-term brand equity and future deal flow with competing networks.

**Unknowns Requiring Owner Input (Do Not Guess)**
The following data points are external to this analysis and must be supplied by leadership or real market research before a final decision can be made. No assumptions have been fabricated for these slots:

*   **Exclusivity Clause Status:** Does the Apex contract legally prevent signing Competitor Network A or B?
    *   *Source Needed:* Legal review of the draft term sheet.
*   **Competitor Sensitivity:** Will Competitor Networks A and B refuse to buy if they know Apex uses our tech, even without our logo?
    *   *Source Needed:* Direct feedback from current negotiations (ask: "Does it matter who builds the engine if you can't see the badge?").
*   **Brand Dependency Rate:** What percentage of our current 15% YoY growth is driven specifically by brand recognition vs. product utility?
    *   *Source Needed:* Analysis of recent churn interviews or "very disappointed" survey comments citing brand trust.
*   **Unit Economics of White-Label:** Does the $250k deal require disproportionate custom engineering or support compared to standard licenses?
    *   *Source Needed:* Engineering lead estimate on maintenance hours for the white-label fork.

**Strategic Trade-Off Framework**

| Dimension | Argument for Accepting (Revenue Focus) | Argument for Rejecting (Equity Focus) |
| :--- | :--- | :--- |
| **Financial** | Guarantees $125k/year ARR. Validates enterprise readiness. | Risks capping total addressable market if exclusivity blocks larger networks. |
| **Brand** | Allows focus on product over marketing; brand may be less critical than assumed. | Removes visible proof of scale; makes future "trusted by 40+ clinics" claims harder to verify publicly. |
| **Market Position** | Secures a foothold in the regional clinic sector immediately. | Sets a precedent that the tech is a commodity; competitors may demand same terms. |
| **Risk** | Low execution risk if tech is stable. | High opportunity cost if this blocks a $1M+ deal with a national chain later. |

**Recommended Next Steps (Evidence Gathering)**
Before the strategy offsite, run these three low-cost tests to fill the unknowns above:

1.  **The "Blind" Negotiation Test:** In ongoing talks with Competitor Networks A and B, describe the solution as "powered by MedBrand" but ask if they would object if the end-user interface was co-branded or white-labeled for a specific health system. Record their specific objection, if any.
2.  **Contract Stress-Test:** Ask Apex for a version of the contract that allows MedBrand to list them as a customer in press releases (even if the UI is white-labeled). If they refuse, the risk to brand equity is confirmed high.
3.  **Customer Attribution Audit:** Review the last 10 sales cycles. Did customers sign because they knew the MedBrand name, or because the demo solved their problem? If the latter, brand equity risk is lower than assumed.

**Preliminary Non-Goals for This Stage**
*   **Do not** build a fully separate codebase fork until the exclusivity clause is resolved.
*   **Do not** assume brand confusion is a fact; treat it as a hypothesis to be disproven by the Customer Attribution Audit.

**Verdict Status:** `UNVERIFIED`. The decision depends entirely on the exclusivity clause and the results of the "Blind" Negotiation Test. Do not sign based on revenue alone.
