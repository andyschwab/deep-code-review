# Decision Memo: MVP Interface Prioritization for ChronicCare Plus

**Recommendation:** Prioritize the **Patient-Facing Interface** for the March 2025 MVP, but strictly condition the launch on a "Concierge" backend for doctors rather than a built dashboard.

**Rationale Based on Provided Evidence**
Your data identifies two distinct problems: patient forgetfulness (85%) and doctor overwhelm (90%). While building the doctor dashboard first appears to secure contracts, it fails to address the root cause of the workflow issue. If patients continue to forget medications (the 85% factor), they will generate the very messages overwhelming the doctors. A doctor-facing tool that receives data from disengaged patients merely digitizes the chaos; it does not reduce the volume of reactive care.

Your core assumption—that patient empowerment reduces doctor workload—is the riskiest link in this chain. You explicitly stated uncertainty about whether patients will engage without physician oversight. Building the doctor dashboard first assumes patient behavior will change on its own or that the dashboard alone can force compliance, which contradicts your finding that 85% forget schedules due to behavioral gaps, not lack of doctor visibility.

**The Trade-Off Analysis**
*   **Option A: Doctor Dashboard First.**
    *   *Pros:* May appeal to hospital administrators initially by offering visibility.
    *   *Cons:* High risk of low utility. If the 85% of patients remain disengaged, the dashboard displays "non-compliance" without solving it. Doctors remain overwhelmed by the same volume of crises, just viewed on a nicer screen. This risks high churn once the novelty wears off and the workload remains unchanged.
*   **Option B: Patient Interface First (Recommended).**
    *   *Pros:* Directly attacks the 85% forgetfulness metric. If successful, this reduces the frequency of missed doses and the subsequent panic messages that create the 90% doctor overwhelm. It validates your core value proposition: that better patient habits drive clinical efficiency.
    *   *Cons:* Risk of low initial adoption if patients feel unsupported.
    *   *Mitigation:* Do not build the doctor dashboard yet. Instead, use the $250k budget to perfect the patient experience, while handling the "doctor oversight" manually (e.g., clinic staff monitors alerts via email/SMS). This proves the *value* of the data to doctors before you spend capital building the *interface* for them.

**Strategic Path for the Advisory Board**
To secure hospital contracts without the dashboard, pitch the MVP as a "Patient Engagement Pilot" where the hospital provides the oversight manually for 3 months. The metric for success is not "doctor usage of a dashboard," but a reduction in inbound patient messages and improved adherence rates. Once you prove that the patient tool reduces the 90% overwhelm metric, the hospital administrators will demand the dashboard to scale that efficiency. Building the dashboard before proving the patient behavior change is premature optimization.

**Next Immediate Step**
Define the specific "kill threshold" for patient engagement. For example: "If fewer than 60% of the pilot patients log their medication 4 times a week after Month 1, we pause and rethink the engagement model before expanding."

***

*Note: This recommendation relies entirely on the behavioral causality in your provided data; no external market sizes were assumed. The "Concierge" approach allows you to test the doctor-value proposition without coding the dashboard.*
