### Decision Framework: Real-Time Translation vs. Human Interpreter Line

**Status:** **DO NOT BUILD YET.** The riskiest assumption (trust in high-stress scenarios) is untested, and the current technical constraint (2s latency) potentially violates the "sub-second" safety requirement. Proceeding to full integration before validating these factors introduces unacceptable liability.

#### 1. Riskiest Assumption Gate
*   **Assumption:** Paramedics will trust and act on machine-translated medical terminology during high-stress resuscitation, even with a 2-second delay.
*   **Impact:** Critical (Life/Death). **Uncertainty:** Extreme.
*   **Required Test Before Build:** A "Wizard-of-Oz" simulation drill.
    *   **Method:** Simulate the 2s delay and machine terminology in a controlled high-stress drill with 10 paramedics. Do not write code yet; manually inject the delay and translation.
    *   **Metric:** % of instructions followed without verbal verification or hesitation.
    *   **Kill Threshold:** If >10% of teams hesitate, double-check, or revert to the human line during the drill, **drop the feature** or pivot to "asynchronous only."
    *   **Deadline:** Run this drill by September 1st to allow time for a pivot before the October 15th deadline.

#### 2. Safety Implications Breakdown

| Feature | Potential Safety Benefit | Potential Safety Risk (Operational Bottleneck) |
| :--- | :--- | :--- |
| **Real-Time Machine Translation** | Immediate access to 12 dialects; removes wait time for human connector. | **Latency Hazard:** 2s delay disrupts CPR rhythm and rapid sequence intubation flows. <br> **Hallucination Risk:** Incorrect dosage or allergy translation could be fatal. <br> **Automation Bias:** Paramedics may override their own judgment due to over-reliance on the tool. |
| **Human Interpreter Line** | High accuracy; nuanced understanding of cultural context; legal liability shield. | **Connection Latency:** Time to connect to a human may exceed 2s initially. <br> **Availability:** Potential shortage of specific dialect speakers at 3 AM. |

#### 3. Operational Bottlenecks & Non-Goals
*   **Non-Goal for This Stage:** Full automation of life-critical instructions. The system must not replace human judgment until the "trust" metric is proven.
*   **Bottleneck (Machine):** The 2s guarantee is a hard ceiling. If the medical advisory board defines "sub-second" as <800ms, this vendor is disqualified regardless of accuracy.
*   **Bottleneck (Human):** Scaling the human line to cover 12 dialects instantly during mass-casualty events.

#### 4. Recommended Next Steps (Evidence Gathering)
To answer the Medical Advisory Board, you must generate **outside evidence**, not internal debate:
1.  **Run the Drill (Behavioral Evidence):** Execute the simulation described in the Riskiest Assumption Gate. Record exactly where the 2s delay causes workflow breakdown.
2.  **Mom-Test Interviews:** Ask paramedics about *past* errors involving language barriers. Do not ask "Would you use this?" Ask "Tell me about the last time you hesitated due to language."
3.  **Vendor Challenge:** Request a trial of the translation engine with *medical-specific* terminology to verify if the 2s delay holds true for complex terms or if it degrades further.

**Verdict:** Without the drill results, the safety implication is **Unknown/High Risk**. The operational bottleneck is the **latency vs. trust trade-off**. Do not commit to the feature set until the drill proves the 2s delay is survivable in a resuscitation context.
