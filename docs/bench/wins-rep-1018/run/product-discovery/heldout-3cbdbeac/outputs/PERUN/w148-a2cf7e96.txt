**Decision: Do not build the prediction tool yet.**

Your current plan fails the **Riskiest-Assumption Gate**. You are assuming the primary cause of spoilage is *unpredictable transit conditions* (solving for "when to harvest"). However, you explicitly stated a critical unknown: spoilage may be caused by *destination port delays* (a factor you cannot control).

If the root cause is port delays, a perfect prediction model will not reduce rejections; it will only accurately predict failure after the fact. Building the tool now risks spending engineering resources on a dashboard that confirms losses rather than preventing them.

**Immediate Action Plan (Before Friday's Meeting):**

1.  **Isolate the Failure Point (The Cheap Test)**
    Before modeling, you must distinguish between "Transit Spoilage" and "Port Delay Spoilage."
    *   **Action:** Install simple, low-cost data loggers in 3 upcoming containers.
    *   **Metric:** Compare the timestamp when fruit exceeds the ripeness threshold against the vessel's arrival timestamp and the actual unload timestamp.
    *   **Kill Threshold:** If >50% of the over-ripening occurs *after* the ship docks but *before* the gate exit, the prediction tool is **invalid** for solving this specific problem. The solution shifts to logistics negotiation or routing changes, not software.

2.  **Reframe the CEO/Sales Narrative**
    Do not promise "near zero rejections" based on a tool you haven't validated. Instead, present a **Diagnostic Roadmap**:
    *   *Current State:* We are guessing the cause (transit vs. port).
    *   *Proposal:* We are running a 2-week diagnostic to pinpoint the exact failure window.
    *   *Value Prop:* "We are not just building a predictor; we are engineering a guarantee. If the fault is transit, our model optimizes harvest. If the fault is port delays, our data gives you the evidence to demand compensation or change carriers."
    *   *Why this wins:* It shows buyers you are solving the *actual* problem, not just selling a feature.

3.  **Define the Real "Build" Criteria**
    Only proceed with the prediction engine if the diagnostic proves that:
    *   Spoilage happens primarily during ocean transit.
    *   Harvest timing adjustments (based on data) can materially extend shelf life to survive that specific transit duration.

**Summary for Stakeholders:**
Investing in prediction without knowing *where* the spoilage happens is like buying a better weather app when your roof is already leaking. We must first prove the leak is from the sky (transit) and not the foundation (port delays). The $120k contract depends on fixing the right leak.

**Non-Goals for This Stage:**
*   Building the full integration dashboard.
*   Training complex ML models on historical averages.
*   Promising specific reduction percentages until the failure point is isolated.

*Note: The "kill threshold" above is a starting proposal based on your volume; adjust if your margin for error is tighter. Do not fabricate data for the Friday meeting; present the *plan* to get the data as the strategic asset.*
