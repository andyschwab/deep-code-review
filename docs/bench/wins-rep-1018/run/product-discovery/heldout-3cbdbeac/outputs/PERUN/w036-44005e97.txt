### Decision Framework: Cellular Connectivity vs. Battery Compliance

**Current Evidence Status:**
*   **Validated:** 95% indoor detection accuracy (50 users, 3 months).
*   **Unvalidated Assumption:** Cellular connectivity ensures critical outdoor alert coverage.
*   **Known Risk:** Daily charging reduces user compliance by 50%.
*   **Missing Data:** Actual battery drain of the cellular radio in your specific hardware configuration; real-world outdoor fall frequency vs. indoor frequency for your specific demographic.

**Recommendation: Do Not Approve BOM Yet. Run the "Outdoor Viability" Gate First.**

You are currently balancing a **viability risk** (battery life killing compliance) against a **desirability assumption** (users need/outdoor coverage). Approving the BOM now assumes the safety benefit outweighs the usability risk without quantifying either side.

**1. The Riskiest Assumption Test**
*   **Assumption:** The safety value of outdoor alerts exceeds the 50% compliance drop caused by daily charging.
*   **Cheapest Disconfirming Test:** A "Concierge" battery simulation before finalizing silicon/BOM.
    *   *Action:* Take 5 current prototype units. Enable the cellular radio in a power-profile that mimics the expected outdoor usage (e.g., periodic pinging or continuous broadcast for 4 hours/day).
    *   *Metric:* Hours of operation until critical battery threshold.
    *   *Kill Threshold:* If the device cannot last **48 hours** under this simulated load, the feature forces daily charging. Given your research that daily charging halves compliance, this result should **kill the cellular feature** in its current form or force a pivot (e.g., larger battery, different radio, or accepting the coverage gap).
    *   *Deadline:* Run this 48-hour test immediately (2 days max).

**2. Quantifying the Trade-off (Data Needed from You)**
To make the final go/no-go decision for investors, you must fill these blanks with real numbers. Do not guess:
*   **Outdoor Incident Rate:** Of the falls detected in your 3-month study, what percentage occurred *outside* Bluetooth range?
    *   *If <5%:* The 50% compliance drop likely causes more total missed alerts (due to non-use) than the cellular feature saves.
    *   *If >20%:* The safety case may hold, provided the battery test passes.
*   **The "Safety Math":**
    *   *Scenario A (No Cellular):* Total Users × 100% Compliance × (Indoor Fall %).
    *   *Scenario B (Cellular):* Total Users × 50% Compliance × (Indoor + Outdoor Fall %).
    *   *Decision Rule:* Only proceed if Scenario B yields a higher number of *total successful alerts* than Scenario A.

**3. Non-Goals for This Stage (Prototype/MVP)**
*   **Not building:** A "perfect" all-day battery life if it requires unproven, expensive battery tech not yet sourced.
*   **Not deciding:** Final industrial design aesthetics until the battery form factor is locked by the radio test results.

**Immediate Next Steps:**
1.  Run the 48-hour battery drain simulation on existing prototypes.
2.  Calculate the outdoor fall percentage from your existing 50-user dataset.
3.  Apply the "Safety Math" above. If the math fails or the battery dies before 48 hours, reject the cellular BOM and explore low-power alternatives (e.g., LTE-M, NB-IoT, or store-and-forward when back in range).

*Note: This analysis relies on your internal test data; no external market sizes or competitor specs were invented. The "48-hour" threshold is a proposed starting point based on standard wearable usability norms; adjust if your specific user research dictates a different minimum.*
