**Decision: Do not build the full engine yet. Run the riskiest-assumption test first.**

**The Riskiest Assumption**
Your investment depends entirely on one unproven variable: *Can off-the-shelf models achieve >95% accuracy in a live, noisy OR?* If this is false, adoption is zero, and the $320,000 is lost. You currently have **no evidence** for this specific technical constraint, only user desire for time savings.

**The Required Test (Before November 30)**
Do not spend $320k or six months building a robust engine. Instead, execute a **Wizard-of-Oz / Concierge test** within the next 14 days:
1.  **Setup:** Equip one OR with a standard microphone and a human scribe (or a basic, unpolished API) hidden from the surgeon.
2.  **Action:** Have 3 of the 15 interested surgeons attempt to dictate notes during live procedures using "voice commands."
3.  **Metric:** Measure the actual **Word Error Rate (WER)** in the specific noise environment of your ORs.
4.  **Kill Threshold:** If the error rate exceeds 5% (accuracy <95%) requiring more than 2 corrections per minute, **drop the idea** or pivot to a different solution. Do not proceed to full development.

**Financial & Strategic Assessment**
*   **Potential Upside:** 15 surgeons × 12 mins/surgery × ~20 surgeries/month = ~60 hours saved monthly. This is significant operational value *if* the tech works.
*   **Downside Risk:** $320,000 sunk cost with zero adoption if accuracy fails.
*   **Verdict:** The *problem* is validated (15/18 surgeons frustrated). The *solution* is unvalidated. Building the full engine now is gambling $320k on an untested technical hypothesis.
