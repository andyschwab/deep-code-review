### Riskiest-Assumption Gate: Scheduling vs. Capacity

**Decision:** Do not build the dynamic scheduler yet. The riskiest assumption is that **shortages are caused by scheduling inefficiencies rather than a fundamental lack of truck capacity.**

If the root cause is a regional shortage of trucks during peak season, a dynamic scheduler cannot create supply; it will only optimize the order in which sites go dry. Building software now risks solving the wrong problem while the $30,000/hour losses continue.

#### The Cheapest Disconfirming Test
Before writing code, you must run a **Capacity Audit** to isolate the variable.

*   **The Test:** Manually reconstruct the logistics for the three recent emergency incidents using your static spreadsheets. For each incident, calculate the total water required versus the total truck-hours available in that region during that specific week.
*   **The Metric:** **Unmet Demand Volume** vs. **Idle Truck Time**.
    *   *Scenario A (Scheduling Failure):* Trucks were available in the region but were assigned to low-priority sites or sat idle while the pit ran dry.
    *   *Scenario B (Capacity Failure):* The sum of all requested water exceeded the total physical capacity of every truck available in the region, regardless of how they were scheduled.
*   **Proposed Kill Threshold:**
    *   If **>90%** of the shortage volume correlates with periods where **total regional demand > total regional truck capacity**, the assumption is **false**. Drop the software build; the solution requires leasing more trucks or securing local water sources.
    *   If **>20%** of the shortage volume correlates with **idle truck time** or **misaligned delivery windows** while water was available elsewhere, the assumption is **plausible**. Proceed to a prototype.
*   **Sample Size:** The 3 recent emergency incidents + 2 normal weeks for baseline.
*   **Deadline:** 48 hours (using existing spreadsheet data).
