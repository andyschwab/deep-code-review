### Decision Readiness Assessment: Telehealth Video Feature

**Current Verdict:** **UNVERIFIED / DO NOT BUILD YET**
The evidence provided is insufficient to allocate Q3 engineering budget. You have strong *stated intent* from three directors, but zero *behavioral evidence* regarding your riskiest assumption (bandwidth viability) and a critical unknown regarding usability (privacy). Building now risks delivering a feature that technically fails in the field or cannot be used due to environmental constraints.

#### 1. The Riskiest-Assumption Gate
Your primary technical assumption is: *"Low bandwidth will not prevent stable video calls if we compress the stream heavily."*
*   **Status:** Untested.
*   **The Trap:** "Heavily compressed" is a hypothesis, not a fact. If the stream drops below a usable frame rate or audio sync, the diagnostic value vanishes, and trust in the platform erodes.
*   **Required Cheap Test (Before Q3 Build):**
    *   **Action:** Do not build the feature into the app yet. Use a standalone, low-bandwidth video tool (e.g., a specialized lightweight client or even WhatsApp video as a proxy) on the *actual* clinic hardware and networks.
    *   **Metric:** Call stability rate (percentage of calls lasting 10+ minutes without dropout) and diagnostic sufficiency score (can the doctor actually see the symptom?).
    *   **Kill Threshold:** If <80% of test calls remain stable for 10 minutes on the clinic's current connection, **drop the video feature** for now. Optimize the messaging queue instead, as video will fail regardless of your compression algorithm.

#### 2. Critical Evidence Gaps (The "Unknowns")
You explicitly noted you do not know if staff have private rooms. This is a **viability/usability blocker**, not a minor detail.
*   **The Gap:** Telehealth requires patient confidentiality. If a doctor cannot speak freely due to lack of privacy, the feature is legally and ethically unusable, regardless of bandwidth.
*   **Required Evidence:**
    *   **Method:** Site visit or structured photo audit requested from the three directors.
    *   **Question:** "Show us the specific spot where a video call would happen. Can a patient speak about sensitive symptoms without being overheard by the waiting room?"
    *   **Decision Rule:** If 0 out of 3 clinics have a viable private space, the adoption rate will be near zero. The solution might require hardware (booths) before software (video).

#### 3. Adoption Rate Analysis
*   **Claimed Benefit:** 4 hours travel time saved per visit.
*   **Reality Check:** This is a *potential* benefit, not a *realized* one.
*   **Adoption Driver:** Adoption will not happen because directors *asked* for it; it will happen only if the video call is **reliable enough to replace the physical visit**.
*   **Projected Failure Mode:** If the video quality is poor, doctors will revert to text/images + phone calls, rendering the new feature shelf-ware. The "adoption rate" is directly correlated to the success of the bandwidth test above.

#### 4. Recommendation for Board Meeting
Do not request the full Q3 engineering budget for a full build. Instead, propose a **2-week Validation Sprint**:
1.  **Bandwidth Probe:** Run 20 simulated video calls using existing low-bandwidth tools on-site. Measure drop rates.
2.  **Privacy Audit:** Confirm physical space availability in all 3 clinics.
3.  **Decision Point:**
    *   **Go:** If bandwidth is stable AND privacy exists → Allocate budget for MVP.
    *   **No-Go/Pivot:** If bandwidth fails OR privacy is missing → Reallocate Q3 budget to optimizing the messaging queue (which you know works) and explore asynchronous video (record & send) instead of live streaming.

**Summary of Missing Data for Board:**
*   Empirical data on packet loss/latency in the target villages during peak hours.
*   Confirmation of physical privacy infrastructure.
*   A defined "minimum viable quality" threshold agreed upon by the doctors.

---
*Note: Do not present the "4-hour savings" as a guaranteed ROI until the technical feasibility is proven. The board should fund the *test*, not the *build*.*
