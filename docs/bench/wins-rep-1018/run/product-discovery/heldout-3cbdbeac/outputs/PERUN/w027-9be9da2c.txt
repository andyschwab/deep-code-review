### Evaluation: HomeHelp Dynamic Matching Viability

**Current Evidence Status:**
Your desirability evidence is strong. Interviewing 40 independent aides and finding that 35 (87.5%) prefer same-day shifts validates the *user need* for dynamic matching. This supports the hypothesis that workers want this feature.

However, your **viability evidence regarding infrastructure is currently insufficient** to greenlight the build. You have identified a specific, high-impact risk (monsoon internet outages) but lack the data to quantify its effect on your core metric (utilization).

**Critical Evidence Gaps:**

1.  **Infrastructure Failure Rate vs. User Base:**
    *   **Gap:** You suspect real-time matching is impossible for "20% of the user base" during outages, but this is an unverified estimate.
    *   **Impact:** If the 20% most affected by outages are also the 20% most likely to accept same-day shifts, your utilization target (85%) is mathematically unreachable regardless of algorithm quality.
    *   **Required Data:** You need historical uptime data specific to the neighborhoods where your 40 interviewed aides live/work, correlated with the November monsoon dates. Do not rely on general city-wide stats.

2.  **Behavioral Response to Failure:**
    *   **Gap:** You know users *prefer* same-day shifts, but you do not know what they *do* when the app fails to load during an outage. Do they switch to SMS/phone calls (which your platform might not track), or do they simply miss the shift?
    *   **Impact:** If they bypass the app during outages, your platform's recorded utilization will drop, even if the worker is actually working. This creates a false negative in your metrics.

3.  **The "Kill Threshold" for Reliability:**
    *   **Gap:** There is no defined threshold for acceptable downtime.
    *   **Required Decision Rule:** Define the maximum allowable outage duration before the dynamic matching feature is deemed a failure. For example: "If more than 15% of shift offers fail to deliver due to connectivity during a monsoon event, the real-time model is not viable without an offline-first fallback."

**Recommendation for Lead Developer & Operations Manager:**

Do not proceed to full build of the real-time matching engine yet. The riskiest assumption is not "will workers use it?" (answered: yes), but "can the infrastructure support it reliably enough to hit 85% utilization?"

**Immediate Next Steps (Cheap Tests):**
1.  **Concierge Test with Simulated Outage:** Select 5 of the 35 interested aides. Manually match them via SMS during a simulated "outage" window. Measure if they can successfully coordinate the shift without the app. This validates the *process* without building the *tech*.
2.  **Infrastructure Audit:** The Operations Manager must map the home locations of the 35 interested aides against known cell tower coverage maps and historical outage reports for those specific zones.
3.  **Define Fallback Logic:** Before coding, decide: If the API times out, does the system queue the request, switch to SMS, or fail? The "dynamic" feature must include a defined "degraded mode" to count toward utilization during outages.

**Verdict:** The feature is **desirable but unverified for viability**. The projected jump from 60% to 85% utilization assumes 100% system availability. Until the monsoon impact is quantified with local data, treat the 85% target as theoretical. Build the "offline-first" or SMS-fallback mechanism first; the real-time matching is secondary to ensuring the connection survives the rain.

**Note:** Do not fabricate uptime percentages; use only measured data from the proposed audit.
**Note:** Re-evaluate the 85% target if the audit shows >15% of the target workforce is in high-risk outage zones.
