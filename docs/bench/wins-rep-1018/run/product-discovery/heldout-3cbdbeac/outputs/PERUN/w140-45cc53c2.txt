### Critical Risks and Missing Data for Sensor Pilot Decision

**1. Unvalidated Root Cause Assumption**
You assume inconsistent fermentation is caused by unmonitored temperature/pH drift. However, flavor defects may stem from harvest timing, pod variety mixing, or drying inconsistencies post-fermentation.
*   **Missing Data:** A retrospective analysis of the two rejected batches' full process logs (harvest date, drying duration, transport time) compared to accepted batches. Without confirming that temperature/pH was the *actual* variable that deviated, sensors will only measure noise, not the cause of rejection.

**2. Operational Viability & Technical Capacity**
The success of this pilot hinges on cooperative staff correctly installing, calibrating, and maintaining $40 units in humid, remote environments.
*   **Missing Data:** A "capacity audit" of the specific cooperatives involved. Do they have reliable electricity for charging? Do staff possess basic digital literacy?
*   **Required Test:** Before buying hardware, conduct a "paper prototype" test where staff manually log temperature/pH twice daily for one week using cheap thermometers and strips. If they fail to maintain this low-tech log due to workload or complexity, high-tech sensors will likely be ignored or broken.

**3. Economic Thresholds and Margin Impact**
At $40/unit, the cost structure must be proven against the value of saved rejections.
*   **Missing Data:**
    *   **Volume per Batch:** How many kilograms does a single "batch" represent?
    *   **Rejection Cost:** What is the exact financial loss of one rejected batch (freight + product value + penalty)?
    *   **Break-even Calculation:** If a sensor costs $40, how many kilograms of rejected cocoa must it save to pay for itself?
    *   **Failure Rate Baseline:** Is the "inconsistent profile" happening in 2% of batches or 50%? If rejection is rare, the ROI on universal sensor deployment may never materialize.

**4. The "Kill Threshold" for the Pilot**
You cannot proceed to a full Q1 budget allocation without a defined failure condition for a micro-pilot.
*   **Missing Metric:** Define the numeric threshold for success. Example: "If the pilot sensors fail to predict a flavor defect in 3 out of 3 known bad batches, or if data upload compliance drops below 80% in week one, the hardware strategy is abandoned."

**5. Opportunity Cost vs. Driver Training**
The alternative investment (driver training) targets the transport leg of the chain.
*   **Missing Comparison:** What percentage of flavor degradation occurs *during* transit versus *during* fermentation? If 70% of damage happens in the truck due to heat exposure or delay, fixing fermentation monitoring solves only a fraction of the problem. You need a spoilage timeline analysis to determine where the biggest lever actually is.

**Immediate Next Steps (Before Jan 15):**
1.  **Run the Paper Prototype:** Test manual logging capacity for 5 days.
2.  **Calculate Unit Economics:** Determine the exact cost-of-rejection per kg to validate the $40 sensor price point.
3.  **Isolate the Variable:** Confirm via existing records that temperature/pH was the primary differentiator in the Oct/Nov failures.

Do not commit capital until the paper prototype proves the teams will use the tools, and the math proves a single saved rejection pays for multiple sensors.
