### Executive Recommendation: Proceed with a "Software-Only" Prototype, Do Not Commit to Hardware

Based on the evidence provided, you should **authorize the six-week prototype build immediately**, but strictly scope it as a **software-only simulation** that assumes hardware data exists. Do not allocate resources to physical sensor integration or supply chain commitments until the December tender clarifies the budget model.

#### Evidence Summary
*   **Problem Validation (Strong):** 11 of 14 interviewed coordinators confirmed manual logs cause a 20% spoilage rate. The pain point is verified; the "why" is clear.
*   **Solution Risk (High):** Your assumption that the tender will mandate *real-time alerts* is untested. More critically, the funding model for **hardware sensors** is unknown. If the government only funds software, a hardware-dependent solution is dead on arrival.
*   **Opportunity Cost:** Waiting until December compresses your delivery window for the Q2 2025 deadline. Building nothing now guarantees a rushed, high-risk implementation later.

#### The Riskiest Assumption & Required Test
Your critical unknown is not "can we build it?" but **"will the budget cover the sensors required to make it work?"**

*   **Assumption:** The final tender allocates budget for hardware sensors, not just software licensing.
*   **Cheapest Test (Before December):** Do not wait for the tender. Conduct 3 follow-up interviews with the same logistics coordinators or procurement officers.
    *   *Question:* "In previous rural health tech rollouts, was the budget for sensors CAPEX (hospital buys) or OPEX (software bundle)? Who currently pays for broken thermometers?"
    *   *Kill Threshold:* If 2 of 3 indicate hospitals have **zero** budget for new hardware, you must pivot the prototype to work with *existing* low-tech inputs (e.g., SMS gateways, manual batch uploads) rather than real-time IoT sensors.

#### Decision Rule for the Board
1.  **Approve** the 6-week sprint to build the **tracking dashboard and alert logic** using mock data.
2.  **Freeze** all decisions on sensor procurement or integration APIs.
3.  **Mandate** the 3 targeted interviews above to resolve the hardware funding question before the December tender release.
4.  **Re-evaluate** in December: If hardware is funded, integrate sensors. If not, deploy the simulator with manual/SMS inputs.

This approach secures your technical lead time while insulating the company from the financial risk of the hardware unknown.

*Note: This analysis relies solely on the 14 interviews and RFP hint provided; it does not include external market sizing or competitor analysis.*
*Ensure the "mock data" in the prototype realistically reflects the 20% spoilage scenarios described by coordinators to maximize demo impact.*
