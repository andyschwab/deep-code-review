### Decision Framework: Centralized vs. Federated Architecture

You cannot make this decision yet because two critical assumptions remain untested. Building either system now risks violating your Series B timeline or failing user needs. Below are the unknowns you must resolve with real evidence before committing.

#### 1. The Contract Velocity Assumption (Viability)
**Assumption:** Hospitals will sign contracts 50% faster if data never leaves their premises.
**Status:** Unverified. This is a hypothesis about buyer behavior, not a technical fact.
**Required Test:** Conduct "Mom Test" interviews with 5–7 hospital legal/procurement officers from your current network or target list.
*   **Ask:** "Tell me about the last time you signed a data-sharing agreement. What specifically caused delays? Can you walk me through a recent instance where data location was the primary blocker?"
*   **Do Not Ask:** "Would you sign faster if data stayed local?" (This invites hypothetical lying).
*   **Kill Threshold:** If fewer than 3 of 7 interviewees cite *data residency* as a top-3 bottleneck in past deals, the "50% faster" assumption is likely false. Pivot to optimizing other contract friction points.

#### 2. The Latency Tolerance Assumption (Usability)
**Assumption:** Researchers will be frustrated if query results exceed two seconds.
**Status:** Unverified. You do not know the actual workflow tolerance for oncology research queries.
**Required Test:** Run a Wizard-of-Oz or Concierge test with 3–5 researchers from your existing 10,000-patient dataset.
*   **Method:** Simulate federated query delays manually. Present results with varying latencies (2s, 5s, 10s, 30s) during their actual work.
*   **Metric:** Measure task abandonment or expressed frustration vs. acceptance.
*   **Kill Threshold:** If researchers accept latencies up to 10+ seconds for complex cohort queries without workflow disruption, the "2-second" requirement is over-engineering. If they abandon tasks at 3s, federated learning may need a hybrid caching layer or is currently non-viable for this use case.

#### 3. Scalability & Compliance Unknowns (Feasibility)
These cannot be modeled; they require external verification.
*   **Regulatory Precedent:** Does your specific jurisdiction (and the 100 target hospitals' jurisdictions) explicitly recognize federated learning as fully compliant without additional audits? *Action: Route to legal counsel specializing in health data for a written opinion.*
*   **Infrastructure Reality:** Can your current prototype handle the handshake overhead of 100 nodes? *Action: Build a throw-away prototype connecting only 5 nodes to measure actual overhead, then extrapolate. Do not guess.*

#### Recommended Next Steps (Before January Hiring)
1.  **Stop architectural debates.** The answer depends entirely on the results of the two tests above.
2.  **Run the contract interviews** this week. If data residency is not the primary blocker, the centralized option may be viable despite fears, simplifying your Series B story.
3.  **Run the latency simulation** next week. If researchers tolerate higher latency, federated learning becomes a stronger candidate for compliance without sacrificing utility.
4.  **Define Non-Goals for Prototype Stage:**
    *   *Not* building a production-grade 100-hospital network yet.
    *   *Not* optimizing for sub-second query speed until researcher tolerance is proven.
    *   *Not* hiring the full backend team until the riskiest assumption (likely contract velocity) is validated.

**Verdict:** Do not choose an architecture today. Choose the *test* that invalidates your biggest risk. If the contract speed assumption fails, the entire value prop of federated learning for sales collapses, making centralized the default unless compliance forbids it. If latency tolerance is low, federated learning requires a different technical approach or must be shelved.
