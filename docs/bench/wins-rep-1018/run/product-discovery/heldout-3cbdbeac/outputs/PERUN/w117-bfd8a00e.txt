### Decision: Do Not Greenlight Build Yet (Riskiest Assumption Gate Open)

**Verdict:** The current evidence is **insufficient** to justify the infrastructure investment for a dedicated speedboat fleet. While the financial pain (€200k lost) is real, the data fails to distinguish between a structural market need and a temporary anomaly (Grand Prix). Building now violates the **Riskiest Assumption Gate**: you are preparing to incur high fixed costs (fleet, regulatory compliance, staffing) before validating the core assumption that this demand is consistent year-round.

**1. The Riskiest Assumption**
*   **Assumption:** "The five urgent requests represent a consistent, recurring demand pattern, not a one-off spike caused by the Grand Prix."
*   **Impact:** High (€200k/6 weeks extrapolated).
*   **Uncertainty:** Critical (Unknown if demand persists post-event).
*   **Status:** **UNTESTED.**

**2. The Required Cheap Test (Before Building)**
Do not buy boats or hire crews yet. Run a **Concierge Test** to validate frequency and willingness to pay without the fixed infrastructure.
*   **The Test:** For the next 4–6 weeks, manually fulfill any rush request using *existing* third-party water taxis or charter services, even if it costs you more than the part's margin.
*   **Metric:** Number of organic rush requests per week *after* the Grand Prix effect dissipates.
*   **Kill Threshold:** If fewer than **2 requests per week** occur organically (excluding major events) over a 4-week period, **drop the dedicated fleet idea**. The volume does not support the fixed cost of a dedicated fleet.
*   **Regulatory Check:** Simultaneously, route the regulatory question to a local maritime lawyer. Do not guess; this is a binary go/no-go constraint.

**3. Data Gaps & Investor Notes**
Your current data set (n=5 over 6 weeks including a major event) is statistically noisy. Investors will rightly flag this as "event-driven" rather than "product-market fit."
*   **Action:** Present the €200k loss as the *problem validation*, but present the **Concierge Test plan** as the *solution validation*.
*   **Pivot:** If the concierge test confirms >2 requests/week consistently, *then* calculate the Unit Economics (Cost of third-party boat vs. Cost of owned fleet) to justify the build.

**4. Non-Goals for This Stage**
*   **Not Building:** A dedicated fleet, custom dispatch software, or hiring full-time captains.
*   **Why:** These are scale solutions for a problem that is not yet proven to be permanent. Building them now is gold-plating an unverified hypothesis.

**Note:** The "90% recovery" figure is a hypothesis; the concierge test will reveal the actual capture rate based on real delivery success.
**Note:** Regulatory approval is a hard gate; if commercial speedboats are prohibited in peak season, the business model is dead regardless of demand.
