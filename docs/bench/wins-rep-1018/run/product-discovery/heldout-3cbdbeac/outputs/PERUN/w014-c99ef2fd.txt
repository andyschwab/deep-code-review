### Comparison: SMS Reminders vs. Mobile App for Rural Kenya

| Feature | SMS Reminders (Feature Phone) | Mobile App (Smartphone) |
| :--- | :--- | :--- |
| **Potential Reach** | **High:** Matches 85% of your interviewed patient base (feature phones). | **Low:** Limited to the ~15% with smartphones; excludes the majority. |
| **Data Cost Barrier** | **None:** Uses existing voice/SMS plans; no data bundle required. | **High:** Requires data purchases; conflicts with the $2/month average spend limit. |
| **Reliability Risk** | **Unknown:** Susceptible to network congestion and physical interference during the rainy season (starting April). | **Variable:** Dependent on data signal strength; likely worse than SMS in remote areas but unquantified. |
| **Development Cost** | **Low:** Simple integration with SMS gateways. | **High:** Requires UI/UX, offline synchronization, and ongoing data optimization. |

#### Logistical Gaps in Current Understanding
Your current plan relies on the assumption that SMS is reliable, but you lack evidence for two critical variables:
1.  **Rainy Season Delivery Rates:** You do not know the percentage of SMS messages that fail to deliver or are delayed in your specific remote clinics during heavy rain. Network towers in rural areas may have power or backhaul issues exacerbated by weather.
2.  **Read/Action Latency:** You do not know if patients receive the SMS instantly, hours later, or not at all. High delivery success does not guarantee timely reading if the network is congested.

#### Proposed Two-Week Validation Test
**Objective:** Quantify the SMS delivery failure rate and latency in target regions before the rainy season peaks.

**Test Design:**
*   **Sample:** Select 30 patients across 3 distinct remote clinics (10 per clinic).
*   **Action:** Send 2 standardized SMS messages per day for 14 days (total 28 messages per patient).
    *   Message A (Morning): "Reply YES to confirm you took your medication."
    *   Message B (Evening): "Reply YES if you have your insulin stored safely."
*   **Metric to Track:**
    1.  **Delivery Failure Rate:** % of messages returned as "undelivered" by the carrier.
    2.  **Response Latency:** Time elapsed between send timestamp and reply timestamp.
    3.  **Non-Response Rate:** % of delivered messages where no reply was received (indicates access or literacy issues).

**Kill Threshold (Decision Rule):**
If the **delivery failure rate exceeds 10%** OR the **average latency exceeds 4 hours**, SMS cannot be relied upon as the *sole* channel for critical adherence. You must then investigate hybrid models (e.g., community health worker visits + SMS) or delay the launch until infrastructure is verified.

**Cost Estimate:**
~$0.05 per SMS × 28 messages × 30 patients = ~$42. Well within your $15,000 budget.

**Next Step:** Execute this test immediately. Do not build the full reminder system until you have the failure rate data from this pilot.

*Note: This test measures network capability, not patient behavior. A separate test will be needed to verify if SMS actually changes adherence rates.*
*Ensure your SMS gateway provider gives you delivery receipts (DLR) to accurately track failures.*
