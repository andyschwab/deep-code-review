**Decision Framework: Web Portal vs. Native App for Helix Trials**

**Current State Evidence**
*   **Baseline:** 45 mins/participant (paper); 12 errors/quarter.
*   **Critical Unknown:** Adoption comfort of older oncology participants with smartphone interfaces.
*   **Constraint:** One platform only; Launch by Nov 15.

**Option A: Responsive Web Portal**
*   **Adoption Barrier (Low-Medium):** No installation required. Participants can use site tablets or their own devices via a link. Reduces "anxiety of the new" (JTBD force) as it behaves like a standard website.
*   **Implementation Speed (High):** Single codebase works across all devices. Faster to build and test within the Nov 15 deadline.
*   **Risk:** Reliance on site Wi-Fi/device availability. If the site lacks tablets, participants must use personal phones, which reintroduces the demographic uncertainty.

**Option B: Native Mobile App**
*   **Adoption Barrier (High):** Requires download/installation from an app store. This is a significant friction point for older demographics and requires site staff to manage device storage/updates. High "habit" force against switching.
*   **Implementation Speed (Low):** Requires separate builds (iOS/Android) and app store review cycles, threatening the Nov 15 deadline.
*   **Risk:** Low adoption due to installation friction could render the time-saving benefits null if participants revert to paper or require heavy staff assistance.

**The Riskiest Assumption Gate**
Your primary risk is not technical; it is behavioral: *"Older oncology participants can comfortably complete consent on a smartphone interface without increasing staff assistance time."*
*   **Do not build either full solution yet.** If you build the full platform based on the assumption that time will be cut in half, you risk automating a process users cannot navigate.
*   **The Cheap Test (Run this week):** Create a clickable prototype (Figma or similar) of the *single most complex consent form*.
    *   **Test:** Ask 5 site coordinators to walk 5 older participants (65+) through the prototype on a tablet/phone.
    *   **Metric:** Time to complete + Number of times the participant asks for help.
    *   **Kill Threshold:** If average time > 25 mins OR help requests > 3 per session, the "digital saves time" assumption is false for this demographic. Pivot to a kiosk-mode tablet solution or hybrid paper-digital flow before coding.

**Recommendation for Steering Committee**
1.  **Default Choice:** Lean toward the **Responsive Web Portal**. It minimizes installation friction (critical for older users) and maximizes the probability of meeting the Nov 15 deadline.
2.  **Condition:** Approval is contingent on passing the "Riskiest Assumption Test" above.
3.  **Non-Goal for Phase 1:** Do not build offline synchronization, biometric authentication, or multi-language support yet. These are scope-expansions that threaten the deadline and do not address the primary risk of basic usability.

**Immediate Next Step:** Run the 5-user prototype test within 48 hours. Bring the *time-to-complete* data, not opinions, to tomorrow's meeting.

*Note: This analysis structures your existing data and identifies the missing evidence required for a safe decision. It does not fabricate user behavior or market statistics.*
