### Go/No-Go Recommendation: Conditional No-Go for Generalization

**Verdict:** Proceed with the pilot to validate **technical feasibility** and **local efficacy**, but issue a **No-Go** on claiming the results generalize to the broader UK market or seeking full regulatory clearance based solely on this data.

**Risk Analysis of Current Scope**
Your pilot faces a critical **selection bias risk**. Because all three partners are London teaching hospitals, they possess unique variables: higher specialist density, complex case mixes, and different staffing hierarchies compared to district general hospitals.
*   **The Risk:** An algorithm optimized for the resource-rich, high-complexity environment of a teaching hospital may fail in a resource-constrained general hospital where turnover bottlenecks are caused by porters or cleaning staff shortages rather than surgeon availability.
*   **The Consequence:** If you claim generalizability now, you risk regulatory rejection for insufficient representative data and investor loss of confidence when the system underperforms in the 80% of the market that is non-teaching hospitals.

**Required Evidence to Generalize Findings**
To move from "works in these three hospitals" to "works in the UK," you must gather the following evidence *during* or *immediately after* the initial 6-week run:

1.  **Contextual Variable Mapping (Immediate):**
    *   Before analyzing efficiency gains, document the specific "staffing models" and "case mix indices" of your three pilots.
    *   **Action:** Create a comparison matrix against national averages for UK hospitals. Explicitly list where your pilots deviate (e.g., "Pilot ratio of nurses-to-beds is 20% higher than national average"). This defines the boundary of your current claim.

2.  **The "Mechanism of Action" Test (During Pilot):**
    *   Do not just measure *total* time saved. You must isolate *why* time was saved.
    *   **Action:** Tag every minute of turnover reduction to a specific cause (e.g., "surgeon arrived earlier," "cleaning team rotated faster," "anesthesia prep streamlined"). If your gains rely heavily on factors unique to teaching hospitals (e.g., immediate specialist availability), the algorithm is not yet generalizable.

3.  **Representative Validation (Post-Pilot Requirement):**
    *   You cannot generalize without data from a non-teaching hospital.
    *   **Action:** Secure a commitment for a second-phase pilot in a **district general hospital** (non-teaching) before seeking broad clearance. The sample size of 120 rooms is statistically robust for *signal detection*, but only if the *population* is representative. 120 rooms in one ecosystem is not representative of a diverse ecosystem.

**Revised Pilot Plan for Stakeholders**
*   **Days 1–45 (Current Plan):** Run the 120-room pilot. **Goal:** Prove the algorithm functions without error and reduces turnover in a high-complexity setting. **Report Claim:** "Proven efficacy in teaching hospital environments."
*   **Days 46–90 (Required Addition):** Execute a targeted "stress test" in one non-teaching hospital (even if only 10 rooms). **Goal:** Validate that the *mechanism* of savings holds when staffing models change.
*   **Regulatory Strategy:** Submit the initial data as "Phase 1: Feasibility in Specialized Centers." Use the second-phase data to support "Phase 2: General Population Efficacy."

**Decision Rule for Investors**
*   **GO** for technical deployment in similar teaching hospitals.
*   **NO-GO** for broad market claims or full regulatory clearance until the "non-teaching" variable is tested. Proceeding otherwise creates a liability where early success becomes a false positive.
