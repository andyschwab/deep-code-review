### Decision Framework: Chatbot vs. Live Nurse Video

**Status:** UNVERIFIED. The current evidence is insufficient to authorize full development of either option. The "trust" assumption remains untested with behavioral data, and survey responses (stated intent) contradict the abandonment metrics (actual behavior).

#### 1. The Riskiest Assumption Gate
**Assumption:** Users will trust an algorithm's medical advice for severe symptoms without human verification.
**Current Evidence:** Weak. Survey data shows 40% explicitly demand humans; 60% want speed. Stated preference for speed does not prove trust in a medical crisis.
**Required Test Before Build:** A "Wizard-of-Oz" or Concierge test.
*   **Method:** Simulate the chatbot flow manually for a small cohort. When the "bot" gives advice, measure if users proceed to the next step or demand a human override.
*   **Metric:** Conversion rate from "Advice Given" to "Action Taken."
*   **Proposed Kill Threshold:** If fewer than **70%** of users follow the automated advice without requesting human verification in a controlled test, the pure-chatbot model fails for high-acuity use cases. (Derived from your 80% automation goal; if trust is lower than the volume of queries you hope to automate, the safety risk is too high).
*   **Decision Rule:** Do not build the AI logic until this threshold is met in a low-fidelity test.

#### 2. Structured Comparison of Options

| Feature | Option A: Automated Chatbot | Option B: Live Nurse Video |
| :--- | :--- | :--- |
| **Primary Value** | Speed & Scalability. Targets the 10-min signup drop-off. | Trust & Safety. Addresses the 40% demanding human contact. |
| **Evidence Status** | **Hypothesis Only.** Assumes 2-min signup solves abandonment. | **High Confidence.** Directly satisfies the 40% segment, but costly. |
| **Key Risk** | **Safety/Liability.** Users ignoring bad advice or missing severe symptoms due to lack of trust. | **Unit Economics.** High cost per interaction may prevent scaling; does not solve the 30% abandonment if wait times are long. |
| **Missing Data** | Behavioral proof of trust in triage advice. | Cost-per-acquisition vs. Lifetime Value at scale. |
| **Recommendation** | **Do not build yet.** Run the trust test defined above. | **Viable for MVP** only if restricted to high-acuity cases identified by a *simple* rule-based screener (not full AI). |

#### 3. Interpretation of Current Metrics
*   **30% Abandonment (10-min signup):** This confirms friction exists, but does not prove a chatbot is the solution. The friction may be privacy concerns, not just time. Reducing time to 2 minutes via a bot might increase abandonment if users feel unsafe providing symptom data to a machine immediately.
*   **Survey (60% Speed vs. 40% Human):** This is a segmentation signal, not a verdict. In medical contexts, "speed" is often desired for minor issues, while "human" is demanded for serious ones. A binary choice (Chatbot OR Video) likely fails both segments.

#### 4. Strategic Path Forward (Non-Goals & Next Steps)
*   **Immediate Non-Goal:** Building complex AI logic for full automation. The riskiest assumption (trust) is unproven.
*   **Immediate Non-Goal:** Hiring a large nursing staff for general triage. This ignores the scalability constraint.
*   **Proposed Hybrid Experiment:**
    1.  Implement a **rule-based** (not AI) 2-minute screener to categorize urgency.
    2.  Route "Low Urgency" to a content library (no chatbot yet).
    3.  Route "High Urgency" to Live Nurse Video.
    4.  **Measure:** Does the 2-minute screener reduce abandonment? Do high-urgency users accept the video handoff?

**Conclusion for CMO:** Do not authorize the full build of the AI chatbot. The safety risk is unquantified. Authorize a **2-week concierge experiment** to validate the "trust threshold." If users reject automated advice in the test, the product must be a human-led triage with software support, not an automated bot.

*Note: All thresholds above are proposed starting points for your team to validate against your specific risk tolerance and unit economics.*
*Do not proceed to code generation until the "trust threshold" test yields real behavioral data.*
