**Strategic Assessment: Billing Automation Investment**

**Recommendation:** Do **not** sign the vendor contract by November 1st. Proceeding now violates the "Riskiest-Assumption Gate." Your primary risk is not the speed of generation, but the validity of the input data. Automating a process fed by dirty data will not reduce errors; it will accelerate dispute generation at scale, worsening cash flow rather than improving it.

**The Critical Gap**
Your assumption that automation eliminates 90% of errors relies entirely on data cleanliness, yet you explicitly state uncertainty regarding current customer data. The four internal complaints validate a *capacity* problem (backlog), but they do not validate a *data quality* baseline. If 30% of your manual invoices currently require human correction due to bad addresses, pricing tiers, or contract terms, an automated system will instantly generate 200+ incorrect invoices per month with zero human friction to catch them. This shifts the bottleneck from "invoice generation" to "dispute resolution," likely increasing the total time-to-cash.

**Required Action: The Cheap Test**
Before any capital commitment, you must run a **disconfirming test** on your data, not your process.
1.  **Sample Audit:** Randomly select 50 invoices from the current backlog.
2.  **Automation Simulation:** Manually attempt to process these 50 records through the vendor's proposed logic (or a spreadsheet macro mimicking it) without human intervention.
3.  **Kill Threshold:** If more than 5 out of 50 (10%) fail to generate a dispute-free invoice due to data errors, the project **fails** the go/no-go criteria. Do not sign the contract until data cleansing reduces this failure rate below the threshold.

**ROI Reality Check for the CFO**
The projected ROI (3 days → 4 hours) is theoretical until the data risk is retired.
*   **Current State:** 200 invoices/month backlog; 3-day cycle.
*   **Worst-Case Automation:** 200 invoices generated in 4 hours, but 40% trigger immediate customer disputes. Result: Cash flow stalls further while accounting manages angry clients.
*   **Revised Path:** Dedicate the next 3 weeks to data cleansing and the sample audit above. If the test passes, the 6-month ROI target is achievable. If it fails, the investment must be redirected to data remediation first.

**Conclusion**
The internal complaints justify solving the backlog, but they do not justify *this specific solution* at *this specific time*. The business case is incomplete without empirical evidence of data readiness. Delay the signature, run the data audit, and let the failure rate of the sample set dictate the decision.
