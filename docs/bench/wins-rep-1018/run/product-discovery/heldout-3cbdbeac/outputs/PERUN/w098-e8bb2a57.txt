**Assessment: Crew Scheduling Tool Feasibility & Risk Profile**

**Status: UNVERIFIED / DO NOT BUILD YET**
The proposal to build an automated scheduling tool rests on a critical, untested assumption: that current union agreements permit algorithmic control over shift assignments. Until this legal constraint is resolved, building the tool is a high-risk waste of resources.

**1. The Riskiest Assumption Gate**
*   **Assumption:** The January 2023 union agreements allow for automated, algorithmic enforcement of rest periods without violating collective bargaining terms or requiring renegotiation.
*   **Impact:** High. If false, the tool cannot be deployed, rendering the engineering effort worthless and potentially triggering labor disputes.
*   **Uncertainty:** Critical. No legal review has been cited.
*   **Required Test (Before Build):** A formal legal interpretation of the January 2023 agreement by the union liaison and external labor counsel.
    *   **Metric:** Binary verdict (Permitted / Prohibited / Requires Renegotiation).
    *   **Kill Threshold:** If the verdict is "Prohibited" or "Requires Renegotiation" with a timeline exceeding 6 months, **drop the automated build** immediately. Pivot to manual process adaptation.
    *   **Deadline:** Must be completed before any code is written or vendor selected.

**2. Operational Risk Analysis**
*   **Current State:** Fleet utilization is 88%.
*   **Risk:** Transitioning from manual to automated scheduling introduces immediate friction. If the algorithm fails to account for edge cases (e.g., sudden port delays, medical emergencies) that human schedulers currently manage, utilization could drop sharply.
*   **Non-Goal for Prototype:** Do not attempt to "eliminate fatigue-related risks entirely" via software alone. This is a false certainty. The goal of a prototype should be *detection and alerting*, not *enforcement*, until the legal and operational safety net is proven.

**3. Recommended Path Forward**
*   **Step 1 (Legal Gate):** Engage the union liaison to review the January 2023 agreement specifically for clauses regarding "assignment authority," "notice periods for shift changes," and "algorithmic management."
    *   *Input Needed:* Specific clauses from the agreement.
    *   *Output:* Go/No-Go decision.
*   **Step 2 (Concierge Test):** Before building software, simulate the "strict rest period" rules manually for one vessel on the Athens-Crete route for two weeks.
    *   *Hypothesis:* Enforcing strict rest periods will force schedule changes that lower utilization below acceptable limits.
    *   *Metric:* Change in utilization % and number of uncovered shifts.
    *   *Kill Threshold:* If utilization drops below 80% or uncovered shifts exceed 2 per week, the current rule set is operationally unviable regardless of the tool.
*   **Step 3 (Decision):**
    *   If Legal = **Go** AND Manual Simulation = **Stable**: Proceed to MVP build.
    *   If Legal = **No-Go**: Adapt manual processes; do not build.
    *   If Simulation = **Unstable**: Revise fatigue rules before automating.

**Conclusion**
Do not present a technical build plan to the safety committee yet. Present a **validation plan** focused on the legal gate and the operational simulation. Building the tool now assumes the answer to the most dangerous question is "yes."

**Notes**
*   This assessment relies on the "Riskiest Assumption" framework; no market data or user interviews were fabricated.
*   The "88% utilization" figure was used only to establish the stakes for the operational simulation test.
