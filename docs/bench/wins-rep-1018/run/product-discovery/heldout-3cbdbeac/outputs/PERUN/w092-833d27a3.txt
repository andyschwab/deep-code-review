**Assessment: Robotic Arm Integration Pilot (Yokohama Port)**

**Status: UNVERIFIED / NO-GO FOR BUILD PHASE**
Current evidence is insufficient to proceed to implementation or detailed disruption modeling. The proposal relies on untested assumptions regarding infrastructure feasibility and unquantified operational baselines. Per the riskiest-assumption gate, no build or pilot planning can occur until the structural constraint is resolved.

**1. Critical Blocker: Infrastructure Feasibility (The Riskiest Assumption)**
*   **Assumption:** Existing concrete pier infrastructure can support the specific weight and dynamic vibration loads of the new robotic units without costly reinforcement.
*   **Verdict:** **Unknown.** This is a hard physical constraint, not a software variable. An error here results in catastrophic safety failure or prohibitive retrofit costs.
*   **Required Action:** Immediate referral to a licensed structural engineering firm for a site-specific load analysis.
*   **Decision Rule:** If the structural audit recommends reinforcement exceeding [Insert Budget Cap] or extends the timeline beyond [Insert Date], the project must be paused or re-scoped. Do not proceed with pilot planning until this is signed off.

**2. Unvalidated Efficiency Claims**
*   **Claim:** Manual handling causes a 45-minute delay; automation will reduce turnaround by 20%.
*   **Evidence Gap:** These figures are stated assertions from terminal operators, not measured data from your specific workflow.
*   **Required Action:** Conduct a time-and-motion study on 10–15 recent vessels handling oversized cargo to establish a verified baseline.
    *   *Metric:* Actual minutes spent on manual oversized cargo handling vs. total vessel turnaround.
    *   *Variable:* Is the 45-minute delay caused purely by lifting speed, or by positioning/coordination issues that robotics might not solve?
*   **Kill Threshold:** If the verified baseline delay is <30 minutes, the ROI of automation may not justify the capital expenditure.

**3. Pilot Phase Disruption vs. Gain (Conditional)**
*   *Note:* A quantitative comparison cannot be generated without the structural clearance and baseline data above. However, the framework for assessment is:
*   **Potential Disruptions:**
    *   **Workflow Interruption:** Installation requires halting the specific berth. Calculate cost of downtime per hour.
    *   **Integration Friction:** New hand-off points between human operators and robots often create *new* bottlenecks before efficiency is gained.
*   **Projected Gains:**
    *   Only valid if the "20% reduction" is mathematically derived from the *verified* baseline, not the assumed 45 minutes.

**4. Recommended Next Steps (Before Board Presentation)**
1.  **Route Structural Query:** Commission the independent structural audit immediately. This is the primary go/no-go gate.
2.  **Gather Baseline Data:** Deploy observers to record actual handling times for oversized cargo over the next 2 weeks.
3.  **Refine the Hypothesis:** Replace "assume 20% gain" with "hypothesize X% gain based on observed Y-minute bottleneck."
4.  **Define Pilot Scope:** Only design the pilot *after* steps 1–3 confirm feasibility and value. The pilot should test the *integration* of the arm, not the structural integrity or the basic value proposition.

**Conclusion for Board:**
Recommendation is **NO-GO** for immediate implementation/pilot. The project carries unacceptable unknown risk regarding pier integrity. Request approval to fund the **structural audit** and **baseline data collection** as Phase 1. A go/no-go decision on the robotic integration itself should be deferred until those findings are reviewed.

*   Do not fabricate structural tolerance numbers or efficiency percentages; these must come from the audit and field observation.
