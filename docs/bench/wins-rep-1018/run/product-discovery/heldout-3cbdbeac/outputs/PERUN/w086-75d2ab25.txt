### Decision Evaluation: AI Forecasting Tool Viability

**Current Status:** **UNVERIFIED**. The project cannot be declared a failure nor a success based on current evidence. The three demonstrations provided negative behavioral data (rejection of automated suggestions), but the root cause remains an untested assumption.

**Key Decision Factors**

1.  **The Riskiest Assumption Gate**
    Your working assumption is that rejection stems from *interface design* (lack of explainability) rather than *algorithmic distrust* or *workflow mismatch*. This is currently unproven.
    *   **The Risk:** Building a complex "hybrid human-in-the-loop" system based on the hope that explainability fixes trust is a high-cost bet on an untested hypothesis.
    *   **The Required Test:** Before pivoting architecture, you must run the cheapest possible test to disconfirm the assumption that "explainability drives adoption."
    *   **Proposed Cheap Test:** Do not build the hybrid engine yet. Take the existing "black box" output and manually append a plain-English "reason code" (e.g., "Forecast increased 20% due to upcoming bank holiday trend") to the next weekly spreadsheet update for *one* partner.
    *   **Kill Threshold:** If the officer ignores the manual reason code or still prefers their six-hour manual model despite the explanation, the assumption is false. The issue is likely workflow integration or fundamental distrust of AI, not just UI. **Decision Rule:** If <1 out of 3 partners engages with the manual reason codes within two weeks, abandon the "explainability pivot" and reassess the core value prop.

2.  **Behavioral Evidence vs. Stated Intent**
    *   **Fact:** Officers rejected the tool in favor of a 6-hour manual process.
    *   **Interpretation:** The "cost" of the current pain (6 hours/week) is currently lower than the "risk" of using your tool. This indicates the perceived risk of error (stockouts/overstock) is extremely high.
    *   **Implication:** A hybrid model adds complexity. If the core algorithm is not perceived as significantly *more* accurate than their heuristics, adding a "human-in-the-loop" step may simply add friction without solving the trust deficit. The metric for success is not "willingness to try" but "reduction in manual adjustment time."

3.  **Scope Defense & Non-Goals**
    *   **Non-Goal for Next Review:** Building the full hybrid infrastructure. Until the "explainability" assumption is validated via the manual test above, engineering effort on loop mechanics is premature optimization.
    *   **Immediate Goal:** Validate whether *any* form of AI suggestion is acceptable when the "why" is visible.

**Recommendation for VP Review**
Do not decide between "pivot to hybrid" or "abandon" today. The data is insufficient.
1.  **Pause major engineering work** on the hybrid model.
2.  **Execute the manual "reason code" test** immediately with the existing partners.
3.  **Re-evaluate on the 15th:**
    *   *If partners use the manual reasons:* Proceed to spec the hybrid model (explainability is the key).
    *   *If partners ignore the manual reasons:* The problem is not the UI. Abandon the current approach and investigate if the algorithmic accuracy actually beats their 6-hour manual models. If the AI cannot demonstrably beat the spreadsheet, no amount of explanation will save it.

**Note:** Do not fabricate a "trust score" or estimate market adoption; these must come from the proposed manual test results.
**Note:** The 6-hour manual process is your baseline metric; any solution must reduce this time to be viable.
