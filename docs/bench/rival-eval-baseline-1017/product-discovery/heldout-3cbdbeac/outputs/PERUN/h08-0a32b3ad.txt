### Demand Test Design: Corporate Wellness Intent

**Test Type:** Two-Step "Fake Door" with Commitment Signal
**Audience:** 500 existing individual users at large companies.
**Duration:** 10 business days.

#### 1. The Mechanism (Beyond the Click)
Do not stop at the "Learn More" click. A click measures curiosity, not purchase intent. To gauge willingness to pay, the landing page must require a low-friction but high-intent action before showing details.

*   **Step 1 (Email):** Subject line focuses on a specific corporate pain point (e.g., "Reduce your team's healthcare costs with FitTrack"). CTA: "See Corporate Plans."
*   **Step 2 (Landing Page):** Briefly describe the value prop. Instead of a generic "Contact Us," offer two distinct paths:
    *   **Path A (High Intent):** "Request Pricing & Pilot Proposal." Requires: Work Email, Company Size, and a checkbox: "I have budget authority or can influence the Q3 wellness budget."
    *   **Path B (Low Intent):** "Download One-Pager." Requires: Email only.
*   **Step 3 (The Gate):** Upon clicking Path A, show a message: "Thanks. Due to high demand, we are onboarding 5 pilot partners this month. Enter your email to join the priority waitlist." (This captures the lead without over-promising immediate delivery).

#### 2. Metrics & Kill Thresholds
Define success *before* sending the email. Use these thresholds to make the Go/No-Go decision.

| Metric | Definition | Go Threshold | No-Go / Pivot Threshold |
| :--- | :--- | :--- | :--- |
| **Click-Through Rate (CTR)** | % of emails clicked. | > 4% | < 2% |
| **Commitment Rate** | % of clickers who choose Path A (Request Pricing). | > 20% of clickers | < 5% of clickers |
| **Qualified Lead Rate** | % of total recipients who complete Path A + check "Budget Authority". | **> 1% (5 leads)** | **< 0.4% (2 leads)** |

**Decision Rule:**
*   **GO:** If you secure ≥ 5 Qualified Leads. This indicates sufficient demand to build a prototype.
*   **NO-GO:** If Qualified Leads < 2. The idea is not compelling enough to risk engineering time.
*   **PIVOT:** If CTR is high (>4%) but Commitment Rate is low (<5%). The problem is interesting, but the solution or pricing model is unclear.

#### 3. Follow-Up Actions for Lukewarm Response
If results fall into the "Pivot" or "No-Go" zones, do not build. Execute these immediate follow-ups to diagnose the failure:

1.  **The "Almost" Interview:** Email the users who clicked but *did not* request pricing (Path B or drop-offs). Ask one question: "What was the missing piece that stopped you from requesting a pilot today?" (Look for price sensitivity, lack of authority, or feature gaps).
2.  **The "Yes" Interview:** Call the few who *did* request pricing. Ask: "What specific event triggered your interest today?" and "What happens if you don't solve this problem this quarter?"
3.  **Offer Adjustment:** If interest was high but budget authority was low, test a "Self-Serve Team Plan" (credit card entry) vs. "Enterprise Contract" in a second micro-test to see if the friction was the contract term or the price.

#### 4. Executive Presentation Format
Present the data as evidence, not opinion:
*   "We tested demand against 500 targeted users."
*   "X% showed curiosity (clicked), but only Y% showed intent (requested pricing)."
*   "Our threshold for building was 5 qualified leads. We achieved [Number]."
*   "Recommendation: [Build / Kill / Pivot] based on this behavioral data."

**Note:** Do not fabricate results or estimate these numbers; the validity of the decision depends entirely on the actual data collected from this test.
**Note:** Ensure your email compliance settings allow for this type of testing with your existing user list.
