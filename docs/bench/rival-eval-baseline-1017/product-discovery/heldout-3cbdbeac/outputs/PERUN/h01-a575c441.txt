### Decision Status: **Do Not Build Yet**

The current evidence is insufficient to justify allocating two developers for six weeks. While 60% of your interviewed sample (3 out of 5) requested scheduling, this represents only **0.75% of your total active user base** (3 out of 400). Proceeding now risks building a feature for a vocal minority while ignoring the 99.25% who have not voiced this need.

#### 1. Evidence Gap Analysis
*   **Sample Bias Risk:** Your data comes entirely from users who agreed to calls. These users are often more engaged or have specific pain points than the average user. The "silent majority" (395 users) may prefer the current list view or use external calendars successfully.
*   **Intent vs. Behavior:** Users stated a *need* ("we need scheduling"), but you have no behavioral evidence that they will *use* it enough to impact retention. Stated intent is the weakest form of validation.
*   **Unverified Assumption:** The hypothesis that this feature drives a **10% retention lift** is currently a guess. No data in your prompt links calendar usage to churn.

#### 2. Riskiest Assumption Gate
Before writing code, you must test the single riskiest assumption: **"Lack of built-in scheduling is a primary driver of churn for a significant portion of our 400 users."**

**Required Cheap Test (Run before development):**
*   **Method:** Fake-Door Test or Concierge Pilot.
*   **Action:** Add a "Add to Calendar" button in the current UI that doesn't exist yet, or manually offer a scheduling service to 20 random users.
*   **Metric:** Click-through rate or adoption rate.
*   **Kill Threshold:** If fewer than **15% of active users** interact with the prompt or adopt the manual workaround within one week, **do not build**. The engineering cost outweighs the potential retention gain.

#### 3. Recommended Next Steps for Investor Deck
Instead of requesting resources for a full build, present this **Validation Plan** to your investor:

| Phase | Action | Cost | Success Metric (Go/No-Go) |

Request approval for **Phase 1 and 2 only**. Committing to the full six-week build now is an unjustified gamble on unrepresentative data. Secure behavioral proof from the broader 400-user base first.
