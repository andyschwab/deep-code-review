**Reasoning**

A 10% absolute increase in completion (80% vs. 90%) represents a massive potential uplift in gross merchandise value. Mathematically, if you send 1,000 users to checkout:
*   **Prototype A:** 800 completed orders.
*   **Prototype B:** 900 completed orders.
*   **Net Gain:** 100 additional orders per 1,000 users.

While friction is generally undesirable, users typically tolerate longer processes if they successfully complete the transaction, whereas a failed checkout (the 20% failure rate in Prototype A) guarantees zero revenue and high frustration. The "speed" of Prototype A is illusory if it results in abandoned carts.
