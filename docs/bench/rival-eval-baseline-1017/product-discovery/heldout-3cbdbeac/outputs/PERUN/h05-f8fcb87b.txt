### Survey Result Interpretation: TeamSync

**Verdict: UNVERIFIED Product-Market Fit (PMF)**

Based strictly on the data provided, your assumption is correct: you have not yet crossed the standard threshold for strong product-market fit, but the data is currently incomplete due to non-response bias.

**1. The "Very Disappointed" Metric**
*   **Respondent Data:** 45 out of 300 respondents = **15%**.
*   **Standard Benchmark:** Strong PMF is typically indicated when **40% or more** of users say they would be "very disappointed" without the product.
*   **Implication:** Among those who cared enough to reply, only a minority derive critical value. This suggests the core value proposition is not yet resonating deeply with the active user base.

**2. The Non-Respondent Risk (The 85% Gap)**
*   **Data Gap:** 1,700 users (85%) did not respond.
*   **Interpretation:** In satisfaction surveys, non-respondents are rarely more satisfied than respondents; they are usually indifferent or have churned mentally. It is statistically unsafe to assume the missing 85% would push your score above 40%. Even if every single non-respondent voted "very disappointed" (highly improbable), the total would be ~87%, but realistic modeling suggests the true population score likely remains below the 40% threshold.
*   **Conclusion:** You cannot claim PMF until you have evidence from a representative sample or behavioral data (retention) that contradicts this survey signal.

**3. Strategic Recommendation: Do Not Pivot Yet; Double Down on Core Value**
Your instinct to focus on core value rather than new features is the correct strategic move.
*   **Avoid Pivoting:** A pivot implies the current problem space is wrong. You do not know that yet; you only know the *solution* isn't sticking deeply enough.
*   **Stop Feature Expansion:** Adding features to a product that 85% of users are indifferent to will increase complexity without solving the core engagement issue.
*   **Action Plan:**
    1.  **Qualitative Deep Dive:** Conduct "Jobs-to-be-Done" interviews specifically with the 45 "very disappointed" users. What specific workflow do they rely on? Isolate the "magic" they experience.
    2.  **Analyze the Indifferent:** Interview 5–10 users who said "somewhat disappointed." What is missing? Why isn't it essential?
    3.  **Behavioral Check:** Correlate survey responses with usage logs. Do the "very disappointed" users have a higher retention rate or frequency of use? If yes, your goal is to move more users into that usage pattern.

**Growth Trajectory Outlook (Next 6 Months)**
If you continue adding features without raising the "very disappointed" score above 40%, growth will likely remain flat or decline due to churn. If you successfully iterate on the core value drivers identified by your top fans, you can expect retention cohorts to flatten (stop dropping off), which is the leading indicator of sustainable growth.

*Note: This analysis relies solely on the survey figures provided; true PMF also requires verifying that retention cohorts are flattening over time.
