### Prioritized Assumptions for Validation

Based on your constraint of running only two experiments in three weeks and the need to maximize learning while minimizing risk, here is the prioritized list using an **Impact vs. Ease of Testing** framework.

#### 1. Top Priority: User Adoption Frequency (The "Value" Risk)
*   **Assumption:** "Managers will check the predictive modeling dashboard at least once a week."
*   **Rationale:** While data accuracy is foundational, a perfectly accurate model provides zero value if no one uses it. In new product development, **Value Risk** (will they use it?) often outweighs **Feasibility Risk** (can we build it accurately?) in the early stages. If managers do not have a habit of checking such dashboards, the product fails regardless of data precision.
*   **Impact:** High. If false, the product has no market fit.
*   **Ease of Testing:** High. You can validate this without building the full model.
*   **Recommended Experiment:** **Concierge Test / Fake Door.**
    *   Create a low-fidelity prototype or even a static PDF report simulating the dashboard insights.
    *   Manually deliver this to 5–10 target managers for one week.
    *   **Success Metric:** Track how many open the report, how long they spend reviewing it, and if they ask follow-up questions. Do not ask "Would you use this?"; observe if they *actually* look at it.

#### 2. Second Priority: Data Accuracy Threshold (The "Usability/Feasibility" Risk)
*   **Assumption:** "The predictive model must be 95% accurate to be trusted by managers."
*   **Rationale:** Your team assumes high accuracy is required, but this is unverified. Managers might trust a model with 80% accuracy if it saves them time, or they might distrust even 99% accuracy if they don't understand the logic. Validating the *required* threshold prevents over-engineering.
*   **Impact:** High. If the threshold is higher than your technical capability, the product is not viable. If lower, you save significant engineering time.
*   **Ease of Testing:** Medium. Requires some data simulation but not full deployment.
*   **Recommended Experiment:** **Wizard of Oz Test.**
    *   Present managers with predictions generated by a simpler, faster method (or even manual analysis) labeled as "model output" with varying stated confidence levels (e.g., "80% confident").
    *   **Success Metric:** Measure at what accuracy level managers make a decision based on the data versus ignoring it. Determine the minimum viable accuracy, not the ideal.

#### Why "Data Accuracy" (Binary) Was Not First
Testing if "data is wrong" is a binary engineering check. However, the business risk isn't just whether the data *can* be accurate, but whether the *level* of accuracy achieved drives adoption. By testing adoption first, you ensure you are solving a problem people care about. By testing the accuracy *threshold* second, you define the specific technical target needed to succeed, rather than aiming for an arbitrary 100% perfection that might delay your roadmap.
