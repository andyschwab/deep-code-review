### Risk & Value Analysis: TaskFlow Scheduling Feature

**Executive Summary**
The current evidence is **insufficient** to justify allocating two developers for six weeks. While the potential value is high if the assumption holds, the risk of building a feature for a "vocal minority" rather than a broad market need is critical. The sample size (5 users, 1.25% of the base) introduces significant selection bias, and the reliance on stated needs ("we need this") rather than observed behavior violates core discovery principles.

#### 1. Value Risk (Will it create value?)
*   **Current Evidence:** 3/5 users requested the feature; 2/5 are satisfied with the status quo.
*   **The Gap:** Stated interest does not equal usage. Users often request features they believe they *should* want, not what they will actually use daily.
*   **Critical Question:** Did the three users describe specific instances where the lack of scheduling caused a workflow failure, or did they simply express a desire? Without stories of past struggle, the "value" is hypothetical.
*   **Retention Assumption:** The projection of a 10% retention increase is unvalidated. There is no data linking calendar integration to churn in your current analytics.

#### 2. Usability & Adoption Risk
*   **Fragmentation:** If only a subset of users needs this, does it complicate the interface for the 60% who prefer the list view?
*   **Onboarding:** Can users discover and configure the scheduling feature without support? External calendar apps are deeply entrenched; switching costs (mental and practical) are high.
*   **Integration Depth:** A "built-in" scheduler must match the robustness of Google/Outlook/Apple Calendar to be viable. A "lite" version may frustrate users who expect full functionality.

#### 3. Viability & Feasibility Risk
*   **Opportunity Cost:** Two developers for six weeks equals ~480 engineering hours. This is a massive investment for a 400-user base.
    *   *Alternative:* Could those 480 hours be spent on performance improvements, mobile optimization, or sales outreach that impacts *all* users?
*   **Maintenance Burden:** Scheduling involves complex logic (time zones, recurring events, conflicts). This introduces long-term technical debt and support overhead.
*   **Market Fit:** With only 400 active users, the Total Addressable Market (TAM) for this specific feature within your current base is tiny. If the feature doesn't attract *new* users, the ROI timeline is dangerously long.

#### 4. Strategic Risk (The "Vocal Minority" Trap)
*   **Selection Bias:** You interviewed users who agreed to calls. These are often your most engaged (or most frustrated) users, not the silent majority.
*   **False Positive:** Building based on 3 requests risks solving a niche problem while neglecting broader bottlenecks that affect the other 395 users.

### Recommendation for the Board
**Do not approve the full build yet.** The risk of waste is too high given the limited data.

**Proposed Next Steps (Low-Cost Validation):**
1.  **Fake Door Test:** Add a "Schedule" button to the list view for all 400 users. Track click-through rates. If <15% click, the demand is not broad enough.
2.  **Concierge MVP:** Manually create calendar invites for the 3 requesting users for one week. Measure if they actually use them and if it solves their bottleneck.
3.  **Survey the Silent Majority:** Send a targeted survey to the 395 non-interviewed users focusing on *past behavior*: "When was the last time you missed a deadline because of calendar sync issues?"

**Decision Criteria:** Proceed to development only if the Fake Door test shows >15% engagement OR if the manual concierge test proves a direct link to reduced churn in a pilot group.

---
*Note: This analysis prioritizes validated learning over feature output to protect investor capital. Presenting a validation plan rather than a build request demonstrates mature product governance.*
