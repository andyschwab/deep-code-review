### Enhanced Demand Test Framework: FitTrack Corporate Wellness

**Objective:** Move beyond "interest" (clicks) to validate "purchase intent" and "willingness to pay" before building the prototype.

#### 1. The Experiment Design: "The Concierge Landing Page"
Instead of a simple "Learn More" link, direct users to a dedicated landing page that simulates the buying process without requiring code.

*   **The Hook:** Clearly articulate the corporate value proposition (e.g., "Reduce employee healthcare costs by 15%").
*   **The Friction (Crucial Step):** Do not just ask for an email. To gauge real intent, introduce friction that mimics a purchase decision:
    *   **Option A (Soft Commitment):** "Request a Pilot Proposal." Require the user to input their company name, estimated employee count, and select a budget range dropdown (e.g., <$5k, $5k-$10k, >$10k).
    *   **Option B (Hard Commitment - Recommended):** "Reserve a Spot for Our Beta Program." State that spots are limited. Ask for a refundable $50 deposit or a signed Letter of Intent (LOI) to secure a 20% discount on the first year.
*   **The Price Anchor:** Explicitly display a price range (e.g., "$8 per employee/month") on the page. If they click through and fill out the form *knowing* the price, the signal is significantly stronger than a blind click.

#### 2. Specific Metrics for Go/No-Go
Replace the generic 5% click-through rate (CTR) with these higher-fidelity metrics:

*   **Primary Metric: Conversion to Qualified Lead (CQL)**
    *   *Definition:* % of email recipients who complete the form with company details and budget selection.
    *   *Target:* Aim for **1–2%** of the total list (5–10 companies). This is a harder action than clicking, so the percentage will be lower but more valuable.
*   **Secondary Metric: Deposit/LOI Rate**
    *   *Definition:* % of CQLs who provide a refundable deposit or sign an LOI.
    *   *Target:* **20–30%** of those who filled the form. If 10 people fill the form, 2–3 should be willing to put money or a signature on the line.
*   **Qualitative Signal: Sales Conversations Booked**
    *   *Definition:* Number of recipients who reply to the email or book a demo call directly.
    *   *Target:* **5+** meaningful conversations with decision-makers (HR Directors, Benefits Managers).

#### 3. Follow-Up Actions for Lukewarm Response
If the initial test yields low conversion (<0.5% CQL or zero deposits):

*   **Immediate Action: The "Break-Up" Email**
    *   Send a follow-up to non-converters 3 days later: "We noticed you didn't reserve a spot. Was it the price, the features, or timing?" Include a single-click poll. This often uncovers the specific blocker (e.g., "Too expensive" vs. "Not the right time").
*   **Pivot to Qualitative Discovery**
    *   Stop sending broad emails. Select the 10–15 users who *did* click but didn't convert. Interview them directly. Ask: "What would have needed to be different for you to request a proposal today?"
*   **Reframe the Offer**
    *   If price is the blocker, test a "Freemium Pilot" model where the first 50 employees are free for 3 months, but require a formal contract signature to activate. This tests legal/procurement friction rather than just price sensitivity.
*   **Go/No-Go Recommendation**
    *   **GO:** If you secure 3+ LOIs or deposits.
    *   **NO-GO:** If you get <2 qualified leads and zero willingness to discuss budget after follow-ups. Do not build the prototype; return to problem discovery.

**Note:** Ensure your email compliance (CAN-SPAM/GDPR) is checked when asking for detailed corporate data.
**Note:** A refundable deposit is the strongest possible signal short of a full contract.
