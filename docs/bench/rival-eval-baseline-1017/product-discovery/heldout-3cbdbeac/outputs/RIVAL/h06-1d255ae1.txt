### Risk Assessment: DataStream Reporting Feature

**Executive Summary**
The decision to deprecate the reporting feature carries a disproportionate financial risk relative to its engineering cost. While the feature serves only 3% of users, it supports MegaCorp, a client representing significant annual revenue ($100k). The current maintenance cost (20 engineering hours/month) is likely lower than the Customer Acquisition Cost (CAC) and lifetime value (LTV) lost if MegaCorp churns due to a broken compliance workflow.

**Risk Analysis by Category**

1.  **Viability Risk (High)**
    *   **Assumption:** The $100k annual revenue from MegaCorp exceeds the annual cost of maintaining the feature.
    *   **Analysis:** 20 hours/month × 12 months = 240 hours/year. Unless your fully loaded engineering cost exceeds ~$416/hour, the feature is financially viable solely based on MegaCorp's subscription. Deprecating the feature risks immediate churn, resulting in a net negative financial impact.
    *   **Verdict:** High risk of revenue loss if deprecated without a migration path.

2.  **Feasibility & Technical Debt (Medium)**
    *   **Assumption:** The 20 hours spent on bug fixes are purely reactive and prevent work on the 97% user base.
    *   **Analysis:** While 20 hours is a drain, it represents a small fraction of a typical sprint capacity. However, if the module is unstable, it poses a risk of catastrophic failure during MegaCorp's compliance audits, which could trigger churn regardless of feature availability.
    *   **Verdict:** The code is a liability, but the cost is quantifiable and contained.

3.  **Value Risk (Segmented)**
    *   **Assumption:** The feature provides low value because only 3% use it.
    *   **Analysis:** This is a false metric for B2B compliance tools. Value is not distributed evenly; for MegaCorp, this feature is critical (binary value: works or doesn't). For the other 2.9%, it may be negligible. The "97%" argument fails if the 3% includes other high-value enterprise clients not yet vocal.
    *   **Verdict:** High value for specific high-revenue segments; low value for the mass market.

4.  **Strategy & Objectives (Medium)**
    *   **Assumption:** Resources spent here delay improvements for the majority.
    *   **Analysis:** Diverting 20 hours/month may delay minor features for the mass market but is unlikely to block strategic initiatives unless the team is extremely small. The strategic objective of retaining enterprise clients outweighs incremental gains for smaller accounts.

**Recommendation**

**Do not deprecate the feature at this time.** The financial risk of losing MegaCorp ($100k/year) outweighs the efficiency gain of saving 20 engineering hours/month.

**Action Plan:**
1.  **Stabilize, Don't Expand:** Allocate the 20 hours strictly to stability and monitoring, not new features. Treat this as "maintenance mode."
2.  **Validate Scope:** Immediately interview 5–10 other users in the 3% activation group. Determine if MegaCorp is an outlier or if there are other silent enterprise clients relying on this.
3.  **Migration Strategy:** Only consider deprecation once a viable alternative exists that satisfies MegaCorp's specific compliance requirements. Engage MegaCorp now to discuss their long-term needs before building a replacement.
4.  **Isolate the Module:** If technically feasible, containerize this module to prevent its bugs from affecting the core platform, reducing the risk to the 97%.

**Conclusion for CTO:**
Retain the feature as a targeted enterprise offering. The technical debt is the "cost of goods sold" for retaining a key account. Reframe the 20 hours not as waste, but as insurance against $100k revenue loss. Prioritize isolating the code to protect the main codebase rather than removing the functionality.

*Note: This assessment assumes MegaCorp has no immediate workaround and that churn would occur upon deprecation. Validate this assumption with the client before finalizing the roadmap.*
*Engineering hours calculated at a blended rate; adjust if your specific cost per hour exceeds $416.*
