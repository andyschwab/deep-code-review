### Demand Test Design: Corporate Wellness Intent

**Test Type:** Two-Step "Fake Door" with Commitment Signal
**Audience:** 500 existing individual users at large companies.
**Duration:** 10 business days.

#### 1. The Mechanism (Beyond the Click)
Do not stop at the "Learn More" click. A click measures curiosity, not purchase intent. To gauge willingness to pay, the landing page must require a low-friction but high-intent action before showing details.

*   **Step 1 (Email):** Subject line focuses on a specific corporate pain point (e.g., "Reduce your team's healthcare costs"). Body describes the outcome, not the features. Call to Action (CTA): "See if your company qualifies."
*   **Step 2 (Landing Page):** Briefly restate the value prop. Instead of a generic "Learn More" button, present two distinct paths:
    *   **Path A (High Intent):** "Request Pilot Proposal" – Requires entering work email and company size. *Optional but recommended:* A checkbox stating "I have budget authority or can influence Q3 spending."
    *   **Path B (Low Intent):** "Just show me the features" – Leads to a static PDF or video.
*   **Step 3 (The Gate):** Upon clicking Path A, display a message: "Thanks. Due to high demand, we are onboarding 5 pilot partners this month. Enter your email to join the waitlist, or reply to this email to schedule a 15-min fit assessment."

#### 2. Metrics & Kill Thresholds
Define success before sending. Do not rely on a single vanity metric.

| Metric | Definition | Target (Hypothesis) | Kill Threshold (Stop/Pivot) |
| :--- | :--- | :--- | : |
| **Qualified Lead Rate** | % of total emails who complete Path A (Work Email + Company Size). | > 2.0% (10 leads) | < 0.5% (2 leads) |
| **Budget Signal Rate** | % of Qualified Leads who check "Budget Authority" or reply to schedule. | > 20% of Qualified Leads | < 5% of Qualified Leads |
| **Reply Rate** | % of total emails resulting in a direct reply asking about price/terms. | > 1.0% (5 replies) | 0 replies |

**Decision Logic:**
*   **GO:** If you hit both the Qualified Lead Rate and the Budget Signal Rate.
*   **NO-GO:** If you fall below the Kill Thresholds. Do not build the prototype; the problem is not painful enough or the channel is wrong.
*   **ITERATE:** If you hit Qualified Lead Rate but miss Budget Signal Rate. The interest is real, but the offer or pricing model is unclear. Proceed to concierge interviews (see below).

#### 3. Follow-Up Actions for Lukewarm Response
If results are below targets but not zero (e.g., 5 clicks, 0 sign-ups):

1.  **Stop the Test:** Do not extend the timeline hoping for more data.
2.  **Conduct "Switch" Interviews:** Select the 5 people who clicked but did not sign up. Send a personal email: "I noticed you checked out our corporate page but didn't request a pilot. I'm not selling anything; I'm trying to understand what was missing. Was it timing, budget, or feature fit?"
    *   *Goal:* Identify the specific friction (Anxiety vs. Lack of Pull).
3.  **Pivot the Offer:** If the issue is price uncertainty, run a second micro-test with a specific price anchor (e.g., "$5/employee/month") to see if the drop-off is due to cost or lack of need.

#### 4. Executive Presentation Format
Present the data as evidence, not opinion:
*   "We tested demand with 500 targeted users."
*   "X% expressed curiosity (clicks), but only Y% demonstrated purchase intent (qualified leads)."
*   "Based on our pre-defined kill threshold of Z%, the decision is [GO/NO-GO/ITERATE]."
*   "Next step: [Build Prototype / Cancel Project / Run 5 Interviews]."

**Note:** Ensure your email compliance (unsubscribe links) is active before sending to your existing list.
**Note:** Do not fabricate competitor pricing or market size in your executive deck; route those questions to external research if the internal test passes.
