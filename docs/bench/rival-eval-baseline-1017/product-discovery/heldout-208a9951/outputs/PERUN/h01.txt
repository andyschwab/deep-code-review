### Evidence Status: UNVERIFIED / INSUFFICIENT FOR BUILD DECISION

**Current Data Summary**
*   **Signal:** 3 of 5 interviewed users requested built-in scheduling; 2 were neutral.
*   **Sample Size:** 5 users (1.25% of 400 active users).
*   **Proposed Investment:** 2 developers × 6 weeks (approx. 480 engineering hours).
*   **Hypothesis:** Feature will increase retention by ≥10% in Q1.

**Risk Analysis**
1.  **Sampling Bias (High Risk):** Five users do not represent the broader market of 400. This sample size cannot distinguish between a "vocal minority" and a majority need. Basing a 6-week build on 1.25% coverage is statistically unsafe.
2.  **Stated Intent vs. Behavior (High Risk):** Users *saying* they need a feature is not evidence they will *use* it or that it drives retention. This is "stated intent," not behavioral evidence. The assumption that this specific feature drives the 10% retention lift is currently a guess.
3.  **Opportunity Cost:** Allocating two developers for six weeks removes capacity from other potential retention drivers or bug fixes. If the feature fails to move the needle, the cost is not just time, but lost momentum.

**Required Next Steps (Before Build Approval)**
Do not approve the 6-week build yet. Execute the following cheap tests to validate the assumption:

*   **Test 1: The "Fake Door" Quantitative Check**
    *   **Action:** Add a "Connect Calendar" or "Schedule View" button to the current list view for *all* 400 users. When clicked, show a message: "Coming Soon – Join Waitlist."
    *   **Metric:** Click-through rate (CTR).
    *   **Kill Threshold:** If fewer than 15–20% of active users click this button over one week, the demand is likely too low to justify the build.
    *   **Why:** This tests actual behavior across the whole user base, not just five voices.

*   **Test 2: The Retention Correlation Check**
    *   **Action:** Analyze the current usage data of the 3 users who asked for scheduling. Are they your *most* retained users? Or are they churning despite using the tool?
    *   **Metric:** Correlation between "users who manage dates externally" and "high retention."
    *   **Kill Threshold:** If users who already juggle external calendars are *not* your most loyal segment, adding an internal calendar may not fix the retention problem.

*   **Test 3: Concierge/Wizard-of-Oz (Optional but Stronger)**
    *   **Action:** Manually create a schedule view for the 3 interested users using existing data and send them a PDF/screenshot weekly.
    *   **Metric:** Do they open it? Do they reference it in their work?
    *   **Kill Threshold:** If they don't use the manual version, they won't use the automated one.

**Recommendation for Investor**
*   **Decision:** **NO GO** on the 6-week build at this time.
*   **Reasoning:** The evidence is anecdotal and statistically insignificant. The risk of building the wrong thing is high.
*   **Proposal:** Approve a **1-week validation sprint** instead. Use this time to run the "Fake Door" test and analyze retention correlations.
*   **Success Criteria for Build:** Proceed to the 6-week build *only if* the Fake Door CTR exceeds [Insert Target %] AND data shows a clear link between scheduling needs and current churn.
