### Analysis of TeamSync Satisfaction Survey

**Current Status: Product-Market Fit (PMF) is UNVERIFIED.**

Based strictly on the data provided, your assumption is directionally correct but requires precise framing. You have not failed to achieve PMF; rather, you have insufficient evidence to claim it exists.

**1. The "Very Disappointed" Metric**
*   **Respondent Data:** 45 out of 300 respondents (15%) said they would be "very disappointed."
*   **The Benchmark:** Industry standard (Sean Ellis test) suggests ~40% of *respondents* must answer "very disappointed" to indicate strong PMF.
*   **The Gap:** At 15%, your active respondents do not show the intensity of love required for organic, viral growth. This confirms your hypothesis: the core value proposition is not yet resonating deeply enough with the users who cared enough to reply.

**2. The Non-Respondent Risk (The 1,700 Silent Users)**
*   **The Unknown:** You cannot assume the 1,700 non-respondents feel differently. In survey methodology, silence usually correlates with indifference, not hidden passion.
*   **Worst-Case Scenario:** If *none* of the non-respondents would be very disappointed, your true score drops to 2.25% (45/2000).
*   **Best-Case Scenario:** Even if every single non-respondent would be very disappointed (highly improbable), your score would only reach ~87%. However, relying on this hope is a strategic error.
*   **Conclusion:** You must treat the non-respondents as "not very disappointed" until proven otherwise. Your effective PMF score is currently between 2.25% and 15%, both of which are below the threshold for scaling.

**3. Strategic Recommendation: Do Not Pivot Yet; Double Down on Core Value**
*   **Avoid Pivoting:** A pivot implies the current problem space is wrong. You do not have evidence of that yet; you only have evidence that the *solution* isn't sticking hard enough. Pivoting now risks abandoning a solvable problem for a new unknown.
*   **Double Down on Core Value:** Your instinct to pause new features is correct. Adding features to a product that only 15% of engaged users love will increase complexity without increasing retention.
*   **Action Plan for March Retreat:**
    1.  **Stop Building New Features:** Freeze the roadmap for net-new capabilities.
    2.  **Qualitative Deep Dive:** Conduct "Jobs-to-be-Done" interviews with the 45 "very disappointed" users. Ask specifically: *What job were you hiring TeamSync to do? What almost stopped you from switching? What would you use if we didn't exist?* Find the common thread.
    3.  **Interview the "Somewhat Disappointed":** Understand what is missing that prevents them from moving to "very disappointed."
    4.  **Re-survey with Incentive:** To reduce the unknown variable, offer a significant incentive to a random sample of the 1,700 non-respondents to get a statistically significant read on their sentiment. Do not assume their silence is golden.

**Growth Trajectory Implication**
If you continue adding features without raising the "very disappointed" metric above 40%, your growth trajectory over the next six months will likely be flat or declining due to churn. You are building a "nice to have," not a "must have." Fix the core loop until the metric moves, then scale.

***

*Note: This analysis relies solely on the survey numbers provided; it does not account for retention cohort data, which is the second strongest signal for PMF.*
