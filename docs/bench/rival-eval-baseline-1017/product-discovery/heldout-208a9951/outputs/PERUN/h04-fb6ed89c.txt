# Research Plan: LedgerLite Onboarding Friction Diagnosis

## 1. Recruitment Strategy: Prioritize "Immediate Drop-offs"
To test your hypothesis that length is the primary blocker, you must interview the **users who dropped off immediately** (e.g., within the first 2 steps).
*   **Reasoning:** Users who partially completed the flow have already demonstrated some tolerance for the length. The "zero completion" statistic is driven by those who quit before finishing. If the flow is too long, the signal will be strongest in the group that never started or quit instantly.
*   **Action:** Contact all 15 beta users. Prioritize scheduling the 6 interviews with those who triggered the onboarding but completed 0–20% of the steps. If you cannot fill 6 slots with immediate drop-offs, supplement with partial completers, but tag their data separately.

## 2. Interview Guide: The Mom Test Approach
Do not ask users if the tutorial was "too long" or "confusing." They will guess to be polite. Instead, reconstruct the specific timeline of their last attempt to uncover the **behavioral cause** of the drop-off.

**Opening Context (2 mins):**
*   "Walk me through the last time you opened LedgerLite after signing up. What was happening in your work/life right before you opened the app?"
*   "What specific task were you trying to accomplish in that session?"

**Reconstructing the Event (10 mins):**
*   "Take me back to the moment you saw the new tutorial steps. What was the very first thing you read or clicked?"
*   "At what exact point did you decide to stop or close the window? What was on the screen?"
*   "What did you do immediately after closing the app?" (This reveals if they sought an alternative solution or gave up entirely).
*   "Had you tried to set up your finance data before? How did you handle it last time?"

**Testing the Hypothesis Indirectly:**
*   *Instead of:* "Was it too long?"
*   *Ask:* "What were you expecting to see after step one? Did what you saw match that expectation?"
*   *Instead of:* "Did you find it boring?"
*   *Ask:* "What was going through your mind when you reached [Step X]?"

## 3. Synthesis & Success Metrics
*   **Friction Point Mapping:** Create a simple table listing the 6 users vs. the specific step where they stopped. Look for clustering. If 5/6 stopped at Step 2, the issue is likely content-specific, not just general length.
*   **Value Gap Identification:** Count how many users could not articulate *why* they were doing the steps. If they don't see the value proposition before Step 3, length is irrelevant; clarity is the killer.
*   **Decision Threshold:** If 4+ of the 6 interviews reveal users quitting because they "didn't understand why this mattered" or "got stuck on a specific input," your hypothesis shifts from "too lengthy" to "unclear value" or "technical friction."

## 4. Immediate Next Steps
1.  **Segment the list:** Tag the 15 users by completion % (0–20% vs. 21–90%).
2.  **Schedule:** Aim for 6 interviews with the 0–20% group by next Monday.
3.  **Record:** Ensure sessions are recorded/transcribed for exact quote extraction.

**Note:** Do not promise users you will "fix it" based on their feedback; promise only that you are listening to understand their workflow.
**Reminder:** If all 6 users say they "forgot" or "got busy," your problem may be engagement timing, not flow design.
