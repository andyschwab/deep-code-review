### Prioritized Assumptions for Validation

Based on your constraint of running only two experiments in three weeks and the need to maximize learning while minimizing risk, here is the prioritized list using an **Impact vs. Ease of Testing** framework.

#### 1. Top Priority: Manager Adoption Frequency (Value Risk)
*   **Assumption:** "Managers will check the predictive dashboard at least twice a week to make decisions."
*   **Rationale:** While data accuracy is a foundational requirement, it is a **feasibility** issue that your engineering team likely has high confidence in solving eventually. However, if managers do not find the predictions actionable or relevant enough to check regularly, the product has **zero value**, regardless of data precision. This is a **High Impact, High Risk** assumption because if false, the product fails entirely.
*   **Why Test First:** It addresses the core "Job to Be Done." If the frequency of use is low, perfect data accuracy is irrelevant. Validating this early prevents building a perfectly accurate tool that no one uses.
*   **Suggested Experiment (Low Effort/High Speed):** Conduct a "Concierge Test" or "Fake Door" test.
    *   *Action:* Manually generate 5 predictive insights for 3 pilot managers via email/Slack over one week. Ask them to make one decision based on it.
    *   *Metric:* Did they open the insight? Did they act on it? Did they ask for more?
    *   *Success Threshold:* At least 2 out of 3 managers act on the insight within 48 hours.

#### 2. Second Priority: Data Accuracy Threshold (Feasibility/Value Risk)
*   **Assumption:** "The model's prediction accuracy must be within 5% error margin for managers to trust it."
*   **Rationale:** You assumed 5% is the magic number, but this is unverified. Managers might tolerate 15% error if the trend direction is correct, or they might require 1% for specific high-stakes decisions. Testing the *specific threshold* rather than just "is data accurate" reduces the scope of the experiment to fit your timeline.
*   **Why Test Second:** Once you know they *will* use the tool (Assumption #1), you must ensure the quality meets their specific trust threshold. This is **High Impact** (trust) but slightly **Lower Risk** than adoption because you can iterate on model tuning if the threshold is missed, whereas you cannot easily force adoption if the need isn't there.
*   **Suggested Experiment (Moderate Effort):** "Wizard of Oz" Prototype Review.
    *   *Action:* Present two sample reports to the same pilot managers: one with 95% accuracy (labeled) and one with 85% accuracy. Ask which they would base a budget decision on and why.
    *   *Metric:* Qualitative feedback on trust levels and the specific error margin where trust breaks.
    *   *Success Threshold:* Clear consensus on the minimum acceptable accuracy level.

### Why Not Other Assumptions?
Assumptions regarding UI preferences, specific feature sets, or long-term retention are **Lower Impact** or **Lower Risk** at this stage. They rely on the first two assumptions being true. Validating them now would be premature optimization.

### Summary for Agile Coach
*   **Focus:** Shift from "Is the tech working?" to "Is the value real?"
*   **Strategy:** Validate the *need* (Adoption) before optimizing the *quality* (Accuracy threshold).
*   **Timeline:** Both experiments can be executed and analyzed within 10 days, leaving buffer time before the stakeholder presentation on the 20th.

*Note: Ensure the pilot managers selected for these tests are representative of your actual target persona to avoid skewed data.*
