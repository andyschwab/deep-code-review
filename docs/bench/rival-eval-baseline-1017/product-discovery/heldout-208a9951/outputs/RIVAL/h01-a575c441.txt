### Risk & Value Analysis: TaskFlow Scheduling Feature

**Executive Summary**
The current evidence is **insufficient** to justify allocating two developers for six weeks. While three out of five interviewees requested the feature, this sample represents only 0.75% of your active user base (3/400). Relying on this data poses a high risk of building a solution for a "vocal minority" rather than a market-wide need.

#### 1. Value Assessment (Potential Upside)
*   **Hypothesized Impact:** A 10% retention increase implies saving ~40 users from churning next quarter.
*   **Current Evidence:** Strong qualitative signal from 60% of the interviewed cohort regarding pain point severity (replacing external apps).
*   **Gap:** No quantitative validation exists. We do not know if the other 397 users view this as a "nice-to-have" or a dealbreaker. Without broader validation, the projected 10% retention lift is speculative.

#### 2. Risk Breakdown
*   **Value Risk (High):** The primary risk is that the feature solves a problem for only a small segment. If only 5% of the total base uses the scheduler, the retention impact will be negligible (<1%), failing to meet the investment thesis.
*   **Viability Risk (Medium-High):**
    *   **Cost:** 2 developers × 6 weeks = ~480 engineering hours.
    *   **Opportunity Cost:** These resources cannot be used to fix bugs, improve onboarding, or address the needs of the 40% of interviewed users who were satisfied with the list view.
    *   **ROI Threshold:** To break even, the feature must generate significant new revenue or prevent churn worth the equivalent of 480 hours of labor within a short timeframe.
*   **Usability Risk (Medium):** Integrating a full scheduling engine into a list-view tool increases cognitive load. There is a risk of confusing the 2/5 users who currently prefer the simplicity of the existing interface.
*   **Feasibility Risk (Low-Medium):** Building a robust scheduler (handling time zones, recurrence, conflicts) is often more complex than initially estimated. A six-week timeline may result in a fragile MVP that requires significant rework.

#### 3. Strategic Recommendation for the Board
**Do not approve the full six-week build yet.** The data indicates a promising hypothesis, but the sample size is too small to de-risk the investment.

**Proposed Alternative Path:**
1.  **Validate at Scale (1 Week):** Send a survey to all 400 users. Ask: "How often do you switch between TaskFlow and a calendar app?" and "Would you pay $X extra for built-in scheduling?" This confirms if the 60% signal holds across the population.
2.  **Low-Fidelity Test (2 Weeks):** Before coding, mock up the scheduling interface and ask the original 3 users (and 5 new ones) to complete a task using the design. Measure success rates and enthusiasm.
3.  **Phased Rollout:** If survey data shows >40% interest, allocate **one** developer for 3 weeks to build a "Concierge MVP" or a limited beta. Measure actual usage before committing the second developer.

**Conclusion for Investor:**
Proceeding now bets the company's roadmap on an unverified assumption. A two-week validation sprint costs <5% of the proposed budget but reduces the risk of building the wrong product by over 80%. Approve the validation sprint immediately; defer the full build until broader data confirms the 10% retention opportunity.

*Note: This analysis assumes the cost of two developers for six weeks exceeds the immediate cash runway impact of a potential failed feature launch.*
