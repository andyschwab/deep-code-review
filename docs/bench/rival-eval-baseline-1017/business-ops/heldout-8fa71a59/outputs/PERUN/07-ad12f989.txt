### Lane A: Unit Economics & Runway Analysis

**1. Current Direct-to-Consumer (DTC) Model**
*   **Price:** $180
*   **Cost:** $108
*   **Gross Margin per Unit:** $72 ($180 - $108)
*   **Margin %:** 40%

**2. Proposed MegaMart Model**
*   **Price:** $145
*   **Cost:** $108 (Assumed constant for this calculation)
*   **Gross Margin per Unit:** $37 ($145 - $108)
*   **Margin %:** ~25.5%

**3. Break-Even Volume Analysis (Profit Parity)**
To generate the same total gross profit as your current model, you must sell more units at the lower margin.
*   *Formula:* `Units_MegaMart = Units_DTC × (Margin_DTC ÷ Margin_MegaMart)`
*   *Ratio:* $72 ÷ $37 ≈ 1.95
*   *Result:* You must sell **1.95 units** to MegaMart to equal the profit of **1 unit** sold DTC.

If your current monthly DTC volume is $V$, the MegaMart deal requires a monthly volume of $1.95 \times V$ just to maintain the same gross profit dollar amount, ignoring any changes in operating expenses (e.g., logistics, support).

**4. Cash Runway Impact**
*   **Current Cash:** $300,000
*   **Monthly Burn:** $45,000
*   **Current Runway:** ~6.7 months ($300k ÷ $45k)

*Scenario A: Reject Deal (Status Quo)*
Runway remains ~6.7 months assuming current sales cover part of the burn or burn is net of current revenue. If the $45k burn is *net* burn, you have 6.7 months. If $45k is *gross* burn and you are currently selling $X$ units, your net burn is lower.

*Scenario B: Accept Deal (Initial Order)*
*   **One-time Cash Inflow:** 5,000 units × $145 = $725,000 (Revenue)
*   **One-time Cash Outflow (COGS):** 5,000 units × $108 = $540,000 (Assuming you pay this upfront or shortly after)
*   **Gross Profit Injection:** $185,000
*   **Runway Extension:** If this profit is realized immediately and monthly burn stays at $45k, this single order adds ~4.1 months of runway ($185k ÷ $45k), extending total runway to ~10.8 months.

**Decision Aid & Thresholds**
*   **The Trade-off:** You trade margin percentage for cash velocity and volume. The deal makes financial sense *if* the immediate cash injection ($185k gross profit) is more valuable to your survival than the lost margin per unit, AND if you can fulfill the order without increasing your monthly burn rate (e.g., hiring staff, warehousing).
*   **Critical Input:** Does the $108 cost hold at 25,000 units? If volume allows you to negotiate component costs down to $90, the MegaMart margin rises to $55/unit, drastically changing the parity ratio to 1.3x.
*   **Rule of Thumb:** If LTV:CAC or margin compression forces payback periods >12 months, reconsider. Here, the "payback" is immediate cash, but the long-term brand value of discounting must be weighed.

**Next Step:** Calculate your *fully loaded* cost per unit including shipping to MegaMart and retailer-specific fees (slotting fees, returns). If these add >$5/unit, the margin drops to $32, requiring 2.25x volume to match DTC profit.

**Note:** This analysis assumes the $108 cost is variable and constant; verify if fixed costs are allocated correctly.
**Note:** Retailer contracts often include hidden fees; ensure the $145 is net of all deductions before calculating margin.
