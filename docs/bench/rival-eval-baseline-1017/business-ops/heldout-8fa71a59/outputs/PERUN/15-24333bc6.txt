### Lane A: Unit Economics & Opportunity Cost Analysis

To evaluate the true net value of the grant, we must apply your specific inputs to an opportunity cost framework. This arithmetic isolates the "hidden" labor costs you identified.

**1. Direct Labor Cost of Compliance**
*Formula:* `Weekly Hours × Blended Rate × Weeks in Quarter × Quarters`
*Assumptions:* The pilot lasts 12 months (4 quarters). Your billing rate ($200/hr) is used as the proxy for opportunity cost (the value of that time if spent on revenue-generating work).
*Inputs:*
- CTO: 15 hrs/week
- Ops Lead: 10 hrs/week
- Total: 25 hrs/week
- Rate: $200/hr
- Duration: 52 weeks (1 year)

*Calculation:*
25 hours × $200 × 52 weeks = **$260,000** in implied labor cost.

**2. Net Value Calculation**
*Formula:* `Grant Amount − (Direct Labor Cost + Estimated Professional Fees)`
*Inputs:*
- Grant: $60,000
- Labor Cost: $260,000
- Professional Fees (Legal/Audit): Unknown (Variable X)

*Result:*
$60,000 − $260,000 − X = **−$200,000 (minus X)**

**Sensitivity & Reality Check**
Even if you value internal time at 50% of your billing rate ($100/hr), the cost is $130,000, resulting in a net loss of $70,000. For this grant to break even on labor alone, your effective hourly value would need to be below **$44/hour** ($60,000 ÷ 1,300 total hours).

**3. Runway Impact (Lane A Context)**
*Formula:* `Cash Reserves ÷ (Monthly Burn − Grant Inflow)`
*Context:* You have $50,000 cash.
- If you accept: You gain $60,000 (likely staggered), but burn increases due to 100+ hours/month of non-revenue work delaying commercial sales.
- If you reject: You preserve 1,300 hours of high-value engineering/ops time to secure paid pilots.
