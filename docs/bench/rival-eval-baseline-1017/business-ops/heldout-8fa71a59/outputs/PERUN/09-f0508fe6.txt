### Lane A: Unit Economics & Pricing Structure Analysis

Your situation describes a classic misalignment between your **value metric** (API calls) and your **packaging model** (uncapped base plan). The "bill shock" indicates that your current $99 plan effectively sells unlimited usage for a fixed price until the user hits an implicit, painful overage threshold.

Here is the arithmetic framework to model the impact of capping usage versus introducing hard tiers ($299/$499).

#### 1. The Baseline Variables (Your Inputs)
*   **Total Subscribers:** 2,000
*   **High-Usage Segment:** 10% (200 users)
*   **Low/Mid-Usage Segment:** 90% (1,800 users)
*   **Current ARPU:** $145
*   **Current Total MRR:** $290,000 (2,000 × $145)
*   **High-Usage Churn:** 15% (vs. unknown baseline for others)
*   **High-Usage Avg Revenue:** ~$800 (implied by "often end up paying")

#### 2. The Modeling Formulas

**A. Current State Revenue from High-Usage Users**
$$Revenue_{current} = Users_{high} \times ARPU_{high}$$
$$Revenue_{current} = 200 \times \$800 = \$160,000$$
*(Note: This implies your low/mid users average ~$72 to reach the global $145 ARPU.)*

**B. Proposed Tiered State Revenue**
If you introduce hard caps at $299 and $499, you must estimate the **migration distribution**. Let:
*   $U_{299}$ = % of high users migrating to the $299 tier.
*   $U_{499}$ = % of high users migrating to the $499 tier.
*   $U_{churn}$ = % of high users who churn because the cap is too low.

$$Revenue_{new} = (200 \times U_{299} \times \$299) + (200 \times U_{499} \times \$499)$$

**C. The Trade-off Equation (Net Impact)**
$$\Delta Revenue = Revenue_{new} - Revenue_{current}$$
$$\Delta Retention = (Churn_{current} - Churn_{new}) \times LTV_{avg}$$

#### 3. Sensitivity Analysis (Apply Your Assumptions)

You cannot know the outcome without estimating how your 200 power users distribute across the new caps. Consider these three scenarios:

*   **Scenario A (Downgrade Heavy):** 80% move to $299, 10% to $499, 10% churn.
    *   New Revenue: $(160 \times 299) + (20 \times 499) = \$47,840 + \$9,980 = \$57,820$.
    *   **Impact:** Massive revenue loss ($160k → $58k) from this segment. Likely unsustainable unless volume increases drastically.

*   **Scenario B (Balanced):** 40% move to $299, 40% to $499, 20% churn.
    *   New Revenue: $(80 \times 299) + (80 \times 499) = \$23,920 + \$39,920 = \$63,840$.
    *   **Impact:** Still significant revenue compression.

*   **Scenario C (Value Realization):** Your current $800 users are outliers. Perhaps the "cap" forces a conversation where you sell them an **Enterprise Tier** at $1,200+ with higher limits, while the $299/$499 tiers catch the "bill shock" users who were previously paying $800 unexpectedly.
    *   If you repackage the $800 users into a dedicated "Pro/Unlimited" tier at $899, and move the rest to $299/$499, you stabilize revenue without capping the whales.

#### 4. Decision Aid & Next Steps

*   **The Flip Input:** The variable that decides this is **Willingness-to-Pay (WTP) Elasticity**. If users paying $800 are doing so reluctantly (due to shock), they may leave anyway. If they value the API calls at >$800, they will accept a higher fixed tier.
*   **Rule of Thumb:** If **LTV:CAC < 3** or **Payback > 12 months** due to churn, pricing stability is more valuable than short-term revenue spikes. High churn (15%) suggests your current model is destroying LTV.
*   **Recommended Method:** Do not guess the distribution. Run a **Van Westendorp Price Sensitivity Survey** with your 200 high-usage accounts before changing the billing page. Ask:
    1.  At what price is this too expensive?
    2.  At what price is it a bargain?
    3.  At what price is it getting expensive but you'd still consider it?
    4.  At what price is it so cheap you question the quality?
    *This reveals the acceptable price band for a "capped" plan.*

**Immediate Action:** Segment your 200 high-usage users. Calculate their exact average API call volume. Set your $299 and $499 caps slightly *below* the usage level of the 50th and 80th percentile of this group, respectively. Create a fourth "Enterprise" tier for anyone above the $499 cap to prevent revenue leakage.

**Note:** This analysis applies arithmetic to your provided numbers; the final pricing decision and contract terms are yours to set. If these changes involve modifying existing customer contracts, consult legal counsel regarding notice periods and terms of service updates.
