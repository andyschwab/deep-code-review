### Lane A: Arithmetic and Strategy Application

**Current State Baseline**
*   **Revenue:** $40,000/mo ($20k × 2 clients).
*   **Hours:** 260/mo (130 × 2).
*   **Effective Rate:** ~$154/hr (matches your $150 target).
*   **Current Net Profit:** $7,200/mo ($40k × 18%).
*   **Target Revenue:** $48,000/mo (20% increase).
*   **Target New Price Per Client:** $24,000/mo.

**Value-Based Pricing Structure**
To move from hourly to value-based without increasing headcount, you must decouple price from time and anchor it to the client's economic gain.

1.  **The Formula:** `Price = (Client Value Generated × Capture Rate)`.
    *   *Application:* If TechCorp generates $500k in sales from your work, a $24k fee is a 4.8% capture rate. If the value is only $100k, a $24k fee (24% capture) may feel unfair.
    *   *Action:* You must calculate the specific revenue lift or cost savings each client attributes to your work. Without this input, the "value" number is unknown.

2.  **Packaging Models (Apply, Don't Direct)**
    *   **Flat Fee (Retainer):** Set at $24k/mo.
        *   *Risk:* If results fluctuate downward, clients perceive low ROI and may churn. You bear the performance risk.
        *   *Mitigation:* Define a strict "Scope of Value" (e.g., "We manage channels X, Y, Z") rather than guaranteeing a specific revenue number.
    *   **Performance/Hybrid Model:** Base Fee + % of Upside.
        *   *Structure:* Lower base (e.g., $18k) + 10-15% of revenue growth above a baseline.
        *   *Risk:* Revenue fluctuation makes your income volatile.
        *   *Mitigation:* Set a "floor" (minimum fee) equal to your cost-plus margin to protect the 18% net profit.

**Decision Aid & Thresholds**
*   **Read:** To justify $24k/mo, the client must perceive at least $100k–$240k in monthly value (assuming a 10–25% fair capture rate).
*   **Flip Input:** The break-even value perception is `New Fee ÷ Acceptable Capture %`. If the client only perceives $50k in value, they will reject a $24k fee.
*   **Rule of Thumb:** If the client cannot quantify the revenue impact of your work, value-based pricing will fail; stick to hourly or cost-plus until attribution data exists.
*   **Next Step:** Before negotiating, instrument tracking to establish a "baseline" performance metric for the last 6 months. Present this data to show: "Current baseline is X; our proposed strategy targets Y. The fee is tied to closing that gap."

**Negotiation Handling**
If they resist leaving hourly billing:
*   Do not defend the rate; defend the *outcome*.
*   Offer a transition clause: "We can run a 3-month pilot at the new flat fee with a 'look-back' provision. If the effective hourly rate exceeds $200 based on actual hours worked, we credit the difference." This caps their risk while setting a higher anchor.

### Lane R: Regulatory & Contractual Routing

**Regime:** Contract Law and Consumer Protection / Advertising Standards.

**Route To:** Licensed Attorney.

**Reasoning:**
*   **Performance Clauses:** Drafting "performance models" involves defining legal obligations, liability caps, and what constitutes "results" (e.g., leads vs. closed sales). Ambiguity here leads to breach of contract disputes.
*   **Advertising Claims:** Guaranteeing specific performance results in marketing contracts can trigger regulatory scrutiny regarding false advertising or unsubstantiated claims depending on your jurisdiction.
*   **Contract Restructuring:** Terminating or modifying existing hourly agreements to new terms requires specific notice periods and consideration to avoid legal friction.

**Preparation for Counsel:** Bring your current Master Services Agreements (MSAs), the proposed "value" metrics you intend to track, and draft language for the "floor" and "cap" in any performance fee structure.
