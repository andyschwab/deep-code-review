**1. Entity Type (LLC vs. S-Corp)**
*   **Regime:** Internal Revenue Code (Subchapter S) and California Secretary of State rules.
*   **Key Factors:** The primary trade-off is often between administrative simplicity (LLC) and potential self-employment tax savings (S-Corp). In an S-Corp, you pay yourself a "reasonable salary" (subject to payroll taxes) and take remaining profits as distributions (not subject to self-employment tax). However, California imposes an additional 1.5% franchise tax on S-Corps (with a minimum fee), which can negate federal savings for some businesses.
*   **Action:** A CPA must run a "reasonable compensation" study and model the net tax impact of the S-Corp election versus the default LLC taxation given your projected $220,000 revenue and $100,000 in new wages.

**2. Jurisdiction and Nexus**
*   **Regime:** State corporate law and "nexus" rules for income/franchise tax.
*   **Key Factors:** Since you are based in California and managing the business from there, California will likely consider you "domestic" regardless of where you incorporate. Forming in Delaware or Wyoming while operating in California usually requires you to register as a "foreign entity" in California anyway, resulting in double filing fees and ongoing compliance in both states.
*   **Remote Team:** Having employees in New York and Texas creates "nexus" in those states, potentially requiring you to register to do business there and withhold state income taxes for those specific employees.
*   **Action:** An attorney should confirm if a California LLC is sufficient for your liability goals ($30k contracts) or if a different structure is warranted. Do not assume an out-of-state incorporation avoids California taxes.

**3. Liability Protection**
*   **Regime:** State limited liability laws.
*   **Key Factors:** Both LLCs and Corporations offer limited liability, protecting personal assets from business debts *if* corporate formalities are maintained (separate bank accounts, documented meetings, no commingling of funds). The protection level is generally similar for a small creative agency; the difference lies in governance and tax treatment.
