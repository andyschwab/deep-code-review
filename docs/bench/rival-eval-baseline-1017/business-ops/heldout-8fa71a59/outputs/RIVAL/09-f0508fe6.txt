### Framework: Usage Cap Impact Model

To evaluate the trade-off between revenue stability and potential loss from capping power users, build a spreadsheet model using the following four-step framework. This will quantify the impact on Monthly Recurring Revenue (MRR) and Customer Lifetime Value (LTV) before you change your billing page.

#### 1. Segment Your Current Base
Break your 2,000 subscribers into three distinct cohorts based on current spend:
*   **Standard Users:** Paying <$299/mo.
*   **At-Risk Power Users:** Currently paying $300–$799/mo (those who would hit a cap).
*   **Extreme Power Users:** Currently paying >$800/mo.

*Action:* Export the last 3 months of usage data. Map every user to what their bill *would have been* under your proposed caps (e.g., $299 and $499 hard limits).

#### 2. Calculate the "Cap Gap" vs. "Churn Savings"
For the **At-Risk** and **Extreme** cohorts, calculate two opposing forces:

*   **Revenue Compression (The Cost):**
    Sum the difference between what these users *currently pay* and what they *would pay* under the cap.
    *(Current Bill - Capped Price) × Number of Users = Monthly Revenue Loss*

*   **Churn Reduction (The Gain):**
    Apply your current 15% churn rate to the high-usage cohort. Estimate a reduced churn rate (e.g., 5%) if bill shock is eliminated.
    *(Current Churn % - Projected Churn %) × Cohort Size × Average Capped Price = Monthly Revenue Saved from Retention*

*   **Net Impact:** Subtract Revenue Compression from Churn Reduction. If positive, the cap increases net MRR.

#### 3. Model LTV Sensitivity
High churn destroys LTV disproportionately. Recalculate LTV for the power user segment under both scenarios:
*   **Scenario A (Status Quo):** High ARPU ($800+) but short lifespan due to 15% monthly churn.
*   **Scenario B (Capped):** Lower ARPU ($299/$499) but extended lifespan due to predictable pricing.

*Formula:* `LTV = (Average Monthly Price) / (Monthly Churn Rate)`
Compare the total cohort LTV. Often, stabilizing a volatile customer yields higher long-term value than extracting maximum short-term revenue.

#### 4. Design the "Safety Valve" Tier
Instead of a hard stop that frustrates users, model a **Tiered Overage** structure to capture upside without shock:
*   **Base Tier:** Includes X API calls for $299.
*   **Overage Buffer:** Auto-purchase blocks of 10k calls at a premium rate once the limit is reached, *but* send an immediate email notification before charging.
*   **Hard Ceiling:** A strict limit only after 20% overage, forcing an upgrade conversation rather than a surprise bill.

**Recommendation:** Do not implement a hard cap immediately. Test a "Soft Cap" with email alerts on a small segment of your power users first. Measure if their usage self-regulates or if they proactively upgrade. This validates your pricing elasticity without risking the entire 10% high-value segment.

***

*Note: Ensure your billing system can handle real-time usage alerts to prevent the very "bill shock" you are trying to solve.*
*Consider offering an annual pre-pay option for heavy users to lock in their rate and stabilize your forecasting.*
