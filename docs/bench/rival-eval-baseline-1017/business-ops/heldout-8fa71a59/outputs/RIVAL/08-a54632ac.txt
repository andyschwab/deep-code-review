### Key Factors for Incorporation Type and Location

**1. LLC vs. S-Corp Selection**
*   **LLC (Limited Liability Company):** Provides the personal liability protection you need for $30k contracts immediately. It is flexible and easier to maintain. As a single-member LLC, you are currently taxed as a sole proprietorship (Schedule C), meaning you pay self-employment tax on all net income.
*   **S-Corp Election:** This is a tax status, not a business entity. You form an LLC and *elect* to be taxed as an S-Corp.
    *   **The Threshold:** With $220k revenue and $100k in new salaries, your net profit may justify an S-Corp. S-Corps allow you to split income into "salary" (subject to payroll tax) and "distributions" (not subject to self-employment tax).
    *   **Rule of Thumb:** Many advisors suggest considering S-Corp election when net profits exceed $80,000–$100,000 annually, as the tax savings often outweigh the increased administrative costs (payroll processing, separate tax returns). Given your projected growth and hiring, an LLC taxed as an S-Corp is a common path for agencies at your scale.

**2. Jurisdiction (Where to Incorporate)**
*   **Home State Rule:** Since you are based in California and operating there, you should almost certainly incorporate in **California**.
*   **The Delaware/Nevada Myth:** While Delaware is popular for venture-backed startups planning to raise capital or go public, it offers little benefit for a profitable, bootstrapped service agency. If you incorporate in Delaware but operate in California, you will still need to register as a "foreign entity" in California and pay California franchise taxes and fees anyway. This creates double the paperwork and double the fees.
*   **Remote Team:** The location of your remote employees (NY, TX) does not dictate where *you* incorporate. It does, however, require you to register for payroll taxes in those specific states where your employees reside.

**3. Liability Protection**
*   Both LLCs and S-Corps provide a "corporate veil" protecting personal assets from business liabilities.
*   To maintain this protection, you must keep business and personal finances strictly separate (separate bank accounts, credit cards, and formal record-keeping).
