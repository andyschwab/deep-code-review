**Recommendation: Renew the self-hosted contract for one year with a parallel, non-binding migration pilot.**

Do not migrate by the November 30th deadline. The financial risk of the migration unknowns outweighs the projected operational gains at this specific juncture. While the managed service offers a theoretical reduction in incident response time (4 hours to <30 minutes), the 40% cost increase ($50,400 annually) cannot be justified without a verified migration plan. The "unknown variable" of moving 12 terabytes of data without downtime is a critical failure point; attempting this within a two-week window without vendor quantification creates a high probability of budget overruns or service interruption.

By renewing the current contract, you secure system stability for the upcoming fiscal year while purchasing the time necessary to de-risk the migration. Use the next 6–9 months to run a controlled pilot: replicate a subset of data to the managed service, validate the true migration duration, and measure actual incident response improvements in a staging environment. This approach converts the "unknown" into a verified metric before committing to the permanent 40% spend increase.

**Stress Test of the Two-Week Migration Window:**
The assumption that 12 TB can be migrated in two weeks is fragile. At a conservative sustained transfer rate of 50 MB/s (common in cross-region or constrained network environments), moving 12 TB requires approximately 2.7 days of pure transfer time, excluding validation, schema conversion, and cutover rehearsals. However, if network throughput drops to 10 MB/s due to throttling or latency, the transfer alone extends to nearly 14 days, leaving zero margin for error, testing, or rollback. Without vendor-confirmed bandwidth guarantees and a dry-run result, the two-week window is statistically insufficient and poses an unacceptable risk to the deadline.

**Note:** This recommendation prioritizes avoiding a catastrophic, unquantified migration failure over immediate, theoretical efficiency gains.
