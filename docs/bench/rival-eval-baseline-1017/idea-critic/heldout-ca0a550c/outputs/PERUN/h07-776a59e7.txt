**Recommendation: Proceed with a "Go-If" Sunset Plan, Not an Immediate Cut.**

Do not execute a hard sunset immediately. Instead, adopt a **Go-If** strategy: Commit to sunsetting the legacy line by year-end *only if* a 6-week pilot migration program (starting immediately) demonstrates that at least 45% of users convert to the new platform without generating a net-negative sentiment spike. If the pilot fails to hit 45% conversion or triggers significant brand backlash, pause the sunset and transition to a "maintenance-only" mode (zero new features, minimal support) to stabilize revenue while protecting the core brand.

**Stress Test of the 60% Migration Assumption:**
Your assumption that 60% of users will migrate is likely **overly optimistic and unsupported**. Critical workflows imply high switching costs and risk aversion; users reliant on legacy stability often view forced migration as a threat, not an opportunity. Historical data in B2B sunsets typically shows conversion rates between 30–40% unless the legacy product is actively broken or unsupported. A discounted package may attract price-sensitive users but often fails to convince those who prioritize workflow continuity over cost. Without verified data on *why* these 3,000 users stay (e.g., specific features missing in the new platform), betting on 60% conversion risks underestimating churn and the associated reputational damage.
