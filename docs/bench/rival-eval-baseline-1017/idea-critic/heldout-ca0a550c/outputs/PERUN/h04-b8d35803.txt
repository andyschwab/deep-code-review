**Recommendation: Build In-House (Go-No-Go contingent on a 5-day code audit)**

**Decision Logic**
While acquisition offers immediate market presence, your critical assumption—that integration will take less than three weeks—is an unverified high-risk variable. If the DataPeek architecture is incompatible with your stack, the "three-week" timeline could balloon into months of refactoring, missing your October 10th board deadline and burning the $600k capital.

Building in-house costs $150k and takes three months. This path has higher initial time-to-market but **zero integration risk** and full architectural control. It guarantees delivery by January, whereas the acquisition path has a binary outcome: success in one month or failure indefinitely.

**Stress Test: The Integration Assumption**
Your assumption that "integrating DataPeek's code will be straightforward" is the single point of failure. Without seeing their codebase, you are betting $600k on the hope that their tech stack, coding standards, and data models align with yours.
*   **Worst Case:** Their reporting module relies on deprecated libraries or a database schema that requires a complete rewrite. You spend $600k + 6 months of engineering time to rebuild what you could have built for $150k.
*   **Customer Risk:** The 200 users may churn immediately if forced to migrate to your higher pricing tier, rendering the primary asset (the customer list) worthless.

Do not present a final "Buy" or "Build" decision yet. Instead, present a **conditional strategy**:
1.  **Immediate Due Diligence (5 Days):** Request read-only access to DataPeek's repository. Have your engineering lead perform a rapid architecture audit specifically looking for stack mismatches and technical debt.
2.  **Decision Matrix:**
    *   **IF** the audit confirms high compatibility AND you secure a clause allowing user migration testing: **BUY**.
    *   **IF** the audit reveals significant refactoring needs OR data migration risks: **BUILD**.

**Leading Metric & Kill Criteria**
*   **Metric:** Engineering hours required to compile and run DataPeek's module in your staging environment.
*   **Kill Criterion:** If the engineering lead estimates more than 40 hours of refactoring to achieve a basic "hello world" integration, abort the acquisition immediately and switch to the build plan.
