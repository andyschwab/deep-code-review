**Recommendation: Build In-House**

Despite the allure of immediate market entry, the financial and technical risk profile heavily favors building the feature internally. The acquisition asks for a 4x premium ($600k vs. $150k) based on two unverified assets: code quality and customer conversion. Your assumption that integration will take "less than three weeks" is the single point of failure in the buy scenario and requires immediate stress testing before any capital is deployed.

**Financial Reality Check**
*   **Buy Scenario:** $600,000 upfront. If the 200 users churn due to price hikes or integration friction, your effective Customer Acquisition Cost (CAC) is $3,000 per user, which is likely unsustainable for SMB software. You are also inheriting unknown technical debt.
*   **Build Scenario:** $150,000 sunk cost over three months. You own the IP, the architecture matches your existing stack, and there are no integration surprises. Even if development slips to four months, the total cost remains under $200,000—still $400,000 cheaper than the acquisition.

**Stress Testing the "Easy Integration" Assumption**
Your engineering lead's three-week integration estimate is dangerously optimistic and likely ignores the "hidden factory" of software mergers. Consider these high-probability failure modes:
1.  **Architecture Mismatch:** DataPeek's reporting module may rely on different database schemas, authentication protocols, or library versions. Reconciling these often requires rewriting the module, not just copying it.
2.  **The "Black Box" Problem:** Without deep familiarity, your team will spend weeks simply reading and understanding their code before making a single change. Debugging a stranger's code is consistently 2x–3x slower than writing new code.
3.  **Data Migration Friction:** Merging 200 user accounts, billing histories, and data permissions into your system without downtime or data loss is rarely a "copy-paste" job. One schema conflict can halt the entire rollout.

If integration drags to 8–10 weeks (a common reality for "simple" merges), you lose the speed advantage entirely while still paying the $600k premium.

**Strategic Verdict**
Acquire only if due diligence proves the code is pristine *and* the customers are locked in. Without that proof, you are gambling $450,000 on hope. Building guarantees a clean architecture, aligns with your team's expertise, and preserves capital for actual growth rather than debt repayment.

1.  **Commission a Technical Audit:** Before the meeting, demand access to DataPeek's repository. Have your lead engineer spend 48 hours reviewing their code structure. If they find spaghetti code or incompatible stacks, the "buy" option is dead.
