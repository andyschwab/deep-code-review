**Recommendation: Proceed with Open-Sourcing, but with a "Core + Enterprise" Strategy**

Release the library publicly under a permissive license (e.g., Apache 2.0) to capture the employer branding and community acceleration benefits, but retain a proprietary "Enterprise Edition" or managed service layer that holds your true competitive moat. Do not keep the entire tool proprietary; the risk of stagnation and missed talent acquisition outweighs the current 30% speed advantage, which is likely erodable by competitors internally regardless of your open-source status.

**Stress Test of Your Assumption: Community Contributions vs. Competitor Forking**

Your assumption that community contributions will accelerate development by 20% is **plausible but conditional**, while the fear of a competitor undercutting you via a fork is **overstated in the short term but valid as a long-term strategic threat**.

1.  **The Contribution Reality Check:**
    *   **Optimistic View:** Open-sourcing attracts external engineers who fix edge cases, add integrations, and improve documentation. This frees your internal team to focus on high-value architecture, potentially exceeding your 20% acceleration target.
    *   **Risk:** Most open-source projects receive negligible code contributions. The real value often comes from *adoption* (setting the standard) rather than *code*. If your library is too niche or tightly coupled to Prism Tech's internal infrastructure, external contributions may be near zero. You must decouple internal dependencies to make it viable for outsiders.
    *   **Verdict:** The 20% acceleration is achievable only if you invest significantly in community management and documentation. It is not automatic.

2.  **The Competitor Fork Risk:**
    *   **The Threat:** A larger competitor *can* fork your project. However, copying code is easy; maintaining parity with the original creators is hard. If you remain the primary committers, you dictate the roadmap.
    *   **The Moat:** Speed alone is a fragile moat. If a competitor can undercut you simply by using your own tool, your advantage was never the code—it was your execution speed. By open-sourcing, you force competitors to compete on *service, integration, and proprietary data*, not just raw processing speed.
    *   **Mitigation:** Use a "Open Core" model. Release the core engine (the 30% booster) but keep advanced monitoring, proprietary connectors, or managed cloud hosting as paid/proprietary features. This ensures that even if they fork the core, they cannot replicate your full service offering.

**Strategic Advice for the August 5th Session:**

*   **Frame the Narrative:** Position this not as "giving away secret sauce" but as "setting the industry standard." If Prism Tech's library becomes the default, you win the talent war and influence the ecosystem.
*   **Define the Moat:** Explicitly identify what remains proprietary. If the 30% speed boost is entirely in the open-source core, ensure your service layer relies on proprietary data or infrastructure that cannot be forked.
*   **Timeline:** Aim for a Q4 release to allow time for decoupling internal dependencies and preparing the community infrastructure (docs, contribution guidelines, license).

**Note:** Ensure your legal team reviews the code for any accidental inclusion of third-party proprietary code before release. The "Enterprise Edition" strategy protects revenue while the open core drives brand and recruitment.
