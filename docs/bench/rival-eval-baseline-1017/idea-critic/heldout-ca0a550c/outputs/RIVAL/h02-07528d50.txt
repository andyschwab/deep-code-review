**Recommendation: Apply the 15% increase to new customers only; grandfather existing contracts for this cycle.**

This approach maximizes net revenue over the next twelve months while protecting the brand trust required for future upsells. Your assumption of ≤5% churn among existing customers is highly fragile and likely incorrect. A blanket hike on renewals treats your most loyal users as price-inelastic commodities, ignoring the "loyalty penalty" perception that drives disproportionate churn in enterprise software.

**Revenue Stress Test & Elasticity Analysis**
Your current model assumes a linear trade-off: Price Up vs. Volume Down. However, enterprise churn is rarely linear; it is threshold-based. If you apply the 15% hike to all 450 customers:
*   **Best Case (Your Assumption):** You lose 5% (22 customers). You retain 95% of the base at a higher rate. Net revenue increases significantly.
*   **Likely Case (Elasticity Reality):** Enterprise buyers operate on approved budgets. A sudden 15% line-item increase often requires re-approval from finance committees who are actively looking to cut costs. If 12% of customers (54 accounts) churn or downgrade to zero due to budget friction, you lose the entire revenue gain from the price hike.
*   **Worst Case (Reputational Cascade):** If key industry players churn and share their reasoning ("Northwind punished loyalty"), your Customer Acquisition Cost (CAC) for new business rises as trust erodes. Sales cycles lengthen as legal teams insert price-cap clauses.

By restricting the hike to new customers, you secure immediate margin expansion on all new logos without triggering a renewal crisis. You preserve the 450 existing accounts as a stable revenue floor, allowing you to monetize them later through feature-based upsells (value-add) rather than punitive rate hikes (tax).

**Strategic Rationale**
1.  **Brand Trust:** Grandfathering signals that you value long-term partnerships. It turns your existing base into advocates rather than adversaries during renewal season.
2.  **Sales Leverage:** Your sales team can use the "locked-in rate" as a closing tool for expansions within existing accounts, whereas a forced hike makes every conversation adversarial.
3.  **Risk Mitigation:** You avoid the binary risk of a mass exodus. If infrastructure costs are critical, you can target the top 10% of heavy users with a specific "infrastructure surcharge" tied to usage metrics, which is perceived as fairer than a blanket tax.

**Immediate Action Plan**
*   **Announce today:** New pricing effective immediately for all new signatures.
*   **Communicate to existing base:** Reaffirm current rates for the upcoming renewal to stabilize sentiment.
*   **Prepare Q3 Strategy:** Develop a value-based tier increase (new features) for the next cycle to capture willingness-to-pay without triggering price-only churn.

Do not gamble the company's stable recurring revenue on an unverified elasticity assumption. Secure the base, grow the top line with new business, and earn the price increase through delivered value next year.

*Note: This analysis assumes standard B2B SaaS elasticity curves; if your product is a regulated monopoly for these clients, the risk profile shifts slightly but the reputational damage remains.*
