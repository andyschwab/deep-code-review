4.  **Segment the Remainder:** For users who refuse to migrate, calculate the cost of maintaining a "stripped-down" legacy version (security patches only, no new features) versus the cost of acquiring new customers to replace the lost revenue if they churn and speak negatively.

**Stress Test: The 60% Migration Assumption**
Your assumption that 60% of users will switch is fragile for three reasons:
*   **Workflow Inertia:** Users relying on "critical workflows" face high switching costs. Even with a discount, the risk of disrupting their operations often outweighs price incentives. They will stay put rather than risk failure on a new platform.
*   **Feature Parity Gaps:** Legacy users often depend on specific, deprecated features. If the new platform lacks exact functional equivalents, migration is technically impossible for them, regardless of price.
*   **Change Fatigue:** A loyal base of 3,000 has likely endured previous updates. If they perceive this as a forced obsolescence rather than an upgrade, resistance will harden.

**Financial Reality Check:**
*   **Current Burn:** $60,000/quarter ($240,000/year).
*   **Risk Scenario:** If you sunset now and 50% of users churn (1,500 users) and just 10% of those (150 users) become vocal detractors affecting your new platform's sales, the reputational cost could easily exceed $500,000 in lost new business opportunities.
*   **Mitigation Cost:** Maintaining the legacy line for 6 more months costs $120,000. This is an insurance premium against brand damage and provides the time needed to genuinely convert users or build a bridge product.
