**Stress Test: The 15ms Latency Assumption**
Your assumption that a 15ms increase is "negligible" is the highest-risk variable in this equation and likely flawed for real-time applications.
*   **Compounding Effect:** In microservices architectures, a single user request often triggers 10-50 internal service calls. A 15ms increase per hop compounds rapidly, potentially adding 200-750ms to end-user perceived latency, which can destroy conversion rates and user experience.
*   **Tail Latency:** Averages hide outliers. If the new provider has higher variance (jitter), your 99th percentile latency could spike by 100ms+, causing timeouts and circuit breakers to trip, leading to cascading failures.
*   **Database Consistency:** For distributed databases, increased latency directly impacts write throughput and consistency guarantees, potentially forcing a downgrade in data durability or availability settings.

The net first-year benefit may be closer to $100k, which may not justify the operational risk of migrating core infrastructure without a validated success path.
