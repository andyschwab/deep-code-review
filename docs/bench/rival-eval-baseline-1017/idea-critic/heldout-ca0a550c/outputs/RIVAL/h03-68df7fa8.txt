**Stress Test: Two-Week Maintenance Window Assumption**

The assumption that 12TB can be migrated within a two-week window is **high-risk and likely optimistic** without specific architectural validation. Consider the following constraints:

1.  **Throughput Reality:** To move 12TB in 14 days, you need a sustained throughput of roughly 10 MB/s. While this seems low, database migration is not a simple file copy. It involves logical replication, schema validation, index rebuilding, and data integrity checks. If your current network egress is throttled or if the target service imposes ingestion limits during the cutover phase, this timeline will slip.
2.  **The "Last Mile" Problem:** The two-week window likely assumes a "lift and shift" followed by a brief cutover. In reality, you must run dual-write or bi-directional replication to minimize downtime. Setting up, testing, and validating this replication layer often consumes 60-70% of the total project time. If the vendor has not quantified the complexity of setting up this replication for your specific schema and write volume, the two-week estimate is a guess, not a plan.
3.  **Rollback Complexity:** A safe migration requires a verified rollback path. If the cutover fails at hour 13 of your maintenance window, can you revert to the self-hosted instance without data loss? Testing this rollback scenario alone could consume several days of your window.
