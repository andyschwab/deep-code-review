**Recommendation: Apply the 15% increase to new customers only; grandfather existing contracts for this cycle.**

This approach maximizes net revenue over the next twelve months while preserving the brand trust required for future upsells. Your assumption of ≤5% churn among existing customers is highly fragile and likely incorrect. A blanket 15% hike on renewals risks triggering a "negotiation cascade" where even satisfied customers feel compelled to contest the increase, driving effective churn (downgrades + cancellations) well beyond your 5% tolerance.

**Revenue Stress Test (12-Month Projection)**

*Baseline:* 450 customers. Let $X$ be the current average contract value. Total baseline revenue = $450X$.

**Scenario A: 15% Hike on Everyone (The Risky Path)**
*   **Assumption:** You lose 5% of customers (22.5 clients) immediately.
*   **Reality Check:** In enterprise software, a double-digit price hike on renewals often triggers a formal RFP process. Even if they don't leave, 20–30% may demand concessions that erode the effective increase to <10%.
*   **Conservative Math:**
    *   Remaining customers: 427 (5% churn).
    *   New Rate: $1.15X$.
    *   Revenue: $427 \times 1.15X = 491X$.
    *   **Net Gain:** +$41X$.
*   **Stress Test (Realistic Elasticity):** If actual churn hits 12% (common for unexpected double-digit hikes) and 15% of survivors negotiate discounts:
    *   Remaining customers: 396.
    *   Effective Rate: ~$1.05X$ (blended).
    *   Revenue: $396 \times 1.05X = 415.8X$.
    *   **Net Gain:** -$34.2X$ (Revenue Decline).
*   **Reputational Cost:** High. Sales teams spend Q1 fighting fires instead of selling. Brand perception shifts from "partner" to "vendor."

**Scenario B: New Customers Only (The Recommended Path)**
*   **Assumption:** 0% churn on existing base. New business grows at a conservative 10% (45 new logos).
*   **Math:**
    *   Existing Revenue: $450X$ (unchanged).
    *   New Revenue: $45 \times 1.15X = 51.75X$.
    *   Total Revenue: $501.75X$.
    *   **Net Gain:** +$51.75X$.
*   **Upside:** Existing customers feel valued, increasing retention and openness to *optional* add-on sales (which carry higher margin). Sales leadership can pitch the "locked-in rate" as a competitive advantage to prospects, creating urgency.
*   **Reputational Benefit:** High. Reinforces stability and partnership.

**Strategic Rationale**
The "Grandfather Strategy" protects your installed base—the source of recurring revenue and case studies—while capturing market rate from new entrants. Enterprise buyers have long memories; punishing loyalty now will suppress expansion revenue (cross-sell/up-sell) for years. The 15% infrastructure cost increase should be absorbed by efficiency gains and new logo acquisition, not extracted from current partners.

**Immediate Action Plan**
1.  **Announce** the price increase for new contracts effective immediately.
2.  **Communicate** to existing customers that their renewal terms are protected for this cycle as a "loyalty commitment."
3.  **Task Sales** with identifying the top 20% of existing users who have expanded usage since last year; offer them voluntary "premium" tiers with added value rather than a blanket tax.

This strategy delivers a higher probable revenue floor ($501X$ vs. a volatile $415X–$491X$) and secures the brand equity necessary for long-term growth.

*Note: This analysis assumes a standard B2B SaaS elasticity curve; if your product is a single-point utility with easy substitutes, churn risks in Scenario A are even higher.*
