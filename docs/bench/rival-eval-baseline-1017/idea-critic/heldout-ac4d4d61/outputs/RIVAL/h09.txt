**Recommendation: Do not promote Alex to Team Lead at this time.**

Promoting Alex based on the hope that responsibility will cure behavioral issues is a high-risk strategy that threatens the stability of your new team. While Alex's technical velocity is a significant asset (40% above average), leadership requires emotional intelligence and the ability to multiply the output of others, not just individual coding speed.

**Stress Test: The "Responsibility Cures Behavior" Assumption**
Your assumption that the promotion will force Alex to improve is flawed for three reasons:
1.  **Incentive Misalignment:** Alex has been rewarded for speed, not collaboration. Suddenly changing the reward structure without explicit coaching creates confusion, not growth. High-performers often double down on the behaviors that got them promoted when faced with stress.
2.  **Power Dynamics:** Giving authority to someone with a history of abrasiveness often amplifies negative traits rather than suppressing them. Instead of feeling compelled to be nicer, Alex may feel empowered to be more demanding, viewing past complaints as obstacles now removed by their new title.
3.  **Lack of Mechanism:** There is no mechanism in a job title change that teaches empathy or conflict resolution. Without a parallel, rigorous executive coaching plan, the behavior will likely remain static or worsen under the pressure of managing eight people.

**Risk Analysis: Immediate Attrition**
The risk of attrition is critical. You noted three formal complaints in the past year. In a new team of eight, even one or two departures represent a 12–25% loss of headcount immediately upon formation.
*   **The Signal:** Promoting Alex signals to the organization that technical output outweighs cultural health. This can demoralize other engineers who value collaboration.
*   **The Cost:** Replacing a specialized engineer costs 1.5x–2x their salary and takes months. The short-term gain of Alex's coding speed will be obliterated by the recruitment and onboarding costs of those who leave.

**Strategic Alternative**
Do not choose between "Promote Alex" and "Hire External" as a binary. Consider a third path:
1.  **Hire an External Lead:** Bring in a leader with proven people-management skills to build the team culture and processes. This ensures the new suite has a stable foundation.
2.  **Create a "Principal Engineer" Track for Alex:** Promote Alex technically, not managerially. Give them ownership of the most complex architectural challenges where their speed is a force multiplier.
3.  **Conditional Leadership Path:** Make it clear to Alex that a future move into people management is possible, but *only* after demonstrating sustained improvement in communication over the next 6–12 months, verified by 360-degree feedback and executive coaching.

**Final Verdict**
Building a sustainable team culture requires prioritizing psychological safety over individual velocity. Promoting Alex now gambles the success of the entire automation suite on an unproven behavioral change. Secure the team's foundation with an experienced leader first; leverage Alex's genius where it is safest and most effective: in the code.

*Note: This recommendation assumes the goal is long-term team retention and product stability. If the goal were purely short-term feature delivery regardless of turnover, the calculus would differ, but that approach is rarely sustainable for a core platform team.*
