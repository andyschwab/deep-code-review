**Strategic Rationale**
Turning down 25% of your capacity is painful, but turning down a validated use case from your largest customer is dangerous. The risk isn't building a one-off; the risk is building a one-off *in secret*. By formalizing this as a Design Partner Program, you force the generalization conversation before a single line of code is written.

**Stress Test: The "Five Other Clients" Assumption**
Your assumption that this feature will sell to five other enterprises within a year is currently a hypothesis, not a fact. Here is the breakdown of where that assumption likely fails:

1.  **The "GlobalCorp Exception" Trap:** Large enterprises often have unique compliance, legacy integration, or workflow requirements that no one else shares. If the feature requires building connectors specifically for GlobalCorp's 1990s ERP system, the addressable market is exactly one.
    *   *Validation Step:* Before coding, your product team must map every requirement against the top 10 prospects' public tech stacks and compliance needs. If >40% of the requirements are specific to GlobalCorp's internal legacy, the generalization potential is low.

2.  **Configuration vs. Code:** Often, what looks like a custom feature is actually a gap in your configuration layer. If you build this as hard-coded logic for GlobalCorp, you create technical debt. If you build it as a configurable module, the effort might exceed the 6-engineer/quarter estimate.
    *   *Validation Step:* Architect the solution as a "plugin" or "module" from day one. If the engineering lead says this adds >30% to the timeline, the generalization path is harder than anticipated.

3.  **Sales Cycle Reality:** Even if the feature is technically generalizable, selling it to five other clients in 12 months requires those clients to be in an active buying cycle *now*. Enterprise sales cycles are 6–9 months. You cannot count on revenue from this feature for at least 9 months post-launch.
    *   *Validation Step:* Have your VP of Sales commit to introducing this specific capability in the next 20 discovery calls. If prospects don't bite during the sales pitch, they won't buy the finished product.

**Decision Framework for the Board**
Present this not as "Custom Work vs. Roadmap," but as "Market Validation vs. Speculation."

*   **Accept if:** GlobalCorp agrees to IP retention by Meridian, allows you to market the feature (even anonymously) as a case study, and commits to quarterly strategy reviews to prune custom requirements.
*   **Decline if:** GlobalCorp demands exclusivity, owns the IP, or refuses to engage in product strategy discussions. In this scenario, the $500k is a "golden handcuff" that distracts your team from the core product without offering long-term leverage.
