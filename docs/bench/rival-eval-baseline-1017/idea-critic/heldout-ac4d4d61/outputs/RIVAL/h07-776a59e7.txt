**Recommendation: Do not sunset the legacy line yet. Execute a "Managed Migration Pilot" immediately.**

Cutting the line now is a financial and reputational error. While the product loses $60,000 quarterly, the risk of losing 40% of your user base (1,200 customers) outweighs the short-term operational savings. If even half of those churned users are vocal enterprise clients, the brand damage to your new platform's sales will exceed the $240,000 annual loss you are trying to eliminate.

Your assumption that 60% of users will migrate is unverified and likely optimistic. Legacy users often stay due to specific, hard-to-replicate workflows or fear of change, not just price. A forced migration without validated fit will trigger the exact churn and negative sentiment you fear.

**Action Plan: The 45-Day Validation Sprint**

1.  **Segment and Survey (Weeks 1-2):** Isolate the top 20% of users by revenue/usage. Conduct deep-dive interviews to identify the "critical workflows" they cannot lose. Simultaneously, survey the broader base with a concrete migration offer (not a hypothetical one) to measure actual intent versus stated interest.
2.  **The "Golden Path" Pilot (Weeks 3-6):** Select 50 friendly users for a white-glove migration. Track the real cost, technical friction, and feature gaps. This data will validate if your 60% target is realistic or if the migration cost per user exceeds their lifetime value.
3.  **Decision Gate (September 1st):**
    *   **If Pilot Success > 70%:** Proceed with sunset, using the pilot group as case studies to drive adoption.
    *   **If Pilot Success < 50%:** Halt the sunset. Either invest in bridging the feature gap to make migration viable, or maintain the legacy line as a "cash cow" with minimized support costs while you build a better replacement.

**Stress Test: The 60% Migration Assumption**

The belief that a majority will switch rests on three fragile premises:
*   **Feature Parity Illusion:** You assume the new platform does what the old one does. Legacy users often rely on obscure features deemed "low priority" by modern product teams. If the new tool lacks one critical function, adoption drops to zero for that user, regardless of discounts.
*   **Switching Cost Underestimation:** You are calculating financial cost, not operational friction. Retraining staff, migrating data, and altering established workflows create massive inertia. A 20% discount rarely offsets the internal political capital a customer must spend to change tools.
*   **Silent Churn Risk:** Your loyalists are quiet because the product works. Breaking their workflow turns them into vocal detractors. In B2B markets, one angry power user can stall multiple new deals through peer networks.

**Conclusion**

Do not bet the company's reputation on an untested migration rate. Use the next 45 days to replace assumptions with data. If the migration proves too costly or technically impossible for the majority, it is cheaper to run the legacy line at a reduced, break-even capacity than to actively drive customers to competitors who will happily listen to their complaints.

*Note: This approach shifts the narrative from "cutting losses" to "de-risking transition," which aligns better with both CFO fiscal goals and CSO retention mandates.*
