**Stress Test of Assumption:** The assumption that "added responsibility will force improvement in soft skills" is critically flawed and likely to fail. Behavioral psychology and organizational data suggest that without prior foundational change, adding pressure (leadership responsibility) to an individual with a history of abrasive behavior typically exacerbates the negative traits rather than resolving them. The "Peter Principle" risk is high: promoting a top technical performer into a role requiring diametrically opposite skills (empathy, mediation, coaching) based solely on technical output often results in the loss of a great engineer and the gain of a poor manager. There is no evidence provided that Alex desires to change or has received specific, actionable feedback that resulted in prior improvement; three formal complaints indicate a entrenched pattern, not a temporary lapse. Responsibility alone is not a training mechanism.

High performers often leave due to toxic management faster than they leave due to technical challenges. **Decision:** No-go on the promotion to People Lead. Go-if on a structured behavioral development plan with a 6-month review for potential future leadership eligibility.
