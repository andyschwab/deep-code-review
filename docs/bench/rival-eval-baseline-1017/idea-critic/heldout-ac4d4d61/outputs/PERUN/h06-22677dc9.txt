**Recommendation: Proceed with a "Go-If" Open-Source Strategy**

Release the library under a strong copyleft license (e.g., AGPLv3) or a source-available license (e.g., BSL 1.1) rather than a permissive one (MIT/Apache). This approach captures the employer branding and community contribution benefits while legally mitigating the risk of competitors fork-and-undercutting your service.

**Decision Framework:**
*   **Verdict:** Go-If.
*   **Condition:** The release is contingent on selecting a license that prevents proprietary forks by direct competitors. If Legal cannot approve a restrictive license by July 25, default to "No-Go" (keep proprietary).
*   **Kill Criteria:** If a competitor releases a compatible proprietary tool within 3 months of your launch that matches your performance, immediately pause community marketing efforts and revert to internal-only development.
*   **Leading Metric:** Ratio of external code contributions (PRs merged) to internal engineering hours spent on maintenance. Target: >0.2 by Q4.
*   **Review Date:** August 5th (Strategic Planning Session).

**Stress Test of Assumption (Community Contributions vs. Competitor Risk):**
Your assumption that community contributions will accelerate development by 20% is plausible but fragile. It relies on the library solving a *generic* pain point for the wider market, not just Prism Tech's specific workflow. If the tool is too tightly coupled to your internal infrastructure, external adoption will be low, yielding zero contributions while still exposing your logic. Conversely, the risk of a competitor undercutting you is high *only* if you use a permissive license. With a copyleft/source-available license, a competitor cannot legally optimize and re-close the source to undercut you without violating the license or exposing their own proprietary improvements, neutralizing the "fork-and-undercut" threat. The primary risk shifts from competitor theft to "maintenance drag," where low-quality external PRs consume more engineering time than they save.

**Note:** Verify with Legal immediately whether AGPL or BSL aligns with your company's definition of "open-source" for branding purposes, as purists may reject restrictive licenses. Ensure your internal documentation is sanitized of proprietary secrets before any public release.
