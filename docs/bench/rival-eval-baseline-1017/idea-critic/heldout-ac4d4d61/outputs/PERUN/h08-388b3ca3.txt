**Recommendation: Do not proceed with the switch yet; immediately initiate a targeted proof-of-concept (PoC) and a detailed refactoring audit before making a final decision.**

While a $240,000 annual saving is significant, the six-week migration estimate and the unverified 15ms latency assumption present unacceptable risks for real-time applications without empirical validation. The potential hidden costs of pipeline refactoring and unplanned downtime could easily erode the projected savings in the first year alone.

**Action Plan:**
1.  **Commission a 48-Hour Latency PoC:** Before any commitment, deploy a non-critical, real-time workload to the new provider's network. Measure actual latency variance under load, not just averages. If latency spikes exceed 10ms during peak simulation, the 15ms assumption fails, and the switch threatens core service levels.
2.  **Audit Refactoring Effort:** Assign two senior engineers to spend three days mapping current deployment pipelines against the new provider's native tools. Produce a line-item estimate for code changes, testing, and potential third-party tool replacements. This converts "unknown hidden costs" into a concrete dollar figure.
3.  **Leverage for Renegotiation:** Use the competing bid as leverage with your current provider. Present the 20% lower offer and request a matching reduction or enhanced service terms. This path offers immediate financial upside with zero migration risk or downtime.

*   **Go with Switch:** Only if the PoC confirms latency increases are <10ms AND the refactoring audit reveals costs < $50,000.
*   **Renegotiate:** If the PoC shows latency risks >10ms OR refactoring costs exceed $80,000.
*   **Stay Put:** If the current provider cannot match at least a 10% reduction and the new provider fails the latency test.
