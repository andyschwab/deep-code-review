### Kickoff Session Plan: Code Review Latency Dashboard

**Objective:** Resolve conflicting requirements (real-time vs. historical) and validate technical feasibility on an 85% capacity cluster before writing code.

#### Phase 1: Pre-Session Data Gathering (Manager Actions)
Before convening the team, you must provide the following hard constraints to ground the discussion in reality:
1.  **Compute Headroom Report:** Exact available CPU/RAM during peak hours on the internal cluster. Confirm if "85% capacity" allows for *any* new persistent processes or if this requires batch-only execution.
2.  **Data Source Inventory:** List of existing logs/tables containing code review timestamps. Confirm retention policy (do we actually have 6 months of accessible data?).
3.  **Business Impact Statement:** A one-sentence definition of the primary problem. Are we trying to stop bottlenecks *as they happen* (Marcus) or identify long-term process decay (Elena)?

#### Phase 2: The Alignment Workshop (90 Minutes)
**Attendees:** You (Facilitator), Marcus, Elena.
**Rule:** No discussion of UI or implementation details until the "Why" and "How Much" are agreed upon.

**Step A: Problem Reframing (15 mins)**
*   Ask Marcus: "What specific action will you take within 5 minutes of seeing a latency spike?" If no immediate action exists, real-time is unnecessary.
*   Ask Elena: "What specific process change results from seeing a 6-month trend?" If the feedback loop is too slow to matter, deep history may be low priority.
*   **Goal:** Map features to *decisions*, not preferences.

**Step B: The Constraint Stress Test (30 mins)**
*   Present the 85% capacity constraint.
*   Task the pair with a joint whiteboard sketch: "How do we deliver Marcus's need for speed AND Elena's need for trends without exceeding current compute limits?"
*   Force a trade-off discussion: Does real-time require sampling? Can historical data be pre-computed overnight to save peak resources?
*   **Goal:** Shift from "My way vs. Your way" to "Our solution within these walls."

**Step C: Define the "Smallest Valuable Slice" (30 mins)**
*   Agree on a single metric that satisfies the most urgent business need identified in Step A.
*   Define the MVP scope: e.g., "Daily batch-updated latency averages for the last 30 days." (This often satisfies both: frequent enough for ops, historical enough for trends, cheap enough for the cluster).
*   **Goal:** A written agreement on the V1 scope that fits the infrastructure.

**Step D: Success Criteria & Exit Conditions (15 mins)**
*   Define what "done" looks like for the prototype.
*   Agree on a "kill switch": If the prototype exceeds X% compute usage, it is paused, not optimized indefinitely.

#### Phase 3: Immediate Next Steps
1.  **Draft the One-Page Brief:** Document the agreed "Smallest Valuable Slice," the specific compute budget allocated, and the primary decision the dashboard supports.
2.  **Technical Spike (2 Days):** Assign Marcus and Elena to jointly produce a *non-functional* proof-of-concept query that calculates the agreed metric on the actual cluster. They must return with actual resource usage numbers, not estimates.
3.  **Go/No-Go Review:** Review the spike results. If resource usage is within bounds, approve full build. If not, return to Phase 2 to further reduce scope.

**Note:** Do not allow the team to split up and build two versions. The constraint requires a unified, optimized approach.
**Note:** If the "Technical Spike" reveals the cluster cannot handle even the reduced scope, the only valid output is a recommendation to acquire new infrastructure, not to destabilize existing services.
