**Recommendation: Do Not Implement Globally; Archive as a Learning**

Based on the data provided, the green button is a statistically significant winner but a business loser. While the 95% confidence level confirms the lift is real and not random noise, the 0.4% effect size fails to justify the engineering cost or the strategic risk.

**Interpretation for the CMO**
Statistical significance answers "Did something change?" (Yes). Business significance answers "Does it matter?" (No). A 0.4% lift on 120,000 visitors yields roughly $3,000/month. If the engineering effort to maintain a custom green button variant across devices and future page updates exceeds this return, the project has a negative ROI. Furthermore, your intuition regarding brand perception is a valid "guardrail metric." If ShopSwift's brand relies on a specific blue identity, eroding that equity for a marginal gain is a long-term loss. The test successfully proved that button color is not a primary friction point for your checkout; users are not abandoning carts because the button is blue.

**Decision Factors Beyond Significance**
1.  **Opportunity Cost:** Engineering hours spent maintaining this minor variant are hours not spent on high-impact features (e.g., payment options, load speed) that could drive double-digit lifts.
2.  **Brand Consistency:** If the blue button is part of your design system, fragmenting it creates "design debt." Future redesigns must now account for two button states, increasing complexity and QA time.
3.  **The "Local Maximum" Trap:** You hypothesized a double-digit lift. The failure to achieve this suggests you are optimizing within a narrow constraint (color) rather than addressing the actual barrier (e.g., trust signals, form length, shipping costs).
4.  **Maintenance Burden:** Running two design versions requires ongoing validation to ensure the green button doesn't break on new browsers or mobile updates. The $3,000 gain may vanish after the first bug fix.

**Final Verdict**
Kill the global rollout. Do not run the test longer; extending the duration will only narrow the confidence interval around the same tiny effect size, not increase the lift. Instead, document this result in your experimentation playbook as a key learning: "Checkout conversion is insensitive to button color; focus future resources on friction reduction and trust elements." Reallocate engineering resources to tests with higher hypothesis potential.

Note: Ensure your analytics track "design debt" or engineering maintenance hours to better quantify these hidden costs in future ROI calculations.
Always pair statistical wins with a qualitative brand review before approving UI changes.
