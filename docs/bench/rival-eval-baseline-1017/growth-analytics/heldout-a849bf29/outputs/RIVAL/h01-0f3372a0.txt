### North Star Metric Analysis for Acme Connect

Based on your hypothesis that cross-departmental collaboration drives stickiness, here are three candidate North Star metrics to align your teams, ranked by their potential to predict long-term value.

#### 1. Weekly Active Collaborative Projects (WACP)
**Definition:** The count of unique projects created within a 7-day window that include members from at least two distinct departments within the same enterprise account.
*   **Pros:** Directly validates your hypothesis regarding cross-departmental stickiness. It filters out "noise" activity (single-user notes or single-team chats) and focuses on the complex workflows that are hardest to replace. It is a leading indicator; if project creation slows, renewals are at risk months before the contract ends.
*   **Cons:** Requires robust departmental tagging of users, which may not exist in your current schema. It ignores the *value* or *activity level* within the project—a created but abandoned project still counts.

#### 2. Depth of Inter-Departmental Engagement (DIDE)
**Definition:** The weekly average number of meaningful interactions (messages, file shares, task completions) occurring between users of different departments within active projects.
*   **Pros:** Measures actual adoption and value realization rather than just setup. It captures the "habit" formation across silos, which is likely the true barrier to churn. High DIDE suggests the product is embedded in critical cross-functional processes.
*   **Cons:** Computationally complex to define and track without event-level granularity. "Meaningful interaction" is subjective and requires strict definition to avoid gaming (e.g., spamming messages to boost the metric).

#### 3. Enterprise Network Density Score
**Definition:** A composite score (0–100) per account representing the percentage of possible cross-departmental connections that are actively utilized monthly.
*   **Pros:** Holistic view of account health. It accounts for the size of the customer; a large enterprise with only one cross-dept project is under-penetrated, while a small team with full connectivity is maxed out. Excellent for Customer Success prioritization.
*   **Cons:** Too lagging and abstract for daily engineering/design decisions. Hard to explain as a single "number" to the whole company. Changes slowly, making it difficult to correlate with specific product releases in the short term.

### Recommendation & Tracking Gaps

**Recommended North Star:** **Weekly Active Collaborative Projects (WACP)**.
It offers the best balance of actionability for engineering/design and predictive power for retention. It directly measures the *expansion* of value into new silos, which correlates with your 88% renewal rate better than raw volume metrics.

**Critical Tracking Gaps Preventing Certainty:**
1.  **Missing Departmental Metadata:** You cannot currently verify if a project is "cross-departmental" because user profiles likely lack standardized department tags or group affiliations.
2.  **Event Granularity:** You track "total messages" but likely lack event properties linking specific actions to specific `project_id`s and `user_department` pairs. Without this, you cannot isolate cross-functional behavior from intra-team chatter.
3.  **Cohort Linkage:** There is no established data pipeline linking specific behavioral cohorts (e.g., "teams with 3+ cross-dept projects") to renewal outcomes over time to statistically validate the correlation.

*Action Required: Implement a tracking plan to capture `project_created` events with `participant_department_list` properties immediately to validate the WACP hypothesis before Q1.*
