# Diagnostic Plan: TaskMaster Retention Flatline

To determine whether the flat 22% week-four retention is caused by lower-quality traffic or a broken onboarding experience for specific segments, execute the following five data slices immediately. These comparisons isolate the drop-off point between your legacy cohorts and the new 8,500 signups.

### 1. Cohort Overlap Comparison (The "Quality" Test)
Plot two distinct retention curves on the same axis:
*   **Control Group:** Users who signed up 7–12 weeks ago (pre-spike).
*   **Test Group:** Users who signed up in the last 6 weeks (the 35% spike).
*   **Action:** If the Test Group curve drops significantly lower than the Control Group *within the first 3 days*, the issue is likely immediate relevance (user quality or misleading ad copy). If the curves track identically until Day 10 and then diverge, the issue is likely long-term engagement or feature fatigue, not initial quality.

### 2. Onboarding Funnel Step-by-Step Drop-off
Break down the onboarding flow into discrete steps (e.g., Account Created → First Project Created → First Task Added → First Invite Sent).
*   **Slice:** Compare conversion rates for each step between the Control and Test groups.
*   **Diagnostic:** Identify the specific step where the Test Group underperforms by >15%.
    *   *Drop at "Account Created":* Traffic source mismatch (wrong audience).
    *   *Drop at "First Task Added":* UX friction or confusion for new user types.
    *   *No difference in onboarding:* The users are successfully onboarded but failing to find recurring value later.

### 3. Source/Medium Retention Heatmap
Segment the week-four retention rate by `utm_source` and `utm_medium` for the last six weeks only.
*   **Action:** Rank channels from highest to lowest retention.
*   **Diagnostic:** If one specific channel (e.g., a new paid social campaign) has a retention rate <10% while organic or search remains at 22%+, you have identified the low-quality traffic source. Pause spend on that specific channel immediately. If *all* new channels show uniformly lower retention, the issue is likely a change in the product experience or onboarding flow affecting everyone.

### 4. "Aha Moment" Correlation Analysis
Identify the specific action in TaskMaster that correlates most strongly with week-four retention (e.g., "Users who create 3+ tasks in week 1 have 60% retention").
*   **Slice:** Compare the percentage of Control vs. Test users who hit this "Aha Moment" within their first 7 days.
*   **Diagnostic:** If the Test Group is hitting the "Aha Moment" at the same rate as the Control Group but still churning by week 4, the core value proposition is not sticky enough for long-term use. If they are *not* hitting the moment, the onboarding is failing to guide them to value.

### 5. Device and Platform Segmentation
Split the new cohorts by device (Mobile Web vs. iOS App vs. Android App vs. Desktop).
*   **Diagnostic:** A sudden influx of mobile traffic often dilutes retention metrics if the mobile experience is inferior to desktop. If mobile signups spiked 200% but mobile retention is 5%, the aggregate 22% figure is being dragged down by a specific platform experience, not general user quality.

**Recommendation:** Do not adjust total ad spend until Slice #3 confirms which channels are underperforming. If Slice #2 shows a specific onboarding step failure, prioritize a product fix over media buying changes.

*Note: Ensure your observation windows match exactly (e.g., compare Day 28 retention for cohorts that have fully elapsed) to avoid skewing results with pending data.*
