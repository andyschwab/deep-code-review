**Recommendation: Do not implement universal price hikes or segmented pricing yet. Treat this result as a strong signal for further hypothesis testing, not a final decision.**

The divergent results you observed are statistically plausible given your sample size, but they do not yet justify the operational complexity of age-based pricing or the risk of a universal increase. Here is the interpretation and strategic implication for each path:

**1. Universal Price Increase (Do Not Proceed)**
*   **Implication:** While the 35–50 segment showed a 5% lift (supporting your "price-as-quality" hypothesis), the 18–24 segment's 18% drop is severe. Even if the net revenue is neutral today, this strategy actively shrinks your future pipeline. Younger users represent long-term lifetime value (LTV); alienating them now creates a "leaky bucket" where you must constantly acquire new users to replace those priced out.
*   **Risk:** You trade future growth for present stability. If the 18% drop is real, you are sacrificing market share in a demographic that is typically most vocal on social channels, potentially damaging brand perception beyond just conversion metrics.

**2. Immediate Segmented Pricing (Do Not Proceed)**
*   **Implication:** Implementing age-based pricing based on a single test introduces significant technical debt and potential legal/compliance risks (age discrimination laws vary by region). Furthermore, "age" may be a proxy for another variable (e.g., income level, device type, or content preference).
*   **Risk:** If the segmentation logic is flawed, you may leave money on the table or inadvertently charge loyal high-value users less than they would willingly pay. The complexity of maintaining two price structures often outweighs the marginal gain unless the elasticity difference is proven stable over time.

**3. Maintain Current Pricing & Run Follow-up Tests (Recommended)**
*   **Implication:** Acknowledge that price elasticity varies by cohort but requires validation before structural changes. Your sample size (10k per segment) provides a directional signal, but the confidence intervals likely overlap with zero for the net impact, making the "neutral" revenue finding fragile.
*   **Action Plan:**
    *   **Validate the Signal:** Run a targeted follow-up test specifically on the 18–24 demographic with smaller price increments (e.g., 3% and 5%) to pinpoint the exact elasticity threshold.
    *   **Test Value Perception:** For the 35–50 group, test if the conversion lift holds when the price increase is paired with a specific quality signal (e.g., "4K Premium" badge) versus a bare price hike. This confirms if it's truly a quality signal or just lower sensitivity.
    *   **Analyze LTV, Not Just Conversion:** Determine if the converting 18–24 users have higher retention or upsell potential than the 35–50 group. A lower conversion rate might be acceptable if the acquired users are stickier.

**Conclusion for the VP:**
The data suggests distinct behavioral cohorts, but acting on this single experiment is premature. A universal hike risks long-term growth; segmented pricing introduces unnecessary complexity without confirmed causality. The prudent path is to keep pricing flat while designing a second-wave experiment to isolate *why* these groups reacted differently. Once we confirm the driver (price sensitivity vs. quality signaling), we can build a robust strategy—whether that involves tiered features rather than age-based rates.

*Note: Ensure your analytics tracking captures post-conversion retention by cohort to fully assess the LTV impact before finalizing any pricing architecture.*
