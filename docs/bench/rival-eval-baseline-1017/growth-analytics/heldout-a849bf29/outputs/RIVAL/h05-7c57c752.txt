# Testing Strategy: High-Risk, High-Speed Decision Framework

Given your constraint of 400 signups/week and a hard deadline of November 1, a standard fixed-horizon A/B test is mathematically impossible if you require 95% confidence for a 15% lift. You would need approximately 12,000 users per variant (24,000 total), requiring 60 weeks of traffic.

To make a confident go/no-go decision by November 1, you must shift from "statistical proof" to "risk-managed decision making." I recommend a **Sequential Bayes Factor Test** with a **Guardrail-First Launch Strategy**.

### 1. The Method: Sequential Bayes Factor Testing
Do not use standard p-values with sequential peeking; this guarantees false positives. Instead, use a Bayesian approach that calculates the probability that Variant B is better than Control A as data arrives.

*   **Metric:** Activation Rate (Step 2 completion).
*   **Baseline:** Assume current activation is ~40% (industry standard for 5-step flows); you need to detect a move to 46%.
*   **Stopping Rule:** Set a Bayes Factor threshold of 6.0 (strong evidence) rather than the traditional 20.0 (decisive evidence). This accepts a higher risk of false positives (approx. 10-15%) in exchange for reaching a decision 3x faster.
*   **Timeline:** With 400 users/week, you can expect a "strong evidence" signal in 4–5 weeks if the 15% lift is real. If the effect is smaller, the test will remain inconclusive, triggering the fallback plan.

### 2. The Trade-off: Speed vs. Reliability
| Approach | Time to Decision | False Positive Risk | Recommendation |
| :--- | :--- | :--- | : |
| **Standard A/B (95% Conf)** | 60 weeks | 5% | **Reject.** Misses deadline. |
| **Sequential P-Value** | 4 weeks | 40%+ | **Reject.** Too noisy; likely to ship a loser. |
| **Bayes Factor (Threshold 6)** | 4–5 weeks | ~12% | **Accept.** Balanced risk for strategic pivot. |

**The Risk:** You might ship a change that provides only a 5% lift (or no lift) thinking it was 15%.
**The Mitigation:** Since the change *simplifies* the flow (5 steps → 2), the qualitative risk of harming the user experience is lower than adding friction. The primary risk is wasted engineering time if the lift doesn't materialize post-launch.

### 3. Execution Plan (4-Week Sprint)

**Week 1: Setup & Baseline Validation**
*   Implement the 2-step flow behind a feature flag.
*   Verify tracking on both flows immediately.
*   Run the test at 50/50 traffic split.

**Weeks 2–4: Monitoring with Guardrails**
*   Monitor the Bayes Factor daily.
*   **Critical Guardrail:** Track "Support Tickets per Signup" and "Time-to-First-Value." If the new flow increases confusion (higher support load) or delays value realization, stop immediately regardless of activation rates.
*   **Decision Point (End of Week 4):**
    *   *Bayes Factor > 6:* Ship immediately.
    *   *Bayes Factor < 0.16 (Strong evidence against):* Kill the project.
    *   *Inconclusive:* Trigger the "Fallback Launch."

### 4. The Fallback: "Launch & Monitor"
If the test is inconclusive by November 1, do not wait. Since the engineering team must pivot, adopt the new flow as the default but treat it as a **permanent beta**.
*   **Action:** Switch 100% of traffic to the 2-step flow.
*   **Measurement:** Compare activation rates week-over-week against the historical baseline of the 5-step flow (adjusted for seasonality).
*   **Rollback Plan:** Define a specific metric threshold (e.g., if activation drops >5% compared to baseline over 2 weeks) that triggers an immediate code rollback to the 5-step flow.

### Recommendation to Head of Product
Approve the **Sequential Bayes Factor** approach. It respects the November 1 deadline while quantifying the uncertainty. The risk of a false positive is acceptable because the change reduces friction (a low-risk direction), and we have a defined rollback plan if the post-launch data contradicts the test signal. This unblocks the engineering team while maintaining a data-informed safety net.

*Note: Ensure your analytics tool supports Bayesian analysis (e.g., PostHog, Optimizely Stats Engine) before starting.*
*If the tool only supports frequentist stats, reduce the confidence level to 90% and accept a 2-week extension.*
