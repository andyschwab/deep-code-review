### Diagnostic Plan: TaskMaster Acquisition vs. Retention Disconnect

**Verdict:** Your assumption of "lower-quality users" is unverified. A flat retention rate (22%) amidst a 35% signup surge often indicates an **onboarding bottleneck** or **segmentation mismatch**, not necessarily poor user intent. Pouring more ad spend into this funnel without isolating the leak will waste budget. We must diagnose bottom-up (Retention → Activation → Acquisition) before adjusting acquisition channels.

**Immediate Actions (This Week):**

1.  **Split Cohort Retention by Traffic Source (The Quality Test)**
    *   **Action:** Segment your week-four retention curve by specific acquisition channel (e.g., Paid Search, Social, Referral) for the last six weeks vs. the prior baseline.
    *   **Question:** Is the 22% average hiding a collapse in one specific new channel while others hold steady?
    *   **Decision Trigger:** If one new channel shows <15% retention while others match the 22% baseline, pause spend on that specific channel immediately. If *all* new channels show flat retention, the issue is product/onboarding, not traffic quality.

2.  **Activation Funnel Drop-off by Cohort (The Onboarding Test)**
    *   **Action:** Define your "core action" (e.g., creating the first task, completing a project). Create a funnel visualization: `Signup → Onboarding Step 1 → Core Action Completed`. Compare the conversion rates of the **new 8,500 users** against the **previous 6-week cohort**.
    *   **Question:** Do the new users drop off at a specific onboarding step where older users succeeded?
    *   **Decision Trigger:** If new users drop off 20% more than old users at "Onboarding Step 2," your recent UI changes or traffic expectations are misaligned. Fix the step; do not cut ads.

3.  **Time-to-Value Analysis (The Engagement Test)**
    *   **Action:** Measure the time elapsed between `Signup` and `First Core Action` for both cohorts.
    *   **Question:** Are new users taking significantly longer to reach the "aha" moment?
    *   **Decision Trigger:** If time-to-value has increased by >24 hours for new users, they are getting lost or confused. This confirms an experience breakdown, not user quality.

**Required Instrumentation Check:**
Ensure you are tracking a **stable, pseudonymous user ID** across sessions to accurately follow these users from ad click to week-four retention. If your current data is too aggregated to split by source or day, you cannot make a data-driven decision; you must instrument these specific events before scaling spend.

**Conclusion:**
Do not adjust total ad spend yet. If the split cohort analysis shows uniform retention across all sources, the problem is your product's ability to convert *any* user at this scale (onboarding friction). If only specific sources fail, reallocate budget to the winners.

*Note: All metrics above must be calculated from your actual analytics platform; no external benchmarks apply. Ensure PII handling for user IDs complies with your privacy policy.*
