**Testing Strategy: Sequential Design with Guardrails for Low-Traffic Onboarding**

Do not use a higher significance threshold (e.g., p < 0.10) to speed up results; this explicitly increases your false-positive rate, risking a pivot to a feature that does not actually work. Instead, adopt a **Group Sequential Design** with pre-committed interim looks. This allows you to check results at specific intervals without inflating the error rate, provided you adjust the significance threshold for each look using an alpha-spending function (e.g., O'Brien-Fleming).

**The Plan:**
1.  **Primary Metric:** Define "Activation" strictly as the completion of the core value action (not just finishing the two-step setup).
2.  **Guardrail Metric:** Select one stability metric (e.g., support ticket volume regarding setup or Day-1 retention) that must not degrade. If the guardrail drops significantly, stop immediately regardless of activation gains.
3.  **Schedule & Stopping Rules:**
    *   **Total Duration:** 6 weeks (ending mid-October to meet the Nov 1 deadline).
    *   **Interim Looks:** Check data only at Week 3 and Week 6.
    *   **Thresholds:** At Week 3, require a very high bar (e.g., p < 0.005) to stop early for efficacy. If not met, continue to Week 6 and test at the standard adjusted level (e.g., p < 0.048).
4.  **Sample Size Reality:** With 400 signups/week, you have 2,400 total users. Detecting a 15% lift on a baseline activation rate (assume ~40% for illustration) requires roughly 1,800–2,200 users per variant for 80% power. Your total pool (2,400) splits to 1,200 per variant. You are underpowered for a guaranteed detection of a 15% lift.
5.  **Decision Logic:**
    *   **Go:** If Week 3 shows massive significance OR Week 6 shows significance above the adjusted threshold AND the guardrail holds.
    *   **No-Go:** If Week 6 shows no significance or the guardrail fails.
    *   **Extend (Only if critical):** If Week 6 shows a strong trend (p < 0.15) but misses significance, you must explicitly request a deadline extension. Do not ship on a "hunch."

**Trade-offs:**
*   **Speed vs. Reliability:** Sequential testing maximizes reliability for your speed constraint. It prevents the "peeking" error where daily checks fabricate wins. However, with your low traffic, the trade-off is **sensitivity**: you may conclude "No-Go" simply because you lacked the users to detect the lift, even if the change was good.
*   **Risk:** The risk of a false positive is controlled by the math. The risk of a false negative (missing a win) is high due to sample size.

**This Week's Step:**
Calculate the exact Minimum Detectable Effect (MDE) for your specific baseline activation rate using an online calculator for group sequential designs (input N=1,200 per variant, alpha=0.05, power=0.80). If the MDE is higher than 15%, present the Head of Product with two options: (A) Accept a lower confidence level (higher risk of false positive) explicitly documented, or (B) Delay the engineering pivot by two weeks to gather sufficient data. Do not proceed without this calculation.

*Note: This plan assumes your traffic is consistent; seasonality or concurrent marketing campaigns will confound these results.*
*Ensure user identifiers are pseudonymous and stable across sessions to track activation correctly.*
