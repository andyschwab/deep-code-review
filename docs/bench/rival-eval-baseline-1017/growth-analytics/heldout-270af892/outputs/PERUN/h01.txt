**North Star Metric Recommendation for Acme Connect**

Based on your hypothesis that cross-departmental collaboration drives stickiness, here are three candidate North Star metrics. Note that without instrumented data linking specific user actions to your 88% renewal rate, any claim about which "best predicts" retention is currently **UNVERIFIED**. You must validate these against your own baseline before adoption.

**1. Weekly Active Teams Creating Cross-Departmental Projects**
*Definition:* The count of unique teams (not individuals) that initiate at least one project involving members from two or more distinct departments within a 7-day window.
*   **Pros:** Directly measures your hypothesized value driver (cross-functional stickiness). It filters out "noisy" activity like single-user messaging or intra-team chatter that doesn't deepen platform dependency. It aligns engineering efforts toward features that lower the friction of multi-department setup.
*   **Cons:** Requires robust team/department taxonomy in your event data. If your current tracking only logs "project created" without department metadata, this metric is impossible to calculate historically. It may lag behind individual adoption signals.

**2. Weekly Active Users Completing a "Value Loop" (Create + Invite + Collaborate)**
*Definition:* Users who not only create a project but also invite a cross-departmental member and receive a response/action within 7 days.
*   **Pros:** Measures realized value rather than just intent (creation). It captures the successful *activation* of the collaboration, which is likely a stronger leading indicator of renewal than creation alone. It forces the product to optimize the entire flow, not just the entry point.
*   **Cons:** Complex to instrument if you do not currently track the linkage between "invite sent," "invite accepted," and "subsequent action" as a single funnel. High variance; small friction points in the invite flow could tank the metric even if overall value is high.

**3. Percentage of Teams with >3 Active Cross-Departmental Projects (Retention Cohort Metric)**
*Definition:* The proportion of active teams maintaining a backlog of more than three ongoing cross-departmental projects.
*   **Pros:** Strongly correlates with "habit" and deep integration. Teams with multiple active projects are likely too embedded to churn, making this a powerful predictor of the 88% renewal rate. It shifts focus from acquisition (new projects) to depth of usage (sustained projects).
*   **Cons:** A lagging indicator; it takes time to build up to three projects. It may not react quickly enough to guide weekly engineering sprints. Requires historical data to establish what "normal" looks like for your 450 teams.

**Critical Data Gaps Preventing Certainty**
You cannot yet determine which metric best predicts retention because of two specific instrumentation failures:

2.  **No User-to-Renewal Linkage:** Your renewal rate is an aggregate (88%). Unless you can join individual or team-level activity logs to specific contract renewal outcomes, you cannot run a correlation analysis to prove that "cross-departmental creation" drives renewals more than "message volume."
