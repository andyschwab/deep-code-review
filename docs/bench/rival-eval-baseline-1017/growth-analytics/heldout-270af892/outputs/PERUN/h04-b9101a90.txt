### Diagnostic Plan: Acquisition Quality vs. Onboarding Friction

Your hypothesis that new traffic sources are lowering user quality is plausible, but the flat retention rate despite a 35% signup surge suggests a specific leak in the activation funnel rather than a general quality issue. To distinguish between "low-quality users" and "broken onboarding for new segments," execute the following three data slices immediately. Do not increase ad spend until these are resolved; pouring acquisition into a leaky bucket wastes capital.

**1. Segment Retention by Traffic Source (The Quality Test)**
Aggregate retention masks source-specific performance. Break down your Week-4 retention rate by `acquisition_channel` and `campaign_id` for the last six weeks compared to the prior baseline.
*   **What to look for:** If specific new channels show significantly lower retention (e.g., <15%) while organic or legacy paid channels remain stable (~22% or higher), your hypothesis is correct: those specific sources are driving low-intent users.
*   **Action:** If confirmed, pause or recalibrate bids on the underperforming channels immediately. Do not blame the product; blame the match between the ad promise and the product reality.

**2. Activation Funnel Drop-off by Cohort (The Onboarding Test)**
Define your "core action" (the specific event that correlates with long-term retention, e.g., "created first task" or "completed onboarding checklist"). Compare the conversion rate from `Signup` → `Core Action` for the **New Cohort** (last 6 weeks) vs. the **Old Cohort** (previous 6 weeks).
*   **What to look for:**
    *   **Scenario A:** If the New Cohort converts to the core action at the same rate as the Old Cohort but still churns by Week 4, the issue is **long-term value/fit** (supporting your "low quality" theory).
    *   **Scenario B:** If the New Cohort drops off significantly *before* hitting the core action (e.g., during onboarding steps), the issue is **friction**. The new traffic might have different expectations or device profiles that your current onboarding flow doesn't accommodate.
*   **Action:** If Scenario B, audit the onboarding steps for the specific devices or referrers dominating the new traffic.

**3. Time-to-Value Analysis (The Engagement Speed Test)**
Measure the time elapsed between `Signup` and `Core Action` for both cohorts.
*   **What to look for:** If new users take 2x longer to reach the core action than historical users, they are losing interest before experiencing value. This often happens when marketing attracts users who don't understand the product's primary use case.
*   **Action:** If time-to-value has increased, simplify the path to the core action for new signups or adjust ad creative to set clearer expectations.

**This Week's Steps:**
1.  **Query:** Run the retention-by-source report to isolate underperforming campaigns.
2.  **Instrument:** Verify that the `Core Action` event is firing correctly for all new traffic sources (ensure no tracking gaps).
3.  **Decide:** Pause any channel with Week-4 retention <15% until creative or landing page alignment is fixed.

**Note:** Retention is the leading indicator; acquisition is the lagging result. Fix the leak before turning up the tap.
**Note:** Ensure user identifiers are pseudonymous and consistent across sessions to accurately track these cohorts without violating privacy compliance.
