### Testing Strategy: Low-Traffic Onboarding Redesign

**Recommendation:** Do not use a higher significance threshold (e.g., p < 0.10) to speed up results; this directly increases false positives, risking a pivot to a feature that does not actually improve value. Instead, adopt a **Group Sequential Design** with pre-committed interim looks. This allows you to stop early for a clear win or loss without inflating error rates, balancing your November 1 deadline with statistical rigor.

**1. The Design: Group Sequential Testing**
Since you cannot wait for a fixed sample size calculated for a single final look, pre-commit to a schedule of **three specific analysis points** (e.g., every 2 weeks). At each look, apply a stricter significance boundary (alpha spending) than the standard 0.05.
*   **Look 1 (Week 2):** Require very strong evidence (e.g., p < 0.01) to stop. This prevents "peeking" errors when variance is high.
*   **Look 2 (Week 4):** Moderate boundary (e.g., p < 0.025).
*   **Look 3 (Week 6 - Final):** Standard boundary (p < 0.05) if the test runs to completion.

This approach mathematically preserves your overall false-positive rate at 5% while offering the option to decide in 2–4 weeks if the effect is large.

**2. Metrics and Guardrails**
*   **Primary Metric:** Activation Rate (completion of the new 2-step setup). This is your single source of truth for the hypothesis.
*   **Guardrail Metric:** Day-7 Retention. A simpler setup might increase activation but attract lower-quality users who churn faster. If Activation rises but Day-7 Retention drops significantly, the result is a "No-Go" regardless of the primary metric's p-value.
*   **Baseline:** Use your current 5-step flow data as the control. Do not invent industry benchmarks for "good" activation; compare only against your own historical baseline.

**3. Sample Size Reality Check**
With 400 signups/week (approx. 57/day), detecting a 15% relative lift requires careful power management.
*   If your current activation is ~20%, a 15% lift moves it to 23%.
*   A standard fixed-horizon test might require ~6,000 users per variant (too slow).
*   However, if the redesign delivers a **larger effect** (e.g., 25%+ lift due to removing friction), the sequential design will likely trigger a stop at Look 1 or 2. If the effect is truly only 15%, you may not reach significance by November 1. **Accept this risk:** it is better to have an inconclusive result than a false positive.

**4. Execution Plan for Head of Product**
*   **Pre-Commitment:** Document the exact dates of the three looks and the alpha spending function before launching. No deviations allowed.
*   **Decision Rule:**
    *   **Go:** Primary metric crosses the sequential boundary AND guardrail is stable.
    *   **No-Go:** Primary metric crosses the boundary for futility (statistically impossible to win) OR guardrail degrades.
    *   **Extend/Pivot:** If no boundary is crossed by Nov 1, the result is `UNVERIFIED`. Do not ship based on "trending positive." Either extend the test (delaying other features) or shelve the redesign.

**Trade-off Summary:** Speed is purchased by accepting that you will only detect *large* effects quickly. Small effects (like exactly 15%) may remain inconclusive by your deadline. This is the cost of low traffic; attempting to force a decision via relaxed thresholds creates technical debt in the form of broken features.
