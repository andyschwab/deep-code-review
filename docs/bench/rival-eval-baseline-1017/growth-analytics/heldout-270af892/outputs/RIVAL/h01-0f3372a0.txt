### North Star Metric Analysis for Acme Connect

Based on your hypothesis that cross-departmental collaboration drives stickiness, here are three candidate North Star metrics to evaluate for the upcoming fiscal year.

#### 1. Weekly Active Collaborative Projects (WACP)
**Definition:** The count of unique projects created in the last 7 days that include members from at least two distinct departments within the same enterprise team.

*   **Pros:** Directly validates your hypothesis regarding cross-departmental value. It filters out "noise" activity (like single-user notes or intra-team chats) that may not drive renewal. It is a clear leading indicator of platform adoption depth.
*   **Cons:** Requires robust departmental metadata which may currently be missing. It ignores the *quality* or *activity level* of the project after creation; a dormant project counts the same as an active one.

#### 2. Cross-Departmental Interaction Density
**Definition:** The average number of meaningful interactions (messages, file shares, task completions) per week between users of different departments within active enterprise accounts.

*   **Pros:** Measures ongoing engagement rather than just setup. It captures the "habit" formation across silos, which is likely the true driver of the 88% renewal rate. It scales with account size naturally.
*   **Cons:** Computationally complex to define and track without event-level granularity. "Meaningful interaction" is hard to define without risking vanity metrics (e.g., counting emoji reactions equally to file uploads).

#### 3. Enterprise Network Breadth Score
**Definition:** The percentage of an enterprise customer's total departments that have actively collaborated on at least one project in the last 30 days.

*   **Pros:** Aligns perfectly with B2B expansion and retention; if more departments are involved, the churn risk drops significantly. It shifts focus from individual user activity to organizational penetration.
*   **Cons:** Lags behind immediate user behavior (30-day window). It may not reflect the intensity of usage, only the breadth, potentially missing deep usage within a few key departments.

### Recommendation & Data Gaps

**Primary Recommendation:** **Weekly Active Collaborative Projects (WACP)** is the strongest immediate candidate. It is the most actionable proxy for your hypothesis: if teams are building cross-functional projects, they are deriving unique value that single-department tools cannot provide. It is easier to engineer and explain to squads than a complex density score.

**Critical Tracking Gaps Preventing Certainty:**
1.  **Missing Departmental Metadata:** You cannot currently verify "cross-departmental" activity because user profiles likely lack verified department tags. Without this, you cannot distinguish a high-value cross-functional project from a standard team chat.
2.  **Event Granularity:** You track "total messages" but likely lack event properties linking specific actions to specific project IDs and participant lists. You need to implement `project_created` and `member_added` events with user attributes to reconstruct the collaboration graph.
3.  **Cohort Correlation:** You have not yet segmented renewal rates by *how* accounts use the product. You need to correlate the 12% churn against specific usage patterns (e.g., do churned accounts have zero cross-departmental projects?) to statistically validate the hypothesis before locking in the North Star.

*Action Item: Prioritize implementing departmental tagging and project-level event tracking immediately to validate WACP against historical renewal data before January 1.*
