# Diagnostic Plan: TaskMaster Retention Flatline

**Objective:** Determine if flat week-four retention (22%) amidst a 35% signup surge is caused by lower-quality traffic or a segmented onboarding failure.

### 1. Isolate the "New Traffic" Cohort
Do not analyze all 8,500 new accounts as one block. The aggregate 22% rate likely masks a sharp divide between legacy and new users.
*   **Action:** Split the last six weeks of signups into two distinct cohorts: **Pre-Surge** (baseline traffic) and **Surge** (the new 35% volume).
*   **Comparison:** Calculate week-four retention separately for each.
    *   *If Surge Cohort is ~22% and Pre-Surge is ~22%:* The issue is universal; retention has degraded for everyone, suggesting a product change or seasonal factor, not traffic quality.
    *   *If Surge Cohort is <15% and Pre-Surge is >30%:* Your hypothesis is correct; the new traffic is the drag. Proceed to step 2.

### 2. Map the Drop-Off Curve (Day 0 to Day 28)
Aggregate week-four data hides *when* users leave. You need daily resolution to distinguish between "wrong audience" and "broken onboarding."
*   **Action:** Plot a daily retention curve for the **Surge Cohort** vs. **Pre-Surge Cohort**.
*   **Diagnosis:**
    *   **Immediate Cliff (Day 0–2):** If the Surge cohort drops off significantly before completing onboarding, the issue is **User Quality** or **Message Mismatch**. The ads are promising something the app doesn't deliver, or attracting non-target users.
    *   **Gradual Decay (Day 3–14):** If they finish onboarding but fail to return after the first week, the issue is **Value Realization**. They signed up correctly but didn't find the "aha" moment in the core features.
    *   **Sudden Drop at Specific Step:** Overlay event completion rates. If 80% of Pre-Surge users complete "Create First Task" but only 40% of Surge users do, the friction is in that specific onboarding step for the new demographic.

### 3. Slice by Acquisition Source & Campaign
"New traffic" is rarely uniform. One bad channel can skew the whole average.
*   **Action:** Break down the Surge Cohort by `utm_source` and `utm_medium`.
*   **Comparison:** Calculate week-four retention for each distinct channel.
    *   *Identify the Outlier:* Look for channels with high volume but retention <10%. These are your budget drains.
    *   *Validate Quality:* Compare the "Time to First Value" (e.g., time from signup to first task created) across channels. Low-quality traffic often exhibits high latency to value or zero value events.

### 4. Segment by User Attributes (If Available)
If the new campaigns targeted a different persona (e.g., "Enterprise Managers" vs. "Freelancers"), their natural retention baselines may differ.
*   **Action:** Segment the Surge Cohort by any captured attribute (role, company size, platform).
*   **Comparison:** Compare retention of new users in Segment A vs. historical users in Segment A.
    *   *Result:* If new "Freelancers" retain worse than old "Freelancers," the traffic quality is down. If new "Managers" retain normally but make up a larger % of the mix, the flat aggregate is just a composition change, not a failure.

### Recommendation for Ad Spend
*   **Pause/Reduce:** Any channel where the drop-off occurs at Day 0–2 (wrong audience).
*   **Optimize:** Any channel where drop-off occurs at Day 3–7 (fix the onboarding flow for that specific segment before cutting spend).
*   **Scale:** Channels matching or exceeding the Pre-Surge baseline retention.

*Note: Ensure your observation windows match; do not calculate retention for cohorts that have not yet reached day 28. Label incomplete cohorts as "pending" to avoid false negatives.*
