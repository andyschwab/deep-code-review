**Recommendation: Do not implement universal price increases or segmented pricing yet. Treat these results as a strong signal for hypothesis generation, not a directive for immediate rollout.**

The divergent results you observed are statistically plausible given your sample size, but acting on them immediately carries significant risk. Here is the interpretation and strategic implication for each path:

**1. Universal Price Increase (Roll out to everyone)**
*   **Implication:** This is the highest-risk option. While the 35–50 segment showed a 5% lift, the 18–24 segment's 18% drop suggests high elasticity. If the 18–24 group represents a substantial portion of your Lifetime Value (LTV) or future retention base, a universal hike could erode long-term revenue despite short-term neutrality.
*   **Verdict:** **Reject.** The net neutral revenue masks potential churn risks in a key demographic. A universal hike assumes homogeneous price sensitivity, which your data explicitly disproves.

**2. Immediate Segmented Pricing (Age-based rates)**
*   **Implication:** While theoretically sound (price discrimination based on elasticity), implementing this based on a single test is premature.
    *   **Statistical Risk:** With 10,000 users per segment, an 18% drop is likely significant, but a 5% lift is often within the margin of error (noise) unless your baseline conversion is very high. You risk building complex billing infrastructure for a gain that might disappear upon re-testing.
    *   **Perception Risk:** Explicit age-based pricing can trigger fairness backlash ("price discrimination") if discovered, damaging brand trust.
*   **Verdict:** **Pause.** Validate the 35–50 lift with a dedicated follow-up test before engineering a segmented solution.

**3. Keep Prices Flat (Status Quo)**
*   **Implication:** This preserves current revenue and avoids alienating the price-sensitive younger cohort. It buys time to investigate *why* the older segment responded positively. Was it truly "quality signaling," or did the higher price filter out low-intent users, artificially inflating the conversion rate of the remaining pool?
*   **Verdict:** **Adopt as interim strategy.** Maintain current pricing while designing a validation experiment.

**Strategic Next Steps for the VP of Monetization:**

1.  **Validate the Upside:** Run a targeted A/B test *only* on the 35–50 demographic with a larger sample size to confirm if the 5% lift is real and sustainable. Do not mix segments in this test.
2.  **Analyze Cohort Quality:** Investigate if the 5% higher conversion in the older segment led to higher retention or just higher initial signups. If the higher price attracted better customers, the strategy shifts toward premium positioning rather than simple price hiking.
3.  **Explore Indirect Segmentation:** Instead of explicit age-gating, consider bundling strategies (e.g., "Premium Plus" with extra features at a higher price point). This allows price-sensitive users to stay on the base plan while capturing surplus value from less sensitive users without the reputational risk of age-based pricing.

**Conclusion:** Your data successfully identified a segmentation opportunity, but it did not quantify the reliability of the opportunity. Prioritize validation over implementation.
