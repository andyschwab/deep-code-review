### Pre-Launch Evaluation Framework: High-Stakes Health Translation

**1. Harm Inventory & Risk Stratification**
Before testing, classify every notice by risk. "High-risk" includes dosage instructions, eligibility criteria, and contraindications. "Medium-risk" includes location/timing. "Low-risk" includes general background.
*   **Action:** Tag all source content. High-risk items automatically trigger the strictest evaluation gates below. No high-risk content ships without passing these specific probes.

**2. Adversarial "Red-Team" Probe Sets (Measure)**
Since you cannot hire native speakers for full review, build automated and semi-automated evals targeting specific failure modes known in medical translation:
*   **The "False Friend" Probe:** Test known medical false cognates (e.g., words that look similar in the target language but mean something dangerous or opposite). Create a "known-bad" control set where the model *must* fail if it translates literally.
*   **The "Negation Flip" Probe:** Input sentences with critical negations ("Do *not* take if..."). Verify the output preserves the negation. A flipped negation is an immediate stop condition.
*   **The "Unit & Number" Stress Test:** Inject varied dosage formats (mg, ml, drops, frequency). Any deviation in numbers or units between source and target is a critical failure.
*   **Cultural Urgency Check:** Use back-translation (Target → English via a *different* model) to check if the tone shifts from "urgent directive" to "suggestion." If the back-translated intent differs significantly from the source, flag for human review.

**3. The Human-in-the-Loop Gate (Manage)**
You cannot review everything, but you must gate the irreversible actions.
*   **Rule:** Fully automated publishing is **forbidden** for High-Risk content.
*   **Mechanism:** Implement a "Confidence Tier" system.
    *   *Tier 1 (Verified):* Passes all automated probes + matches known safe patterns. Requires spot-check by a generalist (non-native) speaker who can verify number/unit consistency using visual comparison tools.
    *   *Tier 2 (Unverified/Flagged):* Fails any probe or contains complex eligibility logic. **STOP.** These must be held for emergency human review, even if it delays the pilot. Do not ship Tier 2 automatically.
*   **Uncertainty Display:** If a translation is generated but not fully vetted, the interface must display a clear warning: "Machine-translated. Critical medical decisions should be verified with a health worker." Do not show a percentage confidence (e.g., "95% accurate"); use coarse, defined tiers like "Verified" or "Needs Review."

**4. Transparency Artifact (Model Card)**
Create a one-page "Translation Limitations Card" for your internal team and partners. It must list:
*   **Intended Use:** Public health notices only; not for individual diagnosis.
*   **Known Gaps:** Explicitly list which language pairs lack native speaker validation.
*   **Residual Risk:** State clearly that cultural nuance and idiomatic medical terms are `UNVERIFIED` for specific low-resource languages.
*   **Owner:** Name the specific team member responsible for the final "Go/No-Go" on each language pair.

**5. Legal & Ethical Routing**
*   **Disclosure:** Whether you are *legally required* to disclose machine translation in these specific jurisdictions is a legal determination. Route this immediately to counsel or your `business-ops` lead. Do not assume silence is safe.
*   **Regulatory Duty:** If these notices constitute regulated medical advice in the target regions, autonomous translation may be prohibited. Confirm this with legal before the pilot launches.

**Definition of Done for Pilot:**
1.  Harm inventory completed for all pilot languages.
2.  Automated probe suite running with "stop conditions" defined for negation flips and unit errors.
3.  High-risk content blocked from auto-publishing; routed to a human gate.
4.  Limitations Card published internally; no metric fabricated as "100% accurate."

**Note:** This framework manages risk; it does not certify safety. If a probe fails, the system must halt, not average the score.
**Note:** Prioritize getting native speaker validation for the single highest-volume language pair post-pilot to close the largest gap.
