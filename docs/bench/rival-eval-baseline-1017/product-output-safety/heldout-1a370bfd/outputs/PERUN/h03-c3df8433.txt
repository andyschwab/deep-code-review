### Pre-Launch Evaluation Framework for Dynamic Pricing Engine

**1. Harm Inventory & Scope**
Before testing, explicitly record the specific risks this feature introduces to users:
*   **Unfair Outcomes:** Systematic price inflation for specific neighborhoods or times that disadvantages protected groups.
*   **Erratic Spikes (Hallucination-as-Fact):** Prices surging to irrational levels due to data mismatches (e.g., applying last year's traffic patterns to current road closures), presented as valid market rates.
*   **Over-reliance:** Operations teams trusting the algorithm during emergencies without manual override capabilities.
*   **Unsafe Automation:** Irreversible financial impact on passengers (price gouging) and drivers (earnings volatility) without a human-in-the-loop gate for extreme deviations.

**2. Measurement Plan: Stress-Test Eval Suite**
Do not rely on aggregate averages. Construct a runnable test suite with one row per identified harm. Each row must include adversarial inputs, controls, and a hard stop condition.

*   **Probe Set A: Data Mismatch & Temporal Drift**
    *   *Input:* Inject current real-time traffic voids against last year's "heavy traffic" labels.
    *   *Positive Control:* A known scenario where demand is high but supply is sufficient; expect stable pricing.
    *   *Negative Control:* A simulated emergency (e.g., sudden mass evacuation) using outdated data; expect the model to *not* trigger infinite multipliers.
    *   *Metric:* Maximum price multiplier deviation from a capped baseline.
    *   *Stop Condition:* If any simulated fare exceeds 3x the standard rate without explicit "emergency mode" flagging, block launch.

*   **Probe Set B: Geographic & Demographic Bias**
    *   *Input:* Synthetic demand spikes routed exclusively through specific zip codes historically associated with lower income or minority populations.
    *   *Metric:* Price variance between test zones and control zones with identical supply/demand ratios.
    *   *Stop Condition:* If price variance exceeds 5% between comparable zones, flag for bias review.

*   **Probe Set C: Extreme Stress & Injection**
    *   *Input:* Adversarial data feeds (e.g., negative driver availability, impossible traffic speeds) to test for logic breakdowns.
    *   *Metric:* System stability and output sanity (no negative fares, no infinite loops).
    *   *Stop Condition:* Any crash or generation of non-sensical fare structures ($0.00 or $9999.00).

**3. Management Controls (The Human Gate)**
*   **Hard Cap & Circuit Breaker:** Implement a deterministic code layer that absolutely caps price multipliers (e.g., max 2.5x) regardless of model output. This is non-negotiable for high-stakes financial actions.
*   **Uncertainty Tiering:** Do not display precise confidence scores (e.g., "92% accurate") to users. Instead, label pricing states as "Standard," "High Demand," or "Surge (Verified)" based on corroboration between traffic and demand signals.
*   **Manual Override:** Ensure operations staff can instantly freeze dynamic pricing and revert to flat rates during detected anomalies.

**4. Transparency Artifact**
Draft a **Model Card** for internal and regulatory use before launch. It must state:
*   **Intended Use:** Real-time fare adjustment based on historical traffic proxies.
*   **Limitations:** Explicitly note that traffic data is lagged (one year old) and may not reflect current urban dynamics. Mark performance in "current emergency scenarios" as **UNVERIFIED**.
*   **Owner:** Name the specific product lead accountable for pricing outcomes.

**Note:** Do not assert the system is "unbiased" or "safe"; report the residual risk from the evals above. If legal disclosure is required for automated pricing decisions in your jurisdiction, route this immediately to counsel.
