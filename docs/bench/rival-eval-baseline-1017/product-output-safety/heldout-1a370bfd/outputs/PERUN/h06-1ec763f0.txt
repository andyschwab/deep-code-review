### Pre-Launch Fairness Review Workflow

**1. Construct a Harm Inventory**
Before testing, document specific risks:
*   **Bias/Unfair Outcomes:** Does the model disproportionately flag claims from specific demographics or regions (e.g., areas prone to natural disasters) due to missing context in anonymized data?
*   **Hallucination as Fact:** Are holds placed based on spurious correlations rather than verified fraud indicators?
*   **Unsafe Automation:** The automatic hold is a high-stakes, reversible-but-harmful action. Define the "worst realistic case" (e.g., a disaster victim unable to access funds for temporary housing).

**2. Design Runnable Evaluation Tests**
Create one test row per identified harm. Do not rely on aggregate accuracy.
*   **Probe Sets:** Construct synthetic test cases representing known edge cases (e.g., "legitimate disaster claim with atypical timing") since historical data lacks this context. Include positive controls (known fraud) and negative controls (known legitimate).
*   **Subgroup Slicing:** Run the model against these probes sliced by available demographic proxies and claim types.
*   **Metrics & Thresholds:** Define a pass threshold for false positive rates *per subgroup*. If the false positive rate for any subgroup exceeds the owner-set threshold, the launch is blocked.
*   **Stop Condition:** Explicitly state: "If false positives for disaster-related claims exceed X%, the automatic hold feature is disabled, and human review is mandated."

**3. Implement a Human-in-the-Loop Gate**
Because an incorrect hold causes significant hardship, full autonomous execution is unsafe given the data limitations.
*   **Mandatory Review:** Route all flags generated by the model to a human adjuster for confirmation before funds are held, or at minimum, for any claim falling into high-risk categories identified in your inventory.
*   **Override Mechanism:** Ensure adjusters can easily reverse a hold with a single click and record the reason.

**4. Generate Transparency Artifacts**
Draft a **Model Card** and **Data Card** for internal and regulatory review.
*   **Intended Use & Limitations:** Explicitly state that the model was trained on anonymized data lacking disaster context, and therefore has known limitations in those scenarios.
*   **Measured Performance:** Report fairness metrics by subgroup. If a metric was not measured (e.g., impact on a specific demographic due to data absence), label it **UNVERIFIED**. Do not invent a "0% bias" figure.
*   **Owner & Version:** List the product owner and model version.

**5. Governance and Legal Routing**
*   **Ownership:** Assign a specific individual as the owner of the "false positive" risk.
*   **Regulatory Duty:** You mentioned a regulatory requirement for fairness. **Route the specific legal definition of "fairness" and the sufficiency of your evidence to legal counsel.** Do not assert internally that you are compliant; let counsel validate that your testing meets the regulatory standard.

**6. Final Decision**
Present the harm inventory, the test results (including any UNVERIFIED areas), and the proposed human gate to leadership. The decision to ship rests with the owner, acknowledging the residual risk of false positives due to data constraints.

***

*Note: Never certify the model as "unbiased" or "safe"; only report measured residual risks. If data gaps prevent measuring fairness for a specific group, explicitly mark those findings as UNVERIFIED rather than assuming neutrality.*
