### Internal Test Plan for Contract-Clause Summarizer

To identify dangerous divergences between original legal text and your plain-English summaries without external consultants, execute the following five targeted test suites using your existing team. Focus on "false negatives" (missing risks) rather than stylistic imperfections.

**1. The "Absolute Liability" Stress Test**
*   **Objective:** Detect oversimplification of unlimited financial exposure.
*   **Input Clauses:** Insert clauses containing "indemnify against all claims," "joint and several liability," or "consequential damages."
*   **Expected Failure Mode:** The summary suggests "pay for damages" without highlighting that the user could be liable for *third-party* lawsuits or amounts exceeding the contract value.
*   **Pass Criteria:** The summary must explicitly flag that liability is uncapped or extends beyond direct losses. If the summary says "you pay for mistakes" instead of "you pay for *any* lawsuit filed against the other party," mark as critical fail.

**2. The "Termination Trap" Scenario**
*   **Objective:** Ensure notice periods and automatic renewal risks are preserved.
*   **Input Clauses:** Use terms like "auto-renew for 24 months unless cancelled 90 days prior" or "termination for convenience requires 6-month notice."
*   **Expected Failure Mode:** The summary simplifies this to "you can cancel anytime" or "renews automatically," omitting the specific, restrictive window.
*   **Pass Criteria:** The summary must include the specific number of days required for notice and the consequence of missing that window (e.g., "locked in for another year").

**3. The "Waiver of Rights" Check**
*   **Objective:** Verify that loss of legal recourse is not softened.
*   **Input Clauses:** Include mandatory arbitration clauses, class-action waivers, or "governing law in [foreign jurisdiction]" stipulations.
*   **Expected Failure Mode:** The summary states "disputes settled out of court" without clarifying that the user *cannot* sue in their local court or join a class action.
*   **Pass Criteria:** The output must use strong warning language (e.g., "You give up your right to a jury trial" or "You cannot join a group lawsuit").

**4. The "Ambiguity Injection" Test (Adversarial)**
*   **Objective:** Test how the model handles intentionally vague but dangerous legalese.
*   **Input Clauses:** Create clauses with broad definitions, such as "Confidential Information includes any data deemed sensitive by Discloser."
*   **Expected Failure Mode:** The summary treats this as a standard confidentiality clause without highlighting that the *other party* unilaterally decides what is secret.
*   **Pass Criteria:** The summary must identify the power imbalance, noting that the definition is subjective and controlled solely by the counterparty.

**5. The "Cross-Clause Contradiction" Review**
*   **Objective:** Ensure the summarizer reads the whole context, not just isolated sentences.
*   **Input Setup:** Provide a contract where Clause 5 says "No liability for data loss" but Clause 12 says "Provider guarantees 99.9% uptime and data integrity."
*   **Expected Failure Mode:** The summary treats these independently, missing the contradiction that leaves the user unprotected despite the guarantee.
*   **Pass Criteria:** The summary must flag the conflict, explicitly stating that the liability waiver may override the performance guarantee.

**Execution Protocol:**
Have two team members independently review each output against the original text using a "Red Flag" checklist: Did the summary remove a number? Did it change a conditional ("if") to a definitive statement? Did it soften a mandatory requirement ("must") to a suggestion ("should")? Any instance where the summary makes a risk sound smaller than the original text is an immediate block for launch.

*Note: This plan relies on your team's ability to recognize the legal significance of the input clauses; consider using publicly available "bad contract" datasets for unbiased inputs.*
*Ensure all test data is synthetic or properly redacted to maintain client confidentiality.*
