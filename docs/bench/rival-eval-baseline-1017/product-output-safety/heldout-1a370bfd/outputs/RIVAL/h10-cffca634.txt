**Pre-Launch Evaluation Framework for GlobalHealth Comms**

Given the critical nature of dosage and eligibility instructions and the constraint of limited native speaker access, this framework prioritizes **risk-based triage** and **structural validation** over comprehensive fluency checks. The goal is to filter out catastrophic failures before human review of a reduced, high-priority subset.

**1. Content Stratification & Risk Tagging**
Do not treat all text equally. Categorize every source sentence into three tiers:
*   **Tier 1 (Critical):** Dosage amounts, frequencies, eligibility criteria (ages/conditions), contraindications, and emergency actions. *Requirement: 100% verification.*
*   **Tier 2 (High):** Locations, times, appointment mechanisms, and required documents. *Requirement: Spot check.*
*   **Tier 3 (Standard):** Contextual background, reassurance, and general health information. *Requirement: Automated pass only.*

**2. The "Back-Translation" Stress Test (Automated)**
Run all Tier 1 and Tier 2 content through an automated back-translation loop (Source → Target → Source) using a different engine than your primary translator if possible.
*   **Flagging Logic:** Automatically flag any segment where the back-translated English differs semantically from the original English.
*   **Numeric Integrity Check:** Implement a script to extract all numbers, units (mg, ml), and ages from the source and the target translation. If the values do not match exactly (accounting for unit conversions), flag as a **Critical Failure**. This catches dosage errors without needing language fluency.

**3. Synthetic "Adversarial" Persona Review**
Since you cannot hire eight native speakers, simulate them using advanced AI personas with specific constraints. Prompt your AI reviewer: *"Act as a skeptical community health worker in [Target Region] who speaks only [Target Language] and has low health literacy. Read this translation. Identify any instructions that are confusing, culturally offensive, or sound like magic/folklore rather than medicine. Specifically, look for passive voice that obscures who must take action."*
*   Focus this review on **cultural appropriateness** and **tone** (e.g., ensuring urgency is conveyed without causing panic).

**4. The "Blind Spot" Human Sprint**
Reserve your limited budget for a targeted "Blind Spot" review.
*   Extract only the **Tier 1** segments that triggered flags in steps 2 or 3.
*   Hire native speakers *only* for these specific high-risk fragments across your language pairs.
*   Provide them with a binary task: "Is this instruction safe to follow? Yes/No." Do not ask for polishing; ask for safety validation.

**5. Failure Mode Simulation**
Before launch, manually inject known errors into a test batch (e.g., swap "twice daily" with "twice weekly", change "eligible" to "ineligible"). Run your framework to ensure it catches these specific injected faults. If your automated filters miss these, do not launch.

**Deliverable Summary:**
This approach shifts your resource allocation from "reviewing everything poorly" to "ignoring the safe 80% and rigorously validating the dangerous 20%." It relies on deterministic checks for numbers (the highest risk) and targeted AI simulation for cultural nuance, reserving human expertise solely for adjudicating flagged critical errors.

*Note: This framework assumes access to standard LLM APIs for the persona simulation and back-translation steps. Ensure all data handling complies with your privacy mandates regarding public health information.*
