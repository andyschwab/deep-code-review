**The Arithmetic (Lane A)**

We compare two scenarios based on your provided figures: 2,000 units, $8 shipping per unit, and the new COGS of $14.50.

*Scenario 1: Absorb Cost (Price $45)*

*   Less COGS: $14.50
*   Less Shipping: $8.00
*   **Contribution Margin per unit:** $22.50
*   **Total Net Profit (2,000 units):** $45,000
*   *Note:* This assumes volume stays at 2,000 despite the margin squeeze limiting your marketing reach.

*Scenario 2: Raise Price (Price $52)*
*   Revenue per unit: $52
*   Less COGS: $14.50
*   Less Shipping: $8.00
*   **Contribution Margin per unit:** $29.50
*   **Total Net Profit (at 2,000 units):** $59,000

**The Break-Even Analysis (The Flip Input)**
The critical unknown is how many sales you lose due to the $7 increase. To match the $45,000 profit from Scenario 1, you only need to sell **1,526 units** at the $52 price point ($45,000 ÷ $29.50).

This means you can afford to lose up to **474 backers (23.7% of your projected volume)** and still generate the same profit as the lower-price scenario. If the price hike causes a drop of less than 24%, raising the price is the superior financial move. Given that board game backers on Kickstarter often pledge for perceived value and exclusivity rather than lowest cost, a 15.5% price increase ($7 on $45) resulting in a >24% volume collapse is a high-risk assumption without data.

**Decision Aid**
1.  **Read:** At the current cost structure, the $52 price point creates a $7.00 higher margin per unit, requiring significantly fewer sales to hit profit targets.
2.  **Formula:** `Max Acceptable Volume Drop = 1 - (Old Margin / New Margin)` → `1 - (22.50 / 29.50) = 23.7%`.
3.  **Threshold:** If you believe the price hike will reduce pledges by more than 24%, do not raise the price. If you believe the drop will be less than 24%, raise the price.
4.  **Next Step:** Before locking the price, run a quick "Van Westendorp" style check on your email list or social media: ask potential backers at what price the game becomes "too expensive." If the majority say a number higher than $52, proceed with the hike. If many balk below $50, consider a "Super Early Bird" at $45 (limited quantity) to gauge elasticity before setting the standard tier at $52.

Regarding the alternative of reducing the marketing budget: this is generally dangerous for a Kickstarter launch, as visibility drives the algorithmic momentum required to reach your 2,000-unit goal. Reducing spend lowers the probability of hitting *any* volume target, whereas a price increase only risks volume if elasticity is high.
