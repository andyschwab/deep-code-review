**Verdict:** Proceed with caution; your current reserves are sufficient to bridge the next 45-day gap even if 40% of customers choose the installment plan, but you will operate with a razor-thin safety margin that leaves no room for error.

**The Arithmetic**
To determine if you can fund the next $18,000 material run within 45 days, we must compare your projected cash inflows against your starting balance of $22,500.

*   **Current State (Baseline):** You sell frames at $2,400 upfront. To trigger a production run, you need to sell roughly 7.5 frames ($18,000 / $2,400) to replenish materials immediately.
*   **Proposed State (40% Installment Adoption):**
    *   **Upfront Cash per Frame:** If 60% pay full price ($2,400) and 40% pay the first installment ($800), your weighted average cash collection per order drops to $1,760.
        *   Calculation: $(0.60 \times 2400) + (0.40 \times 800) = 1440 + 320 = 1760$.
    *   **Future Inflows:** The 40% group pays $800 in Month 2 and $800 in Month 3.

**45-Day Cash Flow Projection**
The critical risk is the timing mismatch. You need $18,000 cash on hand by Day 45.
1.  **Starting Cash:** $22,500.
2.  **Cash Outflow:** You must spend $18,000 on materials before Day 45 to maintain the 6-week cycle.
3.  **Remaining Buffer (Pre-Revenue):** $22,500 - $18,000 = **$4,500**.

You can afford to dip into your reserves by $18,000 as long as new order inflows replenish the account before the *following* batch is due. However, your specific question is whether the *delayed* cash flow drops you below the threshold *before* you can build.

Since you start with $22,500 and the batch costs $18,000, you physically have the cash to buy the materials today regardless of sales velocity. The threat is not inability to pay for the *next* batch, but rather the depletion of your operating buffer for labor and overhead during the 45-day window.

**The Flip Variable**
The input that flips this from "safe" to "dangerous" is **order volume**.
*   If you sell **10 frames** in the next 45 days under the new model:
    *   Cash In: $10 \times \$1,760$ (avg upfront) = $17,600.
    *   Net Position after Material Buy: $22,500 (start) + $17,600 (in) - $18,000 (out) = $22,100.
    *   Result: Healthy.
*   If you sell **0 frames** (worst case, demand doesn't increase):
    *   Net Position: $22,500 - $18,000 = $4,500.
    *   Result: You can build the batch, but you have only $4,500 left for labor and rent until Month 2 payments arrive.

**Decision Aid**
1.  **Read:** Your reserves cover the immediate $18,000 hit, so the strategy does not threaten the *ability* to build the next batch on time based solely on cash availability.
2.  **Formula:** $Minimum Orders Needed = (\$18,000 - \$4,500 buffer) / \$1,760 weighted avg$. You need roughly **8 new orders** in 45 days to restore your cash buffer to a safe level ($22k+).
3.  **Threshold:** If you anticipate fewer than 8 orders in 45 days, the delayed cash flow creates a liquidity crunch for labor costs, even if material costs are covered.
4.  **Next Step:** Before launching, calculate your fixed labor/overhead costs for 45 days. If those costs exceed $4,500, do not launch the plan without a line of credit, as your remaining cash after buying materials will not cover operations.
