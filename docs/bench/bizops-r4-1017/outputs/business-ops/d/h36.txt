**Financial Impact Analysis (Lane A: Arithmetic)**

Your current baseline:
*   **Annual Revenue:** 50,000 units × $12.50 = $625,000
*   **Tariff Impact:** $1.80/unit × 50,000 = **$90,000 annual cost increase.**

**Option 1: Price Increase to $14.50**
*   **New Price:** $14.50 (16% increase).
*   **Break-Even Volume Calculation:** To maintain current *revenue* ($625,000), you need: $625,000 / $14.50 = **43,104 units**.
    *   This allows a volume drop of ~13.8%.
*   **Profit Protection Calculation:** If your goal is to maintain *gross profit dollars* (assuming the $1.80 is pure margin erosion), the math tightens.
    *   Current Margin per unit (unknown total cost, but tariff is variable): Let $M$ be current margin. New Margin = $M - 1.80 + 2.00$ (price hike) = $M + 0.20$.
    *   Actually, simpler view: You need to cover the $90,000 loss.
    *   Additional margin per unit = $14.50 - $12.50 = $2.00.
    *   Units needed to cover tariff: $90,000 / $2.00 = 45,000 units.
    *   **Verdict:** You can afford to lose only **5,000 units (10%)** to break even on the tariff cost. If volume drops 15% (to 42,500 units), you will be **$5,000 worse off** than absorbing the cost, plus you risk long-term customer churn.
*   **Risk:** Specialty food items often have elastic demand above psychological price points ($15.00). A 16% hike is significant.

**Option 2: Reformulation (Local Fillers)**
*   **Cost Savings:** Potentially removes the $1.80 tariff exposure.
*   **Revenue Risk:** If "quality perception" drops, volume could fall.
*   **Break-Even Loyalty:** If reformulation saves the full $1.80 but causes a price-sensitive backlash equivalent to a 5% volume drop (2,500 units), you lose $31,250 in revenue (at $12.50/unit). Unless the cost savings exceed the lost revenue from brand damage, this is dangerous for a "specialty" brand where authenticity is the value proposition.

**Decision Framework & Next Steps**

1.  **The Flip Input:** The decision hinges entirely on **Price Elasticity of Demand**. You stated you do not know this.
    *   *Threshold:* If a 1% price increase causes >0.6% volume drop, the price hike fails.
2.  **Sensitivity Test (Do this week):** Do not change the label yet. Run an A/B test on your website or with a small retailer segment. Offer the product at $13.25 (partial pass-through) to 10% of traffic. Measure conversion rate changes.
3.  **Packaging Strategy:** Instead of raising the price of the core 100g jar, introduce a **40g "Trial/Travel" size** at a higher price-per-gram or a **Premium Reserve** (100% ghost pepper) at $18.00, keeping the standard blend at $13.25 with slightly adjusted filler. This segments your customers by willingness-to-pay.
4.  **Reformulation Guardrail:** If you must reformulate, label it transparently as "Adapted Recipe due to Global Supply Conditions" rather than hiding it. Trust damage from hidden changes is often permanent; trust damage from honest adaptation is often forgivable.

**Summary Rule of Thumb:** If your projected volume loss exceeds 10% at the new price, do not implement the full $2.00 hike. Absorb a portion of the cost or reduce pack size (shrinkflation) before risking a 15% volume collapse.
