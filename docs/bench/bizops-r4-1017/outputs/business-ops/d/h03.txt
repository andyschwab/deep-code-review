**Financial Reality Check (Lane A)**
Your current position allows for **8.0 months** of operations without new income (`€120,000 ÷ €15,000`).
*   **The Risk:** If you replace a €50,000 upfront fee with a promise of future revenue share, you lose €50,000 in immediate liquidity.
*   **The Impact:** Losing just one such deal drops your runway to **4.6 months** (`(€120k - €50k) ÷ €15k`). Losing all three leaves you unable to pay leases within 3 months.
*   **The Threshold:** You cannot afford any deal structure that delays cash receipt beyond 45 days without an alternative cash source.

**Proposed Deal Structures (Balancing Cash & Upside)**
Since you cannot accept pure equity, structure these as **debt-like instruments with revenue participation** that prioritize your cash flow:

1.  **Deferred Cash with Senior Claim:**
    *   **Structure:** The museum signs a binding obligation to pay the full €50,000 fee, deferred over 12–18 months.
    *   **Protection:** This debt is "senior," meaning it must be paid out of the *first* euros of any licensing revenue before the museum retains any profit.
    *   **Upside:** Add a 5–10% royalty on gross licensing revenue *only after* the €50,000 principal is fully repaid.
    *   **Why:** This keeps the asset on your books as receivable (potentially bankable) and ensures you get paid before the museum sees profit.

2.  **Hybrid Cash/Revenue Share:**
    *   **Structure:** Require an immediate **40% cash down payment** (€20,000) to cover your direct equipment lease costs for that specific project.
    *   **Deferral:** The remaining 60% (€30,000) is converted into a revenue share agreement.
    *   **Math:** If they hit €200k/year revenue in Year 3, a 15% share yields €30k/year. You recover your balance in one year of success, but you secure immediate survival cash.

3.  **Milestone-Based Licensing (No Equity):**
    *   **Structure:** Instead of taking equity in the museum (likely impossible), retain **ownership of the digital files** until paid in full. Grant the museum a license to use them, revocable if payments are missed.
    *   **Upside:** Charge a lower upfront fee (e.g., €25k) but retain a 20% perpetual commission on all third-party licensing deals you facilitate or they report.

**Legal Restrictions (Lane R)**
You absolutely must seek specialized Italian administrative legal counsel before signing any equity or revenue-sharing agreement with state-owned cultural assets.
*   **The Regime:** Italian public entities (museums) are governed by strict public accounting laws (*contabilità pubblica*) and the **Code of Cultural Heritage and Landscape**.
*   **The Constraint:** Public bodies generally cannot distribute "profits" or issue "equity" in state-owned cultural assets to private companies in the manner a corporation issues stock. Their revenue often must be remitted to the state treasury (*erario*) rather than distributed to private partners.
*   **The Risk:** A contract promising you a percentage of future public revenue may be deemed *ultra vires* (beyond the legal power of the official signing it) and declared void by a court, leaving you with neither cash nor rights.
*   **Action:** Do not draft a final contract until a lawyer confirms the specific museum has the statutory authority to enter into a *partnership for value sharing* rather than a simple service procurement.
