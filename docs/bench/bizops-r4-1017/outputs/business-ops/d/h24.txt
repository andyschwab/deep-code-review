**The Arithmetic (Lane A)**

Your current risk profile provides the baseline for the "Standard Tier" of your new warranty.
*   **Volume:** 120 repairs/month × 12 months = 1,440 repairs/year.
*   **Standard Claim Frequency:** 3% of 1,440 = ~43 claims/year.
*   **Standard Claim Cost:** Assuming these follow-ups are labor/minor adjustments (conservative estimate of 150 CHF/claim given your 850 CHF average bill), the annual liability is ~6,450 CHF.
*   **Reserve Per Job (Standard):** 6,450 CHF ÷ 1,440 jobs = **4.48 CHF/job**.

However, your concern is the "Catastrophic Tier" (rare part replacements up to 2,000 CHF).
*   **Current Reserve:** 40,000 CHF.
*   **Required Liquidity:** 25,000 CHF.
*   **Available for Warranty Risk:** 15,000 CHF.

If you offer an unlimited one-year warranty, a single cluster of 8 catastrophic failures (8 × 2,000 CHF) wipes out your available risk capital. Since the frequency of these events under a *doubled* warranty period (6 months → 12 months) is unknown, you cannot mathematically derive a safe "per job" reserve without inventing a failure rate.

**The Strategy: Tiered Model vs. Flat Fee**

A flat fee model forces you to price for the worst-case scenario to ensure solvency, which makes your service uncompetitive or hoards cash. A tiered model aligns price with risk:

1.  **Standard Warranty (Included in Price):** Covers labor and minor adjustments for 12 months.
    *   *Reserve Impact:* Low (~4.50 CHF/job).
2.  **Premium Component Warranty (Add-on or Capped):** Covers major part replacements.
    *   *Structure:* Either charge a separate premium (e.g., +5% of repair cost) specifically for parts coverage, or explicitly cap the liability for the standard warranty at 1,000 CHF per incident.
    *   *Benefit:* This transfers the "unknown frequency" risk. If a 2,000 CHF part fails, the warranty pays 1,000 CHF (covered by your reserve) and the customer or a separate insurance policy covers the rest, or the customer paid a specific premium that funded this risk.

**Decision Thresholds & Next Steps**

*   **Break-Even on Reserves:** If you set aside **25.50 CHF per job** (totaling ~36,720 CHF annually), you cover ~18 catastrophic events (at 2,000 CHF each) plus standard claims. This exceeds your 15,000 CHF available buffer, threatening your liquidity.
*   **The Flip Input:** The specific frequency of part failures >1,000 CHF between month 6 and month 12.
*   **Rule of Thumb:** If the projected annual payout exceeds 15,000 CHF, the flat model fails your liquidity constraint.
*   **Actionable Step:** Before launching, run a "shadow warranty" for 30 days: quote every new job with a line-item cost for "12-month parts coverage." Track the attach rate (how many customers buy it). If <20% buy it, the market signals that an unlimited flat warranty is over-priced; if >80% buy it, your risk assessment is too conservative. Use this data to set the final reserve.
