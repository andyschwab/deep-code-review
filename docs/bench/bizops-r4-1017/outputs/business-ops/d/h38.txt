Adopt a **non-refundable deposit tied to metal weight** combined with a **7-day quote validity period**. This structure directly neutralizes your specific risk (gold swings wiping out the $400 labor margin) while keeping the customer experience simple.

Here is the arithmetic applied to your numbers:
*   **Current Exposure:** Each ring uses 0.5 oz of gold. A $50/oz swing equals a **$25 cost variance per ring**.
*   **Margin Risk:** Your fixed labor fee is $400. A $25 spike represents a **6.25% erosion** of your total fee. If swings hit $80/oz (possible in volatile weeks), you lose $40 (10% of fee).
*   **The Deposit Solution:** Require a deposit equal to the current spot cost of the gold (0.5 oz × current price) plus a small buffer (e.g., 5%).
    *   *Example:* If gold is $2,000/oz, the deposit covers $1,000 of material cost.
    *   *Mechanism:* The final invoice is `Total Price - Deposit Paid`. If gold rises before you buy the metal, the customer pays the difference from their remaining balance. If gold falls, they pay less. Your $400 labor fee remains untouched because the material cost is now a pass-through variable secured by the deposit.
*   **The Validity Period:** State clearly: "Quotes are valid for 7 days." This creates a contractual boundary. If a client delays beyond 7 days, you re-quote based on the new spot price. This prevents indefinite exposure without needing complex clauses.

**Why this beats the alternatives:**
*   **Building a larger buffer into the base price:** To cover a worst-case $50 swing safely, you would need to add ~$30–$40 to every quote. On 200 orders, that is $6,000–$8,000 in extra cost to customers annually. In a competitive bespoke market, this makes you appear ~2–3% more expensive than necessary during stable weeks, potentially reducing conversion.
*   **Validity period alone:** This protects future quotes but does nothing if a client accepts today and you wait two weeks to buy the metal. You remain exposed during the production cycle.

**Decision Thresholds & Next Steps:**
*   **Rule of Thumb:** If your material cost variance exceeds 5% of your labor fee (which happens at a $20/oz swing), a fixed-price quote without a deposit is mathematically unsafe.
*   **Flip Input:** The strategy changes only if your clients demand 100% upfront payment; in that case, you can absorb the risk because you hold the cash to buy immediately.
*   **Action This Week:** Draft a one-sentence addendum to your estimate: *"A 50% non-refundable deposit is required to lock in materials. Final pricing adjusts solely based on the spot price of gold at the time of metal purchase if the deposit is not received within 7 days."* Test this on the next 5 inquiries.

This approach shifts the commodity risk back to the client (who owns the asset's value) while guaranteeing your $400 labor margin. It is standard practice in custom jewelry and metalworking to separate "material at market" from "fixed labor."

*Note: While this structure is common, specific contract language regarding non-refundable deposits and price adjustment clauses should be reviewed by a local attorney to ensure compliance with New York consumer protection laws.*
