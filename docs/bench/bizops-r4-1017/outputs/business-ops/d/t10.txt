**The Compliance Regime (Lane R)**
This is a regulatory question, not a math problem. The obligation depends on the **EU VAT e-commerce package** and specific country laws (Germany *Umsatzsteuergesetz*, France *CGI*).

1.  **The "Deemed Supplier" Rule:** For digital services and certain event tickets sold via a platform to EU consumers (B2C), the platform is often treated as the seller for VAT purposes. This means you are liable for collecting and remitting VAT on the *full ticket price*, not just your commission.
2.  **Registration Thresholds:** The EU abolished the previous distance-selling thresholds for digital services in 2021. If you sell B2C to *any* customer in the EU, you generally must account for VAT immediately. There is no "safe" volume threshold for a single event if the customers are private consumers.
    *   **Germany/France:** Both have strict enforcement. Germany requires a specific questionnaire (*Fragebogen zur steuerlichen Erfassung*) before you can legally collect. Doing it post-event without prior registration often triggers penalties ranging from 10% to 100% of the evaded tax, plus interest.
3.  **B2B vs. B2C:** If all 3,000 attendees are registered businesses providing valid VAT IDs, the reverse-charge mechanism may apply (they pay the tax, not you). If *any* are private individuals (students, general public), you likely have a collection obligation.

**Routing Instruction**
You cannot self-determine if you are a "deemed supplier" or an "agent." This requires a licensed tax adviser to review your terms of service and payment flow.

Manual post-event calculation is rarely accepted by EU authorities for B2C sales and invites audits.
