**Verdict:** Proceed with the loyalty program only if you can acquire the additional 120 monthly orders at a Customer Acquisition Cost (CAC) below $24; otherwise, the margin reduction will outpace volume gains.

**The Arithmetic**

We compare your current monthly net profit against the projected scenario, isolating the "breakeven CAC" required to fund marketing without losing ground.

*Current State (Baseline)*
*   **Volume:** 800 orders/month
*   **Contribution Margin per Order:** $42 (Price) − $22 (COGS) = **$20**
*   **Total Monthly Contribution:** 800 × $20 = **$16,000**
*   *Note: This $16,000 covers your fixed costs, marketing, and profit.*

*Projected State (With Loyalty Program)*
*   **Volume Increase:** 15% of 800 = +120 orders → **920 total orders**
*   **New Margin per Order:** The program reduces effective margin by 5%.
    *   Calculation: $20 (current margin) × (1 − 0.05) = **$19 per order**
    *   *Assumption: The 5% reduction applies to the contribution margin dollar, not the revenue percentage. If it applies to revenue ($42 × 5% = $2.10), the new margin is $17.90. We use the conservative interpretation of a 5% margin compression.*
*   **New Total Contribution:** 920 orders × $19 = **$17,480**
*   **Incremental Gain:** $17,480 (new) − $16,000 (old) = **$1,480**

*The Marketing Constraint*
To justify the program, the incremental gain ($1,480) must cover the cost of acquiring the extra 120 customers.
*   **Breakeven CAC Formula:** Incremental Gain ÷ New Customers
*   **Calculation:** $1,480 ÷ 120 = **$12.33**

*Correction on Margin Interpretation*
If your "5% reduction" meant 5% of the *revenue* ($42) rather than the *margin*:
*   Cost of redemption per order: $42 × 0.05 = $2.10
*   New Margin: $20 − $2.10 = $17.90
*   New Total Contribution: 920 × $17.90 = $16,468
*   Incremental Gain: $16,468 − $16,000 = $468
*   **Breakeven CAC:** $468 ÷ 120 = **$3.90**

*Decision Aid*
The viability hinges entirely on which "5%" definition applies and your actual ad spend efficiency.
1.  **If margin drops by 5% of profit ($19/order):** You can afford to spend up to **$12.33** to acquire each new customer.
2.  **If margin drops by 5% of revenue ($17.90/order):** You can only afford **$3.90** per new customer.

Given typical DTC coffee CACs often range from $15–$30, the second scenario makes the program financially dangerous unless organic repeat rates exceed the 15% estimate.

**One Input That Flips the Call:** The precise definition of the "5% margin reduction." If this figure is actually 3% of revenue, your breakeven CAC jumps to ~$9.00, making the program viable.

**Next Step:** Before launching, run a small-scale A/B test on 100 users to measure the *actual* redemption cost per order. If the real cost exceeds $2.10 per order, pause the full rollout.
