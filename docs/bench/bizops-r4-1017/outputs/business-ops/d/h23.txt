**The Regulatory Boundary (Lane R)**

The core conflict—whether the grant is taxable income or non-taxable capital contribution—is a matter of Canadian tax law (CRA) and Ontario provincial regulations, specifically depending on whether the Oakwood History Network is a registered charity, a non-profit organization (NPO), or a taxable corporation.

*   **The Rule:** In Canada, government grants are generally included in income under the *Income Tax Act* unless a specific exemption applies (e.g., certain capital contributions or specific programs for registered charities/NPOs). Even for NPOs, "income from property" or business-like activities can be taxable.
*   **The Risk:** If you treat the $45,000 as non-taxable and the CRA determines it is income, you could face back taxes, penalties, and interest. Conversely, setting aside tax on money you cannot spend on operations creates a cash flow crisis.
