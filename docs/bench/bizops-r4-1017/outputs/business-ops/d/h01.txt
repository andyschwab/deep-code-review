**Financial Impact Analysis**
Your current monthly revenue is approximately $54,545 (derived from $12,000 burn ÷ 22% gross margin). A 15% freight hike on an $85 order increases costs by $12.75 per order. With roughly 642 orders monthly, this adds $8,185 in monthly costs.

*   **Option A: Absorb the Cost.** Your gross margin drops from 22% to roughly 7% ($12,000 gross profit - $8,185 new cost = $3,815). Since your fixed burn is $12,000, you would operate at a net loss of ~$8,185/month. Your remaining cash (6 months × $12,000 = $72,000) would last only **8.8 months** total, but effectively less as the loss accelerates cash depletion. You would wipe out the quarter's profit immediately.
*   **Option B: Pass on the Cost.** To maintain the 22% margin, you must raise prices by ~$16 (covering the $12.75 cost plus the margin on that cost). Based on last year's elasticity (3% churn for a $5 increase), a $16 increase could risk 9–10% customer loss. Losing 10% of revenue ($5,454) hurts, but retaining 90% of customers while preserving margins keeps you near break-even or slightly profitable, extending your runway significantly compared to Option A.

**Decision Thresholds**
The flip input is **churn rate**. If a price increase causes churn greater than **14%**, absorbing the cost becomes the lesser evil mathematically (though still dangerous). Given your historical data suggests ~10% churn at the required price point, passing the cost is the arithmetic winner.
