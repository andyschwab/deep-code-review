Adopt a **Cost-Plus with Volatility Surcharge** model immediately. This structure separates your base price (covering fixed costs and target margin) from a variable line item that fluctuates with your raw material index, protecting your 12% net margin without renegotiating the entire contract when costs spike.

**The Arithmetic (Lane A)**
Your current vulnerability is that raw materials are 60% of COGS. With an $800 sales price and a 12% net margin, your profit is $96 per drum.
*   **Current Cost Structure:** If Net Margin is 12%, and assuming operating expenses are roughly stable, your COGS is approximately 88% of revenue ($704).
*   **Material Exposure:** 60% of $704 = ~$422 per drum in raw materials.
*   **The Shock:** A 20% monthly spike in materials adds $84.40 to your cost ($422 × 0.20).
*   **The Result:** Since your profit is only $96, a single 20% spike consumes 88% of your entire profit margin for that batch. A second consecutive spike erases profitability.

**Proposed Mechanism: The Index-Linked Surcharge**
Instead of changing the $800 base price, introduce a "Raw Material Adjustment Factor" (RMAF) in your invoices.
1.  **Establish a Baseline:** Set a baseline material cost (e.g., $422/drum) based on current averages.
2.  **Define the Trigger:** The surcharge activates only when the specific raw material index (e.g., a relevant chemical commodity index) moves ±5% from the baseline.
3.  **The Formula:** `Final Price = $800 + (Current Material Cost - Baseline Material Cost)`.
    *   *Example:* If material costs rise 20% to $506.40, the surcharge is $84.40. The customer pays $884.40. Your margin remains intact.
4.  **Frequency:** Update the surcharge monthly or quarterly to match your inventory turnover (6x/year implies a 2-month cycle), aligning billing with your actual replacement costs.

**Decision Aid**
*   **Read:** Your break-even point shifts from a fixed volume to a fixed *spread*. As long as the surcharge passes through 100% of the material variance, your margin is mathematically insulated.
*   **Flip Input:** The critical variable is **customer acceptance threshold**. If clients reject any variable pricing, your only alternative is to hedge raw materials financially (futures contracts), which requires capital you may not want to tie up given your $200k inventory load.
*   **Threshold Rule:** If the surcharge exceeds 15% of the base price ($120) for more than two consecutive months, trigger a formal contract renegotiation clause rather than relying on the automatic adjustment.
*   **Next Step:** Draft a "Price Adjustment Amendment" for your top 3 clients this week, offering a 1% discount on the *base* $800 price in exchange for accepting the RMAF clause.

**Legal Regime & Routing (Lane R)**

However, clauses that are deemed "unconscionable" or lack a definite standard for measurement can be challenged. The clause must reference an external, verifiable market index rather than your internal costs to be most enforceable.
