Lock in the rate on the 12 million yen obligation using a forward contract; dynamic pricing is too slow and risky for a transaction due in three months with your current margin profile.

**The Arithmetic (Lane A)**
Your exposure is immediate and quantifiable. At 150 JPY/USD, your 12 million yen cost is $80,000. If the yen strengthens to 110 JPY/USD (a move seen in the last year), that same cost jumps to ~$109,090. This $29,090 swing exceeds your entire projected profit on the deal. With a typical 12% net margin, your profit on $80,000 of cost (implying ~$91,400 revenue) is only ~$11,000. A moderate shift in rates wipes this out entirely.

*   **Forward Contract:** You agree today to buy 12 million yen in three months at a fixed rate (e.g., 145 JPY/USD).
    *   *Result:* Your cost is locked at ~$82,758 regardless of market movement. You sacrifice potential upside if the yen weakens further, but you guarantee survival of the margin.
    *   *Cost:* Usually embedded in the spread (0.5%–1.5% typically), often no upfront fee for small businesses.
*   **Options:** You buy the right, but not the obligation, to buy yen at a set rate.
    *   *Result:* Protects against upside risk while allowing you to benefit if the yen weakens.
    *   *Cost:* Requires an upfront premium (cash outlay now). For a transaction of this size ($80k–$100k), the premium cost may disproportionately eat into the already thin 12% margin, making it less efficient than a forward.
*   **Dynamic Pricing:** Attempting to raise USD prices for retailers mid-quarter.
    *   *Risk:* Retail contracts are likely already set. Even if not, passing on volatile costs damages relationships and sales velocity. The lag between rate shift and price adjustment creates a "danger zone" where you absorb the loss.

**The Decision Trigger**
Use a **forward contract** if your priority is preserving the known 12% margin. The "flip" input is the cost of the forward spread versus your risk tolerance. If the bank quotes a forward rate worse than 140 JPY/USD, the hedge becomes too expensive relative to the current spot, and you might accept some risk. However, given that currency swings already cost you $18,000 last quarter, the certainty of a forward is mathematically superior to gambling on stability.
