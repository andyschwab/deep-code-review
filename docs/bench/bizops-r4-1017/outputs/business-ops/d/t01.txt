Keep the flat $29/month model for now; switching to per-word pricing based on your current assumptions increases revenue volatility rather than smoothing it, and the math does not yet justify the added billing complexity.

**Current Model (Flat Rate)**
*   **Formula:** `Revenue = Subscribers × Monthly Fee`
*   **Calculation:** 120 users × $29 = **$3,480/month** (assuming no churn fluctuation in this snapshot).
*   **Volatility Driver:** User count. Your 8% monthly churn means you lose ~10 users/month. To stay flat, you need 10 new users monthly. Revenue drops in discrete steps when users leave.

**Proposed Model (Per-Word)**
*   **Formula:** `Revenue = Subscribers × Avg Words/User × Rate`
*   **Calculation:** 120 users × 15,000 words × $0.002 = **$3,600/month** (Base Case).
*   **Volatility Driver:** Usage volume. You explicitly noted users cancel during "slow seasons." If a user's volume drops by 50% during a slow month (7,500 words), their bill drops to $15. If they stop entirely, revenue is $0.
*   **The Variance Problem:** You stated you do not know the word count variance across your base. Without this data, you cannot model the downside. If 20% of your users have a "slow month" simultaneously (industry-wide lull), your revenue could dip significantly below the $3,480 flat baseline.

**The Churn vs. Complexity Trade-off**
Your hypothesis is that usage-based pricing reduces churn because users pay less when work is slow. While this may improve retention, it transfers the risk from the user (fixed cost) to you (variable revenue).
*   **Break-even Analysis:** To match the $3,480 flat revenue at the $0.002 rate, your total platform volume must stay above **1,740,000 words/month**.
*   **Flip Input:** The decision flips if your average user volume consistently exceeds **14,500 words** *and* the variance between peak and slow seasons is less than **15%**. If variance is higher, the per-word model creates a "double dip": users pay less *and* you still face churn risk if they leave the platform entirely.

**Decision Aid**
1.  **Read:** The per-word model currently projects higher base revenue ($3,600 vs $3,480) but introduces unknown downside risk due to unmeasured volume variance.
2.  **Formula:** `Min Volume Needed = (Current Revenue) / (Rate per Word)`. Your threshold is 1.74M words.
3.  **Threshold Rule:** If LTV:CAC is healthy under the flat model, do not introduce billing complexity unless you can verify that 80% of your user base maintains >10,000 words/month even during their slowest season.
4.  **Next Step:** Before switching, instrument your database to calculate the actual word count distribution for the last 3 months. Run a "shadow billing" test for one month where you calculate what users *would* have paid under the new model without charging them. If the shadow revenue variance is >10% of your current flat revenue, stick to the flat fee or consider a hybrid (e.g., $15 base + $0.001 per word).
