Keep the flat $5,000 license for now; switching to usage-based pricing at your current volumes would immediately cut your largest clients' bills by 20%, reducing total revenue without a guaranteed increase in new sales to offset the loss.

**The Arithmetic**
Your current baseline is 10 clients × $5,000 = **$50,000 MRR**.
Under the proposed usage model ($0.05/call) with current volume (80,000 calls/client):
*   Revenue per client: 80,000 × $0.05 = $4,000.
*   Total MRR: 10 × $4,000 = **$40,000**.
*   **Immediate Impact:** A **$10,000 (20%) revenue drop** before any recessionary volume changes occur.

**Recessionary Stress Test (20% Volume Drop)**
You noted a potential 20% drop in transaction volumes during the downturn.
*   *Flat Model:* Revenue remains **$50,000**. The client absorbs the efficiency hit; your revenue is insulated.
*   *Usage Model:* Volume drops to 64,000 calls/client (80,000 × 0.8).
    *   Revenue per client: 64,000 × $0.05 = $3,200.
    *   Total MRR: **$32,000**.
    *   **Total Swing:** You lose $18,000 (36%) compared to your current baseline.

**The Flip Input**
The usage model only equals your current $50,000 revenue if the average client exceeds **100,000 calls/month** ($5,000 ÷ $0.05). Since your largest clients are currently at 80,000, they would need to increase usage by 25% just to break even on their current spend, which contradicts your expectation of falling volumes.

**Decision Thresholds**
*   If average client volume < 100,000 calls, the flat fee yields higher revenue.
*   If you fear volume contraction, the flat fee acts as a revenue hedge; the usage model amplifies your downside risk.
*   Rule of thumb: Do not switch to usage-based pricing unless your "base" volume assumption is significantly higher than your clients' current steady state, or unless the lower entry price ($0.05) is projected to double your client count immediately (which is unlikely in a downturn).
