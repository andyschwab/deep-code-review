Adopt the **per-hour model** for complex cases (those exceeding 3.3 hours) and retain the **per-case flat fee** for standard disputes, or transition to a hybrid "base + overage" structure immediately. Under your current pure flat-fee model, you lose margin on any case taking longer than 3.3 hours, directly threatening your $50,000 quarterly goal if complex cases dominate the peak season.

**The Arithmetic**
Your current unit economics per case are:
*   **Revenue:** $200 (fixed)
*   **Variable Cost:** $60/hour × 2.5 hours (avg) = $150
*   **Contribution Margin:** $50 per case
*   **Break-even Duration:** $200 revenue ÷ $60/hr cost = **3.33 hours**.

Any case lasting 4 or 5 hours erodes your margin. At 4 hours, your cost is $240, resulting in a **$40 loss per case**. At 5 hours, the loss is **$100 per case**.

To hit your $50,000 quarterly revenue goal (~$16,667/month):
*   **Current Model:** You need ~84 cases/month ($16,667 ÷ $200). With 400 cases available, volume is not the constraint; margin leakage on long cases is.
*   **Hourly Model Assumption:** If you charge clients $100/hour (a standard 1.67x markup on mediator cost) and behavior remains static (2.5 hours avg):
    *   Revenue: $250/case.
    *   Cost: $150/case.
    *   Margin: $100/case (double your current margin).
    *   Cases needed for goal: ~67 cases/month.

**The Flip Input & Sensitivity**
The decision hinges entirely on **client behavior elasticity**: how much the average resolution time drops if clients are billed hourly.
*   **Scenario A (High Elasticity):** Clients settle faster, dropping average time to 1.5 hours.
    *   Hourly Revenue ($100/hr × 1.5h): $150.
    *   Cost ($60/hr × 1.5h): $90.
    *   Margin: $60.
    *   *Result:* Still profitable, but lower total revenue per case than the static scenario. However, you eliminate the risk of $100 losses on 5-hour cases.
*   **Scenario B (Low Elasticity):** Time stays at 2.5 hours.
    *   *Result:* Hourly billing maximizes revenue and eliminates losses on outliers.

**Decision Thresholds**
*   **Rule of Thumb:** If more than **15%** of your cases exceed 3.3 hours, the pure flat-fee model fails to maximize margin.
*   **Risk Trigger:** If a single month sees >20 cases lasting 4+ hours under the flat-fee model, you will miss your quarterly revenue target due to negative contribution on those units.
