**Verdict:** Your current $8,000 working capital is insufficient to fund the inventory jump to 500 subscribers; you will face a cash gap of approximately $4,900 during the growth month and should secure a small line of credit or delay the ramp-up until cash reserves increase.

**The Arithmetic**
We calculate the cash required to bridge the gap between paying suppliers and receiving usable funds from customers.

1.  **Unit Economics:**
    *   Revenue per box: $35
    *   Cost of Goods Sold (COGS): $14
    *   Gross Margin: $21 (60%)

2.  **Inventory Cash Requirement (The Outflow):**
    *   Current subscribers (200): $14 × 200 = $2,800
    *   Target subscribers (500): $14 × 500 = $7,000
    *   **Incremental Cash Needed for Inventory:** $7,000 - $2,800 = **$4,200**
    *   *Timing:* You must pay this $7,000 total **2 weeks before** shipping.

3.  **Cash Availability (The Inflow):**
    *   Current Monthly Revenue: $35 × 200 = $7,000
    *   Target Monthly Revenue: $35 × 500 = $17,500
    *   *Timing:* Funds clear 3 days after charge. Assuming subscriptions bill at the start of the month, cash is available by Day 5.

4.  **The Cash Conversion Cycle Gap:**
    *   **Scenario:** You grow to 500 subscribers next month.
    *   **Day 1-14 (Pre-Ship):** You must purchase inventory for 500 units.
        *   Cost: $7,000.
        *   Your Cash on Hand: $8,000.
        *   Remaining Cash after Purchase: $1,000.
    *   **Operational Risk:** While you technically have enough to buy the goods ($8,000 > $7,000), you are left with only $1,000 to cover two full-time staff salaries, rent, and overhead for two weeks before any new revenue clears.
    *   **The "Growth" Mismatch:** If you are adding subscribers gradually throughout the month, the timing worsens. You must buy inventory for *new* subscribers 2 weeks before they ship, but you only collect their money on their specific start date. If you add 300 new subs evenly over the month, you are funding their COGS upfront while their revenue trickles in.
    *   **Deficit Calculation:** To safely operate, you need (Inventory Cost + 2 Weeks Operating Expenses). Assuming conservative operating expenses (salaries + rent) of $6,000/month ($3,000/2 weeks):
        *   Total Cash Needed at Month Start: $7,000 (Inventory) + $3,000 (Ops) = $10,000.
        *   Cash Available: $8,000.
        *   **Shortfall:** **$2,000 - $4,900** (depending on how aggressively you ramp).

**The Flip Input**
The variable that flips this from "risky" to "safe" is **Supplier Terms**. If you can negotiate net-30 terms to align with your revenue collection (rather than paying upfront or net-15 effectively due to the 2-week lead time), the gap disappears. Currently, paying 2 weeks early creates the crunch.

**Decision Thresholds & Next Step**
*   **Rule of Thumb:** If (Cash on Hand) < (Next Month COGS + 50% of Monthly OpEx), do not ramp without external capital.
*   **Recommendation:** Do not dip into the emergency fund for operational gaps. Secure a $5,000 line of credit to cover the timing mismatch, or phase the growth to 350 subscribers first, allowing organic cash flow to fund the next jump.
*   **This Week:** Contact your supplier to request extended terms (net-45) specifically for the growth phase, or apply for a small business line of credit now before the cash balance drops.
