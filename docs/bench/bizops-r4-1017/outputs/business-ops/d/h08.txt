**1. The Regulatory Regime**
Notary law is state-specific, not federal. There is no "national" notary license. The governing rule is that the notary must be commissioned in the state where the *signer* is physically located at the time of the notarization, regardless of where your company is headquartered.
*   **Registrations/Commissions:** You generally need a traditional notary commission plus a specific "Remote Online Notarization" (RON) endorsement or separate registration in every target state. Some states (e.g., Virginia, Texas, Florida) have mature RON statutes; others have temporary emergency orders or no RON laws at all.
*   **Bonds and Insurance:** Most states require a surety bond (typically $10,000–$50,000 per notary) and Errors & Omissions (E&O) insurance. Since you plan to hire staff or contract notaries, you may need a "agency" bond or individual bonds for every commissioned agent.
*   **Technology Standards:** States mandate specific technology providers for the audio-visual recording and identity proofing (knowledge-based authentication or credential analysis). Your platform must integrate with state-approved vendors (e.g., IDology, Jumio) and ensure recordings are stored for a statutory period (often 5–10 years).

**2. Estimated Time and Cost (Illustrative Only)**
*   **Time:** Securing commissions in 50 states is a multi-year effort if done sequentially. Even with parallel processing, background checks and state processing times vary from 2 weeks to 6 months per state.
*   **Cost:** Application fees range from $50 to $500 per state. Bonds and insurance could total $2,000–$5,000 annually per notary agent. Legal counsel fees to navigate 50 distinct statutes will likely exceed your current seed runway if attempted all at once.

**3. Restrictive Jurisdictions Requiring Immediate Counsel**
You must hire local counsel before accepting customers in states with:
*   **No RON Statute:** Operating here is illegal.
*   **Prescriptive Technology Mandates:** States that require you to use *their* specific approved vendor list, which may not integrate with your current stack.
*   **Residency Requirements:** Some states still require the notary to be a physical resident of that state, preventing you from using a centralized remote workforce.
*   **Attorney-Only Closings:** Certain states (e.g., New York, Massachusetts, Georgia) often require an attorney to be present or supervise real estate closings, which complicates a pure "notary" model for real estate transactions.
