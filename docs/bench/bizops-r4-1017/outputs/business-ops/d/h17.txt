**The Arithmetic**
Your current situation is mathematically unsustainable. At $8.50 revenue and $7.40 cost, your contribution margin is $1.10 per bar.
*   **Monthly Contribution:** 15,000 units × $1.10 = $16,500.
*   **Monthly Burn:** $45,000 (fixed) - $16,500 (contribution) = $28,500 net loss.
*   **Runway:** $120,000 cash ÷ $28,500 burn ≈ **4.2 months**. This confirms your fear; without change, you are insolvent by month 5.

**Option Analysis**

1.  **Absorb Cost (Status Quo):**
    *   *Cash Flow:* Negative $28,500/month.
    *   *Brand:* Preserves short-term shelf space but guarantees business failure in 4 months.
    *   *Verdict:* Reject.

2.  **Raise Core Price to $14.00:**
    *   *Unit Economics:* $14.00 - $7.40 = $6.60 margin.
    *   *Break-even Volume:* To cover $45,000 fixed costs, you need to sell ~6,818 units ($45,000 ÷ $6.60).
    *   *Risk:* If sales drop by more than 54% (falling below 6,818 units), you still lose money. However, even at a 40% volume drop (9,000 units), you generate $14,400 in profit.
    *   *Brand:* Risks delisting from price-sensitive partners, but positions the product as a premium luxury good consistent with "artisanal" branding.

3.  **Reformulate (Cheaper Oils):**
    *   *Cash Flow:* Potentially restores margins if costs drop below $4.00.
    *   *Brand:* High risk. "Organic essential oil blends" is your core value proposition. Changing ingredients during a shortage looks like deception, potentially destroying long-term LTV (Lifetime Value) to save short-term margin.
    *   *Verdict:* Only viable if clearly marketed as a new, temporary SKU, not a secret swap.

4.  **Reduce Bar Size (Shrinkflation):**
    *   *Cash Flow:* Reduces material cost proportionally. To hit a $4.00 cost target, you must reduce size by ~46%.
    *   *Brand:* Often perceived negatively by consumers as "hidden price hikes."
    *   *Verdict:* Lower brand damage than secret reformulation, but higher friction than transparent pricing.

**Recommended Strategy: The Two-Tier Pivot**
Do not choose one option; split the portfolio to manage cash flow and channel conflict.

*   **Tier 1 (Core Brand):** Raise the original Lavender Bar to **$14.00**. Market this transparently: "Due to unprecedented global harvest failure in Bulgaria, we are maintaining our 100% organic Bulgarian Lavender standard at a revised price." This targets your direct-to-consumer channel and premium retailers who value authenticity over price.
*   **Tier 2 (Retention SKU):** Launch a temporary **"PureSuds Calm Blend"** at **$9.50**. Reformulate this specific SKU using available, lower-cost organic oils (e.g., Lavandin or US-grown lavender). Clearly label it as a "Limited Harvest Blend." This gives retail partners a sub-$10 option to keep shelf space without devaluing your core SKU.

**Decision Thresholds**
*   **Volume Trigger:** If Tier 1 sales fall below 7,000 units/month after 60 days, the premium positioning has failed; consider pausing production of the core SKU rather than lowering the price.
*   **Cash Trigger:** If the combined mix does not generate positive net cash flow within 45 days, immediate overhead reduction (staffing/rent) is required regardless of pricing.
