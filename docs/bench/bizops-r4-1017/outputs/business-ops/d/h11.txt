Keep the upfront sale model for now; the leasing scenario burns your runway in 30 months without a guaranteed valuation multiple expansion to offset the risk.

**The Arithmetic (Lane A)**
Your current unit economics show a gross profit of $2,400 per unit ($4,200 price - $1,800 COGS). Under the proposed lease ($150/month), you recover the $1,800 COGS in 12 months. The remaining 24 months of the license term generate $3,600 in pure margin, theoretically increasing Lifetime Value (LTV) from $2,400 to $3,600 per unit.

However, the cash flow impact is immediate and severe. You stated leasing increases monthly cash outflow by $40,000 due to inventory financing. With $1.2 million in reserves:
*   **Current Runway:** Unknown without current burn, but positive unit cash flow supports growth.
*   **Lease Scenario Runway:** $1,200,000 / $40,000 = **30 months** if revenue collection lags or fails to cover the incremental financing cost immediately. Since you are fundraising in 18 months, this leaves a dangerous 12-month buffer where any sales slowdown creates a liquidity crisis before the round closes.

**The Valuation Trade-off**
Venture capitalists do value recurring revenue (ARR) higher than one-time sales, often applying 5–10x multiples on ARR versus 1–3x on transactional revenue. However, they penalize "asset-heavy" models where the startup holds depreciating hardware on the balance sheet.
*   **Upfront Model:** High cash efficiency, clean balance sheet, lower valuation multiple.
*   **Lease Model:** Higher theoretical LTV, but the balance sheet becomes bloated with leased assets. Investors may view the $40k/month financing cost as debt-service risk rather than pure growth investment, potentially compressing the multiple you hope to gain.

**The Flip Input**
The decision flips if your sales cycle is longer than 6 months. If it takes >6 months to sign a lease, your cash gap widens beyond the 30-month runway calculated above, making the fundraise impossible before insolvency.

**Decision Thresholds**
*   **Rule of Thumb:** If LTV:CAC > 3 and Payback < 12 months, scale. Here, lease payback is exactly 12 months on COGS alone, ignoring sales commissions and the $40k financing overhead. This is borderline inefficient for early-stage capital.
*   **Trigger:** If you cannot secure non-dilutive debt financing specifically for the inventory (separate from your equity round), do not shift models.

**Next Step**
Before the 18-month mark, run a pilot with **three** golf courses on the lease model only. Instrument the actual collection timeline and financing drag. If the pilot confirms the $40k/month burn scales linearly without accelerating sales velocity by >20%, reject the full pivot and instead offer a "hardware-as-a-service" option only to customers who cannot afford the $4,200 upfront, preserving your primary cash-efficient model.
