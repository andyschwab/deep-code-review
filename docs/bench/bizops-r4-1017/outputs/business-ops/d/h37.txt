**Verdict:** Do not take the venture debt as structured; the monthly debt service will push you into negative cash flow before reaching 150 subscribers, and the unit economics rely on an unsustainable 100% retention rate over 12 months to break even on hardware.

**The Arithmetic**
Your current model creates a timing mismatch where upfront costs exceed early inflows.
*   **Upfront Cash Need:** $160,000 (hardware) + $40,000 (fit-out) = **$200,000**.
*   **Monthly Fixed Burn:** $35,000 (ops).
*   **Revenue per Unit:** $99/month.
*   **Hardware Subsidy Loss per Unit:** You spend $800 but collect $50 upfront. You must recover $750 via subscription. At $99/month, it takes **7.6 months** of full payments just to recoup the hardware cost per user, ignoring operating expenses.

**Debt Service Impact**
If you borrow $250,000 at 12% annual interest:
*   *Assumption (ASSUMED):* Interest-only payments for the first year (common in bridge scenarios), or a standard amortization.
    *   Scenario A (Interest Only): Monthly interest = $250,000 × 0.01 = **$2,500/month**.
    *   Scenario B (Amortized over 3 years): Principal + Interest ≈ **$8,300/month**.
*   **Total Monthly Burn (with Ops):**
    *   Scenario A: $35,000 + $2,500 = **$37,500**.
    *   Scenario B: $35,000 + $8,300 = **$43,300**.

**Break-Even Analysis**
To cover *only* the monthly operating burn (excluding hardware payback):
*   Subscribers needed (Scenario A): $37,500 ÷ $99 ≈ **379 subscribers**.
*   Subscribers needed (Scenario B): $43,300 ÷ $99 ≈ **438 subscribers**.

**The Flip Input:** Your target of **150 subscribers** generates only $14,850/month. This covers less than 40% of your operating expenses, let alone the debt service or hardware payback. At 150 users, you are losing roughly $20,000–$28,000 every month in cash flow, compounding the initial $200k outlay.

**Decision Thresholds**
*   **Rule of Thumb:** If Payback Period on Customer Acquisition Cost (here, the hardware subsidy) exceeds 12 months, the model is high-risk for early-stage ventures. Your hardware payback is ~8 months *before* contributing to opex.
*   **Sustainability Check:** To sustain this model without running out of cash in 6 months, you would need roughly 400+ subscribers immediately, which contradicts your gradual deployment plan.

**Next Step**
Before signing any debt agreement, run a "Bring-Your-Own-Device" (BYOD) pilot with 20 users next week. Remove the $800 upfront cost from your burn rate and test if the $99/month value proposition holds when users provide their own hardware. If conversion drops below 30% in the BYOD model, the issue is product-market fit, not capital structure.
