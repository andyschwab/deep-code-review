**The Rule and The Risk**
Sales tax obligation for digital services like interior design is not determined by federal law but by individual state statutes. The critical concept you are missing is **Economic Nexus**. Following the *South Dakota v. Wayfair* Supreme Court decision, most states no longer require a physical presence (like an office or warehouse) to owe tax. Instead, they trigger liability based on economic activity within their borders.

Your assumption that a "virtual presence" exempts you is dangerous and likely incorrect for a significant portion of your customer base. While some states exempt professional services or specifically exempt digital design, many states tax "specified digital products" or have broad definitions of taxable services that could encompass your deliverables. Furthermore, even if your service is exempt in a state, the act of crossing the economic nexus threshold often triggers a requirement to *register* and file returns stating zero tax, rather than simply ignoring the state.

**Key Factors Determining Liability**
You must evaluate your activity against two primary thresholds for every state where you have clients:
1.  **Revenue Threshold:** Most states set this at $100,000 in gross revenue derived from customers in that state within a 12-month period.
2.  **Transaction Count Threshold:** Many states also set a limit on the number of separate transactions (often 200) into the state.

**Applied Analysis of Your Numbers**
Last quarter, you generated $149,700 across "every major US city."
*   **Average Revenue per State:** If your 300 clients are evenly distributed across just 10 major states, you are averaging ~$15,000 per state per quarter. Annualized, this is $60,000 per state—approaching the $100,000 threshold quickly.
*   **Transaction Count:** 300 clients distributed across 50 states averages 6 transactions per state. However, if you are concentrated in fewer states (e.g., CA, NY, TX, FL), you could easily exceed 200 transactions annually in those specific jurisdictions.
*   **The Trap:** If you exceed *either* the revenue OR the transaction count in a state that taxes your service, you are liable for back taxes from the moment you crossed the threshold, plus penalties and interest. Your $25,000 reserve may be insufficient if you owe back taxes for multiple quarters in high-tax jurisdictions.
