### Lane A: Arithmetic & Unit Economics Application

**1. Current Model (Upfront Sale)**
*   **Gross Margin per Unit:** $4,200 (Price) − $1,800 (COGS) = **$2,400**.
*   **Cash Impact:** Immediate inflow of $4,200 per unit sold.
*   **Runway Context:** With $1.2M cash, you can cover significant fixed costs before needing new capital, assuming sales velocity covers the $1,800 unit cost replenishment.

**2. Proposed Model (Leasing)**
*   **Monthly Revenue:** $150/unit.
*   **Break-even Horizon (Hardware Only):** $1,800 (COGS) ÷ $150/mo = **12 months** just to recover hardware cost, excluding sales commissions, support, and the stated $40k/mo inventory financing increase.
*   **Cash Flow Strain:** You incur the $1,800 cost immediately but recover it over 12+ months.
*   **Runway Impact Formula:** `New Runway = Cash Reserves ÷ (Existing Burn + $40,000 + [COGS × Units Deployed])`.
    *   *Application:* If you deploy 100 units in Month 1, cash outflow spikes by $180,000 (COGS) + $40,000 (financing) = $220,000 before receiving the first $15,000 in revenue. This drastically accelerates burn.

**3. Valuation Trade-off Structure**
*   **Multiple Expansion Hypothesis:** Investors often apply higher revenue multiples to Annual Recurring Revenue (ARR) than one-time hardware sales.
*   **The "Asset-Heavy" Drag:** Your balance sheet shifts from cash-heavy to asset-heavy (inventory + accounts receivable) with increased debt (financing costs).
*   **Sensitivity Check:**
    *   *Scenario A (High Churn):* If a course cancels at month 10, you have not recovered the $1,800 COGS, resulting in a net loss per unit.
    *   *Scenario B (Long Tenure):* If the average life is 36 months, total revenue is $5,400. Gross profit becomes $3,600 ($5,400 − $1,800), which is higher than the upfront model's $2,400.
    *   *Decision Threshold:* Does the projected increase in valuation multiple (e.g., moving from a 2x revenue multiple to a 6x ARR multiple) mathematically offset the dilution required to raise enough capital to survive the extended payback period?

**Decision Aid:**
*   **Read:** The lease model flips your cash flow from positive (at sale) to deeply negative for the first 12 months per unit.
*   **Flip Input:** The critical variable is **Customer Lifetime (Months)**. If Average Lifetime < 14 months (covering COGS + financing overhead), the model destroys value compared to selling.
*   **Rule of Thumb:** If LTV:CAC falls below 3.0 or payback period exceeds 18 months under the lease model, venture investors may view the capital efficiency as poor despite the "recurring" label.
*   **Next Step:** Before pitching, model the cash burn for deploying 50, 100, and 200 units under the lease scenario to determine the exact raise size needed to reach 18 months.

### Lane R: Regulation & Securities Routing

**Securities & Fundraising Regime:**
Your question regarding "company valuation during our next fundraising round" and the structure of the lease offering touches on **securities laws**.
*   **The Boundary:** Determining how investors value your specific mix of recurring revenue vs. asset-heavy debt, and structuring the equity round itself, is a securities matter.
*   **Action Required:** You must consult with **securities counsel** and an **investment banker** specializing in agritech/hardware. They will advise on how the "asset-heavy balance sheet" impacts term sheets and whether the lease contracts themselves require specific disclosures or registrations.
*   **Tax & Accounting:** The shift from recognizing revenue upfront to over 36 months, and the treatment of the hardware as a depreciating asset on your books, is a **tax and GAAP/IFRS accounting determination**. A **CPA** must model the tax implications of the $40,000 monthly financing cost deductibility and the depreciation schedule.

**Note:** Do not present the lease model to investors without a CPA-verified pro forma showing the impact on EBITDA and cash burn.
**Note:** Worker classification for your sales team (commission structures under a lease model) should be reviewed by employment counsel.
