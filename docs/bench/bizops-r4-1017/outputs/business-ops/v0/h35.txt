### Common Distribution Structures for Film Funds

In film finance, where revenue is lumpy and asynchronous, the standard approach to fairness is not paying investors deal-by-deal, but rather using a **Fund-Level Waterfall** with specific mechanisms to handle timing disparities.

**1. The Whole-Fund Waterfall (vs. Deal-by-Deal)**
To prevent early investors from being paid back while later capital sits idle, most independent funds use a "whole-fund" model. In this structure, all revenue from all three films flows into a single pool. Distributions to investors do not begin until the **Total Committed Capital** (£2M) is returned to all investors pro-rata.
*   **Application:** If Film A returns £150k in six months, that cash is held (or temporarily reinvested in short-term instruments) until the total fund recoups its £2M basis. Only then do profits begin to flow. This ensures every investor, regardless of when their specific film monetizes, gets their principal back before anyone takes profit.

**2. Preferred Return (The "Hurdle")**
You asked about protecting early capital. A **Preferred Return** (or Hurdle Rate) is the standard mechanism for this. It stipulates that investors must receive their capital back *plus* a specific annualized return (e.g., 8% simple interest) before the fund managers (you) receive any "carried interest" (profit share).
*   **Arithmetic Logic:** If an investor put in £100k, the waterfall calculates that they are owed £100k + (£100k × 8% × time elapsed) before you participate in the upside. This compensates early capital for the time value of money without requiring you to guess exact payout dates.

**3. Handling Asynchronous Deals: The "Clawback" Provision**
If you choose to distribute profits deal-by-deal (to avoid idle cash), you legally require a **Clawback Provision**.
*   **Mechanism:** If Film A pays out profits early, but Film B fails completely, the early recipients must return enough money to ensure the whole fund met its preferred return threshold.
*   **Risk:** This creates liquidity risk for your angels (they might have to write a check back to the fund). Given your small size (10 angels), a whole-fund waterfall with a defined reinvestment period for early cash is often cleaner than relying on clawbacks.

**4. Tax and Legal Regime (Route to Professional)**
Your question regarding "not triggering complex tax events" falls squarely into **Lane R**.
*   **Regime:** UK Partnership Tax Law and Securities Regulation (FCA rules on promoting financial promotions).
*   **Action Required:** You must route the specific drafting of the Limited Partnership Agreement (LPA) to a UK media-finance solicitor. They will determine if holding cash triggers "trading" status vs. "investment" status for tax purposes, and whether your distribution schedule complies with UK securities laws for angel investors. Do not implement a hurdle rate or clawback without legal documentation, as the tax treatment of "returned" capital vs. "income" varies significantly based on the exact wording.

**Decision Aid:**
*   **Structure Choice:** For a £2M fund with only 3 assets, a **Whole-Fund Waterfall** with an **8% Preferred Return** is the industry standard to balance fairness and cash drag.
*   **Next Step:** Draft a term sheet specifying "Return of Capital + 8% Preferred Return before Carry" and take it to a UK entertainment lawyer to encode into your LPA. Do not distribute any profits until this agreement is signed.
