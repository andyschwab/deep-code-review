### Risk Evaluation: Price Increase vs. Sponsorship Threshold

To evaluate whether the price hike from $49 to $79 is net-positive, we must model the trade-off between increased subscription revenue and the risk of losing sponsorship revenue due to high-value user churn.

**1. Current Baseline (Monthly)**
*   **Total Subscribers:** 3,200
*   **High-Value Users (15%):** 480 users
*   **Subscription Revenue:** 3,200 × $49 = $156,800
*   **Sponsorship Requirement:** 40% of $85,000 operating cost = $34,000/month
*   **Total Current Revenue:** $156,800 + $34,000 = **$190,800**

**2. Post-Hike Scenario (Assumptions)**
*   **New Price:** $79
*   **Churn Risk:** You fear losing 20% of high-value users.
    *   Lost High-Value Users: 480 × 20% = 96 users
    *   Remaining High-Value Users: 384
    *   *Critical Assumption:* If high-value users drop below a specific threshold (e.g., if losing 96 users breaks the "access" promise), sponsorship revenue ($34,000) drops to $0. If the threshold is maintained, sponsorship remains.

**3. Break-Even Analysis: Subscription Gain vs. Sponsorship Loss**
We calculate the maximum number of total subscribers you can lose before the price hike becomes net-negative, assuming **total loss of sponsorship** if the high-value segment shrinks by 20%.

*   **Target Total Revenue:** Must exceed $190,800 (current baseline).
*   **New Subscription Revenue Needed:** $190,800 (since sponsorship = $0 in worst case).
*   **Required Subscribers at $79:** $190,800 ÷ $79 ≈ **2,416 subscribers**.
*   **Current Subscribers:** 3,200.
*   **Allowable Total Churn:** 3,200 - 2,416 = **784 users**.

**The Decision Logic:**
*   **Scenario A (Sponsorship Lost):** If losing 96 high-value users (20% of that segment) causes sponsors to leave entirely, you can afford to lose up to **784 total users** (24.5% of your base) and still match current revenue. Since your feared loss (96 users) is well below 784, the price hike is mathematically safe *even if* you lose all sponsorship revenue, provided general churn doesn't spike drastically.
*   **Scenario B (Sponsorship Retained):** If sponsors stay despite the 20% drop in high-value users, your revenue increases significantly:
    *   New Sub Revenue (assuming only 96 users leave): (3,200 - 96) × $79 = $245,016.
    *   Total Revenue: $245,016 + $34,000 = $279,016.
    *   **Net Gain:** +$88,216/month.

**4. Sensitivity Check: The "Flip" Input**
The variable that flips the answer is the **Sponsorship Cliff Threshold**.
*   If sponsors require *exactly* 480 users and leave at 479, the risk is high.
*   If sponsors accept a range (e.g., >400 users), the risk is low.
*   **Formula for Break-Even High-Value Churn (if sponsorship is lost):**
    $$ \text{Max Allowable Churn} = \text{Current Users} - \frac{(\text{Current Sub Rev} + \text{Sponsorship Rev})}{\text{New Price}} $$
    $$ 3,200 - \frac{190,800}{79} = 784 \text{ users} $$

**Conclusion & Next Step**
Mathematically, the price hike is robust. You would need to lose more than 784 total subscribers (far more than the 96 high-value users at risk) to make the move net-negative, even in the worst-case scenario where sponsorship revenue vanishes completely.

**Decision Aid:**
1.  **Read:** The price hike is safe unless total churn exceeds 24.5%.
2.  **Threshold:** If LTV:CAC < 3 or payback > 12 months post-hike, reconsider (apply your own CAC data).
3.  **Next Step:** Before announcing the hike, **confirm the specific contractual threshold** with your top sponsor. Ask: "Does your agreement require a minimum of 480 high-value users, or is there a buffer?" This single fact determines if Scenario A or B applies.

*Note: This model assumes stable churn for the remaining 85% of users; verify if price elasticity affects the broader base.*
