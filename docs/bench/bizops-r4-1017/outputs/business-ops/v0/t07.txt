### Lane A: Pricing Model Arithmetic & Sensitivity Analysis

This analysis applies your provided numbers to compare the flat-fee model against the proposed usage-based model under a stress test of a 20% volume drop.

**1. Current State (Flat Fee Model)**
*   **Formula:** `Total Revenue = Clients × Monthly Fee`
*   **Inputs:** 10 clients × $5,000
*   **Current MRR:** $50,000
*   **Recession Impact (Volume Drop):** $0 impact on revenue. Under a flat license, revenue is decoupled from usage volume.
*   **Projected MRR (20% volume drop):** $50,000

**2. Proposed State (Usage-Based Model)**
*   **Formula:** `Client Revenue = Volume × Rate per Call`
*   **Inputs (Baseline):** 80,000 calls × $0.05/call = $4,000 per client.
*   **Baseline MRR:** 10 clients × $4,000 = $40,000.
    *   *Observation:* At current volumes, switching immediately reduces your MRR from $50,000 to $40,000 (a 20% decrease) before any recessionary drop occurs.
*   **Recession Impact (20% Volume Drop):**
    *   New Volume: 80,000 × (1 - 0.20) = 64,000 calls.
    *   New Client Revenue: 64,000 × $0.05 = $3,200.
    *   **Projected MRR:** 10 clients × $3,200 = $32,000.

**3. Revenue Swing Analysis**
*   **Flat Fee Swing:** $50,000 → $50,000 (0% variance).
*   **Usage-Based Swing:** $40,000 (baseline) → $32,000 (stress case).
*   **Total Potential Loss vs. Current State:** Switching models exposes you to an immediate $10,000/month revenue reduction at current volumes, expanding to an $18,000/month reduction if volumes fall by 20%.

**Decision Aid**
*   **Read:** At current usage levels, the usage-based model yields lower revenue than the flat fee, and it introduces direct correlation between client budget cuts and your revenue.
*   **Break-even Volume:** To match the current $5,000 flat fee at $0.05/call, a client must make **100,000 calls/month** (`$5,000 ÷ $0.05`). Your largest clients are currently at 80,000.
*   **Threshold Rule of Thumb:** If your goal is revenue stability during a downturn, variable pricing increases volatility. If your goal is lowering the entry barrier for smaller clients, variable pricing helps, but you must ensure the rate card captures enough volume from large clients to offset the risk of volume contraction.
*   **Next Step:** If you proceed with usage-based pricing, consider a "committed use" tier (e.g., pre-purchase $5,000 worth of calls for a discount) to secure cash flow while offering the flexibility of overage rates.

**Note:** You identified unknown volatility data; the 20% drop used here is a hypothetical stress test, not a forecast.
**Note:** This arithmetic assumes all 10 clients have identical volume; real-world segmentation may alter the aggregate result.
