### Cash Flow Analysis: Current Model vs. Installment Proposal

This analysis applies your provided numbers to two scenarios to illustrate the timing mismatch between customer inflows and supplier outflows. It does not predict future sales or advise on which model to choose.

#### 1. Current Model (50% Deposit)
*   **Inflow Timing:** You collect $225 (50% of $450) immediately upon order.
*   **Outflow Timing:** You must pay the supplier the full fabric cost 14 days before shipping. (Note: You did not provide the fabric cost per suit; this analysis assumes the supplier requires the *full* suit value or a specific fabric fee. For this liquidity check, we assume the worst-case cash outflow equals the total order value ($450) to test the reserve against total liability, though in reality, your outflow is likely just the fabric cost).
*   **Liquidity Position on 20 New Orders:**
    *   Immediate Cash In: 20 orders × $225 = **$4,500**.
    *   Pending Liability (due in ~2-4 weeks depending on production speed): 20 orders × $450 (max exposure) = $9,000.
    *   **Gap:** You hold $4,500 cash against a future $9,000 obligation. If the fabric cost is less than $225 per suit, you are cash-flow positive before using reserves. If fabric cost > $225, you dip into reserves 14 days before shipping.

#### 2. Proposed Installment Model (4 payments of $112.50)
*   **Inflow Timing:**
    *   Month 1 (Order): $112.50
    *   Month 2: $112.50
    *   Month 3: $112.50
    *   Month 4: $112.50 (Final payment)
*   **The Critical Mismatch:**
    *   You stated suppliers require full payment **14 days before shipping**.
    *   Under installments, you do not receive the full $450 until the *end* of Month 4.
    *   If you ship at the end of Month 4, you must pay the supplier in mid-Month 4. At that exact moment, the customer has only paid 3 installments ($337.50).
    *   **Cash Collected by Payment Due Date:** $337.50 per suit.
    *   **Cash Required for Supplier:** 100% of fabric cost.
*   **Liquidity Impact on 20 New Orders (Month 1 Cohort):**
    *   Immediate Cash In (Month 1): 20 × $112.50 = **$2,250**.
    *   Cash In by Mid-Month 4 (when supplier payment is due): 20 × $337.50 = **$6,750**.
    *   **Comparison to Current Model:** The installment plan delays your cumulative cash intake by roughly 75% in the first month compared to the deposit model ($2,250 vs $4,500).

#### Decision Aid: The "Flip" Input
The variable that determines if you breach your $20,000 reserve is **Fabric Cost Per Suit**.

*   **Formula:** `Reserve Dip = (Fabric Cost × 20 orders) - Cash Collected by Supplier Due Date`
*   **Scenario A (Current Model):** You collect $4,500 upfront. If Fabric Cost > $225, you use reserves.
*   **Scenario B (Installments):** You collect only $337.50 by the time the supplier needs payment (assuming shipping occurs after final payment). If Fabric Cost > $337.50, you use reserves.
*   **The Risk:** If your production cycle is longer than the installment period, or if you must pay suppliers before the 4th installment arrives, the cash gap widens.

**Sensitivity Check:**
If you offer installments but still require the supplier payment 14 days before shipping, you are effectively financing the customer's purchase for 3 months.
*   **Monthly Outflow (20 orders):** If fabric cost is $200/suit, outflow = $4,000.
*   **Monthly Inflow (Installments):** $2,250 (Month 1) + collections from previous months.
*   **Runway Impact:** In a steady state of 20 new orders/month, the installment plan reduces your immediate cash buffer by **$2,250 per month** compared to the deposit model. Over 3 months, this is a $6,750 difference in available cash.
