### Market Sizing Model: CityHive (Northeast US)

To create a realistic, conservative estimate that accounts for grant overlap and budget constraints, we must move from a "Total Available" count to a "Serviceable Obtainable" count using a funnel approach. Since specific grant allocation percentages are unknown, we will treat them as variables you must validate rather than fixed inputs.

#### 1. The Formula
The core arithmetic for your Two-Year Serviceable Addressable Market (SAM) in units (contracts) is:

$$ \text{SAM}_{\text{units}} = (\text{Total Muni} \times \text{Interest Rate} \times \text{Budget Fit Factor} \times \text{Conversion Rate}) \times \text{Time Capture} $$

#### 2. Applying Your Inputs & Identifying Unknowns
*   **Total Municipalities:** 450
*   **Interest Rate:** 30% (Expressed interest)
    *   *Calculation:* $450 \times 0.30 = 135$ interested municipalities.
*   **Budget Fit Factor (The Unknown):** This is the percentage of interested municipalities where pollinator programs are explicitly funded or can compete successfully against other green initiatives.
    *   *Status:* **UNVERIFIED.** You noted this is unknown. Do not assume 100%.
    *   *Sensitivity:* If only 20% of grants allow beekeeping, your pool drops significantly.
*   **Conversion Rate:** The percentage of qualified leads that sign a contract.
    *   *Status:* **UNVERIFIED.** For B2G (Business-to-Government) sales, this is often lower than B2B due to procurement cycles.
*   **Time Capture:** The portion of the market you can close in 2 years.
    *   *Assumption:* Government sales cycles are long (12–18 months). Capturing 100% of the SAM in 2 years is unlikely. A conservative estimate might be 25–40% of the annualizable demand.

#### 3. Scenario Analysis (Conservative vs. Optimistic)
Since we cannot fabricate the "Budget Fit" or "Conversion" numbers, here is the math structured for you to plug in real data:

**Scenario A: Conservative (High Friction)**
*   *Assumption:* Only 20% of interested municipalities have usable funds; 30% conversion; 25% time capture.
*   *Math:* $135 \times 0.20 \times 0.30 \times 0.25 = \mathbf{2.0 \text{ contracts}}$
*   *Revenue:* $2 \times \$2,500 = \$5,000$

**Scenario B: Moderate (Validated Fit)**
*   *Assumption:* 40% of interested municipalities have usable funds; 40% conversion; 35% time capture.
*   *Math:* $135 \times 0.40 \times 0.40 \times 0.35 = \mathbf{7.6 \text{ contracts}}$
*   *Revenue:* $8 \times \$2,500 = \$20,000$

**Scenario C: Optimistic (Low Friction)**
*   *Assumption:* 60% fit; 50% conversion; 50% time capture.
*   *Math:* $135 \times 0.60 \times 0.50 \times 0.50 = \mathbf{20.25 \text{ contracts}}$
*   *Revenue:* $20 \times \$2,500 = \$50,000$

#### 4. Decision Aid & Next Steps
*   **The Flip Input:** The **Budget Fit Factor** is the variable that most drastically changes your outcome. If this number is below 15%, the two-year revenue may not support significant overhead regardless of interest levels.
*   **Rule of Thumb:** In B2G sales, if your sales cycle exceeds 6 months, assume you can only close 2–4 major deals per salesperson per year.
*   **Immediate Action:** Do not pitch the "135 municipality" number to investors. Instead, run a **validation sprint** this week: Contact 10 of the "interested" municipalities and ask specifically: *"Does your current sustainability grant allow line-item spending for pollinator habitats, or is it restricted to infrastructure?"* Use the resulting percentage to replace the "Budget Fit Factor" above.

**Note:** Investors typically scrutinize "expressed interest" heavily; distinguishing between "interested in sustainability" and "interested in paying for bees" is critical for credibility.
Ensure your unit economics (CAC vs. $2,500 LTV) are modeled separately, as government acquisition costs can be high.
