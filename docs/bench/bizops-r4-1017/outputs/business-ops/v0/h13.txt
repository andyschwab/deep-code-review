### Lane A: Unit Economics & Pricing Structure Analysis

**1. Current State Arithmetic (Apply)**
Using your provided inputs, here is the clinic's economic reality per pair under your current unbundled model:
*   **Clinic Revenue (Insurance Cap):** $185
*   **Clinic Cost of Goods (Your Price):** $210
*   **Gross Margin per Pair:** $185 - $210 = **-$25 (Loss)**

The additional $12,000 software fee deepens this hole. Mathematically, the current model is unsustainable for the clinic given the $185 reimbursement cap.

**2. Pricing Model Sensitivity (Bundle vs. Separate)**
You asked whether to bundle or separate the software. Let's apply the math to both scenarios to see if the clinic breaks even.

*   **Scenario A: Separate SaaS Fee (Current)**
    *   Hardware Cost: $210/pair
    *   Software Allocation (at 1,200 units): $12,000 / 1,200 = $10/pair
    *   Total Clinic Cost: $220/pair
    *   Clinic Margin: $185 - $220 = **-$35/pair**

*   **Scenario B: Bundled Price (Absorbing Software into Unit Cost)**
    *   Total Monthly Cost Required: ($210 × 1,200) + $12,000 = $264,000
    *   Required Bundle Price per Pair: $264,000 / 1,200 = $220/pair
    *   Clinic Margin: $185 - $220 = **-$35/pair**

*Result:* The mathematical outcome is identical. Whether you charge $210 + $12k fee or a $220 bundled price, the clinic pays $220 total against a $185 cap. The structure (SaaS vs. Bundle) does not solve the fundamental deficit; the price exceeds the reimbursement ceiling.

**3. Break-Even Analysis for the Clinic**
To sustain your current total revenue ($264,000/mo) without the clinic losing money, the reimbursement rate would need to be:
*   `Break-Even Reimbursement = Total Cost / Volume`
*   $264,000 / 1,200 = **$220 per pair**
*   *Gap:* The current cap ($185) is $35 below the break-even point.

**Decision Aid & Next Steps**
*   **Read:** Your current pricing exceeds the market's ability to pay (the insurance cap) by ~19%. No pricing structure (bundle vs. SaaS) fixes a price that is higher than the customer's maximum revenue source.
*   **The Flip Input:** The variable that changes the answer is **Volume** or **Price**.
    *   *Volume Path:* If clinics could print 2,400 pairs (doubling volume) while keeping fixed software costs at $12k, the software cost per unit drops to $5. Total cost becomes $215. Still a loss, but smaller.
    *   *Price Path:* To break even at 1,200 units, your price must drop to $185 (matching the cap), reducing your monthly revenue to $222,000. Your margin per pair drops from $165 ($210-$45) to $140 ($185-$45).
*   **Rule of Thumb:** If `Price > Customer Max Reimbursement`, the value proposition must shift to non-reimbursable value (e.g., speed, patient experience) that allows the clinic to charge the patient directly, or your price must lower.
