**Lane A Application: Hedging Cost-Benefit Arithmetic**

Your exposure is a known liability: **12,000,000 JPY** due in 3 months.
At your historical low (110 JPY/USD), this cost **$109,090**.
At your historical high (150 JPY/USD), this cost **$80,000**.
**Volatility Range:** $29,090.

Your projected net profit on this volume (assuming similar sales scale to the $250k quarter mentioned) is roughly **12%**. If sales are proportional to the previous $250k quarter, revenue might be ~$133k (based on the $109k cost basis), yielding ~$16k profit. A shift from 110 to 135 JPY/USD alone could erase this margin entirely.

**Decision Framework (Apply, Do Not Direct):**

1.  **The "Do Nothing" Scenario (Dynamic Pricing):**
    *   *Formula:* `Realized Margin = (USD Sales Price) - (JPY Liability / Spot Rate at Payment)`
    *   *Risk:* If the yen strengthens to 120, your cost rises to $100,000. If you cannot raise USD prices immediately due to retailer contracts, your margin compresses by $9,090.
    *   *Threshold:* Can your customers absorb a **5–10% price increase** with <48 hours notice? If no, dynamic pricing fails to protect the 12% margin.

2.  **The Hedging Scenario (Forward Contract):**
    *   *Mechanism:* Locks a specific rate today for payment in 3 months.
    *   *Cost:* Usually embedded in the spread between the "spot" rate and the "forward" rate. Banks often charge 1–3% above the mid-market rate for small business forwards.
    *   *Arithmetic:* If the forward rate is 130 JPY/USD, your cost is fixed at **$92,307**.
    *   *Trade-off:* You guarantee a cost. If the rate moves to 150, you "save" $12,307 vs. spot. If the rate moves to 110, you "lose" $17,783 vs. spot (opportunity cost), but you preserve budget certainty.
    *   *Justification Test:* Is the cost of the forward (the spread) less than the **probability-weighted loss** of an adverse move? Given your margin is only 12%, a 5% adverse move wipes out nearly half your profit.

3.  **The Options Scenario:**
    *   *Mechanism:* Pays an upfront premium for the *right* (not obligation) to buy JPY at a set rate.
    *   *Cost:* Explicit premium (e.g., 2–4% of notional value). On 12M JPY, a 3% premium is ~$3,000–$4,000 cash outlay today.
    *   *Trade-off:* Protects against upside risk (yen getting expensive) while allowing you to benefit if the yen gets cheaper.
    *   *Justification Test:* Does the potential profit from a favorable rate shift exceed the **upfront premium cost**? If the premium ($3k+) exceeds 20% of your expected net profit on this order, it may be too expensive for your risk tolerance.

**Decision Aid:**
*   **Read:** Your margin (12%) is thinner than the historical currency swing range (~26%). Unhedged, you are speculating on FX, not just selling tea.
*   **Flip Input:** The deciding variable is your **pricing power**. If `(Max Price Increase Customers Accept) < (Potential FX Cost Increase)`, hedging is mathematically necessary to survive.
*   **Threshold Rule:** If a single FX event can reduce net profit to <5%, most treasuries hedge at least 50–80% of known exposures.
*   **Next Step:** Contact your bank or a specialized FX broker to request a **quote for a 3-month forward rate** and a **quote for a call option premium** on 12M JPY. Compare the guaranteed cost against your break-even price.

**Note:** Selecting specific instruments (forwards vs. options) involves suitability determinations based on your balance sheet and accounting treatment; discuss quotes with a qualified financial advisor or treasury specialist.
