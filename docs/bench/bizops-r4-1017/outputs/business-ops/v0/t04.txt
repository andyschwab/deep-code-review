### Lane A: Unit Economics & Pricing Sensitivity Analysis

You face a classic margin-vs-volume trade-off. Since you lack specific price elasticity data (how much demand drops when price rises), we cannot calculate the exact outcome. Instead, we will apply a **sensitivity analysis** to show the break-even point where the price increase becomes harmful.

**1. The Inputs (Verified vs. Assumed)**
*   **COGS:** $14.50 (Verified by you).
*   **Shipping:** $8.00 (Verified by you).
*   **Total Variable Cost:** $22.50 per unit.
*   **Scenario A (Absorb Cost):** Price $45.00 | Volume 2,000.
*   **Scenario B (Raise Price):** Price $52.00 | Volume Unknown ($X$).
*   **Marketing Budget Impact:** Not quantified in your request; treated as a fixed reduction in net profit if Scenario A is chosen without other offsets.

**2. The Formulas**
*   `Contribution Margin per Unit = Price − Total Variable Cost`
*   `Total Contribution = Contribution Margin per Unit × Volume`
*   `Net Profit = Total Contribution − Fixed Costs (including Marketing)`

**3. Applied Arithmetic**

**Scenario A: Maintain $45 Price**
*   Margin per Unit: $45.00 − $22.50 = **$22.50**
*   Total Contribution (2,000 units): $22.50 × 2,000 = **$45,000**
*   *Note:* This scenario requires you to absorb the full cost increase. If you reduce your marketing budget to compensate, your total volume (2,000) may organically drop due to lower visibility, further reducing this number.

**Scenario B: Raise Price to $52**
*   Margin per Unit: $52.00 − $22.50 = **$29.50**
*   To match Scenario A's total contribution ($45,000), how many units must you sell?
    *   `Break-even Volume = Target Contribution ÷ New Margin`
    *   `Break-even Volume = $45,000 ÷ $29.50 ≈ 1,526 units`

**4. Decision Aid & Sensitivity**
*   **The Tipping Point:** You can afford to lose up to **474 backers** (2,000 − 1,526) before raising the price yields less money than keeping it low. This represents a **23.7% drop in volume**.
*   **Rule of Thumb:** In crowdfunding, a price increase of ~15% ($7 on $45) often results in a volume drop of 10–20% for established brands, but can be higher for new IPs. If your drop is less than 23.7%, raising the price is mathematically superior for gross contribution.
*   **Marketing Trade-off:** If absorbing the cost forces a marketing cut that reduces visibility by more than 23.7%, Scenario A is strictly worse.
