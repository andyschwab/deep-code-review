### Lane A: Runway Arithmetic & Cash Flow Gap Analysis

**1. Baseline Scenario (Net-15 Terms)**
*   **Formula:** `Runway = Cash Balance ÷ Net Monthly Burn`
*   **Inputs:** $150,000 cash; $18,000 burn.
*   **Calculation:** $150,000 ÷ $18,000 = **8.33 months**.
*   **Context:** This aligns with your internal expectation of ~8 months.

**2. 90-Day Term Scenario (The Cash Gap)**
To model the impact, we must isolate the timing difference between revenue recognition and cash receipt.
*   **Contract Value:** 3 clients × $25,000/year = $75,000 total annual value.
*   **Monthly Revenue Recognized:** $75,000 ÷ 12 = **$6,250/month**.
*   **Payment Delay:** 90 days vs. 15 days = **75-day additional delay**.
*   **Cash Flow Impact:** You will perform ~2.5 months of work ($6,250 × 2.5 ≈ **$15,625**) before seeing the first dollar from these specific contracts.

**The Critical Variable: Administrative Cost of Factoring**
You noted unknown costs for selling invoices (factoring). Factoring typically advances 80–90% of the invoice value immediately, charging a fee (often 1–3% per month until paid).
*   **If you factor:** You receive ~$14,000–$15,000 immediately instead of waiting 90 days, but your net revenue decreases by the fee.
*   **If you do not factor:** Your cash balance drops by $18,000/month while waiting for the $15,625 inflow to start.

**Sensitivity Analysis (Decision Aid)**
Since the exact factoring fee is unknown, here is the structural break-even logic:
*   **Gap Severity:** In the first 3 months under 90-day terms, you pay out ~$54,000 in burn. Without factoring, you receive $0 from these new clients during this window.
*   **Adjusted Runway Estimate:** If you absorb the full 90-day delay without factoring, your effective cash inflow is delayed by one quarter. While the *total* profit over a year remains the same (minus any late fees), your *monthly cash position* worsens immediately.
    *   *Rough Projection:* If you rely solely on the bank balance, the delay does not change the *total* months until zero if the contracts were already counted in the "8 months" baseline. However, if the "8 months" assumed Net-15 cash flow timing, shifting to Net-90 creates a **liquidity cliff** in months 1–3.
    *   *Formula for Decision:* `Required Bridge = (Monthly Burn × Delay Months) - (Factored Advance Amount)`.
    *   If the delay is 2.5 months: $18,000 × 2.5 = **$45,000** potential cash strain before inflows normalize.

**Decision Thresholds & Next Steps**
*   **Rule of Thumb:** If your projected cash balance drops below 3 months of burn ($54,000) before the first invoice clears, you are in a high-risk zone requiring immediate action.
*   **The Flip Input:** The **factoring fee rate**. If the fee is <5% of the invoice value, factoring likely preserves more runway than depleting cash reserves and risking a desperate raise later.
*   **Actionable Step:** Contact two factoring firms today to get a quote on "recourse factoring" for commercial invoices. Plug that specific percentage into the equation: `Net Cash Received = $25,000 × (1 - Fee%)`. If the net cash covers >90% of your burn during the gap, factoring may negate the need for a bridge round.
