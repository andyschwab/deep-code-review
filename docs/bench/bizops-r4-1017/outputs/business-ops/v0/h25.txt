### Lane A: Unit Economics Application

**Current Model Viability**
Based on your inputs, the current transaction fee model is mathematically unsustainable at an $8.50 average order value (AOV).

*   **Revenue per Transaction:** ($8.50 × 1.5%) + $0.30 = $0.1275 + $0.30 = **$0.4275**
*   **Estimated Payment Processor Cost:** Industry standard is often ~2.9% + $0.30. Assuming you pay this: ($8.50 × 2.9%) + $0.30 = $0.2465 + $0.30 = **$0.5465**
*   **Net Revenue per Transaction:** $0.4275 (Your Fee) - $0.5465 (Processor Cost) = **-$0.119** (Loss per order)

Even if your "payment processor fees" are lower than the standard estimate, your revenue of $0.43 per transaction must cover both the processor cost and your fixed costs. With 5,000 transactions, total monthly revenue is **$2,137.50**. After subtracting fixed server costs ($1,200), you have **$937.50** remaining to cover two developer salaries and any processor fees. This leaves negative contribution margin if processor fees exceed ~$0.18 per transaction.

**Break-Even Analysis for 20% Gross Margin**
To achieve a 20% gross margin on the transaction itself (excluding fixed costs), we solve for the required AOV where your fee covers the processor cost plus the margin.

*Assumption:* Processor fees are 2.9% + $0.30.
*Formula:* `Your Fee = Processor Cost + (Your Fee × 0.20)` is incorrect; margin is usually on revenue.
*Correct Formula for 20% Margin on Revenue:* `Net Revenue = Gross Revenue × 0.20`.
Therefore: `Gross Revenue - Processor Cost = 0.20 × Gross Revenue`.
`0.80 × Gross Revenue = Processor Cost`.

Let $x$ = Average Order Value.
Your Gross Revenue = $0.015x + 0.30$
Processor Cost = $0.029x + 0.30$

Equation: $(0.015x + 0.30) - (0.029x + 0.30) = 0.20(0.015x + 0.30)$
$-0.014x = 0.003x + 0.06$
This yields a negative $x$, indicating **it is mathematically impossible to achieve a positive margin, let alone 20%, with a 1.5% fee if the processor charges 2.9%**. You are arbitraging a spread that does not exist.

**Revised Scenario: Passing fees to customers or negotiating processor rates**
If you negotiate processor rates down to 2.0% + $0.30 and charge the user 1.5% + $0.30, you still lose on the percentage spread. The only way this model works is if the **customer** pays the full processor fee plus your markup, or if you switch to a flat fee that exceeds costs.

**Minimum AOV Calculation (Flat Fee Alternative)**
If you charged a flat $1.00 fee per order (customer paid):
Revenue: $1.00. Cost (2.9% + $0.30 on $8.50): $0.55. Net: $0.45.
Margin: 45%.
To get 20% margin with your current *percentage* model, your fee percentage must exceed the processor's percentage. Since 1.5% < 2.9%, no AOV fixes this. You must raise the percentage fee above the processor's rate (e.g., charge 4%) or switch to a higher flat fee.
