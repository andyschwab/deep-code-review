### Lane A: Financial Impact Analysis (Arithmetic)

We can model the impact of the freight hike on your margins and runway using your provided numbers. We cannot fabricate your total order volume, so we will analyze the impact **per order** and show how that scales to your runway.

**1. The Cost Shock**
*   **Current Average Order Value (AOV):** $85.00
*   **Current Gross Profit (22%):** $18.70
*   **Current Cost of Goods + Freight:** $66.30
*   **Freight Increase (15% of cost base):** Assuming freight is part of the cost base, a precise calculation requires knowing what portion of the $66.30 is freight.
    *   *Scenario Assumption:* If we assume freight represents roughly 20% of your total costs (a common benchmark for heavy goods), current freight is ~$13.26.
    *   **New Freight Cost:** $13.26 × 1.15 = $15.25.
    *   **Incremental Cost per Order:** ~$1.99.
    *   **New Gross Profit (if absorbed):** $18.70 - $1.99 = **$16.71** (Margin drops from 22% to ~19.7%).

**2. Runway Sensitivity (The "Absorb" Option)**
*   **Current Monthly Burn:** $12,000.
*   **Current Cash:** $12,000 × 6 months = $72,000.
*   **Impact of Absorbing:** To maintain the same net profit, you must sell more units to cover the fixed costs.
    *   If your fixed costs are high and the $12k burn includes COGS, the math shifts. If $12k is *operating* burn (rent, salaries) and COGS is separate:
    *   Let $N$ be monthly orders. Current Contribution = $N \times 18.70$.
    *   New Contribution = $N \times 16.71$.
    *   **Break-even Volume Increase:** You need to sell $\frac{18.70}{16.71} \approx 12\%$ more units just to maintain the same gross profit dollar amount. If volume stays flat, your monthly profit contribution drops by ~12%, directly shortening runway.

**3. The "Pass-It-On" Option (Price Increase)**
*   **Required Price Hike:** To maintain the $18.70 margin, you must pass the full ~$1.99 cost to the customer.
*   **New Price:** $86.99 (roughly a $2.00 increase).
*   **Churn Risk:** Last year, a $5.00 increase (5.9%) caused 3% churn.
    *   *Linear Extrapolation (Rough Estimate):* A $2.00 increase (2.3%) might cause ~1.2% churn.
    *   **Revenue Impact:** Losing 1.2% of 400 farms = ~5 farms.
    *   **Net Result:** You retain full margin on remaining orders but lose the contribution from 5 farms.
    *   *Comparison:* Losing 5 farms (at $18.70 profit each) = ~$93.50 lost monthly profit. Absorbing the cost on 395 orders = $1.99 × 395 = ~$786 lost monthly profit.
    *   **Mathematical Indication:** Based on your historical elasticity, **passing the cost appears less damaging to monthly cash flow** than absorbing it, provided the churn does not exceed ~6% (the point where lost volume profit equals absorbed cost).

**Decision Aid:**
*   **Formula:** `Break-even Churn % = (Cost Increase per Unit) / (New Price per Unit)`.
*   **Threshold:** If your actual churn from a $2 hike is < 6%, passing the cost preserves more runway.
*   **Next Step:** Survey 20 loyal customers this week: "If we adjust prices by $2 to offset carrier hikes, will you continue ordering?" Use this to validate the churn assumption before changing rates for all 400.
