### Lane A: Arithmetic & Structural Options
Your current model exposes you to a specific arithmetic risk. With 0.5 oz of gold per ring, a $50/oz swing equals a **$25 variance in material cost per unit**. Since your fixed labor fee is $400, a $25 increase represents a **6.25% erosion of your total contribution margin** ($25 / $400) on that specific job. If gold spikes $100/oz during your three-week cycle, you lose $50 per ring, wiping out 12.5% of your labor margin.

Here are three structural options applied to your numbers. The decision on which to adopt is yours.

**Option 1: Quote Validity Period (Time-Bound Pricing)**
*   **Structure:** Explicitly state that quotes are valid for $X$ days (e.g., 48 hours or 7 days).
*   **Math:** If the spot price changes after expiration, the quote is recalculated: $New Price = (New Spot \times 0.5) + 400$.
*   **Trade-off:** Zero financial risk to you; potential friction if clients hesitate.
*   **Application:** "Quote valid for 7 days. Subject to gold spot price adjustment thereafter."

**Option 2: Non-Refundable Deposit Tied to Metal Weight**
*   **Structure:** Require an upfront deposit equal to the current cost of the gold content.
*   **Math:** Deposit = $Current Spot \times 0.5$. This locks your material cost immediately.
*   **Trade-off:** Eliminates commodity risk entirely; requires higher upfront commitment from the client.
*   **Application:** "A deposit of $[Spot Price] \times 0.5$ is required to secure materials. Balance due upon completion."

**Option 3: Volatility Buffer (Cost-Plus Adjustment)**
*   **Structure:** Add a fixed percentage buffer to the gold cost to absorb average swings.
*   **Math:** If weekly volatility averages $25/oz ($12.50/ring), you might add a $15–$20 buffer per ring.
    *   $New Base = (Spot + Buffer per oz) \times 0.5 + 400$.
*   **Trade-off:** Simpler for the customer (one price); you retain some risk if swings exceed the buffer, or you may overprice in stable weeks.

**Decision Aid:**
*   **Break-even protection:** To ensure you *never* lose money on material swings (your stated goal), **Option 2** is the only arithmetic certainty, as it transfers the spot-price timing risk to the client at the moment of agreement.
*   **Sensitivity:** If you choose Option 3, a buffer of $30/oz ($15/ring) covers a $60 swing. If swings exceed $60/oz, your margin is again at risk.
*   **Next Step:** Calculate your average weekly gold volatility over the last 6 months. If the average swing is >$40/oz, consider that **Option 1** (short validity) combined with **Option 2** (material deposit) is the standard approach for bespoke manufacturers to isolate labor margin from commodity futures.

### Lane R: Regulatory & Legal Routing
**Regime:** Contract Law and Consumer Protection Regulations (New York State/City).

**Routing Required:**
You asked how to "structure pricing agreements" and ensure you "never lose money." While the math above shows how to calculate the numbers, the **enforceability** of these clauses is a legal determination.
*   **Validity Periods & Price Adjustments:** New York consumer protection laws may have specific requirements on how price increases post-agreement are communicated or permitted, especially for retail goods.
*   **Non-Refundable Deposits:** NY General Business Law and specific jewelry regulations often strictly govern what can be labeled "non-refundable" and under what conditions deposits must be returned. An improperly drafted clause could be deemed an unfair trade practice.
