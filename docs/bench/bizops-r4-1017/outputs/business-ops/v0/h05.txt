### Lane A: Capital Allocation Arithmetic

Your current cash reserve (£50k) covers less than two months of operations (£30k/month burn) and is insufficient to cover a single maximum-liability event (£250k). Allocating enough cash to fully self-insure this risk would require holding £250k in liquid assets, which exceeds your current revenue and would cripple operations by removing working capital.

**The Math of Self-Insurance vs. Operations**
*   **Maximum Exposure:** £250,000
*   **Current Reserve:** £50,000
*   **Monthly Burn:** £30,000
*   **Runway with Current Reserve:** ~1.7 months (`£50k ÷ £30k`)
*   **Capital Required to Fully Reserve Risk:** £250,000 (which is 8.3 months of burn).

**Applied Strategy: The "Layered" Approach**
Since holding £250k in cash is likely impossible without starving growth, the arithmetic suggests a layered defense rather than a single cash bucket:
1.  **Operational Floor:** Maintain 3–4 months of burn (£90k–£120k) for liquidity. You are currently below this.
2.  **Risk Retention Limit:** Decide the maximum cash loss you can absorb without bankruptcy (e.g., £50k). This is the amount you effectively "self-insure."
3.  **The Gap:** Any liability above your retention limit (£50k) must be covered by insurance or contractual caps, not cash reserves.
    *   *Formula:* `Required Coverage = Max Loss (£250k) - Cash Retention (£50k) = £200k`.
    *   Your current insurance premium (£15k) buys coverage against this gap. If premiums double again, the math may force a reduction in the volume of certifications or an increase in fees to maintain the margin required to pay the premium.

**Decision Aid:**
*   **Read:** Your current reserve covers survival, not liability.
*   **Flip Input:** The variable that changes the outcome is the **Insurance Deductible**. If you raise your deductible to £50k (matching your cash reserve), your premium should drop, aligning your cash holdings with your retained risk.
*   **Threshold Rule:** If `Annual Premium > 5% of Revenue` (£30k), the cost of risk transfer is threatening unit economics; reconsider pricing or volume.
