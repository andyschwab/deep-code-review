### Lane A: Unit Economics & Pricing Sensitivity Analysis

You are facing a classic marketplace leakage problem where the commission fee exceeds the perceived value of the platform's services (lead generation, payment processing, trust/safety). To determine if lowering the commission from 20% to 10% increases net revenue, we must model the **volume elasticity** required to offset the rate cut.

**1. The Break-Even Volume Formula**
To maintain the same total revenue after a price (commission) cut, your transaction volume must increase by a specific percentage. The formula is:

$$ \text{Required Volume Increase} = \frac{\text{Old Rate} - \text{New Rate}}{\text{New Rate}} $$

Applying your numbers:
*   **Old Rate:** 20% ($0.20)
*   **New Rate:** 10% ($0.10)
*   **Calculation:** $(0.20 - 0.10) / 0.10 = 1.0$ or **100%**

**Analysis:** If you drop your commission to 10%, you must **double (100% increase)** the number of booked lessons just to generate the same $42,000 in gross revenue you made last month at 20%. If volume increases by less than 100%, your total revenue declines.

**2. Estimating the Leakage Threshold (Supply Side Elasticity)**
You asked at what rate tutors feel compelled to leave. While exact elasticity requires A/B testing, we can estimate the "churn point" based on the tutor's alternative cost.
*   **Current Tutor Net:** $20/hour.
*   **Off-Platform Net:** ~$25/hour (minus their own payment processing fees, typically ~3%).
*   **Incentive to Defect:** The tutor gains ~$4.25/hour by going off-platform.
*   **Proposed Tutor Net (at 10%):** $22.50/hour.
*   **Remaining Incentive to Defect:** The tutor still gains ~$1.75/hour by going off-platform.

**Implication:** Lowering the commission to 10% reduces the financial incentive to defect but does not eliminate it. Unless the platform provides non-monetary value (scheduling tools, curriculum, dispute resolution) worth at least $1.75/hour to the tutor, leakage may persist even at the lower rate.

**3. Decision Framework**
Do not guess the elasticity; instrument it.
*   **Scenario A (Lower Rate):** Viable only if you believe the 15% decline in repeat bookings is purely price-sensitive and that a rate cut will trigger >100% volume growth (via retained tutors + new referrals).
*   **Scenario B (Enforce/Add Value):** Viable if the 15% decline is due to lack of stickiness. If you can add tools worth $2.50/hour to the tutor, the 20% fee becomes justified ($20 net + $2.50 value > $25 off-platform).

**Next Step:** Run a controlled experiment. Select a cohort of 50 tutors and offer them a temporary 12% rate for 30 days in exchange for strict adherence to platform rules. Measure if their repeat booking rate stabilizes compared to a control group. This provides real data on your specific elasticity before committing to a global price change.

*Note: The 100% volume requirement to break even on a rate halving is a steep hurdle; ensure your marketing funnel can support this scale before cutting prices.*
