### Break-Even and Pricing Analysis (Lane A)

**1. The Math: Revised Depreciation & Break-Even**
We will recalculate your monthly costs and break-even point using your actual inputs and the revised two-year equipment lifespan.

*   **Inputs Provided:**
    *   Operational Costs (OpEx): £8,500/month
    *   Drone Fleet Cost: 4 drones × £3,000 = £12,000 total
    *   Revised Lifespan: 2 years (24 months)
    *   Volume: 15 weddings/month
    *   Target Net Margin: 25%

*   **Step A: Calculate Monthly Depreciation (CapEx)**
    *   *Old Assumption (4 years):* £12,000 ÷ 48 months = £250/month
    *   *New Reality (2 years):* £12,000 ÷ 24 months = **£500/month**
    *   *Impact:* Your fixed costs have increased by £250/month due to faster depreciation.

*   **Step B: Calculate Total Monthly Cost**
    *   £8,500 (OpEx) + £500 (Depreciation) = **£9,000 Total Monthly Cost**

*   **Step C: Calculate Break-Even Price Per Wedding**
    *   Formula: `Break-Even Price = Total Monthly Cost ÷ Monthly Weddings`
    *   Calculation: £9,000 ÷ 15 = **£600 per wedding**
    *   *Note:* This is the price where you make £0 profit. Your current price of £1,200 is well above this.

*   **Step D: Calculate Price for 25% Net Margin**
    *   Formula: `Target Price = Total Monthly Cost ÷ (1 - Target Margin)`
    *   Calculation: £9,000 ÷ (1 - 0.25) = £9,000 ÷ 0.75 = **£12,000 Total Revenue Needed**
    *   Price Per Wedding: £12,000 ÷ 15 = **£800 per wedding**

**2. Decision Aid & Sensitivity**
*   **One-line read:** Even with accelerated depreciation, your required price to hit a 25% margin (£800) is significantly lower than your current price (£1,200), suggesting your current pricing already covers the risk if volume holds.
*   **The Flip Input:** The variable that most threatens this model is **volume**. If bookings drop from 15 to 10 weddings/month, your cost per unit rises to £900 (£9,000 ÷ 10), pushing the target price to £1,200 exactly.
*   **Threshold Rule of Thumb:** If your LTV:CAC ratio drops below 3 or your monthly volume consistently falls below 12 weddings, re-evaluate your fixed cost structure before changing prices.
