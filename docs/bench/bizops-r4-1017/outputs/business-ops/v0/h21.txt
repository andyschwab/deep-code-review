### Cash Flow Analysis: Acme Frames Payment Plan Scenario

Based on the figures provided, here is the arithmetic applied to your 45-day horizon to determine if reserves dip below the $18,000 production threshold.

**1. The Constraints & Inputs**
*   **Current Cash:** $22,500
*   **Critical Threshold:** $18,000 (Material cost for next batch)
*   **Available Buffer:** $4,500 ($22,500 - $18,000)
*   **Timeframe:** 45 days (~1.5 months)
*   **Production Cycle:** Every 6 weeks (approx. 42 days). A new batch is due within your 45-day window.
*   **Pricing:** $2,400 per frame.
*   **Installment Terms:** $800/month for 3 months.
*   **Adoption Rate:** 40% of customers choose installments; 60% pay upfront ($2,400).

**2. The Cash Flow Gap Calculation**
To assess the threat, we must compare the cash collected under the new plan versus the current all-upfront model over the first 45 days.

*   **Scenario A: Current Model (100% Upfront)**
    *   Cash collected per order: $2,400 immediately.
    *   *Result:* Every order adds full liquidity instantly.

*   **Scenario B: Proposed Model (40% Installment / 60% Upfront)**
    *   **Upfront Payers (60%):** Collect $2,400 immediately.
    *   **Installment Payers (40%):**
        *   Month 1 (Day 0-30): Collect $800.
        *   Month 2 (Day 31-45): Collect $800 (prorated or full depending on billing date, assume full second payment hits within 45 days for conservative estimation, or partial. Let's assume strict monthly cycles: Payment 1 at Day 0, Payment 2 at Day 30).
        *   Total collected per installment customer in 45 days: $1,600 ($800 + $800).
    *   **Deferred Revenue per Installment Order:** $2,400 (full price) - $1,600 (collected) = **$800 delayed per installment order.**

**3. Break-Even Volume Analysis**
You have a **$4,500 buffer** before hitting the danger zone ($18,000).
The strategy threatens the next batch only if the *total deferred cash* from installment plans exceeds $4,500 within this 45-day window.

*   **Formula:** `Max Installment Orders = Available Buffer ÷ Deferred Cash per Order`
*   **Calculation:** $4,500 ÷ $800 = **5.625 orders**.

**Interpretation:**
If you accept **6 or more** orders via the installment plan within the next 45 days, your cash balance will drop below $18,000 before the second payments fully replenish the account, potentially delaying the material purchase for the next batch.

Since 40% of your total orders are expected to be installments:
*   **Total Order Threshold:** If 6 orders represent 40% of your volume, then `Total Orders = 6 ÷ 0.40 = 15 orders`.
*   **Risk Zone:** If you sell **more than 15 frames** in the next 45 days, the delayed cash flow from the 40% mix will likely breach your $18,000 threshold.
*   **Safety Zone:** If you sell **15 frames or fewer**, your current $22,500 reserve is sufficient to bridge the gap.

**Decision Aid**
*   **The Read:** Your reserves can absorb the delay for roughly 6 installment customers (15 total frames) over 45 days.
*   **The Flip Input:** The critical variable is **Total Order Volume**. If demand exceeds 15 frames in this period, the plan creates a liquidity crunch.
*   **Rule of Thumb:** If LTV (here, price) is fixed but cash timing shifts, ensure `Cash Buffer > (Volume × Adoption % × Deferred Amount)`.
*   **Next Step:** If you anticipate >15 orders in 45 days, consider requiring a larger down payment (e.g., $1,200) for the installment plan to reduce the deferred amount per unit, or secure a small line of credit specifically for material bridging before launching the offer.

*Note: This analysis assumes no other operating expenses (wages, rent) are paid from this specific cash bucket; if wages must be paid from the $22,500, your buffer is significantly smaller, lowering the safe order threshold.*
*Churn rate (2%) affects future pipeline volume but does not alter the immediate cash math on confirmed orders within this 45-day window.*
