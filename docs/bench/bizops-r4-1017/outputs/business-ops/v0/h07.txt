### Lane A: Pricing Mechanism Application (Arithmetic)

To protect your 12% net margin against 20% monthly raw material volatility without abandoning long-term clients, you should apply a **Cost-Plus with Floating Surcharge** model rather than a fixed price. This separates the stable value of your service from the volatile commodity cost.

**The Formula:**
`Final Price = Base Price + (Raw Material Index × Volume × Adjustment Factor)`

**Applied to ChemClean's Numbers:**
1.  **Current Unit Economics:**
    *   Sale Price: $800/drum
    *   Net Margin: 12% ($96/drum)
    *   Implied Total Cost: $704/drum
    *   Raw Material Cost (60% of COGS): $422.40/drum
    *   Other Costs (Labor, Overhead, etc.): $281.60/drum

2.  **Volatility Impact:**
    *   A 20% spike in raw materials = $422.40 × 0.20 = **$84.48 increase per drum**.
    *   Since your net profit is only $96, this single spike reduces your margin to $11.52 (approx. 1.4%), effectively erasing profitability as you noted.

3.  **Proposed Mechanism Structure:**
    *   **Base Price ($720):** Covers non-volatile costs ($281.60) + Target Profit ($96) + A buffered portion of material costs. This remains fixed for contract stability.
    *   **Floating Surcharge:** Tied directly to a specific chemical index (e.g., ICIS or Platts) relevant to your primary raw material.
    *   **Calculation:** `Surcharge = (Current Index Price - Base Index Price) × Conversion Ratio`.
    *   **Threshold (Collar):** To ease client adoption, set a "dead band" where fluctuations under 5% are absorbed by you, and anything above 5% triggers the surcharge.

**Decision Aid:**
*   **Read:** At current volatility, a fixed price guarantees margin erosion; a floating surcharge transfers the commodity risk back to the market while locking in your processing fee.
*   **Flip Input:** The critical variable is the **Base Index Price**. If set too high, you lose bids; if too low, you trigger surcharges immediately, causing friction.
*   **Rule of Thumb:** If the surcharge triggers more than 3 months in a rolling year, your base price buffer is too thin.
