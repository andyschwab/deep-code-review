### Applied Unit Economics: Per-Case vs. Per-Hour

**Current State (Per-Case Model)**
*   **Revenue per case:** $200 (Fixed)
*   **Cost per case:** 2.5 hours × $60/hr = $150
*   **Margin per case:** $50
*   **Monthly Revenue (400 cases):** $80,000
*   **Monthly Cost:** $60,000
*   **Monthly Margin:** $20,000
*   **Quarterly Revenue:** $240,000 (Exceeds your $50k goal)

**Scenario A: Complex Cases Dragging (Hourly Model Risk)**
If clients do not change behavior and cases average 2.5 hours:
*   **Revenue per case:** 2.5 hours × $X (Your Hourly Rate)
*   **Break-even Hourly Rate:** To match current $50 margin, you must charge >$80/hr ($150 cost + $50 margin).
*   **Risk:** If you set the rate too low (e.g., $70/hr), revenue drops to $175/case, reducing margin to $25.

**Scenario B: Clients Settle Faster (Hourly Model Benefit)**
If hourly billing incentivizes faster settlement (e.g., avg. 1.5 hours):
*   **Cost per case:** 1.5 × $60 = $90
*   **Revenue per case (@ $100/hr example):** $150
*   **Margin per case:** $60
*   **Volume Impact:** If higher perceived cost reduces volume by 20% (320 cases):
    *   Total Margin: 320 × $60 = $19,200 (Lower than current $20,000).

**The "Money Left on the Table" Analysis**
Under the flat fee, a 5-hour case costs you $300 (5 × $60) but earns $200, resulting in a **-$100 loss per complex case**.
*   **Hourly Protection:** An hourly model eliminates negative margin on long cases, provided your rate exceeds $60/hr.
*   **Trade-off:** You trade guaranteed margin on standard cases for protection against loss leaders (complex cases).

### Decision Aid

1.  **One-line read:** Your current model generates strong aggregate margin ($20k/mo) but hides specific losses on any case exceeding 3.3 hours; an hourly model fixes the loss leakage but introduces volume risk if clients resist open-ended billing.
2.  **The Flip Input:** The break-even duration for your current flat fee is **3.33 hours** ($200 fee ÷ $60 cost). Any case longer than this erodes profit. The decision hinges on the **percentage of cases exceeding 3.33 hours**.
3.  **Thresholds:**
    *   If >15% of your cases exceed 3.33 hours, the flat fee is likely dragging down total margin.
    *   If switching to hourly causes volume to drop by >10%, the gain from efficiency may not offset the lost volume.
4.  **Next Step:** Before changing the public model, run a "hybrid" test next week: Keep the $200 flat fee for cases estimated under 3 hours, but apply an hourly rate (e.g., $90/hr) for any time exceeding that threshold. This captures upside on complex disputes without scaring off standard users.

**Note:** You have 15 mediators; ensure their utilization rates are tracked separately from pricing, as idle time affects margin regardless of the billing model.
