**The Regime:** US State Sales & Use Tax Laws (Post-*South Dakota v. Wayfair*).
**The Risk:** Your current approach of collecting *no* sales tax based on an assumption of "virtual presence exemption" carries significant liability risk. Since the 2018 *Wayfair* ruling, physical presence is no longer the sole determinant for nexus. Most states now enforce **Economic Nexus**, where liability is triggered strictly by revenue or transaction volume into that state, regardless of physical offices.

**Key Factors Determining Liability (To Discuss with Counsel):**
1.  **Service Classification:** States differ on whether "virtual interior design" is a taxable digital product, a taxable service, or a non-taxable professional service. Some states tax the delivery of digital files; others exempt professional consulting.
2.  **Economic Nexus Thresholds:** While many states use a standard of $100,000 in gross revenue or 200 transactions annually, these figures are not universal. Some states have lower thresholds or count transactions differently.
3.  **Sourcing Rules:** Determining *where* the sale occurs (origin-based vs. destination-based) dictates which state's rate and rules apply to your remote clients.
4.  **Accumulating Liability:** By not collecting tax now, you may be accruing "use tax" liability that the end-client is theoretically responsible for but rarely pays, leaving your business exposed to audits, back-taxes, penalties, and interest.
