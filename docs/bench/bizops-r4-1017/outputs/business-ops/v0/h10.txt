### Lane A: Revenue Forecasting & Cash Flow Modeling (Arithmetic Application)

Because your volume is driven by external court schedules rather than steady demand, a standard linear forecast will fail. You should apply a **probabilistic scenario model** using your historical range ($90k–$140k) rather than a single point estimate.

**The Modeling Approach:**
1.  **Define Inputs:** Use your knowns: Fixed Overhead ($25k), Avg Case Value ($3k), and Historical Volume (30–47 cases/month derived from revenue range).
2.  **Scenario Construction:** Build three monthly columns for the next 12 months:
    *   *Conservative:* 75% of your average volume (approx. 30 cases → $90k revenue).
    *   *Base:* Your average volume (approx. 37 cases → $111k revenue).
    *   *Optimistic:* 90th percentile volume (approx. 47 cases → $140k revenue).
3.  **Variable Cost Calculation:** Since translators are paid per project, estimate your cost-per-case (e.g., if you pay 50% to translators, variable cost is $1.5k/case).
    *   *Contribution Margin* = Case Value − Variable Cost per Case.
    *   *Monthly Net Burn/Profit* = (Cases × Contribution Margin) − Fixed Overhead ($25k).
4.  **Cash Flow Projection:** Apply a **payment lag assumption**. If clients pay in 45 days, shift the revenue input from the "Case Month" to the "Collection Month."
    *   *Formula:* `Ending Cash = Beginning Cash + (Prior Month Revenue × Collection %) − (Current Month Variable Costs + Fixed Overhead)`.

**Decision Aid:**
*   **Read:** If your "Conservative" scenario shows negative cash flow for >2 months, your fixed overhead is too high for your volatility, regardless of average performance.
*   **Flip Input:** The break-even volume is `Fixed Overhead ÷ Contribution Margin per Case`. If your margin is $1.5k, you need ~17 cases just to cover overhead; anything below this burns cash.
*   **Threshold Rule:** If the probability of dipping below 20 cases in any month exceeds 10%, secure a minimum cash reserve equal to 3 months of fixed overhead ($75k) before expanding.
*   **Next Step:** Instrument your last 12 months of case data by "court" or "law firm" to see if specific clients drive the volatility, then consider retainer pricing for your top 3 most frequent filers to smooth the baseline.
