### Financial Impact Evaluation (Lane A: Arithmetic)

**Current Baseline**
*   **Annual Revenue:** 50,000 units × $12.50 = $625,000
*   **New COGS per Unit:** Current COGS + $1.80 tariff
*   **Immediate Margin Erosion:** 50,000 units × $1.80 = **$90,000 annual profit reduction** if no action is taken.

**Option 1: Price Increase to $14.50**
*   **New Price:** $14.50 (+$2.00 increase covers the $1.80 tariff + $0.20 buffer).
*   **Break-Even Volume Analysis:** To maintain current *gross profit dollars* (assuming the $1.80 is the only cost change), you need to calculate the volume drop you can sustain.
    *   *Formula:* `Required Volume = Current Gross Profit / (New Price - New COGS)`
    *   *Application:* Without your exact current COGS, we use the margin impact. If you raise price by $2.00 and cost rose by $1.80, your net gain per unit is $0.20 *higher* than pre-tariff margins, provided volume stays flat.
    *   *Sensitivity Check (The 15% Question):* If volume drops by 15% (selling 42,500 units):
        *   New Revenue: 42,500 × $14.50 = $616,250 (vs $625,000 baseline).
        *   You lose $8,750 in top-line revenue.
        *   *Decision Variable:* Does the $0.20 extra margin per unit on 42,500 units outweigh the loss of contribution margin from the 7,500 lost units?
        *   *Rule of Thumb:* If your pre-tariff contribution margin was high (>60%), a 15% volume drop is likely dangerous. If margins were thin, the price hike might still yield higher total profit despite lower volume.

**Option 2: Reformulation (Local Fillers)**
*   **Cost Impact:** Potentially eliminates the $1.80 tariff exposure.
*   **Risk Variable:** Brand damage.
*   **Arithmetic of Churn:** If reformulation causes a one-time brand reputation hit resulting in a permanent 10% volume drop (5,000 units) without a price increase:
    *   Lost Revenue: 5,000 × $12.50 = $62,500 annually.
    *   Compare this $62,500 loss against the $90,000 tariff cost. Reformulation is financially superior *only if* the volume loss remains below ~7,200 units (assuming zero cost savings from fillers other than tariff avoidance).

### Strategic Decision Aid

1.  **One-Line Read:** The $90,000 tariff hit is immediate; the price increase risks revenue loss only if demand is highly elastic (sensitive), while reformulation risks permanent brand erosion that is mathematically costly if churn exceeds 14.4%.
2.  **The Flip Input:** The critical unknown is **Price Elasticity of Demand**.
    *   *Formula:* `% Change in Quantity Demanded / % Change in Price`.
    *   Your scenario: Price rises 16% ($12.50 to $14.50). If volume drops >15%, elasticity is >1 (elastic), and the price hike destroys value.
3.  **Thresholds:**
    *   If you believe volume loss will be **<12%**, raise prices (mathematically safer than brand risk).
    *   If you believe volume loss will be **>18%**, do not raise prices; consider reformulation or absorbing costs temporarily.
4.  **Next Step (This Week):** Do not guess the elasticity. Run a **Van Westendorp Price Sensitivity Survey** with a sample of your existing retailers or end-consumers. Ask: "At what price would this spice mix be too expensive to consider?" and "At what price would it be a bargain?" Use the resulting price band to estimate the likely volume drop before committing to the $14.50 tag.

**Note:** Since you export to the EU, verify if "Made in USA" labeling requirements change if you alter the recipe with local fillers; this is a regulatory question (Lane R) requiring counsel.
