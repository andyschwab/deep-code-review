### Cash Flow Modeling for Extended Lead Times

To model the impact of shifting from a two-week to a three-month (12-week) material lead time, you must calculate the additional **Working Capital Gap** created by holding inventory longer before production and billing can begin.

**1. The Core Formula: Additional Working Capital Required**
The additional cash needed is the cost of materials you must purchase upfront but cannot convert to revenue for an extended period.

$$ \text{Additional Capital} = (\text{New Lead Time} - \text{Old Lead Time}) \times \text{Weekly Material Burn Rate} $$

*   **Current State:** 2-week lead time.
*   **New State:** 12-week lead time.
*   **Delta:** +10 weeks of inventory holding.

**2. Applying Your Numbers (Assumptions Required)**
You provided fixed costs ($900/unit) and headcount (12 builders), but not the **production rate** (units per week). This is the critical missing variable.

*   *Variable A (Production Rate):* Let $R$ = Kayaks built per week.
*   *Variable B (Weekly Material Cost):* $R \times \$900$.
*   *Calculation:* $\text{Gap} = 10 \text{ weeks} \times (R \times \$900)$.

*Example Scenario:* If your 12 builders produce 4 kayaks/week:
*   Weekly Material Cost = $4 \times \$900 = \$3,600$.
*   Additional Capital Needed = $10 \times \$3,600 = \$36,000$.

If production is 10 kayaks/week:
*   Weekly Material Cost = $10 \times \$900 = \$9,000$.
*   Additional Capital Needed = $10 \times \$9,000 = \$90,000$.

**3. Solvency vs. Liquidity Threshold**
*   **Liquidity Squeeze:** You have cash ($150k) but it is tied up in inventory (wood/resin) rather than being available for payroll. You cannot pay bills despite having assets.
*   **Solvency Issue:** This occurs if $\text{Total Cash Required} > \$150,000$ AND you cannot secure external financing.
    *   *Threshold Formula:* Solve for $R$ where $(\text{Weekly Overhead} + \text{Weekly Payroll}) \times \text{Months to Revenue} > \$150,000$.
    *   Since you cannot bill until production starts, your **Cash Conversion Cycle** has expanded by ~2.5 months. If your monthly burn rate (payroll + overhead) exceeds $\$60,000$ ($\$150k / 2.5$), you face insolvency before the first new boat sells.

**4. Key Variables to Adjust in Projections**
*   **Days Inventory Outstanding (DIO):** Increase this metric to reflect 12 weeks + production time, up from ~2 weeks.
*   **Cash Conversion Cycle (CCC):** Recalculate as $DIO + DSO - DPO$. Your CCC will spike significantly.
*   **Reorder Point:** Adjust to trigger orders 12 weeks out, not 2.
*   **Safety Stock:** You may need to increase this buffer if supply reliability is now volatile.

**5. Risks to Present to the Bank**
When requesting a line of credit, frame the request around these specific risks:
*   **Inventory Concentration Risk:** A larger percentage of assets is now illiquid (raw wood) for longer periods.
*   **Supplier Reliability:** The shift from bi-weekly to quarterly delivery indicates systemic supply chain fragility; the loan bridges this structural gap, not a performance failure.
*   **Revenue Timing Mismatch:** Cash outflows (material purchases) now precede cash inflows (customer billing) by an additional 10 weeks, creating a temporary but severe working capital hole.
*   **Mitigation:** Show that once the new cycle stabilizes, the cash flow returns to normal; the need is for a one-time bridge, not permanent subsidy.

**Decision Aid:**
Calculate your exact weekly production rate ($R$). If $10 \times R \times \$900$ plus your payroll for those 10 weeks exceeds $\$150,000$, you are in the solvency zone and must secure funding immediately to avoid halting operations.
