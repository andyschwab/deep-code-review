### Lane A: Unit Economics & Pricing Scenarios (Applied Math)

**Current Baseline:**
*   **Monthly Revenue:** $48,000
*   **Avg Ticket:** $120 → **Volume:** 400 jobs/month ($48,000 / $120)
*   **Variable Cost (excl. fuel spike):** $65
*   **Contribution Margin (CM) per job:** $120 - $65 = $55
*   **Total Monthly CM:** 400 * $55 = $22,000
*   **Net Margin:** 18% of $48,000 = $8,640 Net Profit
*   **Implied Fixed Costs:** Total CM ($22,000) - Net Profit ($8,640) = $13,360/month
*   **Fuel Spike Impact:** 12 vans * $8/day * ~22 work days = **$2,112 additional monthly cost** if absorbed.

**Elasticity Assumption:**
*   Rule: 10% volume drop per $5 price increase.
*   Sensitivity: 2% volume drop per $1 price increase.

**Scenario Modeling (Passing cost to consumer):**
*Assumption: The surcharge is added to the $120 base price. Volume reacts to the total price increase.*

| Scenario | Surcharge Amount (Est.) | New Total Price | Est. Volume Drop | New Volume | New Revenue | New Variable Costs* | New CM | Net Profit Estimate** |
| :--- | :--- | :--- | :--- | :--- | :--- | :--- | :--- | :--- |
| **1. Flat Fee** | $8.00 (avg per job equiv) | $128.00 | ~3.2% (8/5 * 10%) | 387 | $49,536 | $25,155 | $24,381 | $11,021 |
| **2. Percentage** | ~6.7% ($8.04) | $128.04 | ~3.2% | 387 | $49,551 | $25,163 | $24,388 | $11,028 |
| **3. Distance** | Variable (Avg $8.00) | $128.00 (avg) | ~3.2% | 387 | $49,536 | $25,155 | $24,381 | $11,021 |

*\*Variable Costs include original $65/job. Fuel surcharge revenue offsets the $2,112 fixed fuel cost increase, effectively neutralizing the fuel expense line in the P&L, but volume loss reduces total contribution.*
*\**Net Profit = New CM - Fixed Costs ($13,360).*

**Analysis:**
*   **Absorbing Cost:** Net Profit drops from $8,640 to $6,528 ($8,640 - $2,112).
*   **Passing Cost (Any Model):** Net Profit rises to ~$11,020 despite a ~3.2% volume drop.
*   **Decision Aid:** Mathematically, passing the charge yields ~$2,400 more monthly profit than absorbing it, even with projected churn. The "flip input" is elasticity; if your actual volume drop exceeds 6.4% (roughly double the historical rate), absorbing the cost becomes safer.
*   **Next Step:** If you proceed, test the flat fee on 20% of new bookings only to validate the real-world elasticity before full rollout.

### Lane R: Regulatory Compliance (Route to Professional)

**Regime Identified:** Texas Deceptive Trade Practices-Consumer Protection Act (DTPA) and Texas Administrative Code regarding price advertising and receipt disclosure.

**Routing Required:**
Whether a "mandatory surcharge" must be included in the advertised headline price versus disclosed as a line item, and specific receipt formatting requirements for fuel surcharges in Texas, is a legal determination. Texas law has specific precedents on "drip pricing" (advertising a base price then adding mandatory fees).

**Action:** Consult a Texas-licensed consumer protection attorney before updating invoices or advertising materials to ensure the surcharge structure does not constitute a deceptive trade practice. Do not implement the surcharge on receipts until counsel confirms the disclosure language and placement.
