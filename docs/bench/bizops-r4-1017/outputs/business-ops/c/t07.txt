**The call.** Do not switch to a pure usage-based model ($0.05/call) for your largest clients; under a 20% volume drop, this change immediately reduces your revenue from those accounts by 20% ($8,000/month) while introducing volatility that discourages the multi-year commitments you seek. Retain the flat $5,000 license for enterprise tiers to guarantee revenue stability, and optionally offer usage-based pricing only as a lower-tier "starter" plan for smaller, budget-constrained banks.

**The numbers.**
*Current State (Flat Fee):*
10 clients × $5,000 = **$50,000 MRR**.
Impact of 20% volume drop: **$0 revenue change** (contract value is fixed).

*Proposed State (Usage-Based):*
Current Volume: 80,000 calls/client × $0.05 = $4,000 per client.
*Note:* Your largest clients are currently *overpaying* on the flat fee ($5,000) compared to the proposed usage rate ($4,000). Switching now would voluntarily cut your revenue by 20% immediately, even before a recession hits.

Scenario: 20% Drop in Transaction Volume (Recessionary Stress Test).
New Volume: 80,000 × 0.80 = 64,000 calls.
New Revenue per Client: 64,000 × $0.05 = $3,200.
Total MRR (10 clients): 10 × $3,200 = **$32,000**.
**Revenue Swing:** A loss of **$18,000/month** (36% total revenue reduction) compared to your current flat-fee baseline.

*Assumptions:*
- **ASSUMED (Low/Base/High):** Volatility is uniform across all 10 clients. If volatility is higher (e.g., 40% drop), usage revenue falls to $24,000 MRR.
- **ASSUMED:** Clients will not increase call volume to compensate for lower per-call costs during a downturn (unlikely in a credit contraction).

**What flips it, and the trigger.**
The single input that changes this recommendation is **Volume Growth Potential**. If you have data showing these specific clients will *increase* API calls by >25% despite the economic downturn (e.g., due to automated collections or fraud monitoring spikes), usage pricing could outperform flat fees.
*Trigger:* If any enterprise client projects >100,000 monthly calls in the next 6 months, consider a hybrid model.
*Rule of Thumb:* Enterprise clients prefer predictable OpEx during recessions. Variable costs are perceived as riskier when budgets are frozen.
*Step this week:* If your sales team reports that "budget uncertainty" is the primary objection, consider a **hybrid model** (e.g., $3,000 base + $0.03 overage) rather than a pure usage swap, to lower the entry barrier without capping your upside or exposing yourself to full downside volatility.

**What to confirm, and with whom.**
Confirm the specific contractual terms regarding "minimum commit" clauses with your legal counsel. If you introduce usage-based pricing, you must ensure your Terms of Service allow for audit rights and define "API Call" precisely to prevent billing disputes. This is a contractual/commercial law matter, not just a pricing math problem.
