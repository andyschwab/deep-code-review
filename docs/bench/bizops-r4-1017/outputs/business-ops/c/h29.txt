**The Call**
Wholesale remains viable under current conditions, but a 5% energy increase next quarter erodes your buffer significantly; do not pivot to direct-to-consumer yet. Instead, treat the 5% rise as a trigger to immediately audit equipment efficiency and renegotiate wholesale terms indexed to energy costs.

**The Numbers**
*Formula:* Monthly Profit = (Revenue per Wheel − Variable Costs per Wheel) × Volume − Fixed Costs.
*Assumptions:*
1.  **Revenue:** $12/lb × 8 lbs = **$96 per wheel**.
2.  **Volume:** 5,000 wheels/month.
3.  **Total Revenue:** $480,000/month.
4.  **Energy Cost (Current):** $4,500/month ($0.90/wheel).
5.  **Energy Cost (Projected +5%):** $4,725/month ($0.945/wheel).
6.  **Other Costs (ASSUMED):** To hit your $60k annual profit target ($5k/month), your non-energy costs (labor, milk, packaging, rent) must be approximately **$470,500/month** ($94.10/wheel). This leaves a current net margin of **$1.00/wheel**.

*Results:*
*   **Current State:** $480k Revenue − $475k Total Costs = **$5,000 Monthly Profit** (meets annual target).
*   **With 5% Energy Hike:** Costs rise by $225. New Profit = **$4,775 Monthly**.
*   **Break-Even Point:** If energy costs rise by **556%** (to ~$29,500/month), you hit zero profit. A more realistic threat is if "other costs" creep up just 1% while energy rises; that combination breaks the model.

**What Flips It, and the Trigger**
The single input that changes the call is **non-energy variable cost creep**. Your current $1.00/wheel net margin is too thin to absorb any other inflation alongside energy hikes.
*   **Trigger Value:** If energy costs exceed **$5,000/month** (an 11% rise from today) *and* you cannot raise prices by $0.05/lb, the wholesale model fails to meet your $60k annual target.
*   **Date:** Review utility bills on the 1st of next quarter.
*   **Rule of Thumb:** If utility costs exceed 1.5% of gross revenue (currently 0.9%), investigate immediate efficiency gains.
*   **Step this week:** If your current HVAC system is over 10 years old, consider requesting quotes for high-efficiency dehumidifiers; if X (quotes show <2 year payback), consider Y (financing the upgrade now to lock in lower operating costs).

**What to Confirm, and with Whom**
*   **Contract Law:** Confirm whether your wholesale contracts allow for price adjustments based on utility indexation. Bring your existing contracts to a **commercial attorney** to draft an energy-surcharge clause.
