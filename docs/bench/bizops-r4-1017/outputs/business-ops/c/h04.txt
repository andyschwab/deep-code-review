**The Call**
Do not implement a mandatory line-item fuel surcharge; the projected 20% volume drop from a $4–$8 price increase destroys more profit than absorbing the cost or raising base prices. Absorb the cost temporarily or bake a general price increase into the base ticket, as the elasticity penalty on a visible "fee" is likely higher than on a base price adjustment.

**The Numbers**
*Current Baseline:*
*   Monthly Jobs: $48,000 Revenue / $120 Ticket = 400 jobs.
*   Current Contribution Margin: $120 - $65 = $55/job.
*   Current Monthly Contribution: 400 * $55 = $22,000.
*   Fixed Costs (implied): $22,000 - (18% * $48,000 Net Profit) = $13,360.
*   New Fuel Cost: 12 vans * $8/day * 30 days = $2,880/month additional variable cost.
*   New Variable Cost per Job (if absorbed): $65 + ($2,880 / 400) = $72.20.

*Scenario Analysis (Passing cost to consumer):*
Assumption: Average surcharge needed to cover fuel is ~$7.20/job. Elasticity rule: -$5 price = -10% volume.
1.  **Flat Fee ($7.20):** Total Price $127.20 (+$7.20).
    *   Volume Impact: ~14% drop (1.44 increments of $5). New Volume: 342 jobs.
    *   New Revenue: 342 * $127.20 = $43,502.
    *   New Variable Cost: 342 * $65 (labor/supplies only) = $22,230.
    *   Contribution: $21,272.
    *   Net Profit: $21,272 - $13,360 (Fixed) = **$7,912** (Down from $8,640).
2.  **Percentage Based (6%):** Total Price $127.20.
    *   Mathematically identical to flat fee at current average; results in **$7,912** profit.
3.  **Distance Based (Avg $7.20):**
    *   If average holds, math matches above. If variance causes perceived unfairness, churn may exceed the 14% model, lowering profit further.

*Comparison (Absorbing Cost):*
*   Volume: 400 jobs (no drop).
*   Revenue: $48,000.
*   Variable Cost: 400 * $72.20 = $28,880.
*   Contribution: $19,120.
*   Net Profit: $19,120 - $13,360 = **$5,760**.

*Wait, re-evaluation:* The calculation above shows absorbing costs yields $5,760 profit, while passing them yields $7,912. **Correction:** Passing the fee *increases* profit despite volume loss ($7,912 vs $5,760). However, this assumes elasticity applies strictly to total price. If the *method* (a visible surcharge) triggers higher churn than a base price hike, the flat fee scenario could underperform.
*Revised Call:* Pass the cost, but **do not** use a separate "fuel surcharge" line item if possible; instead, raise the base service price by $7–$8. This achieves the $7,912 profit level while minimizing the psychological "fee" penalty that might worsen the 14% volume drop.

**What Flips It, and the Trigger**
The single input that changes the call is **Price Elasticity of Demand**. If your actual churn exceeds 1.8% per $1 price increase (steeper than the historical 2% per $1 used here), passing the cost becomes net negative.
*Trigger:* If next month's job count drops below 360 (10% drop) after a $5 price test, immediately revert to absorbing costs and cut non-labor variable expenses.
*Step:* This week, A/B test a $5 price increase on 20% of quotes without labeling it a fuel surcharge; measure conversion rates before rolling out fleet-wide.

**Tier 3 (Legal):** You asked about Texas consumer protection laws regarding mandatory surcharges. Texas Business and Commerce Code and the Deceptive Trade Practices Act (DTPA) generally require that advertised prices include all mandatory fees; adding mandatory fees only at checkout/invoice can be deemed deceptive.
*Action:* Consult a Texas consumer protection attorney to confirm if "fuel surcharges" must be included in your advertised "starting at" prices on vans and website, and whether specific disclosure language is required on receipts to avoid DTPA liability.
