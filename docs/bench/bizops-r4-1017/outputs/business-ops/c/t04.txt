**The call.** Raise the pledge to $52; absorbing the $2.50 cost increase on 2,000 units destroys $5,000 of profit, whereas the price hike only breaks even if you lose more than 19% of your backers—a risk worth taking given the magnitude of the cost shock.

**The numbers.**
*Formula:* Net Profit = (Units × (Pledge − Shipping − COGS)) − Fixed Costs (Marketing/Overhead).
*Assumptions:* Base volume is 2,000 units. Shipping is $8/unit. Fixed costs are excluded from the delta analysis as they remain constant unless you explicitly cut marketing (which reduces reach, not just cost).

*Scenario A: Absorb Cost (Price $45)*
*   Revenue per unit: $45
*   Variable Cost per unit: $8 (shipping) + $14.50 (COGS) = $22.50
*   Contribution Margin per unit: $22.50
*   **Total Contribution:** 2,000 × $22.50 = **$45,000**

*Scenario B: Raise Price (Price $52)*
*   Revenue per unit: $52
*   Variable Cost per unit: $22.50
*   Contribution Margin per unit: $29.50
*   **Total Contribution (at 2,000 units):** 2,000 × $29.50 = **$59,000**

*Break-even Volume Analysis:*
To match the $45,000 profit from Scenario A using the new $29.50 margin:
$45,000 / $29.50 = 1,526 units.
You can lose up to 474 backers (23.7% drop) and still equal the profit of the lower price point.

**What flips it, and the trigger.**
The single input that changes this call is **price elasticity** (backer drop-off rate).
*Trigger:* If your pre-launch survey or early traffic data indicates a conversion drop greater than **24%**, reconsider the hike.
*Rule of Thumb:* In crowdfunding, a 15% price increase often yields a <10% volume drop for unique strategy games with no direct substitutes.
*Step this week:* If your email list engagement is high, run a "Van Westendorp" price sensitivity survey asking backers at what price the game becomes "too expensive" before locking the $52 tier.

**What to confirm, and with whom.**
*Confirmation:* Verify if the $8 shipping estimate holds true at the new price point (sometimes higher pledges allow for better shipping consolidation or tiers) and ensure your Kickstarter terms allow mid-campaign price adjustments if needed.
*With whom:* No licensed professional is required for this arithmetic; however, if cutting the marketing budget involves terminating signed contracts with agencies, consult your business agreements.
