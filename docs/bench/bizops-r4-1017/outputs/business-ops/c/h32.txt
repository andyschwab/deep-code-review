**The call.** Do not absorb the premium increase; immediately restructure pricing to decouple liability risk from the flat fee by introducing a value-based surcharge for high-value coins, as the current model subsidizes high-risk items with low-margin volume.

**The numbers.**
Your unit economics have shifted materially due to the fixed cost increase.
*   **Formula:** Net Margin per Unit = Price – Variable Cost – (Fixed Costs / Volume).
*   **Inputs:** Price ($75), Variable Cost ($40), Volume (8,000 units).
*   **Fixed Costs:** Previous ($30,000) vs. Current ($48,000).

*Scenario A: Absorb the cost (Status Quo)*
*   Previous Fixed Cost/Unit: $30,000 / 8,000 = $3.75.
    *   Previous Margin: $75 - $40 - $3.75 = **$31.25 per coin**.
*   Current Fixed Cost/Unit: $48,000 / 8,000 = $6.00.
    *   Current Margin: $75 - $40 - $6.00 = **$29.00 per coin**.
*   **Impact:** A 7.2% reduction in net margin per unit. While painful, this is not immediately unsustainable if volume holds.

*Scenario B: Introduce Value-Based Tier (Illustrative)*
Assume 10% of volume (800 coins) are high-value (> $5,000) and carry 80% of the liability risk driving the premium hike.
*   **Proposal:** Keep base fee at $75; add a $25 "High-Value Handling" surcharge for declared values > $5,000.
*   **New Revenue:** (7,200 × $75) + (800 × $100) = $540,000 + $80,000 = $620,000.
*   **New Margin:** ($620,000 - $320,000 variable - $48,000 fixed) / 8,000 = **$31.50 per coin**.
*   **Result:** This restores margins to previous levels while aligning price with risk exposure.

**What flips it, and the trigger.**
The critical input is the **concentration of high-value items**. If more than 15% of your volume consists of high-value coins, the flat fee model creates a negative expected value on those specific units once a claim occurs.
*   **Trigger Rule:** If a single misgrading claim exceeds $15,000 (roughly 50% of your annual premium), the flat-fee model fails to capitalize the risk pool.
*   **Action:** If you cannot identify which coins are high-value at intake, consider a mandatory declaration step. If >10% of customers decline to declare value, **pause high-volume intake** and implement the tiered surcharge immediately.

**What to confirm, and with whom.**
The determination of whether your insurance premium is "too high" relative to industry standards is a **Tier 3** question requiring a licensed **commercial insurance broker** specializing in appraisal and numismatic services. They must verify if the $48,000 premium reflects a change in your specific loss history or a sector-wide rate hardening. Additionally, consult **legal counsel** to ensure that tiered pricing based on declared value does not inadvertently alter your liability limitations or terms of service regarding undeclared high-value items.
