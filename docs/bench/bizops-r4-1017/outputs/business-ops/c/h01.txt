**The Call**
Pass the freight increase directly to customers as a separate line item rather than raising base product prices; absorbing the cost eliminates your net profit and threatens runway, while a transparent surcharge minimizes churn compared to a blanket price hike.

**The Numbers**
*Current Unit Economics:*
*   Average Order Value (AOV): $85
*   Gross Profit (22%): $18.70
*   Implied Cost of Goods + Freight: $66.30

*Impact of 15% Freight Hike:*
Assuming freight is approximately 10% of your total costs (~$6.63 per order), a 15% hike adds **~$1.00 per order** in direct costs.
*   **Option A (Absorb):** New Gross Profit = $17.70.
    *   Annual Impact: If you process ~4,700 orders/year (estimated from $85 AOV and typical distributor volume to cover $12k/mo burn), you lose ~$4,700 in annual gross profit. Given your "thin margins," this likely pushes quarterly net profit to zero or negative, accelerating burn.
    *   Runway Effect: Current cash = $72,000 ($12k × 6). If net profit vanishes, burn remains $12k/mo. Runway stays at 6 months but with zero buffer for error.
*   **Option B (Pass Through):** Maintain $18.70 gross profit per order.
    *   Churn Risk Assumption: You lost 3% of clients on a $5 base price hike. A $1.00 specific freight surcharge is 80% smaller and psychologically distinct from a price gouge on seeds.
    *   Conservative Estimate: Assume 0.5% churn (ASSUMED, as data is thin for surcharges vs. base hikes).
    *   Result: Retained margin protects the $12k/mo burn coverage. Even with slight volume loss, the preserved margin per order extends runway compared to Option A.

**What Flips It, and the Trigger**
The deciding input is the **price elasticity of your specific customer base regarding surcharges versus base price hikes**.
*   **Trigger:** If more than 1.2% of customers cancel in the first month after implementing the surcharge, reconsider absorbing the cost for high-volume accounts only.
*   **Rule of Thumb:** If the surcharge exceeds 2% of the total order value, churn risk rises non-linearly. Your estimated $1.00 surcharge on an $85 order is ~1.2%, sitting near the danger zone but likely safer than absorbing the cost.
*   **Step this week:** If your churn exceeds 1% post-announcement, consider offering a "freight cap" for your top 10% of customers to retain volume while charging the rest.
