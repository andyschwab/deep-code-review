**The call.** Reject the pure lease model as structured; it collapses your runway to 30 months while creating an asset-heavy balance sheet that typically depresses early-stage agritech valuations unless you secure non-dilutive debt financing. Stick to the upfront sale or pivot to a "hardware-as-a-service" model where the customer finances the asset, preserving your cash for growth.

**The numbers.**
*Current Model (Upfront):*
Margin per unit = Price ($4,200) - COGS ($1,800) = **$2,400**.
Cash inflow per unit = **$4,200** immediately.

*Proposed Lease Model:*
Monthly margin = $150 - ($40,000 financing cost / estimated units).
*Assumption (ASSUMED):* To generate $40k in monthly financing costs, you are likely deploying significant inventory. If we assume the $40k covers interest/leasing costs on a growing fleet, your net cash flow per unit becomes negative initially.
Payback period on hardware cost: $1,800 COGS / $150 monthly revenue = **12 months** just to recover hardware cost, ignoring the $40k monthly overhead burn.
Runway impact: Current Cash ($1.2M) / Increased Monthly Burn ($40k + existing ops) = **< 30 months** (assuming zero other burn, which is impossible with a 4-person sales team). In reality, with sales salaries, runway likely drops to **12–18 months**.

*Valuation Trade-off:*
Investors apply higher multiples to recurring revenue (ARR), but they discount valuations for high capital intensity (owning the hardware) and short runway. The "net" effect is often a lower pre-money valuation due to the risk of running out of cash before proving the LTV.

**What flips it, and the trigger.**
The single input that changes this call is **access to non-dilutive debt financing**. If you can secure a dedicated inventory credit line that covers the $1,800 unit cost + financing fees without touching your $1.2M equity cash, the lease model becomes viable.
*Trigger:* If you cannot term-sheet a debt facility covering 100% of hardware costs within **90 days**, abandon the lease model.
*Rule of thumb:* Never use equity cash reserves to fund hardware inventory for leases unless your gross margin exceeds 80% and payback is under 6 months.
*Step this week:* If your projected monthly burn exceeds $80k total, consider offering a "lease-to-own" option where a third-party financier owns the hardware, and you collect the software/management fee only.

**What to confirm, and with whom.**
*Tier 3 (Route):* The accounting treatment of the leased hardware (operating vs. finance lease) and its impact on your balance sheet ratios during due diligence. Specifically, ask a CPA: "Will owning these depreciating assets classify us as an 'asset-heavy' company, disqualifying us from standard SaaS multiples?" Additionally, consult securities counsel regarding whether offering leases constitutes a regulated financial product in Arizona.
