**The call:** Stick to the $15,000 flat license for now; do not pivot to per-acre pricing until you have validated that price sensitivity is the specific blocker preventing conversion, as your unit economics currently rely on high average revenue per user (ARPU) to survive a small total addressable market (TAM).

**The numbers:**
Your current baseline projection uses a flat fee model:
*   **Formula:** `Revenue = Total Farms × Penetration Rate × Annual License Fee`
*   **Inputs:** 500 farms (Base TAM), 10% penetration (Assumed), $15,000 fee (Given).
*   **Result:** $750,000 ARR.
*   **Margin:** $750,000 - $600,000 costs = $150,000 surplus (20% margin).

If you expand the TAM to 2,000 farms but fail to increase penetration or lower costs, the math scales linearly only if the product fit holds.
*   **Scenario A (Current Model, Expanded TAM):** 2,000 farms × 5% penetration (conservative assumption for new regions) × $15,000 = $1.5M ARR.
*   **Scenario B (Per-Acre Pivot):** To match the $750k target with 500 farms, you need an average farm size assumption. If the average eligible acreage is 2,000 acres, you would need to charge $75/acre to match the flat fee.
    *   **Risk:** If farms perceive value at only $20/acre, revenue drops to $200,000, creating a $400,000 deficit against your $600k costs.
    *   **Assumption Label:** Average farm acreage and willingness-to-pay per acre are **UNVERIFIED** and must be sourced from your 12 pilot customers.

**What flips it, and the trigger:**
The single input that changes the call is **Price Elasticity vs. Farm Size Variance**. If your 12 pilot customers represent the "ideal" large farm, smaller farms in the expanded 2,000-farm TAM may find $15,000 prohibitive, but a per-acre model might not cover your fixed support costs.
*   **Trigger Value:** If the average contract value (ACV) drops below $12,000 under a per-acre model, the business becomes unsustainable at 500 farms.
*   **Decision Rule:** Do not change pricing models until you have asked all 12 pilots: "At what price point would this be an obvious buy?" and "At what price point is it too expensive?" (Van Westendorp method).
*   **Step this week:** Contact your 12 pilots. Ask specifically if they would have signed faster at a lower per-acre rate or if the flat fee was acceptable. If 3+ say the flat fee was a barrier *and* their farm size is significantly smaller than your average, consider a hybrid model (base fee + per-acre overage).

**What to confirm, and with whom:**
There are no immediate tier-3 legal or securities issues in choosing a pricing model. However, if you expand to "adjacent crops," confirm with an agronomist or crop specialist whether pesticide regulations differ significantly, as this could alter your software's liability profile (tier-2 consideration). If you move to per-acre billing, ensure your terms of service clearly define how "billable acres" are measured to avoid contract disputes (tier-2 operational risk).
