**The call.** Reject the blanket price hike to $79; the risk of losing >20% of your high-value segment creates a sponsorship shortfall that outweighs subscription gains unless you can prove those users are price-inelastic.

**The numbers.**
We compare Annual Recurring Revenue (ARR) under two scenarios: Status Quo vs. Price Hike with 20% High-Value Churn.

*Formula:*
`Total ARR = (Subscribers × Price × 12) + Sponsorship Revenue`
*Sponsorship Requirement:* Must cover 40% of $85k/mo operating cost = **$34,000/mo** ($408k/year).
*Assumption (UNVERIFIED):* Current sponsorship revenue exactly meets the $34k/mo target at 480 high-value users (15% of 3,200). We assume sponsors pay pro-rata per high-value user or require a minimum headcount threshold.

**Scenario A: Status Quo ($49/mo, 0% extra churn)**
*   Subscribers: 3,200
*   High-Value Users: 480 (15%)
*   Subscription ARR: 3,200 × $49 × 12 = **$1,881,600**
*   Sponsorship ARR (Assumed): **$408,000**
*   **Total ARR: $2,289,600**

**Scenario B: Price Hike ($79/mo, 20% High-Value Churn)**
*   *Impact:* You lose 96 high-value users (20% of 480). Remaining high-value: 384.
*   *Churn Assumption:* Base churn remains 4%; no additional base churn assumed for this specific risk model.
*   Remaining Subscribers: 3,104 (3,200 - 96)
*   Subscription ARR: 3,104 × $79 × 12 = **$2,942,592**
*   *Sponsorship Risk:* If sponsors require the original 480-user threshold, revenue drops to **$0**. If they pay pro-rata (384/480), revenue drops 20% to **$326,400**.
*   **Total ARR (Pro-Rata): $3,268,992** (Net Positive)
*   **Total ARR (Threshold Breach): $2,942,592** (Net Positive vs Status Quo, but fails operating cost coverage).

*Critical Failure Mode:* The prompt states you *need* sponsorship to cover 40% of ops costs ($34k/mo).
*   In Scenario B (Pro-Rata), sponsorship yields ~$27,200/mo. **You miss the 40% coverage target.**
*   In Scenario B (Threshold Breach), sponsorship yields $0. **You miss the target entirely.**

**What flips it, and the trigger.**
The deciding input is the **sponsor contract structure**.
*   *Trigger:* If your contracts have a "minimum audience size" clause (e.g., "must maintain 450 PMs"), losing 96 users (dropping to 384) voids the deal.
*   *Break-even Churn:* To maintain the $34k/mo sponsorship target under a pro-rata model, you cannot lose more than **0%** of the high-value base if the rate is fixed, or you must raise sponsorship CPMs by 25% to offset the 20% volume loss.
*   *Rule of Thumb:* If high-value churn exceeds **6%** (approx. 29 users), you likely breach typical "material adverse change" clauses in sponsorship deals.
*   *Step this week:* If your sponsor contracts define a minimum user count, consider **segmented pricing** (raise to $79 for general users, keep $49 or custom enterprise tiers for verified Portfolio Managers) rather than a blanket hike.

**What to confirm, and with whom.**
Bring your specific sponsorship agreements to a **commercial contracts attorney**. The binding question is whether a 20% drop in the defined "qualified audience" constitutes a breach of contract or allows for rate renegotiation. Do not implement the price increase until legal confirms the definition of "audience" and any termination clauses tied to subscriber metrics.
