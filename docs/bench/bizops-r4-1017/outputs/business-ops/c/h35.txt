**The Call**
Adopt a "European Waterfall" (deal-by-deal) structure with a clawback provision and a cumulative preferred return, rather than holding cash or using a strict "American Waterfall" (fund-as-a-whole). This allows you to distribute profits to investors as soon as individual territory deals close (solving the idle capital risk) while the clawback mechanism legally obligates early recipients to return money if later films underperform, ensuring fairness across the entire £2 million pool.

**The Numbers**
The fairness mechanism relies on the **Preferred Return (Hurdle)** and the **Clawback Liability**.
*Formula:* Distributable Cash = Net Proceeds from Territory Sale.
*Allocation Logic:*
1.  **Return of Capital:** 100% of proceeds go to investors until total contributed capital (£2M) is returned.
2.  **Preferred Return:** If your terms include an 8% annual hurdle (illustrative), calculate accrued interest on unreturned capital before splitting profits.
3.  **Profit Split:** Remaining surplus is split (e.g., 80% Investors / 20% Manager) *per deal*.

*Scenario Application (Illustrative):*
*   **Deal A (Month 6):** Film 1 sells UK rights for £150k.
    *   *Action:* Distribute £150k immediately to investors pro-rata.
    *   *Risk:* If Film 2 and 3 fail, investors have been "overpaid" relative to the total fund performance.
*   **The Fix (Clawback):** The fund agreement must state that if, at the end of the fund's life, total distributions exceed the agreed waterfall (e.g., investors didn't get their full 8% preferred return on the whole £2M), the manager must recapture previous distributions from investors to true-up the accounts.

*Assumptions:*
*   **Hurdle Rate:** 6–10% is market standard for private equity/film funds (ASSUMED: 8% for modeling).
*   **Catch-up:** Often included so the manager receives their share of profits rapidly once the hurdle is met.
*   **Timing:** Distributions occur "as and when" realized, not on a fixed schedule.

**What Flips It, and the Trigger**
The deciding input is the **performance variance between films**. If your three films have highly correlated success (all hit or all miss), a simple pro-rata distribution works. However, if success is uncorrelated (one hit, two flops), the "Deal-by-Deal" approach without a clawback creates severe inequity.
*   **Trigger:** If the projected loss on any single film exceeds 20% of the total fund capital, the complexity of a clawback is mandatory to protect early investors from subsidizing later ones.
*   **Rule of Thumb:** Never use a deal-by-deal payout without a clawback if the fund holds fewer than 5–7 assets; with only three films, the risk of skew is too high.
*   **Step this week:** Draft a term sheet clause specifying a "Clawback Period" (e.g., 2 years post-final distribution) and decide if the manager must provide a bank guarantee or escrow to secure potential clawback obligations.

**What to Confirm, and With Whom**
You must confirm the specific **tax treatment of "clawback" events for UK investors** and the precise drafting of the **Limited Partnership Agreement (LPA)** to ensure the waterfall does not inadvertently create a taxable event when money is recalled.
*   **Regime:** UK Partnership Tax Rules and FCA regulations regarding collective investment schemes.
*   **Action:** Engage a UK-qualified fund formation lawyer and a tax specialist immediately. Do not finalize the LPA without their review of the clawback mechanics, as improper structuring can trigger unexpected capital gains taxes or regulatory breaches.
