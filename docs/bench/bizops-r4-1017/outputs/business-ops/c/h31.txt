**The Call:** You face an immediate liquidity crisis, not just a squeeze; your current $150,000 cash reserve is likely insufficient to cover three months of fixed costs (payroll and overhead) while inventory sits idle, requiring you to secure a line of credit equal to at least 75% of your quarterly burn before production halts.

**The Numbers:**
To model the cash gap, use the formula: *Working Capital Needed = (New Lead Time – Old Lead Time) × Weekly Burn Rate*.
*   **Weekly Burn Rate (ASSUMED):** You have 12 builders. If average fully loaded labor is $40/hour ($1,600/week) plus 20% overhead allocation, weekly fixed cost is ~$23,000. *You must replace this assumption with your actual P&L fixed costs.*
*   **Lead Time Delta:** 12 weeks (new) – 2 weeks (old) = 10 weeks of extra idle time.
*   **Cash Gap Calculation:** 10 weeks × $23,000/week = **$230,000 additional capital needed.**
*   **Current Position:** $150,000 (bank) – $230,000 (gap) = **-$80,000 deficit.**
*   **Sensitivity:** If your actual weekly burn is only $15,000, the gap is $150,000 (breaking even with current cash). If burn is $30,000, the gap is $300,000.

**What Flips It, and the Trigger:**
The single input that changes the call is your **actual weekly fixed burn rate** (payroll + rent + utilities).
*   **Trigger Value:** If your weekly burn exceeds $12,500, your current $150,000 reserve will deplete before the first new boat ships under the 3-month lead time.
*   **Solvency Threshold:** This becomes a solvency issue (insolvency) if the total cash required to bridge the gap exceeds your ability to borrow or raise equity, forcing you to miss payroll. Given your high labor component, missing two weeks of payroll risks losing skilled builders permanently.
*   **Rule of Thumb:** If cash on hand covers less than 1.5x the new lead time in weeks multiplied by weekly burn, you are in the danger zone.
*   **Step this week:** If your calculated weekly burn is over $12,500, consider freezing non-essential hiring and immediately drafting a cash flow forecast showing the 12-week trough to present to your lender.

**What to Confirm, and with Whom:**
*   **With your CPA/CFO:** Confirm the exact classification of your inventory costs during this extended holding period and whether interest on the new line of credit can be capitalized into inventory cost under GAAP.
*   **With your Banker:** Confirm the specific covenants for a working capital line of credit tied to inventory aging; banks often reduce advance rates on inventory held longer than 90 days, which could paradoxically cut off funding right when you need it most.
