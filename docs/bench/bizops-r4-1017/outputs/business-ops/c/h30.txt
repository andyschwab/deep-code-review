**The call.** Stick with the $200 flat fee for now; switching to hourly pricing introduces revenue volatility that threatens your $50,000 quarterly goal unless you can prove clients will settle significantly faster (under 1.5 hours) to offset volume loss.

**The numbers.**
*Current Flat Fee Model:*
Revenue = Cases × Flat Rate
Base: 400 cases × $200 = **$80,000/month** ($240,000/quarter).
Cost: 400 cases × 2.5 hrs × $60/hr = $60,000.
Margin: $20,000/month.
*Risk:* A case taking 5 hours costs $300, losing you $100 on that specific transaction.

*Hypothetical Hourly Model:*
Revenue = Cases × Avg Hours × Hourly Rate.
To match current revenue per case ($200), you must charge $80/hr ($200 / 2.5 hrs).
If you charge $80/hr:
Scenario A (Behavior unchanged, 2.5 hrs): Revenue remains $80,000. No gain.
Scenario B (Clients settle faster, 1.5 hrs): Revenue drops to 400 × 1.5 × $80 = $48,000.
Scenario C (Complex cases drag, 3.5 hrs): Revenue rises to 400 × 3.5 × $80 = $112,000.

*Assumption (UNVERIFIED):* You provided no data on price elasticity. I am assuming a conservative 15% volume drop if switching to hourly due to user friction (340 cases).
Adjusted Hourly Revenue (at 15% volume drop, 2.5 hrs avg): 340 × 2.5 × $80 = $68,000.

**What flips it, and the trigger.**
The single input that changes the call is **average resolution time under hourly billing**.
*Trigger:* If you can demonstrate that hourly billing reduces average resolution time to **1.8 hours or less** while maintaining 90% of current volume, the hourly model becomes viable for margin protection on complex cases.
*Rule of Thumb:* If >20% of your cases exceed 3.3 hours (the break-even point where $60 cost + margin equals $200), the flat fee bleeds margin.
*Step this week:* Run a manual audit of the last 50 cases. If more than 10 took longer than 3.5 hours, consider a **hybrid model** (flat fee up to 3 hours, then hourly) rather than a full switch.

**What to confirm, and with whom.**
Confirm whether your current Terms of Service allow for retroactive pricing changes or if switching models constitutes a material change requiring new user consent. Consult a **contract attorney** to review your user agreements before altering billing structures to avoid breach of contract claims.
