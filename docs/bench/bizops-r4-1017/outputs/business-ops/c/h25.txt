**The call.** Reject the current transaction-fee model immediately; at an $8.50 average order value (AOV), you are losing money on every single transaction before covering fixed costs. Pivot to a flat monthly subscription for the trucks now, as the math proves the transaction model cannot reach a 20% gross margin without an unrealistic doubling of order sizes.

**The numbers.**
*Formula:* Net Revenue per Transaction = (AOV × 1.5%) + $0.30 (Fee charged to customer) − [(AOV + Fee) × Processor Rate].
*Assumption:* Standard processor rate is ~2.9% + $0.30 (industry standard, UNVERIFIED for your specific contract).
*Current State ($8.50 AOV):*
1. Fee collected from customer: ($8.50 × 0.015) + $0.30 = $0.43.
2. Total transaction size: $8.93.
3. Processor cost (est.): ($8.93 × 2.9%) + $0.30 ≈ $0.56.
4. **Net Result:** $0.43 (Revenue) − $0.56 (Cost) = **−$0.13 loss per transaction.**
5. Monthly Loss on Variable Costs: 5,000 × −$0.13 = −$650 (before paying developers or servers).

*Target State (20% Gross Margin):*
To achieve a 20% margin where Revenue = 1.2 × Cost, the required AOV is approximately **$24.50**.
*Calculation logic:* At $24.50, the 1.5% fee is $0.37 + $0.30 = $0.67 revenue. Processor cost on $25.17 is ~$1.03. This still yields a loss.
*Correction:* The 1.5% take-rate is too thin to cover the fixed $0.30 component of processor fees. Even at infinite AOV, your revenue is 1.5% while costs approach 2.9%. **You cannot achieve positive unit economics, let alone 20% margin, on this specific fee structure regardless of AOV.** The fixed $0.30 fee you charge does not scale to cover the fixed $0.30 processor fee plus the percentage gap.

**What flips it, and the trigger.**
The fatal flaw is the mismatch between your revenue cap (1.5% + fixed $0.30) and the cost floor (processor % + fixed $0.30). The only "flip" is abandoning the percentage-of-order model entirely.
*Trigger:* If you process next month's 5,000 orders under this model, you will lock in a variable loss of ~$650+ plus your $1,200 fixed server costs, burning cash with no path to profitability.
*Rule of Thumb:* If your take rate (1.5%) is lower than the processor's variable rate (~2.9%), you lose money on every incremental sale.
