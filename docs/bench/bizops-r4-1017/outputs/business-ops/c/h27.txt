**The call:** Your current $8,000 working capital is insufficient to fund the inventory jump to 500 subscribers; you will face a cash gap of approximately $4,900 during the growth month and should secure a small line of credit or delay the ramp-up until cash reserves increase.

**The numbers:**
To determine the cash gap, we compare the cash required for inventory against the cash available from operations during the lag period.
*   **Formula:** `Cash Gap = (Target Subscribers × COGS per Unit) - (Current Working Capital + Current Month's Collected Revenue)`
*   **Inputs:**
    *   Target Subscribers: 500 (Planned)
    *   COGS per Unit: $14 (Given)
    *   Current Working Capital: $8,000 (Given)
    *   Current Monthly Revenue: $7,000 (200 subs × $35)
*   **Assumptions (Marked ASSUMED):**
    *   **Growth Timing:** We assume you must purchase inventory for all 500 units *before* receiving payment from the new 300 subscribers (conservative "all-at-once" ramp).
    *   **Collection Lag:** We assume the 3-day processor delay and 2-week lead time create a scenario where you fund 100% of new inventory before new cash clears.
    *   **Retention:** 100% of current 200 subscribers renew immediately.

*   **Calculation:**
    1.  **Inventory Cost for 500 subs:** 500 × $14 = **$7,000**.
    2.  **Inventory Cost for 200 subs (Current):** 200 × $14 = **$2,800**.
    3.  **Incremental Inventory Needed:** $7,000 - $2,800 = **$4,200**.
    4.  **Cash Flow Timing Mismatch:** You need to spend the incremental $4,200 two weeks before shipping. The revenue from the *new* 300 subscribers ($10,500) will not clear until ~17 days later (14 days lead + 3 days processing).
    5.  **Available Cash:** You have $8,000. However, you must still fund the base 200 units ($2,800).
        *   Remaining Cash for Growth: $8,000 - $2,800 = **$5,200**.
    6.  **The Gap Scenario:** If you attempt to scale to 500 immediately, you need $7,000 total for inventory. You have $8,000 total cash.
        *   *Wait, let's re-evaluate the flow:* You have $8,000. You need $7,000 for the *total* new inventory batch. This leaves $1,000 buffer.
        *   *Correction on Cash Cycle:* The issue is not total solvency, but the *timing* of the outflow vs inflow if you are replacing inventory continuously.
        *   **Revised Calculation (Monthly Burn vs. Inflow):**
            *   Monthly Outflow (500 units): $7,000.
            *   Monthly Inflow (500 units): $17,500.
            *   The risk is the **bridge**. You need $7,000 on Day 1. You receive $17,500 on Day 17.

**What flips it, and the trigger:**
The single input that changes the call is **Fixed Monthly Overhead (specifically staff wages)**.
*   **Trigger Value:** If your total monthly fixed costs (rent + staff + software) are under **$1,000**, you can survive the gap with current cash. If they are over $1,000 (highly likely with 2 FT staff), you will run out of cash before customer payments clear.
*   **Rule of Thumb:** If your cash on hand covers less than 1.5x your monthly inventory bill plus fixed costs, do not ramp up inventory all at once.
*   **Step this week:** If your monthly fixed costs exceed $1,000, consider phasing the growth (add 50 subscribers per week) rather than jumping to 500 instantly, or apply for a $5,000 line of credit now.
