**The call.** Execute a forward contract for the full 12 million yen exposure immediately; do not use options, and do not rely on dynamic pricing for this specific order.

**The numbers.**
Your exposure is 12,000,000 JPY. At the recent low (110 JPY/USD), this cost $109,090. At the recent high (150 JPY/USD), it cost $80,000. The swing is $29,090.
*   **Current Profit Context:** On $250k sales at 12% margin, profit is $30,000. A move from 135 to 150 JPY/USD on this order alone could erase ~$8,000 of that profit (approx. 27% of quarterly profit).
*   **Forward Contract (Recommended):** Locks in a specific rate (e.g., 140 JPY/USD). Cost is typically the interest rate differential (embedded in the rate), often negligible for small businesses compared to the risk. Result: Guaranteed cost of ~$85,714.
*   **Options (Not Recommended):** Requires paying an upfront premium (typically 1–3% of notional value). On 12M JPY, a 2% premium is ~$1,600–$2,400 cash outlay now. Given your thin 12% margin, this upfront cash drag reduces immediate liquidity without guaranteeing a better outcome than a forward unless the yen strengthens significantly beyond the break-even point (strike + premium).
*   **Dynamic Pricing (Not Recommended):** Passing volatility to retailers risks contract breaches or lost shelf space. Retailers operate on fixed margins; frequent price changes create friction that outweighs the theoretical benefit of passing risk.

**What flips it, and the trigger.**
The decision flips if your suppliers agree to invoice you in USD, shifting the risk to them (unlikely for Japanese farmers) or if your retailers sign contracts with explicit currency adjustment clauses (rare for small importers).
*   **Trigger:** If the USD/JPY rate moves 5% against you from today's spot rate before payment, the unhedged loss exceeds the typical cost of a forward contract.
*   **Rule of Thumb:** If a single currency swing exceeds 25% of your quarterly net profit (your case: $7,500), hedging is mandatory, not optional.
