**What Flips It, and the Trigger:**
The deciding factor is whether the destination country has an **EU Adequacy Decision** or if you can implement **Standard Contractual Clauses (SCCs)** combined with "supplementary measures" that satisfy the *Schrems II* ruling.
*   **Trigger:** If the specific Southeast Asian country lacks an adequacy decision and you cannot technically prevent the local provider from accessing decrypted data (even with encryption), the transfer is prohibited.
*   **Rule of Thumb:** If the data contains "special category" personal data (health, biometrics, political opinions often found in case files), the threshold for lawful transfer is near-impossible without adequacy.
*   **Step this week:** If the target country is not on the EU Commission's adequacy list, consider hybrid architecture: keep metadata and indices in the EU, and only move strictly anonymized or non-personal cold blobs overseas, if contractually permissible.
