**The Call**
Do not offer four-payment installment plans without a third-party financing partner; under your current terms, this structure creates a guaranteed liquidity deficit that will deplete your $20,000 reserve within the first month of scaling to 20 new orders. Stick to the 50% deposit model or integrate a "Buy Now, Pay Later" (BNPL) provider who pays you upfront.

**The Numbers**
*Formula:* `Cash Gap = (Supplier Outflow) - (Customer Inflow collected by Outflow Date)`

*Current Model (50% Deposit):*
*   **Inflow:** 20 orders × $450 × 50% = $4,500 collected immediately.
*   **Outflow:** 20 orders × $450 (fabric cost assumed 100% of price for worst-case, or specific cost if known; here we assume full revenue value as the liability ceiling) = $9,000 due in ~30-45 days (production + shipping time).
*   **Result:** You hold $4,500 interest-free capital to fund operations. If fabric cost is <50% of price, you are cash-flow positive before delivery.

*Proposed Installment Model (4 payments of $112.50):*
*   **Inflow:** 20 orders × $112.50 = $2,250 collected immediately.
*   **Outflow:** $9,000 (assuming $450/suit fabric cost) due 14 days before shipping.
*   **Timing Mismatch:** You must pay suppliers before the 2nd, 3rd, or 4th installments arrive.
*   **Deficit Calculation:**
    *   Immediate Cash: $2,250.
    *   Reserve Available: $20,000.
    *   Total Liquid: $22,250.
    *   If you scale to 60 pending orders (as noted in your pipeline) under this model:
        *   Inflow: 60 × $112.50 = $6,750.
        *   Outflow (assuming 60 suits need fabric soon): 60 × $450 = $27,000.
        *   **Gap:** $27,000 - $6,750 = **$20,250 shortfall**.
    *   **Result:** You exhaust the entire $20,000 reserve and still owe $250, plus you have zero buffer for the next month's 20 new orders.

*Assumptions (Marked ASSUMED):*
1.  **Fabric Cost:** ASSUMED $450/suit (100% of sale price) for worst-case analysis. If actual fabric cost is lower (e.g., $200), the gap shrinks but remains negative until later installments arrive.
2.  **Production Lead Time:** ASSUMED 30 days. If production takes 60 days, you collect more installments before paying suppliers, reducing the gap.
3.  **Installment Timing:** ASSUMED monthly payments. If payments are bi-weekly, cash inflow accelerates slightly.

**What Flips It, and the Trigger**
The single input that changes the call is the **actual fabric cost per suit**.
*   **Trigger Value:** If your fabric cost is below **$187.50** (the sum of the first two installments collected before the supplier payment deadline), the model might survive without dipping into reserves.
*   **Rule of Thumb:** If `Cost of Goods Sold (COGS) > Sum of Installments Collected Before Supplier Due Date`, you will face a cash crunch.
*   **Step this week:** Calculate your exact COGS. If COGS > $225 (50% of price), **do not** launch self-managed installments. Instead, contact a BNPL provider (e.g., Affirm, Klarna) to underwrite the loan; they pay you the full $450 upfront (minus a fee), eliminating the timing mismatch.
