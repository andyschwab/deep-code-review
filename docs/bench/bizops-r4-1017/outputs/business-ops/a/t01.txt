Keep the flat $29/month model for now; switching to per-word pricing based on your current assumptions increases revenue volatility rather than smoothing it, and the math does not yet justify the added billing complexity.

**Current Model (Flat Rate)**
*   **Formula:** `Revenue = Subscribers × Monthly Fee`
*   **Inputs:** 120 users × $29
*   **Projected Revenue:** **$3,480/month** (fixed, regardless of usage volume).
*   **Volatility Driver:** User count only. Your 8% churn creates a revenue risk of ~$278/month if those users leave, but income is predictable while they remain.

**Proposed Model (Per-Word)**
*   **Formula:** `Revenue = Subscribers × Avg Words/User × Rate/Word`
*   **Inputs:** 120 users × 15,000 words × $0.002
*   **Projected Revenue (Base Case):** **$3,600/month**.
*   **Volatility Driver:** Usage volume. You explicitly stated you lack data on word count variance during holiday periods.
    *   *Scenario A (Slow Season -50% volume):* If users process only 7,500 words, revenue drops to **$1,800**.
    *   *Scenario B (Peak Season +50% volume):* If users process 22,500 words, revenue rises to **$5,400**.

**Analysis of the Switch**
Your hypothesis is that per-word pricing stabilizes cash flow because users pay for what they use. However, the arithmetic shows the opposite effect given your inputs. Under the flat model, revenue is stable at $3,480 unless a user cancels. Under the per-word model, revenue fluctuates directly with the translator's workflow. Since freelance translation is inherently seasonal, tying your revenue directly to their volume imports their volatility into your P&L.

Without knowing the variance in word counts (the missing input), you cannot model the downside risk. If your users' volume drops by more than ~3.3% during slow seasons, the per-word model yields less revenue than the flat $29 fee. Given that translators often have "feast or famine" cycles, a 3.3% drop is a low bar to cross, making revenue *less* predictable, not more.

**Decision Aid**
*   **The Flip Input:** The switch only generates more base revenue if the average user processes >14,500 words/month ($29 ÷ $0.002). It only *stabilizes* revenue if user volume is counter-cyclical to churn (i.e., users process *more* words when they are likely to cancel), which is unlikely.
*   **Threshold Rule:** If usage variance exceeds ±5% month-over-month, per-word pricing introduces more financial instability than a flat fee with 8% churn.
*   **Next Step:** Before changing the pricing model, instrument your system to track total words processed per user for 30 days without charging for it. If the coefficient of variation in word counts is lower than your churn rate volatility, reconsider the switch. Otherwise, consider a "hybrid" approach (e.g., $15 base + $0.002 per word) to capture upside while maintaining a revenue floor.

*Note: Server costs ($450) are fixed and do not influence the revenue volatility comparison between the two models, though they determine your break-even point (approx. 16 users on flat rate).*
