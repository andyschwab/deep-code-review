**Option Analysis**
1.  **Forward Contracts (Recommended):** You agree to buy 12M JPY at a fixed rate on a specific date.
    *   *Pros:* Eliminates uncertainty completely. If the rate moves to 110, you still pay the agreed rate (e.g., 145). Cost is typically embedded in the spread (0.5%–1.5%), which is predictable.
    *   *Cons:* You lose upside if the yen weakens further (e.g., to 160).
    *   *Verdict:* Best for preserving your known 12% margin. The cost of the hedge is a known line item; the cost of *not* hedging is an unknown variable that has already proven capable of wiping out profits.

2.  **Currency Options:** You pay a premium for the right, but not the obligation, to buy JPY at a set rate.
    *   *Pros:* Protects against the yen strengthening while allowing you to benefit if it weakens.
    *   *Cons:* Requires an upfront premium (cash outlay). For a small business, this premium might range from 2% to 4% of the notional value ($1,600–$3,200), which immediately reduces your margin before the trade even executes.
    *   *Verdict:* Likely too expensive relative to your transaction volume unless you have excess cash reserves specifically for risk management.

3.  **Dynamic Pricing:** Adjusting USD prices for retailers.
    *   *Pros:* No financial instrument costs.
    *   *Cons:* Retailers operate on their own margins and budgets. Frequent price changes create friction, complicate procurement, and may drive customers to competitors with stable pricing. In the specialty tea market, consistency is a product feature.

**Decision Trigger**
Execute a forward contract if the cost of the hedge (the spread) is less than 1.5% of the transaction value. Based on your history, a 1.5% cost (~$1,200) is significantly cheaper than the $18,000 loss you recently absorbed.
