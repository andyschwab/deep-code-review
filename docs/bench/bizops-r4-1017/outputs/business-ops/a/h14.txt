**Verdict:** Proceed with the price increase only if you can confirm with sponsors that losing up to 20% of your high-value segment (dropping from 480 to 384 users) does not trigger a contract clause reducing their fees; otherwise, the risk to your 40% operating cost coverage is too high.

**The Arithmetic**

Your current monthly recurring revenue (MRR) is **$156,800** (3,200 users × $49). Your target MRR at the new price, assuming no churn, would be **$252,800** (3,200 × $79).

You identified two distinct segments:
1.  **High-Value (HV):** 480 users (15% of 3,200).
2.  **Standard:** 2,720 users (85% of 3,200).

**Sponsorship Dependency:**
You need **$34,000/month** in sponsorship revenue (40% of $85k operating costs). This relies on maintaining a critical mass of HV users. If losing 20% of HV users (96 users) jeopardizes this $34k, your total monthly revenue gap becomes significant.

**Break-Even Analysis on Total Revenue:**
Let's model the scenario where the price hike causes extra churn specifically among HV users.
*   **Base Case (No Price Hike):** Subscription Revenue = $156,800.
*   **Target Case (Price Hike to $79):**
    *   If *zero* extra churn occurs: Subscription Revenue = $252,800. Gain = +$96,000.
    *   If you lose **20% of HV users** (96 users) due to the hike:
        *   Remaining HV: 384 users × $79 = $30,336.
        *   Standard Users (assuming stable 4% churn applies equally or they stay): 2,720 × $79 = $214,880.
        *   New Subscription MRR = $245,216.
        *   Net Subscription Gain vs. Base = $245,216 - $156,800 = **+$88,416**.

**The Risk Trigger:**
Mathematically, the subscription revenue gain (+$88k) vastly outweighs the loss of even all 96 HV users' direct subscription fees (96 × $49 = $4,704 lost base revenue). The subscription model alone improves cash flow significantly.

**However**, your constraint is the **sponsorship threshold**.
*   Required Sponsorship Coverage: $34,000/month.
*   If losing 96 HV users causes sponsors to pull out entirely or reduce fees by more than **$88,416**, the move is net-negative.
*   Since your total operating cost is only $85k, it is highly unlikely sponsors are paying >$120k/month (which would be required to lose >$88k in value). Therefore, strictly on *total revenue*, the price hike is likely positive even if you lose the sponsorship entirely, *provided* the sponsorship value is less than the subscription gain.

**But wait:** You stated you *need* sponsorship to cover 40% of costs. If the price hike kills the sponsorship, you must cover that $34k gap elsewhere or cut costs. The "break-even" on the *strategic goal* (maintaining sponsorship) is not a math problem but a contract one.

**Decision Aid:**
1.  **Read:** The subscription upside ($88k+) likely covers the loss of sponsorship ($34k) in pure cash terms, leaving you ~$54k better off even if sponsors leave.
2.  **Flip Input:** The variable that flips this call is the **sponsor contract structure**. If sponsors have a "minimum seat count" clause that reduces fees disproportionately (e.g., tiered pricing where dropping below 400 users slashes the rate by 80%), calculate that specific penalty.
3.  **Threshold Rule:** If the sponsor penalty > $88,416, do not raise prices. If the penalty < $88,416, raise prices and accept the segment loss.
4.  **Next Step:** Before announcing the hike, send a tentative notice to your top 3 sponsors asking: "If our verified PM count dips temporarily below 400 during a pricing transition, how does that impact our current agreement?"

**Sensitivity Check:**
If standard users also churn at a higher rate due to the price hike (e.g., rising from 4% to 6%), recalculate using: `New MRR = (Remaining Users × $79)`. You can afford to lose roughly **1,100 total users** (not just HV) before subscription revenue drops below your current $156,800 baseline.
