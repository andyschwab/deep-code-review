**The Arithmetic**

*Scenario B: Raise Price (Price $52)*

**The Break-Even Analysis (The Flip Input)**
The decision hinges entirely on how many backers you lose due to the $7 increase. To match the $45,000 profit from Scenario A, Scenario B only needs to sell **1,526 units** ($45,000 ÷ $29.50).

This means you can afford to lose up to **474 backers (23.7% of your projected volume)** and still generate the same total profit as the lower-priced option. If your price elasticity causes a drop in pledges greater than 24%, only then does the lower price become the superior financial choice.

**Decision Aid & Next Step**
1.  **Read:** Raising the price creates a $14,000 surplus at current volume, providing a buffer against volume loss.
2.  **Threshold:** If you believe more than 24% of your potential backers will refuse to pledge at $52, do not raise the price.
3.  **Rule of Thumb:** In crowdfunding, perceived value often outweighs small price increments if the campaign narrative is strong; however, cutting your marketing budget (Scenario A's implicit trade-off) typically reduces total volume more severely than a modest price hike.
4.  **Next Step:** Before locking the tier, run a "Van Westendorp" style micro-survey with your existing email list or a small ad test ($50 spend) asking backers at what price point the game becomes "too expensive." Use this real data to replace the assumed 24% tolerance limit.

*Note: This analysis assumes shipping costs remain fixed at $8 and does not account for Kickstarter platform fees (approx. 5%) or payment processing fees, which would reduce net profit in both scenarios proportionally but not change the break-even volume logic.*
