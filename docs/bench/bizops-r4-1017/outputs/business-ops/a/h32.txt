**The Arithmetic**
Your unit economics have deteriorated due to the fixed cost spike.
*   **Revenue per unit:** $75
*   **Variable cost (labor/material):** $40
*   **Previous insurance allocation:** $30,000 / 8,000 units = $3.75/unit
*   **New insurance allocation:** $48,000 / 8,000 units = $6.00/unit
*   **New Contribution Margin:** $75 - $40 - $6 = **$29 per coin** (down from $31.25).

While a $2.25 drop per coin seems small, it represents a **7.1% reduction in your profit pool** before fixed overhead. If volume dips even slightly, the per-unit insurance burden rises further. There is no industry-standard "ratio" to hit; the correct metric is whether the fee covers the marginal risk. A flat fee charges a collector of a $50 silver dollar the same as a collector of a $50,000 rarity, yet your liability exposure differs by 1,000x.

**The Restructuring Logic**
Adopt a **value-based pricing model** for the top tier. This is not raising prices arbitrarily; it is matching price to the cost of risk.
*   **Standard Tier (Value < $5,000):** Maintain $75. This keeps you competitive for high-volume, lower-risk submissions.
*   **Premium Tier (Value ≥ $5,000):** Calculate a fee that includes a specific risk surcharge. For example, if you estimate the potential liability cost on a $10,000 coin is significantly higher, the fee might move to $150–$250.

**Decision Thresholds**
*   **Break-even on Premium Tier:** If the premium fee is $150 and variable costs rise slightly to $50 (due to extra handling care), your margin is $100 minus the allocated insurance. Even if you allocate double the insurance cost ($12) to these rare items, the margin remains robust ($88).
*   **Trigger Point:** If more than 15% of your volume moves to the premium tier and total revenue does not increase by at least 10% year-over-year, re-evaluate the threshold value (e.g., move the cutoff from $5,000 to $2,500).
