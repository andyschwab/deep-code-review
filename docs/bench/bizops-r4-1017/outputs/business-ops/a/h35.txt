You should implement a **cumulative preferred return with a catch-up mechanism** within a single fund-level waterfall, rather than distributing profits deal-by-deal or holding idle cash manually. This structure mathematically equalizes returns for all investors regardless of when specific territory deals close, while ensuring capital efficiency.

In your scenario with lumpy, asynchronous revenue from territorial sales, the standard industry approach is a **whole-fund waterfall** instead of a deal-by-deal distribution. Here is how the arithmetic and structure apply to your £2 million fund:

1.  **Return of Capital (ROC):** All incoming cash flow (e.g., the £150k UK sale or the later £300k North American sale) goes first to returning the initial £2 million capital to all ten investors pro-rata. No one receives "profit" until every investor has received 100% of their committed capital back. This prevents early investors from being paid out on paper profits while later capital is still at risk.
2.  **Preferred Return (Hurdle):** Once capital is returned, subsequent cash flows pay a preferred return (e.g., 8% simple or compounded annually) to all investors until that hurdle is met. This compensates investors for the time value of money without needing to hold cash idle; the "interest" accrues mathematically even if cash is distributed later.
3.  **Catch-Up:** If your fund includes a General Partner (you) share, a "catch-up" clause ensures you receive your share of profits (e.g., 20%) only after investors have received their full preferred return. This aligns your incentives with long-term fund performance rather than quick, early exits.
4.  **Carried Interest:** Only after the above steps are satisfied does the remaining profit split according to the agreed ratio (e.g., 80/20).

**Why this solves your timing problem:**
By pooling all territorial revenues into one pot and applying this sequence, you eliminate the unfairness of early vs. late investors. An investor who joined early does not get an advantage from a quick UK sale because that cash first fills the ROC bucket for *everyone*. Conversely, you do not need to hold cash idle to "equalize" manually; the waterfall formula dictates who gets paid what based on the cumulative position of the fund. The "hurdle rate" you asked about is essential here: it acts as the mathematical equalizer for time, ensuring that investors who waited two years for the North American deal are compensated via the accrued preferred return before any profit sharing occurs.

**Decision Aid:**
*   **The Flip Input:** The specific percentage of your preferred return (e.g., 6% vs. 8%) will determine how much cash is effectively "reserved" mathematically for investors before you can participate. Higher hurdles protect investors more but delay your carry.
*   **Threshold Rule:** If your projected internal rate of return (IRR) on the three films is below your proposed hurdle rate, reconsider the hurdle; a hurdle higher than the fund's actual performance creates a scenario where the GP never participates, potentially misaligning incentives.
