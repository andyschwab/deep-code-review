**Verdict:** Your current $8,000 working capital is insufficient to fund the inventory jump to 500 subscribers; you will face a cash gap of approximately $4,900 during the growth month and should secure a small line of credit or delay the ramp-up until cash reserves increase.

**The Arithmetic**
We calculate the cash required to bridge the gap between paying suppliers and receiving usable funds from customers.

1.  **Unit Economics:**
    *   Revenue per box: $35
    *   Cost of Goods Sold (COGS): $14
    *   Gross Margin: $21 (60%)

2.  **Inventory Cash Requirement (The Outflow):**
    *   Current subscribers (200): $14 × 200 = $2,800
    *   Target subscribers (500): $14 × 500 = $7,000
    *   **Incremental Inventory Cost:** $7,000 - $2,800 = **$4,200**
    *   *Timing:* You must pay this $7,000 total (or the incremental portion) **2 weeks before** shipping.

3.  **Cash Availability (The Inflow):**
    *   Current Monthly Revenue: $35 × 200 = $7,000
    *   Target Monthly Revenue: $35 × 500 = $17,500
    *   *Timing:* Funds clear 3 days after billing. Assuming billing occurs at the start of the month, funds are usable by Day 4.

4.  **The Cash Conversion Cycle Gap:**
    *   To grow to 500 subscribers next quarter, you must purchase inventory for 500 units two weeks prior to the first shipment of that batch.
    *   If you ramp up immediately, you need **$7,000** cash on hand for inventory before you collect the new subscription revenue.
    *   Your current working capital is **$8,000**.
    *   However, you also have ongoing operational costs (staff, rent, utilities) not detailed in your numbers but covered by current cash flow. Even ignoring operating expenses to look strictly at inventory:
        *   Cash Needed for Target Inventory: $7,000
        *   Cash on Hand: $8,000
        *   Remaining Buffer: $1,000
    *   **The Risk:** This $1,000 buffer must cover your two full-time staff salaries and overhead for the 2-week period before any new money clears. If your semi-monthly payroll exceeds $1,000 (likely for two full-time staff in Vancouver), you will dip into your emergency fund immediately.

**The Flip Input**
The variable that flips this from "risky" to "safe" is the **ramp-up speed**. If you add subscribers gradually (e.g., 50 per week) rather than buying inventory for all 500 at once, the incoming cash from new sign-ups can fund the subsequent weeks' inventory, reducing the upfront cash spike.

**Decision Thresholds & Next Step**
*   **Rule of Thumb:** If (Inventory Cost for Target Volume) > (Current Cash - 1 Month Operating Expenses), you need external financing.
*   **Break-Even Cash Need:** You need roughly $9,500–$10,000 cash on hand to safely cover the $7,000 inventory spike plus two weeks of payroll/overhead without touching emergency reserves.
*   **Action:** Since you have $8,000, you are short by an estimated $1,500–$2,000 depending on your exact burn rate.
