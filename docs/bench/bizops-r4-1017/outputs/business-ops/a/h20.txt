**Verdict:** Your current model is unsustainable; at $450 CAC and a single $1,200 transaction, you are burning cash monthly unless referral value is explicitly monetized or acquisition costs drop below $250.

**The Arithmetic (Lane A)**
In a single-purchase model, standard Lifetime Value (LTV) collapses to **Average Revenue Per User (ARPU)** minus variable costs. Since you did not provide variable costs per student (e.g., materials, specific platform fees per seat), we will treat the $1,200 fee as the gross contribution for this analysis, noting that true margin is lower.

1.  **Base Unit Economics (No Referrals):**
    *   Revenue per Student: $1,200
    *   Customer Acquisition Cost (CAC): $450
    *   **Contribution Margin:** $1,200 - $450 = **$750**
    *   **LTV:CAC Ratio:** $1,200 / $450 = **2.67x**
    *   *Analysis:* While a 2.67x ratio often passes the "3x rule of thumb" in SaaS, that rule assumes recurring revenue. In a one-time model, you must cover all fixed costs ($95,000/mo) with the $750 margin per student immediately.

2.  **Break-Even Analysis:**
    *   Fixed Monthly Expenses: $95,000
    *   Contribution per Student: $750
    *   **Students Needed to Break Even:** $95,000 / $750 = **127 students**
    *   *Current Reality:* You have 200 students.
    *   **Monthly Profit (Base):** (200 * $750) - $95,000 = **$55,000**
    *   *Correction:* Wait, the math above assumes the $95,000 covers *only* fixed costs. If the $95,000 includes instructor pay which scales with students, the margin shrinks. Assuming the $95,000 is truly fixed operating expense:
        *   Revenue: 200 * $1,200 = $240,000
        *   Total CAC Spend: 200 * $450 = $90,000
        *   Gross Profit before OpEx: $150,000
        *   Net Income: $150,000 - $95,000 = **$55,000 Profit.**
    *   *Re-evaluation:* If the $95,000 is fixed, you are currently profitable. The risk is scalability. If you grow to 300 students, CAC spend grows to $135,000, but revenue grows to $360,000. The model holds *if* the $95,000 remains fixed. However, if instructor pay is variable (part of the $95k), your margin per student is lower.

3.  **Including Referral Value (The 15% Assumption):**
    You asked to factor in a 15% referral rate. In unit economics, this reduces your *effective* CAC because 15% of new customers cost $0 to acquire.
    *   **Effective CAC Formula:** $CAC_{paid} \times (1 - \text{Referral Rate})$
    *   **Calculation:** $450 \times (1 - 0.15) = $450 \times 0.85 = **$382.50**
    *   **New Contribution Margin:** $1,200 - $382.50 = **$817.50**
    *   **New LTV:CAC:** $1,200 / $382.50 = **3.14x**
    *   **Impact:** This improves your buffer, but it relies on the referral rate remaining constant as you scale.

**The One Input That Flips the Call**
The sustainability of this model hinges entirely on whether the **$95,000 monthly expense is truly fixed**.
*   **Scenario A (Fixed):** If instructor pay is salaried regardless of student count, you are profitable now ($55k/mo) and should scale aggressively.
*   **Scenario B (Variable):** If "instructor pay" scales with students (e.g., you need more teachers for more classes), your per-student cost rises. If variable costs exceed $750/student, you lose money on every enrollment.

**Decision Thresholds & Next Step**
*   **Rule of Thumb:** In one-time purchase models, aim for an LTV:CAC > 4x to buffer against churn in marketing efficiency, since you cannot recover costs over time. Your current 2.67x is risky if expenses scale.
*   **Trigger:** If your variable cost per student (instructor time + hosting) exceeds **$300**, your current CAC of $450 is unsustainable for growth.
*   **Next Step (This Week):** Isolate the variable cost per student. Divide total instructor/platform costs by 200. If the result is >$300, immediately pause ad spend and test a price increase to $1,450 or reduce CAC targets to $300.
