**The Arithmetic of Leakage vs. Volume**
Your current unit economics show a Gross Transaction Value (GTV) of $210,000 last month ($42,000 revenue ÷ 20% commission). With a 15% month-over-month decline in repeat bookings among top tutors, you are losing roughly $6,300 in monthly revenue purely from churn, assuming static transaction values. If this trend continues linearly, you will lose over $75,000 in annualized revenue from this cohort alone.

To determine if lowering the commission increases net revenue, we apply a break-even volume analysis. Let $R$ be current revenue ($42,000), $c_1$ be the current rate (20%), and $c_2$ be the proposed rate (10%).
The formula for the required volume increase to maintain revenue is:
$$ \text{Required GTV Increase} = \frac{c_1}{c_2} - 1 $$
$$ \text{Required GTV Increase} = \frac{0.20}{0.10} - 1 = 100\% $$
At a 10% commission, you must **double** your total transaction volume just to match today's $42,000 revenue. This is a high bar. However, if you lower the rate to 15% instead:
$$ \text{Required GTV Increase} = \frac{0.20}{0.15} - 1 = 33.3\% $$
You only need a 33% increase in volume to break even against current revenue. Given that you are currently *losing* 15% of your repeat volume monthly, capturing that lost volume plus preventing future churn likely makes the 15% scenario more profitable than the 20% scenario within two quarters.

**The Decision Trigger**
The "flip" input here is the **off-platform migration rate**. If more than 33% of your potential repeat bookings are being lost to off-platform arrangements (or will be lost if fees stay at 20%), then a 15% commission is mathematically superior. Your observed 15% monthly decline suggests you are on a trajectory to exceed this loss threshold quickly.

**Pricing Method Application**
Do not guess the exact optimal rate. Apply a **Van Westendorp** price-sensitivity approach to your tutors:
1.  Survey your top 50 churn-risk tutors asking: "At what commission rate would you consider our platform too expensive to use?" and "At what rate would you question the quality/viability of the platform?"
2.  Use their responses to find the "Optimal Price Point" where the percentage of tutors finding it "too expensive" equals those finding it "a bargain."
3.  Test a **Good-Better-Best** tier: Keep 20% for new tutors needing heavy lead gen, but offer 15% (or 12%) to top tutors who bring their own students, effectively monetizing the platform as a payment/tooling layer rather than a lead source for them.

**Next Step**

Instrument the conversion rate of these specific tutors. If their repeat booking rate stabilizes or grows by >10% within 30 days, roll out the lower tier broadly. If volume does not recover, the issue is not price, and you must pivot to enforcement tools.

*Note: This analysis assumes your cost of goods sold (payment processing, server costs) scales linearly with GTV; verify your marginal costs do not erode the gain from volume.*
