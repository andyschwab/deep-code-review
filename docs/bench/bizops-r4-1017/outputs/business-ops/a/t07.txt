Keep the flat $5,000 license for now; switching to usage-based pricing at your current volumes exposes you to immediate revenue loss and amplifies downside risk during a downturn.

**The Arithmetic**
Your current baseline is 10 clients × $5,000 = **$50,000 MRR**.
Under the proposed usage model ($0.05/call) with your largest clients' volume (80,000 calls):
*   Revenue per client = 80,000 × $0.05 = **$4,000**.
*   Total MRR (assuming all 10 clients match this volume) = 10 × $4,000 = **$40,000**.

Even without a recession, the switch reduces your recurring revenue by **20% ($10,000/month)** based on the volume data provided.

**Recession Stress Test (20% Volume Drop)**
You noted a potential 20% drop in transaction volumes due to budget cuts.
*   **Flat Model:** Revenue remains **$50,000**. The client bears the volume risk; your contract value is stable.
*   **Usage Model:** Volume drops to 64,000 calls (80,000 × 0.8).
    *   Revenue per client = 64,000 × $0.05 = **$3,200**.
    *   Total MRR = **$32,000**.
    *   **Total Swing:** You lose **$18,000/month (36%)** compared to your current baseline.

**The Flip Input**
The usage model only equals your current $5,000 flat fee if a client makes **100,000 calls/month** ($5,000 ÷ $0.05).
*   If average volume < 100,000: Flat fee yields higher revenue.
*   If average volume > 100,000: Usage-based yields higher revenue.
Since your largest clients are currently at 80,000, they must grow volume by 25% just to break even with the old model, which contradicts your expectation of a downturn.

**Decision Thresholds & Next Step**
*   **Rule of Thumb:** If LTV volatility increases (as it does with usage pricing in a recession) without a corresponding increase in total contract value, the pricing change destroys enterprise value.
*   **Trigger:** Do not switch unless you have data showing >100,000 monthly calls per client or a strategic need to lower the entry barrier for *new* small clients who cannot afford $5,000.
*   **Action This Week:** Run a "hybrid" simulation for your top 3 clients only. Propose a lower base fee (e.g., $2,500) + usage overage, rather than a pure usage swap, to test price sensitivity without capping your upside or guaranteeing a revenue cut.

While usage-based models can align incentives, applying them when your current volumes are below the break-even point and macro trends suggest volume contraction mathematically guarantees a reduction in Total Contract Value.
