Your current transaction-fee model is mathematically non-viable at an $8.50 average order value (AOV), generating a loss on every single transaction; you must either increase the AOV to at least $16.67 or pivot to a subscription model immediately rather than waiting to influence user behavior.

**Unit Economics Analysis (Current State)**
At your current volume and pricing, the math shows a negative contribution margin per order:
*   **Revenue per order:** ($8.50 × 1.5%) + $0.30 = $0.1275 + $0.30 = **$0.4275**
*   **Estimated Payment Processor Cost:** Standard rates are typically ~2.9% + $0.30. Assuming you pay this on the gross amount: ($8.50 × 2.9%) + $0.30 = $0.2465 + $0.30 = **$0.5465**
*   **Net Revenue per Order:** $0.4275 (your fee) - $0.5465 (processor cost) = **-$0.1190 loss per order**.
*   **Monthly Impact:** 5,000 orders × -$0.1190 = **-$595 monthly loss** before even accounting for your $1,200 fixed server costs or developer payroll. You are effectively paying customers to use the platform.

**Break-Even for 20% Gross Margin**
To achieve a 20% gross margin, your net revenue must cover the payment processor fees plus leave 20% of the AOV as profit. Assuming the standard processor fee of 2.9% + $0.30 applies to the total transaction value:

Let $P$ be the required Average Order Value.
*   Your Revenue = $0.015P + 0.30$
*   Processor Cost = $0.029P + 0.30$
*   Target Gross Profit = $0.20P$

The equation for viability is:
$(Your Revenue) - (Processor Cost) \geq (Target Gross Profit)$
$(0.015P + 0.30) - (0.029P + 0.30) \geq 0.20P$
$-0.014P \geq 0.20P$

This results in a mathematical impossibility under the current fee structure because your fee (1.5%) is lower than the likely processor cost (2.9%). You cannot achieve a positive margin, let alone 20%, if your revenue percentage is lower than your variable cost percentage, regardless of how high the AOV goes. The fixed $0.30 components cancel out, leaving you with a negative spread of -1.4% on every dollar.

For this model to work, your fee percentage must exceed the processor's percentage. **Decision Thresholds & Next Steps**
1.  **Immediate Pivot Required:** Do not wait to test user behavior on order size. The structural deficit (1.5% revenue vs ~2.9% cost) means higher volume increases your total loss.
2.  **Subscription Model:** Switch to a flat SaaS fee (e.g., $49–$99/truck/month). With 50 trucks, a $50/month fee generates $2,500/month, covering servers and providing a surplus, while decoupling your revenue from the low AOV problem.
3.  **If Keeping Transaction Model:** You must reprice immediately. Your fee must be at least **3.5% + $0.30** (assuming 2.9% processor cost) to begin generating any gross profit. At 3.5% + $0.30, your net per order on $8.50 becomes: ($0.3025 + $0.30) - ($0.2465 + $0.30) = $0.056 profit/order. This is still too low to cover fixed costs.

**Recommendation:** Abandon the 1.5% transaction model. It fails the unit economics test at any AOV because your take-rate is below your cost-of-goods-sold (payment processing). Adopt a flat monthly subscription per truck to ensure positive unit economics immediately.
