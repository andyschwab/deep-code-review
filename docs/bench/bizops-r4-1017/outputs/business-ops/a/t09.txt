**The Arithmetic (Applied to Your Numbers)**

We analyze a typical month with **20 new orders** at **$450/suit**.
*   **Total Revenue Potential:** $9,000.
*   **Supplier Obligation:** Assuming fabric costs are a significant portion of the $450 (even if only 40%, or $180/suit), your total outflow obligation for 20 suits is **$3,600 minimum**, due 14 days before shipping.

**Scenario B: Proposed Installment Model (4 payments of $112.50)**

*   **Inflow (Day 30):** 2nd payment ($2,250).

    *   **Critical Risk:** If you attempt to ship earlier (e.g., after the 2nd payment to improve customer satisfaction), you must pay suppliers before collecting the full balance.
    *   **Worst Case (Standard Retail Practice):** If production starts immediately to meet delivery expectations, suppliers demand payment roughly 30–45 days after order.

**The Flip Input:**
The variable that flips this from "crunch" to "viable" is the **Fabric Cost %** and the **Production Lead Time**.
*   *Break-even Fabric Cost:* To survive on only the first two payments ($225 collected), your fabric cost must be under **$225/suit**. If it is higher, you burn cash.
*   *Time Lag:* If the time from Order → Supplier Payment is > 60 days, the 4-payment plan works. If it is < 60 days (likely), you fund the gap.

**Decision Aid:**
1.  **Read:** With a 50% deposit, you are self-funding. With 25% down, you become a bank for your customers.
2.  **Formula:** `Cash Gap = (Fabric Cost × Orders) - (Down Payment × Orders + Installments Received before Supplier Due Date)`.
3.  **Threshold:** If `Cash Gap` > $0, you cannot execute without external capital. Given standard suit margins, your gap will be positive.
4.  **Next Step:** Before changing terms, calculate your exact **Cost of Goods Sold (COGS)** per suit. If COGS > $225, reject the 4-payment plan unless you switch to "Pay in Full before Shipping" or secure a revolving credit line equal to at least 3 months of fabric costs.
