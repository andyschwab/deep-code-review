Adopt a **non-refundable deposit tied to metal weight** combined with a **72-hour quote validity window**. This structure directly neutralizes your specific risk (gold swings wiping out the $400 labor margin) while keeping the customer experience simple.

Here is the arithmetic applied to your numbers:
*   **Risk Exposure:** 0.5 oz × $50 swing = **$25 potential loss per ring**.
*   **Current Margin:** $400 labor fee. A $25 loss represents a **6.25% erosion** of your margin on a single bad timing event.
*   **Deposit Calculation:** Require a deposit equal to the current spot value of the gold (0.5 oz × current price) or a fixed buffer (e.g., $300) that covers the maximum expected weekly swing.
*   **Validity Window:** Set quotes to expire in 3 days. Given your 3-week production cycle, locking the price for longer than a few days without committed capital is an uninsured bet against the commodities market.

**Why this works better than the alternatives:**
1.  **Vs. Larger Buffer:** Building a $50+ buffer into every base price raises your average sale price from $1,800 to ~$1,850+. In a competitive market, this "hidden tax" may reduce conversion rates on jobs where gold prices actually remain stable.
2.  **Vs. Validity Period Only:** A time limit alone protects you only if the customer signs quickly. It does not protect you if they sign on day 3, and you buy materials on day 4 when prices spike.
3.  **The Deposit Solution:** By collecting the metal cost (or a significant portion) upfront, you purchase the metal immediately upon order confirmation. The customer effectively hedges their own ring. If gold drops before you buy, you can either refund the difference (delighting the customer) or retain it as additional margin (protecting against future spikes).

**Implementation Steps:**
*   **Quote Structure:** Present the price as "Metal Cost (Market Rate at Order) + $400 Artisan Fee."
*   **Agreement Clause:** "To secure production scheduling and lock in the metal price, a deposit of $X (covering estimated material costs) is required. Final settlement occurs upon delivery, adjusting only for precise weight variances."
*   **Threshold Rule:** If the spot price moves more than 2% ($4/oz) between quote and deposit, issue a revised quote automatically.

This approach shifts the commodity risk from your P&L to the timing of the customer's commitment, which is standard practice for bespoke goods involving volatile raw materials. It ensures you never lose money on the spread because you do not purchase the metal until the deposit clears.

*Note: While this structure mitigates financial risk, ensure your deposit terms comply with New York State general business laws regarding non-refundable fees and clear disclosure of final pricing mechanisms.*
