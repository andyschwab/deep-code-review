**Regulatory Regime Identification**
The activity you are describing falls under **State Notary Public Laws** and **Remote Online Notarization (RON) Statutes**. There is no single "national" license. Each of the 50 states acts as a separate sovereign regime with its own application process, technology standards, and bonding requirements. Additionally, because you are handling real estate transactions, you may trigger **State Real Estate Settlement Procedures** and **Data Privacy Laws** (such as CCPA in California or specific data residency rules in other states).

**General Categories of Requirements (Lane R – Route to Counsel)**
While I cannot provide legal advice on which specific forms to file, the general categories you will face in every jurisdiction include:
1.  **Commissioning:** You or your appointed notaries must hold a valid commission in *each* specific state where the signer is located at the time of notarization. A Delaware commission does not authorize you to notarize a document for a signer in Texas.
2.  **RON Registration:** Many states require a separate registration specifically for performing remote notarizations, often requiring proof of identity verification knowledge.
3.  **Surety Bonds & E&O Insurance:** States mandate specific bond amounts (ranging from $5,000 to $50,000+) and Errors & Omissions insurance policies tailored to that state's statutes.
4.  **Technology Compliance:** States maintain approved lists of RON technology providers. You cannot simply build your own video stack; it must often be certified by the state secretary of state to ensure specific recording, storage, and identity-proofing (KBA/biometric) standards are met.
5.  **Journal & Record Retention:** Laws vary wildly on how long recordings and journals must be kept (from 3 to 10+ years) and where they must be stored physically or digitally.
