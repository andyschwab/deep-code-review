Keep the upfront sale model for now; the leasing scenario burns your cash reserves in 30 months without a guaranteed valuation multiple expansion to offset the risk.

**The Arithmetic**
Your current unit economics show a gross profit of $2,400 per unit ($4,200 price - $1,800 COGS). Under the proposed lease, you collect $150/month.
*   **Payback Period on Hardware Cost:** $1,800 COGS ÷ $150/month = **12 months** just to recover the hard cost, ignoring sales commissions, support, and the $40,000 monthly inventory financing hit.
*   **Runway Impact:** You stated leasing increases monthly cash outflow by $40,000. With $1.2M in reserves, your runway under the lease model is $1,200,000 ÷ $40,000 = **30 months** assuming zero other operating expenses. If your existing burn (salaries, R&D) is $100k/month, total burn becomes $140k/month, collapsing runway to **~8.5 months**.
*   **Revenue Recognition:** While $150/month creates recurring revenue, it is heavily discounted compared to the present value of $4,200 upfront. To match one upfront sale, you need 28 months of lease payments ($4,200 ÷ $150), not accounting for the time value of money or the cost of capital.

**The Valuation Trade-off**
Investors do value recurring revenue (ARR) higher than one-time sales, often applying 5–10x multiples on ARR versus 1–3x on transactional revenue. However, this premium applies to *software* margins. In your leasing model, you are effectively becoming a hardware financing company with depreciating assets on the balance sheet.
*   **The Asset Drag:** Owning the hardware means you carry depreciation risk and maintenance liability. If a unit fails in month 18, you bear the replacement cost, eroding the LTV.
*   **The "Fake" ARR:** Sophisticated agritech investors will strip out the hardware cost basis from your valuation. If your COGS is 60% of the potential lifetime value (as seen in the 12-month hardware payback), your gross margins on the lease look like a services business (40–50%), not a SaaS business (70–80%). This compresses the valuation multiple you hope to gain.

**Decision Thresholds**
*   **Flip Point:** Leasing only outperforms upfront sales if you can reduce the hardware COGS below $900 (achieving 6-month hardware payback) or if the lease price increases to >$250/month.
*   **Runway Trigger:** If adopting leasing reduces your total runway to under 18 months (your fundraising target), you cannot execute the pivot. You must raise debt specifically for inventory before switching models, not use operating cash.

**Recommendation**
Do not shift the entire fleet to leasing before the raise. The immediate strain on liquidity creates a "distressed sale" signal to investors, which lowers valuation more than the revenue model change raises it. Instead, run a pilot: lease to 5–10 courses using a dedicated equipment loan (keeping the $1.2M operational cash intact). Prove that churn is <2% annually and that the lifetime margin exceeds the upfront margin after 36 months. Use that data to negotiate better terms, rather than betting the company's survival on a theoretical multiple expansion.
