**Verdict:** Do not attempt to offset a 30% fuel spike solely by increasing volume; the required ~4 extra jobs per van daily is likely operationally impossible given your current 12-job density. Immediately implement a variable fuel surcharge or switch to a hub-and-spoke drop-off model to protect margins.

**The Arithmetic**
Your current baseline per van is 12 jobs/day × 22 working days ≈ 264 jobs/month.
*   **Current Revenue:** 264 jobs × $85 = $22,440.
*   **Current Variable Costs (excl. fuel):** 264 × $22 = $5,808.
*   **Current Fuel Cost:** $1,200.
*   **Current Contribution Margin:** $22,440 - $5,808 - $1,200 = $15,432 per van.
*   **Fleet Total:** $15,432 × 6 = $92,592. (Note: Your stated $18k net profit implies ~$74.5k in fixed costs/owner pay, which we will hold constant).

**The Shock Scenario ($4.00+ Diesel)**
A jump from $3.10 to $4.00 is a ~29% increase. You estimated a 30% fuel cost rise.
*   **New Fuel Cost:** $1,200 × 1.30 = $1,560 per van.
*   **New Monthly Loss in Margin:** $360 per van × 6 vans = **$2,160 total monthly erosion.**

**Option A: Volume Offset (No Price Change)**
To recover the $360/van loss purely through volume:
*   **Contribution per *new* job:** $85 (price) - $22 (variable) = $63. (Note: We assume the marginal fuel cost per *additional* job is low if routed efficiently, but even if we include a prorated fuel share, the math is stark).
*   **Jobs needed per van:** $360 loss ÷ $63 contribution = **5.7 extra jobs/day.**
*   *Conservative Check:* If the extra jobs also incur full fuel costs ($1,560/264 jobs ≈ $5.45/job), the contribution drops to $57.55.
*   **Revised Jobs needed:** $360 ÷ $57.55 = **6.25 extra jobs/day.**

**Reality Check:** Increasing from 12 to ~18 jobs per day is a **50% volume increase**. Given travel time between Chicago appointments, this is almost certainly unrealistic without sacrificing service quality or violating labor constraints. Relying on volume alone will fail.

**Option B: Fuel Surcharge (Recommended)**
Apply the cost directly to the consumer.
*   **Surcharge per job:** $360 loss ÷ 264 jobs = **$1.36 per appointment.**
*   Rounding to **$1.50 or $2.00** creates a buffer. This is invisible to most customers compared to a base price hike from $85 to $87.

**Option C: Hub-and-Spoke**
If pets are dropped off at a central location, fuel costs drop precipitously (likely >50% reduction in miles).
*   **Estimated New Fuel:** $600/van.
*   **Savings:** $600/van vs the shock scenario.
*   **Trade-off:** You lose the "mobile" value proposition. Only pursue if customers value price/convenience over the mobile experience, or offer this as a discounted "drop-off" tier.

**Decision Thresholds & Next Steps**
*   **Rule of Thumb:** If a cost spike cannot be absorbed by <10% volume growth, it must be passed to the customer or engineered out (route change). You are at +50% volume requirement; **do not choose volume.**
*   **Flip Input:** If your average job duration is under 45 minutes, volume *might* work. If over 60 minutes, it is mathematically impossible.
*   **Action This Week:** Calculate the exact mileage per job. If average miles > 8, implement a **$2.00 "Fuel Stability Fee"** on all new bookings immediately. Simultaneously, survey 20 loyal clients on their willingness to drop off pets for a 10% discount to test the hub-and-spoke viability.
