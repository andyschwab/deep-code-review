### Lane A: Unit Economics & Pricing Sensitivity Analysis

You are facing a classic marketplace leakage problem where the commission fee exceeds the perceived value of the platform's services (lead generation, payment processing, trust). Since you lack specific data on the "churn threshold," we cannot calculate a precise optimal rate, but we can model the **break-even volume shift** required to make a rate cut profitable.

**1. The Current Baseline**
*   **Gross Transaction Value (GTV):** $42,000 (Revenue) ÷ 20% (Commission) = **$210,000**.
*   **Current Revenue:** $42,000.
*   **Implied Leakage Signal:** A 15% month-over-month decline in repeat bookings for top tutors suggests the effective "tax" of 20% is driving behavior off-platform.

**2. The "Cut to 10%" Scenario (Apply, Don't Direct)**
If you lower the commission to 10%, your revenue per transaction halves. To maintain your current revenue of $42,000, your GTV must double.
*   **Formula:** `Required GTV = Target Revenue ÷ New Commission Rate`
*   **Calculation:** $42,000 ÷ 0.10 = **$420,000**.
*   **The Hurdle:** You need to grow your platform's transaction volume by **100%** (from $210k to $420k) just to break even on revenue, assuming no change in operating costs.

**3. Sensitivity Analysis: Finding the Tipping Point**
Since you do not know the exact elasticity, you must test for the point where `(New Rate × New Volume) > (Old Rate × Old Volume)`.
*   **Scenario A (Conservative):** You cut to 15%. You only need a **33% increase** in GTV to match current revenue.
    *   Math: $42,000 ÷ 0.15 = $280,000 GTV needed.
*   **Scenario B (Aggressive):** You cut to 10%. You need a **100% increase** in GTV.
*   **The Unknown Variable:** The "Leakage Rate." If 20% of your current GMV is already lost to off-platform deals because of the 20% fee, capturing even half of that leakage might justify a cut. However, if the leakage is only 5%, a rate cut destroys margin without recovering enough volume.

**Decision Aid & Next Steps**
*   **Read:** A 10% rate requires doubling your active transaction volume to maintain revenue; this is a high bar unless off-platform migration is massive.
*   **Flip Input:** The critical unknown is the **percentage of lessons currently moving off-platform**. If this number is >30%, a rate cut likely pays off. If <10%, a rate cut destroys profit.
*   **Threshold Rule:** Do not cut rates broadly until you verify the leakage volume. Standard marketplace heuristic: If LTV (Lifetime Value of a tutor) drops faster than commission revenue when rates rise, the rate is too high.
*   **Actionable Step:** Run a targeted A/B test this week. Select 50 tutors with declining repeat bookings and offer them a temporary 12% rate for next month in exchange for a commitment to keep bookings on-platform. Measure if their volume rebounds enough to offset the lower rate. Use this data to estimate the elasticity before changing pricing for everyone.

**Note:** This analysis assumes your cost to serve (payment processing, support) scales linearly with volume; if costs are fixed, the break-even volume target is slightly lower.
**Note:** Ensure any temporary rate changes are documented in updated terms of service to avoid contract disputes.
