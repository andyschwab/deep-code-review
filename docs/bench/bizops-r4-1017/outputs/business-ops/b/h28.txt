### Lane R: Regulation-Dependent Analysis (Sales Tax Nexus)

Your situation involves **state sales tax nexus**, a legal determination that depends on specific state statutes regarding "economic nexus" and the taxability of digital services. Because the correct answer depends on laws that vary wildly and change frequently, this is a **Lane R** issue. I cannot tell you definitively which states you owe tax in or give a binding "yes/no" on registration; only a tax professional can make that determination.

However, I can explain the general rules, the regime, and the factors that create risk in your current approach.

**The Regime: Economic Nexus**
Following the US Supreme Court's *South Dakota v. Wayfair* decision, most states have adopted "economic nexus" laws. This means you can owe sales tax in a state even without a physical presence (like an office or employees) if you exceed specific economic thresholds.
*   **The General Rule:** Many states trigger nexus if you have either **$100,000 in gross revenue** OR **200 separate transactions** delivered into that state within a 12-month period.
*   **The Variation:** Some states use only a revenue threshold (e.g., $100k), some use only a transaction count, and some (like California) have a higher revenue threshold ($500k) but still count transactions. A few states have different rules for "digital goods" versus "services."

**Key Risks in Your Current Approach**
1.  **The "Virtual Presence" Misconception:** Your assumption that a virtual presence exempts you is likely incorrect under modern economic nexus laws. Physical presence is no longer the sole determinant.
2.  **Transaction Count Vulnerability:** With 300 clients last quarter (~100/month), if your clients are evenly distributed, you might be safe. However, if you have concentrated sales in specific states, you could easily hit the **200-transaction threshold** in a single state quickly, even if your revenue there is under $100,000. For example, if 210 clients were in Texas last year, you likely have nexus there regardless of revenue.
3.  **Service Taxability:** Not all states tax "interior design services," especially if delivered virtually. Some tax digital products but not services; others tax both. You need to verify if your specific service code is taxable in each state where you have nexus.
4.  **Accumulating Liability:** If you have already crossed a threshold in a state and failed to collect tax, you may be liable for the uncollected tax plus penalties and interest. Your $25,000 reserve is a prudent start, but liability could exceed this if multiple states are involved.

**Factors Determining Liability**
To assess your exposure, a professional will need you to break down your last 12 months of sales by:
*   **State:** Total revenue and total transaction count per state.
*   **Service Classification:** How each state categorizes "virtual interior design."

**Routing the Binding Call**
You should not wait to hit thresholds to seek advice; you need to audit your past data immediately.
*   **Action:** Engage a CPA or sales tax specialist familiar with multi-state e-commerce.
*   **Question to bring:** "Given my revenue and transaction count per state for the last 12 months, in which states have I established economic nexus, and is my specific service taxable in those jurisdictions?"
*   **Documents to prepare:** A spreadsheet listing every transaction from the last 12 months with the customer's state, the amount charged, and the date.

Do not register or start collecting tax in specific states until a professional confirms your nexus status, as improper registration can create new filing obligations unnecessarily. Conversely, do not delay this review, as penalties for non-compliance can grow over time.
