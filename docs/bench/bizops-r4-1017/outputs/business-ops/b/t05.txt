### Lane A: Runway Arithmetic & Cash Flow Gap Analysis

**1. Baseline Scenario (Net-15 Terms)**
*   **Formula:** `Runway = Cash Balance ÷ Net Monthly Burn`
*   **Inputs:** $150,000 cash; $18,000 burn.
*   **Calculation:** $150,000 / $18,000 = **8.33 months**.
*   **Context:** This aligns with your expectation of ~8 months.

**2. 90-Day Term Scenario (The Cash Gap)**
To model the impact, we must isolate the timing difference between when you incur costs (burn) and when you receive revenue.

*   **Revenue Profile:** 3 clients × $25,000/year = $75,000 total annual contract value (ACV).
*   **Monthly Recognized Revenue:** $75,000 / 12 = $6,250/month.
*   **Payment Delay:** 90 days vs. 15 days = **75-day additional delay**.
*   **Cash Inflow Block:** You will perform ~2.5 months of work ($6,250 × 2.5 = ~$15,625) before receiving the first payment for that work.

**The "Gap" Calculation:**
While waiting for the first payments, your burn continues at $18,000/month.
*   **Cash consumed during the 90-day wait:** $18,000 × 3 months = $54,000.
*   **Cash remaining after 3 months:** $150,000 - $54,000 = $96,000.
*   **Status at Month 3:** You have $96,000 left. You finally receive the first quarter's payments (~$18,750 assuming even billing cycles, or potentially lump sum depending on contract start dates).
    *   *Note:* If billed annually in arrears (common with net-90), you receive $0 at Month 3, extending the cash drain. If billed quarterly, you receive ~$18,750.

**Revised Runway Estimate (Conservative/Arrears Assumption):**
If payments are truly delayed 90 days from invoice (and invoices are sent monthly or quarterly), your effective cash balance drops faster initially.
*   **Adjusted Monthly Burn (until payments hit):** $18,000 (no offsetting cash inflow).
*   **Runway without new cash:** Still roughly **8.3 months** from today *if* the revenue eventually covers the burn rate completely.
*   **The Critical Risk:** Your burn ($18k) exceeds your new monthly revenue run-rate ($6.25k).
    *   **Net Burn with Revenue:** $18,000 - $6,250 = $11,750/month (once payments stabilize).
    *   **Runway with Revenue Stabilized:** $150,000 / $11,750 = **12.7 months**.

**However, the "Valley of Death" (Months 1–4):**
You face a liquidity crunch before the stabilized runway kicks in.
*   **Months 1–3 Cash Out:** $54,000.
*   **Cash Remaining:** $96,000.
*   **If invoices are paid in arrears (lump sum after 90 days):** You might not see cash until Month 4.
*   **Decision Threshold:** If your cash drops below **$40,000** (approx. 2 months of emergency buffer) before the first check clears, you are in the danger zone.

**Sensitivity & Factoring Cost Unknown:**

*   **Factoring Benefit:** Immediate cash of ~$15,625 (first quarter value) minus fee `F`.
*   **Break-even Fee:** If factoring extends your runway by preventing a crisis, a fee of 3–5% ($470–$780) is often mathematically preferable to a bridge round (which carries legal fees and equity dilution).

**Decision Aid:**

3.  **Rule of Thumb:** If cash on hand falls below 3x monthly burn ($54,000) before the first payment arrives, consider factoring.
