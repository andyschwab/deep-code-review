### Lane A: Unit Economics & Strategic Trade-off Analysis

To decide whether to pivot exclusively to institutional clients, we must compare the Lifetime Value (LTV), Customer Acquisition Cost (CAC), and cash flow implications of both segments using your provided numbers.

#### 1. Individual Segment (B2C)
*   **Monthly Revenue:** $15
*   **Monthly Churn:** 8% (0.08)
*   **Average Lifespan:** $1 \div 0.08 = 12.5$ months
*   **LTV (Gross):** $15 \times 12.5 = \$187.50$
*   **CAC:** Assumed near $0$ (instant website sign-up), but let's allocate a small marketing overhead if applicable. For this math, we treat CAC as negligible compared to B2B.
*   **Payback Period:** Immediate (first month covers most costs if variable costs are low).
*   **Annual Value per User:** $\$15 \times 12 \text{ months} \times (1 - \text{churn impact}) \approx \$114$ (rough estimate of actual collected revenue before churn).

#### 2. Institutional Segment (B2B)
*   **Annual Revenue:** $2,500
*   **Monthly Revenue Equivalent:** $\approx \$208$
*   **Churn:** Near 0% (Assume 100% retention for calculation, or a multi-year lifespan).
*   **LTV (1 Year):** $2,500
*   **LTV (3 Years):** $7,500 (assuming no discounting for simplicity)
*   **CAC:** $1,200 (commissions) + 6 months of sales effort.
*   **LTV:CAC Ratio (Year 1):** $2,500 \div 1,200 = \mathbf{2.08x}$
*   **Payback Period:** Since the fee is annual, if paid upfront, payback is immediate upon signing. If paid monthly, it takes $\approx 6$ months just to cover the commission, ignoring the 6-month sales cycle.

#### 3. The Cash Flow & Risk Trade-off
*   **Current Annual Run Rate:**
    *   Institutional: $300 \times \$2,500 = \$750,000$
    *   Individual: $4,000 \times \$15 \times 12 = \$720,000$ (ignoring churn decay for top-line potential).
    *   **Total:** $\approx \$1.47M$
*   **The Pivot Risk:**
    *   **Sales Cycle Gap:** Acquiring a new institutional client takes 6 months. If you shift resources away from individuals today, your individual revenue will decay rapidly due to the 8% monthly churn.
    *   **Decay Math:** In 6 months, remaining individual subscribers = $4,000 \times (0.92)^6 \approx 2,420$. You lose $\approx 40\%$ of this revenue stream before a single new institutional deal closes.
    *   **Replacement Rate:** To replace the lost revenue of 1,580 churned individuals ($\approx \$284k/year$), you need $\approx 114$ new institutional clients. At a 6-month cycle, this requires a massive sales team expansion and upfront cash to cover commissions ($114 \times \$1,200 = \$136k$) before revenue arrives.

#### Decision Aid
*   **Read:** The institutional model offers higher stability and a healthy LTV:CAC (>2.0x), but the **6-month sales cycle combined with 8% monthly B2C churn creates a dangerous cash flow valley** if you pivot exclusively now.
*   **Flip Input:** The critical variable is your **cash runway**. Can you survive 6–9 months with declining individual revenue while paying sales commissions upfront?
*   **Rule of Thumb:** If LTV:CAC > 3x and payback < 12 months, scale. Here, LTV:CAC is ~2.1x (Year 1), and payback is delayed by the sales cycle length.
*   **Next Step:** Do not pivot exclusively yet. Consider a hybrid approach: maintain the individual channel to fund cash flow while dedicating a specific, capped portion of resources (e.g., 1 sales rep) to test institutional acquisition efficiency. Instrument your CAC precisely for individuals; if it is truly near-zero, they are your funding engine for the slower B2B growth.

**Note:** The 8% monthly churn is exceptionally high; reducing this by even 2% drastically changes the B2C LTV math.
**Note:** Verify if institutional contracts are prepaid annually; if paid monthly, the cash flow risk doubles.
