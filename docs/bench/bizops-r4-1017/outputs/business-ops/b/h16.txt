**1. The Regime: GDPR and Cross-Border Transfers**
The primary regime is the EU General Data Protection Regulation (GDPR). Since you handle case files and client communications for EU firms, you are processing personal data.
*   **The General Rule:** GDPR Chapter V restricts transferring personal data outside the European Economic Area (EEA) to countries without an "adequacy decision" from the European Commission. Most Southeast Asian jurisdictions do not currently have full adequacy decisions.
*   **The Mechanism:** To transfer data legally to these regions, controllers typically rely on "Standard Contractual Clauses" (SCCs). However, following the *Schrems II* ruling, relying on SCCs requires a "Transfer Impact Assessment" (TIA). You must assess whether the local laws of the destination country (e.g., government surveillance laws in the specific Southeast Asian nation) undermine the protections guaranteed by the SCCs.
*   **Encryption:** While encryption is a vital supplementary measure, it is not an automatic exemption. Regulators examine whether the encryption keys remain solely under your control within the EU and whether the destination country's laws allow authorities to compel decryption or key handover.

**2. The "Archival" Question**
You asked if exemptions exist for archived, inactive data.
*   **General Rule:** The GDPR does not provide a blanket exemption for "cold storage" or inactive data. If the data identifies a living person, it remains personal data regardless of access frequency.
*   **Nuance:** There are specific provisions for "processing for archiving purposes in the public interest" (Article 89), but this usually applies to historical/scientific research contexts, not commercial legal archive services retaining data for potential future litigation or client retrieval. Assuming this exemption applies to your commercial model without legal validation is high-risk.
