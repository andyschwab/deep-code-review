### Lane A: Arithmetic & Unit Economics Application

**1. Current Model (Upfront Sale)**
*   **Gross Margin per Unit:** $4,200 (Price) − $1,800 (COGS) = **$2,400**.
*   **Cash Impact:** Immediate inflow of $4,200 per unit.
*   **Runway Context:** With $1.2M cash, you can cover ~30 months of your *current* burn (assuming no other costs), but adding the sales team salaries reduces this.

**2. Proposed Model (Leasing)**
*   **Monthly Revenue:** $150/unit.
*   **Break-even on Hardware Cost:** $1,800 (COGS) ÷ $150/month = **12 months** just to recover the hardware cost, ignoring the cost of capital and sales commissions.
*   **Cash Flow Strain:** You stated a **$40,000/month** increase in cash outflow for inventory financing.
*   **Runway Impact:** $1,200,000 (Cash) ÷ $40,000 (Incremental Burn) = **30 months** of runway *specifically allocated to covering this new financing gap*, assuming zero other operating expenses. If your total monthly burn (salaries + overhead + this $40k) exceeds $66,666, your 18-month fundraising goal is at risk without new capital.

**3. Valuation Trade-off Analysis (Applied to Your Inputs)**
*   **The Multiple Shift:** Investors often apply higher revenue multiples to recurring revenue (e.g., 5x–10x ARR) vs. one-time hardware sales (e.g., 1x–3x revenue).
*   **The Math of Perception:**
    *   *Scenario A (Sell 10 units):* $42,000 recognized immediately. Valuation impact ≈ $42k–$126k (at 1x–3x).
    *   *Scenario B (Lease 10 units):* $1,800 Annual Recurring Revenue (ARR). Valuation impact ≈ $9k–$18k (at 5x–10x) *in year one*.
    *   **Crossover Point:** To match the *immediate* valuation impact of selling 10 units ($42k revenue), you need roughly **$14k–$42k in ARR** under the leasing model (depending on the multiple). At $150/month ($1,800/year) per unit, you need **8 to 24 units leased** to generate equivalent *top-line valuation weight* as 10 sold units, but you have waited 12+ months just to recoup the hardware cost.

**Decision Aid:**
*   **Read:** The leasing model delays positive contribution margin by 12 months per unit and increases immediate burn by $40k/month.
*   **Flip Input:** The model works only if **(New ARR × Investor Multiple) > (Lost Upfront Cash + Cost of Capital)**.
*   **Threshold Rule of Thumb:** If your runway drops below **12 months** post-shift, the "recurring revenue premium" may be irrelevant because you cannot survive to raise the next round.
*   **Next Step:** Calculate the exact number of units you must lease in Month 1 to keep total burn under $66k/month (preserving 18-month runway). If that unit volume exceeds your historical sales velocity by >20%, the cash strain likely outweighs the valuation multiple benefit.

### Lane R: Regulation & Compliance Routing

**Securities & Fundraising Regime:**
Your question regarding "company valuation during fundraising" and "investor preference" touches on **securities laws**. While the arithmetic above shows unit economics, the *legal structure* of a leasing model vs. a sales model changes your balance sheet classification (operating lease vs. capital lease) and potentially how equity rounds are structured.
*   **Route:** A **securities attorney** and **CPA** must determine:
    1.  How the "asset-heavy" balance sheet affects your ability to issue SAFEs or priced equity under current SEC regulations.
    2.  Whether the leasing contracts constitute any form of regulated financial instrument.
    3.  The specific tax implications of depreciation schedules on the leased hardware.

**Note:** Do not finalize the pricing model shift until a CPA verifies the depreciation impact on your P&L, as this directly alters the "gross margin" inputs used above.
