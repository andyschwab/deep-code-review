*   **Upfront Loss per Installment Customer:** $1,600 ($2,400 full price - $800 first payment).

However, since every sale brings in *some* cash ($800 minimum), new orders actually add to your balance, they do not subtract from it.
    *   *Correction on Logic:* The risk is not that sales reduce cash, but that the **inflow is insufficient to replenish the $18,000 outflow** if you rely on sales to fund the *following* cycle or if you have other operating expenses (rent, wages) not listed.
    *   *Strict Interpretation of Your Question:* You asked if the delayed cash flow drops the balance below $18,000 *within 45 days*.

**3. The Missing Variable: Operating Expenses (OpEx)**
Your prompt lists material costs but omits wages for your two builders and one admin, plus rent/utilities.
*   **Formula:** `Ending Cash = Start Cash ($22,500) + Sales Inflow - Material Cost ($18,000) - OpEx`
*   **The Threshold:** To stay above $18,000, your `Sales Inflow - OpEx` must be greater than $0 over 45 days.
*   **Installment Impact:** If you sell 5 frames:
    *   *All Upfront:* 5 × $2,400 = $12,000 inflow.
    *   *40% Installment:* (3 × $2,400) + (2 × $800) = $7,200 + $1,600 = $8,800 inflow.

**Decision Aid & Sensitivity**
*   **One-Line Read:** Your current reserves ($22,500) cover the next material run ($18,000) with a $4,500 cushion, **provided your non-material operating expenses (wages, rent) for the next 6 weeks do not exceed $4,500.**
*   **The Flip Input:** Your monthly **OpEx**. If your wages and rent exceed ~$2,250 per month, the installment plan's reduced upfront cash flow combined with OpEx could dip you below the $18,000 threshold before the next batch revenue clears.
*   **Rule of Thumb:** If `Cash Cushion ($4,500) < (Monthly OpEx × 1.5 months)`, the timing mismatch is a threat.
*   **Next Step:** Calculate your total fixed monthly burn (wages + rent + utilities). If that number is higher than $3,000/month, consider requiring a larger deposit (e.g., $1,200) for the installment plan to preserve the cushion.
