### Lane A: Financial Impact Analysis (Arithmetic)

We can model the impact of the freight hike on your margins and runway using your provided numbers. We cannot fabricate your total order volume, so we will analyze the impact **per order** and show how it scales to your runway.

**Current Unit Economics (Per Order):**
*   Average Order Value (AOV): $85.00
*   Gross Profit Margin: 22%
*   Gross Profit ($): $85.00 × 0.22 = **$18.70**
*   Cost of Goods Sold (COGS) + Current Freight: $85.00 - $18.70 = $66.30

**The Shock:**
*   Freight Rate Hike: 15%
*   *Assumption:* To calculate the specific dollar impact, we must know what portion of the $66.30 cost base is freight. Since this is unknown, let $F$ be the current freight cost per order.
*   New Cost Increase per Order = $F \times 0.15$.

**Scenario 1: Absorb the Cost**
*   New Gross Profit = $18.70 - (F \times 0.15)$.
*   **Runway Impact:** Your current monthly burn is $12,000 with 6 months cash ($72,000 total). If absorbing the cost turns your net profit negative, your burn rate increases.
*   *Formula:* New Monthly Burn = $12,000 + (Monthly Order Volume \times F \times 0.15)$.
*   *Result:* If this new burn exceeds your contribution margin, runway shrinks. Every $1 of lost margin per order reduces your annual runway by $1 \times$ Volume.

**Scenario 2: Pass the Cost (Price Increase)**
*   Required Price Hike to maintain margin: Increase price by $(F \times 0.15)$.
*   **Churn Risk:** You noted a 3% client loss on a $5 increase last year.
*   *Sensitivity:* If the required hike is less than $5, churn *might* be lower, but loyalty is non-linear. If the hike is >$5, expect >3% churn.
*   *Revenue Math:* If you lose 3% of 400 farms (12 farms), and average order frequency is constant, you lose $12 \times \$85 \times \text{Orders/Year}$.
*   **Decision Aid:** Compare `(Lost Margin from Absorbing)` vs. `(Lost Revenue from Churn)`.
    *   If $(F \times 0.15) \times \text{Total Orders} > \text{Revenue Lost from Churn}$, passing the cost is mathematically safer for cash flow.
    *   If the price hike needed is small (e.g., $1.50), the churn risk may be negligible compared to the margin wipeout.

**Next Step:** Calculate $F$ (your current average freight cost per order). Plug it into: `Break-even Churn % = (Freight Increase $) / (AOV + Freight Increase $)`. If your expected churn is lower than this percentage, pass the cost.

### Lane R: Regulatory Restrictions (Rule & Route)

**Regime:** Vermont Consumer Protection and Agricultural Supply Regulations.

**General Rule:** Generally, private businesses in the US have the right to set prices and change them with notice, unless there is a specific contract in place locking rates for a term. However, agricultural supplies can sometimes be subject to specific state statutes regarding "unfair trade practices" or price gouging during emergencies, though a standard carrier rate hike usually does not qualify as an emergency allowing gouging claims. There is no universal federal ban on sudden price changes for seeds, but Vermont may have specific notification requirements for dealers licensed under the Vermont Seed Law.

**Routing:** You must verify if your dealer license or specific contracts with farms require advance notice periods (e.g., 30 days) before price changes.
*   **Action:** Consult a **Vermont-licensed attorney** specializing in agricultural law or the **Vermont Agency of Agriculture, Food & Markets** directly.
*   **Question to Professional:** "Does Vermont law or my specific dealer license require a mandatory notice period before increasing prices on seed orders, and do any existing contracts with my 400 farms lock current pricing terms?"

Do not implement a price change until confirming the notice period to avoid breach of contract or regulatory penalties.
