### Lane A: Arithmetic & Market Sizing Application

You requested a conservative market size estimate and break-even analysis. Since specific conversion rates for your new service and data on "similar niche products" were not provided, I cannot fabricate these inputs. Instead, I will apply the formulas using your known numbers and label the unknowns as variables you must validate.

**1. Break-Even on Marketing Spend**
To recover your $30,000 launch campaign, you must cover that cost with your contribution margin per unit.
*   **Formula:** `Break-Even Units = Marketing Spend ÷ (Price − Variable Cost)`
*   **Your Inputs:** Price = $22; Production Cost = $9.
*   **Contribution Margin:** $22 − $9 = $13 per unit.
*   **Calculation:** $30,000 ÷ $13 ≈ **2,308 units**.

*Read:* You must sell an additional 2,308 copies solely to pay back the marketing budget, excluding any fixed costs for new infrastructure.

**2. Conservative Market Size Estimate (Bottom-Up)**
Avoiding broad industry averages, we build from your existing assets.
*   **Known Base:** 1,500 annual sales from an 8,000 subscriber list implies a current conversion rate of **18.75%** (1,500 ÷ 8,000) for your flagship title among existing fans.
*   **The Unknown Variable:** The size of the *new* audience (Pacific Northwest niche sci-fi readers preferring print-on-demand).
*   **Application:** If you assume your new service converts at a *conservative* fraction of your existing fanbase rate (e.g., 5% due to colder traffic), and you need to sell 2,308 units to break even on marketing alone:
    *   `Required Addressable Audience = Break-Even Units ÷ Estimated Conversion Rate`
    *   `Required Audience = 2,308 ÷ 0.05` = **46,160 people**.

*Decision Aid:* Does a reachable, specific demographic of ~46,000 print-on-demand-preferring sci-fi fans exist in your target region beyond your current list? If your actual conversion rate is lower (e.g., 2%), the required market jumps to ~115,000.

**3. Infrastructure Justification (Unit Threshold)**
To decide between batch printing and dedicated print-on-demand (POD) infrastructure, you must compare the fixed cost of the new infrastructure against the variable cost savings.
*   **Formula:** `Indifference Point = Fixed Infrastructure Cost ÷ (Batch Unit Cost − POD Unit Cost)`
*   **Missing Input:** You have not provided the cost of the new POD infrastructure or the specific unit cost difference between your current batch method and the proposed POD method.
*   **Rule of Thumb:** Dedicated infrastructure is rarely justified unless volume consistently exceeds the indifference point where savings outweigh the upfront capex. Given your current volume (1,500/year) and the break-even need (2,308 extra), total volume would be ~3,800. Unless the POD unit cost is significantly lower than $9 (unlikely for low-volume POD vs. batch) or the "infrastructure" is purely software with low fixed costs, the math often favors sticking to batch or using third-party POD until volume scales significantly higher.

**Note:** This analysis assumes $9 is the only variable cost; shipping, payment processing, and returns will lower your margin and raise the break-even point.
