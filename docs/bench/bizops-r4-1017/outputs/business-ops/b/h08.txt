**Lane R: Regulation-Dependent Analysis (Explain the Rule, Route the Call)**

Your request to operate an online notary service across all 50 states simultaneously triggers a complex web of state-specific statutes, not a single federal regime. Notary law is exclusively state-level; there is no "national" notary license. Consequently, the "general categories" you requested vary significantly by jurisdiction, and providing a unified estimate for time and cost without verifying each of the 50 state codes would be speculative and potentially harmful.

**The Regulatory Landscape**
To operate legally, you generally must navigate three layers of compliance in *each* state where you intend to offer services:
1.  **Entity & Agent Registration:** You likely need to register your Delaware entity as a "foreign corporation" in every target state and maintain a registered agent there.
2.  **RON (Remote Online Notarization) Approval:** Most states require the *platform* itself to be approved or registered, separate from the individual notaries. This often involves security audits, technology standards compliance (e.g., NIST 800-63-3 for identity proofing), and surety bonds ranging from $10,000 to $100,000+ per state.
3.  **Notary Commissioning:** Your individual notaries must typically hold a commission in the specific state where the signer is located at the time of the act. Some states allow "commission portability" or non-resident commissions; others strictly require the notary to be physically present in the state.

**Restrictive Jurisdictions & Legal Counsel**
You specifically asked which states are most restrictive. Regulations change frequently, and "restrictiveness" can depend on whether you are analyzing technology standards, identity proofing methods (knowledge-based authentication vs. credential analysis), or recording retention rules.
*   **High-Risk/Complex States:** Historically, states like **New York**, **California**, **Florida**, and **Texas** have had rigorous, evolving, or highly specific statutory requirements for RON platforms. Some states have moratoriums or specific bans on certain types of remote notarization for real estate deeds.
*   **The Binding Call:** You **must** engage legal counsel specializing in notary law and fintech compliance before accepting customers in any jurisdiction. Do not rely on general lists. Counsel must perform a "state-by-state applicability analysis" to determine:
    *   Which states currently authorize RON for real estate transactions.
    *   The specific technology standards (audio/video recording, encryption) mandated by each.
    *   The exact bonding and insurance limits required.
    *   Whether your Delaware entity structure is sufficient or if local entities are needed.
