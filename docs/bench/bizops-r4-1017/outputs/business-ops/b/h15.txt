### Profitability Model: Diesel at $4.00/gallon

**1. The Arithmetic (Current vs. Stress Scenario)**
*   **Current Fuel Cost:** $1,200/van/month.
*   **Projected Fuel Cost (+30%):** $1,200 × 1.30 = **$1,560/van/month**.
*   **Monthly Fuel Increase:** $360 per van.
*   **Fleet-Wide Impact (6 vans):** $360 × 6 = **$2,160 total monthly profit reduction**.
*   **Projected Net Profit:** $18,000 (current) − $2,160 = **$15,840/month**.

**2. Volume Required to Offset Costs (Without Price Increase)**
To cover the $360/month increase per van solely through volume:
*   **Current Contribution Margin (excl. fuel):** $85 (price) − $22 (variable cost) = **$63/job**.
*   **Jobs Needed to Cover $360:** $360 ÷ $63 ≈ **5.71 jobs**.
*   **Rounded Requirement:** **6 extra jobs per van, per month**.
*   **Daily Impact:** Assuming ~22 working days/month, this equals **~0.27 extra jobs per day per van**.

**3. Realism Check**
You currently complete 12 jobs/day. Adding 0.27 jobs/day represents a **2.25% volume increase**.
*   *Assessment:* Mathematically, this is highly realistic. It requires finding roughly **one additional appointment every 4 days** per van. Given typical no-show rates or minor scheduling gaps, absorbing this via better route density is feasible without changing your model.

### Decision Aid & Next Steps

**One-Line Read:** A 30% fuel hike reduces fleet profit by 12%, but requires only a ~2.3% volume increase (6 jobs/month/van) to break even if prices stay flat.

**The Flip Input:** The break-even volume is sensitive to your *actual* variable costs. If non-fuel variable costs rise, the required volume increases.
*   *Formula:* `Extra Jobs Needed = (Fuel Cost Increase) ÷ ($85 − $22)`

**Thresholds (Rules of Thumb):**
*   If acquiring 6 extra jobs/month proves operationally impossible (e.g., traffic prevents >12 jobs/day consistently), **then** consider a fuel surcharge.
*   If fuel exceeds $4.50/gallon (another ~12% jump), re-run this math; the volume requirement may then exceed scheduling capacity.

**Strategic Options Analysis:**
1.  **Fuel Surcharge:** Mathematically efficient. A $1.25 surcharge per job (on 12 jobs/day × 22 days = 264 jobs) generates $330, nearly covering the $360 gap. *Decision:* Test on new bookings only.
2.  **Reduce Radius:** Lowers fuel cost but risks reducing total addressable market (TAM). Only pursue if route density is currently low.
3.  **Hub-and-Spoke:** High fixed cost (lease) and operational complexity. Likely over-correction for a $360/month/van variance.

**Recommended Next Step:**
If your scheduling software shows >90% utilization between 9 AM–4 PM, **consider** adding a "fuel stability" surcharge of $1.50 to new appointments immediately. If utilization is <85%, **consider** targeting 1 extra job per day per van before changing pricing, as the math shows volume is the cheaper fix right now.
