### Lane A: Unit Economics & Pricing Sensitivity Analysis

You are facing a classic margin-vs-volume trade-off. Since you do not have historical data on your specific backers' price elasticity (how much demand drops when price rises), we cannot calculate the exact break-even conversion rate. Instead, we will apply the arithmetic to your inputs to show the profit outcomes and the "flip point" where the higher price becomes better than the lower price.

**Assumptions & Inputs:**
*   **Units (Base):** 2,000
*   **Shipping Cost:** $8/unit (passed through or included in tier; treated here as a cost against the pledge).
*   **Scenario A (Absorb Cost):** Price $45 | COGS $14.50 | Shipping $8.
*   **Scenario B (Raise Price):** Price $52 | COGS $14.50 | Shipping $8.
*   **Unknown Variable:** The drop in unit sales ($Q_{drop}$) caused by the $7 price increase.

#### 1. Contribution Margin Per Unit
Formula: $Margin = Price - (COGS + Shipping)$

*   **Scenario A ($45):** $45 - ($14.50 + $8) = **$22.50 per unit**
*   **Scenario B ($52):** $52 - ($14.50 + $8) = **$29.50 per unit**

Raising the price increases your margin per unit by **$7.00**.

#### 2. Total Net Profit Projection (at 2,000 units)
Formula: $Total Profit = Margin \times Units$

*   **Scenario A:** $22.50 \times 2,000 = **$45,000**
*   **Scenario B:** $29.50 \times 2,000 = **$59,000**

*Initial Read:* If you sell the exact same number of units, raising the price yields **$14,000 more profit**.

#### 3. The Sensitivity Analysis (The "Flip Point")
The risk is that the $7 increase causes backers to leave. We need to find the maximum number of units you can lose in Scenario B before it becomes worse than Scenario A.

Formula for Break-Even Volume on Price Increase:
$Volume_{B} = \frac{Profit_{A}}{Margin_{B}}$

$Volume_{B} = \frac{45,000}{29.50} \approx 1,526 \text{ units}$

**Analysis:**
*   You can afford to lose **474 units** (2,000 - 1,526) and still make the same total profit as the lower price.
*   This represents a **23.7% drop in conversion**.
*   **Decision Rule:** If you believe the price hike will cause fewer than ~24% of your potential backers to drop out, raising the price to $52 is the mathematically superior move for total profit. If you expect a drop greater than 24%, absorbing the cost (or cutting marketing) preserves more total cash.

#### 4. Decision Aid & Next Steps
*   **One-line read:** Raising the price creates a ~24% safety buffer against lost sales before it hurts your total bottom line compared to absorbing the cost.
*   **The Flip Input:** Your actual **conversion rate elasticity**. If >24% of backers bounce, do not raise the price.
*   **Threshold:** Standard Kickstarter data often shows elasticities between 10-20% for modest increases, but niche strategy games can be more sensitive.
*   **Next Step (This Week):** Run a **Van Westendorp** price sensitivity survey on your email list or social channels before launching. Ask: "At what price would this game be so expensive you wouldn't consider it?" and "At what price would it be a bargain?" Use the resulting acceptable price band to validate if $52 falls within your backers' willingness to pay. Do not guess this number; instrument it.

*Note: Absorbing the cost reduces your margin to $22.50, which may limit future marketing spend or stretch goals; ensure your $45k projected profit covers all fixed overheads.*
*Note: Shipping costs often fluctuate; if shipping rises above $8, recalculate the margin immediately using the formulas above.*
