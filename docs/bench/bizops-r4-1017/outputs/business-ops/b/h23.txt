### Lane R: Tax Treatment (The Regulatory Question)
You asked whether the $45,000 is taxable revenue or restricted capital. This is a **Lane R** question because the answer depends on Canadian tax law (CRA guidelines) and Ontario regulations regarding your entity type.
*   **The Rule:** Generally, if you are a registered charity, certain grants may be non-taxable. If you are a taxable non-profit or a commercial entity, grants for operating purposes or specific projects may be included in income unless specific deferral rules apply. "Restricted" accounting treatment (keeping it off the P&L until spent) does not automatically equal "non-taxable" for CRA purposes.
*   **The Route:** Do not set aside taxes or file returns based on an assumption. Bring the grant agreement and your articles of incorporation to a CPA. Ask: "Does this specific government grant constitute taxable income in the year received, or can it be deferred under Section 12(1)(n) or relevant charity rules?"

**Scenario 2: Grant is Included in General Runway (Unrestricted/Deferrable)**

*   **Available Cash:** $12,000 + $45,000 = $57,000
*   **Formula:** `Runway = Total Cash ÷ Monthly Burn`
