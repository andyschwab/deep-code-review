### Lane A: Pricing Mechanism Arithmetic
To protect your 12% net margin against 20% monthly raw material swings without abandoning long-term clients, you should apply a **Cost-Plus with Floating Surcharge** model rather than a static price.

**The Formula:**
`Final Price = Base Price + (Raw Material Index × Volatility Factor)`

**Application to Your Numbers:**
1.  **Current Cost Structure:**
    *   Selling Price: $800
    *   Net Margin (12%): $96
    *   Total Cost of Goods Sold (COGS): $704
    *   Raw Material Cost (60% of COGS): $422.40
    *   Other Costs (Labor, Overhead, etc.): $281.60

2.  **The Risk:**
    *   A 10% spike in materials adds ~$42.24 to costs.
    *   Your profit is only $96. A 20% spike adds ~$84.48, reducing profit to ~$11.52 (effectively erasing profitability as you noted).

3.  **Proposed Mechanism (The Surcharge):**
    Instead of changing the base $800 price (which upsets clients), keep the base fixed and add a monthly **Raw Material Adjustment (RMA)** line item.
    *   **Base Price:** $800 (covers stable costs + target margin).
    *   **RMA Formula:** `(Current Month Material Cost Index - Base Material Cost Index) × Volume Factor`.
    *   **Simplified Execution:** Define a specific commodity index (e.g., petrochemical spot price) as your "Base." If that index moves >5% from the baseline, the surcharge activates.
    *   **Example Calculation:** If material costs rise 20% ($422.40 → $506.88), the surcharge passed to the client is exactly the difference: **$84.48 per drum**.
    *   **New Invoice:** $800 (Base) + $84.48 (Surcharge) = $884.48.
    *   **Result:** Your margin remains intact at $96.

**Decision Aid:**
*   **Read:** At 20% volatility, static pricing guarantees margin erosion; a surcharge model transfers only the variable risk.
*   **Flip Input:** The threshold where this becomes critical is any material cost increase >11.4% (which would wipe out your entire $96 profit).
*   **Rule of Thumb:** If LTV relies on margin stability, fix the base, float the variable.
*   **Next Step:** Draft a contract addendum defining the specific public index (e.g., "Platts USGC Petrochemical Index") you will use to calculate the surcharge, then present it as a "transparency measure" to clients rather than a price hike.

### Lane R: Legal Regime and Routing
**Regime:** Texas Business & Commerce Code (Uniform Commercial Code adoption) and general contract law regarding price adjustment clauses.

**General Rule:** In B2B transactions, parties generally have freedom of contract to agree on future price adjustments based on objective criteria (like a market index). However, clauses that allow one party to set prices arbitrarily or without notice may be challenged as unconscionable or lacking "mutuality of obligation." To be enforceable, the mechanism must be **definite** (tied to a verifiable external index) and **noticeable** (clear terms on when and how the adjustment applies).

**Routing:**
You must route the drafting and review of these specific clauses to a **Texas-licensed commercial attorney**.
*   **Why:** An attorney must verify that your specific wording complies with Texas UCC § 2.305 (Open Price Term) and ensures the clause survives potential "commercial impracticability" defenses if prices drop sharply later. They will also ensure the notice period satisfies your existing contract terms.
*   **Bring to Counsel:** Your current master service agreements, the specific commodity index you intend to use, and a draft of the proposed "Raw Material Adjustment" language.
*   **Time-Sensitive:** Do not implement the surcharge on existing contracts until counsel confirms the modification process (often requiring new signatures or specific notice windows) is valid.
