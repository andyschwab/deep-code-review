### Lane A: Unit Economics & Break-Even Analysis

Here is the arithmetic applied to your specific numbers to model the impact of increased churn.

**1. Current State (Year 1)**
*   **Revenue:** 600 subscribers × $49 = $29,400/month.
*   **Variable Costs:** Assumed 4 washes/month × $12 = $48/subscriber. Total = $28,800.
*   **Contribution Margin:** $29,400 - $28,800 = $600/month.
*   **Fixed Overhead:** $25,000/month.
*   **Result:** With current assumptions, you are operating at a significant loss ($24,400/month), not breaking even. *Note: To break even with $25k fixed costs, your contribution margin per user must be ~$41.67. At $49 revenue, your variable cost per user must be ≤ $7.33, or users must average <1.5 washes/month.*

**2. Scenario: 20% Annual Churn (Year 2)**
*   **Churn Rate:** 20% annually ≈ 1.83% monthly churn.
*   **Subscribers Lost/Month:** 600 × 1.83% ≈ 11 customers.
*   **Acquisition Needed to Maintain Revenue:** You must acquire **11 new customers/month** just to stay flat at 600 subscribers.
*   **Monthly CAC Spend:** 11 × $35 = $385/month.
*   **Impact on Profitability:** This $385 is an additional variable cost. If your base unit economics are already negative (as shown above), this accelerates the burn.

**3. The Collapse Threshold (Break-Even Formula)**
To find where economics collapse, solve for the maximum allowable CAC or Churn where Contribution Margin covers Fixed Costs + Acquisition Costs.

*   **Formula:** `Max Sustainable CAC = (Price - Variable Cost per User) - (Fixed Overhead / Total Subscribers)`
*   **Application:** If we assume you *are* breaking even currently (implying your actual variable cost is lower or wash frequency is lower than estimated), let $M$ be your current margin per user.
*   **Break-Even Subscriber Count:** `N = Fixed Overhead / (Price - VarCost - (ChurnRate × CAC))`
