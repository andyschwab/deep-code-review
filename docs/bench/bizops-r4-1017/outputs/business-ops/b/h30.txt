**Hypothetical State (Hourly Model)**

*   *Assumption:* You charge clients **$150/hour** (2.5x your $60 cost).
*   **Base Case (No behavior change, 2.5 hrs avg):**
    *   Revenue: 400 cases × 2.5 hrs × $150 = **$150,000/month**.
    *   Cost: $60,000/month.
    *   Gross Margin: **$90,000/month** (60% margin).
*   **Behavioral Sensitivity (The Unknown):**
    *   If hourly billing causes case volume to drop by **40%** (to 240 cases) due to friction:
        *   Revenue: 240 × 2.5 × $150 = $90,000.
        *   Cost: 240 × 2.5 × $60 = $36,000.
        *   Margin: **$54,000**. (Still higher than the current $20k).
    *   If volume drops by **75%** (to 100 cases):
        *   Margin: $22,500. (Break-even with current model).

**Decision Aid**
1.  **Read:** The flat fee model caps your upside and creates unlimited downside risk on long cases. The hourly model protects margins on complex disputes but introduces volume risk.
2.  **The Flip Input:** The model flips if hourly pricing reduces total case volume by more than **75%**. Unless you believe clients will reject hourly billing at that magnitude, the arithmetic favors hourly for margin maximization.
3.  **Threshold Rule:** If LTV (revenue per case) under hourly pricing < $200, revert to flat fee or hybrid. Currently, at 2.5 hours, LTV is $375 (assumed rate).
4.  **Next Step:** Do not switch 100% immediately. Run a "Good-Better-Best" test next week: Offer a **$200 flat fee for "Standard" disputes** (capped at 2 hours) and an **hourly rate for "Complex" disputes**. This captures the volume of simple cases while protecting margins on the 4–5 hour outliers.
