### Lane A: Unit Economics & Pricing Arithmetic

**1. The Math: Revised Break-Even Analysis**
To find your new break-even price, we must recalculate your monthly costs using the accelerated depreciation and your target margin.

*   **Current Asset Base:** 4 drones × £3,000 = £12,000.
*   **Revised Depreciation Schedule:** You are shifting from a 4-year life to a 2-year life due to heavy use.
    *   Annual Depreciation Expense = £12,000 ÷ 2 years = £6,000/year.
    *   Monthly Depreciation Expense = £6,000 ÷ 12 = **£500/month**.
    *   *(Note: Under your old 4-year model, this was only £250/month. Your fixed costs have effectively risen by £250/month).*
*   **Total Monthly Fixed Costs:**
    *   Operations (Insurance/Wages): £8,500
    *   Revised Depreciation: £500
    *   **Total:** £9,000/month.
*   **Volume:** 15 weddings/month.
*   **Cost Per Wedding (Break-Even at 0% margin):**
    *   £9,000 ÷ 15 = **£600 per wedding**.

**2. Applying the Target Margin**
You stated a target **25% net margin**. In pricing arithmetic, margin is typically calculated on the *selling price* (Revenue), not cost.
*   **Formula:** `Price = Cost Per Unit ÷ (1 - Target Margin %)`
*   **Calculation:** £600 ÷ (1 - 0.25) = £600 ÷ 0.75 = **£800**.

**Result:** To cover your accelerated depreciation and achieve a 25% net margin, your new break-even price is **£800 per wedding**.

**3. Sensitivity & Decision Aid**
*   **Current Situation:** You are charging £1,200.
*   **Analysis:** Even with the equipment life halved, your required price (£800) remains significantly below your current price (£1,200).
    *   Current Profit per Wedding: £1,200 - £600 = £600.
    *   Current Net Margin: £600 ÷ £1,200 = **50%**.
*   **The Flip Input:** The variable that most drastically changes this outcome is **volume**. If bookings drop below 10 weddings/month, your cost per unit rises to £900, requiring a price of £1,200 just to hit your 25% margin target.

**Recommendation Structure (Apply, Don't Direct):**
Since your current price (£1,200) already exceeds the recalculated requirement (£800) by a wide margin, you do not *mathematically* need to raise prices to cover depreciation alone.
*   **Option A (Maintain Price):** Keep £1,200. This builds a "replacement reserve" automatically. At 15 weddings, you generate £9,000 in monthly net profit, easily funding future drone purchases without a surcharge.
*   **Option B (Usage-Based Model):** If you fear drone failure mid-contract, consider an "Equipment Assurance" line item (e.g., £50) explicitly marketed as guaranteeing backup gear availability, rather than raising the base rate.
*   **Option C (Volume Protection):** If you reduce shoots per drone to extend their life, your volume drops. If volume falls to 12 weddings, your break-even price jumps to £1,000. Ensure any reduction in utilization is offset by efficiency gains elsewhere.

**Next Step:** Instrument your actual repair/replacement costs over the next 90 days. If actual cash spend on repairs exceeds the £500/month depreciation allowance, *then* consider adjusting the price. Currently, your pricing power appears sufficient to absorb the risk.

*Note: This calculation assumes straight-line depreciation for simplicity; consult a CPA for tax-specific capital allowance treatments in the UK.*
*Note: "Net margin" definitions vary; ensure your £8,500 operational cost figure includes all variable costs (fuel, travel) to avoid underpricing.*
