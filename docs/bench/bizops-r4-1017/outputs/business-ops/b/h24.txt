### Lane A: Arithmetic & Structural Analysis

**1. Current Liability Baseline (6-Month Window)**
*   **Volume:** 120 repairs/month × 6 months = 720 active repairs in any rolling window.
*   **Follow-up Frequency:** 3% of 720 = ~22 incidents/rolling period.
*   **Cost Assumption:** You distinguish between standard follow-ups (likely labor-only, low cost) and "rare part replacements" (up to 2,000 CHF).
    *   *Unknown Input:* The split between standard vs. rare failures.
    *   *Worst-Case Scenario:* If all 22 incidents were rare part failures (highly unlikely but mathematically possible): 22 × 2,000 CHF = 44,000 CHF.
    *   *Current Reserve:* 40,000 CHF.
    *   *Observation:* Your current reserve barely covers a worst-case 6-month scenario, leaving <25k liquid if a cluster of rare failures occurs.

**2. Projected 1-Year Warranty Exposure**
Extending to 12 months doubles the active window.
*   **Volume:** 120 repairs/month × 12 months = 1,440 active repairs.
*   **Follow-up Frequency:** 3% of 1,440 = ~43 incidents.
*   **Reserve Requirement per Job (Targeting Safety):**
    To maintain 25,000 CHF liquid from your 40,000 CHF total, you can allocate only **15,000 CHF** to the warranty reserve.
    *   *Max Allowable Reserve per Job:* 15,000 CHF ÷ 1,440 jobs = **~10.42 CHF per repair**.
    *   *Risk Check:* If a single rare part failure costs 2,000 CHF, a reserve of 10.42 CHF/job requires a failure rate of less than 0.5% (1 in 200) to remain solvent within that 15k bucket. Your historical rate is 3% (1 in 33).
    *   *Arithmetic Conclusion:* Based on current unknowns, a flat reserve model funded by existing cash **cannot** support a 1-year comprehensive warranty while maintaining 25k liquidity, unless the "rare part" frequency is significantly lower than the general 3% follow-up rate.

**3. Tiered Model vs. Flat Fee Strategy**
Given the high variance in cost (labor vs. 2,000 CHF parts), a flat fee/comprehensive model concentrates risk on your balance sheet. A tiered model shifts specific risks.

*   **Option A: Comprehensive (Flat)**
    *   *Structure:* One price covers labor and parts for 1 year.
    *   *Math Implication:* You must reserve for the 2,000 CHF tail risk on every unit. As shown above, this threatens your 25k liquidity floor unless you raise prices significantly to build a *new* reserve fund rather than using existing cash.
*   **Option B: Tiered (Recommended for Capital Efficiency)**
    *   *Tier 1 (Workmanship):* 12-month guarantee on labor only. Cost to you: Minimal (master watchmaker salary is fixed). Reserve needed: Low.
    *   *Tier 2 (Parts):* 6-month guarantee on standard parts; 12-month guarantee available for an up-front premium or specific high-value components only.
    *   *Arithmetic Benefit:* This isolates the 2,000 CHF risk. If rare parts historically fail at <0.5%, you can self-insure Tier 2. If they fail at 3%, the tiered approach allows you to exclude them from the base warranty or price the premium specifically to fund that reserve (e.g., add 60 CHF to the bill to cover a 2% chance of 2,000 CHF loss).

**Decision Aid**
*   **Read:** Your current liquidity constraints mathematically preclude a comprehensive 1-year parts warranty without new capital accumulation.
*   **Flip Input:** The specific frequency of "rare part failures" (distinct from general follow-ups). If this is <0.5%, a flat model works; if >1%, a tiered model is required.
*   **Threshold:** If (Probability of Rare Failure × 2,000 CHF) > 15 CHF, you cannot fund the reserve from existing cash while keeping 25k liquid.
*   **Next Step:** Instrument your next 50 repairs to explicitly tag "rare part replacement" vs. "adjustment." Use that specific rate to calculate the exact premium needed for a parts warranty.

### Lane R: Regulatory & Compliance Note

**Regime:** Swiss Code of Obligations (Consumer Warranty/Garantie) and Insurance Supervision.
**Rule:** Offering a "comprehensive warranty" that covers random part failures for a fee may legally constitute an **insurance contract** rather than a standard merchant guarantee, depending on how risk is pooled and priced.
**Route:** Before launching, consult a Swiss attorney specializing in commercial law or insurance regulation to determine if your warranty structure requires an insurance license or must be underwritten by a licensed insurer. Do not structure the pricing until this classification is confirmed.
