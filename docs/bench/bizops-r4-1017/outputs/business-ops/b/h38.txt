### Lane A: Pricing Arithmetic & Structure Application

Your current model exposes you to direct commodity risk. With 0.5 oz of gold per ring, a $50/oz swing equals a **$25 variance per unit**. Since your fixed labor fee is $400, a $25 loss represents **6.25% of your total margin** on that job. If gold spikes $100 (possible in volatile weeks), you lose $50, eroding 12.5% of your margin.

You asked to compare three structural options. Here is the arithmetic application for each based on your inputs:

**1. Quote Validity Period (Time-Boxing)**
*   **Mechanism:** The quoted price is locked only for $X$ hours/days.
*   **Arithmetic:** If gold moves >$25/oz (your break-even risk threshold) within your production window, the quote expires.
*   **Application:** Set validity to **24–48 hours**. This forces a decision before typical weekly swings ($50) can fully materialize against you.
*   **Trade-off:** Low friction for customers, but requires you to re-calculate and re-send quotes frequently if clients hesitate.

**2. Non-Refundable Deposit Tied to Metal Weight (Hedging)**
*   **Mechanism:** Customer pays the estimated metal cost upfront; you purchase the metal immediately upon deposit.
*   **Arithmetic:**
    *   Current Spot: $S$
    *   Deposit Required: $0.5 \times S$
    *   Final Bill: $(0.5 \times S_{final}) + \$400 - \text{Deposit}$
    *   *Result:* You hold the physical metal (or cash equivalent) before price risk accrues. Your $400 labor fee is mathematically isolated from spot price.
*   **Application:** Require a **~50% deposit** (covering metal) to commence work.
*   **Trade-off:** Eliminates 100% of your material margin risk. Highest customer friction (upfront cost), but standard for bespoke luxury goods.

**3. Volatility Buffer (Price Padding)**
*   **Mechanism:** Increase the base price to absorb worst-case swings.
*   **Arithmetic:**
    *   Max observed weekly swing: $50/oz $\rightarrow$ $25/ring.
    *   To be safe against a 2-sigma event (e.g., $100 swing), buffer = $50/ring.
    *   New Labor Fee: $\$400 + \$50 = \$450$.
*   **Application:** Raise fixed fee to **$450–$480**.
*   **Trade-off:** Simplest for the customer (one price). However, you effectively self-insure; if gold stays flat, you keep the extra $50 as profit. If gold spikes $150, you still lose money. This does not *guarantee* you never lose money; it only reduces the probability.

### Decision Aid & Next Step

**Read:** The **Deposit Tied to Metal Weight** is the only option that mathematically guarantees you "never lose money" on material swings, as it transfers the timing risk to the client in exchange for price certainty.

**The Flip Input:** Your risk tolerance. If you cannot absorb a $25–$50 loss per ring, the buffer method fails.

**Threshold Rule:** If your goal is **zero material loss**, the deposit must cover 100% of the fluctuating input cost (the gold). If your goal is **simplicity**, accept a buffer equal to 2x the average weekly swing ($50 added to fee).

**Recommended Next Step (This Week):**
Draft a "Material Lock" clause: *"A non-refundable deposit equal to the current spot value of the required gold (approx. 50% of total estimate) secures your metal price. Production begins upon receipt. The remaining balance (labor + final metal adjustment if weight varies) is due on delivery."* Test this on your next 5 inquiries. If conversion drops >20%, revert to a 48-hour quote validity period with a built-in $30 buffer.

**Note:** Standard practice for bespoke jewelers handling volatile commodities is typically the deposit model (Option 2). Ensure your deposit terms are clearly written in your service agreement.
