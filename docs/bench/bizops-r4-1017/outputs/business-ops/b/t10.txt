### Lane R: Regulation-Dependent Analysis (VAT Compliance)

**The Regime:** You are operating under the **EU VAT Directive** and specific national laws (e.g., Germany's UStG, France's CGI). Because ScholarStream supplies "electronically supplied services" (virtual event access) to consumers (B2C) in the EU, the place of supply is generally the customer's location. This triggers an obligation to charge, collect, and remit VAT at the local rate of each attendee's country.

**The Rule & The Risk:**
1.  **Registration Thresholds:** For non-EU businesses selling digital services to EU consumers, the previous €10,000 cross-border threshold often does not apply in the same way as for EU-based sellers, or requires immediate registration via the **OSS (One Stop Shop)** scheme if you exceed the distance-selling threshold in any single member state. However, many jurisdictions require **immediate registration** upon the first taxable supply if you do not qualify for specific small-business exemptions, which vary by country.
2.  **Liability:** If you fail to collect VAT at the point of sale, **ScholarStream remains liable** for the uncollected tax plus penalties and interest. You cannot legally pass this cost to the customer after the fact without their consent, meaning it comes out of your revenue.
3.  **Penalties:** Penalties for failure to register and collect can range from 10% to 100% of the tax due, depending on whether the authority deems the omission "negligent" or "deliberate." Interest accrues daily.

**Illustrative Liability Calculation (UNVERIFIED rates):**
*Assumption:* 3,000 attendees globally. Let us assume conservatively that 1,000 are from high-VAT EU countries (e.g., Germany @ 19%, France @ 20%).
*   **Gross Ticket Revenue (EU portion):** 1,000 attendees × $150 = $150,000.
*   **Estimated VAT Embedded (if not collected):**
    *   Germany (approx. 400 attendees @ 19%): $60,000 / 1.19 × 0.19 ≈ **$9,580**.
    *   France (approx. 400 attendees @ 20%): $60,000 / 1.20 × 0.20 = **$10,000**.
    *   Other EU (approx. 200 attendees @ avg 21%): $30,000 / 1.21 × 0.21 ≈ **$5,200**.
*   **Total Potential Immediate Liability:** ~**$24,780** in unpaid tax, excluding penalties.
*   **Your Net Revenue Impact:** Your 10% fee on $150,000 is $15,000. The uncollected VAT ($24,780) **exceeds your entire gross profit** from these tickets. You would owe more in back-taxes than you earned.

**Routing the Binding Call:**
You must immediately engage a **EU VAT specialist** to determine:
1.  Whether your specific volume triggers immediate registration in Germany, France, or other key nations.
2.  Whether you qualify for the **OSS (One Stop Shop)** scheme to report all EU VAT in a single return, or if local registrations are required.
3.  The exact penalty exposure for selling without collection prior to registration.

**Decision Aid:**
*   **Do not delay sales solely for manual calculation;** manual post-event collection from thousands of international consumers is operationally nearly impossible and rarely accepted by tax authorities as a valid mitigation for failing to collect at source.
*   **Immediate Action:** The risk of liability exceeding your revenue suggests you should **integrate an automated tax engine** (e.g., Quaderno, TaxJar, Stripe Tax) before selling further tickets. These tools automatically determine the customer's location, apply the correct rate, and handle the OSS reporting logic.
*   **Rule of Thumb:** If potential back-tax > 50% of your projected margin on the transaction, automation is not optional; it is a survival requirement.
