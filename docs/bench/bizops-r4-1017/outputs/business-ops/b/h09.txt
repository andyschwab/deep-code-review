### Lane A: Financial Modeling (Arithmetic)

**Current State (Monthly)**
*   **Gross Merchandise Volume (GMV):** $2,000,000
*   **Luxury GMV (> $500):** 10% of volume = $200,000
*   **Standard GMV (≤ $500):** 90% of volume = $1,800,000
*   **Current Revenue:** ($200k × 15%) + ($1.8M × 15%) = $30k + $270k = **$300,000**
*   **Fixed Costs:** $60,000
*   **Current Contribution Margin:** $240,000 (before variable support costs)

**Proposed Tiered Structure**
*   **New Luxury Rate:** 25%
*   **Volume Impact:** 15% reduction in luxury listing volume (assumed to correlate directly to GMV).
*   **Adjusted Luxury GMV:** $200,000 × (1 - 0.15) = $170,000
*   **Standard GMV:** Unchanged at $1,800,000

**Projected Outcome**
*   **New Luxury Revenue:** $170,000 × 25% = $42,500
*   **New Standard Revenue:** $1,800,000 × 15% = $270,000
*   **Total New Revenue:** $312,500
*   **Net Change:** +$12,500 monthly revenue (+4.2%)

**Decision Aid**
The model shows a **$12,500 monthly revenue increase**, but this assumes the 15% volume drop is the only negative effect.
1.  **Formula:** `Net Gain = (New Luxury GMV × 25%) + (Standard GMV × 15%) - Current Revenue`.
2.  **Sensitivity:** If luxury volume drops by **22% or more**, the revenue gain disappears (break-even point).
3.  **Hidden Variable:** You noted luxury items drive 30% of support tickets. If the volume drop reduces support load proportionally, your *net* profit increase will exceed $12,500. If support costs are fixed salaries, the savings may be negligible.
4.  **Next Step:** Instrument your support ticket system this week to calculate the exact cost-per-ticket for luxury vs. standard items. If the cost-to-serve luxury items exceeds the extra 10% commission captured, the tiered model is financially sound even with higher volume churn.

### Lane R: Regulatory Context (Routing Required)

**Regime:** Oregon Unlawful Trade Practices Act (UTPA) and general contract law regarding unconscionability or discrimination in public accommodations.

**General Rule:** While businesses generally have freedom to set pricing tiers based on objective criteria (like item value), disparate fee structures can face scrutiny if they are deemed deceptive, unfairly discriminatory, or if the "luxury" definition is applied inconsistently to the detriment of consumers. Specific Oregon statutes may address "price discrimination" or require clear disclosure of fee differences before a transaction occurs.

**Routing:** You must consult an Oregon-licensed consumer protection attorney before implementing this.
*   **Bring to counsel:** Your definition of "luxury" (is it seller-declared or platform-verified?), your terms of service regarding fee changes, and the data showing the correlation between price and support disputes.
*   **Ask:** "Does a value-based commission tier for resale marketplaces trigger specific disclosure requirements or constitute an unlawful trade practice under current Oregon UTPA interpretations?"

**Note:** The 15% volume reduction assumption is unverified; consider A/B testing the fee on a small segment of new luxury listings first.
**Note:** Support cost savings are excluded from the math above as specific cost-per-ticket data was not provided.
