### Common Distribution Structures for Film Funds

In film financing with asynchronous, territory-by-territory revenue, the industry standard to ensure fairness without hoarding cash is a **Deal-by-Deal Waterfall with a Clawback Provision**, often paired with a **Whole-Fund Hurdle** for the final carry.

**1. The Mechanism: Deal-by-Deal with Clawback**
Instead of waiting for all three films to return capital (Whole-Fund), you distribute profits as each specific film generates revenue.
*   **How it works:** When Film A sells UK rights for £150k, that cash flows immediately to investors according to the waterfall (e.g., 100% to investors until their pro-rata share of *that film's* budget is returned, then a split between investors and the fund manager).
*   **The Fairness Fix:** Because Film B might take two years to pay out, early investors in Film A get paid sooner. To prevent the manager from taking "carry" (performance fees) on Film A if Film B later fails, a **Clawback Provision** is legally required. This mandates that if the fund as a whole has not returned total capital (or a preferred return) by the end of the fund's life, the manager must return previously distributed carry.
*   **Arithmetic Application:** You do not need to hold idle cash. You distribute freely but track a "hypothetical" whole-fund balance. If `Total Distributed Carry > Carry Allowed on Actual Whole-Fund Returns`, the difference is owed back by the manager.

**2. Hurdle Rates vs. Preferred Return**
*   **Preferred Return (Pref):** A fixed percentage (e.g., 8% annually) paid to investors before the manager participates in profits. In lumpy cash-flow scenarios, a **cumulative, compounding pref** is common. It accrues on unreturned capital regardless of when cash arrives.
*   **Hurdle Rate:** Often used interchangeably with pref, but technically can be a "hard hurdle" (manager only participates on profits *above* the hurdle) or "soft hurdle" (manager catches up once the hurdle is met).
*   **Application to Your Model:** Given your irregular inflows, a **cumulative preferred return** protects early capital by increasing the amount owed to investors the longer their money is deployed. If Film B takes two years, the pref accrues, ensuring those investors receive a larger share of the eventual £300k to compensate for the time value of money.

**3. Addressing the "Early vs. Late" Investor Concern**
If all ten angels invested simultaneously into the £2m vehicle, they are typically treated as a single class. They share pro-rata in *every* distribution the fund makes, regardless of which film generated the cash.
*   **Scenario:** Fund receives £150k from Film A.
*   **Distribution:** Each of the 10 investors receives 10% of the distributable portion (after recouping that specific film's costs, depending on your specific waterfall tier).
*   **Result:** No investor is "paid back faster" than another; they all receive their proportional slice of every asynchronous event. The inequality only arises if investors entered at different times (vintages), which requires separate share classes or interest adjustments—rare for a single £2m vehicle.

**4. Tax and Legal Routing (Lane R)**
You specifically asked about avoiding complex tax events for UK investors.
*   **Regime:** UK Tax Law (HMRC) and Financial Conduct Authority (FCA) regulations regarding Collective Investment Schemes.
*   **Risk:** Improperly structured waterfalls or "recycling" of capital can trigger unexpected Income Tax vs. Capital Gains Tax treatments, or VAT liabilities on management fees.
*   **Action:** You must route the drafting of the Limited Partnership Agreement (LPA) and the specific waterfall logic to a **UK media-finance solicitor**. They will determine if your structure qualifies for Enterprise Investment Scheme (EIS) or Seed EIS tax reliefs, which heavily dictate distribution timing and forms. Do not implement a clawback or hurdle without verifying the tax treatment of the "return" of capital versus "income" distribution with a CPA.

**Decision Aid:**
*   **Structure:** Adopt a Deal-by-Deal waterfall with a cumulative preferred return and a mandatory clawback.
*   **Next Step:** Engage counsel to draft the LPA with these specific mechanics; do not rely on template waterfalls from other industries as film recoupment hierarchies (recoupment of negative costs vs. net profits) are unique.
