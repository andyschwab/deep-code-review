### Lane R: Regulatory Boundary (Tax Rates & Reciprocity)
Your request to calculate a specific "total excise tax liability" and determine "reciprocity agreements" falls into **Lane R**. The correct answer depends on specific state laws (Oregon, Washington, Idaho, Nevada), current statutory rates per barrel, and complex interstate compacts that determine if taxes paid in the production state (Oregon) credit against taxes owed in the destination state.

*   **The Rule:** Alcohol excise tax is typically levied by the state where the beer is *consumed* (destination-based), though some states have unique reciprocity or credit mechanisms for beer produced in-state and shipped out, or vice versa. There is no universal "reciprocity agreement" automatically applying across these four states; each pair (OR→WA, OR→ID, OR→NV) operates under distinct statutes.
*   **The Route:** You must engage a tax professional specializing in alcohol beverage control (ABC) and multistate excise tax. They will determine:
    1.  The exact excise tax rate per barrel for craft brewers in WA, ID, and NV.
    2.  Whether Oregon taxes apply to these shipments or if they are exempt zero-rated exports.
    3.  Whether any credit is available for taxes paid elsewhere.
    4.  The registration requirements (bonding, licensing) to legally ship into these states.

**Actionable Prep for Your Professional:** Before your meeting, gather your federal Brewer's Notice, current Oregon license details, and the specific volume (50 barrels/state). Ask specifically: "What is the net excise tax per barrel in WA, ID, and NV after applying any available credits for Oregon-produced beer, and what are the registration costs?"
