### Market Sizing Model: CityHive (Northeast US)

To create a realistic, conservative estimate that accounts for grant overlap and budget constraints, we must move from a "Total Available" count to a "Serviceable Obtainable" count using a funnel approach. Since specific grant allocation percentages are unknown, we will treat them as variables you must validate.

#### 1. The Formula
The core arithmetic for your Two-Year Serviceable Addressable Market (SAM) in units (contracts) is:

$$ \text{SAM}_{\text{units}} = (\text{Total Muni} \times \text{Interest Rate} \times \text{Budget Allocation Factor} \times \text{Conversion Rate}) \times \text{Time Phasing} $$

#### 2. Applying Your Inputs & Identifying Unknowns
*   **Total Municipalities:** 450
*   **Expressed Interest:** 30% ($450 \times 0.30 = 135$ municipalities)
*   **Price per Contract:** $2,500 (Installation + Annual Maintenance)
*   **Unknown Variable A (Budget Allocation Factor):** The percentage of interested municipalities where pollinator programs are a funded line item, not just a general "green" idea.
*   **Unknown Variable B (Conversion Rate):** The percentage of funded opportunities you actually win (competitive bidding).
*   **Unknown Variable C (Time Phasing):** The percentage of the 2-year pipeline that closes in Year 1 vs. Year 2.

#### 3. Scenario Analysis (Conservative vs. Optimistic)
Since you cannot fabricate the budget allocation, we model two scenarios based on assumptions you must test.

**Scenario A: Conservative (High Grant Overlap)**
*Assumption:* Only 20% of "interested" municipalities have specific pollinator budget lines available immediately.
*Assumption:* 50% win rate on available budgets.
*Assumption:* 40% of deals close in Year 1, 60% in Year 2 (sales cycles are long).

*   **Qualified Leads:** $135 \times 0.20 = 27$ municipalities with actual budget.
*   **Winning Contracts (2 Years):** $27 \times 0.50 = 13.5$ (round to 13 contracts).
*   **Revenue Impact:** $13 \times \$2,500 = \$32,500$ over two years.
*   **Year 1 Cash Flow:** $13 \times 40\% \approx 5$ contracts ($\$12,500$).

**Scenario B: Moderate (Medium Grant Overlap)**
*Assumption:* 40% of "interested" municipalities can carve out specific funding.
*Assumption:* 50% win rate.
*Assumption:* Same time phasing.

*   **Qualified Leads:** $135 \times 0.40 = 54$ municipalities.
*   **Winning Contracts (2 Years):** $54 \times 0.50 = 27$ contracts.
*   **Revenue Impact:** $27 \times \$2,500 = \$67,500$ over two years.
*   **Year 1 Cash Flow:** $27 \times 40\% \approx 11$ contracts ($\$27,500$).

#### 4. Decision Aid & Next Steps
*   **The Flip Input:** The **Budget Allocation Factor** is the single variable that changes your outcome by 2x. Your model is highly sensitive to whether "interest" equals "funded mandate."
*   **Rule of Thumb:** If your Customer Acquisition Cost (CAC) to secure one municipal contract exceeds $600 (approx. 25% of first-year revenue), the unit economics may be unsustainable without raising prices or reducing sales effort.
*   **This Week's Action:** Do not assume the 30% interest converts. Contact 5 of the "interested" municipalities and ask specifically: *"Is there a dedicated line item for pollinator habitats in your current sustainability grant, or would this need to be carved out from general landscaping?"* Use the ratio of "Yes" answers to replace the 20%/40% assumptions above with real data.

**Note:** Investors will scrutinize the "Interest Rate" (30%) as vanity metrics unless backed by signed Letters of Intent.
**Note:** Municipal sales cycles often exceed 12 months; ensure your runway calculation accounts for delayed cash inflow.
