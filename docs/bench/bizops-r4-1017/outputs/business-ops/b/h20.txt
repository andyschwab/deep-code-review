### Lane A: Unit Economics & Decision Aid

For a one-time purchase model like CodeGolden, Lifetime Value (LTV) shifts from "subscription duration" to "Initial Margin + Referral Value." Here is the arithmetic applied to your numbers.

**1. Core Unit Economics (Single Transaction)**
*   **Revenue per Student:** $1,200
*   **Variable Costs:** $0 (Assuming instructor pay is fixed in your $95k monthly opex; if instructors are paid per student, subtract that here).
*   **Contribution Margin:** $1,200
*   **Customer Acquisition Cost (CAC):** $450
*   **Payback Period:** Immediate (since $1,200 > $450).
*   **LTV:CAC Ratio (Base):** $1,200 / $450 = **2.67x**

**2. Incorporating Referral Value**
If 15% of students refer a new paying student, and you acquire that referral at no additional ad spend, the "Referral Value" adds to the LTV.
*   **Referral Rate:** 15% (0.15)
*   **Value of One Referral:** Equal to the Contribution Margin of a new student ($1,200).
*   **Expected Referral Value per Student:** 0.15 × $1,200 = $180.
*   **Adjusted LTV:** $1,200 (Direct) + $180 (Referral) = **$1,380**.
*   **Adjusted LTV:CAC Ratio:** $1,380 / $450 = **3.07x**.

**3. Profitability & Runway Check**
*   **Monthly Revenue:** 200 students × $1,200 = $240,000.
*   **Monthly Operating Expenses:** $95,000.
*   **Monthly Marketing Spend:** 200 students × $450 = $90,000.
*   **Total Monthly Outflow:** $185,000.
*   **Net Monthly Profit:** $240,000 - $185,000 = **$55,000**.
*   **Break-even Student Count:** Fixed Costs ($95,000) ÷ Contribution Margin ($1,200 - $450 marketing variable? No, treat marketing as variable).
    *   *Correction for Break-even:* You need to cover Fixed Ops ($95k). Each student contributes ($1,200 - $450 CAC) = $750 toward fixed costs.
    *   Break-even Volume: $95,000 / $750 = **127 students/month**.
    *   Current Volume: 200 students. You are well above break-even.

**Decision Aid**
*   **Read:** Your base LTV:CAC of 2.67x is healthy (above the 3.0x "gold standard" is ideal, but >2.0x is often sustainable if churn is irrelevant). With referrals, you hit 3.07x, indicating your current $450 CAC is sustainable.
*   **The Flip Input:** The metric that flips this answer is **CAC**. If CAC rises above $600, your base ratio drops below 2.0x ($1,200/$600), squeezing margins dangerously thin relative to fixed costs.
*   **Threshold Rule:** If LTV:CAC drops below 2.0x or you fall below 127 students/month, pause paid acquisition immediately.
*   **Next Step:** This week, instrument a tracking code or survey question ("How did you hear about us?") to verify if the 15% referral rate is organic or driven by an existing incentive. If verified, consider increasing CAC slightly to grow faster, as your referrals are subsidizing acquisition.

***

**Note:** Ensure instructor pay is truly fixed; if it scales per student, your contribution margin decreases, lowering your break-even safety buffer.
**Note:** Referral value assumes zero cost to process the referral; if you offer discounts for referrals, subtract that cost from the $180 value.
