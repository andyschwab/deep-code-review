### Cash Flow Analysis for GreenSuds Refillery

**The Math (Lane A)**
We calculate the cash required to fund inventory before subscriber payments clear, then compare it to your working capital.

*   **Inputs:**
    *   Target Subscribers: 500
    *   Price per Box: $35
    *   Cost of Goods Sold (COGS) per Box: $14
    *   Current Working Capital: $8,000
    *   Inventory Lead Time: 2 weeks (14 days)
    *   Payment Clearing Time: 3 days
    *   Supplier Terms: Net-30 (payment due 30 days after invoice)

*   **Step 1: Calculate Total Inventory Cost for Growth**
    To serve 500 subscribers, you must purchase inventory upfront.
    `Total COGS = 500 subscribers × $14/box = $7,000`

*   **Step 2: Analyze the Cash Conversion Cycle (CCC)**
    The "cash gap" exists between when you pay suppliers and when you have usable cash from customers.
    1.  **Day 0:** You receive the supplier invoice.
    2.  **Day 14:** You must have inventory ready to ship (per your constraint). *Note: You do not pay the supplier yet due to Net-30 terms.*
    3.  **Day 14 (Shipping):** You ship boxes and charge credit cards.
    4.  **Day 17:** Credit card funds clear (14 + 3 days). Cash is available.
    5.  **Day 30:** Supplier invoice is due (Net-30).

    *Result:* You receive customer cash on **Day 17**. You pay suppliers on **Day 30**.
    **Cash Gap Status:** Negative. You receive funds **13 days before** the bill is due. The subscription model, combined with Net-30 terms, actually creates a cash *surplus* window, not a deficit, assuming you ship immediately upon inventory arrival.

*   **Step 3: Working Capital Sufficiency**
    Even if the timing above shifts slightly, can you cover the initial outlay if needed?
    *   Cost to stock 500 units: **$7,000**
    *   Available Working Capital: **$8,000**
    *   Remaining Buffer: **$1,000**

**Decision Aid**
1.  **Read:** Based on these numbers, your current cash on hand ($8,000) is sufficient to fund the inventory ($7,000) for 500 subscribers without dipping into an emergency fund or requiring a line of credit.
2.  **The Flip Input:** The critical variable is the **Supplier Terms**. If suppliers demand payment *before* Day 17 (e.g., COD or Net-10), you would face a gap. With Net-30, your customers fund your inventory.
3.  **Threshold Rule:** If your cash buffer drops below 20% of monthly COGS (approx. $1,400 for 500 units), reconsider a line of credit. You are currently above this threshold.
4.  **Next Step:** Before scaling, confirm with your supplier in writing that "Net-30" starts from the *invoice date* (not delivery date) and that no early-payment discounts are mandatory. If confirmed, proceed with purchasing the $7,000 inventory using existing capital.

**Note:** This analysis assumes all 500 subscribers are billed and ship in a single batch; staggered shipping improves cash flow further. Labor costs for your two staff members were not included in COGS here; ensure your $8,000 also covers their wages during the gap if payroll runs before revenue clears.
