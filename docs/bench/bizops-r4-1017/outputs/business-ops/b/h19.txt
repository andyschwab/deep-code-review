### Lane A: Arithmetic and Structural Analysis

You face a known liability of **12,000,000 JPY** due in three months. The risk is the USD cost required to settle this debt.

**1. Quantifying the Exposure**
Using your cited range (110–150 JPY/USD), here is the cost variance for your pending order:
*   **At 110 JPY/USD:** Cost = $109,090
*   **At 150 JPY/USD:** Cost = $80,000
*   **Exposure Range:** $29,090

Your typical net profit margin is 12%. On a quarterly sales base similar to last quarter ($250,000), expected profit is ~$30,000.
*   **Risk Impact:** A shift from 150 to 110 increases your costs by $29,090. This aligns with your observation that swings can wipe out ~$18,000–$29,000 of profit, effectively reducing your margin from 12% to near 0% or negative on that volume.

**2. Evaluating the Hedging Decision (Apply, Don't Direct)**
The decision rests on comparing the **cost of the hedge** against your **risk tolerance** (the potential $29k loss).

*   **Forward Contract:** Locks in a specific rate today for settlement in 3 months.
    *   *Mechanism:* You agree to buy 12M JPY at Rate $X$.
    *   *Cost:* Usually embedded in the spread between the spot rate and the forward rate. If US interest rates are higher than Japanese rates (common recently), the forward rate for JPY is often at a "premium," meaning you lock in a slightly worse rate than the current spot rate.
    *   *Trade-off:* Eliminates downside risk entirely but caps upside benefit if the yen weakens further (e.g., moves to 160).
*   **Options Contract:** Gives you the right, but not the obligation, to buy JPY at a set rate.
    *   *Mechanism:* You pay an upfront premium. If the rate moves against you, you exercise the option. If it moves in your favor, you let it expire and buy at the better spot rate.
    *   *Cost:* Explicit upfront premium (cash outflow now).
    *   *Trade-off:* Protects margins while allowing participation in favorable moves, but the premium reduces immediate cash flow and must be weighed against the 12% margin.

**Decision Aid:**
*   **Break-even Analysis:** Calculate the premium cost of an option or the spread cost of a forward. If the cost is < $5,000 (example threshold), it preserves ~83% of your typical quarterly profit ($30k) regardless of rate movement. If the cost is > $15,000, it may erode too much margin.
*   **Sensitivity:** If the yen strengthens to 120, your cost rises to $100,000 (a $20k increase from the 150 baseline). Does your business have the cash reserves to absorb a $20k hit without hedging? If no, hedging is structurally indicated to ensure survival.
*   **Pricing Alternative:** Dynamic pricing passes risk to retailers. However, if your contracts are fixed in USD for the quarter, you cannot adjust prices mid-stream for this specific 12M JPY order. This strategy works for *future* orders, not existing liabilities.

**Next Step:** Contact a commercial bank or FX specialist to request a quote for a 3-month forward rate on 12M JPY and the premium for a corresponding call option. Compare the quoted cost against your $29,000 maximum exposure to determine if the protection cost fits your margin structure.
