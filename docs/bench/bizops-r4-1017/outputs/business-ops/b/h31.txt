### Lane A: Cash Flow Modeling & Working Capital Arithmetic

To model the impact of the lead time extension from 2 weeks to 3 months (approx. 13 weeks), you must adjust your **Cash Conversion Cycle (CCC)** specifically within the "Days Inventory Outstanding" (DIO) component. Since you cannot bill until production starts, your cash is tied up in raw materials and payroll for a longer duration before any inflow occurs.

**1. The Formula for Additional Working Capital Needed**
The additional capital required is the cost of operations during the *incremental* wait time.
$$ \text{Additional Capital} = (\text{Weekly Burn Rate}) \times (\text{New Lead Time} - \text{Old Lead Time}) $$

**2. Applying Your Numbers (with Assumptions)**
*   **Knowns:**
    *   Current Cash: $150,000
    *   Material Cost per Unit: $900
    *   Sales Price: $4,200
    *   Lead Time Increase: ~11 weeks (13 weeks new - 2 weeks old).
*   **Unknowns (Must be inserted by you):**
    *   **Weekly Payroll:** 12 builders $\times$ average weekly wage.
    *   **Weekly Overhead:** Rent, utilities, insurance, admin.
    *   **Production Throughput:** How many boats can you start per week once materials arrive? (This dictates how quickly you convert materials to cash).

**Illustrative Calculation:**
Assume your total weekly fixed burn (Payroll + Overhead) is $X$.
Assume you need to stockpile materials for $Y$ boats to keep the line moving for the full 3-month gap.
$$ \text{Material Cash Trap} = Y \text{ boats} \times \$900 $$
$$ \text{Operating Gap Cost} = \$X \times 11 \text{ weeks} $$
$$ \text{Total Additional Need} = \text{Material Cash Trap} + \text{Operating Gap Cost} $$

*If your weekly burn is $20,000 and you need to pre-buy materials for 20 boats:*
*   Operating Gap: $20,000 \times 11 = $220,000
*   Material Trap: $20 \times $900 = $18,000
*   Total Need: $238,000.
*   **Result:** With $150,000 on hand, you have a projected deficit of $88,000 before the first new boat is sold.

**3. Solvency vs. Liquidity Threshold**
*   **Liquidity Squeeze:** You have enough assets to cover debts long-term, but cash is tied up in inventory. This is solved by a line of credit if $\text{Current Cash} + \text{Credit Line} > \text{Total Additional Need}$.
*   **Solvency Issue:** This occurs if the cost of the capital (interest) or the delay causes you to miss payroll permanently, or if the delay forces you to raise prices such that demand collapses, making the LTV of a customer negative. Mathematically, if $\text{Total Additional Need} > (\text{Current Cash} + \text{Max Available Credit})$, you cannot bridge the gap.

**Decision Aid:**
*   **Read:** If your calculated "Total Additional Need" exceeds $150,000, you are already in a liquidity deficit without a credit line.
*   **Flip Input:** The variable that flips this answer is **Weekly Burn**. Reduce burn (e.g., reduced hours) or increase throughput (more boats/week) to lower the 11-week multiplier.
*   **Rule of Thumb:** If your projected cash balance drops below 2 weeks of payroll at any point in the 13-week window, it is a critical solvency risk.
*   **Next Step:** Calculate your exact weekly burn today. If (Weekly Burn $\times$ 11) > $150,000, prepare a request for a line of credit immediately covering 125% of that gap.

### Key Risks for Bank Presentation
When requesting the line of credit, present these specific variables:
