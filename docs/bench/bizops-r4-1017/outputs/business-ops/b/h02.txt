### Lane A: Arithmetic & Unit Economics
Based on your inputs, here is the break-even and ROI analysis for the dynamic ad insertion feature.

**Inputs & Assumptions:**
*   **Current Revenue:** $45,000/mo at $18 CPM.
*   **Projected CPM:** $26 (an increase of $8 per thousand impressions).
*   **Adoption Rate:** 40% of existing inventory (conservative estimate).
*   **One-time Cost:** $90,000.
*   **Recurring Cost:** $8,000/mo (developer salary).
*   **Timeframe:** 24 months.

**The Math:**
1.  **Incremental Revenue per Month:**
    *   Current implied impressions: ($45,000 / $18) × 1,000 = 2,500,000 impressions/mo.
    *   Addressable impressions (40% adoption): 1,000,000 impressions/mo.
    *   Revenue lift per 1,000 impressions: $26 - $18 = $8.
    *   **New Monthly Revenue:** (1,000,000 / 1,000) × $8 = **$8,000/mo**.

2.  **Net Monthly Cash Flow Impact:**
    *   Incremental Revenue ($8,000) − Incremental Cost ($8,000) = **$0/mo**.

3.  **Break-Even Point:**
    *   Formula: `One-time Cost ÷ Net Monthly Cash Flow`.
    *   Calculation: $90,000 ÷ $0 = **Undefined (Never breaks even)**.
    *   *Sensitivity:* To break even, you need a net positive cash flow. At your current adoption and CPM lift assumptions, the additional revenue exactly covers the new salary, leaving no surplus to repay the $90,000 build cost.

4.  **Two-Year ROI:**
    *   Total Incremental Revenue (24 mo): $8,000 × 24 = $192,000.
    *   Total Incremental Cost: $90,000 (build) + ($8,000 × 24 maint) = $282,000.
    *   Net Profit/Loss: $192,000 - $282,000 = **-$90,000**.
    *   **ROI:** -31.9%.

**Decision Aid:**
Under these specific assumptions, the project loses money because the recurring cost consumes the entire revenue lift. The "flip input" here is **adoption rate** or **CPM lift**. If adoption rises to 60% (generating $12,000/mo lift), your net monthly cash flow becomes $4,000, resulting in a 22.5-month break-even period. Before proceeding, consider testing the CPM lift on a small subset of shows to validate if the $26 figure holds before committing to the full build.

### Lane R: Regulation & Compliance
**Regime:** California Labor Law, Intellectual Property (Copyright), and potentially SAG-AFTRA agreements.

**General Rule:** Retroactively altering audio content (dynamic insertion) can implicate rights of publicity, contract law, and union residuals if the original agreements did not explicitly permit future modification or new forms of monetization. In California, strict rules govern whether archived work can be repurposed without new consent or compensation.

**Route to Professional:** You must consult an **entertainment attorney** specializing in digital media and labor law before engineering begins.
*   **Specific Question to Bring:** "Do our existing creator contracts and any applicable SAG-AFTRA agreements permit retroactive dynamic ad insertion in archived episodes without additional residual payments or renewed consent?"
*   **Why:** A binding determination on whether you owe residuals or face liability for modifying archived content is a legal verdict, not an arithmetic one.

*Note: The arithmetic above assumes the $8k/month is a fully loaded new cost; if this developer is already on payroll, the marginal cost changes the equation. The legal risk is high if contracts are silent on "future technologies."*
