### Lane A: Unit Economics & Scenario Modeling

**Current Baseline (Monthly)**
*   **Volume:** $48,000 Revenue / $120 Ticket = **400 jobs/month**.
*   **Current Contribution Margin:** $120 Price - $65 Variable Cost = **$55/job**.
*   **Current Fixed Costs (Implied):** Total Revenue ($48k) - Total Variable ($26k) - Net Profit ($8.64k, based on 18% margin) = **$13,360/month**.
*   **Fuel Impact:** 12 vans × $8/day × ~22 work days = **$2,112/month** additional cost if absorbed.

**Elasticity Assumption:** A $5 price increase causes a 10% volume drop.
*   *Sensitivity Factor:* 2% volume drop per $1 price increase.

#### Scenario 1: Flat Fee Surcharge ($10 per job)
*   **Price Increase:** +$10.
*   **Projected Volume Drop:** 20% (2% × $10). New Volume: 320 jobs.
*   **New Revenue:** 320 jobs × ($120 + $10) = $41,600.
*   **New Variable Costs:** 320 jobs × $65 = $20,800.
*   **New Fuel Cost:** $0 (passed to consumer).
*   **New Net Profit:** $41,600 - $20,800 - $13,360 (Fixed) = **$7,440**.
*   **Result:** Profit decreases from $8,640 to $7,440. Retention drops 20%.

#### Scenario 2: Percentage-Based Surcharge (10% of ticket)
*   **Price Increase:** +$12 (10% of $120).
*   **Projected Volume Drop:** 24% (2% × $12). New Volume: 304 jobs.
*   **New Revenue:** 304 jobs × $132 = $40,128.
*   **New Variable Costs:** 304 jobs × $65 = $19,760.
*   **New Net Profit:** $40,128 - $19,760 - $13,360 = **$7,008**.
*   **Result:** Profit decreases further. Retention drops 24%.

#### Scenario 3: Distance-Based (Averaged to $8 equivalent)
*   **Price Increase:** +$8.
*   **Projected Volume Drop:** 16% (2% × $8). New Volume: 336 jobs.
*   **New Revenue:** 336 jobs × $128 = $43,008.
*   **New Variable Costs:** 336 jobs × $65 = $21,840.
*   **New Net Profit:** $43,008 - $21,840 - $13,360 = **$7,808**.
*   **Result:** Best of the three options, but still lower than current baseline profit.

**Decision Aid:**
Under your stated elasticity, passing the full cost results in lower net profit because the volume loss outweighs the margin recovery.
*   **Break-even Volume Drop:** To maintain current profit ($8,640) with a $10 price hike, volume can only drop by **~12.5%**, not 20%.
*   **Next Step:** Before changing pricing, test a smaller surcharge (e.g., $5) on a subset of customers to verify if your actual elasticity is less severe than the historical 10% per $5 assumption.

### Lane R: Regulatory Regime (Texas Consumer Protection)

**Regime:** Texas Deceptive Trade Practices-Consumer Protection Act (DTPA) and Texas Administrative Code (Title 1, Part 4, Chapter 101).

**General Rule:** Texas law generally requires that the total price advertised or quoted to a consumer must include all mandatory fees. Separating mandatory costs into "surcharges" while advertising a lower base price can be considered a deceptive practice if the surcharge is effectively part of the standard service cost. If a fee is unavoidable for the service, it is typically required to be included in the advertised price rather than added later as a line item surprise.

**Routing:** You must consult a **Texas-licensed consumer protection attorney** to determine:
1.  Whether a fuel surcharge qualifies as a "mandatory fee" that must be bundled in your advertised $120 rate.
2.  Specific disclosure language required on invoices to avoid DTPA liability if you do separate it.
3.  Whether your franchise agreement imposes additional branding/pricing disclosure rules.

Do not implement the surcharge on advertising materials until counsel confirms the disclosure structure complies with Texas "all-in" pricing expectations.
