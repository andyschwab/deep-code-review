### Lane A: Pricing Model Arithmetic & Sensitivity Analysis

You are comparing a **Flat License** model against a **Usage-Based** model. Below is the application of your inputs to both structures, including a sensitivity test for a 20% volume drop.

**1. Current State (Flat License)**
*   **Formula:** `Revenue = Clients × Fixed Fee`
*   **Inputs:** 10 clients × $5,000/month
*   **Current MRR:** $50,000
*   **Recession Scenario (-20% volume):** Revenue remains **$50,000**.
    *   *Note:* Under a flat license, your revenue is decoupled from client usage volatility. The client absorbs the risk of lower transaction volumes; you retain revenue stability.

**2. Proposed State (Usage-Based)**
*   **Formula:** `Revenue = Clients × Avg Calls × Rate per Call`
*   **Inputs:** 10 clients × 80,000 calls × $0.05/call
*   **Current Equivalent MRR:** $40,000 ($5,000 × 10 vs $4,000 × 10)
    *   *Immediate Observation:* Based strictly on current volumes (80k calls), switching to $0.05/call results in an immediate **20% revenue decrease** ($50k → $40k) before any recessionary impact. To match current revenue, the break-even volume per client must be 100,000 calls/month ($5,000 ÷ $0.05).

**3. Recession Sensitivity Test (20% Drop in Volume)**
Assuming the switch happens at current volumes, then volumes drop 20%:
*   **New Volume:** 80,000 × (1 - 0.20) = 64,000 calls/client.
*   **New Revenue:** 10 clients × 64,000 calls × $0.05 = **$32,000 MRR**.
*   **Total Revenue Swing:** From $50,000 (Flat) to $32,000 (Usage + Recession).
*   **Impact:** A **36% reduction** in total contract value compared to the status quo.

**Decision Aid & Thresholds**
*   **The Flip Input:** The variable that determines if Usage-Based outperforms Flat is **Volume Growth**.
    *   *Break-even Formula:* `Required Calls = Fixed Fee ÷ Rate per Call`
    *   *Your Threshold:* $5,000 ÷ $0.05 = **100,000 calls/month**.
    *   *Read:* If your clients do not consistently exceed 100k calls/month, the usage model yields less revenue than the flat fee.
*   **Volatility Risk:** In a downturn where volumes contract, usage-based pricing amplifies revenue loss. If volumes drop 20%, your revenue drops 20% *on top of* any baseline gap between the models.
*   **Rule of Thumb:** If `LTV:CAC` relies on stable MRR to cover fixed infrastructure costs, introducing high revenue volatility (usage-based) during a downturn increases burn risk unless the rate per call is raised or a minimum floor is implemented.

**Recommended Next Step (This Week)**
If you believe budget constraints are the primary barrier to signing *new* clients, consider a **hybrid approach** rather than a full switch: implement a "Good-Better-Best" tier where the entry level is usage-based (lowering the barrier to entry) but includes a committed minimum spend or overage rates that protect your downside if volumes rebound. Test this structure on *new* signups only before migrating existing enterprise clients.
