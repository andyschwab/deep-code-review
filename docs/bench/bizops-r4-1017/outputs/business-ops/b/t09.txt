### Cash Flow Analysis: Installment Plan Impact

To determine if installment plans threaten your $20,000 reserve, we must compare the timing of cash inflows against the fixed outflow deadline. The critical variable you identified as unknown is the **production lead time** (time from order to supplier payment due).

**1. The Baseline (Current Model)**
*   **Inflow:** $225 deposit (50%) collected immediately at order.
*   **Outflow:** $450 (100% of suit cost assumed equal to revenue for worst-case liquidity, or actual fabric cost) due 14 days before shipping.
*   **Liquidity Gap:** You hold 50% of cash upfront. If production takes longer than 14 days, you have the customer's remaining balance in hand before paying the supplier. If production is faster than 14 days, you fund the gap with the deposit.

**2. The Installment Scenario (4 payments of $112.50)**
*   **Inflow Schedule:**
    *   Day 0: $112.50 (25%)
    *   Day 30: $112.50 (25%)
    *   Day 60: $112.50 (25%)
    *   Day 90: $112.50 (25%)
*   **Outflow Deadline:** Fixed at **(Production Time − 14 days)**.
*   **The Risk:** Under installments, you only collect 50% of the cash ($225) by Day 30. If your supplier payment is due before Day 30, you cannot fund it entirely from customer cash flow, even if the total order value covers the cost.

**3. The Break-Even Calculation**
To avoid dipping into reserves for a single order, your cumulative collections must exceed the supplier payment due date.
*   **Collection Milestone:** You reach 50% collection ($225) on **Day 30**.
*   **Required Production Time:** To pay suppliers using only customer funds, the payment due date must be **after Day 30**.
    *   Formula: `Payment Due Date = Production Time − 14 days`
    *   Condition: `Production Time − 14 > 30`
    *   Result: **Production Time must exceed 44 days.**

**4. Monthly Volume Stress Test (20 New Orders)**
Assuming 20 new orders/month and a constant production lead time ($T$ days):
*   **Total Monthly Outflow Obligation:** 20 orders × $450 = $9,000 (assuming fabric cost equals sale price for maximum stress; replace with actual fabric cost if lower).
*   **Cash Available Day 0:** 20 × $112.50 = $2,250.
*   **Cash Available Day 30:** Additional $2,250 (Total $4,500 collected).
*   **The Crunch Zone:** If $T < 44$ days, your supplier payments come due before you have collected 50% of the cash.
    *   *Example:* If $T = 30$ days, payment is due on Day 16. You only have $2,250 collected but owe $9,000.
    *   **Reserve Dip:** $9,000 (owed) − $2,250 (collected) = **$6,750 drawn from reserves** for that month's cohort alone.

**Decision Aid**
*   **One-line read:** If your end-to-end production time is under 44 days, installment plans will create an immediate cash deficit requiring reserve usage.
*   **The Flip Input:** The specific number of days in your production cycle. If $T > 44$, installments are cash-flow neutral/positive; if $T < 44$, they require working capital.
*   **Rule of Thumb:** Never offer payment terms that extend beyond your cash conversion cycle unless you have explicit working capital reserves to bridge the gap.
*   **Next Step:** Calculate your exact average $T$. If $T < 44$, consider requiring a larger upfront deposit (e.g., 50% upfront, then 3 installments) to align inflows with the supplier deadline.

*Note: This analysis assumes fabric cost equals revenue for a conservative "worst-case" liquidity view; substitute your actual Cost of Goods Sold (COGS) for precise figures. Consult a CPA to model tax implications of deferred revenue recognition.*
