### Lane A: Unit Economics & Pricing Structure Analysis

**Current State Arithmetic (Last Month)**
*   **Revenue:** 1,200 pairs × $210 = $252,000
*   **Variable Costs:** 1,200 pairs × $45 = $54,000
*   **Fixed Software Costs:** $12,000
*   **Total Contribution Margin:** $252,000 - $54,000 - $12,000 = **$186,000**
*   **Effective Cost Per Pair to StepRight:** ($54,000 + $12,000) ÷ 1,200 = **$55/pair**
*   **Your Margin:** $210 - $55 = **$155/pair** (74% margin)

**Clinic Sustainability Check (The Constraint)**
The clinic's revenue is capped by insurance at **$185/pair**.
*   **Clinic Cost for Insoles:** $210 (your price)
*   **Clinic Revenue:** $185 (insurance cap)
*   **Immediate Deficit:** -$25/pair before the clinic adds its own labor/overhead.

**Conclusion on Current Model:** The current model is mathematically unsustainable for the clinic. They lose $25 on every unit sold before accounting for their own operational costs. They cannot sustain your pricing under the current reimbursement cap.

**Pricing Model Scenarios**

**Option 1: Bundle Software into Unit Price (Keep $210 total)**
*   *Math:* No change to the clinic's per-unit cost ($210).
*   *Result:* Clinics still face a -$25/pair deficit. Rejection is highly probable as the unit economics do not close.

**Option 2: Separate SaaS Fee + Lower Unit Price**
To make the clinic viable, their cost per unit must be below $185. Let's assume they need a 20% gross margin to cover their overhead and profit.
*   **Target Clinic Cost:** $185 × (1 - 0.20) = **$148/pair**.
*   **Proposed Unit Price:** $148 (down from $210).
*   **Software Fee Allocation:** You must recover the $62 difference per unit ($210 - $148) via the SaaS fee to maintain your total revenue.
*   **Required SaaS Fee:** 1,200 units × $62 = **$74,400/month**.
*   *Clinic Perspective:* They pay $148/unit (viable) + $74,400 fixed.
*   *Risk:* If volume drops to 600 pairs, the effective cost per pair for the clinic jumps to $148 + ($74,400/600) = $272/pair. This creates high volume risk for the clinic. Since software fees are likely not reimbursable, clinics may reject a high fixed fee that requires high volume to break even.

**Option 3: Hybrid "Per-Scan" or Usage-Based Model (Recommended for Analysis)**
Align the software cost with the reimbursable event.
*   **Structure:** Lower unit price to near variable cost + small margin; add a "technology fee" per scan that the clinic attempts to bill or absorbs as a differentiator.
*   **Math:** Set Unit Price at $100 ($55 cost + $45 margin).
*   **Remaining Revenue Needed:** $210 (target) - $100 = $110/pair for software.
*   **Total Clinic Cost:** $100 (hardware) + $110 (tech fee) = $210.
*   *Result:* This fails the same test as Option 1 unless the "tech fee" can be billed separately to the patient or insurance, which is unlikely given the cap.

**Decision Aid & Next Steps**
1.  **Read:** Your current model creates a $25/unit loss for the clinic. It will fail without restructuring.
2.  **Flip Input:** The break-even price for the clinic (assuming 0% margin) is **$185**. Any package >$185 requires the clinic to find non-insurance revenue.
3.  **Threshold Rule:** If LTV (clinic lifetime value) relies on volume >1,200, and the clinic cannot survive at current volume, your CAC is effectively infinite because churn will be 100%.
4.  **Next Step:** Run a Van Westendorp price sensitivity survey with 5 clinic owners. Ask: "At what monthly software fee would you consider our scanning tool too expensive regardless of insole price?" Use their answer to set the fixed component, then solve for the maximum unit price: `Max Unit Price = $185 - (Target Clinic Margin)`.

**Note:** Insurance reimbursement rules are regulatory; confirm with a local healthcare billing specialist if any portion of "digital scanning" can be billed under a different code.
**Note:** Do not fabricate a "market standard" SaaS fee; derive it from the clinic's remaining margin headroom ($185 - their costs).
