### Lane A: Arithmetic & Scenario Analysis

Your current situation creates an immediate cash flow crisis. Below is the math applied to your inputs ($3.20 original cost → $7.40 new cost; $8.50 price; 15,000 units; $45k fixed overhead).

**1. The "Do Nothing" Baseline (Current Trajectory)**
*   **Revenue:** $8.50 × 15,000 = $127,500
*   **Variable Cost:** $7.40 × 15,000 = $111,000
*   **Gross Profit:** $16,500
*   **Net Burn:** $16,500 (Gross) − $45,000 (Fixed) = **−$28,500/month**
*   **Runway:** $120,000 ÷ $28,500 ≈ **4.2 months**. (Matches your estimate).

**2. Option A: Raise Price to $14.00 (Maintain ~60% Margin)**
*   *Assumption:* You stated a fear of delisting. Mathematically, if volume holds:
*   **Revenue:** $14.00 × 15,000 = $210,000
*   **Variable Cost:** $7.40 × 15,000 = $111,000
*   **Gross Profit:** $99,000
*   **Net Profit:** $99,000 − $45,000 = **+$54,000/month**
*   *Sensitivity:* If retail partners delist you and volume drops by 40% (to 9,000 units):
    *   Gross Profit: ($14.00 − $7.40) × 9,000 = $59,400.
    *   Net Profit: $59,400 − $45,000 = **+$14,400/month**.
    *   *Result:* Even with a 40% volume loss, you remain cash-flow positive. The break-even volume at $14.00 is roughly 6,818 units.

**3. Option B: Reformulate (Cheaper Oils) or Reduce Size**
*   *Goal:* Return variable cost to ~$3.20–$4.00 range to keep $8.50 price.
*   *Risk:* "Artisanal/Organic" brand equity relies on ingredient integrity.
*   *Math:* If reformulation saves $3.00/bar (new cost $4.40):

    *   Net Profit: $61,500 − $45,000 = **+$16,500/month**.
*   *Decision Factor:* This yields less profit than the "Price Hike with 40% loss" scenario ($16.5k vs $14.4k), but carries high reputational risk if customers perceive lower quality.

**4. Option C: Absorb Cost Temporarily**
*   As calculated in the baseline, this depletes your $120k reserve in ~4 months. This is only viable if the shortage is guaranteed to end in <3 months.

### Decision Aid & Next Steps

**The Read:** Raising prices to $14.00 appears to be the only mathematical path that secures the business even if you lose significant volume. The break-even volume drops drastically at the higher price point.

**The Formula:**
`Break-even Units = Fixed Costs ÷ (Price − Variable Cost)`
*   At $8.50 price: $45,000 ÷ ($8.50 − $7.40) = **40,909 units** (Impossible at current 15k demand).
*   At $14.00 price: $45,000 ÷ ($14.00 − $7.40) = **6,818 units**.

**Thresholds (Rules of Thumb):**
*   If you believe you can retain >45% of current volume (6,818 units) at $14.00, the price hike is mathematically safer than reformulation.
*   If the shortage is expected to last >4 months, "absorbing cost" leads to insolvency.
