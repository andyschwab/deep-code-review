### Lane A: Unit Economics & Risk Modeling

We will model the trade-off between increased subscription revenue and the risk of losing sponsorship revenue due to high-value user churn.

**1. Current State (Baseline)**
*   **Total Subscribers:** 3,200
*   **High-Value Users (15%):** 480
*   **Standard Users:** 2,720
*   **Current MRR (Subscription):** $49 × 3,200 = $156,800
*   **Current Annual Subscription Revenue:** $1,881,600
*   **Sponsorship Requirement:** 40% of $85,000 monthly opex = $34,000/month ($408,000/year).
*   **Implicit Assumption:** Your current 480 high-value users currently satisfy the sponsor threshold to generate this $34k/month.

**2. Proposed State (Price Hike to $79)**
*   **Scenario:** You raise price to $79. You fear losing 20% of high-value users.
*   **Lost High-Value Users:** 20% of 480 = 96 users lost.
*   **Remaining High-Value Users:** 384.
*   **Assumed Churn on Standard Users:** To be conservative, we assume standard users churn at the same rate (20%) for this specific price shock, though often they are less sensitive.
    *   *Conservative Estimate:* 20% loss across the board.
    *   *Remaining Total Subscribers:* 3,200 × 0.80 = 2,560.
*   **New MRR (Subscription):** 2,560 × $79 = $202,240.
*   **New Annual Subscription Revenue:** $2,426,880.
*   **Net Subscription Gain:** $2,426,880 - $1,881,600 = **+$545,280/year**.

**3. The Sponsorship Risk (The Break-Even Analysis)**
The price hike is net-negative only if the lost sponsorship revenue exceeds the gained subscription revenue.
*   **Gain from Price Hike:** +$545,280/year.
*   **Current Sponsorship Revenue Needed:** $408,000/year.
*   **Break-Even Condition:** You lose the deal entirely if the remaining 384 high-value users fall below the sponsor's threshold.
    *   If the sponsor requires *exactly* 480 users and leaves completely if you drop to 384, you lose $408,000.
    *   **Net Result in Worst Case:** +$545,280 (sub gain) - $408,000 (sponsor loss) = **+$137,280 net positive**.

**Sensitivity: How much sponsorship must you lose to make this negative?**
To make the price hike a net loss, you would need to lose **more than 134%** of your current sponsorship revenue (which is impossible unless penalties exist), *assuming* the 20% churn applies to everyone.

**Refined Scenario: High-Value Churn Only**
If standard users do *not* churn significantly, but only the 20% of high-value users leave:
*   **New Subscriber Count:** 3,200 - 96 = 3,104.
*   **New MRR:** (2,720 × $79) + (384 × $79) = $245,216.
*   **Annual Gain:** ($245,216 - $156,800) × 12 = **+$1,060,992**.
*   **Risk Threshold:** You could lose the *entire* $408,000 sponsorship and still be **+$652,992** ahead annually.

**Decision Aid**
*   **One-line read:** Based on these numbers, the subscription revenue uplift is so large that you could lose 100% of your sponsorship revenue and still come out ahead financially, provided the churn doesn't exceed ~55% of your total base.
*   **The Flip Input:** The model flips only if the price hike causes **total churn > 46%** (approx. 1,470 users canceling). If only high-value users leave, you would need to lose **>260%** of your sponsorship value (impossible) to break even negatively.
*   **Rule of Thumb:** If LTV increases (price up) and churn stays under 50% of the gain, the move is mathematically safe on subscription revenue alone.
*   **Next Step:** Before raising prices, confirm the **sponsor contract terms**. Does the contract guarantee $34k/month regardless of user count, or is it variable? If it's variable based on *exact* high-value headcount, calculate the per-user sponsorship value ($34,000 / 480 = ~$71/user/month). If you lose a user, you lose $71 in sponsorship but gain $30 in subscription ($79-$49). In this specific variable case, **losing a high-value user is a net loss of $41/month per user**.

**Conclusion:** If sponsorship is a fixed fee for access to the *audience segment* (and 384 users still qualifies), take the hike. If sponsorship is strictly per-headcount and 384 users disqualifies you entirely, you still net positive unless churn is catastrophic. Verify the sponsor's "threshold clause" immediately.

*Note: This model assumes the 20% churn estimate applies only to the price sensitivity of the high-value segment; actual market reaction may vary.*
*Ensure your sponsorship agreements do not have "most favored nation" or audit clauses that could be triggered by pricing changes.*
