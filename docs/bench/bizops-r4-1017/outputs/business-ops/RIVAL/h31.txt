To model your new cash flow reality, you must shift from a "just-in-time" mindset to a "stockpile" model. Your primary adjustment is calculating the **Inventory Build-Up Gap**. Previously, you held four weeks of inventory; now, to maintain continuous production without stopping, you must hold roughly 12–14 weeks (3 months supply plus safety stock).

**Key Variables to Adjust in Your Projections:**

1.  **Raw Material Inventory Days:** Increase this from 28 days to ~90+ days. This is the single biggest driver of your cash burn. You are no longer paying for materials two weeks before use; you are paying for three months of materials upfront.
2.  **Cash Conversion Cycle (CCC):** Your CCC will explode. Since you cannot bill until production starts, and production cannot start without materials that take 90 days to arrive, your cash is tied up for an additional ~75–80 days compared to your historical baseline.
3.  **Working Capital Requirement:** Calculate the cost of the *extra* inventory needed.
    *   *Current State:* 4 weeks inventory ≈ $900/boat × (12 builders × production rate).
    *   *New State:* 12 weeks inventory required.
    *   *The Gap:* You need to fund the difference between 4 weeks and 12 weeks of material costs immediately. If you build 1 boat per builder per week (example rate), you need materials for 48 extra boats sitting in the warehouse before you can bill a single one. That is 48 boats × $900 = **$43,200** in immediate additional cash outlay, just to stand still. If your throughput is higher, this number scales linearly.
4.  **Payroll Coverage:** With $150,000 in the bank, calculate your weekly burn rate (Payroll + Overhead). Divide $150,000 by this weekly burn to find your "runway." If your material lead time (90 days) exceeds this runway, you have a solvency issue.

**Liquidity vs. Solvency Threshold:**
This becomes a **solvency issue** (not just liquidity) when your total cash reserves cannot cover the full 90-day material lead time *plus* the production time *plus* the collection period, while still meeting payroll. If your $150,000 runs out before the first kayak built with the new long-lead materials is sold and paid for, you are insolvent. Specifically, if:
*(Weekly Burn Rate × Weeks to Cash In) > $150,000 + Available Credit*
You must secure funding *before* you place the first 3-month order.

**Key Risks to Present to Your Bank:**

1.  **Structural Supply Chain Shift:** Emphasize that this is not a temporary delay but a structural change in supplier terms (2 weeks → 3 months). This requires a permanent increase in working capital, not a short-term bridge.
2.  **Order Book vs. Cash Flow Mismatch:** Highlight that you have demand (sales at $4,200/unit are strong), but the cash timing is broken. The risk is not market demand, but the timing mismatch between paying suppliers and collecting revenue.
3.  **Production Stoppage Risk:** Without the line of credit, you will face a hard stop in production in 4 weeks when current inventory runs out. This leads to missed deliveries, reputational damage, and potential contract cancellations.
4.  **Collateral Value:** Remind the bank that the loan is secured by high-value raw materials (wood/resin) and finished goods (custom kayaks), which retain value better than work-in-progress in many industries.
