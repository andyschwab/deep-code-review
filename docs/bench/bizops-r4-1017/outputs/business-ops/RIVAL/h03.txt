**Immediate Recommendation:** Do not accept pure equity deals with Italian public museums. State-owned cultural assets in Italy are generally inalienable, and private equity stakes in the institutions themselves are likely legally prohibited or restricted to specific, highly regulated public-private partnership frameworks. You must engage specialized Italian cultural heritage legal counsel before signing any term sheet.

Given your cash constraints (€120k reserves vs. €15k/month burn = 8 months runway) and lease obligations, accepting zero upfront cash poses an existential risk. Instead, propose these three hybrid structures that secure immediate liquidity while capturing upside:

**1. The "Base + Royalty" Model (Recommended)**
*   **Structure:** Charge a reduced upfront fee of €20,000 per collection (covering immediate equipment lease costs) plus a 15% royalty on gross digital licensing revenue for 7 years.
*   **Why:** This secures €60,000 total immediate cash (extending runway by 4 months) while participating in the projected €200k annual revenue. If projections hold, you earn €30k/year per museum, totaling €90k annually after year 3.
*   **Downside Protection:** Include a "Minimum Annual Guarantee" (MAG) clause starting in Year 2. If licensing revenue falls below €50k, the museum owes the difference to meet a baseline return.

**2. The "Deferred Cash with Warrant" Model**
*   **Structure:** Charge the full €50,000 fee, but allow payment terms of 50% upfront and 50% deferred over 24 months. In exchange for the deferral, receive a "warrant" granting rights to 10% of net profits from the digital archive specifically, not the museum itself.
*   **Why:** Generates €75,000 immediate cash. The profit share acts as the equity substitute without requiring ownership of state assets.
*   **Downside Protection:** The deferred portion is a legally binding debt obligation, not contingent on success. If the project fails, you are still owed the principal.

**3. The "Revenue Share Cap" Model**
*   **Structure:** Waive the upfront fee entirely in exchange for 25% of gross licensing revenue until ClearLens receives €150,000 (3x standard fee), then dropping to 5% in perpetuity.
*   **Why:** High upside if their projections are accurate; you recoup 3x your value quickly.
*   **Downside Protection:** This is the riskiest option for cash flow. Only accept this if you can secure a separate bridge loan against these contracts. Include a "Kill Fee" clause: if the museum cancels the digitization project or fails to launch licensing within 18 months, all fees become immediately due in cash.

**Critical Legal Context:**
Italian law (Codice dei Beni Culturali) strictly governs state-owned assets. Private entities typically cannot hold "equity" in the museum or its collection. Any revenue sharing must be structured as a commercial concession or licensing agreement, not an ownership stake. Attempting to take equity could void the contract and expose you to legal liability.

**Note:** Your current burn rate leaves little room for experiments that delay cash inflow; prioritize Structure 1 to maintain solvency.
