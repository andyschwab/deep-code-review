### Financial Viability Analysis: Alpine Age

**Current Monthly Performance**
*   **Revenue:** 5,000 wheels × 8 lbs × $12/lb = **$480,000**
*   **Climate Control Cost:** **$4,500** (0.9% of revenue)
*   **Cost Per Wheel (Climate):** $0.90
*   **Cost Per Pound (Climate):** $0.11

**Projected Impact (5% Energy Increase)**
*   **New Monthly Cost:** $4,725 (+$225)
*   **New Cost Per Wheel:** $0.95
*   **Margin Erosion:** The increase reduces your monthly profit by exactly **$225**.

**Profitability Assessment**
Your wholesale model is **not unsustainable** due to humidity control costs. While your net margin feels "thin," the climate control expense represents less than 1% of your top-line revenue. Even with a 5% rise in energy costs, the impact on your per-unit economics is negligible ($0.05 more per wheel).

To miss your $60,000 annual profit target ($5,000/month), your *total* operating expenses (including labor for your three managers, rent, milk costs, packaging, and logistics) would need to exceed $475,000 monthly. The humidity system is not the primary driver eroding your margins; it is likely your cost of goods sold (milk/labor) or overhead structure.

**Strategic Recommendations**

1.  **Do Not Pivot Solely on Energy Costs:** Shifting to Direct-to-Consumer (DTC) introduces significant new costs (shipping, cold-chain logistics, marketing, customer acquisition) that far exceed the $225 monthly energy increase. DTC commands higher prices ($20+/lb), but the operational complexity may reduce your net margin if volume drops.
2.  **Renegotiate or Upgrade?** Investing in new equipment now is unjustified. The potential savings from a 20% efficiency gain would only save you ~$45/month, likely failing to provide a return on investment for years. Do not renegotiate wholesale contracts based on energy costs; a $0.05/lb price increase request will be rejected and risks customer relationships.
3.  **Immediate Action:** Conduct a full Cost of Goods Sold (COGS) audit. Focus on milk procurement costs, labor efficiency per wheel, and yield losses (shrinkage) during aging. If margins are truly threatened, the leak is in production efficiency or raw material costs, not the electric bill.

**Conclusion:** Maintain wholesale operations. The energy cost variance is immaterial to your viability. Focus on optimizing production yields and labor rather than altering your sales channel or capital expenditures for climate control.
