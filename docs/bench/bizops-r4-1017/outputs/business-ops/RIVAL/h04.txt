**SparkleWheels Fuel Surcharge Impact Model**

**Current Baseline:**
*   **Monthly Volume:** 400 jobs ($48,000 revenue / $120 avg ticket).
*   **Current Net Profit:** $8,640 (18% of $48,000).
*   **Fuel Cost Increase:** $96/month total ($8/van/day × 12 vans × 30 days). This equals $0.24 per job based on current volume.
*   **Elasticity Rule:** A $5 price increase causes a 10% volume drop (2% drop per $1 increase).

**Scenario Analysis**

**1. Flat Fee Surcharge ($5.00 per job)**
*   **New Price:** $125.00.
*   **Volume Impact:** Price increased by $5.00 → 10% volume drop.
*   **New Volume:** 360 jobs.
*   **Revenue:** $45,000 (Service) + $1,800 (Surcharges) = $46,800.
*   **Variable Costs:** $23,400 (360 jobs × $65).
*   **Fixed Fuel Spike:** $96 (absorbed if not fully covered by surcharge, but here surcharge covers it). *Correction:* The $8/day is a fixed fleet cost. If passed to consumer, it offsets this cost.
*   **Net Profit Calculation:**
    *   Gross Margin per job: $125 - $65 = $60.
    *   Total Contribution: 360 × $60 = $21,600.
    *   Less Fixed Overheads (implied from baseline): Baseline Profit ($8,640) = Baseline Contribution (400 × $55) - Fixed Costs. Fixed Costs = $22,000 - $8,640 = $13,360.
    *   New Net Profit: $21,600 - $13,360 - $96 (uncovered fuel if surcharge is separate revenue? No, surcharge covers cost).
    *   *Simplified View:* You lose 40 customers. You gain $5/job on 360 customers ($1,800). You lose margin on 40 customers ($55 × 40 = $2,200).
    *   **Net Change:** -$400.
    *   **New Monthly Net Profit:** ~$8,240.
    *   **Retention Rate:** 90%.

**2. Percentage-Based Surcharge (5% of ticket)**
*   **Surcharge Amount:** $6.00 (5% of $120).
*   **New Price:** $126.00.
*   **Volume Impact:** Price increased by $6.00 → 12% volume drop (2% per $1).
*   **New Volume:** 352 jobs.
*   **Net Change Calculation:**
    *   Gain: $6 × 352 = $2,112.
    *   Loss: 48 jobs × $55 margin = $2,640.
    *   **Net Change:** -$528.
    *   **New Monthly Net Profit:** ~$8,112.
    *   **Retention Rate:** 88%.

**3. Distance-Based Surcharge (Avg $4.00 estimated)**
*   **New Price:** $124.00.
*   **Volume Impact:** Price increased by $4.00 → 8% volume drop.
*   **New Volume:** 368 jobs.
*   **Net Change Calculation:**
    *   Gain: $4 × 368 = $1,472.
    *   Loss: 32 jobs × $55 margin = $1,760.
    *   **Net Change:** -$288.
    *   **New Monthly Net Profit:** ~$8,352.
    *   **Retention Rate:** 92%.

**Conclusion on Unit Economics**
Under your current elasticity, **passing the cost directly to consumers reduces total net profit** in all three scenarios because the margin lost from churned customers exceeds the revenue gained from the surcharge. The **Distance-Based** model minimizes losses, while the **Flat Fee** maximizes volume retention but still results in a net loss compared to absorbing the $96/month internally. Absorbing the $96 cost internally reduces profit by only $96, whereas the surcharge models reduce profit by $288–$528.

**Texas Consumer Protection Note**
Texas law (Deceptive Trade Practices-Consumer Protection Act) requires that if a surcharge is mandatory, it must be included in the advertised total price; listing it separately as a "mandatory" fee in advertising can be deemed deceptive. On receipts, it must be clearly itemized if not included in the headline price, but you cannot advertise a lower base price and add mandatory fees later without clear disclosure upfront.

*Recommendation: Absorb the $96/month cost. The customer acquisition cost to replace churned clients far exceeds the fuel spike.*
