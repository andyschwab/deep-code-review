### New Break-Even & Pricing Recommendation for SkyLens Weddings

**Current Financial Reality**
Your accelerated depreciation has fundamentally shifted your cost basis. Previously, you likely amortized drone costs over four years. With a 35% annual loss in value and heavy usage, your effective equipment life is now two years.

*   **Equipment Cost:** 4 drones × £3,000 = £12,000 total asset value.
*   **New Annual Depreciation:** £12,000 ÷ 2 years = £6,000/year (£500/month).
*   **Operational Costs:** £8,500/month.
*   **Total Monthly Cost:** £9,000 (£8,500 ops + £500 depreciation).
*   **Volume:** 15 weddings/month.
*   **Current Cost Per Wedding:** £9,000 ÷ 15 = **£600**.

*Note: Your current price of £1,200 yields a £600 gross profit per wedding before taxes and other overheads, resulting in a 50% gross margin. However, to achieve a **25% net margin** after all costs, your pricing must cover the total cost plus the profit target.*

**Break-Even Analysis with Target Margin**
To secure a 25% net margin, your price cannot simply be Cost + 25%. It must be calculated as: `Price = Cost / (1 - Target Margin)`.

*   **Target Price Calculation:** £600 (Total Cost) ÷ 0.75 = **£800**.

**Wait—this suggests your current price of £1,200 is already sufficient?**
Yes, mathematically, £1,200 provides a 50% margin, which exceeds your 25% target. **However**, this calculation assumes the £8,500 operational cost includes *all* cash flow needs for replacement. If the £8,500 covers wages and insurance but you are setting aside cash reserves for replacement separately, or if the "35% depreciation" implies you must replace the fleet every two years out of pocket regardless of book value, the risk is higher.

If the market perceives your service as premium (high-end drones), and you are worried about pricing yourself out, the issue may not be the base price, but the **perceived value vs. cost structure**.

**Strategic Recommendation**
Do not reduce shoot volume; lowering utilization increases your cost per unit. Instead, adjust the packaging:

1.  **Maintain the £1,200 Base Price:** You are currently well above the £800 required for a 25% net margin. Do not lower prices.
2.  **Introduce a "Premium Tech" Tier (£1,550):** Create a new tier explicitly marketing the latest high-end drones, 4K/60fps raw footage, and expedited editing. This captures clients willing to pay for the newest tech, subsidizing the depreciation of the fleet used in the standard package.
3.  **Add an Equipment Refresh Surcharge (£150):** Instead of raising the base rate, itemize a "Latest Generation Drone Fee" of £150. This makes the cost transparent and justifiable to clients who care about video quality, while allowing price-sensitive clients to potentially opt for a "Standard Drone" package at £1,050 (using older inventory).
4.  **Cap Shoots Per Drone:** Limit each drone to 3 weddings per weekend to reduce wear and tear, extending the physical life closer to 3 years. This requires utilizing all 4 drones efficiently across the 15 monthly bookings.

**Conclusion**
Your break-even price for a 25% net margin is **£800 per wedding**. Your current £1,200 price is healthy. The risk is not underpricing, but **cash flow timing** for replacements. Implement the "Premium Tech" tier to increase average revenue per user (ARPU) to ~£1,350, creating a stronger buffer for accelerated replacement cycles.

*Action: Launch the tiered structure immediately to segment the market and protect margins.*
*Monitor: Track actual drone maintenance costs monthly to refine the depreciation model.*
