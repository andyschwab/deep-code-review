Your current capital structure is unsustainable and will push you into negative cash flow well before reaching 150 subscribers. The hardware subsidy model creates a fatal timing mismatch where upfront costs outpace recurring revenue accumulation, regardless of the debt instrument used.

**Cash Flow Analysis at 150 Subscribers**

*   **Monthly Revenue:** 150 members × $99 = $14,850
*   **Monthly Operating Expenses:** $35,000
*   **Monthly Debt Service:** $250,000 loan at 12% annual interest requires ~$2,500/month in interest-only payments (principal repayment would be significantly higher).
*   **Total Monthly Outflow:** $37,500+
*   **Net Monthly Cash Flow:** -$22,650

At 150 subscribers, you are generating only 42% of the cash required to cover operating expenses and debt service. You would burn through your initial capital and the loan proceeds in approximately 8–9 months, failing to reach the 12-month horizon required to recoup hardware costs. The venture debt does not solve the unit economics; it merely delays the insolvency while adding interest expense.

**The Core Problem: Unit Economics vs. Cash Flow**
Your concern about cash flow is secondary to a broken unit economic model. You are spending $800 to acquire a customer who pays you $50 upfront and $99/month. Even ignoring operating expenses, it takes 8 months of pure subscription revenue just to break even on the hardware cost per unit ($750 net cost / $99 mo). When you layer in $35,000 in fixed monthly operating costs, you need roughly 354 subscribers just to cover operations, ignoring hardware repayment entirely. The subsidy model forces you to scale hardware purchases faster than revenue can support, creating a "growth trap" where every new customer initially worsens your cash position.

**Recommendation: Pivot to Bring-Your-Own-Device (BYOD)**
Immediately abandon the hardware subsidy model. Switch to a BYOD model where members bring their own headsets or rent hardware hourly at the studio without a long-term subsidy commitment.

1.  **Eliminate Upfront CapEx:** This removes the $160,000 hardware outlay and the need for $250,000 in debt.
2.  **Improve Margins:** Charge a premium membership fee (e.g., $129–$149/month) for access to guided content and studio space, since you are no longer financing hardware.
3.  **Hybrid Rental Option:** Offer high-end headset rentals at $20–$30 per session for users without equipment, generating immediate cash flow rather than deferred revenue.

This pivot aligns your cost structure with your revenue model, allowing you to become cash-flow positive with far fewer subscribers and removing the risk of aggressive debt service compounding your burn rate.

The math indicates you would run out of cash before hitting 100 subscribers under the current plan.
Do not take the venture debt; it funds a model that fails even with zero interest.
