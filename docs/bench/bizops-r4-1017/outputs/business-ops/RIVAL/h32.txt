### Financial Impact Analysis

Your insurance premium increased by **$18,000** ($48k - $30k). With 8,000 coins processed, this represents an additional **$2.25 per coin** in fixed costs.

**Current Unit Economics (Per Coin):**
*   Revenue: $75.00
*   Variable Costs: $40.00
*   Old Insurance Allocation: $3.75 ($30k / 8k)
*   New Insurance Allocation: $6.00 ($48k / 8k)
*   **New Net Margin:** $29.00 (down from $31.25)

**Verdict:** The premium is **not unsustainable**. While the 60% premium hike is alarming, it only reduces your net margin by roughly 7%. Your business remains highly profitable with a ~39% net margin per unit. A price increase is not strictly necessary for survival, but a tiered structure is advisable for risk management.

### Strategic Recommendation: Implement Value-Based Tiers

Do not raise prices across the board. A flat $75 fee implies equal risk and value for a $50 common coin and a $50,000 rarity, which is economically inefficient. You should restructure pricing to align fees with liability exposure.

**Proposed Tier Structure:**

1.  **Standard Tier (Value ≤ $2,500): $75**
    *   Maintain current pricing. This covers the bulk of volume and absorbs the new insurance cost comfortably.
    *   *Target:* Modern bullion, common date coins.

2.  **Premium Tier (Value $2,501 – $10,000): $125**
    *   Includes enhanced verification steps and higher declared value coverage.
    *   *Rationale:* Customers submitting higher-value items are less price-sensitive and require greater assurance. The extra $50 creates a reserve fund specifically for potential claims on these items.

3.  **Elite Tier (Value > $10,000): Custom Quote (Min $250)**
    *   Requires pre-authorization and specific underwriting for the individual item.
    *   *Rationale:* Misgrading a $50,000 coin carries catastrophic liability. These items should not be processed under a standard flat fee. This tier ensures the fee directly correlates to the risk taken.

### Addressing Your Specific Questions

*   **Is the insurance too high?** No. At 8% of revenue ($48k / $600k), it is within reasonable bounds for a high-liability authentication business, even if it feels steep compared to last year.
*   **Should you introduce a premium tier?** Yes, but primarily for **risk segmentation**, not just cost recovery. Charging more for high-value coins protects you from a single error wiping out a year's profit.
*   **Raise prices, reduce coverage, or accept lower margins?**
    *   **Accept the lower margin** on low-value coins (the $2.25 impact is manageable).
    *   **Raise prices** selectively on high-value coins via the new tiers.
    *   **Do not reduce coverage**; your brand reputation relies on the guarantee your insurance backs.

### Implementation Note
Communicate the new tiers as "Enhanced Protection Levels" rather than a price hike. Frame the higher fees for valuable coins as a necessary step to maintain the industry's highest standard of guarantee and security for collector assets.

*Note: Consult your insurance broker to see if higher deductibles on low-value claims can lower the premium before passing costs to customers.*
