### Cash Flow Analysis: Installment Plan Risk Assessment

**Current Model (50% Deposit)**
*   **Inflow per order:** $225 upfront, $225 on delivery.
*   **Outflow per order:** $450 (paid to supplier 14 days before delivery).
*   **Cash Position:** You collect $225 initially. You must pay $450 later. Without using the second customer payment or reserves, you are **-$225 negative per suit** before the final balance arrives.
*   **Liquidity Reality:** Even your current model requires you to float the supplier cost. You rely on the *second* payment arriving before the supplier deadline, or you use reserves/working capital to bridge the 14-day gap.

**Proposed Model (4 Installments of $112.50)**
Assuming a standard 4-week production/delivery cycle for a "typical month" with 20 new orders:

*   **Week 1 (Order Placement):**
    *   Inflow: $112.50 × 20 orders = **$2,250**
    *   Outflow: $0
    *   *Net Cash:* +$2,250

*   **Week 2 (Payment 2):**
    *   Inflow: $112.50 × 20 = **$2,250**
    *   Outflow: $0
    *   *Net Cash:* +$4,500 (Cumulative)

*   **Week 3 (Payment 3 + Supplier Deadline):**
    *   Inflow: $112.50 × 20 = **$2,250**
    *   Outflow: $450 × 20 orders = **$9,000** (Due 14 days after order, i.e., start of Week 3)
    *   *Net Cash:* $4,500 (prev) + $2,250 (in) - $9,000 (out) = **-$2,250 Cumulative**

*   **Week 4 (Final Payment & Delivery):**
    *   Inflow: $112.50 × 20 = **$2,250**
    *   Outflow: $0
    *   *Net Cash:* -$2,250 + $2,250 = **$0**

**The Verdict: High Risk of Liquidity Crunch**
Under the installment plan, you will face a **negative cash flow of $2,250** in Week 3 for every 20 orders processed. While this specific deficit fits within your $20,000 reserve, the risk compounds if:
1.  **Volume Increases:** If you scale to 60 orders (your current pipeline) in a single month, the Week 3 deficit triples to **-$6,750**.
2.  **Delays Occur:** If customers miss Week 2 or Week 3 payments, you must cover the full $9,000 supplier bill immediately. With only ~$3,375 collected by Week 3 (from on-time payers), you would need to pull **$5,625** from reserves for just 20 orders.
3.  **Cycle Mismatch:** If production takes longer than 4 weeks, the supplier payment (fixed at 14 days pre-delivery) might shift earlier relative to your installment collection, deepening the hole.

**Conclusion:** The installment plan delays your cash inflow such that you **cannot fund the supplier payment solely from customer receipts** before the deadline. You will be forced to dip into your $20,000 reserve every single month to cover the gap between the 3rd installment and the supplier deadline. While the reserve can absorb this for now, it turns your cash flow structurally negative during production, leaving you vulnerable if order volume spikes or customers default.

*Recommendation: Retain the 50% deposit model or require 75% upfront to ensure supplier bills are covered by customer funds before the 14-day deadline.*
