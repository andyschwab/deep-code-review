### CodeGolden Financial Model & LTV Analysis

**Current Unit Economics (Single Transaction Only)**
*   **Revenue per Student:** $1,200
*   **Acquisition Cost (CAC):** $450
*   **Contribution Margin:** $750 ($1,200 - $450)
*   **Monthly Volume:** 200 students
*   **Total Monthly Contribution:** $150,000 (200 × $750)
*   **Operating Expenses:** $95,000
*   **Net Monthly Profit:** $55,000

**Verdict:** Your current model is **profitable and sustainable**. You are generating $1.58 in contribution margin for every $1.00 spent on acquisition. You do not need to lower marketing costs to remain profitable; in fact, you have room to increase CAC to grow faster.

**Lifetime Value (LTV) Calculation Strategy**
In a low-repeat business like yours, traditional LTV (Price × Frequency × Lifespan) is misleading. Instead, use **Adjusted LTV**, which combines the single transaction margin with the monetary value of referrals.

**Referral Value Calculation**
*   **Referral Rate:** 15% (0.15 referrals per student)
*   **Value per Referral:** $750 (The contribution margin of one new student)
*   **Referral Value per Student:** $112.50 (0.15 × $750)

**Adjusted LTV per Student**
*   **Direct Margin:** $750
*   **Referral Margin:** $112.50
*   **Total Adjusted LTV:** **$862.50**

**Sustainability Analysis with Referrals**
When factoring in referrals, your effective acquisition cost drops because every 100 students you pay to acquire generate an additional 15 students for "free."

*   **Effective CAC:** $391 ($450 spent / 1.15 total students acquired)
*   **New Margin Ratio:** 3.07x ($1,200 revenue vs. $391 effective CAC)

**Strategic Recommendations**
1.  **Do not focus on repeat purchases:** Your data shows seniors take one course. Stop trying to force a SaaS-like retention model. Optimize the single experience instead.
2.  **Formalize the Referral Loop:** Since 15% of your value comes from word-of-mouth, incentivize it. Offering a $100 "Thank You" gift card to a referrer still leaves you with $662.50 margin, which is higher than your current organic average.
3.  **Aggressive Growth:** With a net profit of $55,000/month and a healthy margin, you can afford to increase your CAC. If you raised CAC to $600, you would still break even on the first transaction ($600 cost vs. $750 margin), making your growth essentially free while referrals become pure profit.

Your business is healthy. The lack of repeat purchases is not a flaw; it is a characteristic of your specific demographic and product. Focus on maximizing the margin of the first sale and systematizing referrals.

*Note: This model assumes operating expenses remain fixed at $95k; if scaling student volume requires proportional instructor increases, recalculate the contribution margin accordingly.*
*Ensure referral tracking is manual or automated to accurately measure if the 15% rate holds as you scale.*
