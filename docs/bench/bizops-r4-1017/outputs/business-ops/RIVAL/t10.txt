**Compliance Risks and Estimated Tax Liabilities**

By failing to collect VAT at the point of sale for a 3,000-attendee international event, ScholarStream faces severe compliance risks. In the EU, the "place of supply" for digital services and event tickets sold to individuals (B2C) is generally the customer's location. This means you are liable for VAT in every country where an attendee resides, not just where your company is incorporated.

**1. Immediate Registration Thresholds**
You likely do not need to worry about the standard €10,000 EU-wide threshold for *distance sales of goods*, as event tickets often fall under specific "electronically supplied services" or "admission to events" rules which can trigger immediate liability upon the first sale to a consumer in a member state, depending on specific national interpretations post-2021 VAT e-commerce package. However, many countries have specific thresholds for non-resident suppliers.
*   **Germany & France:** Both countries have strict enforcement. While some thresholds exist for small businesses, a single event generating ~€450,000 in gross ticket sales (3,000 attendees × $150) with significant portions from these countries will almost certainly exceed any de minimis thresholds for immediate VAT registration and remittance.
*   **Risk:** Operating without registration when liable is considered tax evasion. Penalties include back taxes, interest (often compounded daily), and fines ranging from 10% to 200% of the evaded tax, depending on the jurisdiction and intent.

**2. Estimated Tax Liabilities**
Assuming an average ticket price of $150 (approx. €140) and a conservative estimate that 40% of attendees (1,200 people) are from high-VAT EU nations (e.g., Germany 19%, France 20%, Italy 22%):
*   **Gross Revenue from EU:** 1,200 × €140 = €168,000.
*   **Estimated VAT Liability:** If the average VAT rate is 20%, the embedded VAT is roughly €28,000 (calculated as Gross × [Rate / (1+Rate)]).
*   **Your Fee:** Your 10% fee (€16,800) does not shield you; you are liable for the tax on the *full ticket price* if you are deemed the organizer/supplier of the service. If you are merely a marketplace agent, rules differ, but you must explicitly state this and ensure the actual organizer is compliant. If ScholarStream is the merchant of record, you owe the full amount.

**3. Manual Post-Event Handling**
Handling this manually post-event is **not viable** without incurring penalties.
*   **Legal Requirement:** VAT must be collected *at the time of supply* (ticket sale). Charging it later is often illegal and practically impossible without contacting thousands of individuals across 40 countries to request additional payment.
*   **Penalties:** Attempting to remit after the fact without prior registration usually triggers audits and higher penalty tiers for "failure to collect."

**Recommendation: Do Not Delay, But Integrate Immediately**
Do not delay ticket sales if the event is imminent, as delays may cause reputational damage and loss of early-bird revenue. However, you **must** integrate a tax engine (e.g., Quaderno, TaxJar, Avalara) or a payment processor with built-in VAT handling (e.g., Stripe Tax, Paddle) before selling another ticket.

These tools automatically:
1.  Determine the attendee's location via IP/billing address.
2.  Calculate the correct VAT rate.
3.  Collect the tax at checkout.
4.  Generate reports for filing.

Trying to manually calculate, register, and file in 40+ jurisdictions is operationally impossible and financially dangerous. The cost of a tax engine is negligible compared to the potential €28,000+ in back taxes and fines.

*Note: This analysis assumes ScholarStream is the merchant of record. If you are purely an agent, verify your contract terms immediately.*
