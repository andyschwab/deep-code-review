### Cash Flow Runway Model: ArchiVault

**Current Financial Position**
*   **Cash on Hand:** $150,000
*   **Monthly Burn:** $18,000
*   **Baseline Runway:** 8.3 months ($150k / $18k)

**The Enterprise Contract Variables**
*   **Total Contract Value (TCV):** $75,000 (3 clients × $25k/year)
*   **Monthly Recurring Revenue (MRR) Impact:** +$6,250 ($75k / 12 months)
*   **Payment Terms:** 90 days (3 months) delayed from invoice date.

#### Scenario A: Baseline (Net-15 Terms)
*Assumption: Clients pay within 15 days, effectively counting as current month revenue for cash flow purposes.*

1.  **New Monthly Burn:** $18,000 (operating) - $6,250 (new revenue) = **$11,750 net burn**.
2.  **Runway Calculation:** $150,000 / $11,750 = **12.8 months**.
3.  **Outcome:** You survive comfortably without immediate fundraising. The new contracts extend your runway by over 4 months immediately.

#### Scenario B: 90-Day Payment Terms
*Assumption: You deliver service Month 1, but receive $0 cash until the end of Month 4 (90 days later). Once payments start, they arrive monthly.*

**The Cash Gap Period (Months 1–3):**
*   **Cash In:** $0 (from new clients).
*   **Cash Out:** $18,000/month (standard burn).
*   **Total Burn over 3 months:** $54,000.
*   **Remaining Cash after Month 3:** $150,000 - $54,000 = **$96,000**.

**The Stabilization Period (Month 4 onwards):**
*   At the start of Month 4, you receive the first 90-days-worth of accrued payments.
    *   *Accrued Revenue:* $6,250 × 3 months = $18,750.
*   From Month 4 forward, you receive $6,250/month regularly.
*   **New Net Burn (Month 4+):** $18,000 - $6,250 = **$11,750**.
*   **Runway from Remaining Cash:** $96,000 / $11,750 = **8.2 months**.

**Total Runway Calculation (Scenario B):**
*   3 months (gap period) + 8.2 months (stabilized period) = **11.2 months total**.

#### Comparative Analysis & Recommendation

| Metric | Baseline (Net-15) | 90-Day Terms | Variance |
| :--- | :--- | :--- | :--- |
| **Immediate Cash Impact** | Positive (Month 1) | Negative (3-month delay) | -$54,000 liquidity hit |
| **Total Runway** | 12.8 Months | 11.2 Months | -1.6 Months |
| **Cash at Month 3** | ~$114,750 | $96,000 | -$18,750 |
| **Risk Level** | Low | Moderate | |

**Do you need a bridge round immediately?**
**No.** Even with the 90-day delay, you retain **11.2 months** of runway. This exceeds your current 8-month baseline expectation under the old model (which didn't account for the new revenue yet). The delayed terms reduce your runway by less than two months compared to the ideal scenario.

**Do you need to factor invoices?**
**Likely No.** Factoring typically costs 1.5%–3% per month. To bridge the $54,000 gap, you would pay roughly $2,500–$5,000 in fees to access cash 3 months early. Since you are not facing an immediate cash crunch (you still have nearly a year of life), paying this premium reduces your long-term capital efficiency without solving a survival threat.

**Strategic Note:**
While you can survive without debt, your cash buffer shrinks from ~$115k to ~$96k by Month 3. If your sales cycle slows or unexpected costs arise, this buffer is thinner. Monitor cash weekly. If you sign *another* enterprise client on 90-day terms immediately, the cumulative gap may force a small credit line or bridge round. For now, hold cash tight and avoid factoring fees.
