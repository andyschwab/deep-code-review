### Cash Flow Analysis for GreenSuds Refillery Growth

**Current State (200 Subscribers)**
*   **Monthly Revenue:** $7,000 (200 × $35)
*   **Monthly COGS:** $2,800 (200 × $14)
*   **Gross Profit:** $4,200
*   **Inventory Lead Time Cost:** You must fund 2 weeks of inventory upfront. Since COGS is monthly, 2 weeks of COGS equals **$1,400**.
*   **Cash Gap:** Your credit card processor holds funds for 3 days, but you pay suppliers Net-30. However, you must *purchase* the goods 2 weeks before shipping.
    *   You spend $1,400 two weeks before shipping.
    *   You ship and invoice.
    *   You receive cash 3 days later.
    *   You pay the supplier 30 days after the invoice date (which is roughly the shipping date).
    *   **Result:** You actually have a **positive cash flow cycle** on the supplier side. You collect money from customers ~33 days before you have to pay the supplier (3 days clearance + 30 days net terms - 14 days lead time = ~19 days float). The $1,400 upfront cost is immediately reimbursed by customer payments before the supplier invoice is due.

**Growth State (500 Subscribers)**
*   **Monthly Revenue:** $17,500 (500 × $35)
*   **Monthly COGS:** $7,000 (500 × $14)
*   **Inventory Lead Time Cost:** 2 weeks of COGS = **$3,500**.
*   **Cash Dynamics:** The timing mechanics remain identical. You collect ~$17,500 roughly 19 days before the $7,000 supplier bill is due.

**Working Capital Assessment**
*   **Current Cash:** $8,000.
*   **Maximum Upfront Inventory Need (Month 1 of Growth):** To jump from 200 to 500 subscribers instantly, you need to buy inventory for the additional 300 users immediately.
    *   Additional COGS needed: 300 × $14 = $4,200.
    *   Since you are already funding the base 200, the *incremental* cash needed to start the growth month is **$4,200**.
*   **Safety Margin:** $8,000 (cash) - $4,200 (incremental inventory) = **$3,800 remaining**.

**Conclusion**
**You do not need a line of credit.** Your current cash position of $8,000 is sufficient to fund the growth to 500 subscribers.

The fear of a "cash gap" is unfounded in this specific scenario because your customer payment speed (3 days) combined with supplier terms (Net-30) creates a ~19-day float where you hold the customer's money before paying the supplier. Even if you grow instantly, the maximum incremental cash required upfront is $4,200, leaving you with a healthy $3,800 buffer for operational expenses or unexpected delays. The subscription model, in this case, acts as a source of working capital rather than a drain.

*Note: This analysis assumes all 500 subscribers pay at the start of the cycle and churn is negligible during the transition. Monitor your actual clearance times closely.*
