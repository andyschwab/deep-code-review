### PawsOnWheels Profitability Model & Strategic Analysis

**Current Financial Baseline (Per Van/Month)**
*   **Revenue:** 12 jobs/day × 22 days × $85 = $22,440
*   **Variable Costs (excl. fuel):** 264 jobs × $22 = $5,808
*   **Fuel Costs:** $1,200
*   **Total Variable Costs:** $7,008
*   **Contribution Margin:** $15,432
*   *Note: Fixed costs (labor, van lease, insurance) across 6 vans are estimated at $74,592/month based on your $18,000 total net profit.*

**Scenario: Diesel at $4.00/gallon (+29% increase)**
*   **New Fuel Cost:** $1,200 × 1.29 = $1,548 per van/month
*   **New Fleet Fuel Cost:** $9,288 (up from $7,200)
*   **Impact:** Monthly net profit drops from $18,000 to **$15,912** (a 11.6% decrease).
*   **Annual Impact:** If unaddressed, you lose **$25,056** in annual profit.

**Volume Required to Offset Cost Increase (Without Price Hike)**
To cover the additional $348/month in fuel costs per van solely through volume:
*   **Contribution Margin per Job:** $85 - $22 = $63 (fuel is treated as a separate line item here for clarity, but effectively the net gain per job must cover the fuel hike).
*   *Correction:* The $63 margin above does not include fuel. The true margin per job at $3.10/gal is $63 - ($1,200/264 jobs) = $58.45.
*   At $4.00/gal, fuel cost per job rises to $5.86. New margin per job = $85 - $22 - $5.86 = **$57.14**.
*   **Shortfall per van:** $348.
*   **Extra Jobs Needed:** $348 / $57.14 = **6.1 jobs per month**.
*   **Daily Increase:** 6.1 jobs / 22 days = **0.28 jobs per day**.
*   **New Daily Target:** ~12.3 jobs per van.

**Feasibility Assessment:**
Increasing from 12 to 12.3 jobs daily is **highly realistic**. It represents less than one extra appointment every three days. Given current scheduling density, this can likely be achieved by reducing transition time between appointments rather than finding new customers.

**Strategic Recommendations**

1.  **Introduce a Fuel Surcharge (Recommended):**
    Implement a transparent $3.50 "Fuel Stability Fee" per appointment.
    *   **Math:** $3.50 × 264 jobs = $924/month revenue.
    *   **Result:** This not only covers the $348 increase but adds $576 to profit per van. It is less disruptive than changing operations and avoids the psychological friction of raising the base $85 price.

2.  **Optimize Routing (Immediate Action):**
    Before changing your business model, audit your routing. A 10% reduction in miles driven offsets the entire price increase. Cluster appointments by neighborhood strictly, even if it means slightly longer gaps between slots.

3.  **Avoid Hub-and-Spoke for Now:**
    Switching to a drop-off model fundamentally changes your value proposition (convenience). The cost of leasing a central location, staffing it, and the likely churn of customers who value mobile convenience outweighs the $348/month fuel risk. Only pursue this if fuel exceeds $5.50/gallon.

4.  **Service Radius Reduction:**
    Do not reduce your radius yet. The volume required to offset costs is negligible (0.28 jobs/day). Shrinking your radius limits growth potential unnecessarily.

**Conclusion:**
Do not alter your operational model. The risk is manageable. Implement a small, line-item fuel surcharge immediately to protect margins and potentially increase profit, while optimizing routes to absorb any customer resistance to the fee.

*Calculations assume 22 working days per month. Labor and fixed overheads remain constant in this model.*
