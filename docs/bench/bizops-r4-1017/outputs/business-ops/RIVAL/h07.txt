### Proposed Pricing Mechanism: The Raw Material Surcharge (RMS) Model

To protect your 12% net margin against 20% monthly raw material volatility without alienating long-term clients, you should decouple your base price from your variable costs. Instead of changing the headline price of $800/drum constantly, implement a **Raw Material Surcharge (RMS)** line item on every invoice.

**How it works:**
1.  **Establish a Base Price:** Set a fixed base price (e.g., $750) that covers your stable costs (labor, overhead, logistics) and your target profit margin.
2.  **Define the Index:** Select a transparent, public industry index for your primary raw material (e.g., a specific chemical commodity index from ICIS, Platts, or a major futures exchange). Do not use your internal purchase orders, as clients will distrust them.
3.  **Set a Baseline Index Value:** Lock in the index value at the time of your current $800 pricing as the "zero point" for the surcharge.
4.  **Calculate the Monthly Surcharge:**
    *   Formula: `(Current Index Value - Baseline Index Value) × Volume Factor`
    *   The Volume Factor converts the index unit price into the cost per 55-gallon drum based on your formulation.
5.  **Implementation Cadence:** Publish the surcharge percentage or dollar amount on the 1st of every month. This gives clients 30 days of visibility before rates apply to new orders.

**Why this protects you:**
With raw materials comprising 60% of your COGS, a 20% spike in material costs increases your total COGS by 12%. Since your net margin is only 12%, this spike wipes out profitability entirely. The RMS ensures that 100% of material inflation is passed through directly to the customer, preserving your margin dollar-for-dollar.

**Client Stability Strategy:**
Long-term clients fear unpredictability, not necessarily higher costs. By keeping the base price stable and making the surcharge a transparent, formulaic pass-through, you shift the conversation from "ChemClean is raising prices" to "The market cost of materials increased." You can further ease adoption by offering a **collar mechanism**: you absorb the first 5% of material fluctuation (sharing the risk), and the surcharge only triggers once volatility exceeds that threshold. This demonstrates partnership while capping your exposure.

### Texas Commercial Code Considerations

Regarding your question on Texas law: The Texas Business and Commerce Code (UCC) generally upholds freedom of contract in B2B transactions, allowing for price adjustment clauses provided they are **conspicuous** and **mutually agreed upon**.

However, enforceability hinges on two critical factors:
1.  **Conspicuousness:** Under Texas law (and the UCC §2.209), modification clauses or variable pricing terms must be clear and unmistakable. Buried fine print may be deemed unenforceable. The mechanism for calculating the price change must be defined objectively in the original contract; a clause allowing you to change prices "at your discretion" without an objective index is likely voidable as illusory.
2.  **Good Faith:** All contracts under the Texas UCC impose a duty of good faith. Your chosen index must be a legitimate market standard. If the index chosen artificially inflates costs compared to the actual market, a court could rule the clause was executed in bad faith.
