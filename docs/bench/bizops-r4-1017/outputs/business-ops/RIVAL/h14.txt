### Risk Evaluation: AlphaSignal Price Increase ($49 → $79)

**Executive Summary**
The proposed price increase carries **extreme risk** to your total revenue model. While the hike improves subscription margins, it threatens the sponsorship revenue that covers 40% of your operating costs. Because your high-value users (Portfolio Managers) are only 15% of your base but likely drive 100% of your sponsorship value, losing even a small fraction of them creates a deficit that increased subscription fees from the remaining mass market cannot easily offset.

**Current Financial Baseline (Monthly)**
*   **Subscription Revenue:** 3,200 users × $49 = **$156,800**
*   **Required Sponsorship Revenue:** $85,000 (OpEx) × 40% = **$34,000**
*   **Total Current Revenue:** $190,800
*   **High-Value User Count:** 3,200 × 15% = **480 users**

**Scenario Analysis: The "20% Churn" Fear**
If the price hike causes 20% of your high-value users (96 people) to cancel, and we assume a conservative 5% churn from the remaining base due to general price sensitivity:

1.  **New Subscriber Count:** ~2,950 users
2.  **New Subscription Revenue:** 2,950 × $79 = **$233,050**
3.  **Subscription Gain:** +$76,250 vs. baseline.
4.  **Sponsorship Risk:** If sponsors require a minimum threshold of high-value users (e.g., 400 users) and you drop to 384, you risk losing the entire **$34,000** sponsorship contract, not just a prorated amount.
5.  **Net Result:** $233,050 (Sub) + $0 (Sponsor) = **$233,050**.
    *   *Comparison:* This is only **$42,250** higher than your current total revenue ($190,800), representing a **22% total revenue gain** while taking on massive risk of total sponsorship loss.

**The Break-Even Model**
To determine when this becomes net-negative, we must find the point where **Lost Sponsorship Value > Gained Subscription Revenue**.

Let $X$ be the number of high-value users lost.
Let $Y$ be the number of standard users lost (assume 5% of 2,720 = 136 users).
Total Users Lost = $X + 136$.
Remaining Users = $3,200 - (X + 136)$.

*   **New Sub Revenue:** $(2,864 - X) \times \$79$
*   **Old Sub Revenue:** $\$156,800$
*   **Subscription Gain:** $[(2,864 - X) \times 79] - 156,800$
*   **Sponsorship Loss:** If $480 - X <$ Threshold, Loss = $\$34,000$.

**The Tipping Point:**
If the sponsorship deal is binary (all or nothing based on a threshold):
You need the Subscription Gain to exceed $34,000 to justify losing the sponsorship.
Max possible Subscription Gain (if 0 high-value users leave) = $(2,864 \times 79) - 156,800 = \$69,456$.
Since $69,456 > $34,000, the math *only* works if you retain enough high-value users to keep the sponsorship active.

**Critical Threshold:**
If the sponsor requires **400 high-value users** (approx. 12.5% of current base):
*   You can afford to lose only **80 high-value users** (16.6% of that segment).
*   If churn in this segment exceeds **16.6%**, you lose the $34,000 sponsorship.
*   At 17% churn (82 users lost):
    *   Sub Revenue: ~$226,000 (Gain of ~$69k)
    *   Sponsor Revenue: $0
    *   Total: $226,000.
    *   *Wait, this is still higher than baseline ($190k).*

**Correction on "Net-Negative":**
The move is only "net-negative" compared to your *current* total revenue if you lose **more than 43% of your entire user base** or if the sponsorship loss is catastrophic (e.g., reputational damage kills future deals).

However, the **real risk is not total revenue decline, but margin volatility and valuation impact.**
1.  **Valuation Hit:** Series A investors value recurring, diversified revenue. Swapping stable sponsorship (contracted) for volatile subscription churn lowers your multiple.
2.  **The "Cliff" Risk:** If sponsors have a hard floor (e.g., "Must have 450 PMs"), losing just 31 users (6.5% of the segment) wipes out $34,000/month.
    *   Gain from price hike on remaining users: ~$70k.
    *   Loss of sponsorship: $34k.
    *   Net Gain: $36k.
    *   *But:* You have now increased your churn rate significantly and alienated your core persona.

**Recommendation**
Do not implement a blanket price increase. The risk of triggering a sponsorship cliff outweighs the marginal gain.
1.  **Segmented Pricing:** Raise prices to $79 only for the 85% non-PM users (if they are less price sensitive) or offer a "Premium" tier at $79 with extra features, keeping the core at $49 for PMs.
2.  **Grandfathering:** Lock existing PMs at $49 for 12 months to guarantee the sponsor threshold, while charging new users $79 immediately.
3.  **Validate the Threshold:** Confirm the exact "minimum user count" clause in your sponsorship contracts before moving a single digit.

*Note: This model assumes sponsorship revenue is binary (all-or-nothing). If sponsors pay prorated rates based on exact user counts, the risk is lower but the complexity higher.*
*Action: Interview your top 20 PM subscribers immediately to gauge price elasticity before Q3.*
