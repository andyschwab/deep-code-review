### Strategic Analysis: LinguaLink Commission Structure

Your concern is valid: a 20% commission on a $25 transaction creates a $5 friction point that is easy for tutors and students to arbitrage once trust is established. The 15% decline in repeat bookings among top tutors is a leading indicator of platform leakage. However, simply slashing your rate to 10% is a dangerous assumption that may destroy revenue without solving the root cause.

**The Math of the "Lower Rate" Bet**
If you drop to 10%, your revenue per lesson halves immediately. To maintain your current $42,000 monthly revenue, you would need to **double your total transaction volume**.
*   **Current State:** $210,000 GMV × 20% = $42,000 Revenue.
*   **10% Scenario:** You need $420,000 GMV to hit $42,000 Revenue.

Given you already have 3,500 active students, doubling GMV requires either acquiring 3,500 *new* students immediately or convincing every existing student to book twice as many hours. This is unlikely to happen organically just because the fee dropped. Furthermore, lowering the price signal might inadvertently devalue the quality of tutors on your platform.

**The Real Problem: Value vs. Fee Timing**
Tutors do not leave because the fee is 20%; they leave because the **value provided decreases over time**.
*   **First Lesson:** The platform provides massive value (marketing, payment processing, trust, scheduling). The 20% fee is justified as a Customer Acquisition Cost (CAC).
*   **Repeat Lessons:** The platform provides only utility (scheduling/payments). The 20% fee now feels like a "tax" on an existing relationship.

**Recommended Strategy: Hybrid or Tiered Model**
Do not flat-rate cut to 10%. Instead, align your pricing with the value lifecycle to stop leakage while protecting revenue.

1.  **Implement a "Decay" Commission Model:**
    *   Charge 20% on the first 4 lessons with a specific student (acquisition phase).
    *   Drop to 10-12% for all subsequent lessons with that same student (retention phase).
    *   *Why:* This acknowledges the tutor's effort in retaining the student while ensuring you get paid for the lead generation. It removes the financial incentive to go off-platform for long-term students.

2.  **Cap the Fee Per Student:**
    *   Charge 20% until the tutor has earned $X (e.g., $200) from a specific student, then switch to a flat low fee or 5% thereafter.

3.  **Justify the 20% with "Stickiness" (If keeping flat rate):**
    *   If you refuse to lower rates, you must make off-platform migration painful. Integrate mandatory tools that tutors lose if they leave: automated curriculum tracking, progress reports for parents, or escrowed dispute protection. If the only value you offer is introductions, 20% is unsustainable for a mature marketplace.

**Conclusion**
Lowering to a flat 10% is likely to reduce your total revenue unless you have a guaranteed plan to double volume. The elasticity threshold isn't a single number; it's a curve based on relationship maturity. Adopt a **tiered commission structure** that rewards retention. This captures the high value of new leads while reducing the "tax" on repeat business, directly addressing the leakage driver without sacrificing your baseline revenue.

*Note: Implement tracking to measure "off-platform migration" by surveying tutors on why they churn, as technical detection is difficult.*
*Consider running a A/B test with 10% of your top tutors on the new decay model before a full rollout.*
