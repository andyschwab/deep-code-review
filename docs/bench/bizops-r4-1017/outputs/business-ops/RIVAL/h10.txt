### Revenue Forecasting Model for LexiTranslate

To stabilize your cash flow projections despite unpredictable court schedules, shift from a "case count" forecast to a **Probability-Weighted Pipeline Model** combined with **Monte Carlo Simulation**.

**1. The Modeling Approach**
Do not rely on a single linear projection. Instead, build a spreadsheet model with three distinct inputs:

*   **Historical Seasonality Index:** Analyze your last 24 months of data to identify monthly multipliers. Legal depositions often dip in July/August and December, and spike in September/October and January. Calculate the average percentage deviation for each month compared to your annual average and apply these indices to your baseline.
*   **Weighted Pipeline Probability:** Categorize your known upcoming work into three buckets:
    *   *Confirmed (90-100% probability):* Scheduled depositions with signed orders.
    *   *Likely (50-75% probability):* Cases where discovery deadlines are approaching but dates aren't set.
    *   *Speculative (10-20% probability):* General retainer relationships or firms with high historical volume.
    *   *Calculation:* Multiply the estimated value of each bucket by its probability percentage to get a "Expected Value" for the month.
*   **Monte Carlo Simulation:** Since court backlogs create volatility, run a simulation (using Excel's Data Table or simple scripting) that generates 1,000 scenarios. Vary your case volume randomly within your historical min/max range (30–50 cases) for each month. This will provide a **confidence interval** (e.g., "There is an 80% chance revenue will be between $95k and $125k in March") rather than a single false-precision number.

**2. Cash Flow Projection Structure**
Create a 12-month rolling forecast with three rows per month:
*   *Conservative Case:* Uses the 20th percentile result from your simulation. Use this for fixed overhead planning ($25k) and translator payouts.
*   *Most Likely Case:* Uses the weighted pipeline average. Use this for operational staffing.
*   *Optimistic Case:* Uses the 80th percentile. Use this to plan line-of-credit paydowns or surplus investment.

**3. Illinois Legal Industry Payment Standards**
Regarding your cash conversion cycle, there is **no specific Illinois statute or universal court rule** mandating payment terms for private deposition translation vendors. Payment terms are purely contractual between your agency and the law firms or court reporting agencies you serve.

However, you can leverage the following industry norms to improve cash flow:
*   **Standard Terms:** The prevailing standard in Chicago for legal services is **Net 30**, though large firms often push for **Net 45 or Net 60**.
*   **Actionable Leverage:**
    *   **Retainer Agreements:** Shift top clients from per-project billing to a monthly retainer model based on estimated volume. This guarantees cash flow regardless of court cancellations.
    *   **Early Pay Discounts:** Offer a 2% discount for payment within 10 days (2/10 Net 30). Law firms have cash but are bureaucratic; this incentivizes their accounts payable team to prioritize your invoice.
    *   **Deposit Requirements:** For any single project exceeding $5,000 or for new clients without established credit, require a 50% deposit upfront before work begins. This is standard practice for specialized, urgent legal work and protects you against last-minute settlement cancellations.

By combining a probability-weighted forecast with stricter upfront deposit policies for large cases, you can reduce reliance on your line of credit and smooth out the volatility caused by court backlogs.

*Note: Consult with a local CPA to validate the Monte Carlo parameters against your specific tax liabilities. Consider automating invoice reminders to shave days off your average collection time.*
