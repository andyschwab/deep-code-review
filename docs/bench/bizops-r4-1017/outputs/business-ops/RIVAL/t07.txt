### Revenue Impact Analysis: Flat Fee vs. Usage-Based Pricing

**Current State (Flat Fee Model)**
*   **Monthly Recurring Revenue (MRR):** $50,000
*   **Client Count:** 10
*   **Price per Client:** $5,000/month
*   **Average Volume:** ~80,000 calls/client/month (implied by largest clients)
*   **Recession Scenario (20% volume drop):** Revenue remains **$50,000**.
    *   *Analysis:* Under a flat license, your revenue is decoupled from usage. Even if your clients' transaction volumes plummet by 20% due to the economic downturn, your contract value remains stable. This model currently acts as a hedge against your customers' volatility.

**Proposed State (Usage-Based Model)**
*   **Rate:** $0.05 per API call
*   **Current Implied Volume:** To match current revenue ($50,000), your total network volume is 1,000,000 calls/month ($50,000 / $0.05). This averages 100,000 calls/client, though you noted your *largest* are at 80,000. This suggests smaller clients are subsidizing the larger ones, or the $5k flat fee is currently a discount for your biggest users.
*   **Recession Scenario (20% volume drop):**
    *   New Total Volume: 800,000 calls (1,000,000 * 0.80)
    *   New MRR: 800,000 * $0.05 = **$40,000**
    *   **Revenue Impact:** Immediate **20% decrease** in your top-line revenue.

**Strategic Assessment**

Switching to usage-based pricing during a downturn presents a significant risk to your total contract value (TCV) and cash flow stability.

1.  **Revenue Volatility:** By moving to usage-based pricing, you are transferring the risk of the economic downturn from your customers to yourself. If banks cut IT budgets and transaction volumes drop 20%, your revenue drops 20% immediately. In the flat model, you absorb no penalty for their reduced activity.
2.  **The "Budget Cut" Paradox:** While you fear variable costs will deter signing, the reality is that a flat $5,000 fee is often easier for enterprise procurement to approve as a predictable line item than an uncapped variable cost. However, your concern is valid: if a bank expects volume to crash, they may prefer paying only for what they use. But if they expect volume to *recover* over a multi-year term, a flat fee locks in a discount for them, whereas usage-based captures their upside.
3.  **The Multi-Year Contract Risk:** You mentioned fearing variable costs will deter multi-year deals. This is accurate. Enterprise clients prefer predictability for long-term planning. A usage model makes forecasting difficult for them during volatile times. Conversely, offering a flat fee *with* a volume cap (e.g., $5,000 for up to 100k calls, then overage fees) might be the compromise.

**Conclusion**

Given a projected 20% drop in transaction volumes, switching to a pure usage-based model ($0.05/call) will likely **reduce your total revenue by approximately 20% ($10,000/month)** immediately.

The flat fee model currently provides a safer financial position for LegacyLink during a recession. It insulates you from your clients' downturns. If you must move to usage-based to lower the entry barrier for new, smaller clients, consider a **hybrid approach**: a lower base platform fee (e.g., $2,000) plus a usage rate, or committed use discounts where clients pre-pay for a volume bucket at a flat rate. Do not abandon the flat fee entirely for existing enterprise clients until the market stabilizes.

*Note: Your largest clients (80k calls) would pay $4,000 under the new model, a $1,000 reduction per client, which may encourage them to expand usage but reduces your immediate yield.*
*Consider running a Van Westendorp survey specifically on "predictability vs. flexibility" before finalizing the switch.*
