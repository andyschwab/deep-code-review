For the Horizon Documentary Fund, the industry standard to address lumpy, asynchronous revenue while ensuring fairness is a **European Waterfall** structure combined with a **Cumulative Preferred Return**.

Unlike the "American Waterfall," which distributes profits deal-by-deal (risking early investors subsidizing later failures), the European model aggregates all cash flows from your three films into a single pool before calculating investor returns. This ensures that no investor receives a profit distribution until the entire fund has returned its initial capital plus a predefined hurdle.

**Recommended Structure:**

1.  **Cumulative Preferred Return (The Hurdle):**
    Implement an annualized preferred return (typically 6–8% for documentary funds) that accrues on unreturned capital. Because your cash inflows are irregular, this must be **cumulative**. If a film generates no revenue in year one, the 8% obligation rolls over to year two. Investors do not receive this interest as cash immediately; it is added to their "unreturned capital" balance. This protects early capital without requiring you to hold idle cash artificially; the obligation simply grows until a deal closes.

2.  **The Distribution Waterfall Sequence:**
    When a territory sale occurs (e.g., UK rights sold for £150k), distribute the cash in this strict order:
    *   **Step 1: Return of Capital.** 100% of cash goes to investors pro-rata until every pound of the £2m principal is repaid. Because you use a cumulative model, you treat accrued preferred return as part of the "capital" bucket for calculation purposes until the principal is fully extinguished.
    *   **Step 2: Catch-Up.** Once total capital (principal + accrued preferred return) is returned, 100% of subsequent cash flows go to the investors until they have received the full amount of their accrued preferred return.
    *   **Step 3: Profit Split (Carried Interest).** Only after Steps 1 and 2 are fully satisfied do you split remaining profits between the investors and the fund manager (e.g., 80% to investors, 20% to Horizon as carried interest).

3.  **Handling Asynchronous Deals:**
    Since you are selling rights territory-by-territory, treat every inflow as a deposit into the central pool. Do not attribute specific sales to specific films for distribution purposes. If Film A sells early but Film B is delayed, the cash from Film A sits in the waterfall queue to pay down the aggregate capital balance. This prevents early investors from being paid out while the fund still has significant exposure on unfinished projects.

4.  **UK Tax Efficiency:**
    To avoid complex tax events for your UK angel investors, structure distributions strictly as **return of capital** first. In the UK, returning an investor's own capital is generally not a taxable event; it is simply a return of basis. Taxable income (capital gains or income tax) is only triggered once the investor has received more than their original contribution plus the accrued preferred return. By adhering to the strict waterfall sequence, you ensure that early cash inflows are classified as non-taxable capital returns, deferring tax liabilities until the fund is genuinely profitable. Avoid "interim profit" distributions, which can trigger premature tax bills and complicate self-assessment filings for your angels.

This approach balances fairness by ensuring all investors share the risk of the entire slate, while the cumulative hurdle protects their capital against the time-value erosion caused by delayed sales.

*Note: This framework assumes a standard Limited Partnership agreement; you must have UK legal counsel draft the specific LPA clauses to ensure compliance with FCA regulations.*
