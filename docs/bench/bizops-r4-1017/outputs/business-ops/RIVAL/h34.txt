### Current State (Year 1)
*   **Revenue:** 600 subscribers × $49 = **$29,400/mo**
*   **Variable Costs:** 600 subs × 4 washes/mo × $12 = **$28,800/mo**
    *(Assuming "weekly" equals ~4 washes/month)*
*   **Contribution Margin:** $29,400 - $28,800 = **$600/mo**
*   **Fixed Overhead:** **$25,000/mo**
*   **Net Profit:** $600 - $25,000 = **-$24,400/mo**

**Critical Finding:** You are not currently breaking even. With a $12 variable cost per wash and a $49 monthly price for unlimited weekly washes, your margin per customer is only $1. You are losing ~$24k monthly. The 92% retention masked this because you likely had significant upfront capital or are accounting for "break-even" differently (e.g., ignoring variable labor costs in your calculation).

### The Collapse Threshold
Your unit economics collapse immediately if the variable cost per wash exceeds $10.25 (assuming 4 washes/month).
*   **Break-even Variable Cost per Wash:** $49 price / 4 washes = $12.25 total allowable cost.
*   Since your cost is $12, you have almost no buffer.
*   **If customers wash 5 times/month:** Cost = $60. Loss = $11/customer.
*   **If customers wash 3 times/month:** Cost = $36. Margin = $13/customer.

### Framework to Model Viability
To determine if you need to raise prices or reduce frequency, use this logic:

1.  **Calculate Actual Usage:** Determine the *average* washes per subscriber per month. If it is >4.08, you are losing money on every active user before marketing.
2.  **Contribution Margin Formula:**
    `Margin = Price - (Avg Washes × $12)`
3.  **Sustainable Growth Equation:**
    `Profit = (Subscribers × Margin) - (Churn Rate × Subscribers × CAC) - Fixed Overhead`
4.  **Viability Test:**
    If `(Avg Washes × $12) > $49`, you must either:
    *   **Raise Price:** Increase to cover usage (e.g., $69/mo).
    *   **Cap Usage:** Change "Unlimited" to "4 washes/mo" with overage fees.
    *   **Reduce Cost:** Lower variable cost below $10/wash.

**Immediate Action:** Your current model is mathematically insolvent at 4+ washes/month regardless of retention. Retention only matters if the unit economics are positive. Focus first on capping usage or raising prices to achieve a minimum $20/customer margin.
