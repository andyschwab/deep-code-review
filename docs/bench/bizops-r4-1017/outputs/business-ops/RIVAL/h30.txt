### Revenue & Margin Analysis: Per Case vs. Per Hour

Based on your current volume of 400 cases/month and an average resolution time of 2.5 hours, here is the financial breakdown for your peak season.

**Current Model: Flat Fee ($200/case)**
*   **Revenue:** $80,000/month ($200 × 400 cases).
*   **Cost:** $60,000/month (400 cases × 2.5 hrs × $60/hr).
*   **Gross Margin:** $20,000/month (25% margin).
*   **Annualized Run Rate:** $240,000 revenue.

**Proposed Model: Hourly Billing (Assuming $120/hr to client)**
*   *Note: To maintain a healthy margin above your $60 cost, a standard 2x markup suggests charging $120/hr.*
*   **Revenue:** $120,000/month (400 cases × 2.5 hrs × $120/hr).
*   **Cost:** $60,000/month (unchanged variable cost).
*   **Gross Margin:** $60,000/month (50% margin).
*   **Annualized Run Rate:** $360,000 revenue.

**The "Complex Case" Risk (Flat Fee Flaw)**
Under the flat fee model, a case taking 5 hours costs you $300 in mediator fees but only generates $200 in revenue. You lose **$100 on every complex case**. If 20% of your cases (80 cases) run long (4+ hours), your monthly profit evaporates entirely, leaving you near break-even or in a loss position despite high volume.

**The Behavioral Variable (Hourly Model Risk)**
Your concern is valid: hourly billing may reduce the average case duration.
*   **Break-even Behavior Change:** If clients settle 33% faster (dropping average time from 2.5h to 1.67h) due to cost pressure, your hourly revenue drops to ~$80,000, matching your current flat-fee top line but with significantly higher margins (approx. 50% vs 25%).
*   **Volume Risk:** If hourly pricing discourages initiation and volume drops below 265 cases/month, you miss your $50k quarterly goal. However, given the high stakes of litigation, demand is likely inelastic to pricing structure compared to price point.

**Recommendation: Hybrid "Cap-and-Collar" Model**
To maximize revenue while mitigating behavioral risk and protecting margins, do not choose strictly between the two. Implement a **Hybrid Model**:

1.  **Base Fee:** Charge **$150** upfront (covers the first 1.5 hours). This lowers the barrier to entry compared to a pure hourly estimate.
2.  **Overtime Rate:** Charge **$110/hour** for every hour beyond 1.5.
3.  **Hard Cap:** Set a maximum fee of **$350** for standard disputes.

**Why this wins:**
*   **Protects Margins:** You stop losing money on 5-hour cases. Even at the cap, revenue ($350) far exceeds max cost ($300).
*   **Encourages Efficiency:** Clients are motivated to settle within the first 1.5 hours to avoid extra fees, potentially lowering your average cost per case.
*   **Psychological Safety:** The "Cap" removes the fear of unlimited hourly bills, reducing friction for new users.
*   **Revenue Upside:** Simple cases remain profitable; complex cases become highly profitable rather than loss leaders.

This approach secures your $50k quarterly goal with roughly 150 cases (vs. 250 needed currently) and eliminates the risk of mediators working at a loss.

*Implementation requires updating your intake forms to clearly display the cap to maintain trust.*
*Monitor the % of cases hitting the 1.5h threshold to refine the base fee later.*
