### Financial Outcome Model: Tiered Commission Structure

**Current Monthly Performance**
*   **Gross Merchandise Volume (GMV):** $2,000,000
*   **Luxury GMV (30% of total):** $600,000
*   **Standard GMV (70% of total):** $1,400,000
*   **Current Revenue (15% flat):** $300,000
*   **Fixed Costs:** $60,000
*   **Current Net Profit:** $240,000

**Projected Performance with Tiered Pricing**
*   **Luxury Volume Adjustment:** A 15% reduction in luxury listings reduces Luxury GMV from $600,000 to $510,000.
*   **Standard GMV:** Remains $1,400,000 (unchanged).
*   **New Total GMV:** $1,910,000.

**Revenue Calculation**
*   **Standard Revenue:** $1,400,000 × 15% = $210,000
*   **Luxury Revenue:** $510,000 × 25% = $127,500
*   **New Total Revenue:** $337,500

**Financial Impact Summary**
*   **Revenue Change:** +$37,500 per month (+12.5% increase).
*   **Net Profit Change:** Increases from $240,000 to $277,500.
*   **Support Efficiency:** While the model assumes a volume drop, the primary benefit is likely operational. Since luxury items drive 30% of support tickets but only 10% of volume, reducing this volume by 15% should disproportionately lower your support burden, potentially allowing you to reduce the $60,000 fixed cost line over time.

**Conclusion:** The tiered structure is financially accretive even with a significant drop in luxury volume. It increases monthly profit by $37,500 while likely improving operational efficiency by filtering out high-maintenance transactions.

**Legal Note on Oregon Consumer Fairness:**
Oregon's Unlawful Trade Practices Act (UTPA) generally prohibits deceptive or unconscionable practices, but disparate fee structures based on item value or risk (like authentication complexity) are typically permissible if clearly disclosed before the transaction. However, because this involves a marketplace altering terms for existing sellers, you must consult an Oregon attorney regarding contract modification notice periods and potential claims of "unconscionability" if the fee hike is applied retroactively or without sufficient consent mechanisms.
