**Financial Impact Analysis: Absorb vs. Pass Through Freight Costs**

Based on your current metrics (400+ farms, $85 AOV, 22% gross margin, $12k monthly burn), here is the projected annual impact of the 15% freight hike.

*Note: Exact freight cost per order was not provided; estimates below assume freight currently represents ~15% of your AOV ($12.75/order), a standard benchmark for small-package agricultural shipping. If your actual freight cost differs, scale these figures proportionally.*

**Option A: Absorb the Cost**
*   **Immediate Impact:** The 15% rate hike on an estimated $12.75 freight cost adds ~$1.91 to your cost per order.
*   **Margin Erosion:** Your current gross profit is $18.70 per order ($85 * 22%). Absorbing $1.91 reduces this to $16.79, a **10.2% drop in gross profit**.
*   **Annual Runway Effect:** Assuming consistent volume (approx. 4,700 orders/year based on $85 AOV and covering $12k burn + fixed costs), you would lose approximately **$9,000 in annual gross profit**.
*   **Cash Flow Consequence:** With only 6 months of cash remaining ($72,000 total), losing $9,000 annually accelerates your burn. You would likely exhaust your runway in **5.4 months** instead of 6, forcing a capital raise or drastic cost-cutting before the next planting season.
*   **Risk:** High. Wiping out net profit for the quarter endangers operational stability.

**Option B: Pass the Cost to Customers**
*   **Price Adjustment:** To cover the $1.91 increase, you would need to raise prices by roughly **$2.00 per order** (approx. 2.4% increase).
*   **Churn Projection:** Last year, a $5.00 increase (5.9%) caused 3% churn. Assuming linear sensitivity, a $2.00 increase might cause ~1% churn.
*   **Revenue Impact:**
    *   *Loss from Churn:* Losing 1% of 400 farms (4 farms) at $85/month AOV = ~$4,080 annual revenue loss.
    *   *Gain from Price Hike:* Remaining 396 farms paying $2 extra = ~$9,500 annual revenue gain.
    *   *Net Positive:* **+$5,420 annually** compared to current baseline.
*   **Cash Flow Consequence:** This option preserves your gross margin and extends your runway slightly beyond the current 6-month projection by maintaining profitability.
*   **Risk:** Moderate. While some alienation is possible, a 2.4% increase is significantly lower than the previous 5.9% hike that triggered churn.

**Recommendation**
**Do not absorb the cost.** Absorbing the hike directly threatens your solvency by accelerating your cash burn, turning a thin-margin business into a loss-making one within half a year. Passing the cost via a modest $2.00 surcharge or price adjustment is the financially viable path. The projected net gain outweighs the minimal churn risk, especially given the increase is less than half the magnitude of your previous successful (albeit painful) hike.

**Regulatory Note on Vermont Agricultural Pricing**
There are **no specific Vermont state statutes** that prohibit sudden price changes for agricultural supplies like seeds, provided you are not engaging in "price gouging" during a declared state of emergency (which typically applies to essential goods like fuel or food during disasters, not B2B seed distribution). However, you must honor existing contracts. If you have signed agreements with farms locking in prices for the upcoming season, you cannot unilaterally raise prices on those specific orders until the contract term expires. For non-contracted, spot-market sales, you are free to adjust prices immediately upon notice.

*Consider framing the increase as a "Freight Stability Surcharge" rather than a base price hike to maintain transparency and allow for easier removal if rates drop later.*
