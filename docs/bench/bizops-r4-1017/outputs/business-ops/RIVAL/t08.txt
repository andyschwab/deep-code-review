*   **Expressed Interest Rate:** 30%

*   **Theoretical Max Revenue (Year 1):** $337,500 (if 100% conversion)

**2. The Grant Overlap Adjustment (Serviceable Obtainable Market)**
You correctly identified that "interest" does not equal "funded budget." Municipal sustainability grants are rarely 100% allocated to single initiatives. We must apply a **Budget Allocation Filter** to account for competition with other green space projects (solar, tree planting, waste reduction).

*   **Assumption A (Conservative):** Pollinator programs typically capture only 10–15% of a general "green space" or "sustainability" line item.
*   **Assumption B (Conversion Friction):** Even with interest, bureaucratic procurement cycles mean only ~40% of interested parties close a deal within a 24-month window.

**4. Strategic Recommendation for Investor Pitch**
Do not pitch the $337k theoretical maximum. Investors will immediately discount it due to the grant overlap risk you identified. Instead, pitch the **Moderate Estimate ($60k ARR in Year 2)** as your base case, with the $337k listed strictly as "Total Addressable Market" (TAM) to show ceiling potential.

To improve these numbers without changing the market size:
1.  **Bundle Services:** Increase the average contract value (ACV) by adding educational workshops or honey harvest sales, moving the price point from $2,500 to $4,000. This reduces the number of sites needed to hit revenue targets.
2.  **Grant Specificity:** Target the subset of the 135 interested municipalities that have *specific* pollinator decline mandates, rather than general sustainability goals. This likely increases the budget allocation filter from 20% to 60%+.

**Final Conservative Estimate:** Expect to secure **10–12 payable contracts** in the next two years, generating approximately **$50,000–$60,000 in total revenue**.

*Note: This model assumes a single site per municipality; upselling additional hives to larger cities could double these figures.*
