**Recommendation: Execute a Partial Hedge via Forward Contracts**

Given your thin 12% net margin and the specific threat of a single transaction wiping out quarterly profitability, **hedging is absolutely justified**. Relying on dynamic pricing to pass currency risk to US retailers is dangerous for a specialty importer; frequent price changes erode trust with B2B partners and make your product less competitive against domestic blenders or larger importers with stable costs.

**The Strategy: A Targeted Forward Contract**
You should utilize a **forward contract** for 50–75% of your pending 12 million yen obligation.

*   **Why a Forward Contract?** Unlike options, which require paying an upfront premium (a direct cost that hurts your already tight margins), forward contracts typically have no upfront fee. They simply lock in a specific exchange rate for a future date. This provides certainty without immediate cash outflow.
*   **Why Not 100%?** Hedging the entire amount removes all downside risk but also eliminates any upside benefit if the yen weakens further (e.g., moves to 160). By hedging 75% (9 million yen), you secure the bulk of your margin while leaving 25% exposed. If the yen drops, you save on that portion; if it spikes, your core profitability remains intact.
*   **Why Not Options?** Currency options act as insurance: you pay a premium to gain the right, but not the obligation, to exchange at a set rate. For a business with a 12% margin, the cost of this premium (often 2–4% of the notional value) is prohibitive. It effectively lowers your margin floor before the trade even begins.

**Financial Impact Analysis**
Your recent loss of $18,000 on $250,000 sales represents a 7.2% margin erosion solely from FX. With a 12% target margin, you have almost no buffer.
*   **Current Exposure:** 12 million JPY.
*   **Risk:** If the rate moves from 150 to 110 (a strong yen scenario), your cost basis increases drastically, turning profit into loss.
*   **The Fix:** Locking in a rate near the current 150 level (or whatever the 3-month forward rate is offered by your bank) guarantees that your cost of goods sold (COGS) remains fixed. This allows you to price your tea to US retailers with confidence, knowing your margin is protected regardless of macroeconomic swings.

**Implementation Steps**
1.  **Contact your commercial bank or a specialized FX provider immediately.** Do not wait for the payment date; rates fluctuate daily.
2.  **Request a quote for a 3-month forward contract** on 9 million JPY.
3.  **Maintain dynamic pricing only for long-term contracts.** For spot orders or short-term reorders, keep prices stable. Use the certainty from your hedge to absorb minor fluctuations on the unhedged 25% rather than annoying customers with constant price updates.

**Conclusion**
Do not gamble your quarterly viability on currency markets. The cost of a potential loss far outweighs the opportunity cost of missing a favorable rate shift. A partial forward contract is the most cost-effective tool to stabilize your COGS and protect your 12% margin.
