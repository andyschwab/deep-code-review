### Profit Projection & Pricing Analysis for IronClad Games

**Scenario A: Absorb Cost (Maintain $45 Pledge)**
*   **Revenue:** $45 × 2,000 units = $90,000
*   **COGS:** $14.50 × 2,000 units = $29,000
*   **Shipping:** $8 × 2,000 units = $16,000
*   **Gross Profit:** $90,000 - $29,000 - $16,000 = **$45,000**
*   *Implication:* Your margin per unit drops from $25.00 to $22.50. To maintain total profit, you would need to cut $5,000 from your marketing budget or other operational costs.

**Scenario B: Raise Price ($52 Pledge)**
*   **Revenue:** $52 × 2,000 units = $104,000
*   **COGS:** $14.50 × 2,000 units = $29,000
*   **Shipping:** $8 × 2,000 units = $16,000
*   **Gross Profit:** $104,000 - $29,000 - $16,000 = **$59,000**
*   *Implication:* This scenario generates **$14,000 more profit** than Scenario A, assuming sales volume remains constant at 2,000 units.

**Elasticity & Strategic Recommendation**

You asked if a $7 increase (approx. 15.5%) will cause pledge numbers to drop significantly. In the Kickstarter board game ecosystem, price elasticity behaves differently than in retail SaaS or commodities.

1.  **Value Perception vs. Price Sensitivity:** Board game backers are generally less sensitive to absolute price increases if the perceived value (art quality, component upgrade, game depth) remains high. A move from $45 to $52 keeps you within the standard "mid-weight strategy game" psychological bracket. You have not crossed the $60–$70 threshold where casual backers often hesitate.
2.  **The "Fairness" Factor:** Kickstarter backers understand supply chain realities. If you communicate transparently that the price increase is solely due to rising material costs to ensure component quality (rather than greed), churn is often minimal.
3.  **The Risk of Absorbing Costs:** Cutting your marketing budget to save $5,000 is dangerous. On Kickstarter, visibility drives algorithms. Reducing ad spend or influencer outreach likely reduces total pledges more than a modest price hike would. Losing 10% of your backers due to lower visibility costs you $4,500 in revenue *plus* the lost momentum, whereas raising prices risks only a small fraction of price-sensitive backers.

**Verdict:**
**Raise the tier to $52.**

The math heavily favors the price increase. Even if you lose **10%** of your projected backers (selling 1,800 units instead of 2,000) due to the higher price, your revenue would be $93,600. After costs ($41,400), your profit would be **$52,200**. This is still **$7,200 higher** than absorbing the cost and selling all 2,000 units.

Unless your specific demographic is extremely budget-constrained, the risk of losing volume is outweighed by the margin protection. Do not cut marketing; protect your visibility.

*Note: Consider offering an "Early Bird" tier at $45 for the first 48 hours to reward speed while keeping the standard tier at $52.*
