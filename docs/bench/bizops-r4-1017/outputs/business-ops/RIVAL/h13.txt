### Unit Economics Analysis: StepRight Solutions

**Current Financial Snapshot (Monthly)**
*   **Volume:** 1,200 pairs
*   **Revenue:** $252,000 (1,200 × $210)
*   **Variable Costs:** $54,000 (1,200 × $45)
*   **Fixed Software Costs:** $12,000
*   **Total Costs:** $66,000
*   **Net Profit:** $186,000
*   **Effective Cost Per Pair to StepRight:** $55 ($45 variable + $10 allocated fixed)

**The Clinic's Constraint**
The core friction is mathematical. The Ontario insurance reimbursement cap is **$185/pair**. Your current charge is **$210/pair**.
*   **Clinic Deficit:** -$25 per pair before accounting for their own overhead (staff, rent, equipment).
*   **Result:** Clinics cannot sustain your current model if they rely 100% on insurance reimbursement for every pair. They are losing money on every transaction unless they charge patients a significant out-of-pocket difference, which may be difficult in this market.

**Model Comparison: Bundled vs. Separated**

**Option A: Current Model (Bundled @ $210/pair)**
*   **Pros:** Simple billing; high margin for you ($155/pair contribution).
*   **Cons:** Immediate rejection by clinics. The price exceeds the reimbursement cap. Clinics cannot justify paying $210 when the insurer only pays $185. This model forces clinics to operate at a loss or aggressively upsell cash pay, creating high churn risk for you.

**Option B: Separated Model (Lower Unit Price + SaaS Fee)**
*   **Strategy:** Drop the per-unit price to meet the reimbursement cap and move the margin recovery to a fixed fee.
*   **Proposed Structure:**
    *   **Per Pair:** $175 (Leaves $10 margin for the clinic under the $185 cap).
    *   **Monthly SaaS Fee:** $42,000 (To recover the $12k cost + maintain your total revenue of $252k).
*   **Analysis:** While this aligns the unit price with insurance, a **$42,000/month fixed fee** is a massive barrier. For a clinic printing 1,200 pairs, this works mathematically, but for smaller clinics, this fee is prohibitive. Furthermore, clinics will fiercely resist a non-reimbursable fixed cost that eats their operational budget. If volume drops to 800 pairs, their unit economics collapse completely under this fee structure.

**Recommendation: Redesign the Revenue Model**

Your current model is unsustainable because it ignores the payer's cap. However, a pure SaaS separation creates too much fixed-risk for the clinic. You must adopt a **Hybrid Tiered Model** that aligns your success with their volume.

1.  **Lower the Base Unit Price:** Reduce the per-pair cost to **$165–$170**. This ensures the clinic stays under the $185 cap, leaving them ~$15–$20 gross margin per pair to cover their overhead. This removes the immediate "we can't afford this" objection.
2.  **Implement a Volume-Based Software Fee:** Instead of a flat $12k (or the inflated $42k required to match current revenue), charge a **per-scan software fee** or a **tiered subscription**.
    *   *Example:* $15 per scan for the software usage.
    *   *New Math:* $170 (Hardware) + $15 (Software) = $185 Total.
    *   *Clinic Reality:* They break even on insurance alone. This is still tight for them.
3.  **The Survival Pivot:** You must acknowledge that at a $185 cap, the clinic cannot pay you $210 worth of value. You have two choices:
    *   **Accept Lower Margins:** Price at $175 total ($130 hardware + $45 software allocation). Your margin drops from $155 to $120 per pair. At 1,200 units, profit falls from $186k to ~$138k. This is painful but secures the contract.
    *   **Enable Cash-Upgrades:** Position your product as "Premium." The insurance covers the base $185. The clinic charges the patient an out-of-pocket upgrade fee for the "StepRight Premium" comfort/tech. You split this upgrade revenue with the clinic. This allows you to keep the $210+ price point without breaking the clinic's insurance math.

**Conclusion:** Do not separate the fees into a large fixed SaaS cost; clinics will reject it as non-reimbursable overhead. Lower your per-unit price to fit under the $185 cap immediately. Recover your margin either by accepting reduced profitability per unit or by creating a revenue-share model on patient out-of-pocket upgrades.

*Note: Your current $186k monthly profit relies on clinics operating at a loss, which is a churn time-bomb. Adjust pricing immediately to align with the $185 reality.*
