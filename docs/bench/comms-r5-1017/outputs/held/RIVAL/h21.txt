**Recommendation: Proceed with Option B (Renegotiate Current Contract)**

Option B offers the most prudent path forward for the upcoming fiscal year. While Option A promises a higher percentage reduction in material costs (10% vs. 7%), it carries unquantified risks that could erase those gains within our 12-month horizon. Specifically, the potential downtime during the transition to a new supplier remains undefined. In raw material sourcing, even brief interruptions can trigger production delays, expedited shipping fees, and inventory shortages that far exceed the $200K difference in upfront investment between the two options.

Option B requires an $800K investment to secure a 7% cost reduction. Crucially, this path leverages our existing supply chain relationships, minimizing operational disruption. Since we are already integrated with the current vendor, the implementation timeline is predictable, and the risk of hidden transition costs is negligible. This stability ensures that the projected savings begin accumulating immediately without the "ramp-up" penalty inherent in onboarding a new partner.

Option A requires a $1M upfront investment for a 10% cut. To justify this over Option B, the new supplier would need to deliver flawless execution immediately. However, without a confirmed figure for switching costs and downtime, we cannot validate the net return. If the transition causes even minor friction, the effective savings for Year 1 will drop below the 7% guaranteed by Option B. Given the hard deadline of the end-of-quarter decision, we do not have sufficient time to audit the new supplier's integration process thoroughly enough to eliminate this uncertainty.

Financially, the gap between the two options is narrow relative to the risk profile. Option A costs $200K more initially. To break even against Option B purely on material savings, the new supplier would need to perform without a single hiccup. If any disruption occurs, Option B becomes the more profitable choice for the 12-month window. Since the primary goal is securing approved budget and immediate cost relief, the certainty of Option B outweighs the theoretical upside of Option A.

**Action Plan:**
1.  Authorize the $800K budget for contract renegotiation immediately.
2.  Direct the procurement team to finalize terms with the current vendor to lock in the 7% reduction before the quarter ends.
3.  Table Option A for a future review once we can fully model the transition downtime and associated switching costs.

This approach secures immediate savings, protects production continuity, and presents a defensible, low-risk budget request to the Director of Procurement.

*Note: This recommendation assumes the 7% and 10% savings figures are net of any standard administrative fees. If the new supplier offers volume incentives beyond the 10% cut, those details would need to be added to re-evaluate Option A.*
