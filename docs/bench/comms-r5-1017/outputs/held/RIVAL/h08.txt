To cut delivery times by 20%, you have two distinct paths with different risk profiles and timelines.

**Option A: Warehouse Automation Pilot**
*   **Cost:** $500,000 plus additional staff training costs.
*   **Timeline:** 3 months to set up.
*   **Scope:** Piloted at one of your five warehouses.
*   **Pros:** High potential to scale across all sites if successful.
*   **Cons:** Highest upfront cost, longest lead time, and hidden training expenses.

**Option B: Last-Mile Delivery Partnership**
*   **Cost:** $350,000.
*   **Timeline:** Starts in 1 month.
*   **Scope:** Trial basis.
*   **Pros:** Lower cost and fastest implementation.
*   **Cons:** Potential reliability issues; less control over the customer experience compared to internal automation.

**Recommendation**
If speed to result is the priority, Option B gets you moving in a third of the time for 30% less capital. However, if the goal is a permanent, scalable infrastructure fix, Option A is the stronger long-term play despite the heavier initial lift. Given the 20% reduction target, Option A likely offers more consistent control over the variable, whereas Option B relies on a third party's performance.

Present both options to the Head of Supply Chain with a clear trade-off: pay more and wait longer for a scalable asset (Option A), or pay less and move fast with a potentially less reliable stopgap (Option B).

Note: Ensure the "extra cost" for staff training in Option A is quantified before the final presentation, as it impacts the ROI comparison.
