**Executive Summary: Expansion vs. Retention Strategy**

**Current Position**
The network serves 12,000 active pediatric patients across Washington and Idaho with an annual churn rate of 18%.

**Option A: Shore Up Existing Operations**
*   **Investment:** $350,000 for a patient-retention program.
*   **Projected Impact:** Reduces churn by 5 percentage points.
*   **Financial Return:** Saves approximately $200,000 annually in acquisition costs.
*   **Payback Period:** 1.75 years.
*   **Primary Risk:** The 5% reduction target relies on data from a single-clinic pilot; results may vary when scaled across the entire network.

**Option B: Expand into Oregon**
*   **Investment:** $500,000 marketing push.
*   **Projected Impact:** Acquires 1,500 new patients in Year 1 (out of an estimated 8,000 potential).
*   **Cost Efficiency:** $333 per acquired patient.
*   **Payback Period:** 3.3 years based on Year 1 volume.
*   **Primary Risk:** Slower initial return on investment.
*   **Upside Potential:** The Oregon market has 25% less competition than current markets, which could accelerate growth beyond the first-year projections.

**Strategic Constraints**
The report recommends a phased approach to pursue both initiatives simultaneously. However, current budget limitations prevent funding both the $350,000 retention program and the $500,000 expansion push at the same time. A choice must be made between immediate efficiency (retention) and market growth (expansion).

**Comparison Summary**
*   **Speed of Return:** Retention offers a faster payback (1.75 years) compared to expansion (3.3 years).
*   **Certainty:** Retention savings are based on internal churn data, though scaling remains a variable. Expansion relies on penetrating a new market with lower competition but higher upfront customer acquisition costs.
*   **Budget Requirement:** Retention requires $150,000 less capital upfront than expansion.

The decision rests on whether the priority is securing current revenue streams with a quicker return or accepting a longer payback period to enter a less competitive market.
