**Executive Summary: Warehouse Automation Vendor Selection**

**Decision Required:** Select a vendor for warehouse automation by end of quarter.

**Financial Comparison**
*   **Vendor A:** $2.4M total upfront cost. Requires a $480K deposit (20%). Annual maintenance is $120K. One-time training costs are $80K.
*   **Vendor B:** $3.1M total upfront cost. Requires a $930K deposit (30%). Annual maintenance is $150K. One-time training costs are $60K.
*   **Note:** Integration costs are excluded from these figures for both options.

**Operational Impact**
*   **Current Capacity:** 5,000 orders/day (Peak: 7,000).
*   **Vendor A Capacity:** 8,000 orders/day. Rollout takes 12 months.
*   **Vendor B Capacity:** 9,200 orders/day (15% higher throughput than A). Rollout takes 9 months.

**Key Trade-offs**
Vendor A offers significant immediate cash flow relief due to a lower total price and a smaller initial deposit ($480K vs. $930K). However, it carries a longer implementation timeline and presents a scalability risk if order volume grows faster than anticipated, as its ceiling (8,000/day) is closer to current peak demands.

Vendor B requires a 29% higher upfront investment and higher annual maintenance. In exchange, it delivers full operational capability three months sooner and provides greater headroom for growth. The additional $700K capital outlay could theoretically be recovered in approximately 18 months through increased throughput, though this projection relies on achieving uncertain volume targets.

**Current Position**
The preliminary preference leans toward Vendor A to minimize capital expenditure and preserve cash flow for the current fiscal year. This stance acknowledges the longer rollout and potential scalability constraints as accepted risks should growth remain moderate.

This summary reflects the data provided in the full report; integration cost estimates are still pending.
