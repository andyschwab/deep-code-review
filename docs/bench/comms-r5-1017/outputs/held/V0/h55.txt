**Maria must decide within two weeks whether to purchase the fleet for $1.2M upfront or lease it at $28K/month to preserve cash flow flexibility during the second state expansion.**

**The Cash-Flow Trade-off**
*   **Purchase Option:** Requires **$1.2M immediate outlay** plus **$60K/year** maintenance.
    *   *Upside:* Saves **$190K over 3 years** if revenue hits the high case (+20%).
    *   *Downside:* Strains cash reserves by **Month 8** if revenue misses by 20%.
    *   *Tax Impact:* Depreciation spread over 5 years.
*   **Lease Option (Author's Lean):** Requires **$28K/month** for 36 months (maintenance included).
    *   *Upside:* Remains **cash-flow comfortable** even if revenue drops 20%. Payments are fully deductible immediately.
    *   *Downside:* Higher total cost in high-revenue scenarios; ends with a **$300K buyout option** rather than automatic ownership.
    *   *Context:* Current reserves are **$4.5M** with **$800K/month** operating cash flow.

**Decision Driver**
The choice hinges on risk tolerance regarding the Month 4 revenue ramp-up ($1.1M/month projected at 40% margin). Purchasing maximizes long-term savings but exposes the company to liquidity strain if adoption lags. Leasing sacrifices long-term savings to guarantee liquidity under downside scenarios. The author leans toward leasing to maintain flexibility but presents both options for your final determination based on these cash-flow projections.

Note: The $300K end-of-lease buyout is optional, not mandatory.
Tax implications differ significantly between immediate deduction (lease) and 5-year depreciation (purchase).
