**Emily must decide between investing $250K in a new premium analytics tier or focusing on improving existing feature adoption to save $150K/year.**

**Option A: Premium Analytics Tier**
*   **Investment:** $250K development cost; 4-month timeline.
*   **Projected Revenue:** $21,600/month ($259,200/year) based on 8% adoption of 45,000 users at $4.99/month.
*   **Break-even:** Approximately 12 months after launch.
*   **Risks:** Potential cannibalization of free features; churn risk if valuable functionality moves behind a paywall.

**Option B: Improve Existing Feature Adoption**
*   **Target:** Increase "Savings Goals" adoption from 35% to 50%.
*   **Impact:** Users engaging with Savings Goals already show 20% lower churn; bill reminder users show 10% lower churn.
*   **Value:** Hitting the 50% adoption target for Savings Goals is projected to reduce churn by an additional 5%, saving $150K annually in acquisition costs.
*   **Current Baseline:** Budget tracking (78% adoption) and expense categorization (65%) are strong; bill reminders (42%) and savings goals (35%) are underutilized.

**The Trade-off**
Option A requires significant upfront capital ($250K) for a new revenue stream that takes a year to pay back, carrying execution and retention risks. Option B requires no stated development cost in the report and offers immediate retention value ($150K/year savings) by deepening engagement with features that already correlate strongly with lower churn. The decision hinges on whether the company prioritizes new revenue generation (Option A) or immediate cost avoidance through retention (Option B).

Current adoption data suggests 35% of users already find value in savings tools; closing the gap to 50% leverages existing infrastructure. Conversely, the premium tier assumes 3,600 users (8% of 45k) will convert to paid without evidence of willingness to pay, while potentially alienating the 65% who do not currently use savings goals.
