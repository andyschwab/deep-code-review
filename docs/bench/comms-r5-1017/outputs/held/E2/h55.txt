**Decision needed: Lease or purchase the expansion fleet; Maria to decide within 2 weeks based on cash-flow risk tolerance.** The author leans toward leasing to preserve flexibility, though both options are viable under base-case revenue projections.

**Situation:** Expanding to the second state requires 15 vans and 3 mobile lifts immediately. The choice balances upfront capital expenditure against monthly operating cash flow, with revenue generation beginning in month 4.

**Option A: Purchase**
*   **Cost:** $1.2M upfront + $60K/year maintenance.
*   **Cash Flow Impact:** Immediate reduction of cash reserves from $4.5M to $3.3M.
*   **Upside:** Saves $190K over 3 years if revenue exceeds projections by 20%.
*   **Risk:** If revenue is 20% below target, cash flow becomes strained by month 8.
*   **Tax:** Depreciation spread over 5 years.

**Option B: Lease (Author's Lean)**
*   **Cost:** $28K/month for 36 months (maintenance included); $300K buyout option at end.
*   **Total 3-Year Cost:** $1.308M (excluding potential buyout).
*   **Cash Flow Impact:** Preserves $1.2M in reserves; monthly outflow aligns with incoming revenue starting month 4.
*   **Upside:** Remains "comfortable" even if revenue is 20% below target.
*   **Tax:** Payments fully deductible immediately.

**Trade-off Analysis:**
The decision hinges on the probability of the "20% lower revenue" scenario. Purchasing offers a hard cost saving of $190K over three years in a high-growth scenario but introduces significant liquidity risk if the expansion ramps up slowly. Leasing costs approximately $108K more over three years (base case) but acts as an insurance policy against cash-flow strain during the critical first eight months of operation. Given projected operating cash flow of $800K/month and new revenue of $1.1M/month (at 40% margin) starting month 4, the lease payments ($28K) are easily covered, whereas the purchase removes a large buffer instantly.
