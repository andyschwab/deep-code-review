# Trip Approval Screen: Prototype A vs. B Recommendation

**Decision needed by:** October 30 (Mei / Engineering)

## Recommendation
**Build B's structure (group by traveler, budget impact) but design it to preserve A's speed, and run one short, targeted follow-up test with high-volume approvers before engineering commits.** The current data doesn't support a clean "A wins" or "B wins," and the biggest unknown (50+ trips/week) is exactly where the two designs are most likely to diverge.

## What the results show (n=6, all mid-sized companies)

| Measure | A (list) | B (grouped + budget) |
|---|---|---|
| Completed main task | 5 of 6 | 6 of 6 |
| Time | Faster | Noticeably slower for 4 of 6 |
| Stated preference | 2 | 3 (1 no preference) |

## Interpretation
- **B is more reliable; A is faster.** B had full completion, A had one failure. With six people, that one miss is a signal to investigate, not a rate. We should find out why that person failed in A.
- **B's value is real but specific.** Three people chose it because of the budget view. That suggests the budget impact matters to some managers.
- **B's slowness may be a design problem, not inherent to grouping.** We have no timing numbers or cause, only "noticeably longer." Is it the grouping, the extra information to read, or unfamiliarity with a prototype? Review session recordings to find out.
- **Preferences were split (2/3/1).** That's not a mandate for either version.
- **Speed matters more at volume.** Two participants preferred A for speed. For someone approving 50+ trips a week, a per-trip time penalty compounds, so B's slowdown could become a serious problem. Grouping by traveler could also help at volume, which is just as plausible. We don't know.

## Limits of this evidence
- **Sample:** 6 participants, all from mid-sized companies. Your assumption that they're typical is untested, and the high-volume segment is not represented.
- **Order effects:** Everyone tried both versions. Unless the order was counterbalanced, familiarity with the task may have affected the second version's results.
- **Prototype speed** may not match real-world use with real data.

## Proposed next steps (fits before Oct 30)
1. **Now (1–2 days):** Rewatch recordings. Identify why one participant failed in A and what slowed B down for the four.
2. **By ~Oct 23:** Run 4–5 short sessions with managers who approve 50+ trips weekly, using realistic volumes. Counterbalance the order. Record time per approval.
3. **In parallel:** Have Mei explore a B variant that keeps the budget view but reduces the time cost, such as a collapsed budget summary or a bulk-approve action.
4. **Decide by Oct 27–28:**
   - If high-volume users are not slowed much by B (or the variant), **go with B.**
   - If they are clearly slower and the budget view isn't valued, **go with A**, with budget info as a possible later addition.
   - If results are mixed, **ship A's list with an optional grouped/budget view.**

## If no further testing is possible
Choose B's structure, since it had full completion and the budget view is a differentiator, but flag the speed risk to engineering. Build so the list view can be added or toggled without rework.
