# Board Summary (draft to adapt)

**Product direction update: Tidewater Notes, November**

**What we did.** We surveyed active users on how they would feel if they could no longer use Tidewater Notes. We emailed about 2,400 and received 312 responses (about 13%).

**What we found.** 24% of respondents (about 75 people) said they would be very disappointed, 48% somewhat disappointed, and 28% not disappointed. The very disappointed group skews toward graduate students who use our citation feature.

**How we read it.** 24% is below the 40% "very disappointed" level commonly used as a signal of strong product-market fit, so we do not yet have broad fit. But the score is not uniform: one segment shows markedly stronger attachment. Responses likely lean toward our more engaged users, so the true figure across the active base is probably lower, not higher. We treat this as a signal about where to look, not proof of where to commit.

**What we will do.**
1. Segment the survey data by role, citation-feature use, and usage frequency, and compute the "very disappointed" rate within graduate students specifically. Target: complete by [date].
2. Interview 8-10 very disappointed users and 5-8 somewhat disappointed or not disappointed users to learn what they rely on us for and what alternatives they would use.
3. Follow up with a sample of non-respondents to check how far the results generalize.
4. Decide on focus by [date before the meeting]: if graduate students clear roughly 40% very disappointed and the segment is large and reachable enough, we will prioritize them and the citation workflow. If not, we will keep the broader scope and investigate further.

**What we are not claiming.** We are not yet committing to a narrower focus, and we are not claiming broad product-market fit.

**Ask of the board.** Input on the segment-size threshold and on how much roadmap we should shift before the interviews are complete.

# What the numbers do and don't tell you

**What they support**
- Attachment is concentrated. Roughly a quarter would be very disappointed, which is a real pocket of value, but it is below the ~40% benchmark.
- 72% somewhat or very disappointed sounds healthy, but "somewhat disappointed" is a weak signal. Those users tend not to churn loudly or recommend you.

**What they don't**
- **Non-response bias.** 13% responded, and your own suspicion is right: responders likely over-represent engaged users. Treat 24% as an upper-leaning estimate for the active base.
- **Small segment counts.** About 75 very disappointed people total. Once split by graduate student status, the cells may be a few dozen, so rates will have wide error bars.
- **Skew is not the same as rate.** "Very disappointed skews toward grad students" could mean grad students are simply most of your base. You need the share within the segment, not the share of the very disappointed group.
- **Causation.** Citation users may be attached because of the feature, or because heavy users use it more. Interviews help here.
- **Representativeness is an assumption.** Compare respondents to the 2,400 on tenure, activity, and role before relying on it.

**Decision logic.** Narrowing is a bet with real cost, and the data is suggestive, not decisive. A cheap step (segmenting plus interviews) before November lets you present a decision rather than a hypothesis.
