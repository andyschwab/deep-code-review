# Interview Plan: First-Payroll Setup Abandonment

**For:** Priya and [PM] | **Recruiting starts:** Nov 3 | **Interviews:** ~8 over 3 weeks

## What we're trying to learn
1. **Why people stop.** Is it length/intimidation (our hypothesis), missing information (e.g., they don't know or can't find their tax IDs), or something else (trust, a competing tool, a delayed start date)?
2. **What happens at the tax registration step.** What do they have in hand, what do they think is being asked, and what do they do when stuck?
3. **How an accountant changes things.** Who does what, and when is the accountant brought in?
4. **What "easy" looks like** to them, in their words.

Decisions this informs: what the new flow should simplify, defer, or help with (e.g., save-and-return, an accountant invite), and which assumptions to test next.

## Who to recruit
Talk to **both groups, weighted toward abandoners**. Finishers show what worked and what workarounds they used; abandoners show the failure. Avoid the two-group trap of comparing them only on stated reasons.

| Segment | Count |
|---|---|
| Abandoned at tax registration, no accountant | 2 |
| Abandoned at tax registration, with accountant | 2 |
| Completed setup, no accountant | 2 |
| Completed setup, with accountant | 2 |

If recruiting is slow, protect the abandoner cells first. Even 2 per cell is thin, so treat findings as patterns to test, not proof. Accountant effects are the least certain, so watch whether it matters before splitting further.

**Criteria:** Signed up in the last ~90 days (so memory is fresh), first-time payroll, small business owner, and the person who actually did the setup. Mix of business types and states if possible, since tax registration varies by location. Exclude people who abandoned at other steps (for now). Offer an incentive and screen for accountant status at recruiting.

**Note:** Nobody has seen the mockups, so keep them out of the first 6 interviews' core discussion. If you want reactions, reserve the last 5 minutes of the final 2 interviews, after the behavioral questions, and treat them as directional only.

## Interview structure (45 min)
**Opening (3 min):** We're learning, not selling. No right answers. Ask to record.

**Context (5 min):**
- Tell me about your business and how you handled payroll before.
- What made you look for payroll software?

**Core: the last time (20 min)**
- Walk me through what happened when you set up Fernhill, from signup onward.
- Tell me about the tax registration step. What did you see? What did you do?
- What did you have on hand? What did you have to look for? Who did you ask?
- What happened next? *(Abandoners: when did you stop, and what did you do afterward? Finishers: was there a point you almost stopped?)*
- How long did it take, and how did that compare to what you expected?
- How did you feel at that point?
- *(With accountant)* What did your accountant do, and when did you involve them? What did you handle yourself?
- What did you try instead (another tool, a manual process, leaving it)?

**Priorities (7 min):**
- What does "good" look like for getting payroll set up?
- What is it costing you now, in time, money, or stress, if payroll isn't running?
- What would have made you continue (or what kept you going)?

**Optional concept reactions (5 min, last 2 interviews only)**

**Wrap-up (3 min):** Anything I didn't ask? Anyone else I should talk to?

## Probing
Use "Tell me more," "Can you give a specific example?", and gentle "Why?" Avoid "Would you use…?" and "Was it too long?" Let them name the cause. Listen for **length** (time, number of steps) versus **missing info** (don't know the number, unsure which registration applies) versus **fear of errors**.

## Open items to align on
- How we identify abandoners and finishers (CRM or product data) and who owns outreach.
- A shared note template (job, what they had, where they stopped, stated vs. observed cause, accountant role, surprises).
- A debrief after interview 4 to adjust the questions.
