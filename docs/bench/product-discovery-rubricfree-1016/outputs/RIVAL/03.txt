**Brief: Pricing page, what to do before committing to a redesign**

**To:** Jane (Design), Head of Sales
**Recommendation:** Don't start the 4-week redesign yet. Spend about 1-2 weeks on cheap diagnosis first, then decide. If the evidence points at the page, we redesign with a clear target. If it points at the tiers, a redesign would polish the wrong thing.

**What we know**
- 12 prospects on sales calls over two months called the plans confusing.
- A few customers have asked what separates Team from Studio.
- The pricing page converts visitors to trial at 3.1% on roughly 1,800 visitors a month (about 56 trials).
- We have no benchmark for 3.1%, and no way yet to say whether the 12 are typical or a vocal minority.

**What we don't know (and why it matters)**
1. **How widespread the confusion is.** The 12 come only from people who reached a sales call, so they're a biased sample. They could be a large share of confused visitors or a small one.
2. **Page problem or tier problem?** If Team vs. Studio is hard to explain in one sentence, better layout and copy won't fix it. The Team/Studio questions from existing customers, who already paid, hint at a tier-design issue, but that's a hint, not proof.
3. **The size of the prize.** Even a 20% relative lift (3.1% to about 3.7%) is roughly 11 extra trials a month. Worth knowing before spending 4 weeks.

**Proposed next steps (1-2 weeks)**
- **Sales (this week):** Tag every call where pricing confusion comes up, and log which tiers are confused and what they asked. Go back through the last two months of call notes or recordings to count how many calls the 12 came from. That gives us a rate, not just a count.
- **Support/CS:** Pull every ticket and email asking about Team vs. Studio, and note what the person was trying to decide.
- **Growth (me):** Add event tracking on the pricing page if missing: scroll depth, tier clicks, toggles, exits. Check where visitors drop off and compare trial rate by traffic source, since confusion matters less for visitors with low intent.
- **Jane:** Run 5 short moderated tests (15 minutes each) with prospects or customers: "Which plan would you pick for your agency, and why?" Do this on the current page. If people pick the right plan but say it was hard, it's the page. If they disagree about what the plans are for, it's the tiers.
- **Optional quick fix:** A low-effort test (a one-line "best for" label per tier plus a short comparison of the 3-4 features that actually differ) can run in days, not weeks, and gives a read on whether copy alone moves conversion.

**Decision rule (review in ~2 weeks)**
- Confusion shows up in a meaningful share of calls AND testers pick the right plan only with difficulty → **page problem**: proceed with the redesign, scoped to the confusing points.
- Testers can't agree on who each tier is for → **tier problem**: fix packaging first with sales and product, then redesign.
- Confusion is rare relative to total calls and drop-off is modest → **defer** and put the 4 weeks elsewhere.

**Ask:** Sales, please start tagging calls and pull the call counts. Jane, please plan the 5 sessions. I'll set up the tracking and report back in two weeks.

*Note: I'm assuming Team and Studio differ in ways sales can describe quickly; if they can't, that's the strongest sign the tiers need rework.*
