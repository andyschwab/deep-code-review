# Recommendation: Retire the report builder, but sequence it. Don't decide on Halvorsen yet.

**Position for next week:** Commit to retiring the feature, and make the date and method conditional on two checks that take about 2-3 weeks. Do not announce anything until Halvorsen is resolved.

## Why retire
- 4% usage (about 24 of 610 accounts) is low, and it carries outsized bug load and ~0.2 engineer FTE.
- The cost is modest in absolute terms, so the real case is opportunity cost and bug drag, not savings. Say this plainly to Engineering.

## Why not retire immediately
- Halvorsen is ~11% of revenue, and CS reports the builder was a key renewal reason. That is secondhand and unverified.
- Your assumption that most other users can move to standard dashboards is untested. It is the load-bearing claim.

## Two checks before deciding (owners and timing)
1. **Usage review (Product + Data, ~1 week):** For the ~24 accounts, pull what reports they build: fields, filters, frequency, whether they are scheduled or recurring. Classify each as (a) replicable in standard dashboards, (b) replicable with an export or scheduled report, (c) needs the builder. Also check revenue by account, since 4% of accounts may not be 4% of revenue.
2. **Halvorsen discovery (CS + Product, ~2 weeks):** Interview their finance users about the actual monthly workflow: inputs, output format, who consumes it, and what breaks if it changes. Ask about past behavior, not whether they'd accept a replacement. Show no solution until you understand the job. Also verify the renewal claim directly with the account manager's notes or the champion.

## Decision rules (agree these next week, before data arrives)
| Finding | Action |
|---|---|
| Halvorsen's need is met by scheduled export or a dashboard template, and most others are in (a)/(b) | Retire with migration support; sunset ~6 months out |
| Halvorsen needs the builder, others don't | Freeze the builder (no new features, critical bugs only), keep it for Halvorsen, or build them a purpose-built report. Revisit after renewal |
| Many of the 24 are (c) | Don't retire; reduce scope instead, or reconsider |

## Handling Halvorsen
- Treat them as a design partner, not a notification recipient. Involve them before any announcement.
- Prototype the replacement and have their finance team run a real month-end on it before March 2027.
- Do not remove anything before the renewal is signed. Working backward, aim to have Halvorsen validated by around January.
- Quantify the tradeoff: 11% of revenue at risk versus about 0.2 FTE. Even a modest churn probability likely outweighs the savings, which is why sequencing matters more than speed.

## Where your VP Eng and head of CS are each right
- **Engineering:** The feature is a persistent drag and should not live forever. Freezing it caps the cost immediately.
- **CS:** Retiring before Halvorsen is secured is the real risk. Nothing here forces that.

## Agenda for the discussion
1. Agree on the decision rules above.
2. Assign owners for the two checks.
3. Agree on an interim freeze policy.
4. Set a decision date, ideally early November.

*Note: Figures like 24 accounts are derived from your 4% of 610. The renewal-driver claim and the migration assumption are both unverified.*
