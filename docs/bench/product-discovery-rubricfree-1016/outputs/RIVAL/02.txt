# Integration Recommendation for Next Quarter (for Oct 28 planning)

## Ranked recommendation

**1. Invoicing tool sync (3 weeks). Recommended default.**
**2. Bank feed (6 weeks).** First if retention evidence favors it.
**3. Calendar integration (2 weeks).** First only if the top-tier revenue case holds up.

## Reasoning

| | Tickets (90d) | Effort | Tickets per week of effort |
|---|---|---|---|
| Bank feed | 34 | 6 wks | ~5.7 |
| Invoicing sync | 21 | 3 wks | 7.0 |
| Calendar | 9 | 2 wks | 4.5 |

- **Invoicing sync** has the best demand-per-effort ratio. It also leaves about half a quarter of slack for bug fixes, polish, or a calendar follow-up if that estimate holds. Because it is the cheapest of the two high-volume requests, it is the lowest-regret choice while we lack retention data.
- **Bank feed** has the most raw demand (34 tickets), but it costs twice the invoicing sync and takes nearly half the quarter. Bank integrations also tend to carry hidden costs (partner approval, security review, ongoing maintenance), though I can't confirm that for your case. If your engineers see those risks, treat 6 weeks as optimistic.
- **Calendar** has the least volume, but 9 requests concentrated in your highest-paying tier could represent more revenue than the ticket count suggests. At 2 weeks it is also the cheapest. It ranks third only because we don't yet know how much revenue those accounts represent.

## Caveats on the data

- Ticket counts measure who complained, not who would leave or pay more. You flagged the loud-user risk yourself. Check **unique customers** per integration, not tickets. 34 tickets from 5 people is weaker than 21 tickets from 18 people.
- None of the requests is yet linked to cancellations, which is the biggest gap.

## What would change the order

Before Oct 28, these checks should be quick (a support-tool export plus a billing lookup):

1. **Unique requesters per integration.** If the bank feed's 34 tickets come from only a few people, it drops. If it has far more distinct requesters than the others, it rises.
2. **Cancellation link.** Check churned accounts and cancellation reasons from the last 6-12 months for mentions of any of the three. If bank feed is cited in cancellations, move it to #1. The same applies to the others.
3. **Revenue of calendar requesters.** Add up the MRR of the accounts that asked for it. If it matches or exceeds the revenue of the invoicing and bank requesters, calendar should go first, since it is also the cheapest.
4. **Overlap.** If the same customers asked for several integrations, demand is less spread out than it looks.
5. **Engineering risk.** If your engineers see bank partner approval or compliance as likely to push 6 weeks out, the bank feed falls further. If the invoicing tool's API is more difficult than estimated, its lead narrows.
6. **Prospect signal.** Ask sales or trial users whether any of these block signups.

**Decision rule for the session:** Default to invoicing sync unless checks 2 or 3 give a clear reason to switch.

*Note: I used only the figures you gave; the effort estimates and ticket counts are unverified on my end.*
