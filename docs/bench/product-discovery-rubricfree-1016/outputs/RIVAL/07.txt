# Larkspur Discovery Plan: Q1 (10 weeks)

**Goal:** Find out where customers struggle, including those who never file tickets, and decide what to build or fix first.

**Starting evidence:** The three biggest ticket clusters are scanning (~280, 20%), stock transfers (~190, 14%) and supplier ordering (~150, 11%). Together they are about 44% of the ~1,400 tickets. The long tail is the other ~56%.

**Two assumptions to test first**
1. Tickets reflect real struggle. Quiet churners are missing from them.
2. Scanning tickets are a mix of bugs and setup mistakes, in unknown proportions.

## Sequence

| Weeks | Workstream | What we do | What we hope to learn |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1-2 | **A. Scanning triage** | Tomas and the researcher re-code a random sample of ~60-80 scanning tickets as bug, setup/onboarding, hardware, or expectation gap. Engineering checks the bug-labelled ones. | The bug vs. setup split. This decides whether scanning goes to engineering, to onboarding design, or both. |
| 1-4 | **B. Quiet-churn interviews** | Recruit now. Interview 6-8 churned or low-activity accounts, using past-behavior questions ("walk me through the last time you…"). | Why people leave without filing tickets. Whether their reasons match the ticket clusters or point elsewhere. |
| 2-3 | **C. Tail check** | Re-code a ~50-ticket sample of the long tail. | Whether the tail hides a large theme or is truly scattered. |
| 3-6 | **D. Transfers** | Interview and observe 5-6 multi-location customers doing a real transfer. Pull usage data on transfer completion and abandonment. | Where the confusion sits (terminology, flow or missing feedback) and whether it blocks the job or only slows it. |
| 5-6 | **A2. Scanning in context** | Only if A shows meaningful setup failure: observe 4-5 first-time setups. | The specific setup steps that fail, and candidate fixes. |
| 6-9 | **E. Supplier ordering** | Interview 6-8 retailers about how they reorder today (tools, time, errors, who is involved). | Whether this is a real, frequent job or just a loud request. Current workarounds and willingness to pay. |
| 9-10 | **Synthesis** | Combine findings into an opportunity list with evidence from tickets, usage data and interviews. | A ranked set of opportunities, with the next experiments proposed for each. |

**Why this order:** A and C are cheap, so they run first and sharpen everything after. B starts early because recruiting churned customers is slow and covers our biggest blind spot. Supplier ordering goes last because it is a growth request, not a failure, and the earlier findings may change what we ask.

**Capacity:** About 25-30 customer sessions across the quarter is realistic for one researcher. A and C are shared with Tomas, and engineering and design join the sessions.

## Decisions this informs
- Scanning: fix bugs, redesign setup, or both, and in what proportions.
- Whether the transfer flow needs redesign or clearer guidance.
- Whether supplier ordering earns a roadmap slot, and what the smallest version would be.
- Whether churn points to a problem the tickets never showed.

## Checkpoints
- **Week 2:** scanning split and tail result. Adjust scope if needed.
- **Week 6:** early churn and transfer findings.
- **Week 10:** readout to your manager and Tomas.

**Notes:** The percentages come from Tomas's rough tags, so the sampling in A and C also checks his tagging. Ticket counts show volume, not severity, so the interviews should weigh the impact on customers' work.
