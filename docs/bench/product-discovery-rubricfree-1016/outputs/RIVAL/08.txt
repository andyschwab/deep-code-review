# Oakmere Pet: 6-Week Demand Test for Dog Health Testing Kits

**Budget: $5,000 | No code, no supplier contract until the gates below are met**

## Questions to answer
1. Will people pay ~$79 (pre-commit with money)?
2. Is demand stronger among existing subscribers or new customers?

## Plan

**Week 1: Setup (~$300)**
- Build a landing page with a no-code tool (Carrd, Shopify, or Webflow): kit description, what it tests, $79 price, "Reserve yours" button.
- Reservation = refundable $10 deposit (Stripe payment link). Deposits measure intent far better than email signups.
- Create separate tracking links per audience (email, ads, organic).
- Email 10 of the 15 people who wrote in. Request 20-minute calls.

**Weeks 2-3: Existing subscribers (~$0-200)**
- Split the 9,000 subscribers into two groups:
  - Supplement buyers (~2,700)
  - Non-supplement buyers (~6,300). Send to a random 2,000 only.
- Send each segment the same email, two sends over the period.
- Run a price split: half of each segment sees $79, half sees $99 (or $59). This is a cheap test of whether $79 is right.
- Hold 6-8 customer calls (offer a $25 account credit). Ask about the last time they had a dog health concern, what they did, and what it cost. Do not pitch.

**Weeks 2-5: New customers (~$3,500 ads)**
- Run Meta and Google ads targeting dog owners to the same landing page. Spend about $3,500, split across 2-3 audiences and creatives. Expect roughly 2,500-4,000 clicks at typical CPCs.
- Track cost per deposit.

**Week 4: Price/format check (~$300)**
- Add a short survey to deposit-makers and non-converters (what worry drove them, what they'd expect to pay, which tests matter). Offer a small incentive.

**Weeks 5-6: Decide (~$500 reserve + buffer)**
- Use the reserve to extend the best-performing channel or run a quick supplier quote request (non-binding).
- Compile results and hold the go/no-go meeting at end of Week 6.

## Decision thresholds
These are starting points. Agree on them with Dana **before** launching, and adjust only now, not after seeing data.

**Subscribers (emailed, deduplicated)**
- **Go signal:** ≥2% deposit rate (e.g. ≥40 of 2,000 emailed non-supplement subscribers' equivalent, or ≥54 of 2,700 supplement buyers), at $79.
- **Stop signal:** <0.5%.
- Between: ambiguous.

**New customers (paid)**
- **Go signal:** ≥3% of landing-page visitors leave a deposit, and cost per deposit ≤ $35. Rationale: at $79 with limited margin, a higher acquisition cost makes the unit economics unlikely to work. Check this against your actual kit cost and lab fees.
- **Stop signal:** <1% or cost per deposit >$80.

**Price**
- $79 holds if its deposit rate is no worse than ~25% below the lower-priced variant. If $99 converts nearly as well, there's room to raise the price.

**Qualitative**
- Go: at least 4 of 8 interviewees describe a real recent spend or action on a dog health concern, and want results they can act on.
- Stop: interviewees are curious but have taken no past action.

**Overall**
- **Go:** subscriber go signal met, or paid go signal met, plus unit economics are viable at $79.
- **Stop:** both stop signals hit.
- **Mixed:** run a second 3-week test on the stronger segment only.

## Ground rules
- Be upfront on the page that kits are not yet available and deposits are refundable. Refund everyone if you stop.
- Count only deposits, not email signups or "interested" replies.
- Check regulatory and lab requirements for dog health tests (claims, shipping samples) before running ads.

**Notes:** The thresholds are judgment-based benchmarks, not facts about your business. Sanity-check them against your kit cost and margins before launch.
