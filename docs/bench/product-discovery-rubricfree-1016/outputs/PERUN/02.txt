# Acme Ledger: next-quarter integration recommendation (draft for Oct 28)

## Provisional ranking

All three rest on one untested assumption: that ticket volume reflects real demand and is tied to retention. Treat this order as provisional until the check below is done.

| Rank | Integration | Tickets (90d) | Effort | Tickets per week of effort |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | Invoicing sync | 21 | 3 wks | 7.0 |
| 2 | Bank feed (regional bank) | 34 | 6 wks | 5.7 |
| 3 | Calendar | 9 | 2 wks | 4.5 |

**Reasoning**
- **Invoicing sync** has the second-highest demand signal at half the bank feed's cost. It is the best balance on the numbers we have.
- **Bank feed** has the most tickets, but it costs the most and is tied to one regional bank. We don't know what share of customers that bank covers, so its real reach may be narrower than 34 tickets suggest.
- **Calendar** has the fewest tickets, but they skew to your highest-paying tier. Revenue-weighted, it could outrank both others. Ticket count alone can't show this.

Tickets per week of effort is a crude proxy. It ignores revenue, churn and how reliable the effort estimates are.

## What would change the order

- **Calendar moves to #1** if its requesters carry a large share of revenue, or if they churn or threaten to. It is also the cheapest to ship.
- **Bank feed moves to #1** if its tickets come from many unique accounts, and especially if they are at the cancellation stage. A missing bank feed may be a core-product gap rather than a nice-to-have.
- **Invoicing drops** if its 21 tickets come from only a few accounts, or if none of them are connected to cancellations.
- **Any item drops** if the requests turn out to be concentrated in a few loud accounts. That would break your core assumption.
- **A 6-week estimate can slip.** Ask the engineers how confident they are in each number. A 3-week item that runs over is less costly than a 6-week one.

## Questions for the room

- How many customers use the regional bank behind the feed request? Check your own customer data, not a market guess.
- Do any of the requesters have a workaround today, and how painful is it? A few short calls with a handful of requesters, asking about what they did last time rather than what they'd like, would answer this faster than more ticket analysis.
