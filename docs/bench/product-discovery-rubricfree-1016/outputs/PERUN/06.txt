# Recommendation: freeze now, decide by a set date, and don't announce retirement yet

Retirement is probably right on usage alone (about 24 of 610 accounts). But the case rests on two assumptions you haven't checked, and one of them touches 11% of revenue. Test both cheaply, then decide.

## Proposed position for next week
1. **Freeze the builder now.** Fix only critical bugs and add no features. This caps the engineering cost while you learn.
2. **Run two tests** (below), finishing in time for a go/no-go decision at a fixed date. Suggested: end of December, leaving about 10 weeks before the March 2027 renewal.
3. **Don't communicate a sunset to anyone** until the Halvorsen test passes.
4. **If both pass, retire in phases.** Halvorsen moves last, with its replacement running in parallel, and no cutoff comes before their renewal is signed.

## Test 1 (riskiest): can Halvorsen's finance team work without it?
- **Method:** Customer success runs a working session with the finance users. Get the list of reports they build monthly, the fields and filters used, who consumes the output, and what they do with it afterward. Then build a scheduled export or dashboard for their top reports and have them run one real month on it.
- **Also:** Ask them directly how important the builder was to renewal. Right now that claim is secondhand, via the account manager.
- **Pass/kill threshold:** Pass if ___ of ___ monthly reports are reproduced to their sign-off by ___ (date). Set the number as the minimum result you'd accept at renewal. If it fails, keep the builder for Halvorsen and reprice the maintenance cost against their revenue.

## Test 2: can the other ~24 accounts move?
- **Method:** Pull the account list and each account's saved reports. Map each report to an existing dashboard, and flag the ones with no match. Interview ___ accounts about the last report they built, asking what they did and what they'd do without the builder. Don't pitch the sunset in these conversations.
- **Pass/kill threshold:** Pass if ___% of reports map to standard dashboards without custom work. Decide in advance how much of the ARR from these accounts you'd accept putting at risk.

## Where each side is right
- **VP Engineering:** The maintenance and bug cost is real, and the freeze stops it growing.
- **Head of CS:** One account with 11% of revenue shouldn't learn about a retirement before its replacement exists.

The disagreement mostly dissolves into one question: does Test 1 pass? That is a fact to gather, not a position to argue.

## Questions for the meeting
- What decision date and pass thresholds can both leaders agree to now, before the results come in?
- If Halvorsen fails the test, is keeping the builder for one customer acceptable, or is that a reason to rebuild it properly?
