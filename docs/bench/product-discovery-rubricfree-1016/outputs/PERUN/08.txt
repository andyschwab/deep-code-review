# Six-week demand test: dog health kits

**Riskiest assumption:** people will put money down for a kit at about $79. The 15 emails and the supplement purchases show interest in the category, not willingness to pay for this. The test measures what people do (refundable pre-orders), not what they say.

## Week 1: Set the rules before spending anything
Fill in these blanks together. They are yours to set, and nothing should launch until they're written down.
- **Contribution per kit** = price − kit cost − lab fee − shipping − payment fees: $____ (get supplier quotes, not contracts)
- **Fixed cost to launch** (inventory minimum, compliance, setup): $____
- **Units needed to break even** = fixed cost ÷ contribution per kit: ____
- **Max cost per pre-order (cold traffic)** = contribution per kit (or less, if you want a margin): $____
- **Needed reserve rate among subscribers** = units needed ÷ (9,000 × expected share of units from subscribers): ____%

Also route two questions to a vet-industry lawyer or regulatory advisor now: what you may claim about at-home canine health tests, and what is required to sell them. Don't advertise health claims until you have that answer.

## Weeks 2–3: Test A, existing subscribers (about $0)
- Email all 9,000, randomly split into three equal groups seeing $59, $79 or $99.
- Page copy: "Coming soon. Reserve yours with a fully refundable $__ deposit." State plainly that it isn't shipping yet.
- Tag each respondent as supplement buyer or not, to check whether that 30% is where demand sits.
- Measure: deposits per group and per segment.

## Weeks 2–4: Test B, new customers (about $2,500 ads)
- Run the same page at $79 only, targeted at dog owners who don't know you.
- Measure: cost per deposit and click-to-deposit rate.
- Wait for roughly ____ clicks (set in Week 1) before judging.

## Weeks 2–5: Interviews (about $500 in gift cards)
- Call the 15 emailers plus 5–10 supplement buyers.
- Ask only about past behavior: the last time they worried about their dog's health, what they did, what they paid, and what they've already tried.
- Don't pitch. Record what they say verbatim.

## Week 6: Decision
- **Go:** subscriber reserve rate ≥ your Week 1 number at $79, and cold cost per deposit ≤ your ceiling. Interviews should show people already spending on the problem. Next step: a supplier quote and a small pilot batch.
- **Stop:** subscriber reserve rate below half your number, and cold cost per deposit above twice your ceiling.
- **In between:** run one more round (different price or audience) using the reserve. Don't build.
- **Subscribers vs. new customers:** if only subscribers convert, size the business to the subscriber base alone. If only cold traffic converts, the acquisition cost is the business case.
- Refund all deposits if you stop.

Note: Reserve rates will be small, so with a few conversions per group, treat price differences as directional only.
