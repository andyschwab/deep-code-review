# Interview plan: first-time payroll setup (Priya + PM alignment draft)

## What we're trying to learn
The riskiest assumption is that people abandon because setup feels long and intimidating, not because they lack the information. The main rival explanation is that they reached the tax registration step without the numbers or IDs and had no easy way to get them. Interviews should separate these two, and surface any others.

Secondary questions:
- What did owners do at the tax step (stop, search, ask someone, come back)?
- Does having an accountant change who does the work and where it stalls?

## Who to recruit (8 interviews)
**Both groups, weighted to abandoners:**
- **5 abandoners** who stopped at or near the tax registration step. Recruit recently, since recall fades. You set the window.
- **3 finishers** who completed their first payroll. They show what got past the same step and what it cost.

**Accountant status:** Spread it within each group (aim for at least 2 with an accountant and 2 without in the abandoner group). Eight interviews can't establish whether the two behave differently, so treat any pattern as a hypothesis for the next round, not a finding.

**Screen for:** a small business owner, first-time payroll setup, and the person who actually did the setup. Record accountant yes/no and who entered the tax details.

## Before interviewing
Pull the signup data you already hold: time spent on the tax step, return visits, and whether abandoners came back. It costs nothing and gives context for the interviews.

## Rough guide (about 40 minutes)
Ask about the past, not hypotheticals. Don't pitch or show mockups in the first part.

1. **Context:** Tell me about the business and how you handled payroll before this.
2. **The trigger:** Walk me through the last time you sat down to set up payroll. What led to that day?
3. **The path:** What did you do first, then next? Where did you stop?
4. **The tax step (core):**
   - What did you need to enter? Did you have it on hand?
   - If not, what did you do to find it? How long did that take?
   - What were you thinking at that moment? What did you do next?
5. **Rival explanations:** Did you ask anyone for help (accountant, friend, support)? What happened?
6. **Aftermath:** Have you gone back? What are you using now to pay people?
7. **Finishers only:** What nearly made you stop? What got you through?
8. **Last 5 minutes, optional:** Show the mockup and ask them to narrate what they'd do. Treat reactions as weak evidence, not validation.

## Decision rule (you set the numbers before recruiting)
- **Kill/pivot threshold:** If fewer than ___ of 5 abandoners describe length or intimidation as the reason they stopped, the assumption is not supported.
- **How to derive it:** Pick the smallest count that would still justify redesigning the flow around length, then fix it before the first interview.
- **Rival check:** If ___ or more of 5 lacked the information, the redesign should prioritize helping them find it.
- **Cheap behavioral follow-up:** Interviews give stated reasons. A later test, such as a prefilled or "finish later" variant of the tax step measured on completion, would give behavior. Sample size and deadline: ___.

**Open items:** recruiting source and incentive, recency window, who runs the interviews vs. takes notes, recording consent.

*Note: I haven't assumed what you'll hear, so the thresholds above are blank for you to fill in.*
