**Bottom line:** We have early signal of strong value for one segment, but we have not yet shown product-market fit across our base. We are testing whether to focus on graduate students who use citations before committing to that direction.

Responders likely lean toward engaged users, so the true figure for the active base is probably no higher. The very-disappointed group skews toward graduate students using the citation feature. That is a hypothesis to test, not yet a conclusion.

## What the numbers do and don't say

- **24% is about 75 people.** The 72% who were at least somewhat disappointed is encouraging but is the weaker signal. "Somewhat" is the group most likely to leave.
- **Non-response is a big gap.** 87% of those invited did not answer. Your "roughly representative" assumption is untested, and the likely bias runs upward. Treat 24% as a ceiling for the active base.
- **Survivorship.** Only active users were surveyed. Churned users, who may be the ones telling you the product isn't working, are absent.
- **The segment finding is a pattern, not proof.** "Skews toward" tells you who is in the 24%, not how that segment feels overall. If grad-student citation users are, say, 30% of responders, the right number is their own very-disappointed rate, which you have the data to compute but I can't. Check n; a small subgroup can mislead.
- **Narrowing is a bet, not yet a finding.** Whether the segment is big or reachable enough to build a company on is a market question I can't answer. It needs real research.
