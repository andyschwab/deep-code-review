# Recommendation: scheduling feature (for Thursday)

**Recommendation: don't commit the 8 weeks (2 developers) yet. Approve a short test phase to find out whether clinics will switch. Decide after it.**

**What the five calls support**
- 3 of 5 raised scheduling unprompted, which is a real signal of interest.
- 2 of 5 pay about $40/month for a separate tool and said they'd drop it.
- 2 of 5 only called it "nice to have" after being asked. That is weak evidence of demand.

**Why five calls isn't enough**
1. **Sample bias.** All five had recently contacted support, so they may not represent the 220 accounts. We don't know whether they're more engaged, more frustrated, or just different.
2. **Size.** 5 of 220 accounts is too few to estimate how common the need is.
3. **Stated intent isn't behavior.** "Would happily drop it" is a statement. Nobody has yet been shown a replacement or asked to switch.
4. **Riskiest assumption: clinics will leave their current tools even though those tools hold their patient reminders.** If reminders lock clinics in, scheduling without equivalent reminders may not replace anything. This is the assumption to test first.

**What to find out before deciding**

| Question | Cheapest test | Result that kills or pivots the idea |
|---|---|---|
| Is the need common beyond support contacts? | Contact a random sample of the 220, excluding recent support contacts, with 10-15 min calls about how they handle booking today. Ask about past behavior, not whether they'd like a feature. | Fewer than __ of __ describe a booking pain or paid tool |
| Will they switch, given reminders? | Add an in-app "scheduling coming soon, join the pilot" button for all accounts, or invite the 3 interested clinics to a manual pilot (you handle booking and reminders by hand). | Fewer than __ sign up or stay through the pilot |
| Is it worth the build cost? | Compare the build cost (8 weeks x 2 developers) with the revenue or retention you'd need to justify it. | Break-even adoption exceeds what the tests show |

Set the blanks (marked `__`) before running the tests, and set each at the minimum result that would still pay for the build. I haven't supplied numbers because the thresholds are yours to choose.

**Also check now, at no cost**
- Ask the two "nice to have" clinics what booking tool they use and what they'd lose by dropping it.
- Count how many support tickets and feature requests across the 220 accounts mention scheduling.
- Ask the clinics paying $40/month whether they'd pay Northwind for it, and how much. Their current spend shows what they'd save, not what they'd pay us.

**Ask for Thursday**
Approve a test period (you choose the length) with a go/no-go meeting at the end. Build only if the thresholds are met.

**Not yet known:** the share of the 220 accounts that need scheduling, and whether reminders block switching. Neither can be answered from the five calls. Check the tests against the real data you collect before presenting any conclusion.
