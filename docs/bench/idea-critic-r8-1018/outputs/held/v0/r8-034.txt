**Recommendation:** Proceed with the €300k pilot only if the printhead supplier provides a binding delivery guarantee by 30 June 2025; otherwise, defer the capex decision to avoid a costly idle period.

**Three Biggest Failure Modes:**
1.  **Adoption Miscalculation:** The plan assumes 70% of B2B clients will shift to smaller, custom orders. If clients prefer bulk pricing and only 30% adopt custom printing, revenue will fail to cover the high per-unit operational costs of digital printing, extending the break-even period beyond 36 months.
2.  **Supply Chain & Capacity Trap:** If printhead lead times slip to Q3 2026 as feared, the €2.2M equipment sits idle while fixed costs accrue. Simultaneously, running low-volume custom jobs (150 sqm) alongside standard runs (300 sqm) on existing lines during the transition could disrupt the current 15,000 sqm/month throughput, damaging core revenue.
3.  **Hidden Operational Costs:** Benchmarking against the competitor's lower energy draw without factoring their higher maintenance contract creates a false cost baseline. If maintenance and ink waste for custom short-runs exceed projections by even 15%, the projected 18% revenue lift will be erased by operational drag.

**Halt Trigger:**
Stop the project immediately if the six-month pilot (Sept 2025–Feb 2026) yields less than **40% client participation** (fewer than 48 of 120 accounts placing orders) or if the average order size drops below **100 sqm**, indicating insufficient margin density to sustain the new machinery.

*Note: The 18-month break-even relies entirely on the 70% adoption rate; a sensitivity analysis on a 40% rate is essential before final sign-off. The competitor's maintenance costs must be quantified before finalizing the OPEX model.*
