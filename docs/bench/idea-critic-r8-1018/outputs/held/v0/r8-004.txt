**Recommendation: Proceed with a "Go-If" Pilot**
Authorize the $285,000 pilot (development + pilot costs + support buffer) strictly under the condition that it runs as a time-boxed experiment from September 1 to November 30, 2024. Do not commit to a full rollout until the pilot validates the conversion math. The potential $240,000 annual revenue lift plus churn reduction justifies the risk, but only if the assumed 30% conversion rate holds true against real user behavior and competitor stability.

**Three Most Plausible Failure Modes**
1.  **Conversion Overestimation:** The projected 30% conversion of 40,000 free visitors (yielding 1,800 subs) fails to materialize because free users are less price-sensitive than assumed, resulting in a net revenue loss once the $285,000 cost is amortized.
2.  **Hidden Operational Drag:** The 15% churn reduction is erased by a spike in support tickets exceeding the $12,000 staffing buffer, causing net revenue to stagnate while customer satisfaction scores drop due to slower response times.
3.  **Competitor Counter-Move:** MetroPulse responds to the redesign by launching a aggressive price war or a superior feature set during the pilot window, suppressing Inkforge's acquisition rate and invalidating the static market assumptions used in the projection.

**Pre-Agreed Stop/Pivot Trigger**
Terminate the pilot and revert to the legacy design immediately if the **conversion rate of free visitors to paid subscribers falls below 18% by October 15, 2024**. This threshold represents the break-even point where the incremental revenue covers the pilot's burn rate; falling below it indicates the 30% target is unachievable and continuing would destroy capital.

*Note: The current projection excludes legal review costs; ensure this line item is quantified before signing the development contract. The "Go-If" structure protects the organization from the MetroPulse uncertainty by limiting exposure to the pilot phase only.*
