**Recommendation: NO-GO in current form; proceed only as a GO-IF pilot.**

Do not sign the three-year, €1.5M license yet. The proposal fails basic stress testing because the cost structure is undefined and the adoption math is dangerously optimistic. You are committing to a fixed €1.5M cost based on a variable per-title fee you do not know, while assuming 9,600 patrons (80% of 12,000) will adopt a new service in six months without a marketing budget or historical precedent. This creates a scenario where you could pay maximum license fees for minimum usage.

Furthermore, your total cost calculation is incomplete. You identified €250k for custom integration but explicitly excluded ongoing maintenance. For a custom bridge between a vendor platform and a legacy catalog, annual maintenance typically runs 15–20% of the build cost (€37.5k–€50k/year), adding over €100k to the three-year term. The "missing something" you suspect is likely this recurring operational debt plus the risk of the per-title fee exceeding your marginal budget.

**Main Failure Modes:**
1.  **The Unit-Economics Trap:** If the undisclosed per-title fee is high, the library hits its content budget cap quickly, leaving the platform empty despite the expensive license, causing patron churn.
2.  **Adoption Delusion:** Achieving 80% adoption in six months requires aggressive behavior change. If actual adoption lands at a more typical 20–30%, the cost-per-active-user skyrockets, making the €1.5M license an inefficient use of funds compared to incremental improvements to current services.
3.  **Integration Fragility:** Custom development without a maintained SLA leads to breakage during catalog updates, rendering the e-books unsearchable and frustrating patrons.

**Kill Criteria & Pivot Trigger:**
Halt or pivot the project immediately if the **Cost Per Active User (CPUA)** exceeds **€5.00** within the first six months post-launch.
*   *Calculation:* (Total Annualized Cost) / (Number of Unique Monthly Active Users).
*   *Action:* If CPUA > €5.00 after Month 6, invoke the contract's exit clause (if negotiable now) or freeze further license renewals, shifting to a pay-per-loan model or renegotiating the flat fee based on actual usage data.

*Note: You must demand the per-title fee schedule and a capped maintenance SLA before any further discussion. Do not rely on the vendor's 30% circulation increase claim without a penalty clause for underperformance.*
