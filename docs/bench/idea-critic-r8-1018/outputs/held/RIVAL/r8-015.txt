**Recommendation: Do not proceed in the current form.**

The financial model contains a critical error that renders the project insolvent before it begins. You have budgeted $150,000 for operations but only accounted for $158,000 in costs ($150k + $8k marketing), ignoring the licensing fees. At €200 per location across 12 stops, this is a minor addition (~$2,600). However, your revenue calculation assumes €30 tickets generate $180,000 USD. If tickets are sold in Euros, €30 × 5,000 visitors = €150,000 revenue. At a standard 1.1 exchange rate, this is only ~$165,000 USD.

If you sell in Euros: Revenue (€150k) minus Costs (€138k ops + €2.4k licenses) leaves a €9,600 surplus (~$10,500). This is a **7% margin**, not 10%, and provides almost no buffer for the "unknown" licensing realities or lower attendance. If you sell in USD but pay costs in EUR, currency fluctuation alone could wipe out your profit. The assumption of "free space" is also a single point of failure; if even three venues require a modest fee, you break even at best.

**Three Most Likely Failure Points:**

1.  **Regulatory Blockade:** The "rough estimate" of €200 for alcohol/cheese licensing is dangerously optimistic. France has strict *débit de boissons* laws. If any region classifies your event as a commercial bar operation rather than a private tasting, fees could jump to €2,000+ per stop or require a local license holder you haven't budgeted for.
2.  **Attendance Concentration Risk:** Aiming for 420 visitors per stop assumes uniform demand. In reality, you may get 800 in Paris and 150 in rural Auvergne. Since venue/staff costs are fixed at $12,500, underperforming stops bleed cash that high-performing stops cannot cover due to the thin margin.
3.  **Venue Withdrawal:** Relying on free venues without signed contracts is fatal. If a host pulls out 30 days prior, last-minute rentals will exceed your $12,500 cap, instantly turning a profit into a loss.

**Kill Trigger:**

Cancel the tour immediately if **pre-sales do not reach 1,500 tickets (30% of forecast) by 1 May 2025**, or if **any single regional licensing fee exceeds €1,000**. The first indicates a fundamental demand mismatch; the second breaks the unit economics irreparably.
