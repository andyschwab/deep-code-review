**Recommendation:** Proceed with the June 1 launch, but strictly as a limited "Founder's Edition" capsule collection rather than a full-scale replacement for human perfumers. Position the AI as a "digital nose" that democratizes access to bespoke scent, while explicitly marketing the human perfumer's role in final quality control. This mitigates the trust deficit by keeping the human artisan as the ultimate guarantor of quality, allowing you to validate the €0.12 inference model and conversion rates without risking the brand's luxury equity on an unproven machine-generated promise.

**Three Biggest Failure Modes:**

1.  **The "Soulless Algorithm" Backlash:** Luxury perfume relies on storytelling, heritage, and the myth of the creator. If customers perceive the scent as "calculated" rather than "crafted," the brand loses its premium justification. A wave of reviews claiming the scent lacks "depth" or "emotion" compared to human-made blends could contaminate the entire Maison Lune portfolio, causing existing customers to question the authenticity of their favorite classics.
2.  **Unit Economics Collapse via Low Conversion:** Your model assumes a 30% conversion rate (12,000 bottles from 40,000 users). In luxury e-commerce, 30% is exceptionally aggressive; a more realistic 5–10% conversion would leave you with 2,000–4,000 bottles sold. At that volume, the €45,000 development cost becomes a massive drag, and the fixed costs of bottling, packaging, and marketing per unit skyrocket, potentially turning the projected €720,000 profit into a significant loss.
3.  **Olfactory Hallucination and Returns:** Unlike visual AI, scent cannot be previewed digitally. If the AI misinterprets user inputs (e.g., confusing "woody" with "smoky") and delivers a disagreeable blend, return rates could spike. Since custom blends often cannot be resold, a 20% return rate due to "scent mismatch" would obliterate margins and create a logistical nightmare of hazardous waste disposal.

**Halting Trigger:**
Halt the project immediately if, within the first 30 days post-launch, the **return rate due to "scent dissatisfaction" exceeds 15%** or if the **actual conversion rate from questionnaire to purchase falls below 8%**. Either metric indicates a fundamental product-market fit failure that additional marketing spend cannot fix.
