**Recommendation:** Proceed with the pilot, but **condition the full RM 1.8 million operational commitment on hitting a strict adoption threshold** at the six-month mark. Do not authorize the three-year operational budget until the pilot proves the 80% faculty participation assumption is realistic.

**Three Most Likely Failure Modes:**

1.  **The "80% Fantasy" Collapse:** Faculty inertia is historically high in repository deployments. If actual deposition rates hover near 20–30% rather than 80%, the cost-per-paper skyrockets from ~RM 50 to over RM 150. The platform becomes an expensive ghost town, undermining the Provost's open-access mandate and making future funding impossible.
2.  **Workflow Friction Rejection:** If the ingestion process requires manual metadata entry or lacks seamless integration with existing HR/research management systems, faculty will abandon it immediately. A "build it and they will come" approach fails when the deposit workflow adds >15 minutes to a researcher's administrative burden.
3.  **Content Quality & Compliance Gaps:** Without automated checks for publisher embargoes and version control (pre-print vs. post-print), the repository risks hosting non-compliant PDFs. This exposes the university to copyright infringement claims and damages credibility with major publishers, potentially leading to takedown notices that empty the repository.

**Kill/Pause Trigger Metric:**

**"Verified Active Deposition Rate" at Month 6.**
Pause the full rollout if **fewer than 4,000 papers** (approx. 33% of the projected 12,000 annual run rate, or ~670 papers/month) have been successfully ingested and verified by the end of the pilot. This indicates the 80% participation assumption is fundamentally flawed and requires a strategic pivot before spending the remaining operational budget.

*Note: The RM 2.5 million capital build should include a mandatory "one-click" integration layer with current faculty profiling systems; without this, adoption will likely fail regardless of incentives.*
