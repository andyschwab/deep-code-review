Do not commit to the firm-wide rollout yet. Authorize the R2.5 million build strictly for the July 1 pilot with the seven specified users, but treat the firm-wide expansion as a separate future decision contingent on two gates: (1) validated efficiency metrics from the pilot and (2) written regulatory clarity. **Three Biggest Failure Modes**

1.  **Regulatory Rejection of Custody Model:** The South African legal regulator may rule that blockchain hashes do not constitute valid "original" file custody or that decentralized storage violates client confidentiality statutes. If the regulator deems the blockchain layer non-compliant, the entire system becomes unusable for client files, rendering the R2.5 million investment a total loss rather than a partial setback.
2.  **The "Last Mile" Adoption Gap:** The 15% overall efficiency gain assumes the 30% retrieval speedup translates linearly to billable work. This often fails because lawyers spend more time managing the new system, verifying blockchain receipts, or dealing with false positives than the raw retrieval speed saves. If the senior associates find the interface cumbersome or the verification steps tedious, they will bypass the system, leaving the R750,000 savings unrealized.
3.  **Hidden Integration & Maintenance Costs:** The 2% annual maintenance quote (R50,000) is likely unrealistic for a bespoke blockchain integration. It probably excludes the cost of node hosting, key management security audits, and the engineering hours required to patch breaks when the underlying chain updates. Real-world maintenance for legal-tech blockchain pilots often runs 10–15% of build cost, which would erase your projected net savings in year two.

**Pre-Agreed Halt/Pivot Trigger**

**Trigger Date:** 31 December 2024 (End of Pilot Phase 1)

*   The regulator has not issued specific guidance permitting blockchain-based file custody by this date; OR

If either condition is met, pivot immediately to a traditional centralized database architecture using the existing codebase where possible, or terminate the project to preserve capital.
