**Recommendation: Do not proceed with the current plan.**

While the capital funding is secured, the proposal relies on fragile assumptions regarding climate stability and labor that threaten the collective's financial solvency. Specifically, allocating 80% of your liquid reserves ($4,000 of $5,000) to cover a cost overrun leaves no buffer for the unknown maintenance fees ($400–$900/year) or immediate operational shocks. You must renegotiate the scope to preserve at least $2,500 in reserves or secure a dedicated maintenance endowment before breaking ground.

**Three Biggest Failure Modes:**

1.  **Climate Variance Risk:** The model assumes rainfall remains static at 110 mm/month. A single dry season matching historical lows (e.g., 20% below average) would drop capture volume below 50% of needs, forcing expensive municipal water purchases that were not budgeted.
2.  **Labor Volatility:** The plan assumes "community maintenance" at zero cost. If volunteer participation drops by even 30% due to seasonal migration or burnout, you will be forced to hire external labor, instantly eroding the project's ROI and draining the already depleted reserves.
3.  **Reserve Depletion:** By committing 80% of your cash reserves to installation, the collective becomes insolvent regarding other emergencies. If the maintenance fee hits the $900 upper bound in Year 1, you will lack the liquidity to pay it without halting other critical garden operations.

**Stop/Redesign Trigger:**

 Halt operations or trigger an immediate redesign if **monthly captured volume falls below 9,000 liters (60% of projection) for two consecutive months during the wet season**, or if **volunteer labor hours drop below 15 hours per month for any single month post-installation.**
