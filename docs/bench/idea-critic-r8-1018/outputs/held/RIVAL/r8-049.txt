**Recommendation: MODIFY & PAUSE CAPITAL COMMITMENT**
Do not approve the full $14.5M spend yet. Proceed immediately with R&D ($2.5M) but place the $12M manufacturing line on hold until Ministry of Health (MoH) clearance is granted in writing. The current plan exposes the company to catastrophic loss by betting on regulatory approval and insurance reimbursement simultaneously without a validated pathway.

**Three Main Failure Modes**
1.  **Regulatory & Reimbursement Misalignment:** The MoH may grant device clearance but delay or deny the specific reimbursement code required to hit the $350 price point. Without the 80% coverage, the device becomes prohibitively expensive for most patients, collapsing the volume model.
2.  **The "Missing Million" Insolvency:** The plan excludes the mandatory $1.2M post-market surveillance and software update costs. In medtech, these are not optional; failing to budget for them creates an immediate liquidity crisis post-launch, potentially halting operations just as sales begin.
3.  **Break-Even Delusion:** The projection assumes selling 150,000 units (75% of total annual capacity) to break even. Achieving 75% capacity utilization in year one is statistically rare for a new medical device, especially with unknown reimbursement timelines. If sales hit only 50,000 units, the company burns cash on idle manufacturing capacity rather than reaching profitability.

**Kill/Pivot Trigger**
**Trigger:** If MoH clearance is not received by **Q3 2025** OR if the confirmed reimbursement rate is **below 60%**.
**Action:** Immediately terminate the manufacturing line investment. Pivot to a licensing model where PulseQuotient Med sells the technology to an established local manufacturer who already holds distribution channels and regulatory leverage, rather than building our own factory.

**Board Math Corrections**
*   **True Break-Even:** You cannot calculate break-even without including the $1.2M surveillance cost. The real break-even point is likely closer to 160,000–170,000 units, which exceeds realistic Year 1 demand.
*   **Capital Efficiency:** Committing $12M to manufacturing before securing the reimbursement code is premature. Use the pause to validate the payer mix; if insurance coverage is <80%, the unit economics fail regardless of production volume.

*Note: The board will likely reject any plan that treats regulatory approval as a certainty rather than a binary risk event. Ensure the revised proposal explicitly ties the manufacturing spend to the receipt of the MoH reimbursement code.*
