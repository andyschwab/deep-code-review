**Recommendation: Proceed with a strictly capped pilot, but reject the current financial model and valuation assumptions.**

Do not approve the full $1.5M integration spend yet. Instead, authorize a "Lite Integration" pilot costing under $200k that manually reconciles points for the first 90 days. Your current model assumes Pacific Retail members value airline points equally to retail points; this is almost certainly false. Airline points are high-aspiration but low-utility for non-travelers, whereas retail points are cash-equivalent. Without data proving conversion, spending $1.5M upfront is speculative gambling, not strategy. Furthermore, your projected revenue implies a massive uptake that contradicts the competitor's 10% lift unless their market saturation was already high. Validate the *conversion rate* before building the pipe.

**How This Plan Could Fail:**
1.  **Valuation Mismatch:** Pacific members ignore the offer because they don't fly often enough to care, resulting in <2% signup versus your implicit ~15-20% requirement to hit revenue targets.
2.  **Margin Erosion:** If redemption hits your 30% estimate but the average ticket price drops to $95 (matching the competitor), your revenue falls to ~$3.3M while liabilities remain high, turning the partnership net-negative after integration costs.
3.  **Data Friction:** The $250k/year data sharing cost underestimates the legal and technical friction of syncing real-time spend data between a retailer and an airline, causing delayed point posting and immediate customer service backlash.

**Kill/Pause Triggers:**
*   **Pause Trigger:** If **signup conversion is below 4%** after the first 60 days of the pilot. This indicates the core value proposition (airline points for retail spend) does not resonate, and scaling would only amplify marketing waste.
*   **Kill Trigger:** If the **cost per acquired active flyer exceeds $85**. Given your $120 average ticket price and typical airline margins, acquiring a customer for $85 leaves no room for profit after operational costs, making the lifetime value (LTV) negative regardless of revenue volume.

*Note: Renegotiate the $1.5M upfront cost to be milestone-based (e.g., $500k on launch, $1M only after 50k active users). Do not sign a fixed multi-year data contract until the pilot proves the unit economics work.*
