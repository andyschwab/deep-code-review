**Recommendation: Proceed with a Conditional Pilot (One Unit Only)**

Do not launch the full three-unit fleet immediately. The financial model relies on unverified assumptions regarding patient volume and regulatory speed, creating excessive exposure. Instead, deploy a single unit as a controlled pilot. This limits capital risk to $150,000 while validating the critical "40 patients per day" assumption and the actual regulatory timeline. If the single unit hits 80% of its volume target within the first 90 days of operation, trigger the expansion to the full fleet. If it fails to hit this mark, halt expansion and re-scope the service model before committing the remaining $300,000.

**Top Three Failure Modes**

1.  **Financial Failure (Volume Shortfall):** The model assumes 40 patients/day/unit immediately. If real-world uptake averages only 25 patients/day due to rural awareness or transport barriers, annual revenue drops by ~37%. Combined with fixed operational costs ($30k/month/unit), the fleet would burn approximately $180,000 annually instead of generating profit, depleting reserves within 18 months.
2.  **Operational Failure (Unit Utilization):** The budget assumes 20 operating days per month. In rural contexts, vehicle breakdowns, severe weather, or staff shortages can easily reduce this to 14 days. Every lost day represents 40 missed patients and unrecoverable fixed costs (staff/fuel), causing marginal costs to exceed marginal revenue on those days.
3.  **Compliance Failure (Regulatory Delay):** The 3–6 month regulatory estimate is informal. If state approval takes 9+ months or imposes unexpected route restrictions, you incur holding costs (storage, insurance, staff retention) with zero revenue. A delay beyond 6 months turns the $450k capital outlay into a sunk cost with no path to ROI in the projected timeframe.

**Concrete Halt/Re-scope Trigger**

**Trigger:** If, after six months of *active operation* (post-approval), the fleet-wide average patient volume is below **30 patients per day per unit** OR if the net cash flow remains negative for three consecutive months despite reaching 25 patients/day.

**Action:** Immediately suspend the deployment of any remaining units. Conduct a root-cause analysis to determine if the deficit is due to market demand (requires marketing pivot) or operational friction (requires process fix). Do not release further capital until the unit economics are proven at the single-unit level.
