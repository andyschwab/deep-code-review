**Recommendation: Proceed with a Conditional Pilot, Not a Full Rollout**

Approve the $165,200 expenditure only as a **90-day limited pilot** covering 4 sensors (not 12) in the densest canopy zones. Release funds for the remaining 8 sensors and the full integration fee only after verifying signal reliability and achieving a minimum 12% water reduction at the 90-day mark. This caps initial exposure at ~$74,000 while stress-testing the vendor's primary risk factor.

**Stress Test: Failure Modes & Flawed Assumptions**

*   **Technical Failure (High Probability):** The vendor's 18% savings claim relies on consistent data transmission. Your central park's dense canopy creates a "unknown" wireless environment. If signal packet loss exceeds 5%, the system cannot optimize irrigation, rendering the sensors useless dumb posts. The assumption that coastal case study data applies to your specific microclimate and canopy density is unverified.
*   **Financial Flaw:** The "within budget" argument ignores the **sunk cost trap**. If the system fails at month 7, you have spent $165k with zero return. The maintenance cost ($1,200/sensor/year) is unusually high for passive sensors, suggesting potential hidden subscription fees for data processing or cellular connectivity that may escalate post-contract.
*   **Operational Risk:** Integration complexity is understated. The $20,000 fee likely covers basic API handshakes, not the engineering hours required to map 12 new data streams into your existing SCADA or maintenance workflows. If your team cannot interpret the alerts, the hardware becomes shelf-ware.

**Kill/Re-evaluate Triggers**

1.  **The "Canopy Kill-Switch":** If data uptime drops below 95% over any 14-day period during the pilot due to signal interference, terminate the contract immediately. Do not accept "firmware updates" as a fix during the pilot; the hardware must work as sold.
2.  **The Efficiency Floor:** If verified water reduction is **below 10%** after 90 days (adjusted for rainfall variance), trigger a re-evaluation. The 18% claim is marketing; 10% is the break-even for operational hassle. Below this, the ROI does not justify the maintenance overhead.
3.  **Cost Creep:** If the vendor identifies any additional costs for data hosting, SIM cards, or "premium support" not explicitly in the $1,200 maintenance line item, pause deployment.

**Hidden Costs & Flaws to Challenge**

*   **Battery Replacement Cycle:** Ask explicitly: "What is the battery life under *our* transmission conditions?" If dense canopy forces higher transmission power, batteries may die in 18 months, not the quoted 5 years, tripling your labor costs for replacements.
*   **The "Integration" Black Box:** Demand a line-item breakdown of the $20,000 integration fee. If it includes custom coding, you are owning that code forever. Ensure it covers full staff training, not just software installation.
*   **Rainfall Variance:** Your 45mm average is a baseline. If the coming year is unusually wet, the *percentage* saved will drop artificially (you can't save water you don't use), making the 18% target harder to hit through no fault of the system. Ensure the contract adjusts savings targets based on total precipitation deviation from the 10-year average.

**Note:** Present the 4-sensor pilot as a fiscally responsible "validation phase" rather than hesitation. This aligns with your duty to protect public funds while remaining open to innovation. Ensure the contract includes a clause allowing immediate termination for cause if the wireless uptime trigger is breached.
