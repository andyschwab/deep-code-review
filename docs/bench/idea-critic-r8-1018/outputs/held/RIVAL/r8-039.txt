**Recommendation: Proceed with a Conditional Pilot, Not Full Deployment.**
Do not commit the full $12M yet. The math is fragile: your $9M Year 2 revenue projection assumes ~150,000 active subscribers (based on $5 ARPU). Covering 250,000 households implies a 60% take rate immediately, which is unrealistic for rural 5G without proven demand. Furthermore, your $9M capex budget ($200k × 45 towers) leaves zero contingency for the "low-six-figure" spectrum fees or power infrastructure upgrades. If spectrum costs $500k per band and you need three bands, you are instantly $1.5M over budget before laying fiber. Approve only the $2M pilot phase to validate the true power costs, spectrum fees, and actual take rate in one district (e.g., Kogi) before releasing funds for Benue and Niger.

**Three Biggest Failure Modes:**
1.  **Power Infrastructure Collapse:** The 95% grid reliability assumption is likely optimistic for rural Nigeria in 2024. If reliability is actually 80%, your diesel/solar hybrid costs will triple, turning profitable towers into cash drains within six months.
2.  **Spectrum Fee Shock:** If the regulator defines "low-six-figure" as $900k per MHz rather than $100k, your licensing bill could exceed the entire tower construction budget, destroying the project's ROI before launch.
3.  **ARPU Delusion:** Rural users may not upgrade to 5G data plans at the projected rate. If the average revenue lift is only $2 instead of $5, you will never recover the $12M investment, as the break-even point moves from Year 3 to Year 7+.

**Specific Halt/Re-scope Trigger:**
**Stop full deployment immediately if, after the 6-month Kogi pilot, the blended ARPU increase is below $3.00 OR if the total cost per connected household (including spectrum and power backup) exceeds $65.**
At $3.00 ARPU, your Year 2 revenue drops to $5.4M, making the $12M spend unrecoverable within a reasonable timeframe. At $65 per household, the total project cost balloons to $16.25M, requiring an unrealistic 80% market penetration to break even.
