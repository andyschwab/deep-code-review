**Recommendation: Do not approve the full rollout plan yet; authorize a strictly capped 45-day technical feasibility study before committing to the June 15 beta.**

The financial model is currently dangerous because it conflates revenue with profit. You are projecting $12,000 in extra *profit* based on a 10% lift in *revenue*. Unless your taproom operates on an 80–90% contribution margin (which is virtually impossible in hospitality due to COGS, labor, and rent), a 10% revenue lift will not yield $12,000 in pure profit. You must re-model this using your actual contribution margin per pint. Additionally, the 3% transaction fee on a $15 pour yields only $0.45 per transaction. Even with high volume, this will likely cover less than 20% of your $1,500 monthly maintenance fee, leaving a significant ongoing cash bleed that the current plan treats as "offset."

**The Three Biggest Failure Modes:**

1.  **Margin Illusion & Cash Burn:** The project fails financially not because adoption is low, but because the unit economics are wrong. If the actual profit lift is only 2–3% of revenue rather than the modeled 10%, you will incur high fixed maintenance costs ($18k/year) against negligible marginal gains, turning a "profit center" into a sunk cost within six months.
2.  **The Square Integration Black Box:** You have unidentified technical debt. If your current Square setup requires custom middleware to talk to this gamified app, the "unknown integration effort" could easily explode from a few days to several weeks of engineering time. This threatens the June 15 launch date and could introduce point-of-sale latency that frustrates staff and customers during peak hours.
3.  **Friction-Heavy Adoption:** You are assuming 40% of visitors (720 people/month) will download a dedicated app. In the current mobile landscape, asking a customer to download a standalone app just to buy a beer is a massive friction point. If adoption lands at 5% instead of 40%, the network effects required for "gamification" (leaderboards, social proof) vanish, and the app becomes a ghost town that signals disengagement rather than loyalty.

**The Kill Trigger:**

Terminate the project immediately if, after the first 60 days of the beta:
*   **Active Usage < 15% of downloads:** If fewer than 15% of users who download the app use it more than twice a month, the engagement model is broken.
*   **OR Net Operational Loss > $1,200/month:** If the combination of maintenance fees and staff time spent troubleshooting the app exceeds the verified *contribution margin* gain by more than $1,200 for two consecutive months.

*Note: Do not mention the $8,000 development cost in the board deck as a recurring risk; that is a sunk one-time cost. Focus the board's anxiety on the recurring $1,500/month burn rate versus unverified margin lifts.*
