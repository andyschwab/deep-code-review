**Recommendation: Proceed with a controlled pilot, not a full launch.**

Do not commit to 100,000 units per quarter immediately. Instead, launch a 25,000-unit pilot in a single region to validate the "unknown" logistics costs and actual conversion rates before scaling. Your current model assumes a gross margin of roughly $8.99 per refillable unit ($12.99 price - $3.20 bottle - $0.80 logistics), which appears healthy compared to the standard $10.89 margin. However, this relies entirely on the unverified $0.80 logistics estimate and an optimistic 30% immediate adoption rate. A pilot limits your exposure to ~$80,000 in additional production costs while generating the real-world data needed to confirm unit economics.

**Primary Failure Modes:**

1.  **Logistics Cost Explosion:** The $0.80 estimate is a critical vulnerability. If return rates are lower than expected or cleaning/damage rates are higher, this cost could easily double to $1.60+, eroding the margin advantage entirely. Unlike standard PET bottles, you are now managing a reverse supply chain, which is historically complex and expensive.
2.  **Adoption Friction:** The survey measures *intent*, not behavior. Customers may say they will switch for a 10% price parity, but the friction of returning empty bottles often suppresses actual conversion to below 10%. If actual conversion is 10% instead of 30%, you will be left with 70,000 units of expensive glass inventory per quarter that cannot be sold through your standard channels.
3.  **Breakage and Shrinkage:** Glass breaks. If your breakage rate during consumer use or return shipping exceeds 5-8%, the replacement cost of the $3.20 glass vessel will destroy profitability faster than logistics costs will.

**Halting Trigger:**

Establish a hard stop if the **Net Loss Per Bottle exceeds $0.40** after the first two quarters of the pilot.

*Calculation logic:* Your current buffer between the refillable margin and the standard PET margin is roughly $1.90 ($10.89 vs $8.99 estimated). However, once you factor in the risk of unsold inventory and potential price promotions to move glass stock, a loss of $0.40 per unit indicates the system is fundamentally broken, not just underperforming. Do not wait for a net loss of $0.50; at that point, you are actively destroying value faster than the brand equity gain can justify. If the pilot shows a unit loss >$0.40 by the end of Q2, terminate the program and revert to standard PET.
