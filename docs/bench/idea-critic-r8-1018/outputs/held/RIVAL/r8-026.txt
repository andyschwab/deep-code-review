**Recommendation:** Proceed with the pilot, but **only** after validating the reverse-logistics cost. Your current €25 shipping assumption is a critical vulnerability; if actual sofa return shipping exceeds €45, your 45% gross margin target collapses. Do not commit to the full Q3 2025 marketing spend until a live test of 50 returns confirms the true landed cost.

**Three Main Failure Modes:**
1.  **Logistics Cost Spiral:** If reverse shipping averages €50+ instead of €25, refurbishment costs rise to €80/unit. Combined with churn, this pushes gross margin below 20%, making the unit economics insolvent.
2.  **Churn Compounding:** An 8% quarterly churn implies ~29% annual churn. If quality issues or competitor pricing (CasaNova) push this to 12% quarterly (~43% annual), you must acquire 650 new customers annually just to hold steady at 1,500 subs, blowing out the CAC payback period.
3.  **Refurbishment Bottleneck:** If only 60% (vs. 80%) of returns are refurbishable within two weeks due to damage or parts scarcity, inventory availability drops. This forces new manufacturing at higher costs to service renewals, eroding the circular economy margin advantage.

**Kill Trigger:** Terminate the initiative if **verified gross margin falls below 30% for two consecutive months** post-pilot launch, or if **quarterly churn exceeds 10% in Q2 2026** despite retention interventions.
