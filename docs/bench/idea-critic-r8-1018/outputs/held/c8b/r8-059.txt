**Recommendation: Postpone** until carrier adoption variance and regulatory audit requirements are quantified. Proceeding with a 1.35M BRL commitment while two fundamental drivers of cost and timeline remain unknown creates an unacceptable risk of stranded assets and operational friction.

**Top Three Failure Modes:**
1. **Carrier Resistance & Fragmentation:** If mandatory hardware upgrades trigger resistance, adoption may stall well below the required threshold. Unlike voluntary pilots, a mandate without proven carrier buy-in risks creating a dual-system environment where manual workarounds persist, eroding the projected 12% efficiency gain and potentially increasing coordination costs.
2. **Regulatory Stop-Work Order:** Brazil's data-localization laws are stringent. If the regulator mandates a separate, lengthy compliance audit for the blockchain ledger's node distribution or data storage logic, the 6-month rollout timeline will collapse. This could lock capital in non-compliant infrastructure while legal reviews proceed indefinitely.
3. **Transaction Fee Volatility:** The business case relies on a static 0.02% fee. Blockchain networks often experience fee spikes during congestion. A modest increase to 0.05% would significantly erode the 2.1M BRL annual savings, extending the payback period beyond the acceptable window and invalidating the core ROI argument.

**Kill Criteria (Stop/Pivot Triggers):**
*   **Adoption Rate:** Less than **70% of carrier partners** fully integrated and using the new protocol within **90 days** of the pilot start.
*   **Cost Overrun:** Total implementation costs (hardware + consulting + licensing) exceed the budget by **>15%** (i.e., total spend > 1.55M BRL).
*   **Regulatory Delay:** No written confirmation of compliance pathway from the regulator within **45 days** of project initiation.

*Note: The "paper-to-system" cost baseline of 2.1M BRL should be re-verified against actual Q3-Q4 invoices to ensure the 12% target is grounded in current spend, not averages.
