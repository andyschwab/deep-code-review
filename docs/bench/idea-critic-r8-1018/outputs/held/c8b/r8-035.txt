**Recommendation:** Proceed with a **Go-If** condition: authorize the RM 2.5M capital build only if a binding pre-commitment from 40% of department heads (representing ~6,000 papers) is secured by 1 March 2025. Do not commit to the full three-year operational budget until the six-month pilot validates a deposit rate of at least 50% of the projected volume.

**Three Most Likely Failure Modes:**
1.  **The "80% Fantasy" Collapse:** Faculty behavior rarely matches administrative assumptions without mandates. If actual adoption lands at 30% (3,600 papers) instead of 80%, the cost-per-paper skyrockets from ~RM 350 to ~RM 940, destroying the ROI case and leaving a costly, empty repository that damages institutional prestige.
2.  **Publisher Embargo Conflicts:** Without a verified legal sweep of existing publishing contracts, the university may ingest papers it lacks the right to make open access. This triggers takedown notices, legal friction with major publishers, and a "broken link" user experience that erodes trust in the hub immediately upon launch.
3.  **Operational Drag:** The estimated RM 600k/year assumes smooth ingestion. If metadata cleaning and format normalization require manual intervention for 50% of submissions (common in early stages), operational costs will double to handle the labor, forcing a budget overrun or a reduction in service quality within year one.

**Kill Criterion:**
Pause all further operational spending if, by 31 December 2025 (end of pilot), the repository contains fewer than **4,000 verified, open-access full texts**. This threshold represents roughly 33% of the optimistic annual target; failing to hit this indicates the voluntary adoption model is non-viable without coercive policy changes that were not part of the original proposal.

**Note:** The 80% adoption assumption is the single point of failure; treat it as a hypothesis to be disproven, not a baseline. Ensure the legal team validates copyright status *before* the July launch to prevent immediate reputational damage.
