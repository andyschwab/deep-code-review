**Recommendation: Proceed with a "Go-If" Pilot, Not Full Deployment.**
Do not commit to the full £105,000 rollout yet. Instead, propose a 3-month pilot on **two** machines (6 sensors) to validate the integration effort and false-positive rates before scaling. This reduces initial exposure to ~£25,000 while generating the specific data needed to confirm the 14-month payback claim.

**Three Biggest Failure Modes:**
1.  **Hidden Integration Costs:** The assumption that data feeds "straight into" the existing ERP without custom work is the highest risk. If API connectors or middleware are required, implementation costs could double, destroying the ROI timeline.
2.  **Alert Fatigue from False Positives:** A 15% false-positive rate on 36 machines could generate hundreds of unnecessary maintenance tickets monthly. This wastes technician hours and risks operators ignoring genuine critical alerts, potentially worsening downtime.
3.  **Overestimated Downtime Savings:** The projection assumes a linear reduction (8 days to 4). If the remaining 4 days of downtime are caused by issues sensors cannot detect (e.g., operator error, supply chain delays, or non-mechanical failures), the actual savings will be far lower than the projected £120,000.

**Pre-Agreed Kill Trigger:**
Terminate the project immediately if, after the 3-month pilot on two machines, the **verified reduction in unplanned downtime is less than 30%** of the baseline or if the **false-positive rate exceeds 10%**, requiring more than 4 hours of weekly manual verification per machine.

*Note: Correct your total capital expenditure slide to £105,000 (£90k sensors + £15k installation) before the meeting; presenting £100,000 undermines credibility on the detailed financials.*
*Ensure the pilot contract explicitly states that any custom integration work discovered during the trial is billed at a fixed cap or absorbed by the vendor.*
