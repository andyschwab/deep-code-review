**Recommendation: Proceed with a conditional "Go" (Go-If)**
Launch the pilot only if a binding legal opinion confirms Greek data-privacy regulations allow the proposed level of personalization before June 1, 2025, and if a pre-pilot willingness-to-pay test validates a premium of at least €150 per booking. Without these two gates, the financial model collapses due to regulatory risk and unverified revenue assumptions.

**Three Most Likely Failure Modes**
1.  **Regulatory Block:** Greek authorities interpret current privacy laws to restrict the specific data inputs required for AI curation, rendering the core product feature illegal or forcing a watered-down version that customers reject.
2.  **Opt-In/Value Mismatch:** The assumed 70% opt-in rate proves optimistic; if customers perceive the AI suggestions as generic or intrusive, uptake may stall below 30%, failing to cover the fixed €15,000 licensing and variable processing fees.
3.  **Hidden Operational Drag:** Unbudgeted staff training and change-management costs exceed 20% of the allocated €120,000 integration budget, eroding the margin needed to achieve the targeted 30% lift in booking value.

**Kill Criteria (Stop/Pivot Triggers)**
*   **Uptake Threshold:** If fewer than 15% of eligible customers opt in after the first 30 days of the pilot.
*   **Revenue Threshold:** If the average booking value for AI-users does not exceed €1,350 (a 12.5% lift, half the target) by the end of Q3 2025.
*   **Cost Threshold:** If the total cost per acquired AI-booking (including processing fees and amortized integration) exceeds €45.

*Note: The current model lacks a contingency for the unknown staff training costs, which should be capped at €24,000 (20% of budget) to protect viability. The €0.12 processing fee creates a variable cost risk if booking values do not rise as projected.*
