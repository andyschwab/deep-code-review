**Recommendation: Re-work**

Do not proceed to installation yet. The current proposal relies on unverified assumptions that could turn a projected gain into a multi-year loss. You must validate the "willingness to pay" and "recycling rate" claims with hard data before committing $1.2M in capital.

**Three Biggest Failure Modes**

1.  **Price Elasticity Mismatch:** The 8% sales lift and 5% price premium rely on a small focus group (n=150). If real-world customers reject the price hike, volume will drop while unit costs rise ($0.45 → $0.48), causing immediate margin compression rather than expansion.
2.  **Infrastructure Reality Gap:** If the new material cannot be recycled in the majority of your target markets' actual municipal facilities (despite being technically "recyclable"), the eco-friendly marketing claim becomes greenwashing, inviting reputational damage and potential regulatory scrutiny without the brand benefit.
3.  **Breakeven Math Error:** The 18-month breakeven assumes the sales lift happens instantly upon launch. If adoption is gradual, the extended timeline increases the effective cost of capital, potentially pushing the true breakeven point beyond the equipment's useful life or the company's risk tolerance.

**Pre-agreed Kill Criteria**

Terminate the project and revert to plastic tubs if, after a 90-day pilot in a representative market:
*   **Sell-through volume** is less than 85% of the forecasted baseline (adjusted for seasonality), OR
*   **Verified recycling capture rate** (actual bins collected vs. distributed) is below 40%, OR
*   **Net margin per unit** (after price premium and new material cost) is lower than the current $0.45 baseline.

***

*Note: The 5% price premium assumption is the single point of failure; a larger A/B test is required before CFO approval. Ensure the "recycling rate" metric tracks actual consumer disposal behavior, not just material capability.*
