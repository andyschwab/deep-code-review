**Recommendation:** Proceed with a **conditional pilot expansion**, not a full commitment. Authorize the $250k development budget only if Glacier Pulse provides a fixed-cost licensing cap and a written guarantee that integration with our current booking engine is included in that price. Do not sign the final contract until the $50k integration risk is contractually transferred to the vendor or validated by a technical audit. The 12% lift is promising, but the undefined licensing fees and hidden integration costs make the current ROI calculation unreliable.

**Three Biggest Failure Modes:**
1.  **Margin Erasure via Hidden Costs:** The project fails financially if the unknown licensing fees exceed 15% of incremental revenue or if the unquoted integration costs balloon beyond $50k, turning the projected $250k profit into a break-even or loss scenario.
2.  **Adoption Delusion:** The project fails operationally if the assumed 70% adoption rate is actually below 25%. Our pilot group was self-selected and highly engaged; the broader, less tech-savvy client base may find VR friction too high, rendering the development spend useless.
3.  **Timing Mismatch:** The project fails strategically if the Q4 2025 rollout misses the critical winter booking window for Northern Lights tours. A delay of even six weeks could push revenue realization into the next fiscal year, breaking the cash-flow assumptions used to approve the budget.

**Kill Metric:** Terminate the initiative if **VR-assisted bookings constitute less than 8% of total bookings** after the first three months of general availability, or if the **Customer Acquisition Cost (CAC) for VR users exceeds non-VR users by more than 20%**.

*Note: The 8% threshold accounts for a realistic drop-off from the pilot's 12% while still ensuring positive unit economics.*
*Ensure the contract includes a "termination for convenience" clause exercisable at the 3-month mark to limit downside.*
