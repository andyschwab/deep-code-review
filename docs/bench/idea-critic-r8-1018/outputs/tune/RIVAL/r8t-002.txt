**Recommendation: Delay Launch (Pilot First)**

Do not commit to the $150,000 van purchase or the central hub lease yet. Your current plan relies on unproven demand assumptions in Mexico City and Monterrey. A 15% quarterly growth rate is aggressive for a cold-start logistics network without prior market validation.

**Action Plan:**
1.  **Run a 60-day pilot** in Mexico City only using 2 leased vans and a shared micro-fulfillment space.
2.  **Cap initial capital** at $25,000 to test unit economics and spoilage rates.
3.  **Greenlight nationwide expansion** only if the pilot achieves >1,200 orders/month with <4% spoilage.

**Three Biggest Failure Modes:**
1.  **Cold-Chain Breakdown:** Insulated vans are not active refrigeration. If ambient temperatures in Monterrey exceed 35°C, product quality will degrade, leading to high return rates and brand damage.
2.  **Demand Density Mismatch:** Nationwide coverage with only 12 vans spreads resources too thin. If order density is low, fuel and driver costs per order will skyrocket, erasing the 3% margin immediately.
3.  **Fixed Cost Trap:** Leasing a central hub ($42,000/year) before proving volume creates a massive burn rate. If demand grows at only 5% instead of 15%, you will bleed cash monthly with no off-ramp.

**Kill/Pivot Trigger:**
Terminate or pivot the project if **cumulative net loss exceeds $35,000 by 30 June 2025** OR if **spoilage/return rates exceed 5% for two consecutive months**.

**Figure Check:**
Your total capital outlay calculation is **correct** ($12,500 × 12 = $150,000). However, you are missing **working capital**. You will need an additional ~$50,000 in cash reserves to cover fuel, driver salaries, and warehouse deposits for the first 4–6 months before positive cash flow kicks in. The 3% net margin is extremely thin for a logistics-heavy startup; one fuel spike or route inefficiency will push you into negative territory.
