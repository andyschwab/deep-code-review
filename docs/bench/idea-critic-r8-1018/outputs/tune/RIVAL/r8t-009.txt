**Recommendation: Do not approve the full rollout yet.** Proceed only with a 5,000-meter pilot phase (2% of total) to validate integration costs and actual adoption behavior before committing the remaining $11.4 million. The current business case relies on unverified assumptions that could render the project financially unviable.

**Top Three Failure Modes:**

1.  **Financial (Integration Cost Shock):** The assumption that existing SCADA can ingest data without additional hardware is the single biggest risk. If middleware, new servers, or database licensing are required, the $12M capex could balloon by 30–50%, destroying the ROI. The vendor's silence on this cost is a major red flag.
2.  **Technical (Data Deluge & Latency):** 250,000 meters transmitting frequently will likely overwhelm legacy SCADA bandwidth, causing data lag or loss. If the system cannot process real-time leaks, the projected 8% water loss reduction will not materialize, leaving you with expensive meters but no operational savings.
3.  **Regulatory/Adoption (Privacy Pushback):** A 90% adoption rate is overly optimistic if customers perceive granular usage data as a privacy violation. Regulatory hurdles or public resistance could stall installation, extending the timeline and increasing labor costs while delaying revenue recovery.

**Stop/Pivot Triggers:**

*   **Halt:** If the pilot reveals integration hardware/software costs exceed $1.5 million.
*   **Pivot:** If first-year pilot adoption falls below 60% or if actual water loss reduction is <4% after six months of operation.
*   **Stop:** If data latency exceeds 15 minutes, rendering leak detection ineffective.

**Hidden Risks & Costs:**

*   **Cybersecurity Liability:** Connecting 250,000 new endpoints vastly expands your attack surface. Budget for immediate security auditing and ongoing monitoring; a breach could incur fines far exceeding project savings.
*   **Maintenance & Battery Replacement:** The proposal ignores the 10-year operational cost of replacing meter batteries or repairing cellular modules. This recurring OPEX could erode annual savings by 20%.
*   **Customer Service Spike:** Detailed bills often confuse customers, leading to a surge in call center volume. You may need to temporarily staff up support, an unbudgeted operational hit.

**Note:** The finance team's arithmetic error (15% of $12M is $1.8M, not $1.2M) suggests the underlying model needs a full audit before any further approval. Correcting this alone changes the payback period significantly.
