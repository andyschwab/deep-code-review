**Recommendation: NO GO (in current form)**

The financial arithmetic in your pitch contains a critical error that undermines the entire business case. You project $4.5M in sales with a 12% margin, which yields **$540,000 in gross profit**, not the **$1.5M profit** claimed. To achieve $1.5M profit on $4.5M sales, you would need a 33% margin, which is highly unlikely for a new apparel line with heavy upfront equipment and marketing costs. Furthermore, your $2.2M budget leaves you with a **$1.66M funding gap** relative to your profit target if the venture stumbles, and your burn rate during the setup phase (Jan–July 2025) is unaccounted for.

**Three Biggest Failure Modes:**

1.  **Margin Collapse:** If raw material costs rise by just 10% or you are forced to discount prices by 15% to move inventory (common in fashion), your already thin 12% margin evaporates, turning the projected $540k profit into a loss.
2.  **Supply Chain Single Point of Failure:** Relying on a single Cebu supplier for 100% of your 150k meters/month creates massive risk. Any disruption (typhoon, labor strike, quality failure) halts your entire Manila launch with no backup.
3.  **Demand Overestimation:** Assuming $4.5M sales requires selling ~150,000 units at a ~$30 average price point. If consumer elasticity proves higher than expected and you only hit 60% of this target ($2.7M), you will not cover your fixed costs and equipment depreciation.

**Pre-agreed Stop/Pivot Trigger:**

*   **Trigger:** If Month 1–3 average sales volume is **< 8,000 units/month** OR if gross margin drops **below 8%**.
*   **Action:** Immediately halt marketing spend and pivot to a direct-to-consumer online-only model to preserve cash, or seek a strategic partner for distribution.

**Hidden Cost/Arithmetic Error:**

*   **Error:** As noted, **$4.5M × 12% = $540k**, not $1.5M. The $1.5M figure implies a 33% margin, which is unrealistic.
*   **Hidden Cost:** You have not budgeted for **working capital**. You must pay the Cebu supplier and your staff *before* you collect revenue from stores (which often have 60–90 day payment terms). You likely need an additional **$500k–$750k** in liquid cash to bridge this gap between production in July and cash collection in Q4.
