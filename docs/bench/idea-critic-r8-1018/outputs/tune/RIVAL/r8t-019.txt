The unit economics are strong enough to justify a test, but the full-scale launch is too risky without real data on customer retention. Your projected Lifetime Value (LTV) is highly sensitive to the unknown churn rate. If customers leave before month 5, you will lose money on every acquisition. Do not commit the full $30,000 budget or the June 1st launch date yet. Instead, run the three-month pilot with a capped spend of $5,000 to acquire roughly 250 subscribers. Use this group strictly to measure actual churn and logistics friction. If the data holds up, unlock the remaining budget and proceed to the full 1,500-subscriber goal.

**Primary Failure Modes & Hidden Risks:**

1.  **Churn Sensitivity:** This is your biggest threat. With a Customer Acquisition Cost (CAC) of $20 ($30k/1,500 subs) and a monthly margin of ~$26.45 (see calculation below), a customer must stay at least 1.5 months just to break even on acquisition. If churn exceeds 25% monthly after the pilot, the model collapses.
2.  **Cannibalization Underestimation:** You assume only 30% of wholesale customers switch, but direct-to-consumer convenience often tempts higher conversion. If 50% switch, your wholesale loss doubles to $24,000, eroding net profit significantly.
3.  **Hidden Cost – Payment Processing & Platform Fees:** You missed credit card processing (approx. 2.9% + $0.30 per transaction) and subscription platform fees (typically 1-2% of recurring revenue). On $35/month, this removes another ~$1.30 per box, lowering your margin.
4.  **Customs Volatility:** Basing duties on a $1 estimate is risky. Tea tariffs fluctuate based on origin and trade agreements. A spike here directly hits your margin.

*   **Revenue:** $35.00

**Kill/Pivot Triggers:**

Establish these hard gates at the end of the three-month pilot. If any are met, **kill** the project immediately:
*   **Churn Threshold:** Monthly churn exceeds **20%** after the first 90 days. (This implies an average customer lifespan of <5 months, risking negative ROI).

**Pivot Trigger:**
