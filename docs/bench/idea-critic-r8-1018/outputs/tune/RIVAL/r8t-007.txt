**Recommendation:** Proceed with the pilot, but strictly condition the full rollout on achieving a **net reduction in total operational cost** (not just error rates) and verifying that staff training time does not exceed 4 hours per robot per week. The current proposal underestimates integration friction; treat the pilot as a feasibility study for workflow adaptation, not just a technology test.

**Top Three Failure Modes:**
1.  **Workflow Rejection:** Staff bypass the robot due to cumbersome login procedures or slow dispensing speeds, rendering the error-reduction data invalid while adding labor costs.
2.  **The "Last Mile" Gap:** The robot dispenses correctly, but human error shifts to the administration phase (handing the pill to the resident), leaving the overall safety metric unchanged.
3.  **Maintenance Latency:** A single robot failure in a understaffed shift forces a manual revert, causing a backlog of medication rounds that increases stress and subsequent human errors elsewhere.

**Kill Switch Metric:** If the pilot sites record a **total medication discrepancy rate** (robot errors + human bypass errors) exceeding **1.5%** by April 30, 2025, terminate the project. Do not measure only robot mechanical errors; measure the net outcome for the resident.

**Hidden Costs & Risks:**
*   **Liability Ambiguity:** Your current insurance likely covers human error. You lack a defined liability framework for robot-induced delays or mechanical dispensing faults. Legal review is required before signing.
*   **Infrastructure Retrofit:** Older Queensland facilities may lack the specific Wi-Fi density or charging infrastructure required for autonomous navigation, potentially adding $50k–$80k per site in unseen capital expenditure.
*   **Data Sovereignty:** Ensure the vendor's data storage complies with Queensland health data residency laws; US-based cloud hosting for patient medication logs may be a regulatory violation.
