**Recommendation: GO-IF**
Proceed with a **12-month, 10,000-meter pilot** in a single district before committing to the full 250,000 rollout. Do not authorize the full capital budget until the pilot validates the integration cost and actual adoption rate.

**Three Biggest Failure Modes**
1.  **Technical (Integration Collapse):** The assumption that the existing SCADA system can ingest high-frequency smart meter data without new hardware is unverified. If the legacy system requires a middleware layer or gateway upgrades, the unquantified integration cost could exceed the $12M capex, destroying the ROI.
2.  **Financial (Adoption Shortfall):** The business case relies on a 90% household adoption rate to achieve the 8% water loss reduction. If actual voluntary adoption mirrors industry averages (closer to 60-70%), the revenue saved will drop by nearly half, extending the payback period from ~10 years to over 20, making the project financially unviable.
3.  **Regulatory (Data Privacy & Consent):** Aggregating granular usage data may trigger new privacy compliance requirements or consumer pushback not currently costed. Mandatory installation clauses could face legal challenges, delaying the rollout and inflating labor costs.

**Kill Criteria (Trigger to Halt/Pivot)**
*   **Integration Cost:** If the pilot reveals integration hardware/software costs exceed **$1.5M** (12.5% of total budget).
*   **Adoption Rate:** If verified household adoption in the pilot district is **<65%** after 6 months of active marketing.
*   **Water Loss Reduction:** If the pilot demonstrates **<4%** reduction in non-revenue water (half the projected target).

**Hidden Risk: The Arithmetic Gap**
Your finance team's arithmetic contains a critical error that masks the true risk. You noted the savings ($1.2M) are "roughly 15% of the upfront cost." In reality, $1.2M is only **10%** of the $12M budget. More dangerously, at a 10% annual return on capital, the simple payback period is **10 years**, not the ~6.7 years implied by a 15% return. Given the 3-year installation window and typical asset lifecycles, this thin margin leaves zero room for the unquantified integration costs or lower adoption rates. The project is currently priced for perfection; any deviation turns it into a loss leader.

**Note:** The vendor's failure to quantify integration costs is a major red flag; treat their silence as a potential cost equal to 20% of the hardware budget until proven otherwise.
