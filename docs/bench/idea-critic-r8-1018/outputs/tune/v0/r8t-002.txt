**Recommendation: Go-If (Conditional Launch)**
Proceed with the launch only if you secure a **6-month exit clause** on the warehouse lease and commit to a **pilot in Mexico City only** for the first 90 days, delaying the Monterrey expansion and the purchase of the full 12-van fleet until pilot metrics are verified. Do not commit the full $150,000 capital outlay immediately.

**Three Biggest Failure Modes:**
1.  **Demand Overestimation:** The assumed 15% quarterly growth fails to materialize in the first three months due to lack of market testing, leaving expensive insulated vans underutilized while fixed lease costs burn cash.
2.  **Margin Erosion:** The 3% net margin is too thin to absorb any spike in fuel prices (contradicting your flat price assumption) or unexpected maintenance costs on the new fleet, turning profitable orders into losses.
3.  **Operational Bottlenecks:** A single central hub creates a logistics single point of failure; any disruption in the hub delays deliveries to both cities simultaneously, risking spoilage and brand reputation before the network proves resilient.

**Kill Criteria (Tripwires):**
*   **Financial:** Cumulative net loss exceeds **$35,000** by 30 June 2025.
*   **Adoption:** Fewer than **400 orders** per month achieved by the end of Quarter 1 (20% of the 2,000 target).
*   **Cost:** Fuel or maintenance costs rise by more than **10%** above the baseline model in any single month.

**Note on Figures:** Your total capital outlay calculation ($12,500 × 12 = $150,000) is mathematically correct, but it excludes the initial lease deposit and working capital required for the first three months of operations before positive cash flow.
**Note on Strategy:** The "Go-If" approach protects you from the unknown customer adoption rate in Mexico City and Monterrey by treating the full fleet purchase as a milestone reward rather than an upfront prerequisite.
