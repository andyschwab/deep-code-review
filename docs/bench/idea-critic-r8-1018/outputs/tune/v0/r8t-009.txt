**Recommendation: GO-IF**
Proceed with a limited 5,000-meter pilot in 2025 before committing to the full 250,000 rollout. Do not sign the full capital contract until the pilot validates integration costs and actual adoption rates. The current business case contains a critical arithmetic error (15% of $12M is $1.8M, not the projected $1.2M savings) and relies on unverified assumptions about SCADA compatibility. A pilot limits exposure while resolving the unknown integration costs that could invalidate the ROI.

**Top Three Failure Modes**
1.  **Technical (Integration Collapse):** The legacy SCADA system cannot ingest the data volume or protocol without expensive middleware, blowing the budget and delaying benefits indefinitely.
2.  **Financial (Adoption Shortfall):** If household adoption hits 60% instead of the assumed 90%, annual savings drop proportionally, extending the payback period from ~10 years to over 16 years, likely failing internal hurdle rates.
3.  **Regulatory (Data Privacy):** New municipal or state laws regarding granular water usage data could require costly security upgrades or limit data utilization, eroding the projected efficiency gains.

**Kill Criteria (Stop/Pivot Triggers)**
*   **Halt:** If the pilot reveals SCADA integration hardware costs exceed $1.5M.
*   **Pivot:** If first-year pilot adoption falls below 60% or if actual water loss reduction is less than 4% (half the projected 8%).
*   **Review Date:** December 2025 (post-pilot analysis).

**Hidden Risks & Notes**
*   **Maintenance Liability:** The proposal budgets for capital installation but ignores the ongoing OPEX for maintaining 250,000 connected devices (battery replacements, network subscriptions, firmware updates), which could consume 20–30% of the annual savings.
*   **Customer Backlash:** Mandatory smart meters often trigger privacy concerns and opt-out movements; without a clear communication strategy, legal challenges could stall the rollout.
