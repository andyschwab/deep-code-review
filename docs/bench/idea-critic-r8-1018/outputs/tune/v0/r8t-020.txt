**Recommendation: Do not proceed yet.** Issue a "Go-If" conditional approval requiring a fixed-price integration contract and a 90-day pilot with explicit kill criteria before signing the full quarterly agreement. The current proposal relies on an unverified "minimal" integration cost and assumes savings that vanish if adoption lags or no-show reductions fail to materialize.

**Three Biggest Failure Modes:**
1.  **Integration Cost Explosion:** The vendor's "minimal" integration claim with Epic-Lite is unsupported. If custom middleware or extensive API mapping is required, upfront costs could exceed the first year's projected savings ($48,000), turning the ROI negative immediately.
2.  **Adoption vs. Savings Gap:** The $120/visit saving assumes 100% conversion of eligible chronic-care patients to virtual visits. If only 40% of patients adopt the platform due to tech barriers or preference, the volume drop fails to cover the $12,000 quarterly fee plus the $950 quarterly training amortization, resulting in a net loss per quarter.
3.  **Uptime Degradation:** Swapping a 99% uptime system for a 95% uptime system increases downtime from ~7 hours to ~36 hours per quarter. For chronic care requiring consistent access, this 5x increase in unavailability could disrupt medication management and erode patient trust, offsetting any convenience gains.

**Stop/Pivot Trigger:**
Terminate the pilot or renegotiate terms if, after 90 days: (a) the total all-in cost (license + training + actual integration labor) exceeds $15,000 for the quarter, OR (b) the realized no-show rate reduction is less than 1.5% (half the projected benefit), OR (c) patient satisfaction scores drop below 80%.

**Hidden Risks:**
The 3% no-show reduction assumes the technology is the primary barrier to attendance; if social determinants or transportation are the real drivers, the platform will not deliver the projected volume recovery. Additionally, the $3,800 training budget is an estimate; if staff turnover is high, recurring training costs will inflate the per-visit cost basis, eroding the margin further.
