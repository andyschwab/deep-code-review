**Recommendation: GO-IF**
Proceed with a restricted 5,000-meter pilot in Q1 2025, but **halt** the full 250,000-unit rollout until the vendor provides a fixed-price integration quote and the finance team corrects the ROI model. The current business case is mathematically broken ($1.2M annual savings is only 10% of the $12M cost, not 15%, implying a 10-year simple payback before operating costs), and the assumption that existing SCADA hardware can ingest high-frequency smart meter data without upgrades is a critical unverified technical risk.

**Three Biggest Failure Modes**
1.  **Financial (Adoption & Payback):** If household adoption stays below 75% instead of the assumed 90%, revenue capture drops disproportionately while fixed network costs remain, extending the payback period beyond the asset life. The current 8% water loss reduction target may be optimistic if leaks are primarily in the transmission main rather than household service lines.
2.  **Technical (SCADA Saturation):** The existing SCADA system was likely designed for low-frequency polling of industrial endpoints, not high-frequency telemetry from 250,000 residential nodes. Without a verified load test, the system could crash or require a $3M+ middleware overhaul, destroying the capital budget.
3.  **Regulatory (Data Privacy & Tariffs):** Aggregated household usage data may trigger new privacy compliance requirements or rate-case challenges if customers perceive the "water loss reduction" savings are not being passed through as lower tariffs, leading to public pushback or regulatory fines.

**Kill Criteria (Tripwires)**
*   **Halt Trigger:** If the Q1 2025 pilot shows integration costs exceeding $150 per meter (implying >$37.5M total) or if actual adoption in the pilot zone falls below 60% after six months.
*   **Pivot Trigger:** If the corrected financial model shows a simple payback period exceeding 7 years, pivot to a targeted rollout focusing only on high-loss districts rather than a city-wide blanket installation.

**Hidden Risk**
**Cybersecurity Surface Area:** Adding 250,000 internet-connected endpoints drastically expands the attack surface. If the meters or the ingestion pipeline lack end-to-end encryption and secure boot, the utility could face ransomware attacks that disable billing or manipulate usage data, a liability not currently quantified in the $12M budget.

*Note: The 15% ROI figure cited by finance appears to be a calculation error or includes unlisted operational savings; this must be reconciled before board approval. The vendor's silence on integration costs is a major red flag suggesting they anticipate significant custom work.*
