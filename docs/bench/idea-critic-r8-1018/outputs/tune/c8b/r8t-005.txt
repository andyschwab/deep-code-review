**Recommendation:** Do not approve the full $250k development budget yet. Instead, authorize a strictly limited, four-week "integration and pricing validation" phase with a cap of $15k. Proceed to the full build only if Glacier Pulse provides a fixed licensing fee model and a technical proof-of-concept confirms the booking engine integration will not exceed $50k.

**Three Biggest Failure Modes:**
1.  **Unit Economics Collapse:** The unpriced Glacier Pulse licensing fees could consume the entire margin from the projected $250k revenue lift, rendering the project profitable only on paper while actually reducing net income.
2.  **Adoption Delusion:** The assumption that 70% of clients will adopt the VR planner is unsupported by data; if actual adoption mirrors industry VR averages (typically <15%) rather than your pilot's cherry-picked enthusiastic users, revenue will fall short by over $150k, failing to cover the build cost.
3.  **Hidden Integration Debt:** The excluded integration cost could spiral beyond the estimated $50k if the VR module requires custom middleware to talk to your current booking engine, potentially stalling the Q4 2025 launch and burning cash before a single ticket is sold.

**Kill Criterion:** Terminate the initiative immediately if, after a three-month post-launch window, verified VR-driven bookings represent less than 8% of total tour sales or if the fully loaded cost per VR-acquired booking exceeds $120.
