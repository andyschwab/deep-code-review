**What Flips It, and the Trigger**
The single input that changes the call is the **legal classification of the output**. If a regulator or court determines your AI provides "legal advice" rather than "legal information," the penalty is not a fine but an injunction shutting down operations and potential criminal liability for UPL.
*Trigger:* If any beta user in CA or NY claims reliance on your tool for a binding legal decision, or if a state bar association issues a cease-and-desist.
*Rule of Thumb:* Tools that draft clauses or predict outcomes are higher risk than tools that only highlight missing standard terms.
*This Week's Step:* If your tool outputs specific clause language or "approve/reject" verdicts, consider pivoting the UI to strictly "highlight potential issues for attorney review" before paying for a formal legal opinion.

**What to Confirm, and With Whom**
You must engage a lawyer specializing in **Legal Tech regulation and UPL** in both California and New York. Do not ask general corporate counsel. Bring these exact questions:
1.  **UPL Boundary:** Does our specific output (summarizing risks vs. drafting clauses) meet the statutory definition of practicing law in CA (Bus. & Prof. Code § 6125) and NY (Judiciary Law § 478)?
2.  **Disclaimer Efficacy:** Will our terms of service disclaimer sufficiently shield us from UPL claims if a user relies on the AI's error?
3.  **Multijurisdictional Exposure:** Since we are Texas-based but serving CA/NY users, which state's bar has jurisdiction over our operation, and do we need local counsel admission or a registered agent?
4.  **Precedent Research:** Ask specifically about *Gilbert v. State Bar of California* and recent bar opinions on "document assembly" vs. "legal advice" software.
