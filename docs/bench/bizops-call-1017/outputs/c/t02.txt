**The Call**
Model your unit economics on a **cash-flow basis** for internal decisions and investor updates, not on GAAP revenue recognition. Your 24-month trust hold creates a working capital deficit, not a profitability problem; investors will scrutinize your **cash burn rate** and **capital efficiency** (cash required to secure $1 of Annual Recurring Revenue) far more than your reported net income. Do not delay pricing or hiring decisions based on the accounting delay; base them on the actual cash entering and leaving the bank.

**The Numbers**
To model the "true" economics, you must separate **Accounting Profit** (delayed) from **Cash Contribution** (immediate).

*Formula for Cash Payback Period:*
$$ \text{Payback (months)} = \frac{\text{CAC}}{\text{Monthly Cash Inflow} - \text{Monthly Service Cost}} $$

*   **Monthly Cash Inflow:** $299 (The contract value collected, even if held in trust, is cash generated by the sale, though restricted). *Correction:* If the funds are legally trapped and cannot be used for operations, your **operational cash inflow** from this specific revenue stream is $0 until month 25.

Since you cannot touch the $299, your monthly cash flow per new customer is **negative** the amount of your service cost.

*   **Total Cash Drag per Customer:** $100 $\times$ 24 = $2,400 cash outlay before seeing $1.

*Formula for Capital Required per Customer:*
$$ \text{Capital Needed} = \text{CAC} + (\text{Monthly Service Cost} \times 24) $$
*   **Base Case:** $1,200 + ($100 $\times$ 24) = **$3,600** of cash required to acquire and serve one customer before revenue recognition.

**What Flips It, and the Trigger**
The single input that changes your viability is the **Monthly Service Cost**. If your variable cost to serve exceeds ~$125/month, your cash drag per customer ($3,000+) combined with CAC ($1,200) may exceed the Lifetime Value (LTV) once recognized, making the business model insolvent regardless of the accounting delay.
*   **Trigger:** If your calculated **Capital Needed per Customer** exceeds 30% of your current operating cash ($45,000), you must raise capital or cut service costs immediately.
*   **Rule of Thumb:** In regulated trust models, investors expect a "capital intensity" ratio where every $1 of ARR requires $2–$3 of upfront equity/debt.
