**The Call:** Do not proceed with the revenue-share structure until you obtain a formal legal opinion on Arizona's municipal code regarding "gift clauses" and public utility privatization; the model is likely viable only if structured as a performance-based service contract rather than an equity interest in public savings.

**The Numbers:**
*   **Unit Economics Skeleton:**
    *   **Upfront Cost:** $150/unit (Hardware) + Installation (Unknown).
    *   **Revenue per Unit:** 10% × (Baseline Usage − Actual Usage) × Water Rate.

    *   **Runway Check:** $500,000 funding / 10 staff ≈ $50k/month burn (assuming $5k/month fully loaded cost). This gives ~10 months of runway.
    *   **Constraint:** If installation costs exceed $50k total for the pilot, you will run out of cash before the first revenue share check arrives (typically billed quarterly or annually by utilities).

**What Flips It, and the Trigger:**
*   **The Flip Input:** The legal classification of the payment. If the city attorney classifies the 10% share as "privatization of public assets" or an illegal "gift of public funds" (a common constitutional restriction in Arizona and other states), the deal dies. If classified as a "variable-cost service fee" for data and maintenance, it may pass.
*   **Trigger:** The moment a City Council or General Manager requests a "legal authority memo" citing the specific statute allowing private profit from public utility efficiency.
*   **Rule of Thumb:** Municipalities rarely reject cost-saving contracts; they reject contracts that look like selling off public infrastructure rights.
*   **Step This Week:** If your current pitch deck says "we take 10% of savings," consider rephrasing immediately to "we charge a performance fee equal to 10% of verified savings" and pause all contract drafting until counsel reviews the distinction.

**What to Confirm, and with Whom:**
*   **The Binding Question:** Does Arizona's Constitution (Article 9, Section 7 – Gift Clause) or the specific municipal code of your pilot cities prohibit a private entity from receiving a percentage of public utility revenue/savings as a payment method?
*   **Who to Consult:** A municipal finance attorney or public utilities counsel licensed in Arizona. Specifically, seek a firm experienced in "Energy Savings Performance Contracts" (ESPCs) or "Water Savings Performance Contracts," as these legal structures often provide the precedent for your model. Do not rely on general corporate counsel; this requires specific public-sector regulatory expertise.
