**The Call**
Do not establish a specific "GDPR fine reserve fund" based on a percentage of revenue; this is not standard accounting practice and misallocates scarce cash. Instead, treat GDPR compliance as a binary operational prerequisite: either you meet the technical and legal requirements (making fines unlikely), or you should not process EU data at all. Your immediate action is to allocate cash toward *prevention* (security engineering and legal counsel) rather than *penalty reserves*.

**The Numbers**
There is no formula to calculate a "standard" fine reserve because fines are discretionary, fact-specific, and not insured against in a way that allows for simple accrual.
*   **Your Unit Economics:** Price $79, COGS $22, Margin $57/unit.
*   **Current Runway:** $200,000 cash / ($22 * 5,000 units/quarter + overhead) ≈ *Unknown without overhead data*.
*   **The "Reserve" Fallacy:** Setting aside, for example, 4% of projected EU revenue ($79 * X units * 0.04) creates a false sense of security. A fine could be €0 (if compliant) or €20M+ (if grossly negligent), rendering a small percentage reserve useless.
*   **Assumption (Labelled ASSUMED):** You assume fines are a predictable operating cost like COGS. **Correction:** Fines are tail-risk events triggered by non-compliance, not volume.

**What Flips It, and The Trigger**
The input that changes your strategy is **Data Processing Scope**.
*   **Trigger:** If you process "special category" data (e.g., genetic data revealing health traits, not just breed) or fail to appoint a representative, your risk shifts from "manageable" to "existential."
*   **Rule of Thumb:** If you cannot afford a Data Protection Impact Assessment (DPIA) before launch, you cannot afford to launch in the EU.
*   **Step this week:** If you are collecting DNA samples, **consider** halting the EU launch until a qualified EU privacy counsel confirms whether your specific test constitutes "health data" under GDPR Article 9, which carries the highest fines.

**What to Confirm, and With Whom**
You must route the following binding determinations to a **licensed EU data privacy attorney** immediately:
1.  **Classification:** Does your dog DNA test infer health data or constitute biometric data under GDPR Article 9? (This dictates the fine tier).
2.  **DPO Requirement:** Based on your "large scale" processing of genetic data, confirm if you are legally mandated to hire a Data Protection Officer (DPO) before day one.
3.  **Representation:** Confirm the requirement to appoint an EU Representative (Article 27) since you have no physical presence in the EU.
4.  **Reserve Strategy:** Ask a **CPA familiar with EU/US cross-border tax** if any jurisdiction allows accruing "contingent regulatory fines" as a tax-deductible expense (generally, they do not).

Do not guess the percentage. The cost of a single breach notification and legal defense will exceed any reasonable percentage reserve you could set aside from $200k. Spend the cash on getting compliant first.
