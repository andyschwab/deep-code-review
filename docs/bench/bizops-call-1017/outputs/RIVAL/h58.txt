You cannot accept unlimited indemnification; it is an existential threat to a company with $150k in reserves. You must restructure the deal to cap liability while offering the airport alternative security. Here is the specific structure and language to propose:

**1. The Liability Cap Clause**
Replace the unlimited indemnity with a "Limitation of Liability" clause that caps your total exposure at a fixed dollar amount or a multiple of your contract value. Given your $56k MRR, a cap of **12 months of fees ($672,000)** or **$1 million** is standard for hardware-as-a-service deals. This protects your solvency while showing you have "skin in the game."

*Proposed Language:*
> "Notwithstanding any other provision in this Agreement, CleanBot's total aggregate liability for any and all claims, damages, losses, or expenses arising out of or relating to this Agreement, including but not limited to indemnification obligations for operational errors or property damage, shall not exceed the lesser of: (a) $1,000,000 USD; or (b) the total fees paid by Airport to CleanBot in the twelve (12) months preceding the claim. In no event shall CleanBot be liable for indirect, incidental, or consequential damages."

**2. The Insurance Backstop**
Airports demand indemnity because they want a guaranteed payer for damages. Since you cannot self-insure beyond your cash reserves, you must transfer this risk to a carrier. Offer to procure a **Commercial General Liability (CGL)** policy and **Product Liability** insurance with limits matching your new liability cap (e.g., $1M per occurrence / $2M aggregate). Name the airport as an "Additional Insured" on the policy. This satisfies their risk team because an A-rated insurer, not your fragile balance sheet, is now the source of funds for any claim.

*Action Item:* Contact a broker specializing in robotics or autonomous systems immediately. Standard policies often exclude "autonomous operations," so you need a specialized rider. Present the certificate of insurance (once bound) as the substitute for unlimited indemnity.

**3. The "Fault-Based" Indemnity Carve-out**
If the airport pushes back on a hard cap, refine the indemnity scope. Agree to indemnify only for damages caused by CleanBot's **gross negligence or willful misconduct**, rather than "all operational errors." This excludes unavoidable mechanical malfunctions or third-party interference from your unlimited liability.

*Proposed Language:*
> "CleanBot shall indemnify and hold harmless the Airport against third-party claims for bodily injury or property damage to the extent such claims are finally adjudicated to be directly caused by CleanBot's gross negligence or willful misconduct. CleanBot shall not be liable for damages resulting from normal wear and tear, force majeure, or the negligence of Airport personnel."

**4. The Negotiation Narrative**
Do not frame this as you refusing to take responsibility. Frame it as **professional risk management**. Tell the airport: "Unlimited liability forces us to operate as a fragile entity. By capping liability at $1M and backing it with an insurance policy where you are an Additional Insured, we ensure that any valid claim is paid promptly by a major carrier, guaranteeing your protection without risking the continuity of our service due to a single bankruptcy event."

If they still demand unlimited liability, walk away. No contract is worth a 100% probability of eventual ruin given the non-zero risk of autonomous malfunction.
