### Analysis of Legal Advice vs. Software Tool

The distinction between providing a software tool and practicing law generally hinges on **discretion**, **customization**, and the **application of judgment** to specific facts.

**1. The "Practice of Law" Threshold**
In most jurisdictions, including Singapore, the unauthorized practice of law typically involves:
*   Applying legal principles to a specific client's unique facts to determine their rights or obligations.
*   Exercising professional legal judgment to recommend a specific course of action.
*   Representing a client before a regulatory body.

If your software simply ingests transaction data and maps it to a fixed regulatory template without interpreting ambiguous situations or making judgment calls on *how* the law applies to edge cases, it is more likely to be classified as a **document automation tool** or **legal technology** rather than a law firm.

**2. Standardization as a Defense**
The fact that your reports are **standardized** and **not customized** per client is a strong indicator that you are selling a product, not a service.
*   **Software Argument:** You are selling access to an engine that formats data according to public, codified rules. The "intelligence" is hardcoded logic, not real-time legal counsel.
*   **Risk Factor:** If your system interprets complex, ambiguous rules where multiple valid interpretations exist, and your software selects one specific interpretation for the user, regulators may view this as exercising legal judgment. If the output says "You must file X because rule Y implies Z," you are closer to giving advice. If the output says "Based on data A, the standard report form requires field B to be populated," you are closer to data processing.

**3. The "Outcome" vs. "Process" Distinction**
The risk increases if your marketing or user agreement implies that using your software guarantees compliance or substitutes for legal review.
*   **Safe Harbor:** Positioning the product as a tool to *assist* compliance officers, with clear disclaimers that the final review and submission responsibility lies with the licensed exchange, is critical.
*   **Danger Zone:** If you market the service as "We handle your legal compliance" or "Our reports are legally certified," you are likely crossing the line into unauthorized practice.
