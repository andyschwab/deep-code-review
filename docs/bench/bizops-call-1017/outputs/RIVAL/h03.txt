### Balance Sheet Presentation & Valuation Guidance

Under standard accounting frameworks (ASC 330 in US GAAP and IAS 2 in IFRS), inventory must be carried at the **lower of cost or net realizable value (NRV)**. You generally **cannot** automatically write down assets monthly simply because time has passed. However, you **must** write down (impair) the inventory if there is evidence that its utility or realizable value has dropped below your production cost of $85,000 per batch.

**1. The Trigger for Write-Downs**
Time alone is not an impairment trigger. The trigger is a change in circumstances indicating the asset is worth less than cost. For BioForge, the critical assessment is:
*   **Probability of Approval:** If regulatory delays are routine and approval is still "probable" (likely >50% or "more likely than not" depending on specific standard interpretation), you may carry the inventory at cost ($2.1M).
*   **Obsolescence Risk:** If the delay suggests the enzyme may no longer meet updated regulatory standards, or if the client contract allows cancellation due to timeline breaches, the NRV may drop to zero or a salvage value.
*   **Contract Terms:** If your contracts are "bill-and-hold" or have specific clauses regarding regulatory failure, the valuation depends on whether you have an unconditional right to payment. If payment is contingent *solely* on approval, and approval is now uncertain, impairment is likely required.

**2. Acceptable Methodology for Calculation**
If impairment is necessary, do not use a flat monthly percentage. Use a **probability-weighted expected cash flow** model:
*   **Scenario A (Approval):** Estimated selling price minus costs to complete/sell × Probability (e.g., 80%).
*   **Scenario B (Rejection/Delay):** Salvage value (scrap) or zero × Probability (e.g., 20%).
*   **Calculation:** If the weighted average is less than $2.1M, record the difference as an impairment loss on the Income Statement and reduce the Inventory asset on the Balance Sheet.

**3. Presentation for Investors & Auditors**
*   **Balance Sheet:** List as "Inventory - Work in Progress." If impaired, show the net value.
*   **Footnotes:** Crucial for your Series B. Disclose the accounting policy explicitly: "Inventory is stated at the lower of cost or net realizable value." Detail the $2.1M exposure, the specific regulatory status of the three batches, and the management's assessment of approval probability. Transparency regarding the *reason* for the delay (regulatory backlog vs. product failure) protects your valuation narrative.

### Required Documentation for Due Diligence

To satisfy auditors and investors, maintain a dedicated "Inventory Valuation File" containing:
1.  **Client Contracts:** Highlighting payment terms, cancellation clauses, and regulatory contingency language.
2.  **Regulatory Correspondence:** All letters/emails from agencies showing active review status (proving the delay is external, not due to product defects).
3.  **Impairment Analysis Memo:** A quarterly document signed by CFO/CEO detailing the probability assessment and NRV calculations for each stuck batch.
4.  **Aging Report:** Specific tracking of WIP duration vs. historical approval timelines to demonstrate that 18 months, while long, is within the realm of normal variance for your sector.
