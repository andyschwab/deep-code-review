**SafeCloud Subpoena Response Protocol**

**1. Immediate Triage & Validation (Do Not Access Data Yet)**
Upon receipt of any request, immediately log it in a secure legal register. Do not access, retrieve, or review user data until the request is validated. Accessing data prematurely may violate GDPR or constitute an unauthorized search.
*   **Validate Authority:** Confirm the requesting agency has jurisdiction over SafeCloud's specific legal entity serving that user.
*   **Validate Form:** Ensure the document is a signed court order, warrant, or valid subpoena. Reject informal emails or letters without legal standing.
*   **Scope Check:** Verify the request targets specific users and data types. Reject "fishing expeditions" or broad data dumps.

**2. Jurisdictional Routing (The "Golden Rule")**
Route requests based strictly on the user's physical location and data residency, not the requester's location.
*   **US Users (US Requests):** Process under US law (e.g., Stored Communications Act). If the warrant is valid, compliance is generally mandatory. Notify the user unless legally gagged.
*   **German/EU Users (US Requests):** **DO NOT COMPLY** directly with US subpoenas for EU data. The US CLOUD Act does not automatically override GDPR. These requests must be routed through Mutual Legal Assistance Treaties (MLATs). Direct compliance risks massive GDPR fines and brand destruction.
*   **German Users (German Requests):** Process under German StPO (Criminal Procedure Code) and GDPR. Ensure the order meets strict proportionality requirements.

**3. The "Necessity & Proportionality" Test**
Before releasing data, apply this filter to every valid request:
*   Is the data strictly necessary for the stated investigation?
*   Is the scope limited to the specific timeframe and user identified?
*   If the request is overbroad, file a motion to quash or narrow the scope before producing anything.

**4. User Notification Strategy**
To honor your privacy promise:
*   **Default:** Notify the user immediately upon receiving a valid request, providing them a copy of the order and a deadline to challenge it legally.
*   **Exception:** Withhold notification *only* if the court order explicitly includes a non-disclosure (gag) provision. Document this exception internally.

**5. Cost Mitigation**
Given the $1,200/request cost:
*   Create a standardized "Legal Sufficiency Checklist" for junior staff to reject invalid requests instantly without attorney review.
*   Reserve attorney time only for valid requests requiring scope negotiation or jurisdictional analysis.

***

**Critical Jurisdictional Conflicts Requiring Immediate Attorney Review**

1.  **US Subpoenas for German Data:** You face a direct conflict between US enforcement pressure and GDPR Article 48 (which forbids transfers based solely on US administrative subpoenas). An attorney must establish your formal refusal stance for non-MLAT requests to prevent accidental GDPR violations.
2.  **Data Residency vs. Corporate Domicile:** If SafeCloud is incorporated in the US but hosts German data on EU servers, you must clarify which entity receives the subpoena. Serving the US parent for EU data creates a legal trap; an attorney must restructure your service terms to ensure EU data is legally held by a distinct EU entity where possible.
3.  **Biometric Data Classification:** In Germany, biometric data is "special category" data under GDPR Article 9, requiring higher protection thresholds than standard logs. An attorney must confirm if your current retention policy inadvertently violates German bans on processing such data for law enforcement purposes without explicit, case-by-case judicial authorization beyond a standard subpoena.

*Note: This protocol is an operational framework, not legal advice. You must retain qualified counsel in both the US and Germany to finalize these procedures.*
