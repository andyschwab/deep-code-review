**Critical Warning: Your Current Pricing Model Likely Violates Colorado Ethics Rules**

Your concern is well-founded. Charging a percentage of a settlement (2% of the final amount) almost certainly constitutes **impermissible fee-sharing with a non-lawyer** and potentially the **unauthorized practice of law (UPL)** under Colorado Rules of Professional Conduct.

**The Legal Problem**
Colorado Rule of Professional Conduct 5.4(a) explicitly prohibits lawyers from sharing legal fees with non-lawyers. The rationale is to protect a lawyer's independent professional judgment from being influenced by a non-lawyer's financial interest in the outcome of a case. By tying your revenue directly to the settlement amount, you create a financial incentive that could be interpreted as influencing litigation strategy or settlement decisions. Even if your tool is passive, the *structure* of the fee mimics a contingency fee arrangement, which is strictly reserved for licensed attorneys.

Furthermore, if your bonus is contingent on "winning" the case, regulators may argue your software is participating in the legal representation itself, crossing the line into UPL. The fact that three clients generated $30,000 in bonuses highlights the scale of the exposure; if audited, your clients could face disciplinary action for improper fee-sharing, and your company could be enjoined from operating in this manner.

**Immediate Action Required**
You should immediately cease collecting the percentage-based bonus on new agreements and consult with a legal ethics attorney in Colorado to review existing contracts. Do not attempt to argue that the "tool" did the work; the fee structure itself is the violation.

**Alternative Pricing Models Aligned with Ethics**

To maintain a "success-aligned" model without violating Rule 5.4, you must decouple your fees from the *outcome* of the litigation and instead tie them to the *value* or *usage* of the tool.

1.  **Tiered Usage-Based Pricing (Recommended)**
    Instead of a % of settlement, charge based on the volume of documents processed or the number of cases active.
    *   *Structure:* Base fee ($500) + Usage tiers (e.g., $0.10 per document reviewed or $250 per active case file).
    *   *Why it works:* You capture more revenue when firms win bigger cases (which usually involve more documents) without technically linking the fee to the settlement dollar amount.

2.  **Fixed "Success" Flat Fees**
    Charge a predetermined bonus triggered by a case milestone, not a percentage of the recovery.
    *   *Structure:* Base fee ($500) + Fixed Bonus (e.g., $2,500 upon case closure/settlement).
    *   *Why it works:* The fee is fixed and known in advance. It does not fluctuate based on the settlement size, preserving the attorney's independent judgment regarding settlement amounts.

3.  **Value-Based Tiering (Good-Better-Best)**
    Create tiers that reflect the intensity of litigation support needed.
    *   *Structure:*
        *   Standard: $500/mo (up to 5 cases).
        *   Pro: $1,200/mo (up to 15 cases + advanced AI analytics).
        *   Enterprise: $2,500/mo (unlimited cases + priority support).
    *   *Why it works:* Firms handling high-stakes, high-settlement cases will naturally gravitate toward higher tiers because they need the capacity, aligning your revenue with their success indirectly.

4.  **Hourly or Credit-Based Model**
    Sell "credits" that attorneys consume as they use the AI for discovery.
    *   *Structure:* Base platform fee + purchase of credit packs (e.g., $1,000 for 5,000 analysis credits).
    *   *Why it works:* This is a standard vendor relationship. The attorney decides how many credits to spend based on their case strategy, maintaining full control.

**Conclusion**
Your current 2% model is a significant regulatory risk. Transition immediately to a model where your compensation is determined by the *input* (documents, cases, time) or a *fixed fee*, rather than the *output* (settlement dollars). This preserves your revenue potential from high-value firms while ensuring full compliance with Colorado bar rules.
