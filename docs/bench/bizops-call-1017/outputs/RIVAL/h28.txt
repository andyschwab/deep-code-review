**Typical Policy Exclusions and Triggers**
Most professional liability policies for tech firms are written on a **"claims-made"** basis. This means the policy only covers claims *made* against you during the active policy period, regardless of when the error occurred. If your client discovered the bug and filed a claim three months after your audit, coverage depends entirely on whether your policy was active at the moment the claim was filed and whether you reported it immediately.
*   **Retroactive Dates:** Policies often exclude work performed before a specific "retroactive date." If your engagement predates this, you may have no coverage.
*   **Known Circumstances:** If you were aware of potential issues before the policy started, claims arising from them are excluded.
*   **Consequential Damages:** Many standard E&O policies explicitly exclude "consequential damages" (like lost user funds due to a hack) rather than just the cost of re-performing the audit. They may cover your legal defense but not the $200,000 loss itself.
*   **Crypto-Specific Exclusions:** General tech E&O policies often have exclusions for cryptocurrency, smart contract failures, or decentralized finance (DeFi) activities unless a specific rider or specialized cyber policy is purchased.
