To model FinalRest's true unit economics under Florida's 24-month trust mandate, you must shift from a standard accrual P&L view to a **Cash Flow & Capital Efficiency** model. Investors will ignore your "booked revenue" until month 25; they will scrutinize how long you survive while funding operations with trapped capital.

### 1. Restructure Your Unit Economics Model

Do not calculate LTV based on monthly recurring revenue (MRR) alone. Instead, build a **Cumulative Cash Flow per Customer** timeline:

*   **Months 0–24 (The Valley of Death):**
    *   **Cash In:** $0 (Funds are locked in trust).
    *   **Cash Out:** Immediate Cost of Goods Sold (COGS) + Customer Acquisition Cost (CAC).
    *   **Net Cash Flow:** Negative $1,200 (CAC) minus monthly service costs.
    *   **Action:** Track the "Cash Burn Duration" per customer. How many months of operating cash does one new customer consume before yielding a dollar?
*   **Month 25+ (Revenue Recognition):**
    *   **Cash In:** Revenue recognition begins (assuming funds release monthly or lump sum per contract terms).
    *   **Net Cash Flow:** Turns positive only after recouping the 24-month deficit.

**The Formula for True LTV:**
$$LTV_{cash} = \sum_{t=25}^{Churn} \frac{Revenue_t}{(1+r)^t} - CAC - \sum_{t=0}^{24} \frac{Cost_t}{(1+r)^t}$$
*(Where $r$ is your cost of capital/discount rate).*

### 2. Accounting for Time Value & Opportunity Cost

You cannot treat a dollar received in Month 25 as equal to a dollar spent today.

*   **Discount Rate ($r$):** Use your actual cost of capital. Since you are pre-profit with $150k cash, your cost of capital is high (likely 20–30% if considering equity dilution, or your credit line interest rate).
*   **Opportunity Cost Calculation:** Calculate what that trapped capital could earn elsewhere or save in interest.
    *   *Example:* If you hold $299/mo in trust that you cannot touch, and your cost of capital is 20% annually, the opportunity cost is roughly $60/year per contract in lost potential value.
*   **Model Input:** Add a line item called "Capital Carry Cost" in your P&L projection. This is not an out-of-pocket expense but a theoretical drag on valuation: `Trapped Capital Balance × Cost of Capital Rate`.

### 3. Critical Metrics for Investors

Given the 24-month delay, investors will disregard standard SaaS metrics like "Quick Ratio" or immediate "ARR Growth." They will focus on:

1.  **Cash Payback Period (Extended):** Standard SaaS aims for <12 months. Yours is structurally 24+ months. You must model exactly when cumulative cash flow turns positive per cohort.
2.  **Burn Multiple vs. Trust Growth:** How much cash are you burning to grow the Trust Assets Under Management (AUM)? Investors want to see that the trapped capital (Trust AUM) is growing faster than your operating burn.
3.  **Cohort Survival Rate at Month 24:** Since you don't get paid until month 25, churn before then is catastrophic (100% loss of CAC with zero revenue). Your 2% monthly churn is acceptable, but if it spikes to 4% before month 24, your unit economics break completely.
4.  **Working Capital Requirement:** The total cash needed to fund the gap between serving customer #1 and collecting revenue from customer #1. With $150k cash and $1,200 CAC, you can only acquire ~125 new homes before running dry, assuming zero operating costs (which is false). You likely have runway for far fewer.

### Immediate Strategic Implication
Your current pricing ($299/mo) likely does not account for the **financing cost** of the 24-month float. You are effectively lending interest-free capital to the trust beneficiaries. To fix unit economics, you must either:
*   Charge an upfront "setup/technology fee" (recognized immediately) to cover CAC.
*   Secure a debt facility using the Trust AUM as collateral to bridge the cash flow gap.
*   Increase prices significantly to absorb the high cost of capital.

Note: Your current cash runway is critically tight given the lag between spend and return; prioritize securing a credit line backed by the trust assets immediately.
