### Financial Implications of Strategic Paths

**1. Raise Prices for Remaining Slots (Recommended Immediate Action)**
With supply capped at 60% of demand, you have a artificial scarcity. Your current unit economics are weak: $450 price - $280 variable cost = $170 contribution margin per box.
*   **Action:** Immediately raise prices for the limited 300 units (60% of 500) you can fulfill. Given the critical nature of prescription compounding, demand is likely inelastic.
*   **Implication:** If you raise the price to $750 (a conservative estimate for scarce, regulated medical ingredients), your margin jumps to $470 per box.
*   **Cash Flow Impact:** 300 units × $470 = $141,000 monthly contribution. This covers your $35,000 burn entirely and generates $106,000 in monthly profit to build a war chest. This extends your runway indefinitely without new capital, provided you maintain the supply cap.

**2. Pause New Sign-ups (Operational Necessity)**
*   **Action:** Halt all sales efforts immediately.
*   **Implication:** This prevents over-promising and reputational damage from failing to deliver. It does not improve cash flow on its own but protects the brand while you implement price increases.
*   **Cash Flow Impact:** Neutral in the short term, but essential to prevent future liability from unfulfilled contracts.

**3. Seek a Waiver (High Risk, Low Probability)**
*   **Action:** Petition the DEA for a quota increase.
*   **Implication:** DEA quota adjustments for controlled substances are notoriously slow (often 6–12 months) and rarely granted for commercial expansion without proof of unmet patient need that competitors cannot fill.
*   **Cash Flow Impact:** Negative. Pursuing this requires legal fees and administrative hours with little chance of immediate relief. Relying on this path risks burning through your $120,000 reserves before approval.

**Recommendation:** Execute **Path 1** immediately. Treat your supply cap as a feature, not a bug. Transition from a volume game to a high-margin boutique supplier. Use the generated surplus cash to fund legal exploration of Path 3 later.

### Legal vs. Internal Actions

**Handle Internally:**
*   **Customer Communication:** Drafting notices to existing customers about supply constraints and new pricing terms (subject to contract review).
*   **Allocation Logic:** Deciding which 60% of orders to fill based on your own criteria (e.g., tenure, prescription urgency), provided you do not violate anti-discrimination laws.
*   **Data Gathering:** Compiling data on specific prescriptions you cannot fill to demonstrate "unmet patient need."

**Hire a Specialized Attorney (Controlled Substances/DEA Specialist):**
*   **Quota Transfer/Reallocation:** Investigating if you can legally purchase unused quota from other registrants (a complex DEA process).
*   **Waiver Petitioning:** Filing formal requests for emergency quota increases based on specific patient shortages.
*   **Contract Review:** Ensuring your sudden price hikes and supply rationing do not breach existing annual contracts or violate state pharmacy board regulations regarding price gouging during shortages.
*   **Compliance Audit:** Verifying that your rationing method does not inadvertently violate the Controlled Substances Act regarding diversion or preferential treatment.

*Note: Do not attempt to negotiate quota reallocations or file waiver petitions without counsel; procedural errors can trigger DEA audits.*
