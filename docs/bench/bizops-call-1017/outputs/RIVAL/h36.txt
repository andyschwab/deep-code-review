### Financial Implications: Income vs. Equity Classification

The classification of your $3 million token sale proceeds fundamentally alters your balance sheet, cash flow, and survival probability.

**Scenario A: Classified as Taxable Income (Revenue)**
If the Kenya Revenue Authority (KRA) or relevant body deems the token sale as revenue (e.g., pre-selling a service or utility):
*   **Balance Sheet:** The $3 million appears as revenue on the Income Statement. After a hypothetical 30% corporate tax rate, you recognize a tax liability of ~$900,000 immediately. Your retained earnings increase by only $2.1 million.
*   **Cash Flow:** You receive $3 million in cash inflow. However, you face an immediate cash outflow of ~$900,000 for tax payments.
*   **Operational Impact:** Your net available capital drops to $2.1 million. With a 12-person team and a original runway of 10 months on $3 million, this tax bill reduces your runway to approximately 7 months. This creates immediate liquidity stress, forcing earlier fundraising or drastic cost-cutting.

**Scenario B: Classified as Equity Capital (or Deferred Liability)**
If the tokens are treated as equity instruments or prepaid services with deferred revenue recognition:
*   **Balance Sheet:** The $3 million is recorded as a liability (Deferred Revenue) or Equity (Share Capital/Reserves), not immediate income. No tax is triggered in the current fiscal year. Tax obligations arise only as revenue is recognized over time (as services are delivered) or upon specific liquidity events.
*   **Cash Flow:** You retain the full $3 million cash inflow with no immediate tax outflow.
*   **Operational Impact:** Your full 10-month runway is preserved. This maximizes your development window and reduces the risk of insolvency before product launch.

**The Critical Trade-off:**
While classifying as equity/deferred preserves cash, it significantly increases regulatory scrutiny. Treating tokens as equity often triggers securities laws, requiring strict compliance with prospectus requirements, investor accreditation checks, and registration with the Capital Markets Authority (CMA) of Kenya. Misclassifying a security token as a utility token to avoid tax can lead to severe penalties, forced refunds, and criminal liability.
