### Risk Analysis: Aggregation vs. Utility Classification

Your current model aggregates 6,750 kWh (6.75 MWh) of capacity across 500 homes. Scaling to 2,000 homes will increase this to 27 MWh. In California, the distinction between a "software marketplace" and a "regulated utility" often hinges on **control**, **ownership of the electron**, and **retail sales authority**, rather than a single fixed megawatt-hour threshold.

**Primary Risks of Scaling:**

1.  **Unauthorized Retail Sales:** If GridShare takes title to the energy (even momentarily) and resells it to the grid or third parties, you may be acting as an unlicensed electricity provider. The California Public Utilities Commission (CPUC) strictly regulates who can sell electricity. Your 20% commission model suggests you are taking a cut of the transaction, which regulators may view as acting as a principal rather than a neutral agent.
2.  **Dispatch Control:** If your software automatically dispatches energy from home batteries without explicit, real-time consent for every transaction, you may be deemed to be operating a "Virtual Power Plant" (VPP) that requires specific utility certification. Regulators look at who controls the asset: if you control the discharge, you look like a utility.
3.  **Rate Regulation Exposure:** If classified as a utility, your pricing (the rate you pay homeowners vs. what you sell for) becomes subject to CPUC approval. You cannot simply set a 20% margin; you must prove your rates are "just and reasonable." This destroys the agility of your marketplace model.
4.  **Reporting & Compliance Burden:** Utility classification triggers mandatory reporting on reliability, safety, financial solvency, and environmental impact. The cost of compliance often exceeds the revenue of early-stage aggregators.

**The "Threshold" Misconception:**
There is rarely a single "safe" number of participants (e.g., "under 1,000 homes is safe"). Instead, risk scales with **visibility** and **impact on grid stability**. At 500 homes, you may be under the radar. At 2,000 homes (27 MWh), you are a significant grid resource. This volume makes it impossible to operate in a regulatory gray zone; you will trigger interconnection studies and regulatory scrutiny regardless of your legal structure.
