### Unit Economics Analysis for VRTrain

**Current State ($200/seat, 40% completion)**
*   **Revenue per Seat:** $200
*   **Variable Cost per Seat:** $15 (server cost for active users). *Note: You stated $15 per "active" user. If 40% complete, we assume 40% are "active." Cost = $15.*
*   **Contribution Margin per Seat:** $185
*   **Hardware Context:** The $350 hardware cost is likely a sunk cost borne by the client or a one-time CapEx. If you are subsidizing this, your current model is insolvent ($200 revenue - $350 hardware = -$150 loss per seat before server costs). Assuming the client buys hardware, your software margin is positive.
*   **The Real Issue:** Low completion (40%) threatens renewal. The $1.2M content development cost is a fixed sunk cost; it does not change with pricing but requires high volume to amortize.

**Scenario A: Lower Price to $100/seat**
*   **Revenue per Seat:** $100
*   **Variable Cost:** $15 (assuming activity remains 40%)
*   **Contribution Margin:** $85
*   **Analysis:** This cuts your margin by 54%. Unless lowering the price doubles your active user base (increasing server costs but proving value) or significantly boosts seat volume sold per client, this accelerates cash burn. It does not solve the engagement problem; it merely discounts a product users aren't finishing. **Do not lower price to fix engagement.**

**Scenario B: Pivot to Usage-Based (Per Active User)**
*   **Model:** Charge only for users who complete training.
*   **Risk:** If completion stays at 40%, your revenue drops 60% immediately unless you raise the per-user price significantly (e.g., to $500 per completer).
*   **Analysis:** This aligns price with value but exposes your low engagement directly to the P&L. Only pursue if you are confident you can fix the completion rate to >80% quickly.

**Lifetime Value (LTV) & Engagement**
You cannot calculate true LTV with low engagement because LTV relies on retention.
*   **Formula:** $LTV = (ARPU \times Gross Margin \%) / Churn Rate$
*   **Current Reality:** With 40% completion, your churn risk is extreme. If clients don't see employees finishing, they will not renew. Your effective LTV is likely limited to **one year** regardless of price.
*   **Strategy:** Focus on increasing completion to 70%+ before changing price. The value is in the *behavior change*, not the seat license.

**Contractual & Refund Clauses**
Standard enterprise SaaS contracts rarely have automatic refund clauses tied to usage metrics (like completion rates) unless explicitly negotiated as a "Success Guarantee."
*   **Risk:** While automatic refunds are unlikely, low utilization is the primary trigger for **non-renewal** and **clawback negotiations** during renewal discussions.
*   **Action:** Audit your current Master Services Agreements (MSAs). Look for "Minimum Usage" or "Adoption Targets" clauses. If none exist, you are safe from immediate refunds, but you are vulnerable to churn.

**Recommendation**
1.  **Hold Price:** Do not drop to $100. It devalues the solution and fails to cover the risk of low adoption.
2.  **Fix Product/Onboarding:** Investigate why completion is 40%. Is the content too long? Is the hardware friction too high?
3.  **Restructure Deal:** Instead of lowering price, offer "Success-Based Pricing." Keep the $200 seat fee but add a rebate if completion falls below 50%, shifting the risk slightly while maintaining price integrity.

*Note: The $1.2M development cost is irrelevant to marginal pricing decisions; focus on variable costs and retention.*
*Ensure your sales team stops selling "seats" and starts selling "completed certifications" to align incentives.*
