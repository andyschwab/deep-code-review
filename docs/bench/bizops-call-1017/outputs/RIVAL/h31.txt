### Unit Economics & Strategic Recommendation for VerifySafe

**Current Financial Reality**
*   **Margin per Check:** $0.75 ($1.20 Price - $0.45 Cost).
*   **Margin Percentage:** 62.5%.
*   **The Churn Leak:** A 15% monthly churn rate implies an average customer lifespan of ~6.7 months. Even with healthy per-unit margins, this churn destroys your Lifetime Value (LTV). If a casino processes 10,000 checks/month, they generate $7,500 in monthly margin. Losing them after 6 months costs you ~$45,000 in future margin per client.
*   **The False Positive Cost:** An 8% false positive rate means 8 out of 100 legitimate players are blocked. For a casino, a blocked player is not just a failed verification; it is a lost depositor. If an average player deposits $200, an 8% error rate on 1,000 verifications potentially blocks $160,000 in gross gaming revenue for your client. Your $1.20 fee is negligible compared to the millions in revenue they lose due to your friction.

**Strategic Pivot: From "Per Check" to "Verified Success"**

Do not move to a flat tiered subscription yet, and do not use legalistic "penalty clauses" which create adversarial relationships. Instead, restructure your pricing to align with **successful, compliant onboarding**.

**Recommended Model: Base Fee + Performance Bonus (or Credit)**

Shift the narrative from "cost per API call" to "cost per verified player."

1.  **Restructure the Unit Price:**
    *   Lower the base upfront cost to **$0.90 per check**. This reduces the client's immediate friction and makes you competitive against cheaper, lower-quality alternatives.
    *   Introduce a **"False Positive Credit"**: If a check is flagged as high-risk (blocked) but the client manually verifies the user as legitimate within 24 hours, issue a credit of **$1.20** (effectively making that specific check free + compensating for the admin time).
    *   *Why this works:* It forces your engineering team to optimize the threshold. If your false positive rate stays at 8%, your effective revenue drops to ~$1.08 per check ($0.90 base - 8% * $1.20 credit). However, your churn should plummet because clients no longer feel punished for your errors.

2.  **Volume Tiers with SLA Guarantees (The Upsell):**
    Once the base model stabilizes churn, introduce tiers based on monthly volume that offer **guaranteed False Positive Rates (FPR)** rather than just price breaks.
    *   **Standard:** $0.90/check, FPR ~8%.
    *   **Premium (Volume > 50k/mo):** $0.85/check, Guaranteed FPR < 4%. If FPR exceeds 4%, the excess checks are free.
    *   **Enterprise (Custom):** Dedicated model tuning for < 2% FPR. Price negotiated based on value of retained high-rollers.

**Addressing the Regulatory vs. Churn Dilemma**

You cannot simply lower thresholds to stop churn; fines in the UK/Malta can exceed £1M, which instantly bankrupts you.
*   **The Solution:** Differentiate via **Manual Review Integration**. Your current model likely auto-rejects. Offer a hybrid flow: "Auto-Pass" (low risk), "Auto-Fail" (high risk/fraud patterns), and "Refer for Review" (the ambiguous 8%).
*   Charge a lower fee for "Refer for Review" ($0.45, covering only your cost) and let the casino's compliance team make the final call, or sell an add-on service where *your* experts perform the manual review for $5.00 per case. This transfers the regulatory decision liability back to the client for the edge cases while keeping their players in the funnel.

**Immediate Action Plan**
1.  **Stop selling "per check."** Start selling "verified onboarding rates."
2.  **Implement the Credit Mechanism.** Immediately cap the financial pain clients feel from your 8% error rate.
3.  **Calculate the Break-Even:** Determine how much churn reduction is needed to offset the lower effective price. Reducing churn from 15% to 5% more than doubles your LTV, easily absorbing a 10-15% price reduction.

*Note: This strategy shifts your risk profile to match the client's, turning a commodity API into a partnership. Ensure your legal team drafts the "Credit" terms carefully to avoid them being classified as penalties.*
