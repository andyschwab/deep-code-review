### Strategic Overview: Pricing vs. Legal Structure

Your concern stems from the fear that your pricing model ($150/bed/month) could be interpreted as sharing in the savings you generate (value-based outcomes), which might look like an inducement for referrals. Here is how pricing strategy intersects with these legal concepts:

**1. Fair Market Value (FMV) as a Defense**
The primary "safe harbor" protection for technology vendors often relies on proving that your fees represent **Fair Market Value (FMV)** for the software itself, independent of the volume or value of referrals.
*   **Current Model Analysis:** Charging per bed is a standard SaaS value metric in healthcare IT. It scales with the hospital's size, not necessarily with the number of referrals or specific patient outcomes.
*   **The Risk:** If your $150/bed fee was calculated *specifically* to match a percentage of the savings the hospital earns (e.g., "We charge you exactly 50% of your readmission penalty avoidance"), regulators may view this as disguised gain-sharing.
*   **Strategic Fix:** Ensure your $150 price point is derived from a **cost-plus** or **market-comparable** analysis, not a percentage of the hospital's downstream savings. You need a formal FMV assessment from a third party to document that $150/bed is what any hospital would pay for this tool, regardless of their specific savings performance.

**2. Avoiding "Gain-Sharing" Appearances**
While your software *delivers* value via reduced readmissions, your *pricing mechanism* should not fluctuate based on those specific outcomes.
*   **Do Not:** Structure contracts where the fee drops if readmissions don't improve, or rises if they do. This directly ties your revenue to clinical outcomes, triggering AKS scrutiny.
*   **Do:** Keep the pricing flat (fixed fee per bed) or based on neutral usage metrics (e.g., number of active monitors). The value proposition in your sales pitch can highlight the 12% reduction, but the invoice must remain detached from that specific metric.

**3. The "Technology Vendor" Safe Harbor**
Federal regulations often provide protections for bona fide technology sales if:
*   The price is set in advance.
*   The price is consistent with FMV.
*   The pricing does not take into account the volume or value of any referrals.
*   The technology is necessary for the patient care (which your RPM dashboard likely is).

**4. Recommended Next Steps for Your Pricing Team**
To support your legal counsel, prepare the following pricing artifacts immediately:
*   **FMV Documentation:** Compile data on competitor pricing for similar RPM dashboards to prove $150/bed is market-standard, not a custom number derived from hospital savings.
*   **Cost-to-Serve Analysis:** Document your development and support costs to show the price covers your expenses plus a reasonable margin, rather than being a slice of the hospital's bonus.
*   **Contract Language Review:** Ensure your Master Services Agreements (MSAs) explicitly state that fees are for the *license and support of the software*, not for patient outcomes or referral generation.

**Summary:** Your per-bed pricing model is generally safer than a "percentage of savings" model, provided the rate is defensible as Fair Market Value. Do not alter your fees to share in savings; instead, rigidly anchor them to the software's standalone value.
