### Pricing & Liability Framework for SentinelAI

**Do not absorb regulatory fines into your per-transaction fee.** Attempting to self-insure a $25,000 risk on a $0.05 transaction creates a pricing model that is either prohibitively expensive (driving away small credit unions) or dangerously undercapitalized (risking your company's solvency). No sustainable SaaS vendor builds a "reserve fund" directly into a micro-usage fee for catastrophic tail risks.

Here is the industry-standard framework for structuring this in the banking sector:

**1. Decouple Usage Pricing from Liability**
Maintain your **$0.05 per transaction** as the pure value metric for monitoring. This keeps the entry price low and predictable for smaller credit unions, aligning cost with their volume. Do not inflate this rate to cover potential fines; it distorts the value proposition and makes you uncompetitive against legacy systems that likely separate these costs.

**2. Implement a "Limited Indemnity" Clause**
Standard practice in fintech is not to accept unlimited liability for regulatory fines, but to offer a **capped indemnity**.
*   **The Cap:** Limit your total liability to a specific multiple of your Annual Recurring Revenue (ARR) from that specific client (e.g., 12x–24x of fees paid) or a fixed dollar amount (e.g., $1M–$5M), whichever is lower.
*   **The Trigger:** Indemnity applies only if the false negative is proven to be a direct result of **gross negligence** or a documented failure of your algorithm against its stated Service Level Agreement (SLA), not merely a market shift or novel fraud pattern outside the model's defined scope.
*   **The Exclusion:** Explicitly exclude liability for fines resulting from the client's failure to act on alerts generated by your system.

**3. Purchase Professional Liability (E&O) Insurance**
Instead of self-insuring via pricing, transfer the risk. Purchase **Errors & Omissions (E&O)** and **Cyber Liability** insurance specifically tailored for AI/fintech vendors.
*   Your premium becomes a fixed operational cost, which you can factor into your overall margin, rather than a variable cost per transaction.
*   When negotiating with credit unions, you can state: *"SentinelAI carries $X million in E&O coverage to back our indemnity clause,"* which provides them security without requiring you to charge $1.00 per transaction to build a reserve.

**4. Tiered Service Levels (Optional)**
If larger credit unions demand higher coverage:
*   **Standard Tier:** $0.05/txn + Standard Indemnity (capped at 12x fees).
*   **Enterprise Tier:** $0.07/txn + Enhanced Indemnity (capped at 24x fees) + Priority SLA.
This allows you to price the *risk transfer* separately from the *monitoring utility*.

**Industry Context on Liability**
In the banking sector, vendors rarely accept full responsibility for regulatory fines. Regulators hold the *bank* ultimately responsible for compliance, not the vendor. Therefore, contracts typically frame the vendor's role as "providing tools to aid compliance" rather than "guaranteeing compliance." Your contract should reflect this: you sell the detection capability; the bank retains the duty of care. By offering a capped indemnity backed by insurance, you match market norms while protecting your balance sheet from a single event wiping out your revenue.

*Note: This framework separates operational pricing from catastrophic risk transfer, a standard approach in regulated fintech. Consult legal counsel to draft specific indemnity language and insurance brokers to quantify premium costs.*
