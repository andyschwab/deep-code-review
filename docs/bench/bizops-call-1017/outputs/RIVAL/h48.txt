To stabilize your unit economics against North Sea weather volatility, you must shift from a pure "pay-per-successful-dive" model to a hybrid **Availability Retainer + Reduced Dive Fee** structure. Charging only for completed dives transfers 100% of the weather risk to you, making your revenue unpredictable while your fixed costs ($120k/month) remain constant.

**Recommended Contract Structure:**
1.  **Monthly Availability Retainer:** Charge a fixed fee (e.g., $60,000) to guarantee drone and technician readiness. This covers your baseline fixed costs regardless of weather.
2.  **Reduced Per-Dive Fee:** Lower the variable price per successful dive (e.g., from $15,000 to $10,000). Since the retainer covers overhead, the per-dive price now reflects mostly variable operational costs ($4,000) plus margin.

This ensures you earn revenue even when seas are too rough to operate, aligning customer incentives (they pay for access) with your cost structure.

**Financial Model & Break-Even Analysis:**
Your current fixed costs are $120,000/month. Under your old model ($15k/dive, $4k variable cost), your contribution margin per dive is $11,000.
*   **Old Break-Even:** $120,000 / $11,000 = **10.9 dives** (round up to 11).
*   **Risk Scenario:** If weather cuts capacity by 40%, your potential max dives drop from 200 (10 rigs × 20 dives) to 120. While 11 dives seems low, if a specific rig gets hit by a storm cluster, you may perform zero dives for that client while still paying technicians.

**Proposed Hybrid Model Math:**
Assume a $60,000 monthly retainer per rig cluster (or aggregated) and a reduced $10,000/dive fee.
*   New Contribution Margin per dive: $10,000 - $4,000 = $6,000.
*   Fixed Costs covered by Retainer: $60,000.
*   Remaining Fixed Costs to cover: $120,000 - $60,000 = $60,000.
*   **New Break-Even Dives:** $60,000 / $6,000 = **10 dives**.

Crucially, in a severe weather month where you only complete 50% of planned dives, the retainer ensures you are already profitable before a single drone launches. The customer accepts a slightly higher effective cost in calm months in exchange for guaranteed priority access and budget predictability, while you secure cash flow during storms.

**Minimum Viable Volume Strategy:**
To cover the full $120,000 fixed cost without a retainer, you need 11 dives. With a 40% weather reduction, you must contract for a minimum of **19 planned dives** to statistically expect 11 completed ones. However, relying on averages is dangerous. Instead, institute a **"Weather Minimum" clause**: The client pays for a minimum of 12 dives per month regardless of completion, or the retainer model applies. This guarantees you never dip below your break-even point due to external factors.

**Strategic Adjustment:**
Stop selling "dives"; start selling "inspection coverage." Frame the retainer as an insurance policy against project delays. By absorbing less weather risk, you protect your margins. If a client refuses a retainer, increase the per-dive price to $18,000+ to build a risk premium into every successful dive, effectively charging them extra for the days you *don't* work.

*Note: Ensure contracts explicitly define "force majeure" vs. "operational weather windows" to prevent disputes over what constitutes a billable delay.*
