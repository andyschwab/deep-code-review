Liability for AI-assisted medical coding is a complex, evolving area of law where responsibility often depends on specific contract terms, the degree of human oversight, and jurisdictional regulations.

However, I can outline the general landscape of risks and standard compliance considerations you must address with qualified counsel before signing new contracts:

**Liability Landscape**
In most current healthcare scenarios, the **provider (the clinic)** retains ultimate legal responsibility for the accuracy of billing submissions submitted under their National Provider Identifier (NPI). Insurers hold the provider accountable for upcoding, regardless of whether an AI tool suggested the code. However, your software company faces significant **commercial and contractual liability**. If your tool systematically suggests incorrect higher codes, clinics may sue you for breach of contract, negligence, or indemnification claims if they face fines, recoupments, or exclusion from insurance programs due to your software's errors. There is currently limited case law specifically defining "algorithmic upcoding" liability, making this a high-risk, undefined precedent area.

**Critical Compliance Safeguards to Discuss with Counsel**
To mitigate audit risks and potential liability, you should consider building the following safeguards, subject to legal validation:
*   **Human-in-the-Loop Enforcement:** The system must never auto-submit codes. It should only offer *suggestions* that require explicit, documented review and approval by a certified human coder or physician.
*   **Audit Trails:** Maintain immutable logs showing exactly what the AI suggested, what the human reviewed, and what was ultimately submitted, including timestamps and user IDs.
*   **Confidence Scoring & Flags:** The AI should display confidence levels and flag suggestions that deviate significantly from historical norms for that provider or specialty, prompting extra scrutiny.
*   **Downcoding Bias:** Consider tuning the model to be conservative (suggesting lower codes when uncertain) rather than aggressive, reducing upcoding risk at the cost of potential underbilling.
*   **Clear Disclaimers & Contracts:** Your Terms of Service must explicitly state that the tool is a decision-support system only, not a billing service, and that the provider assumes all final responsibility. Indemnification clauses need careful drafting.

**Legal Consultation Requirements**
Before signing a large hospital group contract, you must engage:
1.  **A Healthcare Regulatory Attorney:** Specializing in fraud and abuse laws (Anti-Kickback Statute, Stark Law, False Claims Act) to review your business model and contract terms.
2.  **A Technology Liability Attorney:** To assess product liability exposure specific to AI decision-support tools and draft appropriate limitations of liability and indemnification provisions.
3.  **A Compliance Expert:** Preferably a Certified Professional Coder (CPC) with audit experience, to validate your product's logic against current CPT/ICD-10 guidelines and payer policies.

Do not proceed with large contracts until these experts have reviewed your specific architecture, contracts, and risk profile.
