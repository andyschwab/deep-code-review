**Lane R: Regulation-Dependent (Route Immediately)**
Your core question—whether token sale proceeds are taxable income or equity capital, and whether the tokens constitute securities—is a **Lane R** matter. The answer depends entirely on specific determinations under Kenyan law (Kenya Revenue Authority guidelines) and securities regulations (Capital Markets Authority of Kenya).

*   **Tax Regime:** You must route the "income vs. equity" classification to a licensed Kenyan tax adviser or CPA. Do not assume utility token status avoids income tax; jurisdictions increasingly view initial proceeds as revenue unless specific equity instruments are used. A wrong self-classification could trigger the $900,000 liability you fear, plus penalties.
*   **Securities Regime:** Selling tokens to raise $3 million is likely a **securities offering**. Whether your token is a "utility" or "security" is a legal test (often based on the expectation of profit from the efforts of others), not a label you choose. You must engage a securities lawyer licensed in Kenya to structure this offering. Proceeding without counsel risks violating fundraising laws, which can result in forced refunds, fines, or criminal liability.
