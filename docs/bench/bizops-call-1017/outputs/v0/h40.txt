### Protocol Framework for Subpoena Evaluation

You cannot unilaterally decide which subpoenas to honor based on your privacy policy; a valid court order generally supersedes a private contract. However, you can structure an internal triage workflow to minimize legal spend while ensuring every request receives necessary professional scrutiny.

**1. Immediate Triage (Internal Staff)**
Before engaging counsel, perform a strict administrative check to filter invalid requests. Reject or pause review for any request lacking:
*   **Valid Jurisdiction:** Does the issuing court have authority over your entity or the specific data?
*   **Proper Service:** Was the subpoena served according to the rules of the issuing jurisdiction (e.g., Hague Convention requirements for US requests served on German entities)?
*   **Scope Specificity:** Is the request overly broad ("all data for User X") versus specific ("access logs for Date Y")? Overly broad requests may be narrowed by counsel rather than fully challenged.

**2. The "Golden Rule" of Data Location**
Your protocol must hinge on **where the data physically resides** and **which entity controls it**.
*   **US Data / US Users:** Generally subject to US subpoenas if properly served.
*   **German Data / EU Users:** Subject to the GDPR and potentially the German Code of Criminal Procedure. A US subpoena often has no direct extraterritorial force over data stored in Germany without a Mutual Legal Assistance Treaty (MLAT) process.
*   **Action:** Tag every request by user location and data server location. Do not move German data to the US to comply with a US subpoena without legal counsel; this may violate GDPR transfer restrictions.

**3. Legal Review Trigger**
Do not attempt to interpret the validity of the subpoena yourself. Your $1,200/request cost suggests you are paying for full review on every ticket. To optimize:
*   **Batching:** If requests share similar legal questions (e.g., "Does the US CLOUD Act override German blocking statutes in this specific context?"), ask counsel to issue a standing memo for that scenario.
*   **Challenge vs. Comply Decision:** This is a legal determination, not a business one. Counsel must decide if a request violates local "blocking statutes" (laws that forbid complying with foreign orders) or if an MLAT is required.

### Jurisdictional Conflicts Requiring Immediate Attorney Attention

You face a direct conflict between US law enforcement powers and EU data sovereignty. You must engage an international data privacy attorney immediately to address these specific regimes:

1.  **GDPR (General Data Protection Regulation) vs. US Subpoenas:**
    *   **The Conflict:** GDPR Article 48 states that judgments or orders from non-EU countries are only recognizable/enforceable if based on an international agreement (like an MLAT). A direct US subpoena may not be a lawful basis for transfer under GDPR.
    *   **Risk:** Complying with a US subpoena without an MLAT could result in massive fines from German authorities for unlawful data transfer, even if you are penalized by the US court for non-compliance.

2.  **German Blocking Statutes & Sovereignty:**
    *   **The Conflict:** Germany has strict laws regarding data sovereignty. Transferring biometric data (special category data under GDPR Article 9) to US law enforcement without proper treaty channels may violate German criminal or administrative law.

3.  **The CLOUD Act (US) vs. Local Law:**
    *   **The Conflict:** The US CLOUD Act allows US authorities to demand data from US-based providers regardless of where the data is stored. However, the Act allows providers to challenge requests if compliance would violate the laws of a "qualifying foreign government." You need counsel to determine if Germany qualifies and how to file such a challenge.

**Next Step:** Do not process the next subpoena until counsel defines your "challenge threshold." Provide them with the 10 recent requests and your data architecture map (specifically showing where German biometric logs are stored) to assess the MLAT requirement.
