**Regime & Routing:**
This falls under **US Federal Tax Law** (Internal Revenue Code) regarding fixed asset capitalization and depreciation. Specifically, it involves the distinction between tangible personal property (often 5–7 year MACRS life) and internal-use software (often 36-month amortization), and how "bundled" systems are treated. The IRS has specific tests for whether software is "incidental" to hardware or a separate asset. Misclassification can lead to penalties, interest, or forced restatements.

**Action Required:** You must route this to a **Certified Public Accountant (CPA) or tax attorney** specializing in manufacturing or medical device taxation. Do not make this decision based on runway aesthetics or cash flow preferences alone; the tax code dictates the allowable life, not your balance sheet strategy.

**Implications Overview (Educational Only):**
*   **12-Year Schedule:** Matches the physical life. *Potential Upside:* Lower annual expense recognition, higher reported net income now. *Risk:* If the IRS deems the software component has a shorter life, you may be forced to accelerate depreciation later, creating a sudden expense spike (the write-off you fear) and potential penalties for prior under-depreciation.
*   **3-Year Schedule:** Matches the software cycle. *Potential Upside:* Faster cost recovery, reducing taxable income sooner (tax deferral benefit). *Risk:* If the IRS views the unit primarily as hardware, they may disallow the accelerated write-off, requiring you to pay back taxes + interest. This also reduces your book income, which may affect loan covenants or investor perceptions of runway.
