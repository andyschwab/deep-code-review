**The Regime and The Risk**
The core legal regime is the **Investment Advisers Act of 1940**. The SEC has issued guidance indicating that the provision of investment advice via software does not automatically exempt a firm from registration if the software provides specific recommendations based on individual client data. Because YieldPool manages $8.5 million for 150 clients and performs automated rebalancing (shifting funds to maximize returns), regulators may view this as providing "advice" or "management" rather than mere "publishing" of generic data. The distinction often hinges on whether the algorithm interacts with specific client portfolios to make tailored decisions, which your description suggests it does.

**Why This Cannot Be Solved With Arithmetic**
You asked for specific thresholds and compliance steps. There is no simple arithmetic threshold (like a revenue number) that guarantees safety. The test is functional:
1.  **Do you provide advice?** (Likely yes, via the algorithm).
2.  **Are you in the business of doing so?** (Yes, you charge a 1% fee).
3.  **Do you receive compensation for it?** (Yes).

If the answer to all three is yes, you are likely an Investment Advisor (IA) unless a specific exemption applies. The "software exemption" is narrow and often misunderstood; it generally applies to systems that provide generic information without tailoring to specific client needs. Your system tailors rebalancing to client assets, which creates significant regulatory risk.
