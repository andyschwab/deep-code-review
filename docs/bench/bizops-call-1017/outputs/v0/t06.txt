### Lane R: Regulation-Dependent Routing (Withholding Mechanics)
You cannot solve multi-state withholding via arithmetic alone because the correct answer depends on conflicting state laws, nexus rules, and the absence of a unified "gig worker" compact.

**The Regime:** You are operating under the withholding laws of California, New York, and Texas simultaneously, plus potential local city taxes (e.g., NYC). There is no single "interstate tax compact" that automatically pro-rates gig worker withholding across state lines for non-employees. The "nightmare" you describe stems from the fact that states generally assert taxing rights based on **source of income** (where the work was performed) or **residency**, and they do not share real-time data on gig movements.

**Unknown Variables & Risks:**
1.  **Sourcing Rules:** States differ on how to source "gig" income. Is it where the driver picked up the passenger, where they dropped them off, or where they were logged into the app? Some states use "days worked" apportionment; others require exact mileage tracking per jurisdiction.
2.  **Thresholds & Nexus:** Some states have de minimis thresholds (e.g., no withholding if < $X earned or < Y days worked), but these vary wildly and change annually.
3.  **Reciprocity:** While some states have reciprocity agreements for W-2 employees, these rarely apply cleanly to 1099-style gig contractors, leaving you exposed to double-withholding or under-withholding penalties.

**Actionable Routing Steps:**
*   **Retain:** A specialized payroll tax attorney or a CPA firm with a dedicated multi-state withholding practice. Do not use a generalist.
*   **Validate:** Your algorithm cannot be validated internally. It requires a formal opinion letter from counsel confirming your sourcing logic (e.g., "mileage-based apportionment") complies with current statutes in CA, NY, and TX.
