**Lane R: Regulation-Dependent (Route Immediately)**
Your question regarding whether selling to a foreign-owned utility triggers ITAR (International Traffic in Arms Regulations) or EAR (Export Administration Regulations) restrictions is a **Lane R** matter. The correct answer depends entirely on specific legal determinations regarding your software's classification, the origin of the underlying technology, and the specific ownership structure of the South Korean entity. **Do not sign the contract or provide access until a licensed export attorney completes the following compliance checks:**

1.  **Commodity Jurisdiction (CJ) Determination:** You must determine if your software is controlled under ITAR (US Munitions List) or EAR (Commerce Control List). Because your algorithms are derived from US Department of Energy (DOE) research, there is a high risk the technology is classified as "defense articles" or involves restricted nuclear technology (Part 810 of DOE regulations). If it is ITAR-controlled, providing access to a foreign national (even within the US) constitutes an "export" requiring a license.
2.  **Deemed Export Analysis:** Under US law, releasing controlled technology to a foreign national *within* the United States is "deemed" an export to that person's home country. Since your client is foreign-owned, their engineers accessing your software in the US may trigger deemed export rules. The ownership structure matters significantly here; if the foreign owners have access to the source code or technical data, a license may be required regardless of the physical plant location.
3.  **End-User and End-Use Screening:** You must screen the South Korean utility and its parent companies against the US Bureau of Industry and Security (BIS) "Entity List," the Department of Defense's "Section 1260H" list, and other denied-party lists. Nuclear energy applications often face strict scrutiny regarding proliferation concerns.
4.  **Technology Origin Audit:** Counsel must review the specific DOE research agreements. Many federal grants and contracts include "foreign access" clauses or require specific approvals before technology derived from them is shared with foreign entities, regardless of export control classification.

**Decision Aid:**
*   **The Rule:** If the software is ITAR-controlled or contains restricted DOE nuclear technology, providing access to foreign nationals without a license is a federal crime.
*   **The Flip Input:** The classification of your algorithm (ITAR vs. EAR vs. Public Domain) is the single input that flips this from "prohibited" to "potentially licensable."
*   **Threshold:** If you cannot definitively prove the technology is EAR99 (lowest restriction) or public domain based on an attorney's review, assume it is controlled.
*   **Next Step:** Halt all technical data sharing with the South Korean entity immediately. Engage an export control attorney to file a Commodity Jurisdiction request with the State Department or classify the item under the EAR before negotiating further.
