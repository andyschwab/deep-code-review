**Regime Identification & Engineering Leads**
The relevant regime is the **EEOC's enforcement of Title VII** regarding "disparate impact." Under the *Uniform Guidelines on Employee Selection Procedures (UGESP)*, if a selection procedure (your AI screen) has an adverse impact on a protected group, the employer (and potentially the vendor) bears the burden of demonstrating "job relatedness and consistency with business necessity."

While I cannot provide a legal verdict, I can outline the standard engineering obligations often cited in this regime that you should prepare for counsel review:
1.  **Adverse Impact Analysis (The "Four-Fifths Rule"):** Counsel will likely expect you to calculate the selection rate for each protected group. If the rate for any group is less than 80% (four-fifths) of the rate for the group with the highest rate, this creates a *prima facie* case of adverse impact. This is a statistical trigger, not a final legal verdict, but it is the primary screen used by regulators.
2.  **Validation Studies:** To defend against a disparate impact claim, you generally must produce a validation study showing the algorithm predicts actual job performance (criterion-related validity) or accurately measures essential job traits (content validity). Using "historical hiring data" is often insufficient if that history reflects past biases; counsel may advise that this data source itself is the liability.
3.  **Vendor Liability:** The fact that the government client makes the final decision does not automatically insulate you. Vendors can be held liable as "agents" of the employer or as joint employers depending on the degree of control and the nature of the service.

**Immediate Next Steps (Pre-Counsel)**
Before your legal consultation, perform the following arithmetic and data preparation (Lane A):
*   **Calculate Current Selection Rates:** Break down your 20,000 processed applications by demographic categories (where legally permissible to collect/track) and compute the pass-through rates. Apply the 80% threshold formula: `(Rate of Group X) / (Rate of Highest Group)`. Flag any ratio below 0.80.
*   **Document Data Lineage:** Prepare a clear map of your training data sources, specifically noting the time range (past decade) and any known demographic skews in the historical hiring decisions used for training.
*   **Do Not Retrain Yet:** Do not switch to synthetic data or retrain the model until counsel advises. Changing the model now could be seen as spoliation of evidence or an admission that the current model is defective.

**Decision Aid:**
If your internal calculation shows any group's pass rate is below 80% of the highest group, you have a high-probability regulatory trigger that requires immediate legal strategy, not just technical tweaking. The "flip input" here is the validation study: without a legally defensible study proving your tool measures job necessity rather than historical bias, the mathematical disparity creates significant exposure regardless of who clicks the final "hire" button.

**Note:** The EEOC has issued specific technical assistance documents on AI and employment (2023) which your counsel will reference.
**Note:** Synthetic data is an emerging mitigation strategy but has no established "safe harbor" status under current EEOC guidelines; its legal sufficiency is untested.
