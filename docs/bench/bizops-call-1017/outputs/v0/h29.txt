To prepare for a professional review, you should structure your data collection around the legal concept of "procedural fairness," which generally requires notice, an opportunity to be heard, and an impartial tribunal. In a VR context, counsel will likely examine whether your platform can prove these elements existed technically.

**1. Participant Verification and Identity Assurance**
Courts require certainty that the person participating is the authorized representative.
*   **Current Gap:** Standard login credentials may be insufficient for high-stakes arbitration.
*   **Data to Prepare:** Document your current identity verification flow. Can you prove the avatar present was controlled by the specific lawyer? Counsel may recommend multi-factor authentication, biometric verification within the headset, or a pre-session video identity check recorded outside the VR environment.

**2. Continuity of Presence and Connection Integrity**
A core due process right is the ability to hear and respond to evidence in real-time.
*   **Current Gap:** "Lag" could be argued as a denial of the opportunity to cross-examine.
*   **Data to Prepare:** You need immutable logs showing latency metrics per user, timestamped disconnects, and reconnection times.
*   **Actionable Step:** Implement a system that automatically pauses proceedings or flags the record if latency exceeds a threshold defined by your counsel (e.g., >200ms). The log must show that the tribunal was alerted to technical issues and made a ruling on whether to proceed.

**3. Interface Clarity and Consent**
Confusion over the interface could be argued as a lack of meaningful participation.
*   **Current Gap:** If a user cannot find the "submit evidence" button, was evidence improperly excluded?
*   **Data to Prepare:** Record the full session from a neutral observer's perspective, not just individual user views. Maintain a changelog of the interface version used.
*   **Actionable Step:** Develop a mandatory, recorded "competency check" before the hearing starts, where each participant demonstrates they can manipulate evidence and speak, signed digitally as part of the record.

**4. The Official Record**
Arbitration awards can be challenged if the record is incomplete.
*   **Current Gap:** Is the VR recording admissible? Does it capture spatial audio accurately?
*   **Data to Prepare:** Ensure your recording format is non-proprietary or widely viewable. A court cannot review a verdict if it requires specific VR hardware to understand what happened.
*   **Actionable Step:** Create a parallel, traditional 2D video/audio record as a backup evidentiary trail, as this is the current standard for court review.
