### Lane A: Unit Economics Analysis (Apply, Don't Direct)

Your current model shows a significant disconnect between acquisition cost (hardware) and realized value (completion). Below is the arithmetic applied to your provided numbers to test your hypotheses.

**1. Current State Snapshot**
*   **Revenue per Seat:** $200
*   **Variable Cost (Server):** $15 (assuming charged on active users, but fixed costs exist regardless)
*   **Hardware Cost (Sunk/Upfront):** $350 per user
*   **Completion Rate:** 40%
*   **Contribution Margin (per seat sold):** $200 - $15 = $185 (ignoring hardware amortization for cash flow, but note: you are losing $165 cash per user immediately on hardware alone if bought upfront).

**2. Scenario A: Lowering Price to $100**
*   **New Revenue:** $100
*   **Server Cost:** $15 per *active* user.
*   **The Math:** If completion stays at 40%, only 0.4 users are active per seat sold.
    *   Expected Server Cost per Seat Sold = $15 × 0.4 = $6.
    *   New Contribution Margin = $100 - $6 = $94.
*   **The Flip Input:** Does lowering the price increase completion?
    *   If completion remains 40%, you reduce revenue by 50% while hardware loss remains $350/user.
    *   **Break-even Logic:** To cover just the $350 hardware cost over one year at $100 price (ignoring other ops), a single seat must generate $350. At $100/year, that takes 3.5 years *per user*, assuming 100% retention and zero other costs.
    *   **Sensitivity:** If price drops to $100, completion must rise drastically to justify the hardware spend. At $100, you need ~87.5% completion just to match the *revenue volume* of the current 40% rate at $200 (assuming server costs scale linearly).

**3. Scenario B: Usage-Based Model**
*   **Structure:** Charge per hour/module completed rather than per seat.
*   **Application:** If a user completes 40% of the content, they pay 40% of the value.
*   **Risk:** This aligns price with value but caps your upside. If the average user only does 2 hours of training, and your cost to serve + amortized content is higher than that micro-transaction, you bleed cash.
*   **Formula to Apply:** `Price per Unit × Units per User > (Content Dev / Total Users) + Server Cost + Hardware Amortization`.
    *   Your Content Dev ($1.2M) / 300 clients = $4,000 per client needed to break even on dev alone.
    *   At $200/seat, you need 20 seats/client just to cover content dev, ignoring hardware and server costs.

**Decision Aid & Next Step**
*   **Read:** Your current LTV is likely negative when factoring in hardware. The "flip input" is **completion rate**; price changes alone rarely fix engagement.
*   **Threshold Rule of Thumb:** If LTV:CAC (including hardware as CAC) is < 1.0, the model is unsustainable without changing the cost structure (e.g., BYOD hardware).
*   **Action:** Before changing price, instrument *why* completion is 40%. Run a Van Westendorp price sensitivity survey on your *current* 300 clients asking: "At what price would the low completion rate make this an unacceptable expense?" Use their answers to set the new band. Do not drop to $100 unless data shows price is the specific barrier to completion.
