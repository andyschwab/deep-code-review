### Lane A: Applied Unit Economics & Modeling Structure

Your situation requires separating **cash flow** (money moving) from **revenue recognition** (accounting rules). The 24-month hold is a regulatory constraint on *when* you can book revenue, but your unit economics must reflect the *economic reality* of the delay.

#### 1. The Core Formulas (Apply to Your Numbers)

**A. Cash Burn vs. Accounting Loss**
You incur costs immediately but recognize revenue later. Your model needs two parallel tracks:
*   **Cash Track:** Tracks actual money in/out.
    *   *Input:* You collect $299/mo? Or does the funeral home collect and hold?
    *   *Assumption:* If you hold the funds in trust, you cannot use them for ops. If the funeral home holds them and pays you a fee later, your cash inflow is delayed.
    *   *Formula:* `Net Cash Burn = Operating Expenses - (Cash Collected from Fees)`
*   **Accrual Track (GAAP/Regulatory):** Tracks earned revenue.
    *   *Formula:* `Recognized Revenue per Contract = (Monthly Fee × Months Elapsed)`, capped at the 24-month release schedule.

**B. Adjusted LTV (Accounting for the 24-Month Gap)**
Standard LTV formulas fail here because they assume immediate revenue recognition. You must discount future revenue to present value (PV) to account for the opportunity cost of trapped capital.

*   **Standard LTV (Flawed here):** $299 / 0.02 = $14,950
*   **Adjusted LTV (With Delay):**
    1.  **Define the Revenue Stream:** You only recognize revenue after month 24.
    2.  **Apply Discount Rate (r):** Use your cost of capital (e.g., 10% annual or ~0.8% monthly) to reflect the opportunity cost.
    3.  **Formula:**
        $$LTV_{adjusted} = \sum_{t=25}^{\infty} \frac{Revenue_t}{(1+r)^t} \times (1 - Churn)^t$$
    *   *Application:* With 2% churn, the effective life is ~50 months. If revenue starts month 25, you are discounting those cash flows heavily.
    *   *Result:* Your *real* LTV is significantly lower than the naive $14,950 because the first 24 months of potential value are either zero (if you can't bill) or deferred (if you bill but can't recognize).

**C. True CAC Payback Period**
*   **Formula:** `Payback = CAC / (Monthly Contribution Margin)`
*   **The Trap:** If your "Monthly Contribution Margin" is $0 for the first 24 months due to revenue recognition rules, your payback period is mathematically infinite in an accounting sense.
*   **The Fix:** Model **Cash Payback** instead.
    *   *If you collect cash upfront but can't recognize it:* Your cash payback is fast, but your *reported* profitability lags.
    *   *If you cannot collect cash until month 24:* Your cash payback is 24 months + (CAC / Monthly Fee).
    *   *Your Numbers:* If you collect $299/mo but can't book it:
        *   Cash Payback = $1,200 / ($299 - Variable Costs).
        *   Accounting Payback = Delayed by 24 months.

#### 2. Decision Aid & Sensitivity

*   **One-Line Read:** Your accounting LTV is artificially suppressed by the 24-month deferral, but your *cash* viability depends entirely on whether you can collect fees *before* the recognition clock starts.
*   **The Flip Input:** The critical variable is **Fee Collection Timing**.
    *   *Scenario A (Collect Now, Recognize Later):* Cash flow is positive; runway is safe; investors see a "deferred revenue" liability.
    *   *Scenario B (Collect Later):* You fund 24 months of service per customer out of pocket. `Burn Rate = (6 staff × Salary) + (45 homes × Service Cost)`. At $150k cash, this is dangerous.
*   **Threshold Rule of Thumb:** If `Cash Payback > 12 months`, your current model is likely uninvestable without significant capital reserves.
*   **Next Step:** Map the exact cash flow timeline for one contract. If you service the home in Month 1 but get paid in Month 25, calculate the **Net Present Value (NPV)** of that single contract using a 10% discount rate. If NPV is negative, the unit economics do not work under current terms.

### Lane R: Regulatory & Securities Routing

**Regime Identified:**
1.  **Florida Pre-Need Funeral Contract Consumer Protection Act:** Governs the trust requirements and revenue recognition timing.
2.  **SEC Regulations (Regulation D / Securities Act of 1933):** Because your revenue recognition delay creates a capital intensive gap, if you plan to raise funds to bridge this cash flow, you are engaging in a **securities offering**.
3.  **GAAP (ASC 606):** Revenue Recognition standards for deferred income.

**Action Required:**
*   **Do not finalize your investor deck or financial model** without a **CPA** specializing in Florida pre-need regulations to validate your revenue recognition schedule. An error here constitutes financial misrepresentation.
*   If you raise capital to cover the 24-month cash gap, you must consult **securities counsel** to structure the raise (SAFE vs. Equity) and ensure compliance with exemption filings. Do not attempt to model "compliance" yourself.

**Note:** The distinction between "collecting cash" and "recognizing revenue" is the single most important factor in your runway calculation. Clarify this immediately.
