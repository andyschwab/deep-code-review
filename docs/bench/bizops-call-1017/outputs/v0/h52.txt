**Lane R: Regulation-Dependent (Route to Professional)**

Your questions regarding **sales tax**, **income tax classification**, and **taxable event triggers** for automated energy trading fall strictly under **Lane R**. These are not arithmetic problems but legal determinations dependent on the **Texas Tax Code**, **Texas Comptroller rules**, and potentially **Federal IRS guidance** on digital/automated transactions and energy markets.

**Why this must be routed:**
1.  **Taxable Event Determination:** Whether an automated software execution constitutes a taxable event for the platform (GridBalance) versus the building owner depends on how Texas law classifies your role (agent vs. principal) and the nature of the commodity (electricity) in a deregulated market. No arithmetic can resolve this; only a tax attorney or CPA licensed in Texas can interpret the statute.
2.  **Sales Tax Applicability:** Electricity sales tax rules in Texas are complex, involving exemptions for commercial use, resale certificates, and specific treatment of "balancing" services. There is no universal "threshold" where volume automatically changes classification; this depends on statutory definitions of "taxable service" vs. "sale of tangible personal property."
3.  **Income Tax Reporting:** The characterization of your 15% commission (service income vs. trading gain) and the reporting requirements for the building owners (Schedule C, Form 1099-K thresholds) are legal questions.

**Action Required:**
You must engage a **Texas-licensed CPA or tax attorney** specializing in **energy markets** or **SaaS/marketplace taxation**. Do not file next quarter's returns without this counsel, as misclassification of automated trades can lead to penalties.

**Preparation for Your Professional (Data to Track Now):**
While you cannot calculate the tax yourself, you can prepare the factual record your counsel will need. Ensure your system logs the following data points for every transaction:
*   **Transaction Timestamp:** Exact time of the automated trade.
*   **Parties Involved:** Unique ID of the building owner (seller) and the grid entity/buyer.
*   **Gross Transaction Value:** The total dollar amount of the energy sale before commission (e.g., the full $4,500 portion attributable to the specific trade).
*   **Commission Amount:** The exact 15% fee retained by GridBalance.
*   **Net Payout:** The amount remitted to the building owner.
*   **Location Data:** Specific meter location or ERCOT zone (tax jurisdictions can vary by locality in Texas).
*   **Volume:** Megawatt-hours (MWh) traded per transaction.

**Note on Thresholds:**
Do not assume any specific volume threshold triggers a change in classification; this is a common misconception. Your counsel must verify if current federal (e.g., 1099-K) or Texas state thresholds apply to your specific business model.
