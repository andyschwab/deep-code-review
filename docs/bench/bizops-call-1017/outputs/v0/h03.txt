**Lane R: Regulation-Dependent Routing**
Your question regarding whether to write down inventory monthly or carry it at cost under "current accounting standards" is a **Lane R** matter. The correct answer depends entirely on the specific accounting regime you operate under (e.g., US GAAP ASC 330/ASC 450 or IFRS IAS 2) and the legal interpretation of "net realizable value" versus "impairment" in the context of regulatory delays.

*   **The Regime:** Inventory valuation and impairment testing are governed by strict statutory accounting standards (GAAP/IFRS) and enforced by bodies like the SEC (for public companies) or state boards of accountancy.
*   **The Risk:** Concluding that you can "carry at cost" without a formal impairment test could constitute a material misstatement if the delay indicates the inventory is obsolete or unsellable. Conversely, unnecessary write-downs could artificially depress your balance sheet ahead of a Series B.
*   **The Route:** You must engage a CPA firm with experience in life sciences or manufacturing inventory. They will determine if the 12–18 month delay triggers an "other-than-temporary" impairment or if the asset remains viable at cost based on your specific client contracts.

**Lane A: Arithmetic Structure (Apply Your Own Numbers)**
While the *decision* to impair is legal/accounting (Lane R), the *calculation* of that impairment, once the methodology is selected by your CPA, is arithmetic you can prepare.

If your advisor determines an impairment is necessary based on "Net Realizable Value" (NRV), the formula is:
`Impairment Loss = Carrying Cost − Estimated Net Realizable Value`

Where:
*   **Carrying Cost:** Your given $85,000 per batch (or $2.1M total).
*   **Estimated NRV:** (Expected Final Sale Price) − (Costs to Complete/Sell).

*Application to your data:*
You have $2.1M tied up. If your CPA determines the delay reduces the probability of sale or the final price, you must input those estimates.
*   *Scenario A (Full Recovery):* If contracts are firm and price is fixed at $150k/batch regardless of time, NRV > Cost. Impairment = $0.
*   *Scenario B (Partial Recovery):* If delays trigger penalties reducing the final price to $70k/batch, the math is: $85,000 − $70,000 = $15,000 loss per batch.
