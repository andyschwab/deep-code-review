### Lane A: Unit Economics & Weather Variance (Applied Math)

Your core challenge is separating fixed costs from variable revenue under volatility. Here is the arithmetic applied to your inputs:

**1. Contribution Margin per Dive**
*   **Formula:** `Price − Variable Cost`
*   **Your Numbers:** $15,000 − $4,000 = **$11,000 per dive**.
*   *Note:* This assumes technician labor is part of your $120k fixed cost. If labor is paid per dive, subtract it here.

**2. Break-Even Volume (Base Case)**
*   **Formula:** `Fixed Costs ÷ Contribution Margin`
*   **Your Numbers:** $120,000 ÷ $11,000 ≈ **11 dives/month**.
*   *Interpretation:* You must complete 11 dives just to cover fixed costs. Any dive after #12 generates profit.

**3. Weather Stress Test (The 40% Cut)**
*   **Target Volume:** 20 dives/rig.
*   **Severe Weather Scenario:** 20 × (1 − 0.40) = **12 billable dives**.
*   **Result:** 12 dives − 11 break-even dives = **1 dive of buffer**.
*   *Risk:* A single additional cancellation pushes you below break-even. Your current model has almost no safety margin for weather.

**4. Decision Aid & Thresholds**
*   **The Flip Input:** The variable that changes your outcome most is **Billable Volume**.
*   **Rule of Thumb:** If your expected weather-adjusted volume drops below 1.2× your break-even point (here, <13 dives), the risk profile is critical.
*   **Next Step:** Calculate the "Weather-Adjusted Price." If you historically lose 40% of dives, your *effective* price per *scheduled* slot is $15,000 × 0.60 = $9,000. Since $9,000 < $11,000 (your margin needed if costs are fixed per scheduled day), you are currently losing money on weather days if you bear the full risk.

**Contract Structuring Options (Arithmetic Only):**
*   **Option A (Retainer):** Charge a fixed monthly fee covering the 11-dive break-even regardless of weather. Variable fees apply to dives 12–20.
    *   *Math:* Retainer ≥ $120,000.
*   **Option B (Weather Clause):** Define "Force Majeure" strictly. If weather cancels a dive, the client pays a reduced "mobilization fee" (e.g., $2,000) to cover partial variable costs, or the contract guarantees a minimum number of *paid* slots (e.g., 15) regardless of completion.
*   **Option C (Dynamic Pricing):** Increase the per-dive price to subsidize weather days.
    *   *Formula:* `Required Price = (Fixed Costs + (Expected Lost Dives × Variable Cost)) ÷ Expected Billable Dives`.
    *   *Application:* If you expect only 12 dives but must cover costs for 20 scheduled attempts, the math shifts drastically.
