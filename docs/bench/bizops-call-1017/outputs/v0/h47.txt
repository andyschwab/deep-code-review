### Lane A: Unit Economics Analysis (Applied to Your Numbers)

Based on the figures you provided, here is the arithmetic regarding your current pricing model and fixed costs.

**The Break-Even Formula**
To determine the volume required to cover the fixed audit cost per client event, we use:
$$ \text{Break-Even Votes} = \frac{\text{Fixed Audit Cost}}{\text{Price Per Vote}} $$

**Applying Your Inputs:**
*   Fixed Audit Cost: $80,000
*   Price Per Vote: $2.00
*   Calculation: $80,000 / $2.00 = **40,000 votes**

**Current Scenario Analysis:**
*   **Typical Client (30,000 shareholders):**
    *   Revenue: 30,000 × $2.00 = $60,000
    *   Less Audit Cost: $80,000
    *   **Net Result:** -$20,000 (Loss per client/event before other operating expenses).
*   **Enterprise Threshold (100,000 shareholders):**
    *   Revenue: 100,000 × $2.00 = $200,000
    *   Less Audit Cost: $80,000
    *   **Gross Margin:** $120,000 (before other variable costs).

**Sensitivity & Decision Aid:**
Under the strict assumption that *every* client triggers a *fresh, full-scope* $80k audit, your model is mathematically negative for any client with fewer than 40,000 voters.
*   **Rule of Thumb:** If your Customer Acquisition Cost (CAC) and variable server costs are significant, your true break-even point is higher than 40,000 votes.
*   **The Flip Input:** The variable that flips this answer is the **allocation of the audit cost**. If the audit is a platform-wide requirement rather than a per-client requirement, the unit economics change entirely.
