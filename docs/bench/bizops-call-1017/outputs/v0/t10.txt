### Lane R: Liability, Compliance, and Regulatory Regimes

You asked who bears liability for AI-suggested upcoding and what safeguards are required. **This is a Lane R question.** The answer depends on specific interpretations of federal fraud statutes, state medical practice acts, and the terms of your commercial contracts. I cannot tell you who is liable or what specific safeguards will protect you; only qualified counsel can make that determination.

**The Regimes Involved:**
1.  **Federal False Claims Act (FCA):** This is the primary regime for billing errors. Liability often hinges on "scienter" (knowledge of falsity). If your AI systematically suggests higher codes, regulators may argue your company aided and abetted false claims.
2.  **Anti-Kickback Statute (AKS):** If your pricing or arrangement with clinics is viewed as inducing referrals or generating unnecessary costs for federal programs, it could trigger AKS scrutiny.
3.  **State Medical Practice Acts:** These laws define who can practice medicine. If your AI is deemed to be "practicing medicine" by making final coding decisions without physician review, your company could be accused of unauthorized practice of medicine (UPM).
4.  **HIPAA & HITECH:** As a business associate handling PHI, you have direct liability for data security and certain compliance failures.

**The Unknown Precedents:**
There is no settled case law specifically defining liability for *algorithmic* upcoding where the physician signs off. The legal theory could shift between:
*   **Vendor Liability:** If the software is defective or marketed as "automating compliance."
*   **Provider Liability:** If the physician is legally required to review and attest to the code before submission (the typical standard).
*   **Shared Liability:** If the vendor knowingly trains models on biased data to maximize reimbursement.
