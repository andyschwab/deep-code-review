### Lane A: Arithmetic & Pricing Structure Application

Your concern hinges on unit economics and willingness-to-pay (WTP), not a fixed rule about university budgets. Below is the math applied to your provided numbers, followed by alternative model structures.

**1. Current Model Sensitivity (University-Pays)**
*   **Formula:** `Monthly Revenue = Verification Volume × Price Per Request`
*   **Your Inputs:** 3,000 requests × $2 = **$6,000/month**.
*   **Adoption Risk Variable:** If the $2 fee causes universities to block requests, volume ($V$) drops.
    *   *Scenario A (No friction):* 3,000 requests → $6,000 revenue.
    *   *Scenario B (50% friction):* 1,500 requests → $3,000 revenue.
    *   *Break-even Question:* What is your monthly burn? If burn is $10k, you need 5,000 requests at $2 each. If the $2 fee caps volume below 5,000, the model fails.
*   **Decision Aid:** The "flip input" here is the **price elasticity of demand** for the registrars. If a $2 charge reduces request volume by more than your cost savings, the net value decreases.

**2. Alternative Monetization Structures (Apply to Your WTP Data)**
You must determine who captures the most value from a verified credential: the student (higher salary/speed), the employer (risk reduction), or the university (reputation/admin efficiency).

*   **Option A: Employer-Pays (Marketplace Model)**
    *   *Logic:* Employers pay for risk reduction and speed. Universities join for free to attract students.
    *   *Math:* `Revenue = Employers × Avg Verifications/Mo × Fee`.
    *   *Trade-off:* High friction if employers can verify via other means; high value if instant verification is critical.
*   **Option B: Student-Pays (Freemium/Unlock)**
    *   *Logic:* Universities onboard for free. Students get basic verification free; pay $5–$10 for "instant" or "premium" sharing packs.
    *   *Math:* `Revenue = Paying Students × Fee`.
    *   *Trade-off:* Requires high student conversion rates.
*   **Option C: Tiered Subscription (SaaS)**
    *   *Logic:* Charge universities a flat monthly fee (e.g., $500/mo) for unlimited uploads, charging only for excess API calls or premium features.
    *   *Math:* `Revenue = (Universities × Flat Fee) + (Overage × Usage Fee)`.
    *   *Trade-off:* Predictable revenue for you; predictable cost for them.

**3. Recommended Next Step (This Week)**
Do not guess the budget constraints. Run a **Van Westendorp Price Sensitivity Survey** with your 5 current university partners and 10 target employers.
*   Ask: "At what price is this service too expensive to use?" and "At what price is it a bargain?"
*   Use the resulting price band to set your fee, rather than assuming $2 is the barrier.

### Lane R: Regulatory & Compliance Routing

Your operation involves cross-border data (UK/Germany) and potentially sensitive personal data (academic records).

*   **Regime Identified:** **GDPR** (EU) and **UK GDPR/Data Protection Act 2018**.
*   **Routing Required:** Before finalizing who pays (student vs. employer vs. university), you must consult **data privacy counsel**.
    *   *Reason:* Changing the payer often changes the "data controller" vs. "data processor" relationship and the legal basis for processing (e.g., legitimate interest vs. consent).
    *   *Action:* Do not implement a student-pay model without confirming it doesn't violate university data-sharing agreements or create unlawful "pay-to-verify" barriers under local education laws.

**Summary Decision Aid:**
If `LTV (Lifetime Value of a University) < CAC (Cost to onboard them)`, your current model is unsustainable. Use the survey to find the maximum price the *value-capturer* (likely the employer) will pay, then structure the contract to keep university friction near zero.
