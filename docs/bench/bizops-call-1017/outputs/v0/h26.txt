**Lane R: Regulation-Dependent Analysis (Route to Professional)**

Your question regarding whether location (public sidewalk vs. private property) changes liability exposure, premium costs, and the specific type of policy required (general liability vs. auto liability vs. specialized robotics coverage) is a **Lane R** matter. The correct answer depends on:
1.  **Florida Statutes:** Specific state laws governing autonomous vehicle operation on public rights-of-way and premises liability.
2.  **Policy Language:** The specific exclusions in your current General Liability (GL) policy regarding "automated machinery," "vehicles," and operations on public ways.
3.  **Regulatory Classification:** Whether Florida regulators or insurers classify your robots as "vehicles" (triggering auto liability requirements) or "equipment" (potentially covered under GL, often with exclusions).

**Action Required:** You must immediately engage a commercial insurance broker specializing in robotics or autonomous systems and legal counsel. Do not rely on general estimates.

**Preparation for Your Professional Consultation:**
To get an accurate risk profile and quote, prepare the following for your broker/attorney:
*   **Incident Report:** Details of the recent pedestrian nudge (location, robot sensor data, injury severity).
*   **Operational Map:** Exact definitions of where robots operate (private lots only, public sidewalks, street crossings).
*   **Current Policy:** The full text of your current General Liability policy, specifically the "Exclusions" section regarding vehicles and automated systems.
*   **Fleet Data:** Number of units (150), hours of operation, and safety mitigation features (emergency stop, obstacle detection logs).

**Why This Cannot Be Calculated Here:**
Insurers do not use a public formula to calculate premiums for autonomous fleets in mixed spaces. Rates are determined by underwriting guidelines that assess the *legal* liability of operating on public rights-of-way versus private property. In many jurisdictions, operating on a public sidewalk triggers a higher duty of care and potentially different statutory liabilities than on private land. Furthermore, standard GL policies often explicitly exclude liability arising from the use of "autos" or machinery operated on public roads; if your robots are legally classified as autos, you may have zero coverage for the sidewalk incident under your current policy. Only a licensed professional can interpret your policy language against Florida law to determine if you are currently exposed to uninsured losses.

**Immediate Risk Mitigation (Non-Legal):**
Until you have confirmed coverage:
1.  Consider pausing robot operations on public sidewalks if legally permissible, restricting them to private property only.
2.  Document all safety protocols and incident responses meticulously.
3.  Do not assume the $99/month revenue model covers potential legal defense costs; a single lawsuit exceeding your limits could threaten the $500k projected revenue.
