### Lane R: Regulation & Compliance (Route)

**Regime Identified:** US GAAP (ASC 606) Revenue Recognition & FDA Regulatory Constraints.

**Routing Required:**
You asked which accounting rules require a CPA's input versus standard practice. **This entire determination is Lane R.**
*   **Why Route:** Recognizing revenue on "delivered hardware" while "software service" is delayed involves complex **ASC 606 (Revenue from Contracts with Customers)** analysis regarding "distinct performance obligations."
*   **The Risk:** If the hardware and monitoring service are deemed a single bundled performance obligation (common in IoT/medical devices where hardware has limited utility without the service), you may be **prohibited** from recognizing the $150,000 upfront, even if cash is received. It would be recorded as **Deferred Revenue** (a liability), not income, and cannot be used to justify "revenue recognition" for investor reporting in the way you might hope.
*   **FDA Interaction:** Shipping hardware without final 510(k) clearance may violate FDA regulations, potentially turning "inventory" into "non-compliant product," affecting its valuation on the balance sheet.

**Immediate Professional Action:**
Do not book the $150,000 as revenue or rely on it for solvency modeling until a **CPA** confirms:
1.  Whether the hardware and service are distinct under ASC 606.
2.  Whether partial payment triggers revenue recognition or deferred revenue status.
3.  The implications of shipping pre-clearance hardware on financial statement audits.
