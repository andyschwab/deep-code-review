**Lane R: Regulation-Dependent (Route to Professional)**

Your question regarding whether holding client funds in interest-bearing accounts constitutes "taking deposits" requiring a banking charter, trust license, or Electronic Money Institution (EMI) authorization under UK law is a **Lane R** matter. The correct answer depends entirely on specific determinations under the **Financial Services and Markets Act 2000 (FSMA)** and the interpretation of the **Financial Conduct Authority (FCA)** and the **Prudential Regulation Authority (PRA)**.

**The Regime:**
You are operating at the boundary of **client money rules (CASS)** versus **accepting deposits**.
1.  **Accepting Deposits:** Under the FSMA, accepting deposits is a regulated activity. If your arrangement is deemed to be taking deposits (especially if you are using the principal or the interest for your own operational costs, as hinted by "offset our operational costs"), you likely require full banking authorization or a specific deposit-taking license. Doing this without authorization is a criminal offense.
2.  **Electronic Money:** If the funds are stored as electronic value to make payments, you may fall under the **Electronic Money Regulations 2011**, requiring EMI authorization.
3.  **Trust/Escrow:** While pure escrow arrangements can sometimes be exempt, the moment you commingle funds, earn interest for *your* benefit (rather than strictly for the client or as a disclosed fee), or hold funds for indefinite periods, the regulatory characterization often shifts from "holding" to "deposit-taking."

**Why I Cannot Answer the Threshold:**
There is no simple arithmetic threshold (e.g., "under £X million is safe") that I can provide. The determination relies on the specific legal structure of your accounts, the terms of your user agreements, the duration of holding, and the precise use of the interest generated. The FCA assesses these on a case-by-case basis. A confident wrong answer here could lead to enforcement action, fines, or closure of your business.
