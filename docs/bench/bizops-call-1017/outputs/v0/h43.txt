**Why This Cannot Be Answered Here**
You asked if the absence of a human co-pilot invalidates standard bonds or changes the bond type. A bond is a financial guarantee ensuring compliance with laws; however, the *eligibility* to enter a jurisdiction with a specific vehicle class (autonomous) is a separate legal status. If current regulations implicitly or explicitly require a licensed human operator to assume liability upon entry, no amount of bonding may cure the defect until the regulation is amended or a specific waiver is granted. Conversely, if the "importer of record" can be a legal entity regardless of the driver's presence, the bond structure may remain standard. Only a qualified trade attorney can interpret whether existing statutes accommodate driverless commercial entry or if this constitutes a regulatory gap requiring new rulemaking.

Furthermore, the concern that "customs agents may refuse entry" is an operational risk based on legal interpretation. CBP and Mexican Aduana officers enforce current statutes; if the statute is silent on autonomous vehicles, enforcement discretion varies by port and agent. This creates a high risk of detention or refusal that cannot be mitigated by math alone.
