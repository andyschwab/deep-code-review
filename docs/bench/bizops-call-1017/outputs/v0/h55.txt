### Lane R: Regulatory Analysis (Route Immediately)

Your specific question regarding whether your revenue model constitutes the unauthorized practice of law (UPL) or improper fee-sharing is a **Lane R** issue. It depends entirely on the interpretation of the **Colorado Rules of Professional Conduct**, specifically rules governing fee sharing with non-lawyers and contingency fees.

**The Regime:**
In the United States, and specifically in Colorado, **Rule 5.4 of the Rules of Professional Conduct** generally prohibits lawyers from sharing legal fees with non-lawyers. Furthermore, **Rule 5.5** prohibits aiding in the unauthorized practice of law.

**The Risk Factors:**
Your current model (2% of the settlement amount) presents significant red flags under these regimes:
1.  **Fee Sharing:** Charging a percentage of the recovery looks indistinguishable from a contingency fee, which is typically reserved strictly for licensed attorneys who assume the risk of loss and maintain professional independence.
2.  **Unauthorized Practice of Law:** If your bonus is contingent on the *outcome* of the case, regulators may argue your software is not just a tool but an active participant in the legal representation, effectively practicing law without a license.
3.  **Independence:** Ethical rules require attorneys to maintain independent professional judgment. A vendor financially incentivized by a specific settlement amount could be seen as compromising that judgment.

**Action Required:**
Do not calculate further bonuses or sign new contracts under this model until a Colorado ethics counsel reviews your specific arrangement. They will determine if the "performance bonus" is viewed as a prohibited division of fees or a permissible success-based service fee (which is a narrow and fact-specific distinction).

### Lane A: Alternative Pricing Structures (Arithmetic & Application)

While the legal permissibility is for counsel to decide, we can apply arithmetic to structure alternative models that remove the "percentage of settlement" variable, thereby reducing the ethical risk profile. These models shift the value metric from "outcome" to "input" or "fixed success criteria."

**1. Tiered Usage-Based Model (Volume/Complexity)**
Instead of a % of settlement, tie the bonus to the volume of data processed or the complexity of the search, which correlates to value but is not legally contingent on the verdict.
*   *Formula:* `Monthly Fee = Base ($500) + (Documents_Processed × Rate_per_Doc)`
*   *Application:* If a case involves 10,000 documents and you charge $0.05/doc, the bonus is $500. This is a standard service fee, not a legal fee share.

**2. Fixed Success Fee (Non-Percentage)**
Some jurisdictions may allow a fixed bonus for a successful outcome, provided it is not a percentage of the recovery.
*   *Formula:* `Total Fee = Base ($500 × Months) + Fixed_Bonus_Amount`
*   *Application:* Instead of 2% of $1.5M ($30k), you charge a flat $5,000 bonus upon case closure if the client prevails.
*   *Note:* Counsel must still verify if *any* outcome-contingent fee is permissible for non-lawyers in Colorado.

**3. Value-Anchored Tiering (Good-Better-Best)**
Structure pricing based on features that correlate with high-stakes cases without referencing the settlement amount.
*   *Structure:*
    *   **Standard:** $500/mo (Basic search).
    *   **Litigation Pro:** $1,500/mo (Advanced analytics, priority support, unlimited docs).
*   *Application:* High-stakes firms naturally select the higher tier. Your revenue scales with the firm's capacity to handle big cases, not the specific verdict amount.

**Decision Aid:**
Your current model creates a direct mathematical link between your revenue and the legal judgment (`Revenue = 0.02 × Settlement`), which is the primary trigger for ethical violations. To proceed safely, you must decouple this formula. The "Fixed Success Fee" or "Usage-Based" models allow you to capture upside value while changing the legal characterization of the fee from "sharing" to "service payment."
