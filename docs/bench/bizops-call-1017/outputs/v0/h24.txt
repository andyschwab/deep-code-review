**Lane R: Regulation-Dependent Routing (Primary Concern)**
Your question regarding the Anti-Kickback Statute (AKS), safe harbors, and whether your pricing model constitutes an illegal inducement is a **Lane R** matter. It depends entirely on federal law (42 U.S.C. § 1320a-7b), specific Office of Inspector General (OIG) guidance, and the interpretation of "fair market value" (FMV) in the context of value-based care arrangements.

*   **The Regime:** The Federal Anti-Kickback Statute prohibits offering anything of value to induce referrals for services payable by federal healthcare programs (Medicare/Medicaid).
*   **The Risk:** Because your software reduces readmissions and directly impacts hospital bonuses/penalties under value-based care models, there is a non-trivial risk that a regulator could view your pricing (or any shared savings arrangement) as an inducement.
*   **The Route:** You must engage a healthcare attorney specializing in fraud and abuse. Do not attempt to self-determine if you fit into the "Technology Safe Harbor" or the newer "Value-Based Enterprises Safe Harbor." These require strict adherence to specific criteria (e.g., written agreements, term limits, no determination of referral volume based on the arrangement) that only counsel can verify against your specific term sheets.
*   **Preparation for Counsel:** Before your meeting, gather: (1) Your current draft contracts; (2) Documentation of how your $150/bed price was calculated (cost-plus analysis); (3) Any marketing materials mentioning "savings" or "bonuses"; and (4) A list of all current customers paying different rates. This is time-sensitive given your $800k runway; a compliance failure could void contracts and exhaust capital.

While counsel determines the *legality* of your model, we can apply arithmetic to your current *structure* to ensure your internal unit economics are clear for those discussions.

*   **Current Monthly Recurring Revenue (MRR):**

    *   Calculation: `$150 × 300 = $45,000/month`
*   **Annual Contract Value (ACV):**

    *   Calculation: `$45,000 × 12 = $540,000/year`
