**Lane R: Regulatory Interpretation (Route to Counsel)**
Your core question—whether a state-mandated per-page cap on "certified transcripts" extends to a bundled package containing real-time streaming and immediate certification—is a legal determination that cannot be solved via arithmetic.
*   **The Regime:** California Business and Professions Code and relevant Judicial Council rules governing court reporter fees.
*   **The Risk:** If a regulator determines your "premium tier" is effectively a method to circumvent the $4.25/page cap on the transcription component, you could face penalties regardless of how you label the bundle.
*   **Action Required:** You must consult a California attorney specializing in professional licensing or court reporting regulations. Do not launch the premium tier until counsel confirms whether the cap applies to the *transaction* (the whole bundle) or strictly to the *line item* (the transcript pages).
*   **Preparation for Counsel:** Bring your proposed bundle structure, the specific wording of the current statute regarding "certified transcripts," and your revenue breakdown showing the split between appearance fees and per-page charges.

**Lane A: Arithmetic & Fee Structure Options (Apply Your Numbers)**
While legal counsel defines the *permissibility* of the bundle, you can model the *mathematics* of different structures using your current data ($175/hr avg appearance, 15% commission, $4.25/page cap).

**Option 1: Unbundled Pricing (Lowest Regulatory Risk)**
Keep the regulated transcript separate from unregulated services.
*   **Structure:** Charge the statutory max ($4.25/page) for the transcript. Charge a separate, market-rate fee for "Real-Time Streaming" and "Expedited Certification" as distinct line items.
*   **Math:** If a deposition generates 100 pages and lasts 2 hours:
    *   Transcript: 100 pages × $4.25 = $425 (Cap applies here).
    *   Appearance: 2 hours × $175 = $350.
    *   Premium Add-ons: $X (Your variable).
    *   Total Bill: $775 + $X.
    *   DepoTech Revenue: 15% × ($775 + $X).
*   **Assumption:** The regulator views streaming and expedited service as separate from the "certified transcript" product.

**Option 2: Bundled Pricing (High Regulatory Risk – Requires Legal Approval)**
If counsel advises that bundling is permitted provided the *transcript portion* does not exceed the cap:
*   **Structure:** Set a flat "Premium Package" price. You must internally allocate a portion of that price to the per-page count to ensure it does not exceed $4.25/page.
*   **Formula:** `Max Transcript Revenue = Pages × $4.25`.
*   **Constraint:** `Total Package Price - (Pages × $4.25) = Revenue available for Streaming/Appearance`.
*   **Sensitivity:** If your package price is $1,000 for 100 pages, $425 is allocated to transcripts. The remaining $575 covers appearance and streaming. If the package price rises, the allocation to the transcript must remain fixed at or below $4.25/page to remain compliant under this hypothesis.

**Decision Aid**
*   **Read:** Unbundling isolates the regulated variable, making compliance arithmetic simple; bundling conflates them, requiring legal validation of the allocation method.
*   **Flip Input:** The definition of "certified transcript" under CA law. If it includes "immediate certification" or "real-time delivery," bundling may be impossible.
*   **Threshold:** If legal counsel cannot confirm that add-on services are exempt from the cap within 2 weeks, default to Option 1 (Unbundled) to avoid penalty risk.
*   **Next Step:** Draft the two invoice examples above (Unbundled vs. Bundled with internal allocation) and send them to a CA licensing attorney with the question: "Does line-item separation satisfy the statutory cap?"

**Note:** The statute explicitly states the $4.25 cap for certified transcripts; it likely does not explicitly mention "real-time streaming bundles," creating the ambiguity requiring legal interpretation. Do not assume the cap excludes new technologies without written confirmation.
