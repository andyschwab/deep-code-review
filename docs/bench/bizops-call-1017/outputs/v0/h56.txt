### Unit Economics Application (Lane A)

**Inputs Provided:**
*   Selling Price ($P$): $25,000
*   Variable Cost of Goods Sold ($COGS$): $1,200 (hardware only)
*   *Note: You have not provided variable software costs (e.g., cloud hosting per simulation, support hours per unit). These are treated as $0 for this calculation but must be added for accuracy.*
*   Fixed R&D Spend: $800,000 (sunk cost for this specific unit calculation, relevant for break-even).

**1. Gross Margin per Unit**
*   **Formula:** $Gross\ Margin = P - COGS$
*   **Calculation:** $25,000 - $1,200 = $23,800
*   **Gross Margin %:** $(23,800 / 25,000) \times 100 = 95.2\%$
*   **Observation:** This margin is exceptionally high, assuming no additional variable software delivery costs. If there are variable costs (e.g., $500/unit for cloud compute), the margin drops to $23,300 (93.2%).

**2. Contribution Margin**
*   **Definition:** Revenue remaining after variable costs to cover fixed costs (like your $800k R&D and salaries).
*   **Current Figure:** $23,800 per unit (assuming zero other variable costs).

**3. Break-Even Analysis (Units to recover R&D)**
*   **Formula:** $Break\text{-}Even\ Units = Fixed\ Costs \div Contribution\ Margin\ per\ Unit$
*   **Calculation:** $800,000 \div 23,800 \approx 33.6$ units.
*   **Read:** You must sell approximately **34 units** to recover the specific $800,000 R&D spend cited, ignoring other operating expenses (salaries, rent, marketing).

**4. Sensitivity Check**
*   If variable software/support costs are actually $2,000 per unit:
    *   New Contribution: $25,000 - ($1,200 + $2,000) = $21,800.
    *   New Break-Even: $800,000 \div 21,800 \approx 37$ units.
*   **Decision Aid:** The "flip input" here is the **variable cost of software delivery**. If this exceeds $23,800, you lose money on every unit sold.
