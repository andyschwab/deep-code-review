### Lane R: Regulation & Liability (Route Immediately)
You are processing sensitive Personally Identifiable Information (PII)—Social Security Numbers (US) and National IDs (UK)—in an automated system across two distinct jurisdictions. This triggers specific legal regimes:
*   **United States (New York):** The **SHIELD Act** (Stop Hacks and Improve Electronic Data Security) and potentially **GDPR** if any data subjects are EU residents. New York has strict notification requirements for breaches involving SSNs.
*   **United Kingdom:** The **UK GDPR** and **Data Protection Act 2018**. Processing national ID data is considered "high risk" and often requires a Data Protection Impact Assessment (DPIA).
*   **Action Required:** Do **not** rely on your current Terms of Service. You must engage qualified legal counsel in both NY and the UK immediately to:
    1.  Determine if your current collection of this data is lawful or if you are inadvertently violating data minimization principles.
    2.  Draft specific data handling protocols and update your privacy policy.
    3.  Advise on breach notification timelines (often as short as 72 hours under UK GDPR).
    4.  Confirm whether a DPIA is mandatory before you continue processing.

**Operational Triage:** Until counsel advises, the safest operational stance to mitigate catastrophic liability is to halt processing of documents containing detected PII rather than storing them unredacted. "Angering clients" is a business risk; regulatory fines and loss of trust from a breach are existential risks.
