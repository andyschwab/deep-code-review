### Lane R: Legal & Liability (Route Immediately)
Your question regarding "how other vendors handle financial responsibility for regulatory fines" and whether to offer an "indemnity clause" falls strictly under **Lane R**.
*   **The Regime:** Banking regulations (e.g., FFIEC guidelines, state credit union laws) and contract law govern who bears the risk of regulatory fines. In many jurisdictions, a vendor cannot legally indemnify a regulated entity for fines caused by the entity's own failure to maintain adequate oversight, regardless of the vendor's algorithmic error.
*   **The Action:** Do not draft or promise an indemnity clause based on general market trends. You must engage a lawyer to:
    1.  Determine if indemnifying a credit union for *regulatory fines* is enforceable in your specific operating states.
    2.  Review your Errors & Omissions (E&O) and Cyber Liability insurance policies to see if they cover "algorithmic false negatives" leading to regulatory penalties.
    3.  Structure the limitation of liability cap in your Master Services Agreement (MSA).

### Lane A: Unit Economics & Pricing Structure (Apply Your Numbers)
While the legal structure is for counsel, the arithmetic of your risk exposure and pricing sensitivity can be modeled now.

**1. Current Exposure & Revenue Baseline**
*   **Monthly Volume:** 5,000,000 transactions
*   **Current Price:** $0.05/transaction
*   **Monthly Revenue:** $250,000 ($3M annualized)
*   **Fine Event Cost:** $25,000 per false negative incident.

**2. The "Reserve Fund" Math (Break-Even Analysis)**
To self-insure via a price increase, you must estimate your False Negative Rate (FNR). Since you did not provide an FNR, we treat it as a variable $X$ (incidents per month).
*   *Formula:* $\text{Required Price Increase} = (\text{Avg Fine} \times \text{Expected Monthly Incidents}) / \text{Total Transactions}$
*   *Scenario A (Low Risk):* If $X = 0.2$ (1 incident every 5 months):
    *   Monthly Cost = $25,000 \times 0.2 = $5,000.
    *   Price Increase needed = $5,000 / 5,000,000 = **$0.001 per transaction**.
    *   New Price: $0.051.
*   *Scenario B (High Risk):* If $X = 2.0$ (2 incidents per month):
    *   Monthly Cost = $25,000 \times 2 = $50,000.
    *   Price Increase needed = $50,000 / 5,000,000 = **$0.01 per transaction**.
    *   New Price: $0.06.

**3. Pricing Method Application**
Do not use cost-plus (adding the reserve to $0.05) as your primary strategy if it makes you uncompetitive. Instead, apply **Value-Based Pricing** anchored to the cost of the alternative (the fine).
*   **Value Metric:** The price should reflect a fraction of the $25,000 risk mitigated, not just the compute cost.
*   **Packaging Model:** Consider shifting from pure usage ($0.05/tx) to a **Good-Better-Best tier**:
    *   *Base:* Lower per-tx fee, no indemnity discussion (client bears all risk).
    *   *Premium:* Higher per-tx fee or flat monthly retainer that includes a defined service-level agreement (SLA) on accuracy, potentially backed by insurance proceeds (subject to legal review).

**Decision Aid:**
*   **Read:** At a false negative rate of 1 per month, your risk cost is only $0.005/transaction. If your churn risk increases by raising prices above $0.055, self-insuring via price hikes may be cheaper than losing clients.
*   **Flip Input:** The decision hinges entirely on your actual **False Negative Rate**. If unknown, instrument this immediately; without it, any reserve calculation is a guess.
*   **Threshold Rule:** If the calculated price increase exceeds 10% of your current rate ($0.005), do not bake it into the base fee; instead, structure it as an optional "risk-mitigation" add-on tier.
*   **Next Step:** Calculate your actual FNR over the last 6 months. If data is thin, run a pilot with 2 credit unions offering a fixed-fee "accuracy guarantee" tier to test willingness-to-pay before rolling out broadly.

**Note:** Industry standard for SaaS in banking typically limits liability to 12 months of fees paid, explicitly excluding consequential damages like regulatory fines, but this is a legal conclusion you must verify.
