You cannot solve multi-jurisdiction withholding with a simple pro-rata formula; you must route the liability calculation to a specialized tax attorney or CPA because the "correct" amount depends on specific interstate compacts and nexus rules that vary by worker location and work type.

**The Mechanics and Unknowns**
Withholding for gig workers crossing state lines is not purely arithmetic; it is a legal determination of "nexus" (where tax is owed) and "sourcing" (how much income is attributed to that state).
*   **The Rule:** Most states tax income earned within their borders. When a worker operates in CA, NY, and TX simultaneously, you generally must withhold for each state based on the income sourced there. However, Texas has no state income tax, while CA and NY do.
*   **The Complication:** States have different definitions of "work performed." Some use "physical presence" (days worked), others use "market-based sourcing" (where the customer is located). Without knowing which sourcing rule applies to your specific gig vertical in each state, any pro-rated calculation is an estimate, not a liability determination.
*   **Interstate Compacts:** The "unknown variables" are whether specific reciprocity agreements exist between the states where your workers reside versus where they work. For example, some states have agreements to avoid double taxation for residents working across borders, but these often require specific employee elections and forms that your platform must collect and manage. If you miss a filing requirement for a specific compact, you face penalties regardless of your math.

**Data Points You Must Collect**
To enable a professional to build a compliant algorithm, you must instrument your platform to capture:
1.  **Worker Residence State:** The legal domicile of the worker (for resident tax credits).
2.  **Granular Work Location:** GPS coordinates or specific jurisdiction codes for every task/gig completed, timestamped to the pay period.
3.  **Customer Location:** The jurisdiction of the entity paying for the service (critical for market-based sourcing states).
4.  **Worker Election Forms:** Digital collection of state-specific non-resident withholding exemptions or reciprocity election forms (e.g., NY IT-2104.1).
5.  **Time-Spent Metrics:** If physical presence is the sourcing rule, you need hours/minutes worked per jurisdiction, not just revenue generated.

**Professional Routing**
You need to retain a **state and local tax (SALT) specialist** or a **payroll tax attorney** with specific experience in the gig economy and multi-state nexus. Do not use a generalist CPA.
*   **The Binding Call to Make:** Ask them to define the "sourcing rule" (physical vs. market) for your specific service type in CA and NY, and to map the reciprocity agreements relevant to your worker demographic.
*   **The Deliverable You Need:** A written memo detailing the exact withholding logic per jurisdiction pair and a list of required forms for your engineering team to integrate.
