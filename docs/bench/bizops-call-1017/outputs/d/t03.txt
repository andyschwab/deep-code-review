**Verdict:** Your current token distribution model presents a high probability of being classified as an unregistered security offering under U.S. law; you must halt further distribution and engage specialized securities counsel before onboarding new clients.

The core issue is not your revenue model (setup fees) but the nature of the tokens themselves. Under the *SEC v. W.J. Howey Co.* framework, an instrument is a security if it involves: (1) an investment of money, (2) in a common enterprise, (3) with a reasonable expectation of profits, (4) to be derived from the efforts of others.

Your model triggers these prongs as follows:
1.  **Investment of Money:** While board members may not pay cash for tokens, the "money" prong can be satisfied by the value of the HOA's contract (the $2,000 setup fee) being exchanged for a governance mechanism that controls community assets. If the token is a condition of the service purchase, regulators may view the fee as the investment.
2.  **Common Enterprise:** VoteChain and the HOAs are likely linked in a horizontal common enterprise where the success of the token's utility depends on VoteChain's overall platform development and adoption.
3.  **Expectation of Profit:** This is your highest risk area. Even without a secondary market, "profit" can be interpreted broadly as any economic benefit or appreciation in utility value. If board members expect the token's voting power to increase in value (e.g., allowing decisions that increase property values or lower community costs) based on your platform's future features, this satisfies the prong. The lack of a secondary market is a mitigating factor but not a shield; restricted securities still exist.
4.  **Efforts of Others:** If the token's utility and value depend primarily on VoteChain's continued development, marketing, and maintenance of the blockchain infrastructure rather than the immediate actions of the token holders, this prong is met.

**Critical Unknowns to Resolve:**
*   **Utility vs. Speculation:** Can you prove the token has immediate, consumptive utility (voting on specific, existing issues) that is distinct from any future appreciation? Or is the value tied to the hope that your platform will succeed and make their votes more powerful?
*   **Transferability:** Even if you ban secondary sales, does the protocol allow any form of transfer, gifting, or inheritance? Any transferability increases security risk.
*   **Marketing Language:** Have your sales materials inadvertently promised future platform growth or token value appreciation to sell the $2,000 setup?

**Required Action:**
You must consult a **securities litigation and compliance attorney** specializing in **digital assets and blockchain**. Do not use a general corporate lawyer. You need counsel who can:
1.  Perform a formal *Howey* analysis on your specific tokenomics and whitepaper.
2.  Advise on potential exemptions (e.g., Regulation D) if the tokens are deemed securities, or guide a restructuring to a purely utility-based model that avoids security classification.
3.  Draft definitive legal opinions regarding the non-security status before you distribute tokens to 50 more HOAs, which would only compound your liability.

Do not proceed with the next 50 onboardings until you have a written legal opinion. The cost of retroactive registration or defending an SEC enforcement action far exceeds the cost of proper counsel now.
