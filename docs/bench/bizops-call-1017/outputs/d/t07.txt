**Lane R: Regulation and Compliance (The Binding Call)**
The core issue is that medical malpractice liability is typically tied to the *location of the patient* at the time of treatment, not just the doctor's license. While the Interstate Medical Licensure Compact (IMLC) streamlines the process for obtaining multiple licenses, it is not an insurance policy.
*   **The Rule:** Most standard malpractice policies are state-specific. If a doctor licensed in State A treats a patient in State B, the policy must explicitly cover acts occurring in State B. Some carriers offer "national" or "multi-state" policies, but these often require you to declare every state where you have patients and may exclude non-compact states entirely.
*   **The Risk:** If a doctor treats a patient in a non-compact state by accident (or without proper endorsement), you likely have a coverage gap. In a lawsuit, the insurer could deny the claim based on jurisdiction, leaving MindLink and the doctor personally liable for damages.
*   **Action:** Do not expand to new states until a broker confirms in writing that your current or proposed policy covers telehealth acts in those specific jurisdictions.
