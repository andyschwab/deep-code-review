### 1. The Arithmetic: Modeling the "Trust Gap"

Do not blend these metrics. Investors will scrutinize your ability to fund the gap before they care about your long-term LTV.

**A. Cash Burn per New Contract (The Survival Metric)**
This measures how much actual cash you lose each month for every new funeral home signed, before the 24-month unlock.
*   **Formula:** `Monthly Cash Burn = (Service Cost per Account + Amortized CAC) - $0 Recognized Cash Revenue`
*   **Your Inputs:**
    *   CAC: $1,200
    *   Churn: 2% monthly (implies ~42-month average life, but irrelevant for the first 24 months of cash flow).
    *   *Missing Input:* Service Cost per Account (hosting, support allocation). Let's assume **$50/mo** for this model (ASSUMED).
*   **Calculation (First 24 Months):**
    *   Monthly Outflow: $50 (service) + ($1,200 CAC / 24 mo amortization) = $100/mo effective cost.
    *   Monthly Inflow: $0 (funds trapped).
    *   **Net Cash Drain:** -$100 per contract/month for 2 years.
*   **Total Capital Required per Deal:** To support one new customer for the full hold period, you need **$2,400** in operating cash ($100 × 24 months) just to break even on cash flow, excluding the CAC upfront hit.

**B. Time Value & Opportunity Cost**
You asked about the time value of money (TVM). Since the principal is trapped in trust, you cannot invest it. The opportunity cost is the interest you *could* have earned or the cost of capital you pay to bridge the gap.
*   **Formula:** `Opportunity Cost = (Total Trapped Capital) × (Your Cost of Capital Rate) × (Time in Years)`
*   **Application:** If you raise money at a 20% cost of capital (typical for early SaaS), the $299/mo revenue stream is worth significantly less today.
*   **Adjustment:** Discount your future revenue cash flows (starting month 25) back to present value using your cost of capital. If the Net Present Value (NPV) of the contract is less than your CAC ($1,200), the business model fails regardless of the "booked" revenue later.

### 2. Metrics Investors Will Scrutinize

Given the regulatory constraint, standard SaaS metrics are misleading. Expect deep dives into:

1.  **Cash Payback Period (Extended):** Standard payback is CAC / Gross Margin. Yours is infinite until month 25. Investors will calculate: `Months to Positive Cumulative Cash Flow`. With your numbers, if costs are $100/mo, you don't see positive cash flow until roughly month 30-36 (24 mo hold + time to recoup CAC).
2.  **Working Capital Requirement per $1 ARR:** How much cash must you hold in the bank to support $1 of Annual Recurring Revenue?
    *   *Rule of Thumb:* If this ratio exceeds 1.5x, you are capital inefficient.
3.  **Burn Multiple vs. Trust Growth:** Are you burning cash to grow the trust balance? Investors need to see that the trust balance (future revenue) is growing faster than your operating burn.
