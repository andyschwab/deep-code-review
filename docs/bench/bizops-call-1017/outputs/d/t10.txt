**The Liability Landscape (Lane R: Regulation-Dependent)**
The core issue is not arithmetic but legal classification under US healthcare law. When software suggests billing codes, regulators (CMS, OIG) and insurers scrutinize whether the vendor is merely a "tool provider" or an active participant in the billing decision.
*   **Who bears liability?** Typically, the **clinic (the provider)** holds primary liability for submitting claims, as they legally attest to the accuracy of the codes. However, if your AI systematically suggests higher codes without clinical justification, your company could face secondary liability under the **False Claims Act** for "knowing" submission of false records or conspiracy. If your marketing implies the codes are "audit-proof" or you exercise control over the final submission, you shift from a passive tool to an active participant, increasing your exposure.
*   **Unknown Precedents:** There is no settled case law specifically defining liability for *generative* AI upcoding. The precedent relies on older principles regarding computer-assisted coding (CAC): if the vendor knows the algorithm is flawed and fails to correct it, they share liability. The "black box" nature of AI does not excuse systematic errors; regulators may view a lack of explainability as a compliance failure itself.

**Required Safeguards & Legal Routing**
You cannot build "compliance" solely through code; you need legal structuring.
1.  **Contractual Indemnification:** Your contracts must explicitly state that the **clinic** retains final review authority and sole responsibility for code selection. You need an indemnity clause where the clinic agrees to hold you harmless for billing errors, *provided* your software functioned as specified.
2.  **Audit Trails:** The product must log every suggestion, the specific clinical note data used to generate it, and the user's final action (accepted, rejected, or modified). This "human-in-the-loop" evidence is critical to prove you are a tool, not the decision-maker.
3.  **Clinical Validation:** You must implement a guardrail that flags suggestions deviating significantly from historical norms for that provider or specialty for mandatory human review.
