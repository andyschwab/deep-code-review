**Highest Risk Data Activities for PetDNA**
Since you handle genetic data, you are processing **biometric data** for the purpose of uniquely identifying a living organism. While the data is for dogs, it often correlates strongly with owner data (shipping, payment, account linkage), potentially pulling it into the scope of personal data regulation depending on local interpretation.
1.  **Explicit Consent Mechanisms:** The highest fine risk comes from processing without valid, granular consent. If your "I Agree" checkbox is pre-ticked or bundled with general terms, you are vulnerable.
2.  **Data Subject Rights (DSR) Fulfillment:** Failure to allow users to delete their data ("Right to be Forgotten") or export it within 30 days is a frequent trigger for audits and penalties.
3.  **Cross-Border Data Transfers:** Moving EU customer data back to US servers without valid transfer mechanisms (like the EU-US Data Privacy Framework or Standard Contractual Clauses) is a primary enforcement target.

**When to Hire a Data Protection Officer (DPO)**
You likely need to designate a DPO **immediately upon launch** in the EU. Under GDPR Article 37, a DPO is mandatory if your core activities involve:
*   Large-scale processing of special categories of data (genetic/biometric data often falls here if linked to humans).
*   Systematic monitoring of data subjects on a large scale.
Given you are selling genetic testing kits, regulators may view this as high-risk processing regardless of your current volume. Do not wait for a specific revenue threshold; the nature of the data triggers the requirement.
