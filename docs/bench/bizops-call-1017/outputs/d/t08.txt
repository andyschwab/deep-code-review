**The Legal Boundary (Lane R)**
Your concern regarding "privatization of public infrastructure" is the binding constraint. This is not a math problem; it is a question of state administrative law and municipal code.

*   **The Regime:** In Arizona and most US states, municipal utilities are governed by state statutes (e.g., Arizona Revised Statutes Title 9 for cities) and city charters. These often contain provisions restricting the alienation of public assets or the delegation of core governmental functions (like rate-setting or revenue collection) to private entities without specific voter approval or competitive bidding processes.
*   **The Risk:** A revenue-share model where a private entity takes a cut of "savings" can be legally construed as:
    1.  **An encumbrance on public funds:** Committing future public revenue to a private party may violate debt limits or appropriation laws.
    2.  **Unauthorized privatization:** If the meter reading/revenue function is deemed a "public purpose" that cannot be outsourced on a contingency fee basis.
    3.  **Procurement Violations:** Bypassing standard RFP (Request for Proposal) processes required for long-term municipal contracts.
*   **Common Municipal Rejection Arguments:** City attorneys typically reject these models citing: (a) lack of statutory authority to enter profit-sharing agreements, (b) violation of "gift clauses" (prohibiting giving public money/assets to private firms without equal return), and (c) inability to budget for variable, uncapped future liabilities.
*   **Required Action:** You must route the binding determination to a **municipal finance attorney** or a **public utility law specialist** licensed in Arizona.
    *   **Specific Question for Counsel:** "Does a 5-year contingency fee contract based on utility efficiency savings constitute an impermissible encumbrance of public funds or a violation of Arizona's gift clause under ARS §9-401 et seq.?"
    *   **Documents to Prepare:** Bring your pilot data showing exact savings calculations, the draft revenue-share term sheet, and the specific city charters of your target municipalities.

**Next Step:** Before approaching more cities, engage Arizona counsel to review your contract structure. They may suggest restructuring the deal as a fixed-fee "energy performance contract" (EPC) model, which often has clearer statutory authorization than direct revenue sharing.
