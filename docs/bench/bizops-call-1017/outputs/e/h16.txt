**The Call**
Do not itemize a "risk premium" or charge extra for encryption; include secure protocols in the base £2,500 fee to avoid signaling that security is optional, but treat the £2,500 as a loss-leader or break-even acquisition cost rather than a profit center. Your primary financial risk is not the migration fee itself, but the potential liability from a breach during the 48-hour dual-system window, which cannot be fully priced into a £2,500 one-time fee for small clinics without making it unaffordable.

**The Numbers**
*Formula:* `Migration Margin = Fee - (Labor Hours × Hourly Rate) - (Infrastructure Overhead) - (Risk Reserve)`
*User Figures:* Fee = £2,500; Team Cost = £25,000/mo (5 devs); Pilot Customers = 30.
*Labelled Assumptions (ASSUMED):*
1.  **Labor Hours:** 10 hours per migration (low/base) to 20 hours (high) including QA and oversight.
2.  **Hourly Rate:** £50/hr (blended cost of £25k/5 devs/160hrs).
3.  **Risk Reserve:** 10% of fee (base) vs. potential liability exceeding £50k (high).

*Computed Results:*
*   **Base Case:** £2,500 - (10 hrs × £50) - £250 (overhead) = **£1,750 gross margin** per migration.
*   **High Effort Case:** £2,500 - (20 hrs × £50) - £500 (overhead) = **£1,000 gross margin**.
*   **Break-even Trigger:** If labor exceeds 45 hours per migration, the service loses money at this price point.

**What Flips It, and The Trigger**
The single input that changes the call is **liability exposure**, not labor cost. If a breach occurs during the 48-hour window, the cost (fines, legal, reputation) will dwarf the £2,500 fee.
*Trigger:* If your professional indemnity insurance policy does not explicitly cover "data migration activities" or "temporary dual-system states," **stop selling the migration service immediately** until coverage is confirmed.
*Rule of Thumb:* Never price a catastrophic risk into a small transaction fee; manage it via insurance and contractual limitation of liability.
*This Week's Step:* If your current insurance carrier cannot provide a written endorsement for migration risks within 5 days, consider pausing the service launch or requiring customers to sign a specific waiver limiting your liability to the fee amount (subject to legal review).

**Routing Note (Jurisdiction-Specific & High-Stakes)**
You asked which requirements need a formal legal opinion versus standard technical protocols.
*   **Standard Technical Protocols:** Implementing TLS 1.3 for data in transit and AES-256 for data at rest are engineering standards, not legal determinations.
*   **Requires Legal Opinion (UK Specific):** You mentioned "HIPAA-equivalent." HIPAA is US law. In the UK, you are bound by the **UK GDPR** and the **Data Protection Act 2018**, overseen by the ICO. A lawyer must determine: (1) Whether your migration process constitutes a "high risk" processing activity requiring a formal **Data Protection Impact Assessment (DPIA)** filed before starting; (2) The specific wording required in your Data Processing Agreement (DPA) to allocate liability during the 48-hour dual-system window; and (3) Whether your current insurance covers UK GDPR fines (which can be up to 4% of global turnover). Do not rely on "HIPAA equivalence" as a legal defense in the UK.
