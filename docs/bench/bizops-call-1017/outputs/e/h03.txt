**The Call:** Do not automatically write down the inventory monthly; carry the three batches ($2.1M) at cost ($85k/batch average) **only if** you have documented evidence that the specific client contracts remain valid and the regulatory approval is probable despite the delay. If the delay increases the risk of contract cancellation or makes the net realizable value (NRV) fall below cost, you must recognize an impairment loss immediately.

**The Numbers & Methodology:**
Under US GAAP (ASC 330) and IFRS (IAS 2), inventory is valued at the **lower of cost or net realizable value (NRV)**.
*   **Formula:** `Impairment Loss = Carrying Cost - NRV`
*   **Your Inputs:** Carrying Cost = $2.1M (3 batches).
*   **NRV Calculation (ASSUMED):** `Expected Final Sale Price` minus `Costs to Complete/Sell`.
    *   *Scenario A (Base):* Client contract is binding; approval expected in 18 months. NRV > Cost. **Impairment = $0.**
    *   *Scenario B (Risk):* Contract allows cancellation after 12 months; probability of cancellation is 50%. Expected recovery drops. **Impairment = Partial or Full Write-down.**
*   **Result:** You do not amortize inventory over time like an intangible asset. You only adjust the value if the *recoverable amount* drops below the $2.1M cost basis due to obsolescence, damage, or contract risk.

**What Flips It & The Trigger:**
The single input that changes the call is the **legal enforceability of the client contract** past the 12-month mark.
*   **Trigger:** If any of the three contracts contain a "termination for convenience" clause activated by delays >12 months, or if the client has formally expressed intent to cancel, you must move from Tier 2 (explanation) to Tier 3 (specific impairment calculation) immediately.
*   **Rule of Thumb:** If the probability of collection drops below "probable" (typically >75% likelihood), consider writing down the asset to its expected recovery value.
*   **This Week's Step:** If your contracts lack explicit extension clauses for regulatory delays, consider drafting contract amendments with clients to reaffirm commitment before your next board meeting.

**Documentation for Due diligence:**
To satisfy auditors and Series B investors without misrepresenting health, maintain a "Inventory Valuation Memo" containing:
1.  **Contract Status:** Copies of signed SOWs confirming the client remains liable for payment upon approval, regardless of timeline.
2.  **Regulatory Pathway:** A technical note from your QA lead confirming the batches meet specs and the delay is procedural, not technical (proving no obsolescence).
3.  **NRV Analysis:** A spreadsheet showing the expected sale price minus remaining costs, proving NRV > $2.1M.
4.  **Aging Report:** Clearly disclose the >12-month aging in footnotes, explaining the industry-specific regulatory cause to prevent investors from assuming the inventory is "stale."

**Routing Note:**
The determination of whether a specific contract's delay triggers an "other-than-temporary" impairment under ASC 330 or IAS 2 is a binding accounting judgment. Bring your specific client contracts and the corresponding regulatory correspondence to your CPA to finalize the impairment test before closing your Series B data room.
