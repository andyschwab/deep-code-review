**The Call:** Do not offer blanket indemnity for regulatory fines; instead, adopt a "Good-Better-Best" tiered pricing model with a capped liability clause and a service-level agreement (SLA) credit system. Building a reserve fund directly into a flat per-transaction fee will likely overprice you out of the market for low-risk clients while under-collecting from high-risk ones.

**The Numbers:**
Your current revenue is $250,000/month (5M transactions × $0.05).
To evaluate the risk exposure, we must estimate the cost of failure.
*Formula:* Monthly Risk Exposure = (Total Transactions) × (False Negative Rate) × (Fine per Incident).
*Missing Inputs (Labelled ASSUMED):*
1.  **False Negative Rate (FNR):** Industry benchmarks for mature AI fraud systems range from 0.001% (low) to 0.01% (high). Let's bracket this.
2.  **Incident Definition:** Does one false negative equal one fine, or are fines aggregated per breach event? We assume one fine per distinct undetected fraud event for this model.

*Scenario Analysis (Monthly):*
*   **Low Case (FNR 0.001%):** 50 incidents × $25,000 = **$1.25M potential liability.**
*   **High Case (FNR 0.01%):** 500 incidents × $25,000 = **$12.5M potential liability.**

*Current Revenue vs. Low Case Liability:* Your monthly revenue ($250k) covers only 20% of a single low-case month's fines. A "reserve fund" built purely on the $0.05 fee is mathematically insufficient unless the fee increases 5x–50x, which violates your competitiveness constraint.

**What Flips It, and The Trigger:**
The decision flips on your **contractual liability cap**. If you accept uncapped liability for regulatory fines, no pricing model works; the tail risk is infinite relative to your revenue.
*Trigger:* If your historical False Negative Rate exceeds **0.002%** (10 incidents/month), you must pause new sales and retrain the model before adjusting pricing.
*Rule of Thumb:* Vendors typically cap liability at 12 months of fees paid or a fixed dollar amount (e.g., $1M), explicitly excluding "indirect, consequential, or punitive damages" (which regulatory fines often fall under).

**Actionable Step:**
If your FNR is below 0.002%, consider structuring three tiers:
1.  **Basic ($0.04/trans):** Standard detection, liability capped at 6 months' fees, no indemnity for fines.
2.  **Professional ($0.06/trans):** Enhanced rules, liability capped at 12 months' fees, SLA credits for missed detections.
3.  **Enterprise ($0.08/trans + premium):** Custom tuning, liability capped at $2M, *limited* indemnity for specific regulatory penalties up to the cap.

**Industry Practice (Tier 2 Explanation):**
In the banking sector, vendors rarely indemnify clients for regulatory fines resulting from algorithmic errors. Fines are viewed as the client's responsibility for maintaining adequate oversight and controls. Standard practice is to offer **Service Level Credits** (refunds of fees) if performance metrics (like catch rates) drop below a threshold, rather than cash payouts for third-party penalties. Offering full indemnity for fines is an outlier that suggests a lack of confidence in the model and may trigger deeper regulatory scrutiny of your own compliance posture.
