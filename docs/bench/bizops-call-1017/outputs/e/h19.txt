**The Call:** Do not recognize the $150,000 as revenue yet; instead, negotiate immediate partial payments classified as "non-refundable deposits" or "progress billings" to extend your runway, as recognizing revenue before FDA 510(k) clearance and delivery violates standard accrual accounting principles.

**What Flips It, and the Trigger:**
The critical input is the **payment structure**. If you can restructure the $150,000 contract to collect 50% ($75,000) now as a non-refundable deposit for manufacturing, your runway extends by ~1.6 months ($75k/$45k), reducing the deficit to $375,000.
*   **Trigger Rule:** If you cannot secure at least **$20,000 in immediate non-dilutive cash** (via deposits or bridge financing) within 30 days, you will face a liquidity crisis before the 18-month delay resolves.
*   **Step:** This week, draft an amendment to the purchase orders requesting a "manufacturing mobilization fee" equal to 25-50% of the hardware cost, explicitly decoupled from software activation.

**Routing Note (Accounting & Legal):**
Revenue recognition for medical devices subject to regulatory clearance is a **Tier 3** determination. Under ASC 606, you generally cannot recognize revenue until control transfers and performance obligations (including regulatory clearance) are satisfied. You must consult a CPA to determine if "progress billings" can be recorded as a liability (deferred revenue) without triggering tax events, and to confirm if your specific 510(k) delay constitutes a "variable consideration" constraint that prohibits booking any revenue until clearance is granted.
