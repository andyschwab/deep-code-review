**The Call**
Do not sell to additional agencies until you complete a formal disparate impact audit; historical data alone does not automatically trigger liability, but using unvalidated data that results in discriminatory outcomes creates significant legal exposure for both your company and your clients, regardless of who makes the final hiring decision.

**The Numbers & Risk Framework**
Liability under the Equal Employment Opportunity Commission (EEOC) and the Uniform Guidelines on Employee Selection Procedures (UGESP) is determined by the "four-fifths rule" (80% rule), not merely the data source.
*   **Formula:** (Selection Rate of Protected Group) ÷ (Selection Rate of Highest-Performing Group).
*   **Threshold:** If the result is < 0.80, a *prima facie* case of disparate impact exists.
*   **Your Data:** You have processed 20,000 applications. You must immediately calculate this ratio for race, gender, age, and disability status based on your algorithm's *recommendations* (not just final hires).
*   **Assumption (Labelled):** We assume you have access to demographic data for the 20,000 applicants to perform this calculation. If you do not, you cannot currently measure your risk.

**What Flips It, and The Trigger**
The single input that changes your risk profile from "manageable" to "critical" is the **adverse impact ratio** calculated above.
*   **Trigger:** If your internal audit shows a ratio below 0.80 for any protected class, you have a presumptive liability.
*   **Stop/Pivot Rule:** If the ratio is < 0.80, pause sales immediately. You must demonstrate "business necessity" and that no less discriminatory alternative exists. Retraining on synthetic data is a potential mitigation strategy (tier 2 concept), but it is not a legal shield unless the new model passes the 80% test and is validated as job-related.
*   **Actionable Step:** This week, run a retrospective audit on the 20,000 historical screens. If you lack demographic tags, engage a third-party industrial-organizational psychologist or legal counsel to design a validation study before processing another candidate.
