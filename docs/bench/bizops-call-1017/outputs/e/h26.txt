**The Call:** Treat every sidewalk incident as a high-severity auto-liability exposure, not a standard premises-liability claim; immediately route your policy language to a Florida insurance broker specializing in robotics or commercial auto to confirm whether your current General Liability (GL) policy excludes "autonomous vehicles on public rights-of-way," as this single exclusion likely leaves you uninsured for your highest-risk scenario.

**The Numbers:**
Insurers do not publish a universal formula for "sidewalk vs. lawn" premiums because rates are filed per carrier and risk class. However, the cost differential is driven by the **policy limit required** and the **coverage type**, not just the location.
*   **Current State (Assumed):** Standard GL policy. Typical limit: $1M/$2M. Cost: ~3–5% of revenue for low-risk landscaping (~$15k–$25k/year). *Risk:* Likely contains an "auto exclusion" or specific "robotics/unmanned vehicle" exclusion when operating on public roads/sidewalks.
*   **Required State (Illustrative):** Commercial Auto Liability + Specialized Robotics Endorsement. Typical limit for public interaction: $2M–$5M. Cost: Often 2x–4x standard GL for autonomous fleets due to "systemic failure" risk (one bug affects all 150 robots).
*   **Exposure Gap:** If a pedestrian suffers a permanent injury on a sidewalk and your GL denies coverage based on the "auto" or "mobile equipment" exclusion, you bear 100% of the judgment. With $500k revenue, a $1.5M verdict bankrupts the company instantly.
*   **Assumption:** We assume your current policy defines the robot as "equipment" (covered on private property) but potentially "auto" or "uninsured mobile transport" on public rights-of-way. This distinction is the variable that flips your coverage from "protected" to "exposed."

**What Flips It, and the Trigger:**
The deciding factor is the **legal definition of the robot** in your specific policy wording when crossing a property line.
*   **Trigger:** The moment a robot tire touches a public sidewalk, many standard GL policies cease to cover bodily injury caused by the *movement* of the device, categorizing it as an auto risk.
*   **Threshold Rule of Thumb:** If your policy does not explicitly name "autonomous mobile robots" and affirmatively cover them on "public rights-of-way," assume you have zero coverage for sidewalk incidents.
*   **Actionable Step:** If your broker cannot produce a written endorsement within 5 business days explicitly covering "autonomous navigation on public sidewalks," consider pausing sidewalk transit operations or purchasing a separate Commercial Auto policy immediately.
