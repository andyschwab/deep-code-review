**The Call**
Do not restructure or establish local entities yet; the risk of creating a taxable corporate presence (nexus) in the Cayman Islands or Bermuda solely by hosting validating nodes is currently low, but you must immediately verify the "permanent establishment" definitions in your specific hosting contracts to ensure you aren't accidentally leasing "space and personnel" rather than just computing power.

**The Numbers & Assumptions**
There is no single formula for nexus, as it depends on treaty language and local statutes, but we can model the risk exposure based on your operational footprint:
*   **Current Footprint:** 2 jurisdictions (Cayman, Bermuda), 0 local employees, 0 local management, ~$0 local revenue (revenue is recognized in Delaware/EU where customers contract).
*   **Tier 2 Illustrative Scenario (Low Risk):** If your agreements are strictly "cloud/serverless" or "colocation" (you rent rack space/power, no local staff act on your behalf), the taxable base in those jurisdictions is typically **$0**. Most tax havens lack a corporate income tax anyway, but the risk is triggering *reporting* obligations or losing US/EU tax credits.
*   **Tier 2 Illustrative Scenario (High Risk - ASSUMED):** If your hosting agreement inadvertently grants the local provider authority to sign contracts, maintain hardware beyond simple replacement, or if you have any local contractors managing the nodes, tax authorities could argue a "Permanent Establishment" (PE). If a PE is found, and assuming a hypothetical corporate tax rate were applied (e.g., if laws change or if this triggers US GILTI rules), you could be liable on a portion of global profits allocated to those nodes.
    *   *Allocation Formula (ASSUMED):* (Local Server Costs / Total Global OpEx) × Global Profit.
    *   *Example:* If server costs are $10k/mo and total OpEx is $150k/mo, ~6.7% of profits could theoretically be exposed. On a break-even startup, this cash impact is low, but the compliance cost (filing returns in new jurisdictions) could drain **$15k–$30k annually** in legal/accounting fees, impacting your $1.5M runway.

**What Flips It, and the Trigger**
The one input that changes the call from "low risk" to "immediate restructuring required" is the **legal characterization of your hosting agreement**.
*   **Trigger:** If your contract defines the local provider as an "agent" with authority to conclude contracts or if you hire any local personnel (even contractors) to manage the nodes, you likely create a Permanent Establishment.
*   **Rule of Thumb:** "Servers alone rarely create nexus; people and authority do."
*   **Stop/Pivot Date:** Before your next quarterly board meeting or financial audit (approx. 90 days out), you must have this confirmed.
*   **This Week's Step:** If your hosting contracts contain clauses allowing the provider to "act on behalf of" ChainCold or if you have any local contacts managing the hardware, consider engaging a cross-border tax specialist to review the specific "Permanent Establishment" articles in the US-Cayman and US-Bermuda tax information exchange agreements.
