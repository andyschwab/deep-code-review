**The Call**
Assume your users own **nothing** in the AI-generated portions of the draft; under current US guidance, lack of significant human authorship voids copyright protection for those specific elements entirely. You must route the definition of "significant" and your Terms of Service liability to an IP attorney before making any ownership guarantees to users.

**The Numbers**
There is no fixed percentage threshold (e.g., "10%" or "20%") in US law or Copyright Office guidance that automatically grants copyright.
*   **Rule:** Copyright requires human authorship. The US Copyright Office (USCO) states that works generated primarily by AI without human creative control are not registrable.
*   **Application:** If a user contributes <10% of the text via prompts or minor edits, the USCO likely views the AI as the sole author of the remaining 90%+.
*   **Result:** The 90%+ AI-generated text is in the **public domain** immediately upon creation. The user may only claim copyright on their specific original contributions (the <10%), but not the compilation if the AI's arrangement dominates.
*   **Assumption (Labelled):** This analysis assumes the "Thaler v. Perlmutter" precedent and recent USCO guidance on "Zarya of the Dawn" apply strictly to patent drafts, where the legal argumentation is generated by the model.

**What Flips It, and the Trigger**
The deciding factor is not word count, but **creative control**.
*   **Trigger:** If a user can demonstrate they directed the AI's specific expression, structure, and argumentation iteratively (not just a single prompt), they *might* claim authorship.
*   **Flip Input:** The shift from "prompting" to "co-authoring." If your tool allows users to edit, rearrange, and rewrite the AI output significantly *before* finalizing, the human authorship claim strengthens.
*   **Rule of Thumb:** If the AI determines the expressive elements, it's public domain. If the human determines them using the AI as a tool, it's copyrightable.
*   **This Week's Step:** If your current flow is "prompt → generate → download," consider adding a mandatory "human review and edit" checkpoint in the UI to bolster any future user claims of authorship.
