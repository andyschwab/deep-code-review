**The Call**
Do not share any patient medical history with sponsors until you have executed Business Associate Agreements (BAAs) with those sponsors and implemented a specific HIPAA-compliant consent workflow; your current "stripping obvious identifiers" method is likely insufficient for the detailed medical history required for trial eligibility and exposes you to tier-3 regulatory liability.

**The Numbers & Risk Context**
*   **Current Exposure:** 200 referrals/quarter × $500 = $100k revenue.
*   **Potential Penalty:** HIPAA violations range from $100 to $50,000+ per violation record, with an annual cap of $1.5 million per violation category. A single breach affecting your 5,000-user database could theoretically exceed $2.5M in statutory maximums, far surpassing your $400k annualized revenue.
*   **The Data Gap:** "Stripping obvious identifiers" (names, addresses) often fails the HIPAA "Safe Harbor" method if detailed medical history remains, as rare conditions or specific combinations of data can re-identify a patient. For clinical trials, sponsors *need* this detailed history, meaning you cannot simply de-identify the data and sell it; you are disclosing Protected Health Information (PHI).

**What Flips It, and The Trigger**
*   **The Flip Input:** The legal status of your relationship with the pharmaceutical sponsors.
    *   *Scenario A (Current Risk):* If sponsors are third-party buyers without a BAA, you generally cannot disclose PHI to them unless you have specific, study-by-study patient authorization.
    *   *Scenario B (Compliant Path):* If sponsors sign BAAs, they become "Business Associates," allowing data sharing for "health care operations" or "treatment," but strict patient consent rules regarding the *purpose* of the disclosure still apply under the Common Rule (clinical trials) and HIPAA.
*   **The Trigger:** The moment you transmit a profile containing medical history to a sponsor without a signed BAA and specific patient authorization for that transfer.
*   **Stop/Pivot Rule:** If you cannot secure a BAA with a sponsor, do not transfer medical history. Transfer only de-identified data (which may be too vague for trial screening) or stop the referral.
*   **This Week's Step:** If you are transmitting medical data today, consider halting all transfers immediately until counsel drafts your BAA template and reviews your current patient intake forms for specific research authorization language.

**Routing Note (Tier 3: Binding Legal Determination)**
HIPAA compliance, the validity of "broad consent" versus "specific consent" for commercial research matching, and the drafting of Business Associate Agreements are binding legal determinations. You must engage a healthcare attorney specializing in HIPAA and the Common Rule (45 CFR 46) to:
1.  Determine if your model classifies you as a Covered Entity or a Business Associate to the clinics where patients originate.
2.  Draft the specific Authorization forms required for sharing PHI with commercial sponsors.
3.  Confirm whether the "de-identification" you are attempting meets the statistical verification standard if you claim data is anonymized.
