**What Flips It, and the Trigger**
The determining factor is not the math, but your **regulatory status** (Private Adviser vs. Registered Investment Adviser) and the specific **exemption** you are claiming (e.g., Venture Capital Adviser, Private Fund Adviser, or the < $100M AUM state exemption).
*   **The Trigger:** The moment you market to "accredited investors" using a profit-share model, you enter the definition of an investment adviser charging a performance fee.
*   **The Risk:** Under the Advisers Act, performance fees are generally prohibited unless charged to "qualified clients" (net worth > $2.2M or assets > $1.1M, adjusted for inflation), which is a higher bar than "accredited investor." If your 12 accounts include accredited investors who are *not* qualified clients, the fee structure itself may be illegal, not just disclosure-heavy.
*   **High-Water Mark Reality:** This provision prevents charging fees on recovered losses but does **not** mitigate the regulatory classification of the fee or the "qualified client" requirement.
*   **Actionable Step:** If your current clients are not verified "qualified clients," consider Y: freezing new onboarding immediately and restructuring the fee to a flat percentage or verifying client status before the next capital call.
