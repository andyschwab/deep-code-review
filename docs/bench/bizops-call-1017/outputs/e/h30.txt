**The Call:** Your current 10% success-fee model creates a high risk of non-compliance for your non-profit clients under both IRS regulations and standard foundation guidelines; you should immediately pivot to requiring fees be paid from unrestricted operating funds or shift to a flat/hourly pricing structure.

**The Numbers & Compliance Logic:**
The core issue is not arithmetic but regulatory classification. Under IRS rules (specifically Treasury Reg. § 53.4945-5(a)(6)) and the standards of major foundations (e.g., Council on Foundations), grant funds generally cannot be used to pay for fundraising expenses.
*   **Current State:** You charge 10% of the grant award ($200k revenue on $2M secured). If clients pay this from the grant proceeds, they are using restricted charitable assets to pay for fundraising costs.
*   **The Violation:** This is typically classified as an impermissible private benefit to you (the vendor) and a misuse of restricted funds by the client. Many foundations explicitly void grants if any portion is used for contingency fundraising fees.
*   **The Fix:** The fee must be decoupled from the grant award's restricted status.
    *   *Option A (Recommended):* Charge a flat fee or hourly rate for writing services, payable from the non-profit's unrestricted general fund, regardless of the grant outcome.
    *   *Option B (If keeping success element):* The success fee must be contractually obligated to be paid from *unrestricted* funds only, never directly deducted from the grant award before the non-profit receives it. However, even this is scrutinized heavily as "fundraising compensation" tied to a specific gift.

**What Flips It, and The Trigger:**
The triggering fact is the **source of payment**. If the fee is deducted from the grant award or labeled as a "fundraising cost" in the grant budget, it violates standard compliance.
*   **Trigger Threshold:** If >0% of your fees are currently paid directly from grant proceeds, your clients are at risk of audit or grant clawback.
*   **Actionable Step:** This week, draft an addendum to your client contracts stating: "All fees for services are payable from the Client's unrestricted operating funds and shall not be charged against or deducted from specific restricted grant awards."

**Routing Note:**
Because this involves IRS private benefit doctrine and specific foundation grant agreements, you must have a qualified non-profit attorney review your new contract language and pricing model before implementation to ensure it does not jeopardize your clients' 501(c)(3) status.
