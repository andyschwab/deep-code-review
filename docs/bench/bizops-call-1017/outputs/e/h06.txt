**The Call**
Depreciate the $2.2 million asset over the **7-year lease term**, not the 20-year useful life, because the lease term limits the period of economic benefit to the lessee; however, the specific classification of the glass (real property improvement vs. personal property) and the final tax treatment are binding legal determinations requiring a CPA.

**The Numbers**
The governing accounting principle (ASC 842 / GAAP) dictates that the depreciation period for leasehold improvements is the **shorter** of the asset's useful life or the lease term (including reasonably certain renewal periods).

*Formula:* Annual Expense = Capitalized Cost / Depreciation Period

*Scenario A: 7-Year Lease (Base Assumption)*
*   Input: $2,200,000 / 7 years
*   Result: **$314,286 per year**
*   *Assumption:* No renewal options are "reasonably certain" to be exercised.

*Scenario B: 20-Year Useful Life (Incorrect for Lessee)*
*   Input: $2,200,000 / 20 years
*   Result: $110,000 per year
*   *Note:* Using this figure overstates net income and violates the matching principle if the asset reverts to the landlord in 7 years.

*Portfolio Impact ($9M Total):*
*   7-Year Schedule: ~$1.29M annual expense.
*   20-Year Schedule: $450k annual expense.
*   *Risk:* Using the 20-year schedule creates a $840k/year discrepancy in reported expenses across your projects.

**What Flips It, and The Trigger**
The single input that changes the call is **"Reasonably Certain Renewal."** If the lease includes renewal options that the tenant is reasonably certain to exercise (e.g., a 7-year lease + two 5-year renewals where the economics compel renewal), the depreciation period extends to 17 years.
*   *Trigger:* Review lease clauses for renewal penalties or below-market renewal rates. If the present value of renewal payments is significantly below market, consider the renewal term in the depreciation schedule.
*   *Action:* If the lease lacks explicit renewal certainty, file using the 7-year term; if renewal is probable, consult a CPA to document the "reasonably certain" assessment before extending the schedule.
