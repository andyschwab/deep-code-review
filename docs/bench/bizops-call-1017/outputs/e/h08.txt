You should immediately pivot to a tiered pricing structure (e.g., Standard, Complex, Premium) based on case characteristics rather than a single flat rate, or shift to a pure technology licensing fee distinct from the legal service fee.

**The Numbers & Risk Exposure:**
*   **Current Volume:** 1,200 cases/year × £500 = £600,000 annual revenue.
*   **Cost Variance:** If computational/human review costs range from £50 (2 hours) to £1,000 (40 hours), your margin swings from +£450 to -£500 per case.
*   **Regulatory Trigger:** The Solicitors Regulation Authority (SRA) Code of Conduct requires fees to reflect the time, labor, and complexity involved. A fixed fee that results in a windfall for simple cases or a loss for complex ones can be challenged as not "fair and reasonable."
*   **Assumption (Labelled):** We assume the SRA will view a 20x variance in resource consumption (2 vs. 40 hours) without price differentiation as a failure to meet transparency and fairness standards.

**What Flips It & The Trigger:**
The deciding factor is whether the fee is presented as the *legal service fee* or a *technology access fee*.
*   **Trigger:** If your contract states the £500 is the "legal fee" for the case, you are likely non-compliant. If it is a "software processing fee" separate from the solicitor's professional fee, it may be permissible, provided the total cost to the client remains transparent.
*   **Rule of Thumb:** If the variance in cost-to-serve exceeds 25% of the average fee, a single flat rate is rarely defensible as "fair."
*   **Action This Week:** Draft a "Tiered Service Schedule" defining three complexity bands with corresponding fees. Present this to your partner firms as a compliance safeguard. If a case falls outside the "Standard" band, require a pre-engagement estimate.

**Specific Disclosures Required (General Practice):**
To ensure transparency, end-clients typically must receive:
1.  A clear breakdown separating professional legal fees from technology/AI processing costs.
2.  An explanation of how the AI fee is calculated (e.g., per case vs. hourly).
3.  A statement confirming that the use of AI does not diminish the solicitor's supervisory responsibility.
4.  Notification of any potential additional costs if a case escalates beyond the defined "standard" complexity.

There is no explicit "safe harbor" in UK rules that automatically exempts technology-assisted services from fairness tests; the obligation to ensure the total fee is fair remains with the solicitor.
