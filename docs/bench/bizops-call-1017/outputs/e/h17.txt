**The Call**
Do not sign the contract with the $2 million liability cap as currently structured; the mismatch between your $5 million insurance coverage and the $2 million contractual cap creates an immediate, unmitigated exposure of your company's cash reserves and future assets. Proceed only if the airport raises the cap to match your insurance limit ($5M) or provides a specific indemnity clause covering the $3 million gap.

**The Numbers**
*   **Exposure Formula:** (Potential Claim Amount) − (Insurance Limit) = Personal/Corporate Asset Exposure.
*   **Current Scenario:** If a claim hits $4.5 million:
    *   Insurance pays: $4.5M (capped at policy $5M).
    *   Contractual Cap: $2M.
    *   **Result:** You are contractually liable for the full $4.5M, but insurance only covers up to $5M. However, if the claim is $2.5M, insurance covers it. The critical risk is not the math of a single claim, but the **contractual waiver of defense**. By accepting a $2M cap, you may be agreeing to pay the first $2M out of pocket before insurance kicks in, or worse, agreeing to liabilities that your insurance policy explicitly excludes (e.g., contractual liability assumptions).
*   **Financial Impact:**
    *   Pilot Net Profit (Base): $400k (Rev) − $150k (Cost) = **$250k**.
    *   Cash Reserves: **$300k**.
    *   **Risk:** A single incident exceeding $2M could wipe out your $300k reserves and the $250k pilot profit instantly, potentially bankrupting the entity if the judgment exceeds your total assets.
*   **ASSUMED (Low/High):** We assume a "worst-case" autonomous vehicle incident involving passenger injury could easily exceed $2M in medical and liability costs (Low: $1.5M, High: $10M+).

**What Flips It, and the Trigger**
The deal flips from "reject" to "consider" only if the contract includes a **"Savings Clause"** or **"Insurance Requirement Clause"** stating: *"Notwithstanding the liability cap herein, Contractor's liability shall extend up to the limits of its required insurance coverage ($5M)."*
*   **Trigger:** If the airport authority refuses to align the liability cap with your insurance limits or refuses to indemnify you for claims covered by your policy but excluded by the cap, **walk away**. The $250k pilot profit does not justify risking the entire $300k reserve and the company's existence.
*   **Step this week:** If the airport cites "standard policy," ask specifically for their **Certificate of Insurance** requirements for *their* side and propose a mutual cap equal to the lower of the two insurance policies.

**Routing Note (Tier 3: Legal/Regulatory)**
This is a high-stakes transportation and sovereign immunity matter. You must engage a Texas transportation lawyer to review:
