**The Call**
Model your unit economics on a **cash-flow basis** for internal decisions and investor updates, treating the 24-month hold as a working capital requirement rather than a revenue delay; do not attempt to "fix" GAAP revenue recognition, but instead explicitly model the **funding gap** created by the trust mandate. **The Numbers**
To model the true economic impact, you must calculate the **Cash Payback Period** and the **Net Present Value (NPV)** of the trapped capital, not just standard LTV.

*Formula for Cash Payback Period:*
$$ \text{Payback} = \frac{\text{CAC} + (\text{Monthly Service Cost} \times \text{Hold Months})}{\text{Monthly Cash Inflow} - \text{Monthly Service Cost}} $$

**What Flips It, and the Trigger**
The single input that changes the verdict is the **Monthly Service Cost**.
*   **Trigger Value:** If your service cost exceeds **$12.45/month**, you are losing cash every month on every active customer before revenue recognition even begins ($299 revenue is trapped; if you have to fund >$12.45 in costs from operating cash, you are bleeding).
*   **Pivot Date:** Calculate your **Runway Depletion Date**. With $150k cash and 45 homes, if you add 5 new homes/month at $150 cost/home, your monthly burn increases by $750 immediately.
    *   *Rule of Thumb:* If your projected cash runway drops below 30 months, you cannot afford the 24-month hold without raising capital.
