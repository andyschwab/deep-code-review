**The Call**
Reject the current "per-client, full-scope audit" model for mid-sized clients; it guarantees a loss on every deal. Your unit economics only work if you decouple the audit cost from the per-client revenue, either by amortizing a single audit across many clients or by shifting the verification standard. Do not target only enterprise clients yet; first, validate if a shared-audit model or a lighter verification regime is permissible for your specific jurisdiction and client type.

**The Numbers**
*Formula:* Profit = (Price Per Vote × Shareholder Count) – (Allocated Audit Cost + Variable Costs).
*User Inputs:* Price = $2.00; Mid-sized Count = 30,000; Revenue = $60,000.
*Assumptions:*
1.  **Audit Frequency:** ASSUMED 1 audit per client per cycle (Current Model).
2.  **Audit Cost:** $80,000 (Fixed).
3.  **Variable Costs:** ASSUMED $0 for this simplified margin check (ignoring compute/hosting).

*Scenario A: Current Model (1 Client, 1 Audit)*
Revenue: $60,000
Cost: $80,000
**Result:** -$20,000 loss per client.
*Break-even Shareholder Count:* 40,000 voters ($80k / $2). You lose money on any client under 40k shareholders.

*Scenario B: Amortized Model (1 Audit, 10 Clients)*
*Assumption:* One generic smart contract audit covers the platform logic for multiple clients, with only minor configuration reviews per client.
Total Revenue (10 clients × 30k voters × $2): $600,000
Total Audit Cost: $80,000 (plus ASSUMED $2k lightweight review per client = $20k). Total = $100,000.
**Result:** $500,000 gross profit. Margin improves drastically.

*Scenario C: Enterprise Only (1 Client, 100k voters)*
Revenue: $200,000
Cost: $80,000
**Result:** $120,000 profit. Viable, but Total Addressable Market (TAM) shrinks significantly.

**What Flips It, and The Trigger**
The single input that changes the call is **Audit Scope Reusability**. If regulators or clients accept that the *core* smart contract logic needs auditing only once (with subsequent audits focusing only on configuration/parameters), your unit economics flip from negative to positive immediately.
*Trigger:* If you cannot secure a legal or client agreement to amortize the audit cost across multiple elections or clients within 60 days, pivot to an enterprise-only sales strategy.
*Rule of Thumb:* Fixed compliance costs must be spread over at least 3x the break-even volume to ensure a healthy margin buffer.

Whether a "lightweight" configuration review satisfies corporate governance standards or specific securities regulations (e.g., SEC, EU MiCA, or local corporate law) for shareholder voting is a binding legal determination.
