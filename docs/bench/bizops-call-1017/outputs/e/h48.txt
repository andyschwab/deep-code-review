**The Call**
Adopt a hybrid contract structure: a monthly availability retainer covering fixed costs plus a reduced per-dive fee for variable costs and margin. Do not rely solely on pay-per-successful-dive, as weather volatility makes break-even unpredictable; instead, price the risk of downtime into a guaranteed base fee.

**The Numbers**
*Current Unit Economics (Base Case):*
*   Revenue per Dive: $15,000
*   Variable Cost per Dive: $4,000
*   Contribution Margin per Dive: $11,000
*   Fixed Costs: $120,000/month
*   **Break-even Volume:** $120,000 / $11,000 = **10.9 dives** (round to 11 dives).

*Weather Impact Scenario (40% Reduction):*
*   Target Volume: 20 dives/rig.
*   Realized Volume (60% completion): 12 dives.
*   Realized Contribution: 12 * $11,000 = $132,000.
*   Net Profit: $132,000 - $120,000 = $12,000.
*   *Risk:* If weather cuts volume by just 5% more (11 dives), you break even. Any further delay creates a loss. With multiple rigs or slightly worse weather, the pure variable model fails to cover the $120k fixed labor/overhead.

*Proposed Hybrid Model (Illustrative):*
Shift the burden of weather risk to the client via a retainer.
*   **Assumption (ASSUMED):** Clients value guaranteed asset availability enough to pay for 50% of fixed costs upfront.
*   **Retainer Fee:** $60,000/month (covers 50% of fixed costs).
*   **New Fixed Cost to Cover via Dives:** $60,000.
*   **New Break-even Volume:** $60,000 / $11,000 = **5.5 dives**.
*   *Result:* You only need ~6 successful dives to become profitable, drastically reducing weather risk exposure.

**What Flips It, and the Trigger**
The deciding input is the **client's willingness to pay for availability** versus their preference for pure variable spend.
*   **Trigger:** If a client refuses a retainer exceeding $30,000/month, the break-even volume rises to 8.2 dives. If historical weather data shows completion rates consistently below 55% (fewer than 11 dives), the pure pay-per-dive model is financially unsustainable.
*   **Rule of Thumb:** If weather cancellation frequency exceeds 30%, a retainer model is mandatory for survival.
*   **This Week's Step:** If your historical completion rate is below 65%, consider drafting a "Minimum Guarantee" clause requiring payment for 12 dives per month regardless of weather, or a split fee structure ($7,500 retainer + $7,500 per dive).

**Routing Note**
Contractual terms regarding "force majeure" and weather definitions are jurisdiction-specific (North Sea/UK/Norwegian law); have maritime counsel define what constitutes a billable delay versus a non-billable weather event before finalizing the retainer language.
