**The Call**
Do not market therapeutic efficacy claims without clinical data; selling Direct-to-Consumer (DTC) changes your payer, not your regulatory obligations. Proceeding with current claims triggers high-probability FDA enforcement and false advertising liability, endangering your remaining six-month runway. Pivot immediately to "wellness" positioning (non-medical claims) to launch DTC safely, or secure counsel to navigate the medical device pathway before making any efficacy statements.

**The Numbers**
Your runway is a fixed constraint: $45,000 monthly burn × 6 months = **$270,000 total cash remaining**.
*   **Scenario A (Medical Claims without Data):** Probability of FDA Warning Letter or FTC action is high upon first scale. Cost of response (legal + halt sales) typically exceeds $50,000 immediately, reducing runway to <4 months. Revenue delay: Indefinite until compliance is reached.
*   **Scenario B (Wellness Claims / No Efficacy):** Market entry is immediate. Revenue depends on conversion at $2,500 + $150/mo.

    *   *Assumption (ASSUMED):* Small pilot trial costs $150k–$300k and takes 12–18 months.
    *   *Result:* Your current $270k runway is insufficient to fund the trial and survive to reimbursement approval.

**What Flips It, and the Trigger**
The single input that changes the call is the **specific wording of your marketing claims**.
*   **Trigger:** If your website, packaging, or ads mention "stroke rehabilitation," "clinical recovery," "therapy," or "treatment," you are defining the product as a medical device under the Federal Food, Drug, and Cosmetic Act.
*   **Rule of Thumb:** Medical intent = FDA jurisdiction, regardless of whether the buyer is an insurer or a consumer.
*   **Step this week:** If your current copy includes medical terms, strip them immediately and replace with "balance training," "mobility exercise," or "wellness activity" to operate as a general wellness product. If you cannot remove these claims without destroying value, consider Y (pausing launch to consult regulatory counsel).
