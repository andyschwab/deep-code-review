**The Call**
The automated nature of the software does not change the tax liability; the sale of electricity is a taxable event for the building owner (the seller of the commodity), while your 15% commission is taxable service/transaction revenue for GridBalance. You must treat every automated trade as a reportable transaction immediately upon execution, regardless of volume, as Texas generally taxes the retail sale of electricity.

**The Numbers**
*   **GridBalance Revenue (Commission):** $4,500 (total client revenue) × 15% = **$675**.
    *   *Assumption (Base):* The $4,500 represents the gross value of energy sold before your commission.
    *   *Result:* Your reportable gross revenue for the month is $675.
*   **Client Revenue (Energy Sales):** $4,500.
    *   *Assumption (Base):* All 1.2 MWh was sold to taxable end-users or into a market requiring collection.
    *   *Result:* This amount is potentially subject to sales tax collection by the building owner, not you, unless your contract shifts this burden (which is rare and legally complex).
*   **Growth Projection:** 30 buildings + (10 buildings/month × 1 month) = 40 buildings next month.
    *   *Projected Revenue:* $675 × (40/30) = **$900** commission.

**What Flips It, and the Trigger**
The classification flips if the energy is sold to an exempt entity (e.g., a manufacturing plant qualifying for industrial exemption) or if the transaction is classified as a wholesale trade rather than retail.
*   **Trigger:** If >50% of your connected buildings qualify for industrial/resale exemptions, your data tracking requirements change drastically.
*   **Rule of Thumb:** In Texas, electricity sold for residential use is taxable; electricity sold for manufacturing or resale is often exempt.
*   **Step this week:** If your contracts do not explicitly state who is responsible for collecting and remitting sales tax on the energy portion (the building owner vs. GridBalance), consider pausing new onboarding until a Texas tax attorney reviews your Terms of Service to prevent joint liability.

**Routing Note**
Texas electricity taxation involves specific nuances regarding "bundled" vs. "unbundled" services and the distinction between the utility provider and the aggregator. You must consult a Texas CPA or tax attorney to determine: (1) Whether GridBalance is considered the "seller" of the electricity for tax purposes (nexus creation), and (2) The correct application of Texas Comptroller Rule §3.332 regarding electricity sales. Do not rely on general software logic for tax rates; the binding determination of your nexus and collection obligation is a legal conclusion.

**Data Points to Track**
To ensure compliance, your system must log per transaction:
1.  **Buyer Entity Type:** (Residential, Commercial, Industrial) to determine exemption eligibility.
2.  **Point of Delivery:** Specific meter ID to verify jurisdiction (Texas has some municipal utility districts with different rules).
3.  **Gross Energy Amount:** kWh/MWh sold.
4.  **Gross Monetary Value:** Total price before commission.
5.  **Commission Amount:** Your fee.
6.  **Timestamp:** Exact time of trade (critical for rate period verification).
