**The Call**
Do not choose between 3 or 12 years unilaterally; you likely must split the asset. Depreciate the mechanical hardware over its useful life (likely 5–7 years for medical equipment, not necessarily 12) and expense or amortize the software component separately over its 3-year cycle. Attempting to force the entire $120k unit onto a 12-year schedule to inflate current book value creates a high risk of future impairment charges and tax penalties when the software becomes obsolete.

**The Numbers**
*Formula:* Annual Depreciation = (Asset Cost Basis − Salvage Value) / Useful Life.
*User Figures:* Cost = $120,000/unit; Cash = $2.5M; Runway = 18 months.
*Assumptions (ASSUMED):* Hardware constitutes 70% of value ($84k); Software constitutes 30% ($36k). Salvage value is $0 for software, 10% for hardware.

*Note on Runway:* Depreciation is a non-cash expense. It reduces reported net income but does **not** directly reduce your $2.5M cash balance or burn rate. Your runway calculation should exclude depreciation unless you are confusing it with capital expenditure (CapEx) cash outflows which already occurred.

**What Flips It, and the Trigger**
The triggering input is the **capitalization policy threshold** and the **legal structure of the software license**. If the software is sold as a distinct subscription (SaaS) rather than embedded firmware, it may be fully expensed immediately under Section 174 (subject to current tax law nuances regarding R&D amortization) or treated as a separate lease component.
*Trigger Rule:* If the cost to update the software exceeds 15% of the original unit cost in any single year, treat the software as a distinct, shorter-life asset immediately.
*Step:* If your pilot agreement separates "hardware lease" from "software maintenance," consider Y (splitting the books) to avoid a Year 3 cliff.
