**Recommendation: GO-IF**
Proceed with the pilot only if a binding commitment secures at least 20% of the infrastructure grant ($42,000) before the July 1, 2025 start date, and the total project capex is formally capped at $2.35M.

**Primary Failure Modes**
1.  **Grant Shortfall:** If the state grant covers less than 20%, the payback period extends beyond your 48-month threshold, destroying the business case.
2.  **Operational Range Deficit:** Your 15% buffer assumes consistent efficiency. Real-world factors (winter heating, aggressive driving, traffic) can reduce effective range by 20–30%, potentially causing buses to fail mid-route on the longest days without opportunity charging.
3.  **Maintenance Underestimation:** The model assumes savings are purely fuel-based. If high-voltage battery repairs or specialized technician labor costs exceed $25,000 per bus annually, the net savings vanish.

**Kill Criteria**
Terminate the project immediately if, after the first six months of operation, the verified net savings (fuel minus incremental maintenance) annualize to less than $110,000 per bus, or if any single bus fails to complete its assigned route twice due to battery depletion.

**Note**
Your current math shows a ~3.2-year payback ($2.4M capex / $450k annual savings); this leaves almost no margin for error if the grant is missed or energy costs rise. Verify the "5,000 gallons saved" figure against actual telematics from your most efficient diesel buses on these specific routes, not fleet averages.
