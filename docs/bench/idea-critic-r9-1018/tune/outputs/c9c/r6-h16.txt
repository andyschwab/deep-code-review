Do not commit to the full 5,000 sq ft build-out immediately. Instead, execute a **Go-If** decision: Proceed only if you can secure **soft commitments or Letters of Intent (LOIs) for 30% of the space (1,500 sq ft)** from qualified corporate leads before signing any construction contracts. **Three Main Failure Modes:**
1.  **Demand Miscalculation:** The assumption that Fortune 500 firms need 150 sq ft private offices within a co-working environment may be flawed; they often require larger, contiguous suites with private entrances, rendering the 10-office layout unsellable to your target demographic.
2.  **Arithmetic & Cash Flow Error:** Your total cost calculation contains a critical error. 5,000 sq ft at $40/sq ft equals **$200,000**, not $250,000. While this lowers the initial capex, your break-even analysis likely ignores the 6-month ramp-up period where revenue is only at 80% of the 80% target (approx. $17,600/month vs. $22,000/month needed for full run-rate), extending the true payback period beyond 18 months if leasing slips.
3.  **Premium Erosion:** The projected $5/sq ft premium over open-desk rates assumes a scarcity of private offices in the 5-mile radius. If a competitor launches a similar product or if economic conditions force Fortune 500 firms to reduce real estate footprints, you will be forced to discount rates, destroying the projected ROI.

**Stop/Pivot Trigger:**
**Kill Criterion:** If signed leases do not reach **40% occupancy (2,000 sq ft)** by **31 March 2025** (three months post-construction start), immediately pivot the remaining unfinished space back to open-desk or collaborative layouts to minimize further capex exposure.
