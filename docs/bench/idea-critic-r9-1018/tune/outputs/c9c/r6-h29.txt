**Recommendation: GO-IF**
Proceed with a **90-day pilot in the North Zone only** (starting Oct 1, 2025), capped at 500 subscribers. Do not launch across all three zones immediately. The financial model relies on unverified assumptions that a subscription mechanic will behave like a one-off transaction; a full rollout exposes the company to catastrophic margin erosion if refund rates or labor costs deviate slightly from the historical baseline.

**Three Biggest Failure Modes**
1.  **Adverse Selection & Frequency Spike:** The model assumes 2.8% refunds and static usage. Subscription users may maximize value by scheduling all three visits monthly regardless of need, or "churn and burn" (subscribe, deep clean, demand refund, cancel). If the refund rate rises to just 8% (still low for a guarantee) or average visits per user hits 2.5, the $13.50/hr labor cost could exceed the $49 revenue, making every subscriber money-losing.
2.  **Labor Capacity & Cost Inflation:** A 15% conversion (1,800 households) demanding up to 3 visits/month creates 5,400 potential monthly visits. Your 4% operating cost increase assumption likely underestimates the linear labor scaling required. If you cannot staff this without overtime or hiring at higher market rates, margins will collapse before the refund metric even triggers.
3.  **Competitor Price War:** If competitors match the guarantee within 60 days, they may undercut the $49 price point. Since your differentiation is now neutralized, you face a choice between bleeding market share or entering a price war your current cost structure cannot sustain.

**Kill Criteria**
Halt or pivot the program immediately if: **Refund costs exceed 5% of subscription revenue OR average labor cost per subscriber exceeds $35/month for two consecutive months during the pilot.**

*Note: The projected $882k revenue assumes 100% retention and perfect capacity utilization, which is unrealistic for a new subscription tier. The pilot must validate actual churn and visit frequency before scaling.*
