**Recommendation: Go, but as a capped, time-boxed pilot with one client segment, not a full immediate shift.**

Launch the 18% revenue-share model with a single pilot group of 5 clients for 90 days. Do not roll out to all 45 monthly placements yet. This validates the 90% acceptance assumption and the $1,200 onboarding cost before exposing the entire pipeline to competitor undercutting or client rejection. If the pilot succeeds, scale gradually; if it fails, the downside is contained to ~15 placements rather than the full annual volume.

**Three Biggest Failure Modes:**

1.  **Acceptance Rate Collapse:** Your model assumes 90% client acceptance. If even 30% of clients reject the new terms (preferring the predictable $12k flat fee or switching to the $10k competitors), your volume could drop from 45 to ~31 placements. At $15.3k average, 31 placements yield ~$474k/month versus the current $540k/month, immediately erasing the projected revenue gain and failing to cover the increased $8k marketing spend.
2.  **Salary Base Erosion:** The math relies on an $85k average salary. If the economic climate forces clients to hire at lower levels (e.g., $70k average), your fee drops to $12.6k. While slightly higher than the flat fee, this narrow margin may not justify the added administrative complexity of tracking salaries and collecting variable payments, especially if clients negotiate the percentage down to 15%.
3.  **Hidden Cash-Flow & Collection Risk:** Moving to revenue share shifts cash-flow risk to you. You no longer get paid upon placement; you must wait for the client's payroll cycles and verify employment duration. If a candidate leaves before 90 days (common in tech), do you get pro-rated pay or nothing? The $1,200 onboarding cost is sunk regardless. Additionally, auditing client payroll data to verify the 18% adds legal and operational friction not present in flat-fee invoicing.

**Kill Criteria:**

Terminate the pilot and revert to the flat-fee model if, after 90 days:
*   **Client Acceptance Rate** is below 75% (instead of the projected 90%).
*   **Net Revenue Per Placement** (after accounting for collection delays and disputes) falls below $13,500.
*   **Candidate Churn** (leaving before 90 days) exceeds 15%, triggering excessive pro-rated fee reductions or total loss of fees.

*Note: The arithmetic holds only if volume remains stable; the primary risk is volume contraction due to price sensitivity. Ensure contracts explicitly define "first-year salary" and termination clauses to prevent revenue leakage.*
