**Recommendation: GO-IF**
Proceed with a capped, time-boxed pilot launch only if you first secure a committed pre-order volume representing at least 40% of the projected Year 1 sales. Do not commit the full $2 million equipment spend until this threshold is met.

**Three Biggest Failure Modes:**
1.  **Revenue Math Error:** Your projection of $14M is incorrect. A 30% price increase on $12M yields $15.6M; a subsequent 10% volume drop applies to that new base, resulting in ~$14.04M *only if* the volume drop is purely linear. However, you omitted the $0.25/bag packaging cost increase. On current volumes, this alone could erase ~$300k–$400k in margin, significantly lowering the net profit below your implicit expectations and extending the payback period on the $2M investment beyond 3 years.
2.  **Unverified Demand Assumption:** You explicitly state there is "no focus-group data" and the acceptance rate is unknown. Launching a premium product with a 30% price hike without validation risks a volume collapse far exceeding the conservative 10% estimate. If the niche for single-protein is smaller than assumed, volume could drop 30–40%, turning the revenue gain into a net loss.
3.  **Sunk Cost in Specialized Equipment:** The $2M investment in new equipment for a single-protein line creates high fixed costs and low flexibility. If the product fails, this capital cannot be easily repurposed for mixed-protein kibble, creating a stranded asset that drags down overall company profitability.

**Kill Criteria:**
Terminate or pivot the project if **actual uptake is less than 5% of total sales volume after 6 months** OR if the **gross margin per bag fails to exceed the legacy product's margin by at least 15% after accounting for the new packaging and amortization costs.**

*Note: Your current revenue calculation ($14M) appears slightly optimistic given the unmodeled packaging cost drag; re-run the P&L with the $0.25 variable cost included before finalizing the pilot budget.*
*Ensure the "pre-order" validation is binding, not just expressions of interest, to truly de-risk the equipment spend.*
