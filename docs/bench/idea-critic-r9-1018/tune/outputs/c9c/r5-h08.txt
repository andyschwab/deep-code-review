**Recommendation: No-Go (Hold)**
Do not proceed to the CFO with this proposal in its current form. The financial thesis collapses under basic scrutiny: a $2.5M investment yielding $300k in annual savings results in an **8.3-year simple payback**, not 8.5, but more critically, an ~12% unlevered return is likely below your cost of capital for a project with significant execution risk. Without including revenue growth from the 15% demand increase in the return calculation, the project looks like a cost center with a decade-long horizon rather than a growth engine. You must re-model the ROI to include the *margin on new volume* enabled by the line, not just the fee savings, before seeking approval.

**Top Three Failure Modes**
1.  **Demand/Utilization Shortfall:** If canned-beer demand grows at <8% instead of 15%, utilization will drop below the 85% target, extending payback beyond 12 years and stranding capacity.
2.  **Hidden CapEx & Ramp Delays:** The $2.5M likely excludes installation, facility upgrades (compressed air, drainage), and working capital. A 6-month delay in vendor lead-time or ramp-up pushes the first positive cash flow deep into 2027, destroying NPV.
3.  **Margin Erosion:** The model assumes selling extra volume at "current margin." In-house canning introduces variable costs (labor, maintenance, can inventory) that may exceed the current outsourced fee per unit if efficiency targets aren't met immediately, turning volume growth into a loss leader.

**Kill Criteria & Pivot Triggers**
*   **Halt Trigger:** If the all-in installed cost exceeds **$2.8M** (12% buffer) or if the vendor cannot guarantee delivery before **1 May 2025** (threatening the Q4 2026 utilization target), stop the project.
*   **Pivot Trigger:** If actual line utilization is **<60% after six months** of operation (ending Q2 2027), immediately pivot to selling excess capacity to contract brands or pause further capital deployment in packaging.
