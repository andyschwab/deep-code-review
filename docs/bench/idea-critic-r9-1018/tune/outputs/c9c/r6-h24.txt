**Recommendation: Proceed with a capped, conditional pilot (Go-If).**
Do not commit the full $2.5M immediately. Instead, authorize the Riverton launch (May 15) as a time-boxed proof of concept with a strict capital cap of $900k. Delay Lakeview and Hillcrest commitments until Riverton validates the unit economics against the unknown insurance and surcharge variables. This approach secures the first-mover advantage in Riverton while preventing a cascading loss across three cities if the municipal policy shifts unfavorably.

**Top Three Failure Modes:**
1.  **Regulatory Cost Shock:** The debated traffic-management policy enacts the $0.05/bike surcharge plus the hinted $0.12/ride insurance, eroding the $3.50 fare margin below profitability before volume scales.
2.  **Revenue Overestimation:** The assumed 10,000 rides/month/city fails to materialize due to seasonality or local adoption friction, leaving high fixed costs (permits/staffing) uncovered by actual revenue.
3.  **Maintenance Underpricing:** The $150/bike/month maintenance estimate proves insufficient for the specific wear-and-tear of these three municipalities, creating a hidden operational drag that compounds with low ridership.

**Abort/Re-scale Trigger:**
Terminate expansion to Lakeview and Hillcrest, and initiate a wind-down of Riverton, if **net operating loss exceeds $100k in any single city after the first three months** OR if **ridership averages below 4,000 rides/month (40% of target)** over the same period.

*Note: This plan treats the missing insurance premium and potential surcharge as critical risks rather than known costs, necessitating the phased rollout. The $900k cap covers Riverton's hard costs while reserving liquidity for unforeseen regulatory fees.*
