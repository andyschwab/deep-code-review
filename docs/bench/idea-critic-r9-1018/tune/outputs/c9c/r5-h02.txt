**Recommendation: Do not launch the full pilot as planned.** Proceed only with a **capacity-constrained beta** capped at 5,000 subscribers for the first three months, while securing external fulfillment partners for any demand exceeding that limit.

**Why this plan fails in its current form:**
1.  **Immediate Capacity Breach:** You plan to fulfill 7,000 boxes on day one against a hard production limit of 5,000 boxes/month. This creates an instant backlog of 2,000 unfulfilled orders monthly, guaranteeing service failure and reputational damage before the pilot truly begins.
2.  **Conversion & Acquisition Math Error:** Your math is internally contradictory. A 4% conversion of 200,000 visitors yields 8,000 customers, not the 7,000 targeted (which is feasible), but your marketing budget of $100,000 at $10 CPA only buys 10,000 *visits*, not customers. To get 7,000 *subscribers* via paid marketing at $10 CPA, you need $70,000 just for acquisition, leaving only $30,000 for brand building. If relying on organic conversion, you need 175,000 visitors to hit 7,000 subs at 4%; if relying on paid, your CPA assumption likely underestimates the cost to acquire a *paying* subscriber versus a lead.
3.  **Packaging Budget Mismatch:** You budgeted $150,000 for 500,000 units ($0.30/unit). However, a 12-month pilot with 7,000 subs requires 84,000 boxes (7,000 × 12), not 500,000. If you meant 500,000 *units of content* inside boxes, the box cost itself is under-calculated for the actual volume of 84,000 physical shipments. Conversely, if you are printing 500,000 boxes upfront for economies of scale, you are tying up cash in inventory for a pilot with unknown churn.

**Kill Criteria (Stop/Pivot Triggers):**
*   **Capacity Breach:** If pre-orders exceed 5,000 units before a verified third-party logistics (3PL) contract is signed to cover the overflow, **stop** taking new orders immediately.
*   **Churn Threshold:** If monthly churn exceeds **6%** in any of the first three months (annualized >50%, making the 80% renewal goal mathematically impossible), **pivot** to a retention-focused model or halt expansion.
*   **CAC Reality:** If the actual Cost Per Acquisition exceeds **$25** by the end of Month 1, **stop** paid marketing spend and reassess unit economics.

*Note: The projected revenue of $540,000 assumes 100% retention for the year; at 80% renewal, actual Year 1 revenue will be significantly lower due to mid-year dropouts.*
