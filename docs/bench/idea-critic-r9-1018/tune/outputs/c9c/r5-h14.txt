**Recommendation:** Do not approve the full launch yet. Proceed only with a **3-month pilot** capped at $150,000 in incremental setup costs, conditional on securing a fixed-price quote for night-shift security and a written utility surcharge estimate. The current break-even calculation is materially flawed because it excludes these two significant operating expenses, rendering the 14-month payback period unreliable.

**Three Biggest Failure Modes:**
1.  **Margin Erosion from Unpriced Variables:** Night-shift security and 24-hour utility surcharges could easily consume 30–40% of the projected $240,000 monthly cash flow. If night security costs match daytime pro-rata levels (~$9k/month) and utilities surge by 25%, the break-even point likely extends beyond 24 months, destroying the investment thesis.
2.  **Occupancy Volatility:** The model assumes 80% average occupancy ($240k revenue) immediately. If actual sign-ups lag at 50% occupancy ($150k revenue) for the first year due to safety concerns or limited demand for overnight work, the venture will burn cash rapidly without covering the fixed $250k annual lease.
3.  **Liability and Security Incidents:** A single major security incident during unstaffed or minimally staffed night hours could result in lawsuits or reputational damage that outweighs the incremental revenue, especially if the current $9k daytime contract does not cover overnight liabilities.

**Kill/Pivot Criterion:** Terminate or pivot the 24-hour tier if **net operating income remains negative after six months** of operation, specifically if verified occupancy averages below **65%** (not 50%) once all night-shift security and utility costs are fully realized.
