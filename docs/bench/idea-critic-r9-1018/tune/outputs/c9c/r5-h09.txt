**Recommendation:** Do not scale to the full city yet; instead, extend the pilot for 90 days in the current three districts specifically to measure post-promotion adoption rates and battery degradation, capping additional spend at $50,000.

**Three Biggest Failure Modes:**
1.  **Adoption Cliff:** User swapping frequency drops below 4.5 swaps/station/day once the promotional discount ends, eliminating the projected revenue uplift.
2.  **Hidden Degradation Cost:** Frequent swapping accelerates battery wear beyond the 800-cycle assumption, increasing replacement costs by >20% and eroding margins.
3.  **Unit Economics Mismatch:** The $18,600 revenue projection assumes linear growth from downtime reduction, ignoring that fixed operational costs (station maintenance, logistics) may outpace revenue if density does not increase.

**Kill-Switch Metric:** Halt expansion immediately if, after 60 days of post-promotion data (post-2025-02-01), the net profit per station averages less than $250/month (annualized run rate < $3,000/station), failing to cover the pro-rated capital cost.

**Note on Numbers:** Your revenue math implies ~74,400 additional rides/month ($18,600 / $0.25), which requires a massive volume increase that seems disproportionate to a 12% efficiency gain unless your current ride base is already very large; please verify the total ride count used to derive that $18,600 figure.
