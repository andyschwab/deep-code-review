**Recommendation: Go ahead with a capped, time-boxed pilot at one location only.**

Do not roll out to all three locations immediately. Launch a 90-day evening pilot at your highest-traffic site starting July 1. Cap total initial spend at $26,000 (one oven rig and proportional remodel). This validates the "evening crowd" assumption and operational fit before committing the full $60,000. If the pilot hits revenue targets, expand to the remaining two locations in Q4.

**Main Failure Modes & Risks:**
1.  **Cannibalization vs. Addition:** Your model assumes 30% of daytime customers stay *plus* 15% new traffic. If daytime customers simply shift their spending to the evening (cannibalization) without bringing new guests, you incur higher labor and utility costs for the same total revenue, shrinking margins.
2.  **Labor & Operational Drag:** Evening service requires different staffing skills and extends operating hours. If you cannot hire reliable evening staff or if kitchen turnover slows morning prep, core bakery revenue ($45k/day) could suffer.
3.  **Demand Overestimation:** The $5,200/week projection relies heavily on the "new evening crowd." If Friday-Saturday demand from non-regulars is weak, you will fail to cover the fixed costs of the extended hours, let alone repay the $60k investment.
4.  **Undercapitalized Remodel:** The $8,000 remodel budget across three sites (~$2,600 each) may be insufficient for necessary ventilation, grease traps, or health code compliance for hot-line cooking, leading to costly delays or fines.

**Kill Criteria (Stop/Pivot Triggers):**
Stop the pilot and do not expand if **either** of the following occurs:
*   **Revenue Threshold:** Net pizza revenue averages below **$2,000 per week** for two consecutive months during the pilot.
*   **Core Business Impact:** Morning bakery sales drop by more than **5%** compared to the same period last year, attributed to evening operational fatigue or staffing conflicts.

**Leading Metric to Watch:**
Track the **"New Guest Ratio"** (percentage of evening customers who did not visit between 6 AM–4 PM). If this stays below 40% after 60 days, the concept is likely cannibalizing existing sales rather than growing the business.

*Note: The $52k + $8k = $60k total investment requires ~29 weeks of full projected sales ($5.2k/wk) just to break even on revenue, ignoring labor/food costs; ensure your margin calculations support a reasonable payback period.*
