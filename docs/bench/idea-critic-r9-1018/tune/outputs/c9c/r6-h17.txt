**Recommendation:** Proceed with a **capped, time-boxed pilot** rather than a full July 1 rollout. Commit only $65,000 (25% of the budget) to launch in a single region or with one therapist cluster for 90 days. Do not authorize the full $260,000 spend until the pilot validates that virtual visits do not cannibalize higher-margin in-person revenue and that therapist capacity holds under mixed loads.

**How this plan could fail:**
1.  **Revenue Dilution:** If the 18% switching rate draws from your highest-frequency in-person patients rather than new or low-frequency ones, the drop from $115 to $98 per visit reduces total monthly revenue despite volume stability.
2.  **Capacity Illusion:** Your assumption that session length remains static may be false; virtual visits often require additional administrative time for tech troubleshooting and documentation, effectively reducing therapist capacity and forcing overtime or hiring costs not modeled.
3.  **Marketing Misfire:** The projected 420 new patients rely on a regional push that may not convert if the tele-health value proposition is weaker than in-person care for physiotherapy, leaving you with high fixed costs and low utilization.

**Kill Criteria:**
Terminate the project immediately if, after the 90-day pilot: (1) the net revenue per therapist hour (blending virtual and in-person) drops by more than 8% compared to the current baseline, or (2) the conversion rate of existing patients to virtual exceeds 22% *and* the churn rate of those virtual patients exceeds 15% within their first three visits.

*Note: The projected payback period relies heavily on the 420 new patients; verify this acquisition cost separately before scaling. Ensure the pilot explicitly measures administrative time per virtual session to validate the capacity assumption.*
