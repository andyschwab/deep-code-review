**Recommendation: GO-IF**
Proceed only if the licensing fee is contractually capped at $8,000 before any equipment spend, and the pilot is structured as a 6-month gate rather than a committed 12-month run.

**Three Biggest Failure Modes**
1.  **Margin Collapse:** The "around $8,000" licensing fee is a recurring annual cost or scales with volume, not a one-time fee. If recurring, it directly erodes the 30% distribution margin, pushing the break-even point significantly higher than 45,000 cases and turning the projected profit into a loss.
2.  **Sales Velocity Mismatch:** Selling 60,000 cases in 12 months requires moving ~5,000 cases monthly immediately. Hard seltzer markets are saturated; if the ramp-up takes 3–4 months to gain traction, you will miss the annual target even if the product is well-liked, leaving you with excess inventory and underutilized line capacity.
3.  **Retrofit Underestimation:** The $30,000 retrofit estimate often excludes downtime costs and regulatory re-certification for a new flavor profile. If the line is down for 3 weeks instead of 1 week for integration, the lost production capacity from existing SKUs may outweigh the new product's initial margin.

**Kill Criterion**
Stop the project if cumulative sales are below **18,000 cases** (30% of the annual target) after **6 months**, or if the final licensing agreement exceeds **$10,000** in total first-year cost.

**Notes**
Your current break-even math assumes the license is a sunk upfront cost; verify if it is actually an operating expense.
Secure the licensing terms in writing before November 15 to avoid halting production post-retrofit.
