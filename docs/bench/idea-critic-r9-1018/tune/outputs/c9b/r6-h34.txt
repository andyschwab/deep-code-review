**Recommendation: GO-IF**
Proceed with a capped, 6-month pilot limited to 15% of forecast volume (18k units) rather than a full FY2025 rollout. This limits exposure to the unverified supplier capacity and the unpriced shipping surcharge while testing brand tolerance. Do not commit the full $500k tooling spend until the pilot validates the supplier's linear scaling claim and the net margin impact of the surcharge.

**Main Failure Modes**
1.  **Supplier Bottleneck:** The assumption that fabric capacity scales linearly from 80k to 150k yards without cost is unverified; if the supplier hits a hard ceiling or demands price hikes, lead times will balloon beyond the 7-day promise, triggering the $3 surcharge on more than 30% of orders and destroying the $250k warehousing savings.
2.  **Margin Erosion:** The model excludes the shipping surcharge for late orders. If churn rises or late orders exceed 30%, the combined cost of surcharges and lost lifetime value will exceed the warehousing savings, extending the break-even point well beyond 24 months.
3.  **Brand Churn:** Parents may reject the wait time regardless of product quality. If the "made-to-order" narrative fails to offset the delay, sales volume will drop below the 120k forecast, leaving the company with high fixed tooling costs and lower inventory turnover.

**Kill Criteria (Stop/Pivot Triggers)**
Stop the expansion and revert to the current model if either of the following occurs within the first 6 months:
*   **Operational Trigger:** The supplier fails to deliver >95% of fabric orders within the agreed window for two consecutive months, indicating the linear capacity assumption is false.
*   **Financial Trigger:** The effective cost per unit (including any shipping surcharges and adjusted for actual sales volume) exceeds the current model's cost by more than $1.50, or if the order cancellation rate due to wait times exceeds 8%.

*Note: You must verify the supplier's actual maximum capacity and cost structure before signing any tooling contracts. The $3 surcharge impact must be modeled using a worst-case scenario (e.g., 50% late orders) to ensure solvency.*
