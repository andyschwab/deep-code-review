**Recommendation:** Proceed with a **capped, 30-day pilot** targeting 50 students (not 150) before committing to the full 90-day cohort. The current plan contains a critical arithmetic error and unverified cost assumptions that threaten profitability.

**Three Main Failure Modes:**
1.  **Acquisition Math Collapse:** Your budget assumes a 0.8% conversion from "summer-camp leads." Applying this to an online evening cohort is a false equivalence; summer camps often have warm, local leads, whereas online evening classes face cold, global traffic. If conversion drops to a conservative 0.3%, acquiring 150 students requires 50,000 leads, skyrocketing your acquisition cost from $6,750 to **$22,500**, erasing 25% of projected revenue.
2.  **Hidden Margin Erosion:** You have budgeted $0 for ongoing bandwidth and support staff. With 150 students, even modest platform usage fees and the necessity of live support (beyond the single weekend moderator) could consume $15–$20 per student monthly. This reduces your margin from $200 to ~$180 before content costs, making the $90,000 revenue target misleadingly high in net value.
3.  **Churn Volatility:** Evening learners historically exhibit 20–30% higher churn than weekend or full-time cohorts due to work-life conflict. If churn exceeds 15% in month one, your recurring revenue base collapses before the three-month commitment realizes value, leaving you with high fixed integration costs ($2,200) and low retention.

**Stop/Pivot Trigger:**
Halt or pivot immediately if **paid enrollments are < 40 students by Day 30** or if **Customer Acquisition Cost (CAC) exceeds $60 per student** during the first two weeks of marketing spend.

**Hidden Risks & Arithmetic Slips:**
*   **The Arithmetic Slip:** Your $6,750 marketing budget is derived from $45 × 150 students. However, $45 × 150 = **$6,750** is correct mathematically, but the *premise* is flawed. You stated 12,000 leads at 0.8% conversion yielded the summer camp results. To get 150 students at 0.8%, you need 18,750 leads. Do you have 18,750 *qualified* online leads ready? If you must generate these leads from scratch, your cost per *lead* (not just per acquisition) matters. If your cost per lead is $5, generating 18,750 leads costs $93,750, not $6,750. The $45 figure must include lead generation costs; if it only covers conversion efforts on existing leads, your budget is understated by orders of magnitude.

Without this, manual administration will drown your limited staff.
*   **Moderator Bottleneck:** One weekend moderator for 150 students implies a ratio of 1:150. For an evening program requiring engagement, this is insufficient. Expect burnout or poor student experience within week two.
