**Recommendation:** Proceed with a **capped, time-boxed pilot** rather than a full July 1 launch. Commit only $65,000 (25% of the requested budget) to a 90-day proof-of-concept targeting 5% conversion of existing patients. Do not authorize the full $260,000 rollout until the pilot validates that virtual visits do not cannibalize higher-revenue in-person slots and that therapist capacity holds without degradation.

**Main Failure Modes:**
1.  **Cannibalization Risk:** If the 18% of patients switching to tele-health ($98/visit) are simply downgrading from in-person visits ($115/visit) rather than representing new volume or necessary retention, revenue drops by ~$54,000/month immediately, turning the "growth" into a margin crisis.
2.  **Capacity Illusion:** The assumption that session lengths and throughput remain identical is fragile; virtual visits often require different administrative overhead or technical troubleshooting, potentially reducing total daily appointments and creating a bottleneck that stalls the projected 420 new patients.
3.  **Adoption Shortfall:** If the conversion rate of existing patients is below 8% (less than half your projection) due to demographic mismatch or preference for hands-on therapy, the volume required to amortize the $260,000 fixed cost will not materialize, extending the break-even point beyond the project's viable horizon.

**Kill Criteria:**
Terminate the project if, by the end of the 90-day pilot, the **net revenue per therapist hour** (blending in-person and virtual) drops below **$95**, or if the **conversion rate of existing patients** remains under **6%**. These thresholds ensure you stop before the full capital outlay is sunk into a model that erodes core profitability.
