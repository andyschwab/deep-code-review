**Recommendation: Proceed with a capped pilot only (Go-If), not full launch.**
Do not commit to the $4.5M capital outlay or the Q2 2026 full launch yet. Your revenue math is correct ($30M), but the plan relies on unverified demand and flawed cost comparisons. Authorize a small-batch pilot using contract manufacturing (avoiding immediate CNC capex) to validate the 30% switch rate and true logistics costs. Make the full investment contingent on hitting specific kill criteria below.

**Three Biggest Failure Modes:**
1.  **Cannibalization Without Net Growth:** If the 30% switch assumption is wrong and flat-pack sales merely replace assembled units without attracting new apartment renters, revenue stays flat while margins compress due to lower pricing power, failing to cover the new fixed costs.
2.  **Logistics & Waste Erasure:** Your $130 cost excludes real pallet logistics and MDF disposal fees. If these unknowns add >$15/unit, your gross margin drops from $120 to <$105, destroying the 18% ROI target.
3.  **False Cost Advantage:** Comparing your $130 cost to Competitor X's $150 price is invalid if their shipping is significantly lower. If customers pay $40 extra for your shipping but only $10 for theirs, your effective price is higher, killing demand.

**Kill Criteria (Stop the project if any occur):**
*   **Pilot Unit Cost > $145:** If the fully loaded cost (including logistics/waste) in the pilot exceeds $145, the margin buffer is too thin to sustain the 18% ROI.
*   **Confirmed Demand < 40,000 Units:** If the pilot and pre-orders show annualized demand below 40k units (far below the 120k target), the volume cannot support the CNC investment.
*   **Cannibalization Rate > 40%:** If more than 40% of flat-pack buyers are existing customers downgrading from assembled units rather than new renters, the net revenue gain is insufficient.
