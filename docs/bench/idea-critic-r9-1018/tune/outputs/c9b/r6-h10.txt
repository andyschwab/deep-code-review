**Recommendation: GO-IF**
Proceed only if the licensing fee is contractually capped at $8,000 before any equipment spend, and the pilot is structured as a 6-month "kill gate" rather than a committed 12-month run. The current math is fragile; a 10% variance in fees or margin destroys the break-even advantage.

**Three Biggest Failure Modes**
1.  **Margin Erosion:** The assumption that distribution margin stays at 30% is unverified for a new, unproven category. If distributors demand 35–40% to carry a new hard seltzer, your break-even jumps from 45,000 to ~53,000 cases, making the 60,000 target dangerously thin.
2.  **Hidden Licensing Costs:** "Around $8,000" is not a budget line; it is a risk. If the final fee includes per-case royalties or escalates to $15,000, the upfront capital requirement swells, pushing break-even beyond the pilot's sales capacity.
3.  **Production Delay Cascades:** Starting production Nov 15, 2025, leaves no buffer for retrofit delays. A 4-week slip pushes launch into Q1 (post-holiday slump), potentially missing the critical summer sales window required to hit the 60,000 case target within 12 months.

**Kill Criterion**
Terminate the project immediately if **cumulative sales are below 22,500 cases (50% of target) after the first 6 months**, or if the **fully loaded gross margin falls below 25%** after accounting for the final licensing agreement.
