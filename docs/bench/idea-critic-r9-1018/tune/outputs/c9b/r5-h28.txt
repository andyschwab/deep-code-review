**Recommendation:** Proceed with a **capped, phased rollout** (10 stores initially) rather than a full 42-store launch on March 1, 2025. While the pilot's 18% margin is promising, extrapolating it directly to all locations ignores the unverified volatility of niche genre resale prices and the risk of inventory saturation. A phased approach limits exposure to the $185,000 budget while validating the assumption that 20% foot traffic participation and $8 average trade-in value hold true outside the unique downtown Seattle demographic.

**Three Biggest Failure Modes:**
1.  **Financial (Inventory Saturation):** Niche genres with high trade-in volume but low resale velocity tie up capital and storage space, causing the net margin to collapse below the 12% threshold as holding costs exceed revenue.
2.  **Operational (Labor Variance):** The standardized labor cost model fails in smaller stores where staff cannot dedicate specific hours to processing, leading to backlogs, customer friction, and effective labor costs doubling the pilot's $2,340 baseline.
3.  **Brand (Quality Control Inconsistency):** Decentralized cleaning and grading across 42 locations leads to inconsistent product quality, eroding customer trust and devaluing the " Inkwell Books" brand promise compared to the controlled pilot environment.

**Kill Criteria (Stop/Redesign Triggers):**
*   **Net Margin < 12%** for two consecutive months post-launch in any phase.
*   **Inventory Turnover Ratio < 0.8** (books sitting >45 days) for niche genres after the first quarter.
*   **Customer Complaint Rate** regarding trade-in valuation or book quality exceeds 2% of total transactions.

*Note: The current proposal assumes static resale prices; a dynamic pricing mechanism for slow-moving genres is essential before full commitment. The $185k budget should be treated as a hard cap for the initial phase, not a sunk cost for the entire chain.*
