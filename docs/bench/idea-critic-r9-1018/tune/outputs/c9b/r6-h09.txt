**Recommendation: Do not proceed with the full replacement of physical classrooms.**

The proposal contains a critical financial error and an unsupported operational assumption that makes the "replace entirely" strategy high-risk. Your total Year 1 cost is not $900k; it is **$1.12 million** ($800k dev + $120k maintenance + $200k marketing). Against a projected revenue increase of only $420k (and potential revenue loss from churn), this creates an immediate net loss position, not a growth engine. Furthermore, assuming a self-paced app will achieve a 90% completion rate—matching high-touch in-person instruction—contradicts industry standards for asynchronous learning, where completion rates often hover between 10–20%. If completion drops, retention will follow, invalidating the 80% student retention assumption.

**Primary Failure Modes:**
1.  **Financial Miscalculation:** The project is undercapitalized by $220k in Year 1 alone, ignoring the true cash burn required to reach the breakeven point.
2.  **Engagement Collapse:** Without live instructors, completion rates will likely plummet, causing churn to exceed the 20% buffer and eroding the existing $2.1M revenue base.
3.  **Acquisition Cost Underestimation:** The $200k marketing budget may be insufficient to replace the organic foot traffic and local reputation driving current enrollment.

**Kill Criterion:**
Terminate the project if, after 12 months, **net new revenue is less than $150,000**. This metric correctly accounts for both failed acquisition and unexpected churn from the existing base.

*Note: Before presenting, recalculate the 5-year ROI using the corrected $1.12M Year 1 cost and a conservative 40% app completion rate to stress-test solvency.*
*Consider a hybrid pilot (app + reduced physical sites) rather than a full replacement to validate completion rates before closing classrooms.*
