**Recommendation: Go-Forward with Modified Constraints (Go-If)**

Proceed with the pilot only if the following conditions are met before the July 1, 2025 start date:
1.  **Grant Verification:** Secure a written commitment for at least 25% of infrastructure costs. If the grant falls below this, the project pauses until a revised ROI is approved.
2.  **Range Validation:** Confirm via manufacturer data that the specific bus model maintains ≥140 miles range at 100% payload in the coldest expected winter temperature, exceeding your 132-mile daily requirement.
3.  **Cap Adjustment:** Treat the $2.4M capex as a hard ceiling. If charging infrastructure bids exceed $210k, reduce the pilot scope to four buses rather than exceeding the budget.

**Primary Failure Modes**
*   **Grant Shortfall:** If the state covers <20% of infrastructure costs, the payback period extends beyond 48 months, destroying the business case.
*   **Real-World Range Deficit:** Your 15% buffer assumes consistent driving. Heavy passenger loads, HVAC usage in extreme weather, or unexpected detours could drain batteries before route completion, causing service failures.
*   **Maintenance Underestimation:** Electric buses have lower fuel costs but higher specialized maintenance costs early in their lifecycle; if technician training and parts availability lag, downtime will erase fuel savings.

**Kill Criteria**
Terminate the project immediately if, after 12 months of operation:
*   The actual average daily range achievable without mid-day charging drops below 125 miles.
*   The total operational cost savings (fuel + maintenance) fall below $350,000 annually for the three-bus fleet (vs. the projected $450k).
*   The grant reimbursement is denied or reduced such that the total project payback period exceeds 48 months based on actual Year 1 spend.

*Note: Your diesel savings calculation assumes static fuel prices; a drop in diesel prices to ~$3.20/gallon would extend payback to nearly 5 years even with the full grant.*
*Ensure the "buffer" accounts for battery degradation over the 3-year period, not just day-one performance.*
