**Recommendation: GO-IF**
Proceed with the launch only if you cap the initial marketing spend to acquire the first 500 trial users and secure a written commitment to cover up to $20,000 in unexpected integration/support costs. Do not present the $288,000 figure as a baseline forecast to the board; present it strictly as a "theoretical maximum" while modeling a conservative scenario based on a 5–8% conversion rate (approx. $60k–$95k annual revenue). Your current math contains a critical error: 25% of 3,000 *paying* members implies you need 12,000 *trialists*, which equals 100% of your total monthly traffic signing up for a trial immediately. Even if you meant 25% of a smaller trial pool, aiming for 3,000 paid members (25% of total traffic) is statistically improbable given the competitor's 5% benchmark.

**Main Failure Modes**
1.  **Traffic Bottleneck:** You need 12,000 *new* trial sign-ups every month just to sustain the pipeline for 3,000 active paid members (assuming 25% conversion). With only 12,000 *total* visitors, this requires impossible saturation.
2.  **Unit Economics Collapse:** Excluding payment gateway fees (~3%) and tech support will likely turn the $8/month tier unprofitable once churn and support tickets are factored in.
3.  **Conversion Delusion:** Assuming a 5x higher conversion rate than a established competitor ($8 vs $10 price difference rarely drives a 20 percentage point lift) risks over-promising revenue to the board.

**Pre-agreed Stop/Re-engineer Trigger**
 Halt the full rollout or re-engineer the tier if, after 60 days post-launch:
*   **Trial-to-Paid Conversion** is below **8%** (not 25%); OR
*   **Monthly Churn** exceeds **7%** (implying a member lifetime of <15 months, which jeopardizes recovering the $15k integration cost).

*Note: Your revenue calculation ($8 × 3,000 × 12) is correct for the volume, but the volume assumption contradicts your traffic data. Verify the competitor's 5% figure before the meeting, as it strengthens the case for a conservative target.*
