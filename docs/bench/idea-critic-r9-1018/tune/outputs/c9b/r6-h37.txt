**Recommendation:** Do not proceed with a full switch. Instead, propose a **controlled pilot** to 20% of new clients for one quarter, keeping the flat fee for the rest. The current arithmetic contains a critical error that turns the projected revenue gain into a likely loss, and the assumption of 90% client acceptance is unsupported by the competitive landscape you described.

**Three Biggest Failure Modes:**

1.  **Fatal Arithmetic Error (Hidden Cost):** Your projection ignores the variable cost of the new model. You calculated revenue based on 45 placements × $15,300 = $688,500/month. However, you failed to subtract the new $1,200 onboarding cost *per candidate* from the margin, nor did you account for the $8,000 marketing increase.
    *   *Current Monthly Profit (approx):* (45 × $12,000) = $540,000 (ignoring existing costs).
    *   *Proposed Monthly Revenue:* 45 × $15,300 = $688,500.
    *   *Proposed Monthly New Costs:* (45 × $1,200) + $8,000 = $62,000.
    *   *Net Gain:* $688,500 - $62,000 = $626,500.
    *   *The Real Risk:* If the average salary is even slightly lower ($80k) or acceptance drops to 80%, the higher operational drag ($62k fixed/variable increase) eats the margin. More critically, if competitors undercut at $10k, your effective price ($15.3k) makes you uncompetitive unless the "outcome alignment" value is proven, which takes time you don't have in a full switch.

2.  **Client Acceptance Overestimation:** Assuming 90% acceptance is dangerous when competitors charge a flat $10,000. For a client hiring at $85k, your fee is $15,300 vs. a competitor's $10,000. Unless the client believes your candidates are significantly superior, the "cash-flow alignment" argument rarely justifies a 53% price premium. A mass migration to cheaper competitors could drop volume below the break-even point.

3.  **Salary Verification Friction:** Revenue share requires transparent access to client payroll data to verify the "first-year salary." Many clients will resist this audit requirement, causing payment delays or disputes that degrade cash flow, unlike the simple invoice-and-pay flat fee model.

**Kill Criteria:**
Terminate the pilot immediately if, after 60 days:
*   **Client Conversion:** Less than 40% of targeted new clients agree to the revenue-share terms.
*   **Net Margin:** The net profit per placement (Fee minus $1,200 onboarding) drops below $12,500.
