**Recommendation: Proceed with a capped, time-boxed pilot at one location only.**

Do not roll out to all three locations immediately. Commit the $26,000 (one oven rig + partial remodel) to the highest-traffic site for a 90-day trial starting after June 12. This validates the "evening crowd" assumption without exposing the full $60,000 capital risk or operational complexity across three sites. If the pilot hits the kill criteria below, expand to the remaining two locations; if not, stop with minimal loss.

**Primary Failure Modes & Missed Risks:**
1.  **Cannibalization vs. Addition:** The assumption that 30% of daytime customers will stay is unverified. If these customers simply shift their $12 spend from lunch to dinner rather than adding new visits, total revenue may not cover the added labor and utility costs of staying open late.
2.  **Labor & Operational Drag:** The plan misses the cost of evening staff (cooks, servers, cleaners) and extended utility hours. If pizza revenue is $5,200/week but labor/utilities consume 65%+ of that, the net margin could be negative, dragging down the profitable daytime bakery business.
3.  **Brand Dilution:** Tamarind Hearth is known for breads/pastries. A poorly executed pizza service (e.g., slow prep, inconsistent quality due to staff fatigue) could damage the core brand reputation, causing daytime regulars to defect.
4.  **Capital Overhang:** The $8,000 remodel estimate may be low if plumbing/venting upgrades are required for deck ovens in existing spaces, leading to budget overruns before the first pizza is sold.

**Kill Criteria (Stop/Re-evaluate Triggers):**
Stop the pilot and do not expand if **either** of the following occurs for two consecutive months:
*   **Net Revenue Threshold:** Weekly *net* profit from pizza (gross sales minus food cost, dedicated labor, and incremental utilities) falls below $1,500. (Note: Gross revenue of $2,000 is insufficient if margins are thin).
*   **Traffic Validation:** Evening foot traffic from *new* customers (non-daytime regulars) averages fewer than 15 parties per Friday/Saturday night combined, indicating the "new crowd" assumption is false.

**Leading Metric to Watch:**
Track the **attach rate** of daytime customers staying for dinner vs. **new evening-only customers** weekly. If >70% of pizza sales come from existing daytime customers shifting their spend, the "new revenue" thesis is failing even if gross sales look healthy.
