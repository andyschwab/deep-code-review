**Recommendation:** Do not launch the full 20-year program yet; instead, approve a **2-year pilot capped at $500k in upfront commitments** with a mandatory review before extending terms.

**Three Biggest Failure Modes:**
1.  **Revenue Overstatement:** Your projection of $30M is mathematically incorrect. $12,000 × 150 customers × 20 years equals **$36 million**, not $30M. While this seems like a positive error, presenting flawed math to the CFO undermines credibility. More critically, applying a 5% discount rate significantly reduces the *present value* of that future revenue, likely making the net present value (NPV) far lower than the nominal $36M figure implies.
2.  **Unmodeled Cost Escalation:** You identified an $200k annual parts cost increase due to aluminum prices but excluded it from the P&L. Over 20 years, assuming linear escalation, this alone could erase **$4 million** in profits. Additionally, the "last mile" service assumption ignores that labor costs typically outpace inflation; if service crews cost 3% more annually, the $1.8M hiring budget will be insufficient within 5 years.
3.  **Churn & Lifespan Mismatch:** Assuming <5% churn without post-warranty data is a critical gap. If actual churn hits 8% (industry average for post-warranty), you lose 60% of your revenue base by year 10. Furthermore, if system lifespan averages 20 years rather than 25, the contract promises service for non-existent assets in the final 5 years, creating pure liability with zero revenue.

**Pre-agreed Kill Trigger:**
Terminate the program immediately if **cumulative service costs exceed 45% of collected revenue** in any rolling 12-month period during the pilot, or if **annualized churn exceeds 6%** in the first 24 months.

*Note: The 8% aluminum cost increase must be modeled as a recurring annual escalator, not a one-time hit, to avoid undercapitalizing the reserve fund.*
*Ensure the pilot contract includes a clause allowing price adjustments tied to specific commodity indices to mitigate long-term material risks.*
