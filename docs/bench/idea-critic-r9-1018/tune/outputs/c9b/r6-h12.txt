**Recommendation: Proceed with a capped, time-boxed pilot (Go-If), not a full network rollout.**

Do not commit the full $700,000 upfront. Instead, authorize a single-school pilot to validate the integration cost and actual training duration before scaling. The current math relies on two unverified assumptions: that HRIS integration is free and that staff will adopt the system within three weeks. If either fails, your projected savings evaporate, turning a cost-saving measure into a net loss. A pilot limits exposure while generating the data needed to confirm the business case for the remaining five schools.

**Three Biggest Failure Modes:**
1.  **Hidden Integration Costs:** The assumption of "no extra cost" for HRIS integration is unverified. If custom middleware or consultant hours are required, the initial $200,000 implementation fee could double, immediately blowing the first-year budget.
2.  **Adoption Drag:** If staff require six weeks instead of three to reach proficiency, the temporary productivity drop will outweigh the labor savings, delaying the break-even point by quarters and increasing change-management costs.
3.  **Vendor Lock-in & Maintenance Creep:** Centralizing creates a single point of failure. If the vendor raises the $80,000 maintenance fee annually or suffers downtime, all six schools are paralyzed simultaneously, unlike the current distributed risk model.

**Kill Condition & Pivot Trigger:**
Stop or pivot the project immediately if the **first-year net cost for the pilot school exceeds $50,000** (pro-rated from your $300,000 network cap). This calculation must include actual integration spend, the true cost of staff training hours, and the platform fee, minus realized labor savings. If the pilot exceeds this threshold, do not roll out to the remaining schools; revert to the decentralized model and renegotiate the vendor contract or seek alternatives.

*Note: Your current total spend calculation ($720k) is correct, but the proposed solution's total first-year cost ($780k) actually exceeds current spend before any savings are applied, making the pilot's validation of savings critical.*
*Ensure the pilot contract includes a clause allowing exit without penalty if integration costs exceed a predefined cap.*
