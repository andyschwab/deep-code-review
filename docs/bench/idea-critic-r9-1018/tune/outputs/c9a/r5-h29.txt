**Recommendation: GO-IF**
Proceed with the launch only if you immediately secure a binding pre-order or pilot commitment covering at least 15% of your current volume before purchasing the $2M equipment. Do not commit capital based on the current revenue projection, as it contains a critical mathematical error and omits known cost increases.

**Three Biggest Failure Modes:**
1.  **Revenue Overestimation:** Your projected $14M is incorrect. A 30% price increase on $12M yields $15.6M; applying a 10% volume drop to that figure results in **$14.04M**, not $14M. More critically, if the "10% volume dip" refers to units sold, the revenue is $15.6M × 0.90 = $14.04M. However, if the 10% dip applies to the *revenue* potential due to elasticity, the number drops further. The real risk is that a 10% volume loss is optimistic for a 30% price hike in a competitive market; a 20% volume drop wipes out the revenue gain entirely ($15.6M × 0.80 = $12.48M), leaving you with higher costs and flat revenue.
2.  **Margin Erosion from Unmodeled Costs:** You excluded the $0.25 per bag packaging increase. On a 10% volume dip (assuming ~1M bags originally, now 900k), this is an immediate **$225,000 annual profit reduction**. Combined with the $667,000 annual equipment amortization, your fixed cost base rises by nearly $1M. If volume dips more than 10%, the premium pricing may not cover the combined loss of scale and new fixed/variable costs, turning the "profitable" line into a loss leader.
3.  **Consumer Rejection of Single-Protein Constraint:** Without focus-group data, you are betting that "premium" equals "single-protein." If consumers view single-protein as a limitation rather than a benefit, or if supply chain issues cause chicken price volatility that you cannot pass on, the value proposition collapses. Competitors offering mixed-protein premium options could undercut your perceived value.

**Kill Criteria:**
Terminate or pivot the project if, after 6 months of full distribution, the **repeat purchase rate is below 40%** or if **gross margin per bag falls below 55%** (currently ~62.5% on existing mix, likely lower on new line due to packaging/equipment). Specifically, if uptake (conversion of existing customers) is **<15% after 6 months**, the market rejection is too severe to justify the $2M sunk cost, and you should revert to mixed-protein formulations using existing assets.
