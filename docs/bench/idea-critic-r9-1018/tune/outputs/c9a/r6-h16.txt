**Recommendation: Proceed with a "Go-If" conditional approval.**
Do not authorize full construction on January 1. Instead, authorize only the design and permitting phase immediately, with a hard gate to commence physical build-out only if you secure signed Letters of Intent (LOIs) representing at least 40% of the target space (2,000 sq ft) by November 15, 2024. The current plan relies entirely on unverified demand assumptions; committing $250,000 without pre-leasing exposes the project to catastrophic vacancy risk in a specialized corporate niche.

**Three Main Failure Modes:**
1.  **Demand Miscalculation:** The assumption that Fortune 500 firms need small 150 sq ft private offices within a co-working environment may be false; these clients often require larger contiguous suites or have strict security protocols incompatible with shared buildings, leaving you with a product no one wants.
2.  **Arithmetic & Cost Creep:** Your math contains a critical error: 5,000 sq ft × $40/sq ft equals **$200,000**, not $250,000. While this suggests a $50,000 buffer, construction quotes rarely hold; if costs rise to the industry average of $50–$60/sq ft for office build-outs, your budget breaks immediately. Additionally, you have omitted soft costs (permitting, legal, marketing) which typically add 15–20% to the total.
3.  **Lease-Up Velocity Risk:** Achieving 80% occupancy in six months for a premium product targeting a tiny subset of firms (Fortune 500 within 5 miles) is aggressive. If sales cycles drag to 9–12 months, your cash flow will not support the debt service or opportunity cost of the empty space, blowing the 18-month break-even timeline.

**Stop/Pivot Trigger:**
**Hard Kill Criterion:** If signed leases or binding LOIs do not cover 30% of the space (1,500 sq ft) by December 1, 2024, cancel the construction contract and pivot the floor plan back to open-desk or hot-desk configurations which require lower capital expenditure.

**Notes:**
Your revenue projection assumes $55/sq ft, but ensure this covers the increased maintenance and utility load of private offices compared to open desks, which may erode the projected premium.
Verify whether the "5-mile radius" actually contains enough Fortune 500 headquarters to sustain 34+ distinct offices (5,000 sq ft / 150 sq ft per office); the total addressable market may be too small to fill the floor.
