**Recommendation to CEO Maya Patel:** Proceed with a **staged rollout** starting in three diverse regions (urban, suburban, remote) for the first 14 days rather than a full nationwide launch on September 15. Cap initial sign-ups at 1,000 to validate the $49 shipping fee against actual remote pickup costs before exposing the full $200k marketing budget and 8,500 expected users to potential logistical losses.

**Primary Failure Modes:**
1.  **Logistical Bleed:** If remote pickup costs exceed the $49 fee by the projected 30% (or more), the unit economics flip negative on every remote order, eroding the margin from the $800 average order value faster than the 15% conversion can recover.
2.  **Conversion Overestimation:** Assuming a 15% conversion (up from the pilot's 12%) without validating price sensitivity against the competitor's $30 shipping could result in significantly lower volume, leaving the $5,000/month logistics budget underutilized while fixed marketing costs remain sunk.
3.  **Competitive undercutting:** If customers perceive the $49 shipping as excessive compared to the competitor's $30, acquisition costs may spike as marketing efficiency drops, failing to reach the 8,500 sign-up target.

**Kill Criteria & Triggers:**
*   **Trigger 1 (Unit Economics):** If the average net cost per pickup in remote ZIP codes exceeds $55 (approx. 12% over the fee) within the first 500 completed pickups, **pause** nationwide expansion immediately to renegotiate logistics or adjust fees.
*   **Trigger 2 (Conversion):** If the conversion rate falls below **11%** after the first 1,000 sign-ups (statistically significant deviation from the pilot's 12%, let alone the projected 15%), **halt** the $200k marketing spend and pivot to a pricing sensitivity test.
*   **Trigger 3 (Volume):** If sign-ups track below 40% of the projected 8,500 (i.e., <3,400) by day 45, **terminate** the pilot early to preserve capital for a restructured offer.

**Note:** The assumption of a 3% conversion lift (12% to 15%) solely due to a longer trial period contradicts the pilot data where a shorter trial yielded 12%; this optimism requires immediate validation. The $49 fee must be explicitly tested against the competitor's $30 before scaling, as no data currently supports customer acceptance of the higher price.
