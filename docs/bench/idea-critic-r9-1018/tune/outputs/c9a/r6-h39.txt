**Recommendation:** Proceed with the Lanternlight Theatre season-pass pilot, but **revise the financial model immediately** before presenting to Maya Patel. The current math contains a critical error that invalidates the break-even analysis and overstates revenue potential.

**Three Biggest Failure Modes:**
1.  **Cannibalization vs. New Revenue:** The model assumes every pass sale is new money. If existing patrons who would have bought three single tickets ($150 value) switch to the $120 pass, you lose $30 per loyalist. With a $50 average ticket price, a patron attending three shows currently pays $150; dropping this to $120 reduces per-capita revenue by 20% unless the pass drives significantly *more* attendance or attracts entirely new audiences.
2.  **Flawed Break-Even Logic:** Your calculation mixes fixed and variable costs incorrectly. You state production costs are $8,000 per show. If you run 20 shows, that is $160,000 in fixed production costs, not a variable cost tied to pass holders. The break-even of 3,200 passes implies you believe costs scale directly with pass sales, which is false for theatre production. The real break-even is when *Total Revenue* ($120 × passes) exceeds *Fixed Production Costs* + *Marketing* + *Variable Service Costs*.
3.  **Conversion Overconfidence:** A 10% conversion from a cold email list (5,000 sales) is exceptionally optimistic for a $120 upfront commitment. Industry standard for high-ticket renewals often hovers between 2–5%. If conversion hits 5% (2,500 passes), revenue drops to $300,000, likely failing to cover the $150,000 marketing spend plus production overhead.

**Kill Criteria:**
Terminate the program if, after the first **90 days**, the **net contribution margin** (Pass Revenue minus direct variable costs) is negative *and* the pass-holder attendance rate exceeds 1.5x the single-ticket holder rate, indicating severe capacity strain without profitability. Do not wait for full fiscal year results; cash flow damage in Q1 is fatal.
