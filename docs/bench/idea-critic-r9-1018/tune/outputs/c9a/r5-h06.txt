**Recommendation: Proceed with a "Go-If" condition.**
Launch the pilot only if you secure a committed list of at least 50 leads (33% of your target) from existing summer-camp families before spending the $6,750 marketing budget. This validates demand without burning cash on unproven online conversion rates.

**Three Main Failure Modes:**
1.  **Conversion Collapse:** Your $45 acquisition cost assumes the 0.8% conversion rate from summer camps will translate to cold online traffic. Online evening courses typically have significantly lower conversion than in-person camps; if conversion drops to 0.4%, your cost per student doubles to $90, slashing projected profit by 50%.
2.  **Margin Erosion from Hidden Costs:** Your budget excludes ongoing bandwidth, support staff, and the $1,200 moderator fee. If support needs scale linearly with students (e.g., 1 staff per 50 students) and bandwidth costs spike during live evening peaks, your net margin could vanish even if you hit the 150-student target.
3.  **Churn Shock:** Evening-only learners often face higher attrition due to work/family conflicts. If churn exceeds 15% in the first month (vs. a typical 5-10% for committed programs), you will spend your entire second month reacquiring students just to maintain headcount, making the 90-day revenue target impossible.

**Trigger to Stop or Pivot:**
Halt the marketing spend and pivot the model if **paid enrollments are fewer than 40 students by Day 14** of the campaign. This indicates the acquisition channel or pricing is fundamentally broken before you incur significant variable costs.

*   **Arithmetic Slip:** Your revenue calculation is correct ($150 \times \$200 \times 3 = \$90,000$), but your profit projection is missing the $1,200 moderator fee and the unspecified ongoing platform/support costs. You are budgeting $6,750 (marketing) + $2,200 (integration) = $8,950 in fixed costs, but the variable costs per student are currently undefined, making the "profit" figure illusory.

Verify this specifically for evening loads.
