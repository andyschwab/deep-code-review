**Recommendation: Go, but only as a controlled pilot with 20% of clients, not a full immediate shift.**

The arithmetic in your pitch contains a critical error that undermines the projected revenue gain. You estimated annual revenue rising from $6.48M to $7.0M (a ~$520k increase). However, your new model introduces $864k in *new* annual costs ($1,200 onboarding × 45 candidates × 12 months = $648k; plus $8k marketing × 12 = $96k). Even if the per-placement fee rises to $15,300, the **net profit actually decreases** unless you significantly increase volume or the acceptance rate exceeds 90%. At 90% acceptance, you are paying more to acquire and onboard candidates than the extra fee generates. You are trading margin for top-line revenue vanity.

**Three Biggest Failure Modes:**
1.  **Margin Erosion via Hidden Costs:** The $1,200 onboarding cost and $8k marketing bump create a fixed cost drag. If the average salary dips even 5% below $85k (to ~$80.7k), your revenue per placement drops to $14.5k, wiping out the surplus needed to cover the new operational costs, resulting in lower net income than the current flat-fee model.
2.  **Adoption Overestimation:** Your 90% acceptance assumption is fragile. If only 70% of clients switch (a common resistance to variable cost models), your volume stays flat while your per-unit economics worsen due to the added onboarding/marketing overhead, causing an immediate profit dip.
3.  **Competitive Undercutting:** Competitors charging $10k flat fees will aggressively target your price-sensitive clients. In a revenue-share model, clients with lower salary bands (e.g., $60k roles) pay you only $10.8k, making you unprofitable on those placements compared to a flat fee, while competitors steal the high-volume, lower-salary segment.

**Kill Criteria:**
Terminate the full rollout immediately if, after a 90-day pilot:
*   Net profit per placement (after new onboarding/marketing costs) is less than $11,500.
*   Client acceptance rate is below 75%.
