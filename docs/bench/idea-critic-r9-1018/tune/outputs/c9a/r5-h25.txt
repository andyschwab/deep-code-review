**Recommendation: Proceed only on a "Go-If" basis.**
Launch the Digital Stage Pass on July 1 only if you secure a fixed-cost licensing agreement for recorded performances before June 15 and validate that your 30% conversion assumption holds true via a pre-launch survey of at least 500 in-person buyers. Without these two conditions met, the financial model is too fragile to commit the full $195,000 budget.

**Three Most Likely Failure Modes:**
1.  **Licensing Cost Explosion:** The plan currently budgets $0 for performance rights. If licensors demand per-stream fees or a revenue share higher than your thin margin allows, the unit economics turn negative immediately, turning every new subscriber into a loss.
2.  **Conversion Rate Collapse:** Assuming 30% of 8,000 ticket buyers (2,400 users) will convert is aggressive for a new digital product. If actual conversion is closer to the industry standard for ancillary products (often <10%), you will miss your Q1 subscriber targets by over 60%, leaving you with excess platform capacity and insufficient revenue to cover the $150,000 production spend.
3.  **Bandwidth Underestimation:** Your $0.02/minute estimate assumes perfect 1080p efficiency. If peak concurrent viewing during live-streamed events forces tiered pricing or CDN overage fees, or if users consume more than the projected 1.5 hours/show, operational costs could spike 40–50% above budget, eroding the runway before churn data even matures.

**Pre-Agreed Kill Condition:**
Terminate the initiative effective **October 31, 2024**, if the pass fails to reach **4,000 paid subscribers** (33% of the 6-month goal) or if the **net revenue per user** (after licensing and bandwidth) falls below **$4.00** for two consecutive months. This tripwire prevents sinking further capital into a model that cannot prove unit economics before the holiday spending season.
