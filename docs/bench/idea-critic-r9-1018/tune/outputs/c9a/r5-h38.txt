**Recommendation: GO-IF**
Proceed with the pilot only if a binding letter confirms at least 25% of the infrastructure grant ($52,500) and a third-party audit validates that current grid capacity at the depot can support three simultaneous 40-ft chargers without exceeding $50,000 in upgrade costs.

**Primary Failure Modes**
1.  **Grant Shortfall:** If the state covers less than 25%, the ROI extends beyond your 3-year target, turning the project into a capital drain rather than a savings vehicle.
2.  **Grid & Infrastructure Shock:** The $210,000 estimate likely excludes necessary substation upgrades. If the local utility requires transformer replacements to handle the ~1.2 MW load, costs could double, destroying the payback model.
3.  **Operational Reality vs. Buffer:** Your 15% buffer (17 miles) assumes detours are the only variance. It does not account for battery degradation in extreme weather (reducing effective range by 20-30% in winter) or dwell-time charging failures. If a bus cannot complete the 115-mile route on a single charge due to cold or traffic, the schedule collapses.

**Kill Criteria**
Terminate the project immediately if the verified all-in payback period exceeds **48 months** or if **any single bus** fails to complete its assigned route for **three consecutive days** due to range anxiety or charging unavailability during the first 90 days of the pilot.

*Note: Your fuel savings calculation assumes static diesel prices; a drop below $3.50/gallon significantly weakens the business case. Ensure the "audit" in the Go-If condition includes a winter-range simulation, not just summer specs.*
