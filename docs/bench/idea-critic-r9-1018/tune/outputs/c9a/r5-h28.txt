**Recommendation: Proceed with a phased rollout, not a full immediate launch.**
Approve the program but limit the initial expansion to 10 high-traffic locations rather than all 42. The pilot's 18% margin is promising but relies on optimistic assumptions about participation rates (20%) and stable resale values. A phased approach reduces the $185,000 exposure, allows for real-world validation of inventory turnover in diverse markets, and provides data to refine the labor model before committing to the full network.

**Top Three Failure Modes:**
1.  **Financial (Inventory Liquidity):** The secondary market for niche genres is volatile; if books sit unsold for >90 days, the carrying cost and eventual markdowns will erase the 18% margin, turning inventory into a liability rather than an asset.
2.  **Operational (Labor Creep):** The pilot's labor cost ($2,340) may be understated if performed by motivated staff in a controlled environment; scaling to 42 locations could reveal that grading, cleaning, and pricing take 30–50% more time than allocated, destroying unit economics.
3.  **Brand (Customer Friction):** Inconsistent trade-in valuations across 42 stores due to subjective grading standards could lead to customer complaints and perceptions of unfairness, damaging Inkwell's reputation for trust and community focus.

**Kill Criteria (Stop/Redesign Triggers):**
*   **Net Margin < 12%** sustained over any rolling three-month period post-launch.
*   **Inventory Turnover Ratio < 0.6** (books sold vs. books acquired) after six months of operation.
*   **Labor Cost Variance > 25%** above the projected budget per store for two consecutive months.

*Note: The 20% participation assumption should be stress-tested against actual foot traffic data from non-Seattle stores before selecting the first 10 rollout locations. Resale price volatility requires a dynamic pricing mechanism or strict acquisition caps on niche genres.*
