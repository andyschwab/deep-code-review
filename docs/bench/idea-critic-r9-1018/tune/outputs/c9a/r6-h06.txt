**Recommendation: GO-IF**
Proceed with the launch on 2025-03-01 only if you secure a carrier contract guaranteeing shipping costs under $4.50 per unit before February 15. Without this cap, your margin is negative.

**Three Biggest Failure Modes**
1.  **Margin Collapse from Shipping Variance:** Your current math assumes $0 unknown for shipping distance. At $15 revenue, minus $3 (book) and $1.50 (pack/ship base), you have $10.50 left. If variable shipping exceeds $4.50, you lose money on every box before paying for the author chat, platform fees, or marketing. Mixed-size parcels often trigger unexpected dimensional weight penalties.
2.  **Churn Underestimation:** You assume churn drops from 30% to <5% after three months. In subscription boxes, churn often *increases* after the novelty wears off (months 4–6) if the curation isn't perfect. A sustained 10% monthly churn would erase your growth to 800 members by month 6, leaving you with ~350 members instead.
3.  **Author Chat Scalability:** A single monthly virtual chat for 800 members offers low perceived value per user compared to the $15 price point. If attendance is low, the "community" promise fails; if high, the production cost and coordination overhead will exceed your budget, forcing a pivot to recorded content which lowers retention.

**Pre-Agreed Stop/Pivot Trigger**
Stop acquisition immediately if, by the end of Month 2, the **blended cost per shipped unit exceeds $5.00** OR if **net new members (after churn) is below 15% of the starting month's count** for two consecutive months.

**Arithmetic & Logic Slips**
*   **Missing Cost Line Item:** You budgeted for books ($3) and shipping ($1.50 + variable), but omitted the cost of the **virtual author chat**. Even a modest honorarium or platform fee divided by 500 members adds $0.50–$2.00 per unit, further squeezing the margin.
*   **Revenue vs. Profit Confusion:** You listed "scaling to 800 members" as a success metric, but with a potential negative contribution margin (if shipping > $4.50), scaling actually accelerates cash burn. The model currently shows a maximum gross profit of $5.50 per unit *before* overhead, which is dangerously thin for a physical goods subscription.
*   **Churn Math:** A 30% initial churn rate implies losing 150 of your first 500 members in month one alone. To reach 800 by month 6, you must acquire roughly 230 *net* new members every single month after that loss, which is a significantly higher acquisition target than implied.
