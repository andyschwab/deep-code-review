**Recommendation: Proceed with a "Go-If" Conditional Approval**
Approve the $2.5M rollout only if a binding cap on the municipal traffic surcharge ($0.05/ride) and insurance premium ($0.12/ride) is secured before the first permit payment. Without these fixed costs, the unit economics flip to negative at current volume assumptions, risking a $140k monthly burn across the three cities instead of the projected profit.

**Three Most Likely Failure Modes**
1.  **Regulatory Cost Overrun:** The new traffic policy imposes the full $0.05 surcharge plus the hinted $0.12 insurance, adding $0.17/ride in variable costs. This reduces the contribution margin from $3.35 to $3.18 per ride; combined with fixed maintenance ($150/bike), this erodes profitability entirely if fleet utilization dips below 90%.
2.  **Ridership Overestimation:** Achieving only 60% of the 10,000 rides/month target (6,000 rides) while maintaining full fleet size creates a structural loss where fixed maintenance costs outweigh fare revenue, burning ~$47k per city monthly before overhead.
3.  **Permit Fee Escalation:** The assumed $200k permit fee increases due to budget shortfalls in Riverton or Lakeview, immediately destroying the Q1 ROI and delaying payback beyond the 18-month horizon implied by the current model.

**Abort and Re-scale Triggers**
*   **Abort Condition:** If the net operating loss in any single city exceeds **$75,000** (adjusted from $100k to account for unpriced insurance/surcharge risks) after the first 60 days of operation.
*   **Re-scale Condition:** If ridership falls below **4,500 rides/month** (45% of target) for two consecutive months in any city, immediately reduce fleet size by 30% in that location to align maintenance costs with actual demand.

*Note: The current plan lacks a contingency for the $0.17/ride potential cost increase; securing cost caps is the primary gate.*
*Ensure the "Go-If" condition is explicitly written into the permit agreement to avoid retroactive fees.*
