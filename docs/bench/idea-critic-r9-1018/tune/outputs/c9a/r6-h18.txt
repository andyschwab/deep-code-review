**Recommendation: Proceed with a Hybrid Approach (Self-Publish with Contingency)**
Do not commit fully to self-publishing yet. Instead, secure a publishing partner now for distribution and marketing leverage, but negotiate a clause allowing you to buy out the contract if the demo conversion exceeds 5% by July 2025. This caps your downside risk while preserving upside potential.

**Three Biggest Failure Modes**
1.  **Discovery Collapse:** The 5% conversion assumption is dangerously optimistic for a crowded space-sim market; if organic visibility fails, the $80k marketing budget will burn without generating sufficient volume to cover the $250k development cost.
2.  **Revenue Math Error:** Projecting $500k gross revenue implies needing ~16,700 units sold at $30 (post-platform fees). With only a 5% demo conversion, you need 334,000 demo downloads in six months—a massive traffic requirement unsupported by the current budget.
3.  **Cash Flow Gap:** Self-publishing entails 30–90 day payment delays from platforms. Without a partner advance, you risk running out of operating cash before the first royalty check arrives, jeopardizing the final development sprint.

**Abort/Pivot Triggers**
*   **Pivot to Partner:** If demo conversion is <2% after two weeks of paid traffic.
*   **Abort Project:** If total demo downloads are <50,000 by August 1, 2025, indicating insufficient market interest to recoup costs.

**Critical Flaws in Numbers & Assumptions**
*   **The "15% Market Growth" Trap:** Market growth does not guarantee *your* growth; it often attracts more competitors, diluting your share. Basing revenue on macro trends rather than unit economics is a fatal strategic error.
*   **Hidden Fees & Net Revenue:** You projected $500k *gross* but calculated ROI against *development costs* without accounting for the ~30% platform fee (Steam), taxes, and payment processing. Your actual net revenue would be closer to $350k, slashing your projected ROI from 100% to ~40% before marketing costs are even fully deducted.
*   **Marketing Efficiency:** Allocating $80k to acquire enough users for $500k revenue implies a Customer Acquisition Cost (CAC) that may be unsustainable if the conversion rate dips even slightly below 5%.

**Note:** The current plan assumes a best-case scenario for every variable; a single underperformance breaks the ROI model.
