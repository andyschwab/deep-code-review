Proceed only if a targeted market validation sprint confirms at least 18 vessels currently turned away or underserved due to weight limits. Do not commit to the $620,000 purchase before this data is in hand.

**Three Biggest Failure Modes**
1.  **Demand Illusion:** The 20% volume increase assumes latent demand for 12-ton lifts exists *now*. If the 2022 survey is stale and the local fleet hasn't grown into the 8–12-ton range, you will incur the higher operating costs ($30k/year) and debt service without the projected revenue lift, extending payback beyond the vessel's life.
2.  **Hidden Infrastructure & Opportunity Costs:** The "six-week shutdown" likely understates the true cost. Beyond lost revenue during installation, verify if the concrete pad, electrical supply, or rail system requires upgrades to handle the extra 4 tons; these civil works are often excluded from dealer quotes and could add six figures. Additionally, a six-week total blackout may drive transient customers to competitors permanently, not just defer revenue.
3.  **Financing Sensitivity:** The model relies on a 5% rate over five years. If rates rise or the term shortens due to credit conditions, the annual debt service increases sharply, eroding the margin between the $420k revenue gain and the new costs, potentially turning the cash flow negative in year one.

**Cancel/Pivot Trigger**
Cancel the purchase if the validation sprint (completed by 15 Oct 2026) identifies fewer than 18 qualified vessels needing >8-ton capacity, or if civil engineering estimates exceed $75,000 in additional site work. Pivot to a partnership model (renting heavy-lift capacity from a neighboring yard) if the demand exists but is seasonal, avoiding the fixed cost of ownership.
