**Recommendation:** Approve a **six-month, capped pilot** for 5,000 users (not the full 120,000) with a hard budget ceiling of **2.5M KES** (covering 50 tablets and the first year's license). Do not commit to the full five-year, county-wide rollout until this pilot validates the unknown data consumption rates.

**Strongest Objection (The Data Spike Risk):** Your cost model collapses if monthly data usage exceeds **45MB per patient**. You estimated 1M KES over five years based on current rates, but this assumes static usage. If cold-chain alerts include images or frequent pings, usage could easily triple. At 12 KES/MB, a jump to 50MB/user/month for 120,000 users would inflate annual data costs alone to **72M KES**, blowing the five-year budget in less than two years. The pilot's 92% capture rate is irrelevant if the operational cost per captured record becomes unsustainable.

**Better Alternative Missed:** Before purchasing 150 tablets (37.5M KES upfront), mandate a **"Bring Your Own Device" (BYOD) + USSD hybrid** for the 80% of patients with smartphones. Use the tablets *only* for community health workers in zero-connectivity zones. Additionally, negotiate a **zero-rated data agreement** with the provider specifically for the app's IP addresses before signing the license. If the provider refuses zero-rating, the per-megabyte risk is too high to proceed at scale.

**Kill Criteria (The Plug-Pull Trigger):** Stop the rollout immediately if, after three months of the pilot:
1.  **Average data consumption per active user exceeds 40MB/month**, OR
2.  **Total monthly operational cost (license + data) exceeds 450,000 KES**.

If either metric triggers, pivot to a low-bandwidth SMS/USSD-only model and halt tablet procurement.
