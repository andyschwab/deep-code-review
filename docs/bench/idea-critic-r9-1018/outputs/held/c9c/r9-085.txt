**Decision: Proceed only with a "Go-If" condition, not as currently outlined.**

The proposal should not be approved as a fixed $12M, 30-month commitment. Instead, authorize a **capped, time-boxed first step**: release funds strictly for the heritage planning phase and detailed design, with a hard cap of $500k and a deadline of 6 months. Full construction funding is contingent on securing heritage consent within that window and a revised total cost estimate that remains under $13.5M (providing a 12.5% buffer rather than the implied zero buffer).

**Principal Failure Modes:**
1.  **Regulatory Blockade:** The 6-month heritage approval is the critical path. If the site is adjacent to a heritage-listed park, restrictions often extend to 12–18 months or impose design changes that invalidate current cost estimates. A delay here creates immediate cost inflation without revenue.
2.  **Demand Arithmetic Mismatch:** A 15% enrollment rise over three years requires ~5% annual growth. If current enrollment is static, this assumes a market shift that may not materialize. Furthermore, a mere 3% tuition increase may not cover the operational cost of new hybrid infrastructure (tech maintenance, specialized faculty), creating a margin squeeze even if seats are filled.
3.  **Construction Inflation:** A 30-month timeline starting March 2025 exposes the project to potential material cost volatility. The current plan lacks a contingency line item; any overrun immediately erodes the ROI.

**Kill Criteria & Tripwires:**
*   **Stop Condition A (Regulatory):** If final heritage consent is not granted by **1 September 2025** (6 months from start), halt the project. Do not re-scope automatically; trigger a full board review to assess abandonment or site relocation.
*   **Stop Condition B (Financial):** If the tendered construction cost exceeds **$13.5 million**, terminate the current plan. This threshold is lower than your $14M suggestion to preserve capital for alternative investments if the business case margins thin.
*   **Leading Metric:** Monitor "Pre-enrollment commitments" for the new hybrid programs. If commitments are below 40% of the projected 1,800 seats by **Q4 2025**, pause construction procurement.

**Arithmetic/Operating Note:**
The proposal omits the ongoing operating expense (OpEx) for the hybrid technology stack. A 3% tuition increase on a 15% volume gain may not cover the elevated fixed costs of hybrid delivery (licensing, IT support, content updates), potentially making the new seats less profitable than existing ones. Verify the marginal contribution per student before finalizing the revenue model.
