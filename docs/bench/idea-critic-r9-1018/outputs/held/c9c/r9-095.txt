**Recommendation: GO-IF (Conditional Go)**
Proceed with the $120k pilot only if you cap the marketing spend at $20k initially and tie the remaining $30k to a verified engagement milestone. Do not commit to the full six-month marketing push or the citywide rollout until you prove the "75% completion" assumption holds for your specific demographic.

**Strongest Pushback & Failure Modes**
Your plan relies on a fatal contradiction: you aim to serve youth with "unstable housing" using a digital app requiring "three logins/week," yet you assume they will match the national average (75% completion). Unstable housing often means inconsistent device access, data limits, and priority shifts toward survival, not training. The most likely failure is not software quality, but **access inequality**: the app works perfectly, but your target audience cannot reach it consistently. If you spend $50k marketing a tool they physically cannot engage with daily, you will burn cash while the dropout rate remains near 32%. The "optimistic" assumption is not a buffer; it is the single point of failure.

**Alternative Route**
Instead of a full marketing blitz, run a **high-touch, low-tech concierge pilot**. Redirect $30k from marketing to provide prepaid data plans or dedicated tablet access for 50 participants, paired with human navigators who remind them to log in. Test if the barrier is *motivation* (solved by marketing) or *access* (solved by infrastructure). If the app fails even when access is guaranteed, the product is the issue. If it succeeds only with human nudges, your scaling model needs staff, not ads.

**Kill Criteria (The Pivot Trigger)**
Scrap or pivot the project if, by **September 1, 2025** (8 weeks post-launch), the cohort averages fewer than **1.5 logins per week** or achieves a **<40% module completion rate**. These thresholds acknowledge the housing instability reality while rejecting the delusion of national averages. If you cannot hit these lowered bars with a dedicated pilot group, a citywide rollout will simply amplify waste.

**Leading Metric to Watch**
Weekly Active Users (WAU) divided by Total Enrolled, tracked every Friday. If this ratio drops below 30% for two consecutive weeks, trigger the kill switch.

*Note: Your budget allocates zero dollars for technical support or device provisioning, which is risky for this demographic. Ensure the "coordinators" have capacity for IT troubleshooting, not just program management.*
