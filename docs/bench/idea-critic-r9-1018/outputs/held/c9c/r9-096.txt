**Recommendation:** Present the proposal, but strictly as a **"Go-If" pilot** capped at 5 schools, not the full 40. The current math relies on unverified operational assumptions that could turn a 3-year payback into a loss within 18 months.

**Principal Failure Modes:**
1.  **Battery Life Mismatch:** The business case assumes 4-year battery life, but standard lead-acid or low-tier lithium batteries in high-heat rural environments often degrade significantly faster (2–3 years). If replacement is needed at year 3, the payback period extends beyond the 5-year contract, destroying ROI.
2.  **Insolation Variance:** Relying on a flat "4 sunshine hours" average ignores seasonal cloud cover in Limpopo. A 15% drop in generation forces diesel top-ups, eroding the $3,800 annual savings assumption.
3.  **Hidden Maintenance Costs:** Remote site visits for troubleshooting (inverter faults, theft, or connection issues) are exponentially more expensive than urban service calls. If unscheduled visits exceed two per unit annually, maintenance costs will eclipse fuel savings.

**Trigger Condition to Halt:**
Stop the rollout immediately if the **pilot phase (first 5 units)** shows a **monthly energy yield below 240 kWh** (80% of target) for two consecutive months during the dry season, or if **unscheduled maintenance costs exceed $150 per unit** in the first six months. These metrics directly validate the generation and OPEX assumptions before committing the remaining $450,000.

*Note: The battery lifespan assumption is the single largest financial risk; negotiate a performance-based warranty clause before signing the service contract.*
