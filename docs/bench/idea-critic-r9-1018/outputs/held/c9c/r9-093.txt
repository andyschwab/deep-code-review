**Direct-to-Consumer Wine Club Pitch: Critical Risks & Kill Criteria**

**The Primary Sinker:** The projection assumes converting 20% of your retail base (2,400 customers) to reach the 5,000 member goal. This implies acquiring 2,600 *new* customers solely through the $150k budget. At $57.69 per acquisition, this is highly optimistic for wine clubs without accounting for the unknown Chilean export limits. If new regulations restrict direct shipping to key overseas markets, the total addressable market collapses, making the 5,000-member target mathematically impossible regardless of ad spend.

**Hidden Risks & Smarter Routes:**
*   **Regulatory Blockade:** The unknown export regulations are a binary failure point. If limits exist, the "fulfillment hub" becomes stranded inventory.
*   **Variable Cost Underestimation:** $30/member ($2.50/month) for packaging and shipping is likely insufficient for international wine logistics, which involve heavy glass, temperature control, and duties. Real costs could be 2–3x higher, destroying the breakeven timeline.
*   **Smarter Route:** Do not build a proprietary fulfillment hub yet. Partner with an existing wine-specific e-commerce platform (e.g., WineDirect, VinoShipper) that already handles compliance and variable logistics. This shifts fixed costs to variable ones and validates the market before the $150k capital outlay.

**Key Failure Points to Flag for Alejandro:**
1.  **Conversion Fallacy:** Relying on a 20% conversion rate from retail to subscription is unprecedented; industry averages are often <5%.
2.  **Regulatory Dependency:** The entire revenue model hinges on unverified international shipping laws.
3.  **Cash Flow Trap:** The $150k upfront spend assumes linear growth; if acquisition stalls at month 3, the remaining budget cannot sustain the 12-month runway.

**Pull-the-Plug Trigger (Kill Criterion):**
If, by **October 1, 2025** (90 days post-launch), the club has not secured **1,250 active paying members** (25% of the Year 1 goal) AND the verified cost-per-acquisition exceeds **$80**, immediately pause all ad spend and dissolve the fulfillment hub plan. This indicates the unit economics are negative and the market size is insufficient to support the standalone model.

**Note:** The $600k revenue figure is only valid if the 20% conversion assumption holds; stress-test the pitch with a 5% conversion scenario ($150k revenue) to see if the business survives. Resolve the Chilean export regulation status before presenting the July 1 launch date.
