**Recommendation: Proceed with a capped, 12-month pilot on 4 turbines (50 MW) only, conditional on a fixed-price integration clause.**

Do not roll out to all 12 turbines immediately. The financial model relies entirely on the unverified assumption that the new sensor feed requires no data-quality layer. If the legacy SCADA mapping takes significantly longer than expected or reveals dirty data, the $85k integration estimate will explode, and the promised 0.8-day downtime reduction will fail to materialize because the algorithms will be fed garbage. A 4-turbine pilot limits your exposure to ~$105k (pro-rated license + full integration risk) while validating the "clean feed" assumption. If the pilot succeeds, the full rollout is low-risk; if it fails, you have not committed the full $315k annual burn.

**Key Failure Modes:**
1.  **Technical:** The "clean feed" assumption is false. Legacy tag hierarchies often contain historical inconsistencies that require a costly middleware layer to normalize, invalidating the latency and downtime benefits.
2.  **Financial:** Integration costs exceed the $85k cap due to unforeseen mapping complexity, destroying the Year 1 ROI. The $1.2M savings are theoretical until the data pipeline is proven stable.
3.  **Operational:** Engineers spend excessive time on manual mapping instead of maintenance, creating a temporary spike in downtime that offsets the dashboard's benefits during the critical adoption phase.

**Pull-the-Plug Trigger:**
If realized avoided O&M costs fall below **60% of the forecast** (approx. $240k annualized run-rate) after the first 12 months of the pilot, terminate the contract. This threshold accounts for reasonable ramp-up friction while ensuring the project remains accretive to cash flow.

**Note:** You must obtain a fixed-price cap for the integration work before signing; otherwise, the $85k estimate is a liability, not a budget. The "unknown" mapping time is the single biggest risk to your timeline and must be quantified during the pilot's first month.
