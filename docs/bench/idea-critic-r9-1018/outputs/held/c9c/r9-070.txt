**Recommendation:** Proceed with a **Go-If** decision: Launch the pilot only if a binding quote for per-batch DNA sequencing is secured by June 1, 2025, proving margins remain above 40% at the $12 price point. Without this fixed cost, the $2.3M revenue projection is speculative and risks burning $1.43M in upfront capital on an unprofitable unit economy.

**Toughest Objection (The Margin Trap):**
The plan collapses on the unpriced variable cost. Nutrigenomics requires high-fidelity sequencing; if the per-batch fee exceeds $4.50 (a plausible scenario given current biotech processing rates), your gross margin evaporates. At $12 revenue, fixed costs (R&D, marketing, regulatory) already consume ~$6.00 per unit assuming the 30k run. If sequencing pushes total COGS above $7.00, you lose money on every bag sold. The "15% market capture" assumption is irrelevant if the unit economics are negative; you would simply be scaling losses. The regulatory timeline is also a single point of failure: a 7-month delay instead of 6 pushes the launch past the critical Q4 shopping season, deferring revenue while burning cash.

**Better Route:**
Instead of a full 30,000-unit production run, execute a **"Digital Twin" Pre-Sale**. Create the marketing assets and take pre-orders for a limited batch (e.g., 2,000 units) contingent on final regulatory clearance. This validates the $12 price point and the 15% capture assumption with real cash rather than models, locks in the sequencing vendor volume for a better rate, and reduces the initial cash outlay from $1.43M to under $200k. If pre-sales fail, you pivot before tooling up.

**Kill Criteria & Pivot Triggers:**
1.  **Pull the Plug:** If the binding sequencing quote received by June 1, 2025, results in a projected gross margin below 40% at the $12 price point.
2.  **Pivot:** If regulatory approval is not granted by December 15, 2025. Do not wait for the full six-month window to expire; if no substantive progress is made by month 4, pivot to a non-nutrigenomic premium line using the existing R&D formulation without the DNA component.
3.  **Primary Metric to Watch:** **Customer Acquisition Cost (CAC) vs. Lifetime Value (LTV)** during the pilot. If CAC exceeds $45 (based on your $250k marketing budget aiming for ~5,500 customers to hit 15% of a niche segment), the growth model is broken regardless of product quality.

**Note:** The $2.3M revenue figure assumes selling all 30,000 units immediately; ensure your cash flow model accounts for retail payment terms (often Net-60), which may create a liquidity gap before revenue is realized. Verify the "15% of premium market" total addressable market (TAM) calculation; 15% is an exceptionally aggressive penetration for a new CPG brand in year one.
