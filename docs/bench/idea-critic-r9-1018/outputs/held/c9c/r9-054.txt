**Recommendation: GO-IF**  
Proceed with the pilot launch only if the flavor-approval audit cost is confirmed under 45 k € before final budget sign-off, and the first-month sell-through exceeds 12 % of pilot production volume.

**Blind Spots & Failure Modes**  
1. **Margin Erosion:** The projected 30 % margin ignores the historical 0.6 % spoilage rate from specialty tests. At scale, this unaccounted loss could reduce actual margins to ~24 %, destroying the profitability case for the new SKUs.  
2. **Capacity Illusion:** While the Gouda plant runs at 80 % capacity, flavored cheese production often requires separate cleaning cycles or slower line speeds to prevent cross-contamination, effectively reducing available capacity below the required threshold for the 1.8 M € volume.  
3. **Logistics Assumption:** The forecast assumes zero incremental logistics cost for absorbing 1.8 M € in new volume. Existing networks rarely absorb ~3.75 % revenue growth (1.8 M / 48 M) without added freight, storage, or handling fees, which would further compress margins.  
4. **Regulatory Wildcard:** The unknown cost of a potential new flavor-approval audit is a binary risk; if the regulator demands extensive testing, it could exceed the allocated seasoning budget (150 k €) and delay the launch past the board's timeline.

**Abort Signal (Kill Criterion)**  
Abort the full 12-month rollout if, after the first 6 weeks of the pilot, the cumulative sell-through rate is below **8 %** of produced units *or* if the verified net margin (after accounting for spoilage and actual logistics costs) falls below **18 %**. This threshold ensures we stop before committing capital to a line that cannot meet half its projected profitability.

*Note: The 4 % revenue lift calculation (48 M € to 49.92 M €) is mathematically correct but relies entirely on the unverified assumption that distribution can handle the volume without friction.*
