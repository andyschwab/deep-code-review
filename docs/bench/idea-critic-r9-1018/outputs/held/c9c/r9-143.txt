**Recommendation: GO-IF**
Proceed only if written confirmation of regulatory approval is received before the first drone payment is released. If approval is not secured by the start of the next growing season, abort the purchase.

**Three Biggest Failure Modes**
1.  **Regulatory Block:** The aviation regulator denies or indefinitely delays commercial flight permits over the vineyard, rendering the $225,000 hardware and service contract useless immediately.
2.  **Scaling Fallacy:** The 15% yield lift achieved on the uniform 40-acre pilot fails to replicate across the full 200-acre estate due to terrain variance or pest migration patterns, dropping actual revenue gain below the $112,500 break-even point.
3.  **Thermal Battery Collapse:** Summer heatwaves reduce drone flight time by >40%, forcing double the sorties to cover 200 acres, which spikes service fees and labor costs enough to erase the projected 10% pesticide savings.

**Pull-the-Plug Trigger**
Terminate the program if, after six months of active operation, the net yield increase is under 5% **OR** the total cost per acre (amortized hardware + service) exceeds $15.

**Arithmetic & Assumption Flags**
*   **Math Error:** Your first-year outlay is **$225,000** ($105k drones + $120k service), but your projected benefits total **$417,600** ($345k revenue + $54k labor + $18k pesticide). This implies a first-year ROI of ~85%, which is unusually high and suggests the $120k service fee estimate may be too low or the yield projection too optimistic.
*   **Hidden Assumption:** You assume the $2.3M sales figure scales linearly with yield. If the 15% bump creates a supply glut that lowers the price per ton, your revenue projection will fail.
*   **Missing Variable:** The "30% scouting time reduction" assumes current labor is fully redeployable; if those workers are fixed costs, the $54k saving is theoretical cash flow, not actual budget reduction.
