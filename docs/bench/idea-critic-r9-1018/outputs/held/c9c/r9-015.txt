The plan contains a fatal arithmetic contradiction that invalidates the financial model. You estimate 12,000 households at $15/month yields $180,000 in *monthly* revenue, but explicitly note this figure assumes an *annual* fee. If the fee is truly $15/month, monthly revenue is only $180,000 if you have 12,000 customers paying annually upfront, or the math is simply wrong ($15 × 12,000 = $180,000/year, which is $15,000/month). If labor consumes the rest to leave a 15% margin ($2.25), your total cost is $12.75, leaving no room for packaging, van depreciation, or marketing. With a $250k upfront outlay and potentially negative monthly cash flow, you cannot survive the "unknown churn rate" long enough to find product-market fit.

**Toughest Objection:**
The "30% conversion from retail" assumption is unsupported and likely inflated. Retail buyers purchase intermittently; subscription buyers commit to recurring delivery. Without comparable market data, assuming nearly a third of casual buyers will convert to a logistics-heavy subscription is a classic planning fallacy. If actual conversion is 3% (a more typical direct-to-consumer baseline), your revenue drops 90%, making the $250k van investment immediately unrecoverable.
