**Strongest Objection: The financial model collapses if electricity prices stagnate or the tax credit is delayed.**
Your 5-year payback relies entirely on two volatile assumptions: a guaranteed 30% tax credit and rising utility rates similar to Riverdale's historical data. If the legislation tightens post-2026, reducing the credit to 10%, or if regional energy prices flatten due to grid improvements, your payback period likely extends beyond 12 years. Furthermore, a fixed $200k O&M budget without escalation is dangerous; solar maintenance costs typically rise with inflation, eroding the very savings you are counting on. You are presenting a "best-case" financial scenario as a baseline, which risks credibility if the Dean questions the sensitivity of these variables.

**Clearly Better Route: A Power Purchase Agreement (PPA) with a phased rollout.**
Instead of the university bearing the $12M capital risk and operation burden, propose a PPA where a third-party developer owns the array and sells you power at a fixed, discounted rate. This removes the tax credit uncertainty (the developer absorbs that risk) and eliminates the $200k O&M line item. Pair this with a phased rollout: install on 0.5 acres first to validate actual generation against the 8 MWh target before committing the full 2 acres. This lowers the initial decision threshold and allows you to adjust to the 2026 legislative landscape before full deployment.
