**Verdict: REVISE**

**Claim:** Launch a $350k pilot of Grace Beacon robots in three senior homes in Q1 2027 to save $120k/year.

**Steelman:** A controlled 9-month pilot across three sites validates whether AI companionship reduces nurse call volume by 15%, justifying the $350k investment despite regulatory uncertainty.

**Strongest Attack Survived (The Dealbreaker):**
The proposal fails on **unit economics and regulatory timing**. At $2,333 per resident ($350k/150 residents), the pilot cost exceeds your $2,500 walk-away threshold before accounting for the high risk of Health Canada certification delays. If certification slips beyond 6 weeks, the Q1 2027 start date collapses, turning fixed integration costs into sunk waste with zero ROI. Furthermore, the projected $120k annual savings implies a 3-year payback period ignoring maintenance, which is too slow for a hardware-heavy pilot with unproven regulatory clearance. The "15% drop" assumption is unsupported by baseline data in the request; if the actual reduction is only 5%, the payback extends to 9 years, destroying the business case.

**Better Way:**
Do not buy hardware yet. Pursue a **software-only simulation or partnership pilot** with an existing certified provider to validate the "15% call reduction" hypothesis at <10% of the cost. If the data holds, *then* procure hardware. This decouples the regulatory risk of new hardware from the value hypothesis testing.

**Kill Criteria (Go/No-Go Triggers):**
1.  **Regulatory Stop:** If Health Canada certification is not secured by **6 weeks** from approval to proceed, cancel the hardware purchase.
2.  **Cost Cap:** If the fully loaded cost per resident (including hidden integration/maintenance) exceeds **$2,000** (tightening your $2,500 limit to create a safety margin), pivot to a partnership model.
3.  **Efficacy Floor:** If the pilot interim review (Month 3) shows less than a **8% reduction** in call volume (half the target), terminate immediately.

**Decision:** **NO-GO** on the current hardware-heavy plan. **GO-IF** you restructure as a low-cost software/validation pilot first to prove the 15% efficiency gain before committing $350k to uncertified hardware.

**Dissent Ledger:** The current plan bets $350k on an unproven 15% efficiency gain and a 4-week regulatory miracle. The unit economics are fragile; any slip in timeline or efficacy makes the pilot a net loss.

**Remaining Risk:** Even with a software pivot, if the core hypothesis (AI reduces calls) is false, the strategic direction for senior care automation is flawed.

**User Question:** Can we secure a binding commitment from Health Canada on the 4-week timeline, or is that date purely speculative?
