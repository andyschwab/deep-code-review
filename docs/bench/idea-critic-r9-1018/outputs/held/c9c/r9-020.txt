Proceed with the rollout only if the contract includes a capped, time-boxed Phase 1 (50,000 cards) with a hard kill criterion: if net adoption (cash-to-card switch) is below 30% or card loss rates exceed 8% after six months, the program pauses for a strategic pivot to mobile-app expansion. **How This Could Fall Apart**
1.  **Cannibalization & Sunk Costs:** With 45% mobile adoption already, the $3.2M hardware spend may simply subsidize users who would otherwise use the free app, while the $0.50 replacement budget is likely insufficient given the pilot's 6% loss rate (projected $45k–$90k annual bleed vs. budgeted $75k total over 3 years).
2.  **Faulty Break-Even Logic:** Your year-two break-even assumes an 18% cash-handling reduction, but if riders are already using mobile tickets, the addressable "cash" market is smaller than calculated, delaying ROI indefinitely.
3.  **Behavioral Inertia:** The pilot's 12-second throughput gain relies on voluntary adoption; if the remaining cash users are resistant to change or unbanked, forced migration could increase gate congestion rather than reduce it.

**Trigger to Pull or Pivot**
If active card usage remains below 30% of the issued base or the loss rate surpasses 8% within the first six months, immediately halt further card procurement and renegotiate the backend contract to support a mobile-first strategy, treating the initial spend as a capped learning expense rather than a foundation for full deployment.
