**Recommendation: Proceed with a capped, time-boxed pilot only if you first validate the refurbishment labor cost.** Do not launch the full program or commit to the March 1st date until you have a verified cost per cycle. The financial model collapses if the actual labor time exceeds 45 minutes per board (assuming a €20/hour technician rate), turning the €15 assumption into a €40+ reality that erodes margins below viability.

**Principal Failure Modes:**
1.  **Unit Economics Inversion:** The €15 refurbishment estimate is unverified. If re-coating takes longer than expected, the €79 monthly fee may not cover hardware depreciation plus labor, making every additional subscriber a loss-maker rather than an ARR asset.
2.  **Conversion Overestimation:** The 22% conversion target (990 customers) assumes high willingness to pay for upgrades. If actual uptake is below 10%, fixed operational costs for the refurbishment center will outweigh subscription revenue, failing to reach profitability by Q2 2025.
3.  **Churn Underestimation:** If annual churn exceeds 7% due to board damage disputes or lack of new models to upgrade to, the lifetime value (LTV) drops below the customer acquisition cost (CAC) implied by the discount structure.

**Superior Option:**
Run a "Concierge Pilot" with 50 existing customers in Lisbon immediately, manually processing boards without building the full subscription infrastructure. Charge the full €79/month without the upfront discount to test price elasticity and, crucially, measure the *actual* labor hours required for refurbishment in a real-world setting. This validates the core cost driver before locking in 12-month contracts or expanding to Porto.

**Kill Criterion:**
Stop or pivot if the **average refurbishment labor cost per board exceeds €22** (verified over 20 cycles in the pilot) or if **pilot conversion falls below 8%** after 6 weeks. These thresholds directly address the unverified labor assumption and the aggressive conversion target.
