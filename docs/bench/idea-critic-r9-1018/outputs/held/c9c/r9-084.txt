**Recommendation: GO-IF**  
Proceed with the pilot only if capped at **$1.05M** (reducing the user cohort to 10,500) and bound by a hard **kill criterion**: if the default rate exceeds **5.8%** (a 16% relative increase) in any two-week window during the first month, the pilot halts immediately.

**Critical Failure Points & Blind Spots**  
1. **The "Unchanged Default" Fallacy:** Your core assumption—that expanding to new segments keeps defaults at 5%—is the plan's weakest link. New segments lack historical performance data in your current model; assuming parity with existing users is statistically unsupported. A 1% rise in defaults (to 6%) on $1.2M of deployed capital wipes out the profit margin from the 15% approval lift.  
2. **Hidden Cost Underestimation:** You identified $45k in validation costs, but omitted the **regulatory contingency**. Singapore's MAS requires rigorous model validation for alternative data. If the regulator demands a pause for explanation (a high probability given "uncertain" response), the cost isn't just fines; it's the burn rate of a stalled team. The $90k + $45k estimate assumes smooth integration; add a **$75k contingency buffer** (total hidden cost ~$120k) or reduce scope.  
3. **Data Feed Latency:** Real-time scoring relies on external APIs. If latency spikes during peak loan hours, approval rates drop not because of the model, but because of timeout errors, falsely signaling model failure.

**The Plug-Pull Signal**  
Stop immediately if:  
- **Default Rate > 5.8%** in the first 30 days (leading indicator of segment mismatch).  
- **Regulatory Inquiry** remains unanswered for >5 business days (signals impending enforcement action).  

**What Would Change My Mind**  
I would recommend a full **NO-GO** if you cannot isolate the "new segment" users for separate tracking. If the pilot mixes old and new users in a single pool, you will never know if the 5% default rate is holding or if new users are dragging it up while good old users mask the damage. Without segment-level isolation, the pilot generates no learnable data, rendering the $1.2M spend a blind bet.
