**Recommendation: Proceed with a capped, 90-day "Go-If" pilot, not the full six-month plan.**

Do not commit the full £250k or the full six months yet. Instead, authorize a strictly time-boxed and budget-capped initial phase (e.g., £40k for 90 days) targeting only 1,000 households. This approach validates the two critical unknowns you identified—the GDPR audit cost and the CRM integration timeline—before exposing the entire organization to risk. If the vendor cannot produce a fixed-price GDPR compliance certificate or a working CRM sandbox within the first 30 days, the project stops immediately.

**Key Failure Modes:**
1.  **The Integration Trap:** Your 30% call-center reduction goal assumes seamless CRM integration. If the voice engine cannot write back to your legacy CRM in real-time, agents will still have to manually verify every interaction, potentially *increasing* handle time rather than reducing volume.
2.  **The Adoption Illusion:** You are assuming 80% smartphone capability equates to 80% *willingness* to use a voice bot for housing issues. Tenants often prefer human empathy for housing disputes; a "clear win" on paper surveys (£150) vs. digital (£20) ignores that paper surveys are passive, while voice bots require active, trusted engagement. If trust is low, adoption will stall well below your 5% target regardless of device ownership.
3.  **Hidden Compliance Costs:** If the GDPR audit reveals that voice data requires a specific, expensive storage architecture not included in the vendor's base quote, your unit economics (£20/tenant) will collapse, making the solution more expensive than the status quo.

**Kill Criterion:**
Terminate the pilot if **active weekly usage falls below 2% of the invited cohort by Day 45** OR if the **GDPR compliance audit exceeds £15,000**. Do not wait for the six-month mark; these leading indicators will tell you if the fundamental value proposition or legal feasibility is broken.

*Note: Your comparison of £20/tenant vs. £150/year for paper surveys may be flawed if the £150 is a fixed vendor contract fee that doesn't decrease linearly with reduced survey volume.*
*Ensure the "30% drop in call volume" metric isolates calls related to feedback only, not general inquiries, to avoid false positives.*
