**Strongest Objection: The Regulatory-Technical Mismatch**
Your proposal assumes regulatory clearance (IND amendment) and technical integration are parallel, manageable tracks. The fatal flaw is treating "de-identified data on a blockchain" as a settled compliance state. Regulatory bodies (FDA, EMA) and HIPAA enforcers currently view immutable ledgers with extreme skepticism regarding the "right to be forgotten" and data correction mandates inherent in clinical trials. If a participant withdraws consent or data is found erroneous, an immutable chain cannot simply "delete" the record without breaking the chain's integrity or employing complex, unproven cryptographic revocation schemes that regulators have not yet blessed. You are betting $4.5M on a regulatory interpretation that does not yet exist, risking a scenario where you build a technically sound system that is legally unusable.

**Better Alternative: Federated Learning with Off-Chain Storage**
Instead of a decentralized *marketplace* (which implies data movement and ownership transfer), propose a **Federated Data Network**. Keep the raw data within each hospital's existing warehouse (solving your unknown integration cost by minimizing data egress). Use blockchain only as a coordination layer to verify that models were trained locally and to manage access permissions, while the actual clinical data never leaves the hospital's firewall. This reduces regulatory friction by maintaining current data custodianship, lowers integration costs by avoiding massive data migrations, and achieves the goal of collaborative insight without the immutability liability.

**Three Ways This Could Unravel**
1.  **The Integration Cost Black Hole:** Your "unknown" integration cost could easily exceed the $4.5M cap. If each hospital requires custom middleware to map their legacy warehouse schemas to your blockchain standard, the 18-month timeline will collapse under scope creep, burning cash before the first byte is uploaded.
2.  **Regulatory Stop-Order:** Six months post-submission, regulators issue a guidance stating that clinical trial data on public or permissioned immutable ledgers violates patient privacy rights due to the inability to fully purge data. The project becomes a stranded asset with zero salvage value.
3.  **Participant Revolt:** Clinical trial participants, upon learning their de-identified data is on a "blockchain" (a term often associated with public exposure), withdraw consent en masse due to perceived security risks, starving the marketplace of the very asset it needs to function.

**Kill Criterion (Pull the Plug)**
If the regulatory body does not provide **written, specific guidance approving the use of immutable ledger technology for clinical trial data management** within 9 months of submission (by Q1 2026), terminate the project immediately. Do not proceed to development without this explicit written assurance; verbal optimism or "no objection" letters are insufficient given the $4.5M exposure.
