**Pilot Model Corrections & Risks**

Your current model contains a critical arithmetic error and an impossible operational assumption that will derail the pitch immediately:

1.  **Capex Mismatch:** You cited a $3.5M capex but rounded it to $300/household for 12,000 households. $3.5M divided by 12,000 is **$291.67**, which is close, but your revenue math fails. At $120/month, 12,000 households generate $1.44M/month ($17.28M/year). However, 12,000 kW (12 MW) generating 800 kWh/home/month implies a total generation of 9.6 GWh/month. If the subscription covers 100% of usage, the math holds only if the solar array actually produces that much. In DFW, 12 MW typically produces ~1,600 MWh/month (capacity factor ~15%), not the 9,600 MWh required to cover 800 kWh for 12,000 homes. **You are oversubscribed by roughly 6x.** You can only support ~2,000 homes at that usage level with 12 MW, or you must drastically reduce the per-home energy allocation.
2.  **Impossible O&M:** A $0 O&M budget is a non-starter. Solar assets require cleaning, inverter monitoring, and vegetation management. Your noted $150k/year is likely too low for 12 MW across three sites (industry standard is ~$15–$20/kW/year, suggesting $180k–$240k). Ignoring this destroys credibility.
3.  **Revenue Logic:** You cannot "lock in a 5% discount on the state-mandated net-metering tariff" as a revenue driver. Net metering is a credit mechanism for excess generation, not a wholesale price you sell at a discount. Your revenue is the $120 subscription fee; the net metering value is a cost offset for the *utility*, not direct income for you unless you have a specific PPA structure.

**Alternative Route: Commercial PPA**
A Power Purchase Agreement (PPA) with local businesses is lower risk for a pilot. Instead of recruiting 12,000 residential subscribers (high churn, high marketing cost), target 3–5 large commercial rooftops or ground leases.
*   **Structure:** Install capacity directly on their sites or nearby; sell power at a fixed rate (e.g., $0.09/kWh) under a 10-year contract.
*   **Benefit:** Eliminates customer acquisition risk, ensures 100% offtake, and stabilizes cash flow. The 12 MW could be fully utilized by ~10–15 medium-sized commercial loads, avoiding the residential subscription gap.
