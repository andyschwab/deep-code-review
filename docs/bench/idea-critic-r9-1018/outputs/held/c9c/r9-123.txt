**Recommendation:** Do not proceed with the full $150,000 investment yet; instead, propose a **$35,000 capped pilot** to scan 20 flagship works and test the streaming cost model, with a strict "go/no-go" decision gate in Q1 2026 based on verified bandwidth expenses and initial ticket uptake.

**Why this could collapse:**
1.  **The Bandwidth Black Box:** Your break-even math assumes stable hosting costs, but you admitted the per-GB streaming cost for high-res VR is unverified. If actual traffic costs exceed the vendor's low-end estimate by even 40%, your $20,000 hosting budget evaporates, destroying the Q4 2026 break-even target.
2.  **Overestimated Conversion:** You need 2,500 tickets at $12 to break even. This requires converting ~69% of your *entire* current annual revenue base into VR buyers, assuming the 15% boost materializes immediately. Historically, immersive add-ons see <5% attach rates without massive marketing spend; your $15,000 marketing budget (~$6 per acquired customer) is likely insufficient to drive 2,500 sales.
3.  **License Cap Risk:** The $70,000 license covers 500 concurrent users. If a marketing push succeeds and you hit 501 users, does the tour crash, or do you face massive overage fees? The plan lacks a contingency for this hard ceiling.

**Arithmetic & Cost Gaps:**
*   **Revenue Mismatch:** Current revenue is $300,000. A 15% boost is $45,000. Your VR ticket revenue target is $30,000 (2,500 × $12). This leaves only $15,000 to be generated by the 3% commission on physical sales. To get $15,000 at 3%, you need $500,000 in *additional* physical sales. That implies the VR tour must drive a 166% increase in physical sales volume, which contradicts the conservative "15% overall boost" assumption. The numbers do not balance.
*   **Timeline Drag:** A 12-month rollout starting July 2025 means no revenue until mid-2026, yet you project break-even by Q4 2026. This leaves only two quarters to recoup $150k, requiring ~$75k/quarter in net profit, which is highly aggressive given the ramp-up period.

**Kill Criterion:**
Launch the pilot only if the vendor contract caps bandwidth costs at a fixed monthly rate. **Pull the plug** if the pilot's first-month verified bandwidth cost exceeds $1,500 or if ticket sales fall below **400** (not 800, as your total budget requires a lower threshold to remain viable) in the first 60 days of the full launch.
