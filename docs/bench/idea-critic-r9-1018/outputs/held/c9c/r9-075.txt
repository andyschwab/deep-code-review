**Recommendation:** Proceed with a **3-month, capped pilot** (not 12 months) targeting **500 unique users** rather than a percentage of total footfall, with a strict **go/no-go decision** at the 90-day mark based on verified engagement data.

**Strongest Objection (The Adoption Cliff):**
The proposal's financial viability collapses if the **80% adoption assumption** proves false, which is highly probable given that **30% of your patrons are 65+**. VR headsets often present significant usability barriers for seniors (motion sickness, interface complexity, physical weight). If only 40% of the senior demographic participates (a conservative failure mode) alongside 90% of younger users, your total adoption drops to ~65%, missing the target by nearly 20%. This volume shortfall directly invalidates the projected €18,000 in ancillary sales and the 15% footfall increase, leaving the library with €25,500 in stranded hardware costs and €15,000 in licensing fees without the ROI to cover them. The risk is not just low usage, but **active alienation** of your largest single demographic if the experience is perceived as exclusionary or difficult.

**Smarter Alternative:**
Before committing €25,500 to hardware, execute a **zero-capital partnership pilot** with a local university or tech college. Borrow 5 headsets for a 4-week pop-up event in exchange for student-led data collection and technical support. This validates the **senior adoption rate** and **staff training burden** with <€2,000 in content licensing costs. If the data confirms high engagement, *then* purchase the full fleet. This reverses the risk: you prove the value before buying the assets, rather than buying the assets to hope for the value.

**Kill Criteria (The Stop Condition):**
Do not wait three months to fail. Implement a **30-day interim review** with a hard stop condition: If **unique user count < 150** (approx. 5% of monthly traffic) OR if **senior participation < 20%** of the senior visitor base, **terminate the pilot immediately**. Do not extend the timeline to "give it more time"; the friction is inherent to the medium or the setup, not the duration. If these thresholds are met, pivot the budget to the lower-cost Augmented Reality (AR) app alternative, which utilizes patrons' existing smartphones and removes the hardware barrier entirely.

*Note: The 200 staff hours allocated for setup may be underestimated if senior assistance requires 1:1 staffing; verify this during the university partnership phase.*
*Ensure the licensing agreement includes a 30-day exit clause to avoid being locked into the €15,000 annual fee if the pilot fails early.*
