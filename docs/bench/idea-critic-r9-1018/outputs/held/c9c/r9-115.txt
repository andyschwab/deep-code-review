The satellite backhaul cost is an unverified variable that could destroy your unit economics; if actual usage exceeds estimates, the $0.10/GB estimate could double, erasing the $45,000 hardware/marketing buffer. A short, cheap pilot in one specific Fiordland zone validates the connectivity cost and real download rates before scaling.

**Principal Failure Modes:**
1.  **Connectivity Costs:** Satellite data in deep Fiordland often exceeds $0.10/GB during peak transmission; if live tracking requires >50MB/user/month, costs will outpace the $30 fee.
2.  **Adoption Friction:** Tourists in remote areas prioritize battery life and offline maps over live sharing; if <40% download the app despite having smartphones, the network effect fails.
3.  **Hardware Fragility:** Rugged tablets may fail faster than anticipated in wet, cold conditions, creating a replacement cycle not budgeted in the initial $120k.

**Kill Criteria (Stop/Pivot Triggers):**
*   **Cost Trigger:** If verified satellite quotes exceed $0.15/GB, **STOP** immediately; the model is insolvent.
*   **Adoption Trigger:** If <30% of guests with smartphones activate the app within the first 30 days of the pilot, **PIVOT** to a purely offline guide feature (no live backhaul).
*   **Retention Trigger:** If Daily Active Users drop below 10% of installs after week two, **STOP**; the value proposition is weak.

**Overlooked Factors & Better Alternatives:**
*   **Battery Drain:** Live GPS and satellite transmission drain batteries rapidly; guests may disable the app to save power for photos/emergencies.
*   **Alternative:** Instead of building a custom app, license an existing offline-first mapping platform (e.g., Avenza or similar) and add a simple SMS-based "sighting alert" system. This removes the $120k dev cost, shifts infrastructure risk to the vendor, and works on existing guest devices without a heavy download.
