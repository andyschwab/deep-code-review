**Recommendation: Proceed with a "Go-If" condition.**
Approve the MetroPulse Card rollout only if the contract includes a clause allowing a pivot to mobile-first infrastructure should physical card adoption fall below 30% within six months. Do not commit to the full 150,000 unit initial run; cap the first batch at 50,000 to validate the loss rate and cash-switching behavior before scaling.

**How this could fall apart:**
1.  **Hidden Loss Costs:** Your budget assumes $0.50 per card for losses, but a 6% loss rate on 150,000 cards implies 9,000 replacements. If the actual replacement cost (logistics + issuance) exceeds the unit price or if the loss rate spikes in a general population versus a pilot group, the $3.2M production budget will bleed into operational reserves immediately.
2.  **Cannibalization & Redundancy:** With 45% mobile adoption, forcing a hardware rollout risks paying for a parallel system that serves a shrinking demographic. If the "cash-to-card" switch is lower than expected because riders prefer the app you already own, the $850k/year backend fee becomes a sunk cost for underutilized capacity.
3.  **Throughput Illusion:** The 12-second gain relies on users having the card ready. If adoption is low, fare gates remain configured for cash processing for the majority, negating the throughput benefit while maintaining the high cost of cash handling.

**Kill Criteria & Trigger:**
*   **Metric:** Physical card active usage rate.
*   **Tripwire:** If active usage is <30% of issued cards after six months.
*   **Action:** Immediately halt the second production run and reallocate the remaining backend budget to enhance the mobile app's offline capabilities or NFC phone integration.
*   **Review Date:** Set a formal review for exactly six months from launch day.

*Note: Your break-even calculation likely underestimates the drag of the 6% loss rate if logistical overheads are higher than the $0.50 hard cost. Verify the total cost of ownership for a replacement card before signing.*
*Note: The "12 seconds per passenger" metric appears to be a total processing time reduction rather than a per-passenger speedup; clarify this figure to avoid overpromising throughput gains to the Deputy Director.*
