**Recommendation: GO-IF**
Proceed with launch preparation only if a binding path to Ministry certification is secured within 60 days and a pilot study validates that real-world energy savings exceed $250/year. Without these, the project carries unacceptable regulatory and value-proposition risk.

**Principal Failure Modes**
1.  **Regulatory Blockade:** The 3–9 month certification window is a critical path killer. If approval slips past month 6, the May 2025 mass-production start becomes impossible, burning cash on idle inventory and delaying revenue by a full year.
2.  **Value Proposition Collapse:** The $450 annual saving assumption is fragile. If electricity rates drop or the AI underperforms in varied climates, savings could halve. At $149 (a $20 premium over competitors), the payback period stretches from 4 months to over 8 months, destroying the primary sales argument.
3.  **Hidden Cost Erosion:** The $78 BOM excludes installation labor, which for AI thermostats often requires professional HVAC setup. If installation adds $100+ to the customer's out-of-pocket cost, the total price ($249+) makes the unit uncompetitive against simple $129 DIY alternatives.
4.  **Adoption Overreach:** Relying on 80% adoption (16,000 units) from an existing base of 20,000 is optimistic. Smart-home users are often segmented; if only 40% convert, you miss the 15,000 unit target, leaving fixed R&D costs ($2.2M) unrecovered.

**Early Indicator to Pull the Plug**
**The "Pilot Payback" Metric:** Run a 500-unit beta with real users immediately. If the measured average energy saving after 90 days annualizes to less than $250, **kill the premium pricing model or pivot** to a subscription service. Do not proceed to mass production if the hardware cannot prove its core economic promise in a live environment. Additionally, if the Ministry of Trade does not provide a provisional approval letter within 60 days of submission, halt production tooling to prevent sunk cost accumulation.
