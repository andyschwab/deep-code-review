**Recommendation:** Approve the PulseNest rollout only as a **conditional pilot** capped at 5,000 users for six months, with a strict "go/no-go" review based on verified data costs before expanding to the full 120,000 target.

**Strongest Objection (The Data Cost Trap):**
Your financial model relies on two unverified assumptions: that 80% of patients own compatible smartphones and that data rates/caps will remain static. If the actual smartphone penetration is lower (e.g., 60%), you lose 24,000 patients immediately, rendering the "mobile-first" strategy inequitable and ineffective. More critically, if "cold-chain alerts" and rich-media reminders increase monthly data consumption per patient beyond 50MB, your projected operational costs could triple, blowing the 1 million KES five-year budget in just two years. The pilot's 92% capture rate is promising but does not prove financial sustainability at scale under variable network conditions.

**Better Alternative Missed:**
Before committing 37.5 million KES to hardware (150 tablets) and licensing, investigate a **hybrid USSD/SMS fallback** or a **provider-held device model**. Instead of assuming patient ownership, equip community health workers with the 150 tablets to visit patients, ensuring 100% device compatibility and controlled data usage. This shifts the cost from unpredictable patient data plans to a fixed, manageable operational line item and removes the barrier for the 20% of patients without smartphones.

1.  **Cost Threshold:** The average monthly data cost per active patient exceeds **150 KES** (a 25% buffer over current estimates) for two consecutive months.
