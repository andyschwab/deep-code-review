**Key Failure Points for Alejandro Ramirez**

1.  **Conversion Rate Overestimation:** The plan assumes a 20% conversion (2,400 members) from the retail base to hit the 5,000-member target, implying you need nearly double your current customer list or massive new acquisition. If conversion stays at a typical 2–5%, you will secure only 240–600 members, generating $28k–$72k instead of $600k, leaving the $150k budget largely unrecoverable.
2.  **Regulatory Blockade:** The unresolved Chilean export regulations represent a binary kill switch. If new laws restrict direct-to-consumer bottle counts to overseas subscribers, the fulfillment model collapses immediately for international targets, rendering the hub investment useless for that segment.
3.  **Unit Economics Erosion:** The $30 variable cost is fragile. If shipping rates rise or packaging costs increase by just $10, the contribution margin drops significantly, pushing breakeven from 12 months to potentially 24+ months, straining cash flow.

**Smarter Route:**
Before building a proprietary fulfillment hub, partner with an existing wine-specific e-commerce platform (e.g., WineDirect or Vinea). This shifts the $150k capital expenditure to operational fees, validates the 20% conversion assumption with lower risk, and leverages their existing compliance frameworks for international shipping.

**Pull-the-Plug Trigger:**
Abort or pause the launch if, by **April 1, 2025**, you have not secured written confirmation of compliance with Chilean export limits for your target markets, OR if a pilot campaign to 1,000 retail customers yields a conversion rate below **8%**.

*Note: The current math requires acquiring ~2,600 new customers beyond the retail list to hit 5,000 members; verify if the marketing budget supports this volume.*
*Ensure the "fulfillment hub" budget includes bonded warehouse licensing, which is often excluded from initial estimates.*
