**Executive Summary: SunGrid Alliance DFW Pilot Critique**

Your current model contains a fatal arithmetic error and a dangerous cost omission that invalidate the projected 15% return.

**1. Critical Flaws in Current Model**
*   **Revenue Mismatch:** You plan to install 12,000 kW (12 MW) but only sell to 12,000 households at 800 kWh/month.
    *   Total Monthly Demand: 9.6 million kWh.
    *   Total Monthly Generation (DFW avg ~1,300 kWh/kW/yr): ~1.3 million kWh.
    *   **Result:** Your array produces only ~13.5% of the energy your subscribers require. You cannot fulfill 12,000 subscriptions with this hardware. To match demand, you need ~89,000 kW of capacity, increasing CapEx from $3.5M to ~$26M.
*   **The "Free" O&M Trap:** Removing the $150k annual maintenance cost creates a false profit center.
    *   Over 2 years, this is a $300k expense.
    *   Against a $3.5M CapEx, this alone reduces your raw return significantly before financing or land costs. A solar pilot with $0 O&M is operationally impossible; inverters fail, panels degrade, and vegetation management is mandatory.

**2. Stronger Alternative: Commercial PPA Model**
Instead of a residential subscription (high churn, high customer acquisition cost, complex billing), pivot to a **Commercial Power Purchase Agreement (PPA)** with local warehouses or data centers in DFW.
*   **Why:** Commercial loads are stable, daytime-heavy (matching solar peak), and require fewer contracts (1 deal vs. 12,000).
*   **Structure:** Sign a 10-year PPA at a fixed rate (e.g., $0.09/kWh) with 3 large industrial users.
*   **Benefit:** Eliminates the "subscription churn" risk and aligns generation capacity directly with verified load profiles, securing bankable revenue for the 15% IRR.

**3. The Kill Criterion**
Maya should pull the plug immediately if: **Subscriber uptake is < 4,000 households by Month 6.**
*   *Reasoning:* With the current 12 MW design, you only need ~4,000 users to match generation output. If you cannot sign 4,000 users in six months, the unit economics for the residential model are broken, and the project should pivot to the Commercial PPA model or terminate to preserve capital.

***

Additionally, the "5% discount on net-metering" is a regulatory assumption, not a controllable variable; base the stress test on the base tariff rate alone.
