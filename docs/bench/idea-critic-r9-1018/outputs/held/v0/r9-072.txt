**Recommendation:** Proceed with the rollout **only if** you re-baseline the financial model using a verified per-order labor cost from a stress test, and adopt a "Go-If" strategy where full hardware deployment is contingent on hitting a 45% pick-up rate in a 4-week high-volume trial.

**Top Failure Modes:**
1.  **Labor Cost Explosion:** Your model assumes pilot efficiency scales linearly. In reality, scaling from 3 to hundreds of stores often increases per-order labor costs due to training dilution and complex inventory integration, potentially turning the 9% ROI negative.
2.  **Conversion Overestimation:** A 12% conversion from "online browsers" is exceptionally high for a new behavior. If actual conversion is half that (6%), revenue drops to $600k, barely covering the $500k operational cost, leaving no return on the $350k capital spend.
3.  **Hardware Sunk Cost:** Spending $150k on proprietary hardware before validating the unit economics at scale creates a heavy sunk cost fallacy, making it harder to pivot to a leaner software-only or partnership model later.

**Abandonment Condition (Kill Criteria):**
Terminate the expansion immediately if, after six months of full rollout:
*   The **net ROI remains below 4%** (half the projected target), OR
*   The **on-time pick-up rate falls below 55%** (indicating systemic fulfillment breakdown rather than pilot anomalies).

**Note:** The $350k pilot spend is already sunk; do not let it justify further investment if the unit economics fail the stress test. Consider pausing hardware purchases to explore a white-label partnership for the next phase to reduce capex risk.
