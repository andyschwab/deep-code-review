**Recommendation: Do not push ahead in the current form; pause to resolve the royalty fee unknown and re-price before seeking board approval.**

Launching with an unpriced core cost driver (royalties) makes the €120,000 budget and €80,640 revenue projection unreliable. If royalties exceed €0.50 per stream or require a flat fee above €15,000, the pilot becomes insolvent before month three. The current math assumes 70% conversion (840 users) from existing members while offering a 30% discount, yet ignores churn and acquisition costs. This creates a "best-case" revenue bubble that likely vanishes once real-world friction and missing costs are applied.

**Principal Failure Modes:**
1.  **Royalty Insolvency:** Without a fixed royalty model, a hit play could generate high traffic but negative margin, draining the production budget instantly.
2.  **Conversion Overestimation:** Assuming 70% of members will adopt a new paid tier is aggressive; if adoption hits 40% (480 users), revenue drops to ~€46,000, leaving a €79,000 hole against costs.
3.  **Discount Cannibalization:** Offering a 30% discount to existing members may devalue the service without guaranteeing retention, especially if the content library is thin during the pilot.

**Kill Criteria:**
Abandon or fundamentally rethink the concept if **fewer than 350 subscribers (approx. 42% of the 70% target) are active by the end of Month 2**, or if the **confirmed royalty structure requires more than 25% of gross revenue per stream**. These thresholds indicate the unit economics cannot support the production overhead, regardless of marketing spend.
