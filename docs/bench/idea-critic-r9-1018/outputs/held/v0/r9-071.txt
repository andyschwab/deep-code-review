**Recommendation: Proceed with a "Go-If" Pilot**
Approve the pilot strictly under the condition that the €1.2M budget is capped as a hard limit, with the €400k core license payment split: 50% upfront and 50% contingent on achieving the 15% time-saving metric by month two. Do not commit to the full six-month term or the hardware upgrades until the first milestone is verified. The potential €350k annual upside justifies the experiment, but the 12% material waste risk and unquoted plugin costs make a full immediate commitment financially unsafe.

**Top Failure Modes**
1.  **Hidden Integration & Plugin Costs:** The plan explicitly lacks quotes for advanced rendering plugins. If these exceed €50k or require custom integration work not covered in the €250k hardware/training bucket, the ROI collapses.
2.  **Material Waste Outweighs Time Savings:** A 12% rise in material waste could easily erase the labor savings. If material costs are high relative to labor rates, the net financial impact may be negative despite the 30% modeling speedup.
3.  **Skill Gap & Adoption Friction:** The assumption of an immediate 30% efficiency gain ignores the learning curve. If the team requires more than the allocated training time to reach proficiency, the "eight weeks freed" will not materialize within the pilot window.

**Walk-Away Trigger**
Terminate the pilot immediately if, after the first two projects (approx. month two), the verified reduction in modeling time is **less than 15%** OR if the **material waste increase exceeds 8%**. Either condition indicates the tool cannot meet the projected ROI without unacceptable operational drag.

**Notes**
*   The "eight weeks freed" calculation assumes linear scalability; verify this against the specific complexity of the three selected pilot projects before starting.
*   Secure a fixed-price quote for all required plugin licenses before signing the core agreement to prevent budget creep.
