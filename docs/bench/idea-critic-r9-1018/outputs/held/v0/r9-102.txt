**Strongest Objection: The Math Does Not Support the Projection**
Your reported total spend of $55,000 contradicts your unit economics. 150 devices at $350 each equals $52,500, not $55,000. More critically, your $12M ARR projection is mathematically impossible based on the provided conversion assumptions. Even if you captured 100% of the pilot users (not just 15%) at $12/month, the annual revenue would be ~$21,600. To reach $12M ARR at $144/year per user, you need ~83,333 subscribers. Achieving this from a 150-person pilot implies a scaling factor of over 550x with no accounted cost for customer acquisition, manufacturing scale-up, or churn. Presenting these figures as calculated rather than aspirational targets will destroy credibility immediately.

**More Compelling Go-to-Market Alternative**
Pivot the narrative from "mass subscription readiness" to "high-value elite validation." Instead of projecting millions from a 150-person sample, position the pilot as a proof-of-concept for a premium tier ($50+/month) targeting professional teams and high-net-worth individuals first. This validates the technology and wear-rate assumptions with lower volume requirements, reduces the pressure on immediate mass-market conversion, and aligns better with the "athlete-focused" branding before attempting a consumer land-grab.

**Kill Criteria (Pull-the-Plug Events)**
1.  **Regulatory:** FDA clearance not secured by **September 30, 2024**. Any delay beyond this pushes the Q2 2025 ship date into the next fiscal year, invalidating the current cash-flow model.
2.  **Product-Market Fit:** In a follow-up study of 500 users, if daily wear rate drops below **65%** (vs. the assumed 80%) or conversion to paid subscription is below **5%** after 90 days. The current 80% wear rate in a controlled pilot of motivated athletes is likely an outlier; if real-world adherence isn't significantly high, the value proposition collapses.
