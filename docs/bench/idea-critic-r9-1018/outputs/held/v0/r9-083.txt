**First Red Flag:** The financial model ignores downtime risk while claiming a 2-year break-even. If the system suffers just two 5-day downtime incidents annually, you lose ~55,000 liters of potential recycling per incident. At your calculated savings rate (~$0.30 per liter saved), a single major failure wipes out nearly $17k of annual benefit, extending your break-even period significantly beyond the promised 2 years. Presenting a "2-year" certainty to the CFO without modeling this volatility undermines credibility immediately.

**Better Path:** Reframe the pitch from "cost savings" to "risk hedging." Instead of promising a fixed 2-year ROI, present a range: "Best case 2 years, likely case 2.8 years assuming standard maintenance downtime." Propose a phased rollout or a performance-based contract with the vendor where final payment is tied to achieving the 30% recycling target over the first 6 months, shifting the downtime risk to them.

**Abandonment Scenarios (Kill Criteria):**
1.  **Regulatory Delay:** If the discharge permit is not approved by **February 1, 2025**, abandon the March 15 installation start. A delayed permit leaves you with idle capital and no revenue, destroying the ROI math.
2.  **Energy Spike:** If local electricity rates rise above **$0.11 per kWh** before signing, halt. The treatment process is energy-intensive; at this rate, your operational costs will exceed your water savings, making the loop a net loss.
3.  **Vendor Guarantee:** If the vendor refuses to contractually guarantee **95% uptime** or provide penalties for falling below the 30% recycling volume in year one, do not proceed. Without this, you are buying an unproven asset with unlimited downside.
