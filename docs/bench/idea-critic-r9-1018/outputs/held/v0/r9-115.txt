**Recommendation: Pitch as a "Go-If" Pilot, Not a Full Commitment**
Do not ask for approval to spend the full $120,000 yet. Instead, pitch a 3-month, $25,000 proof-of-concept (PoC) strictly to validate the satellite backhaul costs and actual user download rates in Fiordland. Proceed to the full 12-month rollout only if the PoC confirms data costs are under $0.15/GB and at least 60% of guests download the app without heavy staff assistance.

**Principal Failure Modes**
1.  **Connectivity Cost/Reality:** The $0.10/GB estimate is unverified; real-world terrain in Fiordland may require expensive repeaters or result in spotty service that frustrates users rather than delighting them.
2.  **Adoption Friction:** Assuming 80% adoption ignores the friction of downloading an app in remote areas with poor signal before the tour starts; if guests cannot pre-load it, adoption will crash.
3.  **Hardware Burden:** Rugged tablets add weight and maintenance for guides; if the app drains their battery or distracts from safety, guides will sabotage its use.
4.  **Revenue Timing:** Banking on a 15% booking uplift assumes the app drives *new* sales, but if it only serves existing guests, the ROI timeline extends beyond 12 months.

**Kill Criteria (Tripwires)**
*   **Pull the Plug:** If the PoC shows satellite data costs exceed $0.20/GB, or if fewer than 40% of guests successfully activate the app during the trial.
*   **Change Direction:** If guests love the data but hate the download process, pivot immediately to providing pre-loaded rugged devices to all guests instead of a BYOD (Bring Your Own Device) model.

**Overlooked Factors & Better Alternatives**
*   **The "Zero-Signal" Start:** You are overlooking that guests cannot download the app *in* the remote region. You must mandate pre-trip downloads via email/SMS, which requires a robust reminder system you haven't budgeted for.
*   **Better Alternative:** Before building a custom app, test the demand using existing offline mapping tools (like Gaia GPS or Avenza) with custom PeakRover layers. This costs nearly nothing, validates the "live sighting" value proposition, and avoids the $120k risk entirely. If guests pay a premium for the custom layers on existing platforms, *then* build the standalone app.
