**Recommended Course of Action:**
Proceed with a **conditional "Go" limited to a 45-day discovery sprint** rather than full development. Allocate $15,000 immediately to test the conversion rate from social impressions to donations using a low-fidelity landing page. Do not commit the remaining $105,000 or lock in the June 15 start date until this sprint validates that you can achieve a **0.625% conversion rate** (1,250 donors from 80,000 impressions) at a cost per acquisition under $15. If validated, proceed with the full build; if not, pivot to a donor-partnership model or halt.

**Primary Failure Modes:**
1.  **Conversion Collapse:** The 12% growth assumption relies on viral mechanics that rarely translate from organic nonprofit posts to cold crowdfunding. If your actual conversion is 0.2% (industry average for cold traffic) instead of the required 0.625%, you will secure only ~160 donors ($12,000 revenue), leaving a $108,000 budget hole.
2.  **Arithmetic & Fee Error:** Your model ignores payment processing fees. At ~2.9% + $0.30 per transaction, a $75 donation nets only ~$72.50. To net $150,000, you actually need ~2,070 donors, not 2,000. Furthermore, the "5% platform fee" is unclear: if this is revenue *for* you, it adds $7,500; if it is a cost *paid* to a processor, it further erodes margins. Currently, the $150k gross revenue barely covers the $120k budget even before marketing overspend or salary costs are fully accounted for.
3.  **Timeline Mismatch:** You cannot start a pilot in June 2025 if you are still validating assumptions in a 45-day sprint starting now (October 2024/2025 context implies immediate action). If the sprint slips, the six-month window pushes into the holiday giving season or past it, invalidating the "similar nonprofit" 2022 data which likely relied on Q4 generosity.

**Pull-the-Plug Criteria:**
Abort the project entirely if, after spending the initial $15,000 test budget, you fail to secure **250 committed donors** (at the $75 average) or if the Cost Per Acquisition exceeds **$20**. These thresholds prove the unit economics cannot support the scale required to cover the $120,000 operational budget within the six-month window.
