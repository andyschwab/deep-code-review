**Recommendation: Do not proceed to full investment yet; execute a 6-month "Take-Back Validation" pilot limited to logistics and enrollment testing before committing the €2.5M capex.**

The proposal relies on two unverified assumptions that currently invalidate the 15% IRR projection: (1) achieving 30% customer enrollment without firm commitments, and (2) zero marginal transport costs under existing contracts. If enrollment lands at 15% (a common realistic baseline for voluntary schemes) or if haulage fees are triggered, the operational margin collapses given the thin spread between the €180/t scrap price and the €300k annual operating cost. Melting capacity is not the bottleneck; feedstock security is. Investing in the furnace before securing the 5,000t flow creates stranded assets.

**Key Failure Modes:**
*   **Participation Shortfall:** Failure to secure signed take-back agreements from OEMs covering at least 2,500t (50% of target) renders the unit economics negative.
*   **Transport Cost Leakage:** Existing logistics contracts likely exclude reverse-flow heavy haulage; unexpected per-tonne fees will directly erode the reclaimed alloy margin.
*   **Scrap Price Volatility:** A 10% dip in global scrap prices (to €162/t) eliminates the projected surplus, as the model lacks a hedging strategy or fixed-price off-take agreement.

**Abort Trigger:**
Terminate the initiative if, after 12 months of the validation pilot, the verified Internal Rate of Return (IRR) on the operational cash flow falls below **8%**, or if signed customer commitments cover less than **40%** of the required 5,000t volume.
