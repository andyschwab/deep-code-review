**Claim:** Launch a 9-month, $350k pilot of Grace Beacon robots in three senior homes in Q1 2027 to save $120k/year.

**Single Most Potent Objection:** The business case collapses on the timeline mismatch. You are budgeting 4 weeks for Health Canada certification, yet even a minor delay pushes the start date out of Q1 2027. If certification takes the industry-standard 12–16 weeks for novel AI companions, the pilot cannot generate the projected 9 months of operational data or the $120k savings within the fiscal year. The "pilot" becomes a sunk-cost regulatory waiting room with no ROI, forcing a budget overrun before a single resident interacts with a unit.

**Smarter, Lower-Risk Route:** Do not buy hardware yet. Execute a **simulation-only pilot** using existing staff tablets and a software mock-up of the Grace Beacon interface. This validates the core assumption (that residents will actually use the AI to reduce nurse calls) for under $20k. If the behavioral data proves a 15% drop in call volume without physical robots, *then* procure hardware. This decouples the regulatory risk (software certification) from the capital risk ($150k hardware spend).

**Walk-Away/Pivot Triggers:**
1.  **Regulatory:** If Health Canada certification is not granted within **6 weeks** of submission, pause hardware procurement immediately.
2.  **Unit Economics:** If the simulated or actual cost per resident exceeds **$2,300** (total cost divided by 150 residents), pivot to a partnership model where the vendor bears the hardware cost in exchange for a per-interaction fee.

**Note:** The current math assumes 100% utilization immediately; verify if the 15% drop in calls is based on observed behavior or vendor projections. Ensure the $30k accessories cost is not double-counting the $30k/unit price if the vendor bundles them.
