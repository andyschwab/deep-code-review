**Recommendation: No-Go on the dedicated hub; pursue a Hybrid "Pop-Up" Model instead.**

The strongest objection is a **fatal unit-economics error driven by unverified fixed costs and ignored variables**. Your math assumes the hub becomes cheaper after two years based on a 30% delivery time reduction saving 1.1M PHP annually. However, you are comparing a **fixed** cost of 26.4M PHP/year (2.2M × 12) against a **variable** third-party cost of only 1.8M PHP/year. Even if you eliminated *all* third-party fees, the hub costs 24.6M PHP *more* annually than your current setup. The projected savings of 1.1M PHP are negligible (less than 5% of the hub's operating cost) and cannot bridge this gap. Furthermore, ignoring warehousing taxes and regulatory fees in a specialized zone introduces massive downside risk; if these fees exceed 200k PHP/month, the project bleeds cash faster than anticipated. The assumption that courier rates will stay flat for three years is also fragile; if they drop due to market competition, your internal hub becomes even less competitive.

**Better Alternative: The Hybrid "Pop-Up" Model**
Instead of a permanent 2.2M PHP/month commitment, lease a flexible micro-fulfillment space or negotiate a "dedicated zone" within your current 3PL partner's facility in Laguna.
*   **Cost:** Pay only for active storage and pick/pack volume (variable), avoiding the 26.4M PHP fixed burn.
*   **Speed:** Achieve the 2.8-day delivery target by positioning inventory closer to artisans without owning the infrastructure.
*   **Risk:** Reversible within months if volume doesn't hit 15k orders; no long-term equipment sunk costs.

**Failure Modes & Kill Criteria**
1.  **Volume Miss:** If orders stay below 10,000/month by Q2 2025, the cost per unit skyrockets, making the hub unsustainable.
2.  **Regulatory Shock:** If the unknown annual regulatory fee exceeds 5% of projected savings (approx. 55k PHP), the margin vanishes.
3.  **Rate War:** If major couriers drop rates by 15%, your internal cost advantage disappears entirely.

**Abort Trigger:**
Terminate the plan immediately if **monthly operating costs exceed 2.0 million PHP for two consecutive months** while order volume remains below 12,000 units. This indicates the fixed-cost model is fundamentally broken at your current scale.
