**Recommendation: Do not proceed with the two-van purchase as planned.**

Your current math creates an immediate €30,000 funding gap (€380k cost vs. €350k budget), and the unit economics appear flawed. Buying two vans at €190,000 each to serve three villages with a combined population of only 5,300 people is likely overcapitalized. Even capturing 100% of the market in those villages would require an unrealistic spend per customer to justify vehicles that cost double your previous fleet's unit price. Furthermore, your assumption that existing driver contracts can absorb extra routes without renegotiation or overtime pay is a hidden cost that threatens your margin, especially if fuel prices deviate from your static €1.70/litre assumption.

**Primary Failure Points:**
1.  **Capital Mismatch:** You cannot execute the plan without securing an additional €30,000 or reducing the scope to one van, which may not cover the three villages effectively.
2.  **Demand Overestimation:** A 25% volume jump relies on penetrating small villages where price sensitivity is unknown; if adoption is slow, you are left with massive depreciation on underutilized, expensive assets.
3.  **Operational Friction:** Existing drivers may resist or legally require extra compensation for expanded routes, eroding the projected profit before it starts.

**First Red Flag (Kill Criteria):**
As you noted, halt or rethink the rollout immediately if **first-quarter delivery revenue from the new villages stays below €12,000**. This indicates that either the volume or the price point (or both) is insufficient to support the heavy capital expenditure of the new vans.
