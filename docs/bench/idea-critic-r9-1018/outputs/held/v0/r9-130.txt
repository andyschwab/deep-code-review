**Recommendation:** Do not commit the full $3.5M upfront. Instead, execute a "Go-If" pilot: secure Ministry of Primary Industries (MPI) approval first, then launch a limited service to 500 homes in a single dense suburb using leased refrigerated vehicles and a manual booking system. Proceed to full fleet acquisition and custom platform development only if this pilot achieves a 60% six-month retention rate and confirms delivery costs under $6.50 per drop.

**Primary Collapse Vectors:**
1.  **Regulatory Block:** Launching before MPI approval creates immediate legal liability and potential shutdown.
2.  **Unit Economics Failure:** Your math contains a critical error. 10,000 deliveries at $6 each equals $60,000, not $55,000. More dangerously, with 12,000 homes, you project ~144,000 annual deliveries. At $6/drop, annual logistics cost is ~$864k. Annual revenue (12k homes * $28 * 12 months) is ~$4.03M. While this looks profitable, the 30% uptake assumption in six months requires acquiring 3,600 paying customers rapidly; if customer acquisition costs exceed $150 per home, you will burn the $3.5M capital before reaching break-even volume.
3.  **Route Density:** The $6/drop cost assumes perfect route density. In the Bay of Plenty's semi-rural pockets, stops per hour will drop, spiking costs to $9–$12, destroying margins.

**The Plug-Pull Trigger:** Abandon the full rollout immediately if the pilot cannot sustain a **cost-per-drop below $7.50** over four consecutive weeks or if **MPI approval is not granted within 90 days**.

**What You Are Overlooking:**
*   **Seasonality:** Milk consumption and delivery logistics vary by season; a July launch hits winter, potentially masking summer spoilage risks or route difficulties.
*   **Churn:** Subscription models live or die by retention. You have modeled uptake but not churn. If monthly churn exceeds 8%, the 12,000-home target is mathematically impossible without infinite marketing spend.

**Better Alternative:**
Partner with an existing local dairy distributor or supermarket chain to use their cold chain and routes, offering "Moormilk" as a premium line item. This eliminates the $3.5M capex, removes the driver hiring burden, and bypasses the need for a custom mobile platform initially.

**Exact Metric to Abandon Plan:**
If the pilot's **Net Revenue per Delivery** (Subscription Fee minus Cost of Goods minus Delivery Cost) falls below **$18.00** for two consecutive months, the unit economics cannot support the overhead, and the plan must be terminated.
