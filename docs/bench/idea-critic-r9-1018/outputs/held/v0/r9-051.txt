**Strongest Objection: The Financial Model Relies on Static Variables in a Volatile Policy Environment**
The proposal's 5-year payback and $12M valuation are critically fragile because they assume the 30% tax credit is guaranteed and electricity rates remain static relative to inflation. If the anticipated legislation tightens incentives post-2026, the effective project cost could jump by $3.6M instantly, extending payback beyond 7 years. Furthermore, budgeting $200k/year for O&M without escalation ignores historical trends where labor and specialized component costs outpace general inflation. If energy prices rise slower than projected (due to grid decarbonization or gas price drops) while our O&M costs rise faster, the "savings" could vanish, turning the asset into a net liability within the first decade. The Riverdale College comparison is dangerous; their 25% bill reduction may reflect their specific rate structure or consumption profile, not a universal constant we can bank on.

**Clearly Better Route: Phased Rollout via Power Purchase Agreement (PPA)**
Instead of a $12M capital expenditure (CapEx) with full ownership risk, pursue a **Power Purchase Agreement (PPA)** for a **phased 1-acre pilot**.
*   **Why:** A PPA requires $0 upfront capital. A third-party developer owns the system and sells us power at a fixed, discounted rate. They absorb the risk of tax credit changes, O&M escalation, and performance shortfalls.
*   **Phasing:** Install 150 kW now. This validates the actual production vs. the 30% load claim on *our* specific grid infrastructure before committing to the full 2 acres. It preserves capital for other net-zero initiatives and allows us to pivot if the 2026 legislative landscape shifts negatively.

**Kill Criteria: When to Pull the Plug**
You should halt or re-evaluate immediately if:
1.  **Legislative Trigger:** State legislation introduced before Q1 2025 explicitly reduces the solar tax credit below 20% or imposes new interconnection fees that increase the Levelized Cost of Energy (LCOE) by >10%.
2.  **Performance Gap:** The pilot phase (if adopted) or independent irradiance study shows actual generation potential is <85% of the modeled 8 MWh/day target due to campus shading or grid constraints.
3.  **Cost Escalation:** Final vendor bids exceed $14M total (adjusted for current market rates), indicating the $12M estimate was fundamentally disconnected from supply chain realities.

**Recommendation:** Do not approve the $12M CapEx plan yet. Authorize a feasibility study for a PPA-based pilot program to de-risk the financial and regulatory assumptions before the July 2025 deadline.

*Note: The 5-year payback claim appears overly optimistic given current interest rates and hardware costs; typical institutional solar paybacks are currently 7–9 years without aggressive subsidies.*
