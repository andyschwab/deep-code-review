**Recommendation:** Proceed with the pilot, but immediately re-scope the hardware budget and validate the volume conversion before signing contracts. The potential 5% price premium justifies the risk, provided you enforce strict stop-loss metrics.

**Critical Corrections & Risks:**
1.  **Volume Conversion Error:** Your pilot volume math is incorrect. 300,000 beans/month equals 3.6 million beans annually. At ~5,000 beans/kg, this is only ~720 kg (0.72 metric tons). This is **0.006%** of your 12,000-ton annual volume, not 25%. If you meant 300,000 *kilograms* or a different unit, the hardware budget collapses. At $0.12/tag, tagging 300,000 *beans* costs only $432/month, making your $50,000 hardware allocation (20% of $250k) vastly oversized unless you are tagging every single bean in the 25% volume scenario (which would require ~600 million tags/year, costing $72M). **Action:** Clarify the unit immediately; the current budget implies a massive unit mismatch.
2.  **Adoption Black Box:** The biggest collapse vector is farmer rejection. If farmers view RFID tags as surveillance or distrust the data ownership, adoption will stall. Without >85% farmer participation, the traceability chain breaks, rendering the blockchain useless.
3.  **Regulatory Surprise:** Hidden costs will likely emerge from data sovereignty laws (where the ledger is hosted) and export documentation requirements for digital twins. Budget an immediate $15,000 legal audit to prevent customs seizures.

*   **Pull the Plug:** If farmer adoption is **<75%** after Month 2, or if pilot operational costs exceed the budget by **>10%** (tighter than your 15% suggestion due to the small pilot scale).
