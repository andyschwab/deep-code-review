**Recommendation:** Proceed with a **6-week "Wizard of Oz" pilot** (mock AR using standard video calls) before committing the full €500k build. This validates the core assumption—that users will practice pronunciation in public settings—without the heavy engineering cost.

**Main Ways This Could Flop:**
1.  **The "Public Embarrassment" Barrier:** Your plan assumes users will speak a foreign language aloud in real-world AR settings. In reality, social anxiety often prevents this, regardless of gamification. If users feel silly talking to their phones on a bus or street, adoption will stall.
2.  **Hardware Fragmentation:** The 80% AR-capable device assumption is likely optimistic for *consistent* performance. AR drains battery rapidly and overheats devices, leading to poor user experiences that kill retention faster than the content saves it.
3.  **School Integration Friction:** Piloting in schools introduces regulatory hurdles (child data privacy/GDPR) and IT bureaucracy that rarely align with a 12-month consumer product roadmap. Delays here could burn your runway before launch.

**Alternative Approach:**
Consider a **"Camera-First" Non-AR Mode**. Instead of overlaying graphics in real-time (heavy battery/tech load), let users snap photos of objects and record pronunciation asynchronously. This captures 90% of the educational value (contextual learning) with 10% of the technical risk and battery drain, making it viable on older devices too.

**Pull-the-Plug Triggers:**
Abandon the full AR build immediately if:
*   **Pilot Conversion < 10%:** Fewer than 10% of pilot students use the feature more than twice a week after the novelty wears off (first 3 weeks).
*   **Technical Failure Rate > 15%:** More than 15% of sessions crash or fail to track due to device incompatibility or lighting conditions.
*   **Regulatory Delay > 6 Weeks:** If school district approval for data processing takes longer than 6 weeks, the B2B2C channel is likely too slow for your cash flow needs.

**Note:** The €30k UI kit is a sunk cost risk if the core behavior (public speaking) doesn't happen. Focus your pitch on validating the *behavior*, not the *technology*.
