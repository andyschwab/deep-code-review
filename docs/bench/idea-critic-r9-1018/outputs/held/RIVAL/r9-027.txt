**Recommendation to CEO Min‑jun Lee:**
Proceed with a **conditional pilot**, not a full launch. Authorize the $45,000 asset creation and $30,000 marketing budget immediately, but **freeze the $120,000 AR engine spend** until the SDK vendor's royalty fee is contractually defined. The current model assumes a best-case scenario where the royalty is negligible; if the fee exceeds 20% of revenue, the unit economics collapse, turning a projected $105,000 net profit into a loss. Secure the fee structure first to protect the downside.

**Top Three Failure Modes:**
1.  **Royalty Erosion:** The SDK vendor levies a per-user or high-revenue share fee that destroys the margin on the $9.99 price point, making the add-on unprofitable even at target conversion.
2.  **Conversion Friction:** The 15% conversion assumption (6,000 users) is overly optimistic for an AR add-on; if actual conversion lands below 8%, revenue drops to ~$160,000, failing to cover the $195,000 baseline investment.
3.  **Technical Fragmentation:** The AR layer fails to perform consistently across the diverse range of customer devices, leading to high refund rates and brand damage for the "Silk & Scrolls" franchise.

**Pull-the-Plug Metric:**
Abort the full engine rollout if the pilot phase (first 30 days) shows a **conversion rate below 9%** or if the finalized **SDK royalty fee exceeds 25% of gross revenue**.

**Hidden Risks:**
*   **Support & Maintenance Costs:** The plan lists zero ongoing engineering costs. AR requires constant updates for new iOS/Android versions and device-specific debugging. Budget at least $15,000/year for maintenance, or the product will break within 12 months.
*   **Cannibalization vs. Incrementality:** The forecast assumes all 6,000 buyers are *new* revenue. If 40% of these users would have bought the standard book anyway, your *incremental* revenue is far lower than projected, potentially failing to justify the development sprint.
*   **Flawed Comparison:** Do not compare this to successful gaming AR apps. Reading is a passive experience; forcing AR interaction may disrupt immersion rather than enhance it, a risk not present in gamified AR use cases.
