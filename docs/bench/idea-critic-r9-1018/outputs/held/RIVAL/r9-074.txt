**The Single Most Potent Objection: The "Pilot Purgatory" Trap**
The proposal fails because it attempts to validate a hardware deployment risk using a timeline and budget designed for software integration. With Health Canada certification penciled at an optimistic 4 weeks, a single delay pushes the pilot into "purgatory": you incur the full $150,000 hardware sunk cost and $200,000 integration spend without the regulatory clearance to generate the data needed to prove the $120,000 savings. Unlike a software pilot, you cannot iterate around a regulatory block; the hardware sits idle, burning cash while the board waits for a permission slip that determines the entire business model's viability.

**The Smarter, Lower-Risk Route: The "Tenant-Model" Partnership**
Do not buy the hardware or build the integration layer. Instead, approach Grace Beacon (or a competitor like ElliQ) with a **zero-CAPEX partnership proposal**.
*   **The Deal:** They provide the 5 units and handle the Health Canada certification burden as the vendor of record. You provide the three sites and staff access.
*   **The Cost Structure:** Shift from $350k upfront to a monthly "per-resident" fee contingent on active deployment.
*   **The Win:** This transfers the regulatory risk and hardware obsolescence risk entirely to the vendor. If certification takes 6 months, you pay nothing. If the robots don't reduce call volumes, you cancel the subscription. You validate the *outcome* (savings) without betting the capital on the *input* (hardware/compliance).

**The Specific Walk-Away Triggers**
Maya should green-light only under the partnership model. If forced to consider the current purchase plan, these are the hard stops:
1.  **Regulatory Trigger:** If Health Canada certification is not explicitly confirmed in writing within **45 days**. (The 4-week estimate is a hope, not a plan; 45 days is the operational cliff).
2.  **Unit Economics Trigger:** If the all-in cost per resident exceeds **$1,800** (not $2,500). At $2,500, the ROI period stretches beyond 18 months, which is unacceptable for unproven tech in a margin-sensitive sector. The pilot must prove viability at a scalable price point, not a subsidized one.
3.  **Efficacy Trigger:** If the pilot does not demonstrate a **10% reduction** in on-call visits within the first **90 days** of active use. If you aren't hitting 10% by month 3, the projected 15% annualized savings is a fantasy.

**Recommendation:** Reject the $350k CAPEX request. Authorize the negotiation of a vendor-managed pilot with a hard 45-day regulatory deadline.
