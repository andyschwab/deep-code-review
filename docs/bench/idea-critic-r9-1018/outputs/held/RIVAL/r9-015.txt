**The Toughest Objection: The Unit Economics Are Illusory**
Your plan relies on a fatal arithmetic error and an unproven conversion assumption. First, you cited $180,000 in *monthly* revenue based on an *annual* fee structure; if the $15 is indeed monthly, your cash flow timing is wrong, but if it's annual, your monthly operational runway is nonexistent. Second, assuming a 30% conversion from retail to a high-friction home delivery model is delusional; industry standards for cold-chain grocery conversion are typically under 5%. If only 5% convert (2,000 households), your revenue drops to $30,000/month against fixed van and labor costs that don't scale down linearly. You aren't launching a business; you're subsidizing logistics for a handful of customers while burning $250k.

**How This Rollout Could Flop**
1.  **Density Failure:** Refrigerated delivery profits live or die by drop density. If your 12,000 households are spread out, your $8/box delivery cost will balloon to $15+, wiping out the margin entirely.
2.  **Churn Shock:** Without historical data, if churn exceeds 8% monthly (common in subscriptions), you will spend 100% of your marketing budget just replacing lost customers, never growing.
3.  **Cold-Chain Break:** One summer heatwave causing spoilage in transit destroys brand trust instantly. The $250k van investment becomes a stranded asset if the route density doesn't justify the fleet.
