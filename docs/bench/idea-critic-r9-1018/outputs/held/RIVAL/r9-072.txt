**Recommendation:** Proceed with the rollout only if you can contractually cap per-order fulfillment labor at **$4.20**; otherwise, pivot immediately to a revenue-share partnership with an existing delivery platform (e.g., Rappi or Uber Eats) to transfer variable cost risk.

**Top Failure Modes:**
1.  **Labor Cost Escalation:** Your model implies a labor cost of ~$4.20/order to hit the $500k total cost target (based on ~118k projected orders). In the pilot, low volume likely masked inefficiencies. At scale, without strict automation or density, labor often balloons to $6–$8/order, which would turn your projected 9% ROI into a **negative 15% loss**.
2.  **The "70% Smartphone" Fallacy:** Assuming 70% of 2.8M customers are addressable is dangerous. Having a smartphone ≠ using it for grocery shopping. If actual app adoption is only 20% of that segment, your revenue drops by $960k, making the $350k sunk pilot cost impossible to recover.
3.  **Operational Drag:** A 71% on-time pick-up rate means **29% of customers** face friction (waiting, missing items, confusion). In click-and-collect, this friction kills retention faster than it acquires new users, cannibalizing your core in-store sales.

**Arithmetic & Assumption Gaps:**
*   **Order Volume Mismatch:** To generate $1.2M revenue at $38/order, you need **31,579 orders** in Year 1.
*   **Cost Per Order:** Your $500k staffing/fulfillment budget divided by 31,579 orders equals **$15.83 per order** in variable costs. Wait—your math implies $500k covers *all* incremental costs. If your gross margin on groceries is 25% ($9.50 profit per $38 order), and your fulfillment cost is $15.83, you are **losing $6.33 on every single order**.
*   **Correction Needed:** You likely meant $500k is *fixed* overhead, but if it includes variable labor, the unit economics are currently negative. You must isolate variable labor costs. If the $500k is total cost, you need an average order value (AOV) of **$63**, not $38, to break even.

**Pull-the-Plug Trigger:**
Abandon the plan if, after six months of full rollout:
*   **Adoption < 2.5%** of the smartphone-enabled base (not total base), OR
*   **Net ROI < 4%** (half your projection) due to variable fulfillment costs exceeding **$8.00 per order**.

**Leaner Alternative:**
Before spending more CAPEX on hardware, run a "virtual" click-and-collect using a white-label integration with a major delivery app. They handle the last-mile logistics and app interface; you handle the picking. This converts your $500k variable cost risk into a predictable 15–20% commission, preserving cash flow while validating true demand.
