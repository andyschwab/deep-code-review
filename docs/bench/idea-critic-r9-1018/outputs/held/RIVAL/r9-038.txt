**Recommendation: Do not approve full rollout yet.** Proceed only with a targeted winter-stress pilot of 10 units; withhold the remaining $240,000 in procurement and installation funds until cold-weather reliability is proven.

**Key Failure Points:**
1.  **Cold-Weather Blindness:** The sensor’s unverified performance below –20 °C creates a critical single point of failure. If the units drift or freeze during December/January, firefighters lose situational awareness precisely when visibility is lowest, potentially increasing injury risk rather than reducing response time.
2.  **Hidden Integration Costs:** The assumption of automatic pairing with the existing CAD system is a budgetary gap. If a middleware update or custom API bridge is required, the $90,000 installation fee will be exceeded, forcing a mid-project budget amendment or leaving devices unconnected.
3.  **Training Deficit:** The missing $30,000 training line item implies firefighters will receive complex new hardware without dedicated simulation time. In high-stress interior fires, unfamiliar interfaces lead to hesitation or misinterpretation of heat signatures, negating the 12% efficiency gain.

**Specific Trigger to Pull the Plug:**
Initiate an immediate stop-work order if the winter-stress pilot records **any** instance of sensor latency exceeding 2 seconds or signal dropout lasting longer than 10 seconds in temperatures below –20 °C. Additionally, if the CAD vendor confirms that software updates are required and the quote exceeds $15,000, revert to the drawing board to reassess the total cost of ownership.

**What Would Change My Mind:**
I would support an immediate full rollout only if the manufacturer provides a signed, warranty-backed specification guaranteeing operational integrity down to –30 °C *and* furnishes a written confirmation from your CAD vendor stating zero additional software costs are required for integration. Without these two documents, the financial and operational risks outweigh the extrapolated benefits.
