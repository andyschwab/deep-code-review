**Single Factor to Pull Back:**  
The unverified interaction between the AI-controlled micro-climate and Fromage du Mont's native microbial cultures. If the chamber suppresses or alters these cultures, the signature flavor profile of our cheese will be lost, rendering the efficiency gains irrelevant to our brand identity.

**Recommendation:**  
Proceed with the six-month pilot **only** under a strict "Flavor Fidelity" gate. Do not commit to the full €1.2M deployment until blind taste tests and chemical analysis confirm the pilot batch matches our current standard within a 5% variance. Treat the €7,200 annual electricity gap as a baseline operating cost increase, but view the microbial risk as an existential threat to product quality.

**Principal Ways It Could Go Wrong:**  
1.  **Flavor Drift:** The precise temperature control (+/- 0.5°C) inadvertently inhibits specific enzymes required for our unique terroir, producing a generic-tasting cheese.
2.  **Hidden Energy Spikes:** The estimated 5,000 kWh/month draw assumes steady-state operation; if the AI over-compensates for humidity fluctuations without dedicated hardware, energy costs could double, eroding margins.
3.  **Integration Failure:** The assumption that existing humidity systems remain untouched may be false; if the AI's rapid temperature shifts cause condensation spikes that overwhelm current dehumidifiers, mold risk increases rather than decreases.

**Specific Outcome to Abandon or Re-tool:**  
**Abandon:** If the pilot batch fails blind taste tests against current production or shows >10% deviation in volatile organic compound (VOC) profiles.  
**Re-tool:** If energy consumption exceeds 7,500 kWh/month or if humidity instability requires retrofitting the cellar infrastructure, necessitating a revised business case with higher CapEx/OpEx before full deployment.
