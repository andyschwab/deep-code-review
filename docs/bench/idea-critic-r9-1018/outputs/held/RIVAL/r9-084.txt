**Recommendation: NO-GO in current form.**

The plan relies on a single point of failure: the assumption that default rates will remain flat at 5% while expanding to new user segments. This is statistically unsupported. New segments possess different risk profiles; applying a model trained on existing users to them without a recalibration phase is not a rollout, it's an uncontrolled experiment with $1.2M of capital.

**Critical Failure Points:**

1.  **The "Flat Default" Fallacy:** You are assuming stationarity in a non-stationary environment. Expanding to new segments introduces distribution shift. If the model misprices risk in the new cohort by even 2%, your default rate could spike to 7-8%, erasing the profit margin from the 15% approval lift.
2.  **Regulatory Whiplash:** Singapore's MAS is rigorous on alternative data. If your "new segments" rely on data proxies that correlate with protected classes (race, location, device type), you face immediate compliance shutdown. The cost isn't just fines; it's license revocation.
3.  **Infrastructure Debt:** You identified $45k in hidden validation costs. This is likely a floor, not a ceiling. Real-time scoring for 12,000 users with complex alternative data feeds requires latency monitoring and fallback logic you haven't budgeted for. If the API lags, do you deny the loan or guess?

**The Kill Signal (Pull the Plug):**
During the first 2 weeks of the pilot, if the **validation rejection rate** on incoming data feeds exceeds 3%, or if the **observed default rate** in the new segment exceeds 5.5% (a mere 10% tolerance buffer), halt immediately. Do not wait for month-end reports.

**The Pivot Condition:**
Pivot to a **"Shadow Mode" Launch**. Do not approve loans based on the new model for the full 12,000 users yet. Run the model in parallel for 30 days, comparing its decisions against your current engine without funding the loans. This validates the default rate assumption with zero capital risk.

**Blind Spots That Change My Mind:**
I would only support a Go decision if you can demonstrate:
*   **Segment-Specific Backtesting:** Evidence that the model has been tested specifically on data resembling the *new* segments, not just the historical average.
*   **Regulatory Pre-Clearance:** Written confirmation from legal that the specific alternative data sources used for expansion do not violate fair lending guidelines under the current MAS framework.
*   **Dynamic Cap Mechanism:** A hard-coded system limit that automatically throttles approval volumes if default trends tick upward, preventing a runaway loss scenario.

Without these, you are not piloting a product; you are gambling with the bank's balance sheet.
