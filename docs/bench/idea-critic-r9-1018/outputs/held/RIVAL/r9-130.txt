**Recommended Course of Action:**
Pause the $3.5 million capital deployment immediately. Instead, initiate a "Regulatory-First Pilot" in a single suburb (e.g., Mount Maunganui) using leased refrigerated vehicles and a manual booking system. Secure the Ministry of Primary Industries (MPI) approval timeline as the absolute gating factor before purchasing assets or hiring permanent staff. Do not commit to the full fleet until you have written regulatory clearance and verified unit economics from the pilot.

**Primary Failure Modes:**
1.  **Regulatory Black Hole:** MPI approval takes 12+ months or imposes storage/transport standards that invalidate your $6/drop cost model, leaving you with stranded capital.
2.  **Unit Economics Collapse:** Your delivery cost math is fragile. At $55,000 for 10,000 drops, you are at $5.50/drop. However, this likely excludes fuel variance, vehicle maintenance, insurance, and route inefficiency. If the true cost hits $9/drop, the margin vanishes.
3.  **Density Dilution:** The $6/drop assumption relies on high route density. If the 12,000 homes are spread across the wider Bay of Plenty rather than clustered, drive times increase, drops per hour decrease, and costs skyrocket.

**The Plug-Pull Trigger:**
Abandon the venture if **MPI approval is not granted within 90 days** OR if the **pilot pilot's actual cost-per-drop exceeds $7.50** after 60 days of operation.

**What You Are Overlooking:**
*   **Seasonality & Churn:** Milk is a commodity; customers will churn instantly if a supermarket delivery is cheaper or more convenient. You have no moat other than convenience, which is expensive to maintain.
*   **The "Custom Platform" Trap:** Building a custom mobile app before validating demand is wasted capital. Use off-the-shelf route planning and subscription tools (e.g., Shopify Plus with local delivery plugins) for the pilot.
*   **Driver Retention:** Hiring 18 drivers for early morning shifts is operationally difficult; turnover will disrupt delivery consistency, the very value proposition you are selling.

**Better Alternative:**
Partner with an existing local dairy processor or a broad-line food distributor who already has MPI approval and cold-chain logistics. Launch "Moormilk Direct" as a white-label subscription add-on to their existing routes. This reduces your upfront capex from $3.5M to potentially under $200k (marketing and software only) and shifts the regulatory risk to the partner.

**The Exact Metric to Abandon Plan:**
**Contribution Margin per Drop < $4.00** after accounting for all variable logistics costs. If you cannot clear $4.00 profit per drop after paying for the milk, van, fuel, and driver time, the volume required to cover the $3.5M fixed cost is mathematically impossible to achieve.
