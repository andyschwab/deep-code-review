**Actionable Advice:** Immediately reframe your pitch from "capturing plant-based growth" to "defending core dairy revenue." Tell CFO Patel: "We are not betting $3.2M on becoming a plant-based leader; we are spending it to ensure our existing dairy customers don't defect to competitors offering both. The 10% price premium is not a profit driver; it is a risk mitigation buffer. We must validate if our brand equity transfers to plants *before* committing the full $1.5M marketing budget."

**Primary Ways This Could Fall Flat:**
1.  **Brand Dissonance:** Your core dairy customers may view a dairy co-op making plant milk as inauthentic or "greenwashing," rejecting the product entirely while simultaneously eroding trust in your core dairy line.
2.  **The Math Gap:** You have an unexplained $350,000 hole in your capital outlay ($3.2M requested vs. $2.85M itemized). If you cannot instantly account for this during the pitch, you will lose credibility on the entire financial model.
3.  **Margin Illusion:** Achieving a 12% gross margin relies on the 10% price premium holding. If the market forces you to discount to compete with established plant brands (Oatly, Sanitarium), your margins could collapse to single digits, extending the break-even point well beyond 18 months.

**Specific Result to Pull the Plug:**
Conduct a blind taste test and brand association survey with 200 of your top 500 core customers *before* the July launch. If less than **15%** rank your plant milk in the top two for taste, or if more than **20%** associate the product with "confusing" or "inauthentic" regarding your brand, pull the plug on the full rollout. Proceeding without this validation turns the $1.5M marketing spend into a donation to competitors.
