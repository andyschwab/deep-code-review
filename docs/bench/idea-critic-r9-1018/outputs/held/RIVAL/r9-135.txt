**Verdict: Conditional GO** – Proceed with the pitch, but explicitly frame Phase 1 as a **pilot contingent on securing the AI licensing fee at ≤$18,000**. Do not commit to the full $120k budget or the Spring 2026 Phase 2 expansion until the vendor contract is signed.

**Three Biggest Failure Points:**
1.  **Budget Blowout:** If the AI licensing fee lands at $25k (the high end) rather than the $15k midpoint, your total Phase 1 cost rises to $130k+, potentially breaking approval thresholds or forcing cuts to essential training/support.
2.  **Faculty Adoption Gap:** The plan assumes 60% faculty sign-up. If adoption stalls below 40% (common in new platform rollouts), the per-student cost skyrockets, and the "network effect" needed for peer validation fails, leaving the platform empty.
3.  **Overstated Impact:** Betting on an 18% lift in completion rates (jumping from 42% to 60%) is aggressive. If the actual lift is only 5–8%, the ROI narrative collapses, making it hard to justify the Phase 2 graduate expansion.

**Abort/Pivot Trigger:**
If **Phase 1 enrollment falls below 2,800 students** (approx. 62% of the 4,500 target) by the end of the first month of the Fall 2025 semester, **immediately pause Phase 2 planning** and switch to a "rescue mode" focused entirely on faculty incentives and student onboarding before spending another dollar.

**Better Alternative:**
Instead of a hard "Phase 1 vs. Phase 2" split, propose a **"Module-Based Rollout."** Launch the core credentialing engine for *all* students (undergrad + grad) in Fall 2025, but activate the expensive AI assessment engine *only* for high-enrollment courses first. This spreads the fixed cost across 5,700 learners immediately, lowers the risk per student, and lets you prove the 18% completion lift earlier before locking in the full AI license fee.
