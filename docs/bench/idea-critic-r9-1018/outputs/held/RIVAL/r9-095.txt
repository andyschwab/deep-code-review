**How This Effort Falls Apart (Failure Modes):**
1.  **The Access Chasm:** Participants sign up but cannot log in 3x/week due to shared devices, spotty Wi-Fi, or data costs. The app becomes a source of shame rather than empowerment, accelerating dropout.
2.  **Coordinator Burnout:** Two coordinators are hired to manage a digital tool, but end up doing social work (finding housing/food for participants) instead of tracking metrics. The $40,000 labor cost yields no data improvement.
3.  **The "Zombie" Metric:** You hit the login target (3x/week) via gamification or nudges, but the actual skill acquisition remains flat. You prove engagement without proving outcome, wasting the six-month window.

**The Pivot Route:**
Shift to a **"Concierge Pilot"** for 8 weeks with 20 participants.
*   **Cut:** The $50k marketing spend and one coordinator role.
*   **Keep:** The software license ($30k) and one coordinator ($20k).
*   **Action:** The coordinator manually onboards participants, providing devices/hotspots if needed, and tracks usage daily.
*   **Goal:** Prove that *with support*, the specific demographic can hit the 3x/week login rate. If they can't log in even with hand-holding, the app is not the solution; the barrier is infrastructure, not software.

**The Walk-Away Trigger:**
**Scrap the project if, after 4 weeks of the Concierge Pilot, fewer than 50% of participants log in at least 3 times per week.**
If highly supported users cannot engage, the product-market fit is nonexistent for this demographic. Do not proceed to the citywide rollout. Cut losses at $15,000 rather than $120,000.
