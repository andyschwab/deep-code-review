The math supports the revenue lift (4% is achievable), but the plan treats unverified risks as certainties. You must validate the spoilage impact and regulatory costs before scaling beyond the initial pilot batch.

1.  **Margin Erosion from Spoilage:** The plan assumes a 30% margin but ignores the historical 0.6% spoilage rate for specialty cheeses. On 1.8M € volume, this alone could shave ~10.8k € off the top line and disproportionately hit net margin if disposal costs are high.
2.  **Hidden Regulatory & Audit Costs:** The budget allocates zero euros for potential new flavor-approval audits. If the regulator demands a full safety dossier for the truffle or smoked paprika additives, this could cost 15k–25k € and delay the launch by 4–8 weeks, missing the key holiday sales window.
3.  **Logistics Capacity Illusion:** Assuming the existing network absorbs 1.8M € in extra volume without friction is risky. Specialty cheeses often require distinct temperature zones or faster turnover than standard Gouda. If the current warehouses cannot handle the specific storage needs without cross-contamination or spoilage, logistics costs will spike, eroding the projected margin.
