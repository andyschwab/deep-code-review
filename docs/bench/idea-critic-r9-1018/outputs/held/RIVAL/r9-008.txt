**Recommendation:** Do not proceed with the $2.3M LumenBridge investment until the ERP integration effort is quantified with a fixed-price pilot; the unpriced integration risk alone threatens to invalidate the entire business case.

**Strongest Objection:** The proposal treats the ERP integration as a minor variable ("low" effort) while ignoring that grant management lives and dies by financial data accuracy. If the vendor's "low" estimate is wrong—a common occurrence with bespoke ERP hooks—the $300k consulting budget will evaporate immediately. Unlike license fees, integration complexity scales non-linearly with legacy data quirks. If integration requires 2x the estimated effort, you aren't just over budget; you are deploying a system that researchers cannot use because their grant ledgers don't match finance, rendering the $1M license fee a sunk cost for shelf-ware.

**Simpler Alternative:** Run a 90-day "concierge" pilot at the São Paulo lab only. Instead of buying the platform, use the $250k intended license fee to hire two dedicated grant coordinators to manually aggregate data from existing tools and the ERP. This tests the *process* value of centralized management without the technical debt. If the coordinators cannot deliver value manually, the software certainly won't fix it. This caps initial exposure to ~$300k (staff + minimal tooling) and validates the need before committing to a multi-lab rollout.

**Primary Failure Modes:**
1.  **Integration Stall:** The ERP API cannot support real-time grant tracking, forcing dual-entry workflows that researchers reject.
2.  **Adoption Friction:** Senior researchers view the 10% time allocation (120 person-months) as an administrative tax rather than a value-add, leading to ghost accounts and stale data.
3.  **Scope Creep:** The "consulting" line item becomes a black hole for customizations requested by each of the four distinct lab regions, bloating the timeline beyond the 18-month window.

**Abort Triggers:**
You should terminate the project immediately if either of these conditions is met:
*   **Cost Trigger:** The confirmed integration effort exceeds **$460,000** (20% of the total $2.3M budget). This indicates the system architecture is fundamentally misaligned with your ERP.
*   **Timeline Trigger:** A functional end-to-end data sync is not achieved in the São Paulo pilot by **March 2026** (not 2027). If the first lab cannot integrate within 9 months of the July 2025 start, the 18-month rollout plan is mathematically impossible, and delaying the remaining three labs only increases the burn rate without delivering value.

*Note: The 120 person-months calculation implies ~10 senior researchers per lab; verify this headcount assumption, as understaffing the pilot is a silent killer of adoption.*
*Ensure the contract includes an exit clause allowing termination if the March 2026 milestone is missed without penalty.*
