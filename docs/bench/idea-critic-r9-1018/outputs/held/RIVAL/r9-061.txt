**Recommendation:** Proceed with a **restricted regional pilot** (20–30 stores) rather than the full 120-store rollout. While the unit economics look attractive on paper, the assumption that your current distribution partner can absorb a 40% volume increase in targeted outlets without degrading service levels for your core lager business is unproven. A pilot validates the "cannibalization vs. expansion" dynamic and stress-tests the distributor's capacity before committing the full $250k budget.

**Top Three Ways This Could Fall Apart:**
1.  **Distribution Bottleneck:** Your partner lacks the warehouse space or delivery frequency to handle the extra SKU complexity, causing stockouts of *both* the new cocktail and your flagship lager, damaging overall retailer relationships.
2.  **Margin Illusion:** The projected 20% higher margin evaporates once you account for the specific excise taxes on ready-to-drink (RTD) cocktails, higher glass/aluminum costs, and slotting fees required for premium placement, which differ significantly from keg logistics.
3.  **Severe Cannibalization:** Instead of attracting new customers, the "Summer Sunset" blend simply replaces higher-margin keg sales in existing accounts, resulting in a net revenue lift but a net profit decline.

**Pull-the-Plug/Pivot Trigger:**
If the pilot data shows that **more than 35% of cocktail sales are direct substitutes for existing keg orders** (verified by retailer POS data) OR if **out-of-stock incidents for core products rise by >5%** in pilot regions due to distribution strain, you must immediately pivot to a separate distribution model or halt the launch to protect the core $4.5M revenue base.

*Note: Ensure your $250k budget explicitly includes a contingency for potential excise tax adjustments, as RTD rates vary wildly by state.*
