**SINGLE RED FLAG (ABORT TRIGGER): The Regulatory Cliff**
The project burns ~$278k/month. If regulatory approval takes 12 months (the upper bound of the estimate) rather than 6, you will exhaust the entire $2.5M budget *before* you are legally allowed to operate the pipeline. There is no bridge funding listed. If the approval slips to 10 months, you run out of cash 2 months before launch. **Abort condition:** Do not deploy capital beyond the prototyping phase until a written timeline commitment or a temporary operational waiver is secured from the regional regulatory body.

**HOW THIS COULD FALL APART (Failure Modes)**
1.  **The Revenue Delusion:** The plan assumes a 20% revenue boost solely from cutting the design cycle from 8 days to 6 days. This is highly speculative. Research labs order based on *grant cycles* and *experimental needs*, not merely because a design arrived 48 hours earlier. If the speed increase does not actually trigger more frequent orders (elasticity error), you miss your revenue targets by ~20%, extending the break-even point significantly.
2.  **Unit Economics Mismatch:** At $8,000 per design, your projected annual revenue at full capacity (800 designs) is $6.4M. However, your $2.5M investment covers only 9 months of *build time*, not ongoing operational costs (compute, staff, compliance). If the variable cost per design is high, the margin may not support the overhead required to maintain the 15 designs/week throughput.
3.  **Market Saturation:** Targeting 120 labs to sustain 800 designs/year requires every single lab to order ~6.7 times per year. If only 50% of targeted labs convert, you must extract 13+ orders/year from the remainder to hit volume targets, which may exceed their actual research throughput.

**ARITHMETIC & COST CHECK**
*   **Burn Rate:** $2.5M / 9 months = **$277,777/month**.
*   **Revenue Projection:** 800 designs × $8,000 = **$6.4M/year**.
*   **The Gap:** The math holds for *top-line* potential, but the proposal ignores **OpEx**. If your monthly burn continues post-launch (even at 50% of build rate), your annual OpEx could be ~$1.6M. This leaves ~$4.8M gross profit, which seems healthy, *but* only if you hit 100% capacity immediately.
*   **Critical Error:** The 20% revenue uplift is treated as guaranteed gross margin. If the "speed" incentive fails to materialize, and you only achieve 80% of projected volume *and* lose the 20% frequency bump, your actual revenue drops to ~$3.8M. Against a likely $1.5M–$2M annual OpEx, the margin for error is dangerously thin.
