**Recommendation: Proceed with a conditional "Pilot-First" strategy, not the full three-year rollout.**

Do not commit the full $750,000 upfront. The financial model is currently fragile because it treats uncertain regulatory and insurance variables as fixed costs. Your 18% ROI assumption collapses if the permit slips past Q3 2025 or if insurance lands at the 1.3% ceiling. Instead, structure a 6-month paid pilot covering 150 ha with a capped expenditure of $75,000. Make the full rollout contingent on two hard gates: written receipt of the operating permit and a binding insurance quote below 1.0%. This shifts the risk from a capital bet to an operational validation, protecting your downside while keeping the upside intact if the regulatory environment clears.

**Top Ways This Could Fall Apart:**
1.  **Regulatory Slip:** If the national aviation authority delays the permit beyond Q3 2025, your pilot start slides into the 2026 harvest window. Missing one seasonal cycle destroys the Year 1 data needed to justify Year 2 spending, turning the ROI timeline from 24 months to 36+ months.
2.  **Insurance Spiral:** If the liability premium hits 1.3% ($9,750/year initially, scaling with fleet size) rather than 0.8%, and you add the unmodeled $12,000 annual battery replacement, your operating margin shrinks by roughly $22,000 annually. Over three years, this wipes out nearly 15% of your projected net profit, potentially rendering the project cash-flow negative in bad weather years.
3.  **Battery Degradation Cliff:** The vendor's estimate assumes linear degradation. If the Buenos Aires heat accelerates battery failure, requiring replacement every 18 months instead of "after year two," your maintenance costs could double, breaking the unit economics per hectare.

**Pull-the-Plug or Pivot Signals:**
*   **Pull the Plug:** If the operating permit is not issued by **December 31, 2025**. This indicates a systemic regulatory blockade rather than a simple delay, making the 2026 pilot impossible.
*   **Pivot:** If the binding insurance quote exceeds **1.1%** of total spend. At this level, the risk profile suggests you should pivot from owning the fleet to a "Drone-as-a-Service" model where a third-party operator absorbs the insurance and battery liability, even if it lowers your long-term margin.
