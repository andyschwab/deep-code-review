**Key Failure Modes**

*   **Technical (The Data Trap):** The assumption that sensor feeds are "clean enough" is dangerous. Legacy SCADA systems often harbor inconsistent tagging, noise, and gaps. If the mapping effort exceeds 200 engineering hours or requires a custom middleware layer, the $85k integration budget breaks immediately, and the 5-minute latency target becomes unachievable without further spend.
*   **Financial (The Efficiency Gap):** Your $1.2M savings rely entirely on capturing 0.8 days of downtime per turbine. If the dashboard identifies issues but your maintenance crews cannot mobilize faster than the current 30-minute alert window allows, the latency improvement yields zero financial return. You pay for insight you cannot act upon.
*   **Operational (The Adoption Cliff):** If the new schema conflicts with existing workflows, engineers may ignore the dashboard, reverting to legacy tools. The $30k training budget suggests a light touch; if the UI isn't intuitive, the tool becomes shelf-ware, and downtime remains unchanged.

Do not wait for a total failure; if the efficiency gain is merely "good" rather than "transformative," the capital is better spent on direct hardware upgrades.
