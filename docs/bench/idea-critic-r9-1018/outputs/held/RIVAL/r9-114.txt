**Recommendation: NO-GO (in current form).**

The financial model is structurally unsound due to optimistic benchmarking and omitted operational costs. You are comparing a complex, high-risk floating asset against an outdated onshore price ($0.03/kWh) while ignoring the specific cost drivers of marine environments. Until the regulatory fee is defined and the corrosion uplift is modeled, the Levelized Cost of Energy (LCOE) is likely 30–40% higher than presented, destroying the project's margin.

**Top Three Ways This Plan Unravels:**

1.  **The Corrosion Cliff:** You have excluded the 2%/year O&M uplift for marine corrosion. Floating solar faces constant humidity, wave action, and chemical exposure. If anchoring systems or inverters degrade faster than terrestrial units, maintenance costs will skyrocket, turning a profitable asset into a cash drain by Year 5.
2.  **The Submersion Gamble:** The 90% efficiency target hinges entirely on water levels staying within a narrow band (<5% submersion). Climate volatility makes this fragile. A single drought or flood season that breaches this threshold damages cabling and reduces output, invalidating the 200 GWh generation guarantee.
3.  **Regulatory Blind Spot:** Building before the "fee per MW" is defined is speculative. If regulators classify floating installations as "marine infrastructure" rather than "solar," fees could triple, instantly eroding equity returns. You are effectively betting the company on a favorable policy outcome that does not yet exist.

**The Kill/Pivot Metric:**

**LCOE > $0.055/kWh.**
Recalculate the model immediately using the current market benchmark ($0.04/kWh) plus the 2% corrosion uplift and a conservative regulatory fee estimate. If the resulting LCOE exceeds $0.055/kWh, the project cannot compete with emerging battery-plus-storage onshore solutions. At that price, you are building stranded assets.

**Devil's Advocate: What Would Change My Mind?**

I would flip to a conditional GO only if you can secure a **fixed-price EPC contract with a 10-year performance guarantee** that explicitly covers corrosion-related failures and submersion efficiency losses. This transfers the technical risk from your balance sheet to the vendor. Without a vendor willing to sign that guarantee, the technology risk is too high for a first-of-kind deployment at this scale. Additionally, you need a written preliminary ruling on the regulatory fee structure; proceeding without it is not strategy, it's gambling.
