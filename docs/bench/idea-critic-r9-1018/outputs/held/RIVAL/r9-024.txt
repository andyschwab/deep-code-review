**Recommended Course of Action**
Proceed with a **conditional pilot launch**, but publicly reframe the narrative from "revenue certainty" to "conversion validation." Do not claim the $150,000 revenue is guaranteed; instead, present the pilot as a $120,000 investment to test whether the 80,000 impressions can convert at the required **2.5% rate** (2,000 donors ÷ 80,000 impressions). If industry benchmarks for cold social traffic are below 1%, you must explicitly state that the financial model relies on outperforming standard metrics by 2.5x. Secure a contingency reserve of at least $30,000 (25% of the budget) before committing the full amount, acknowledging that the 12% growth assumption is aggressive without paid acceleration.

**Primary Failure Modes**
1.  **Conversion Collapse:** The most critical risk is the conversion rate. If your 80,000 impressions convert at a standard 0.5%–1% rather than the required 2.5%, you will secure only 400–800 donors ($30k–$60k revenue), leaving a $60k+ deficit against your $120k budget.
2.  **Arithmetic & Fee Error:** Your model double-counts the platform fee. If donors give $75 gross, a 5% fee reduces the net to $71.25. To net $150,000, you actually need **2,106 donors**, not 2,000. Furthermore, if the $120,000 budget is meant to be *covered* by the $150,000 gross, you are assuming zero operational overhead beyond the dev/marketing spend, which is dangerous for a six-month pilot.
3.  **Growth Fantasy:** The 12% month-over-month organic growth is unsustainable without viral loops or increased ad spend. If acquisition flattens after month two, you will miss the 2,000-donor target entirely.

**Exact Trigger to Pull the Plug or Pivot**
Establish a **hard stop at Day 45** (mid-pilot). If you have not secured **600 unique donors** (30% of the total target) by this date, immediately **pivot** to a high-touch major donor strategy or **terminate** the paid marketing spend. Continuing to burn budget on a channel that cannot demonstrate a minimum 1.5% conversion rate by the halfway mark will mathematically guarantee a failure to reach 2,000 donors, turning the remaining $60,000+ of the budget into a sunk cost with no path to recovery.
