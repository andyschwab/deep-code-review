**Recommendation: Hold Back**

Do not launch the pilot on November 1, 2025, in its current form. The unit economics are negative before accounting for fixed costs, and the conversion assumption is dangerously optimistic for a hardware subscription model.

**Top Failure Modes**

1.  **Negative Unit Margin:** Your add-on sells for 1,299 SEK. The kit cost is 750 SEK. The 15% logistics surcharge (applied to either cost or price) adds ~113–195 SEK. This brings your direct cost to ~863–945 SEK, leaving a gross margin of only 354–436 SEK per module. This thin margin cannot cover customer acquisition, support, or the capital cost of holding inventory for a subscription model.
2.  **Conversion Overestimation:** Assuming 30% of existing customers (13,500 units) will adopt a new recurring hardware charge within six months is unrealistic. Typical hardware attach rates for existing bases range from 3–8%. If you hit only 8% (3,600 subscribers), your revenue drops to ~467k SEK/month, likely failing to cover the operational overhead of running the subscription program.
3.  **Supply Chain Rigidity:** Modular components require precise inventory matching. If a customer buys the base but the "smart fabric" module is backordered for three weeks, satisfaction plummets. Unlike software, you cannot patch missing inventory instantly.

**Specific Kill Criteria (Pull the Plug If)**

*   **Churn > 8% after Month 2:** If more than 8% of pilot subscribers cancel after the second billing cycle, the lifetime value (LTV) cannot support the hardware cost.
*   **Cash Burn > 400k SEK/month:** Given the thin margins, if operational burn exceeds 400k SEK monthly during the pilot, scaling will amplify losses rather than fix them.
*   **Adoption < 4% by Month 3:** If fewer than 1,800 existing customers subscribe by the end of the pilot, the market demand for this specific modularity is insufficient to justify the supply chain complexity.

**Note:** Run a 30-day pre-order landing page test with your existing email list first to validate the 30% conversion hypothesis before manufacturing any kits. Recalculate the logistics surcharge explicitly into the COGS to see the true margin per unit.
