**Verdict: Proceed with a Conditional Pilot, Not Full Commitment.**

Do not authorize the full £1.2m rollout yet. The financial model relies on a fragile assumption: that a 15% satisfaction boost will mechanically drive a 12% enrolment increase (200 students). In higher education, satisfaction rarely correlates linearly with recruitment this quickly. Approving the full capital outlay based on this unproven link exposes the department to significant deficit risk if enrolment remains flat despite happy students.

Instead, approve only the **£360k for the three stations and initial integration** to run the six-month pilot. Treat the £300k software licence as a negotiable operational expense contingent on pilot success metrics, not an upfront sunk cost. You must decouple the "pilot" from the "rollout." The pilot's sole purpose is to validate the enrolment conversion rate, not just the technology's functionality.

**Three Most Plausible Failure Modes:**

1.  **The "Novelty Decay" Effect:** Student satisfaction spikes in months 1–2 due to the "wow" factor but reverts to baseline by month 4 as the VR exercises become routine. If satisfaction drops before the pilot ends, the projected 15% boost vanishes, destroying the ROI model.
2.  **The Enrolment Lag:** Even if satisfaction stays high, prospective students (and their parents) do not choose universities based on lab tech alone; they prioritize rankings, location, and employability. You may achieve high satisfaction with current students but see zero impact on the next intake, leaving you with high operating costs and no offsetting revenue.
3.  **Hidden Maintenance Bleed:** Without a benchmarked maintenance contract, annual costs could easily exceed 20–25% of hardware value (£60k–£75k/year) rather than the standard 10–15%. If the vendor locks you into proprietary software updates required to keep the hardware running, your operating margin evaporates regardless of enrolment numbers.

**Exact Trigger to Pull the Plug or Redesign:**

**The "Conversion Threshold" at Month 3.**
Conduct a mid-pilot survey of current users asking: *"How much did this specific lab influence your decision to recommend LumenArc to peers?"*
*   **Trigger:** If fewer than **40%** of pilot participants cite the VR lab as a "significant" or "decisive" factor in their recommendation, **halt the rollout immediately.**
*   **Reasoning:** If current users aren't actively evangelizing the tech mid-pilot, it will not drive the 200 new enrolments required to justify the £1.2m spend. Do not wait for the full six months; this leading indicator prevents throwing good money after bad.

**Note:** The £800k AR lab spend is a sunk cost and a dangerous anchor; do not let it justify this investment unless the AR data proves a direct satisfaction-to-enrolment pipeline, which is unlikely given the tech differences. Demand the maintenance quote in writing before signing any pilot agreement; if they refuse to estimate, walk away.
