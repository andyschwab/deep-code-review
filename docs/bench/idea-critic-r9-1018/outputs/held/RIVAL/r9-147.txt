**Strongest Objection (The "Fixed Cost Trap"):**
Your proposal relies on a dangerous asymmetry: you are swapping a **variable cost** (3PL fees that drop if orders drop) for a massive **fixed cost** (2.2M PHP/month rent and utilities).
*   **The Math:** Your annual 3PL cost is 1.8M PHP. Your proposed hub costs **26.4M PHP annually** (2.2M × 12).
*   **The Flaw:** You are increasing your annual fixed overhead by ~24.6M PHP to save only 1.1M PHP in expedited fees. This only makes sense if you assume the hub unlocks massive *new* revenue through faster delivery. If order volume dips even slightly below your 15,000/month forecast, you are locked into a 2.2M PHP monthly bleed with no way to scale down. You are betting the company's cash flow on a volume guarantee that doesn't exist.

**How This Could Fail:**
1.  **Volume Miss:** If you hit only 10,000 orders/month, your cost per unit skyrockets, making the hub significantly more expensive than the 3PL.
2.  **Regulatory Shock:** Since you ignored warehousing taxes, a single unexpected levy could erase your thin 1.1M PHP savings margin immediately.
3.  **Operational Distraction:** Managing a physical hub diverts leadership focus from product and sales to facility management and labor issues.

**Abort Trigger:**
Abort the plan immediately if **monthly operating costs exceed 2.5M PHP for two consecutive months** OR if **actual order volume falls below 12,000 units/month for any single month** during the first year. These triggers indicate that the fixed-cost structure is unsustainable relative to your actual throughput.
