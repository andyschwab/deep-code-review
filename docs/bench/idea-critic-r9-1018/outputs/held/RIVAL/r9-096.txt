**Recommendation:** Do not present this proposal in its current form. The financial model contains a critical arithmetic error regarding battery replacement that invalidates the three-year payback claim, and the maintenance risk for remote Limpopo sites is unquantified.

**Principal Failure Modes:**
1.  **Battery Replacement Cliff:** Your model assumes a 4-year battery life against a 5-year contract. You have budgeted zero capital for replacing the battery bank in Year 5 (approx. $4,000–$5,000 per unit, or $200k total). This single omission destroys the project's net positive cash flow.
2.  **Maintenance Access Cost:** The "unknown" maintenance cost is likely the largest variable. Remote rural schools often incur travel costs exceeding the repair value itself. If a technician requires a 4-hour round trip for every fault, your operational margin will vanish.
3.  **Generation Shortfall:** Relying on a fixed "4 hours sunshine" average ignores seasonal variance and dust accumulation. If actual generation drops to 3.5 hours/day during winter or dusty seasons, the 300 kWh target fails, forcing schools back onto diesel and breaking the savings thesis.
4.  **Theft and Vandalism:** Off-grid solar units in unmonitored rural locations are high-value targets. Without a specific security line item and community ownership model, asset loss is a near-certainty.

**Trigger Condition to Halt:**
 Halt the rollout immediately if a **3-month pilot at 2 sites** reveals that the **cost of reactive maintenance travel exceeds 15% of the projected annual diesel savings**. This metric proves that the logistical reality of remote service destroys the unit economics before you scale to 40 schools.

*Note: Recalculate the NPV including a Year 5 battery replacement reserve before any stakeholder review. The current math implies free energy in the final year, which investors will reject instantly.*
