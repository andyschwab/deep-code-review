**The Most Serious Objection: The "Hidden Labor" Trap**
Carlos will rightly challenge the €15 refurbishment estimate. Fiberglass repair is artisanal, not assembly-line work. Sanding, resin mixing, curing, and polishing cannot be easily rushed without compromising quality. If your technicians spend 30 minutes extra per board fixing imperfections to meet brand standards, your margins evaporate. The objection will be: *"We are selling a subscription, but we are buying a job. If the labor scales linearly and unpredictably, we are building a low-margin service business, not a scalable product."*

**Superior Option: The "Tiered Swap" Model**
Instead of promising a refurbished version of *their* board (which requires custom labor per unit), consider a **Tiered Swap** model.
*   **How it works:** Customers return their old board and receive a certified pre-owned board from a standardized inventory pool (e.g., "Performance Shortboard" tier).
*   **Why it's better:** You can refurbish boards in bulk batches during off-peak seasons, optimizing labor efficiency. You decouple the customer's upgrade cycle from your production bottleneck. It turns a custom job shop into an inventory logistics problem, which is far easier to scale and cost-control.

**Early Signal to Pull the Plug**
**Metric:** **Refurbishment Cycle Time vs. Cost Cap.**
During the Lisbon pilot, track the *actual* labor minutes per board.
*   **The Trigger:** If the average labor time exceeds **90 minutes per board** (assuming a €20/hr fully loaded labor rate, this pushes you near the €15 limit before materials), **stop the rollout.**
*   **The Pivot:** If you hit this trigger, immediately switch to the "Tiered Swap" model or increase the subscription price to €99/month to absorb the operational reality. Do not attempt to "optimize" the artisanal process further; the physics of curing resin dictate the speed.

**Summary of Failure Modes:**
1.  **Labor Creep:** The €15 estimate proves unrealistic, turning high-margin revenue into break-even service work.
2.  **Logistics Bottleneck:** If churn is higher than 7% or turnaround takes >2 weeks, customer satisfaction plummets, increasing churn further (death spiral).
3.  **Inventory Mismatch:** Customers want upgrades you don't have in stock, leading to perceived lack of value despite paying the subscription.
