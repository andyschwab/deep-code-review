**Recommendation: Proceed with a conditional pilot expansion, not the full rollout.**

Do not sign the three-year backend contract or commit to the full 150,000-unit production run yet. The math on card replacement alone threatens your margins: at a 6% loss rate on 150,000 cards, you are looking at 9,000 replacements annually. At $4 per unit, that is $36,000 in hard costs every year, not the $0.50 buffer you budgeted. More critically, launching a $3.2M hardware initiative while 45% of your riders already use the mobile app creates a dangerous split ecosystem. You risk subsidizing plastic for users who have already migrated to a zero-marginal-cost digital channel.

**How this falls apart:**
1.  **The Replacement Spiral:** If the 6% loss rate holds or increases with a broader, less engaged demographic, your operational costs will outpace the 18% cash-handling savings. The "cheap" card becomes a recurring liability.
2.  **Cannibalization & Stagnation:** You may find that 80% of card adopters are simply cannibalizing mobile app users, while cash users (the true target) refuse to switch due to friction. This leaves you with high hardware costs and no net gain in throughput.
3.  **The Data Void:** Without reliable switching data, you are betting $3.2M on the assumption that behavior will change. If the pilot's 7,200 users were tech-early-adopters rather than representative cash-users, the rollout will stall at 20% adoption, leaving you with warehouses of unused cards and an expensive unused backend.

**The Trigger Point:**
Set a hard review gate at **six months post-launch**. If **net new adoption** (users switching from cash, not mobile) is below **30%**, or if the **card loss rate exceeds 7%**, halt the second production run immediately.

At that trigger, pivot the remaining budget to expand the mobile app's offline capabilities and subsidize smartphone access for unbanked riders, rather than forcing a plastic solution on a digital-ready city. Use the existing cards only for edge cases (tourists, emergency backup) and renegotiate the backend contract to a month-to-month basis until stability is proven.

*Note: Bring the exact replacement cost calculation ($36k/year) to the meeting; it is your strongest lever to delay the full commitment.*
*Ensure the "30% adoption" metric is defined strictly as "former cash users" to avoid false positives from app migrants.*
