**Recommendation: Do not move forward to implementation yet; proceed only with a 90-day feasibility pilot focused on securing binding customer commitments and validating logistics costs.**

The current proposal relies on unverified assumptions that create excessive financial risk. Specifically, banking on 30% customer enrollment without firm commitments and assuming zero incremental transport costs are critical vulnerabilities. A €2.5M capital outlay cannot be justified until these variables are converted from estimates to contractual obligations.

**Key Failure Modes:**
1.  **Participation Shortfall:** If OEM enrollment drops below 20%, the volume required to cover the €300k annual operating cost and service the capex will not be met, collapsing the IRR.
2.  **Scrap Price Volatility:** The model breaks even at approximately €145/t. A market dip below this threshold (currently €180/t) eliminates the margin, turning the recycling loop into a cost center.
3.  **Hidden Logistics Costs:** Existing contracts rarely cover reverse logistics for end-of-life parts. If haulage fees exceed €15/t, the net recovery value per ton drops precipitously, invalidating the 15% IRR target.
4.  **Contamination Risk:** Forged steel requires specific alloy separation. If collected parts are contaminated or misidentified, re-melting costs will spike, and the reclaimed alloy may fail quality certification, rendering it unsellable at premium prices.

**Abort Trigger:**
Abort the project if, after a 12-month operational review, the **realized ROI falls below 8%** OR if **binding customer commitments cover less than 2,000t (40% of target volume)** by the start of Q3 2025.
