**Recommendation: Proceed with a Hybrid Approach (Self-Publish with Contingency)**
Do not commit the full $80,000 marketing budget upfront. Instead, allocate $20,000 for the June 1 demo launch to validate conversion, holding the remaining $60,000 in reserve. **Top Three Failure Modes**
1.  **Discovery Collapse:** The 15% market growth assumption does not guarantee visibility; without a publisher's curated relationships, "Nebula Drift" may drown in Steam's saturation regardless of ad spend, rendering the 5% conversion mathematically impossible due to lack of traffic quality.
2.  **Revenue Timing Mismatch:** Projecting $500,000 gross revenue in the "first year" ignores the 30-day Steam payout cycle and potential holiday season delays; if the October 15 launch slips or underperforms initially, you may face a cash flow crisis before the $500k hits the bank, jeopardizing the final months of the 12-month runway.
3.  **Hidden Margin Erosion:** The plan accounts for "hidden platform fees" as an unknown but bases the 100% ROI on gross revenue; failing to deduct Steam's 30% cut, payment processing fees (~1%), and chargebacks from the $500k projection means your actual net revenue is likely ~$345,000, turning the projected 100% ROI into a ~38% loss against the $250k budget.

**Abort/Pivot Trigger**
Abort the self-publishing strategy and immediately seek a publishing partner if the free demo achieves a conversion rate below **2.5%** within the first **14 days** post-launch (June 1–15), provided at least 5,000 unique users have wishlisted or played the demo. This threshold indicates the product-market fit is insufficient to support the required customer acquisition cost for a full self-published launch.

**Critical Flaw in Numbers**
Your ROI calculation treats Gross Revenue as Net Profit. A seasoned reviewer will immediately flag that $500,000 gross minus Steam's 30% platform fee ($150,000) leaves $350,000. After subtracting the $250,000 development and $80,000 marketing costs ($330,000 total spend), your actual net profit is only ~$20,000, not $250,000. This represents an **8% ROI**, not 100%. The plan is financially viable only if "gross revenue" was a typo for "net revenue after fees," or if the $500k figure already accounts for the platform cut, which must be explicitly clarified to avoid misleading the CEO.
