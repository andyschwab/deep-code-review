**Three Biggest Failure Modes:**
1.  **Revenue Overestimation (Arithmetic Error):** Your model assumes 100% adoption immediately upon signing. You project $270k/month based on 250 schools × 180 students × $6. However, you also state schools take up to 12 months to adopt due to budget cycles. If adoption is linear over the year, your Year 1 average revenue is roughly $135k/month, not $270k. This creates a $1.6M revenue gap in Year 1 against your $2.9M total budget (build + marketing).
2.  **Flawed Competitive Comparison:** Comparing your $72/student/year ($6/mo) to a competitor's $15/student/year is dangerous without adjusting for scope. If their $15 fee includes LMS integration, assessment tools, and teacher training that you must build or buy separately, your effective margin shrinks. If you need to spend an extra $200k on LMS integration (not currently budgeted), your unit economics may break.
3.  **Sales Cycle vs. Cash Flow Mismatch:** Signing 250 schools does not equal 250 active subscriptions on Day 1. If the average school takes 6 months to onboard, your cash inflow will lag significantly behind the $400k marketing spend and development burn rate, potentially creating a liquidity crunch before the 180-day development cycle even finishes.

**Kill Criteria:**
Terminate the initiative if the 90-day pilot reveals a **churn rate exceeding 5% per month** after the first 60 days of usage, or if the **fully loaded cost per student (including integration and support) exceeds $4.50**, leaving insufficient margin to sustain the platform upgrades.

**Note:** The competitor's $15/year price point suggests your $72/year positioning may be perceived as premium; ensure your "supplemental digital tools" gap is explicitly priced in your pilot.
