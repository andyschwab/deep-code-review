**Recommendation: GO-IF**
Proceed with a limited pilot in the **North Zone only** starting 1 Oct 2025, rather than a full three-zone launch. Expand to East and West only if the pilot confirms that the refund rate remains under 4.5% and labor costs do not exceed the 4% projection after 90 days.

**Three Biggest Failure Modes**
1.  **Behavioral Shift in Refund Rates:** The historical 2.8% refund rate comes from one-off guarantees. A "subscription" model creates a moral hazard where customers may intentionally trigger dissatisfaction to receive a free month of service repeatedly, potentially spiking refunds to 10–15% and erasing all margin.
2.  **Labor Cost Underestimation:** The plan assumes only a 4% cost rise. However, "deep-clean" visits are labor-intensive. If the 15% conversion (1,800 households) demands maximum usage (3 visits/month), you face 5,400 additional monthly visits. At current rates, this could require hiring ~40 new staff instantly, driving training costs and overtime well beyond the 4% buffer.
3.  **Competitor Price War:** If competitors match the guarantee within 60 days, they may undercut the $49 price point. Since your model relies on volume (15% conversion) to hit the $882k revenue target, a loss of even 5% of sign-ups due to competition drops revenue by ~$44k annually while fixed labor costs remain high, turning the new line unprofitable.

**Kill Criterion**
Halt or pivot the program immediately if **refund costs exceed 5% of subscription revenue** for any single month during the pilot, or if **average labor cost per visit rises above $14.25** (a 5.5% increase over the baseline) due to overtime or rushed training.

*Note: The financial model assumes all 1,800 converted users pay for a full year without churn; realistic churn will lower the $882k projection. Ensure your booking system can cap visits at three per household to prevent unlimited scheduling abuse.*
