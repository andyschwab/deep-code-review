**Recommendation:** Proceed with a **Go-If** launch: authorize the $150k marketing spend and season pass program only if a binding commitment secures at least 2,000 pre-sales (40% of the break-even volume) within the first 30 days of the campaign.

**Three Biggest Failure Modes:**
1.  **Revenue Math Error:** Your model double-counts revenue. You cannot sum $1.8M in pass sales *and* attribute additional "ticket revenue" to those same passes; the $120 pass *is* the ticket revenue. If production costs are $8,000/show and you run enough shows for 5,000 patrons to attend 3 times each (approx. 375 performances assuming 40 seats/show, or fewer with larger capacity), your total production cost could vastly exceed $360,000, potentially turning the projected $600k profit into a loss.
2.  **Conversion Delusion:** A 10% conversion rate (5,000 passes from 50,000 emails) is exceptionally high for a cold or warm email list without prior purchase intent data. If conversion drops to a standard 2–3%, you sell only 1,000–1,500 passes, failing to cover the $150k marketing spend alone, let alone production costs.
3.  **Ancillary Dependency:** Banking on unquantified ancillary sales to save margins is a hidden risk. If pass holders feel they "already paid" and spend less on concessions/merch compared to single-ticket buyers, your actual net profit per patron drops, extending the time to break-even beyond your cash flow runway.

**Kill Criterion:** Terminate the program immediately if, after 45 days, the **Cost Per Acquisition (CPA)** exceeds $45 (meaning you have spent more than $45 in marketing for every pass sold), or if total net cash flow (passes sold minus marketing spend minus incurred production costs) is negative by more than $50,000.

**Note:** Your break-even calculation of 3,200 passes appears to ignore the fixed $150k marketing cost in the numerator; re-verify the formula: (Fixed Costs + Marketing) / (Price - Variable Cost per Patron).
Ensure the $8,000/show cost scales linearly with attendance or clarify if it is a fixed cost per performance regardless of house size.
