**Recommendation: Go, but only as a controlled pilot with a pre-committed kill switch.** Do not roll out MTO company-wide yet. The $250k annual savings does not justify the $500k upfront risk and potential brand damage without validating the core assumption: that parents will wait. Launch a 6-week pilot on a single product line to test elasticity and supplier linearity before committing capital.

**Main Failure Modes:**
1.  **Supplier Linearity Illusion:** The assumption that fabric capacity scales from 80k to 150k yards/month at *zero extra cost* is highly improbable. Suppliers typically charge premiums for rush capacity or require capital investment to expand, which would erode your margin and extend the 24-month break-even point.
2.  **Hidden Logistics Erosion:** You have not priced the $3 surcharge for the 30% of orders missing the 7-day window. If applied to 36k units (30% of 120k), this is a $108k annual cost that cuts nearly half your projected $250k savings, potentially making the ROI negative if churn increases.
3.  **Brand Churn:** If "made-to-order" is perceived as "out of stock," conversion rates may drop before you ever realize the warehousing savings.

**Kill Criteria (Stop/Pivot Triggers):**
*   **Stop immediately** if the pilot reveals the supplier cannot meet the 14-day material lead time without a cost increase greater than $0.50 per unit.
*   **Pivot back to stock** if more than 15% of pilot customers cancel their orders due to wait times, or if Net Promoter Score (NPS) drops by more than 10 points compared to current inventory-fed orders.
*   **Re-evaluate finances** if the actual shipping surcharge incidence exceeds 20% of orders in the pilot phase.
