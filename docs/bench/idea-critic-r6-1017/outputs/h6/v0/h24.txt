**Recommendation: Proceed with a "Go-If" Condition**
Approve the three-city roll-out only if the insurance premium and potential traffic surcharge are contractually capped or excluded from the initial six-month operating agreement. Without these caps, the unpriced liabilities ($0.12/ride insurance + $0.05/ride surcharge) could erase 4.9% of gross revenue per ride, turning the projected $35,000 monthly gross per city into an operational loss before fixed costs. Secure these terms by May 1st; otherwise, delay Riverton and renegotiate the permit structure.

**Top Three Failure Modes**
1.  **Regulatory Cost Shock:** The municipal traffic policy passes post-launch, imposing the $0.05/bike surcharge immediately, invalidating the $1.1M staffing/permit budget allocation and causing immediate negative cash flow.
2.  **Revenue Overestimation:** Ridership fails to hit the 4,000-ride threshold (40% of target) in Month 1 due to seasonal mismatches or kiosk placement errors, making the $150/bike/month maintenance cost unsustainable against lower fare revenue.
3.  **Unpriced Insurance Liability:** The city council finalizes the $0.12/ride insurance fee without a grace period, adding $1,200/month in variable costs per city at target volume, which was not accounted for in the $2.5M total commitment.

**Abort/Re-scale Trigger**
Abort expansion to subsequent cities or re-scale the current pilot if **net operating loss exceeds $100,000 in any single city after the first three months** OR if **ridership averages below 4,000 rides/month (40% of target) across two consecutive months** in the launch city.

*Note: The current plan lacks a contingency buffer for the unpriced insurance and surcharge variables, representing a potential $204,000 annual exposure per city at target volume.*
*Ensure the $800k bike allocation includes a reserve for immediate maintenance spikes, as the $150/bike/month estimate assumes ideal utilization conditions.*
