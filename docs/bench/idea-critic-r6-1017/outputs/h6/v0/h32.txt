**Recommendation: GO-IF**
Proceed with the pilot launch on June 15, 2025, **only if** you immediately cap the initial cohort at 150 members (not 200) and finalize a binding shipping quote that keeps total variable costs under $28 per box before printing any marketing materials. Do not market to the full 5,000-customer list yet; restrict the initial offer to a segmented test group of 1,000 highly engaged customers to validate the conversion rate without over-promising on inventory or logistics.

**Top Three Failure Modes**
1.  **Arithmetic & Margin Collapse:** Your projection assumes $10,000 in revenue (200 members × $45 × ~1.11 months effective in Q3? Or is $9k the net?). If revenue is truly $9,000 in Q3 and ad spend is $8,000, you have only $1,000 left for *everything else*. With 200 members, that allows just $5 per box for the plant, pot, soil, packaging, and labor. If the plant alone costs $15 wholesale, you are losing roughly $2,000–$3,000 in Q3 alone, not making a profit. The math currently implies a negative margin unless the "revenue" figure includes prepaid months beyond Q3.
2.  **Conversion Rate Delusion:** Assuming 30% of existing customers (1,500 people) will sign up is dangerously optimistic for a cold internal offer; typical conversion for niche subscription boxes is 2–5%. Even hitting your scaled goal of 200 members requires a 4% conversion rate from the whole list or a much higher rate from a small segment. If conversion lands at a realistic 2%, you will spend $8,000 to acquire only ~100 members, doubling your customer acquisition cost and destroying the unit economics.
3.  **Logistical Bottleneck:** The "unknown" packaging and shipping cost is a critical blind spot. Live plants require specific packing to survive transit; if breakage rates exceed 5% due to inadequate packaging (to save costs), replacement shipping will wipe out the thin margin. Furthermore, managing 200 distinct shipments monthly alongside regular nursery operations may overwhelm current staff capacity, leading to delayed shipments and immediate churn.

**Pre-Agreed Halt Trigger**
Halt the program immediately if, after the first 45 days, the **Customer Acquisition Cost (CAC) exceeds $35** or if **plant breakage/return rates exceed 7%**. These thresholds indicate that the unit economics are fundamentally broken and cannot be fixed by scaling volume.
