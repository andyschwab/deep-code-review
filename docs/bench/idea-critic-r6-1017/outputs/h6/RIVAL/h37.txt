**Recommendation: Do not proceed with the full shift yet.** Run a 90-day pilot with 20% of your volume instead. Your current math contains a critical arithmetic error that turns your projected revenue gain into a significant loss.

**The Arithmetic Error:**
You calculated the new fee as $15,300 (18% of $85k), which is correct. However, you failed to subtract the new $1,200 onboarding cost you explicitly introduced. Your *net* revenue per placement is actually **$14,100**, not $15,300.
*   **Current Net:** $12,000 (no extra costs mentioned) × 45 placements = **$540,000/month**.
*   **Proposed Net:** ($15,300 fee - $1,200 cost) × 45 placements = **$634,500/month**.
*   **New Expenses:** +$8,000 marketing.
*   **Real Monthly Gain:** $634,500 - $540,000 - $8,000 = **$86,500**.
*   **Real Annual Gain:** ~$1.04M, not the $520k implied by your $6.48M to $7.0M jump.
*Wait, the error is worse:* Your projected annual revenue of $7.0M assumes 100% adoption immediately. If only 90% of clients accept (as you estimated), your volume on the new model drops. More critically, if competitors undercut you at $10k, your "18% premium" vanishes instantly. The $7.0M figure is fragile because it relies on the $85k average salary holding steady; if you place more junior candidates to hit volume, that average drops, and 18% quickly falls below the $12k flat fee.

**Three Biggest Failure Modes:**
1.  **Client Churn on Price Sensitivity:** You assumed 90% acceptance, but clients paying for a $40k role would pay you $7,200 (18%) versus your old $12k. They will leave for the $10k competitors. You only win on salaries above $67k; any placement below that threshold loses money immediately compared to the flat fee.
2.  **Margin Erosion via Salary Negotiation:** Under a flat fee, you don't care if the candidate earns $80k or $90k. Under revenue share, clients have an incentive to lowball offers to reduce your fee, or you may inadvertently prioritize high-salary roles over easy-to-fill ones, hurting your 45-per-month velocity.
3.  **Cash Flow Mismatch:** You are swapping immediate cash ($12k on hire) for contingent collections (18% of first-year salary paid out over time?). If clients pay the percentage monthly or quarterly, your working capital will tighten while your upfront marketing and onboarding costs ($8k + $1.2k) rise immediately.

**Kill Trigger:**
Terminate the pilot if **net revenue per placement drops below $11,500** for two consecutive months, or if **client acceptance falls below 75%**. This protects you from a scenario where the fee discount and added costs permanently degrade your unit economics.
