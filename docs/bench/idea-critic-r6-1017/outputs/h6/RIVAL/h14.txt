**Recommendation: Do not push the guarantee forward yet.**

The current financial model is fundamentally broken. You are projecting $120k in annual costs against a calculated liability of only $60k, yet you have completely excluded the new fixed and variable production costs ($35k one-time + $144k annually). When fully loaded, the pilot costs ~$204k/year against only ~$96k in *gross* lift revenue (not profit). You are guaranteeing a loss of over $100k annually before accounting for the cost of goods sold on the extra units. Fix the unit economics and cap the liability before presenting to the board.

**Three Biggest Failure Modes:**
1.  **Margin Erasure:** The 8% sales lift generates revenue, not profit. If your net margin on the Mid-Century line is below 17%, the cost of the guarantee and new production labor will exceed the gross profit from the additional sales, making growth destructive.
2.  **Capacity Bottleneck:** The supplier is unconfirmed beyond 2,000 units/month. An 8% lift on $15M annual sales implies ~$1.2M in extra volume. If average unit price is $500, that’s 2,400 extra units/year (200/month), which fits. However, if "lifetime repair" triggers a surge in replacement claims rather than new sales, you could hit the 2,000-unit cap immediately, causing fulfillment delays and brand damage.
3.  **Adverse Selection:** The "lifetime" term attracts customers who intend to abuse the warranty or have high-maintenance use cases. If claim frequency exceeds the modeled 5% even slightly, the linear cost model collapses because repairs are labor-intensive and uncapped in duration.

**Halt Trigger:**
Halt the pilot immediately if the **fully loaded cost per repaired unit (materials + labor + overhead) exceeds $185**. Your current $250 threshold is too high given the thin margins implied by your current model errors; breaching $185 suggests the service component alone will wipe out all margin from the incremental sales.

**Hidden Risk:**
**The "Zombie Liability" on the Balance Sheet.** A "lifetime" guarantee creates an open-ended liability that does not expire with the fiscal year. If Juniper Joinery is ever sold, audited, or seeks financing, actuaries will demand a reserve fund for future claims on every unit sold during this pilot. You are not just booking an annual expense; you are accumulating a long-tail debt that could scare off investors or reduce valuation multiples, a cost never reflected in a P&L spreadsheet.
