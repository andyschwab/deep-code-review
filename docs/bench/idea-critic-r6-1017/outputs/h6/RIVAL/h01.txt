**Recommendation: Proceed with a Phased Pilot, Not a Full Rollout**

Do not launch the evening service at all three locations simultaneously. Instead, invest the $60,000 capital in **one** high-traffic location first. Your projection of $5,200/week in new revenue relies on unproven assumptions about evening foot traffic (the "new 15%") and conversion rates. A single-site pilot allows you to validate demand, refine operations, and train staff without exposing the entire company to the risk of three simultaneous failures. If the pilot location hits $4,000+ in weekly pizza revenue within 60 days, proceed to the remaining two sites. If not, you have limited your loss to one kitchen remodel and one oven rig.

**Critical Failure Points & Missing Risks**

1.  **Cannibalization vs. Addition:** You assume 30% of daytime customers will stay. If these customers simply shift their spend from high-margin bread ($5 average) to lower-margin pizza ($12 average but higher food/labor cost), your net profit may stagnate while operational complexity doubles.
2.  **Labor & Culture Burnout:** Baking bread starts at 3:00 AM; pizza service ends at 10:00 PM. Running both shifts risks exhausting your core bakers or forcing you to hire an entirely separate, expensive evening team you haven't budgeted for.
3.  **Brand Dilution:** Tamarind Hearth is known for artisan bread. Rushing out mediocre pizza to fill seats can damage your premium reputation faster than the new revenue builds it.
4.  **Hidden Capital Costs:** Your $60,000 budget covers hardware and remodels but ignores increased utility loads (gas/electric), higher insurance premiums for evening operations, marketing spend to attract the "new 15%," and working capital for new inventory (cheese, toppings) which spoils faster than flour.

**The Stop-Loss Trigger**

Establish a hard review date 90 days after the pilot launch. The specific trigger to **stop or pivot** is:

*   **Net Profit Margin < 15% on Pizza Sales** for two consecutive months, OR
*   **Gross Weekly Pizza Revenue < $3,000** (not $2,000, as your fixed costs for the oven rig require higher volume to justify) for two consecutive months.

If either metric is missed, cease evening operations at that location, sell the deck oven to recoup ~50% of capital, and revert the space to storage or prep. Do not throw good money after bad trying to "make it work" at all three sites.
