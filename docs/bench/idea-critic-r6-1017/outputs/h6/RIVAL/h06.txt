**Recommendation: Do not launch on 2025-03-01 as outlined.**

Your unit economics are currently negative, and the shipping variable creates an uncapped liability. You are budgeting $4.50 in variable costs per unit ($3.00 books + $1.50 ship) against a $15.00 revenue, leaving a $10.50 gross margin. However, you have not accounted for payment processing fees (~$0.60 + 2.9% ≈ $0.74), packaging materials beyond the box (filler, tape, inserts ≈ $0.50), or the labor cost to pick, pack, and handle returns. If your actual shipping average exceeds $1.50 due to zone creep or dimensional weight penalties, you could easily erode 20–40% of your remaining margin before fixed costs. Launching with an unverified carrier contract for mixed-size parcels is financially reckless; you must secure a guaranteed rate card or flat-zone pricing before accepting subscribers.

**Three Biggest Failure Modes:**

1.  **The "Zone Creep" Margin Death Spiral:** You assumed a flat $1.50 shipping cost. In reality, shipping costs vary wildly by distance and package density. If your members are dispersed nationally and your books are heavy hardcovers, your average cost could hit $2.50–$3.00 immediately. Combined with a 5% monthly churn (which is aggressive for a physical goods subscription; industry average is often 7–10%), your Customer Lifetime Value (LTV) drops below your Customer Acquisition Cost (CAC) breakeven point within month 4.
2.  **Inventory Obsolescence & Cash Flow Trap:** Buying 500–800 units of 4 specific books monthly requires ~$6,000–$9,600 in upfront inventory capital every 30 days. If a selected title underperforms or arrives damaged, you cannot return opened books to wholesalers. You risk accumulating dead stock that ties up cash flow, forcing you to borrow against future subscriptions that haven't been sold yet.
3.  **Operational Friction vs. Perceived Value:** A $15 price point is extremely low for physical goods. Customers will expect premium curation and flawless delivery. Any delay in the "monthly virtual author chat" or a single month of damaged books will spike churn instantly. At 5% churn, you lose 25–40 members monthly; acquiring replacements at typical subscription CAC ($30–$50) will burn cash faster than the $10.50 margin can replenish it.

**Arithmetic Slip:**
You stated a 30% churn rate will "stay under 5% monthly." This is a contradiction. A 30% *quarterly* churn rate implies roughly an 11% *monthly* churn rate (compounded), not 5%. If you truly achieve 5% monthly churn, your quarterly churn is only ~14%. If you actually experience 30% quarterly churn, your retention is far worse than modeled, cutting your LTV in half.

**Pre-agreed Pivot Trigger:**
**Stop or pivot immediately if Month 2 gross margin falls below 45% ($6.75 per unit) OR if new member acquisition falls below 150 in the first 45 days.** This ensures you do not scale a leaky bucket or a loss-leading product.
