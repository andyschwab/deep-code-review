**Recommendation: Do not proceed with the current plan.** The financial model contains a critical arithmetic error and a flawed competitive comparison that renders the projected profitability impossible.

**Three Biggest Failure Modes:**

1.  **Revenue Overestimation (The Math Error):** Your model projects $270,000/month based on 250 schools × 180 students × $6. However, $6/month equals **$72 per student annually**. Your competitor charges **$15 per student annually**. Even if your product is superior, pricing it nearly **5x higher** than the incumbent while admitting they include more features (digital tools) makes the "250 schools" target unrealistic. At the competitor's effective price point ($15/year), your actual revenue would be ~$56,000/month, not $270,000. You will not cover your $2.5M platform upgrade cost in the first year.
2.  **Integration Cost Blind Spot:** You have budgeted for platform upgrades and marketing, but omitted **LMS integration and implementation services**. K-12 schools rarely adopt standalone platforms; they require seamless integration with Canvas, Schoology, or Google Classroom. These integrations often require custom professional services per district, which are not accounted for in your $400k marketing or $2.5M platform budget. This will erode margins and extend the 180-day development timeline.
3.  **Sales Cycle Misalignment:** Assuming a 12-month adoption window based on fiscal years ignores the **procurement reality**. Even if a school wants to buy in October (start of their FY), K-12 procurement for new curriculum tools often takes 6–9 months *after* the budget is approved due to committee reviews, pilot requirements, and IT security audits. Signing 250 schools in Year 1 is operationally impossible given these friction points.

**Kill Trigger:**
Terminate the initiative if **pilot churn exceeds 5% within the first 90 days** or if **Customer Acquisition Cost (CAC) exceeds $1,500 per school**. Given the pricing disparity, if you cannot close pilot schools at a low CAC, the unit economics will never support the $2.5M investment.

**Critical Flaws for the COO:**
*   **Arithmetic:** You are comparing your $72/year price to a competitor's $15/year price. This is not a "stress test"; it is a different market segment.
*   **Missing Costs:** No budget for LMS integration engineering or customer success headcount required for 250 school onboarding.
*   **Assumption:** The "180 days development" assumes no scope creep for the necessary LMS integrations, which is highly unlikely.

**Immediate Action:** Recalculate the model at a competitive price point ($15–$20/year) and re-evaluate if the volume required to break even is achievable.
