**Recommendation: Do not proceed with the current plan.**

The financial model relies on a 15% conversion rate, which is dangerously optimistic for a local news archive. Industry benchmarks for niche archival paywalls typically range from 1% to 3%. Even at a generous 5% conversion, you would secure only 6,000 subscribers ($864k annual revenue), failing to cover the $750k upfront capital expenditure and the projected loss in ad revenue within the first year. The plan treats high-value archival access as a mass-market utility rather than a premium product for researchers and dedicated locals.

**Primary Failure Modes**

1.  **Conversion Delusion:** The 15% assumption (20,000 users) ignores that most archive traffic is transient or informational. Locking content immediately behind a hard paywall will likely cause a traffic drop far exceeding the modeled 20% ad revenue loss, destroying SEO value and brand visibility.
2.  **Regulatory & Legal Blind Spot:** Excluding GDPR/CCPA compliance costs is a critical oversight. Monetizing historical data involving living persons (post-1950) creates significant liability. A single right-to-be-forgotten lawsuit could exceed the entire project budget.
3.  **Churn Ignorance:** Without factoring churn, the Lifetime Value (LTV) is inflated. Archive subscriptions often see high initial sign-up followed by rapid cancellation once a specific research task is complete ("burst" usage), unlike daily news subscriptions.

**Kill/Pivot Trigger**

**Trigger:** If Month-One recurring revenue is **below $45,000** (implying a <3% conversion rate).

**Action:** Immediately halt further digitization spending and pivot to a "freemium" model (free search with paid full-text access) or an institutional licensing model (selling bulk access to libraries and universities) rather than direct-to-consumer subscriptions.
