**Recommendation: Proceed with a Phased Pilot, Not Full Launch**

Do not commit the full $150,000 capital expenditure or two full-time hires immediately. Instead, launch a **6-month pilot** using one senior videographer (contractor) and rented equipment to validate the production workflow before scaling.

**Critical Flaws & Hidden Costs**
Your current projection contains a fatal arithmetic error and a dangerous operational bottleneck:

1.  **The Double-Counting Error:** You stated you "added the same $150,000 equipment cost to the $250,000 revenue target." Revenue is income; equipment is an expense. They are not added together.
    *   *Corrected Year 1 P&L:* $288,000 (Revenue) − $140,000 (Salaries) − $150,000 (Equipment) = **−$2,000 Net Loss**.
    *   You have ignored overhead, taxes, insurance, software licenses, and marketing. The business loses money in Year 1 under your current model.
2.  **The Production Bottleneck:** 800 clients × 30% conversion = 240 videos. At 12 hours per edit, that is **2,880 editing hours**.
    *   Two full-time employees provide roughly 4,160 hours annually *total*, but only ~60–70% of that is billable production time after meetings, shooting, and admin.
    *   You will likely need to hire a dedicated third person solely for editing, or outsource post-production, eroding margins further.
3.  **Seasonality Risk:** Your 12-week ramp-up assumes steady work. Wedding seasons are peaked. If 60% of your bookings arrive in 4 months, your in-house team will drown, forcing expensive rush-outs or missed deadlines.

**The Kill Switch Metric**
Track **Net Contribution Margin per Video** monthly.

*   **Formula:** (Package Price − Variable Costs) / Package Price.
*   *Variable Costs include:* Editor hourly wage (actual time tracked), contractor fees, equipment rental/maintenance allocation, and re-shoot costs.
*   **Trigger:** If the Net Contribution Margin drops below **45%** for two consecutive months during the pilot, terminate the expansion plan.
*   *Why 45%?* You need this level of margin to eventually cover the fixed salaries and equipment depreciation. If you are below this, you are essentially paying clients to take videos.

**Summary for the Board**
The demand is likely real, but the unit economics are currently negative. By switching to a pilot model, we cap our downside risk at ~$40,000 (contractor fees + rentals) while proving the editing throughput capacity. We only authorize the $150,000 equipment purchase once the pilot proves we can edit 20 videos/month without exceeding the 45% margin threshold.

*Note: Ensure your legal team reviews contractor vs. employee classification for the pilot videographer to avoid misclassification penalties.*
