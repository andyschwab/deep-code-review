**Recommendation: Proceed with a "Go-If" Pilot, Not Full Build-Out**
Do not commit to the full $250,000 conversion yet. Instead, execute a phased pilot: construct only **four** private offices (600 sq ft) immediately to validate the $5 premium and Fortune 500 demand. Commit to the remaining 4,400 sq ft only if these four units are leased within 90 days of completion. This caps initial exposure at ~$24,000 while proving the core revenue hypothesis before sinking capital into the full floor.

**Three Main Failure Modes**
1.  **Demand Miscalculation:** The assumption that Fortune 500 firms need small (150 sq ft) private offices may be false; they often require larger suites or have strict vendor requirements you haven't verified. If the market wants open desks or larger blocks, you will be left with fragmented, unleaseable space.
2.  **Arithmetic & Cash Flow Error:** Your total cost calculation is incorrect. 5,000 sq ft × $40 = **$200,000**, not $250,000. While this lowers your break-even point, it also suggests your contingency planning is based on flawed data. More critically, at 80% occupancy, annual revenue is ~$220,000 (4,000 sq ft × $55), but you must cover operating expenses and vacancy gaps before hitting the 18-month break-even target.
3.  **Premium Rejection:** The $5 premium over open-desk rates assumes value perception that hasn't been tested. If corporate clients view the 3rd-floor layout as inferior or the price gap as negligible compared to building their own HQ, occupancy will stall at <50%, extending the break-even period beyond 36 months.

**Kill Criteria (Stop/Pivot Trigger)**
Stop further construction immediately if **fewer than two** of the pilot offices are signed on 12-month leases within 60 days of the pilot's completion. This indicates the price point or product type is mismatched to local demand.
