**Primary Failure Modes & Flawed Assumptions**
1.  **Double-Counted Expense & Profit Illusion:** Your suspicion is correct. You added the $150k equipment cost to the $250k revenue target, implying a $400k goal, but then likely subtracted the $150k again from profits. If your $250k target was meant to be *gross revenue*, your projected profit is actually **-$32,000** (Revenue $288k - Equipment $150k - Salaries $140k = -$2k, ignoring taxes/overhead). You are not covering the salaries in year one.
2.  **The "Hidden" Editing Bottleneck:** You assumed 12 hours per edit fits within a 12-week ramp-up without extra editors. With 240 clients (30% of 800), that is **2,880 hours** of editing. Two videographers working 40 hours/week for 52 weeks provide only ~4,160 hours total. If shooting takes 50% of their time, only ~2,080 hours remain for editing. You are short **~800 hours** (approx. 5 months of full-time labor) in year one unless you hire dedicated editors or outsource, which destroys your margin.
3.  **Conversion Rate Risk:** A 30% upsell rate from photo to video is exceptionally high for a new, unproven service. If conversion drops to 15%, revenue halves to $144k, resulting in a **-$146k loss** before fixing the editing gap.

**Kill Criteria (First 6 Months)**

*   **Trigger:** If the Net Contribution falls below **$400 per package** by Month 6, halt the expansion and do not hire full-time staff. This indicates the operational cost (specifically editing time) is too high to sustain the business model at the current price point.
