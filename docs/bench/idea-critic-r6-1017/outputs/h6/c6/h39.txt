Specifically, if 5,000 patrons attend 3 shows each, that is 15,000 seatings. If you produce, for example, 10 shows in a season, that is 1,500 attendees per show. At $8,000 cost per show, total production is $80,000. However, if you meant 5,000 passes *total* revenue is $600k, and costs are $8k per *performance*, you must define how many performances exist. If the $360,000 figure was intended as *cost*, the math ($1.8M revenue minus $360k cost) implies a massive profit, contradicting the need for a 3,200 break-even. The numbers as written ($1.8M revenue vs implied multi-million dollar production cost if every attendance triggers a new show cost, or confused unit economics) do not support the $600k total revenue claim derived from 5,000 × $120. *Correction:* 5,000 passes × $120 is **$600,000 total revenue**, not $1.8M. 3.  **Cannibalization:** Pass holders may displace full-price single-ticket buyers without generating net new attendance, effectively discounting your best customers and reducing overall yield.
