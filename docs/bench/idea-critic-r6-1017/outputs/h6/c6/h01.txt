**Recommendation: Proceed with a "Go-If" Pilot, Not a Full Rollout**

Do not commit the full $60,000 across all three locations immediately. Instead, approve a **single-location pilot** at your highest-traffic site for 90 days. Commit only $30,000 initially (one oven rig + $5,500 remodel + working capital). Expand to the other two locations only if the pilot hits specific revenue targets. This approach protects 50% of your capital while validating the critical assumption that daytime customers will convert to evening diners.

**Primary Failure Modes & Missed Risks**

1.  **Cannibalization vs. Conversion:** Your plan assumes 30% of daytime customers will stay. If these customers simply shift their $12 spend from afternoon pastries to evening pizza without bringing new guests, you gain no net revenue while increasing labor and utility costs. The risk is paying $60k to move existing sales to a more expensive daypart.
2.  **Operational Fatigue & Quality Decay:** Baking bread and firing pizza require different rhythms. Staff working double shifts (morning bake + evening service) may face burnout, leading to slower service or inconsistent product quality during the critical Friday-Saturday peak. This could damage the brand reputation built on your daytime offerings.
3.  **Underestimated Working Capital:** You have budgeted for hardware ($52k) and remodel ($8k), but omitted the cost of new inventory (cheese, toppings, specialized flour), additional evening labor hours, and marketing to attract the "new evening crowd." You may run out of cash flow before the concept proves itself.
4.  **The "Friday-Saturday" Trap:** Relying heavily on weekend nights creates a fragile model. If rain, local events, or economic tightening reduce foot traffic on just eight nights a month, your average weekly revenue will miss projections significantly, leaving you with idle staff and ovens during the week.

**Kill Criteria: When to Stop**

Terminate the pilot or revert to daytime-only operations if **either** of the following occurs:
*   **Revenue Floor:** Net pizza revenue (after food costs) averages below **$2,800 per week** (not $2,000, to account for the higher operational overhead of evening shifts) for **two consecutive months**.
*   **Conversion Failure:** After 60 days, less than **10%** of total pizza sales come from *new* evening customers (verified by transaction timestamps and loyalty data), indicating you are merely cannibalizing existing sales rather than expanding the market.

**Note:** Your current math projects $22,400/month in new sales against a $60k investment; ensure your labor cost for evening shifts doesn't exceed 30% of this new revenue, or the margin will vanish.
