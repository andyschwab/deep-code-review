**Recommendation: Go, but only as a capped 90-day pilot in the North zone starting Oct 1, 2025.** Do not launch across all three zones immediately. The financial model relies on a dangerous false equivalence between a "one-off guarantee" and a "recurring subscription guarantee," creating a high risk of margin collapse if refund rates climb even slightly above the historical 2.8%.

**Three Biggest Failure Modes:**
1.  **Adverse Selection & Moral Hazard:** The historical 2.8% refund rate likely applies to occasional users. Subscription users, paying $49 for up to three visits, are incentivized to demand a refund after *every* visit if standards slip, potentially driving effective refund rates to 15–20%, which would wipe out all revenue and incur net losses on labor.
2.  **Labor Cost Underestimation:** The model assumes only a 4% operating cost increase. However, if refund claims surge, the cost of re-cleaning or administering refunds (at $13.50/hr) will scale linearly with usage, not fixed overhead. A 10% refund rate could double the projected labor impact, turning the "deep clean" into a loss leader.
3.  **Competitor Price War:** If competitors match the guarantee within 60 days, your differentiation vanishes. Since your margin buffer is thin (dependent on low refunds), a price war forces you to either absorb costs or lose the 15% conversion target entirely, leaving you with higher operational complexity and no revenue lift.

**Kill Criterion:** Halt or pivot the program immediately if **refund costs exceed 3.5% of subscription revenue** in any single month of the pilot, or if the **average labor cost per subscribed household exceeds $18/month** for two consecutive weeks.
