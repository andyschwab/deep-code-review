**Recommendation: Go-If (Conditional Approval)**
Proceed with the zero-down leasing plan only under a staged pilot of 50 systems (20% of the target) for the first quarter, capped at $1.5M in initial exposure. This pilot must include a contractual early-termination penalty equivalent to three months' lease payments ($3,600) to mitigate the unpriced 5% default risk. Full scaling to 250 systems is prohibited until the state subsidy renewal is legislatively confirmed or the pilot demonstrates a net positive cash flow excluding subsidy revenue.

**Strongest Objection:**
The financial model relies on a "gross revenue" fallacy that ignores cash flow timing and subsidy dependency. While the theoretical 10-year revenue is $36M, the upfront cash requirement for 250 systems is $3.75M ($15k × 250) plus $2M labor, totaling $5.75M immediately. With only $12M cash on hand, this consumes nearly 48% of liquidity in one quarter. If the $0.08/kWh subsidy expires in 2026 as slated, the effective lease value drops, potentially making the $1,200/month price uncompetitive and spiking churn beyond the 5% assumption. Without the subsidy, the unit economics may fail to cover the inverter replacement reserve ($500/system) and capital costs, turning a paper profit into a cash-flow crisis by Year 3.

**Better Alternative:**
Adopt a "Lease-to-Own" hybrid with a mandatory down payment of $3,000 per system. This reduces the upfront capital burden by $750,000 for the full rollout (or $150k for the pilot), aligns customer skin-in-the-game to lower default rates, and preserves cash reserves against the subsidy cliff. Alternatively, secure a dedicated line of credit for inventory before committing cash, ensuring the $12M operating reserve remains untouched for labor and inverter replacements.

1. The state legislature does not introduce a renewal bill for the $0.08/kWh subsidy by **September 30, 2025**.
2. The pilot program's actual default rate exceeds **8%** (vs. the 5% assumption) within the first six months.
3. Installation labor costs exceed **$8,500 per system** (averaging the $2M total over 250 units), indicating a budget overrun that threatens liquidity.
