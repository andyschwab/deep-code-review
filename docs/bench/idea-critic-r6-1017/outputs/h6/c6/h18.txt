**Three Biggest Failure Modes**

1.  **Discovery Collapse:** The 15% market growth assumption does not guarantee visibility. Without a publisher's curated placement, "Nebula Drift" may drown in Steam's catalog, rendering the $80,000 marketing spend inefficient if the Cost Per Acquisition (CPA) exceeds $45.
2.  **Revenue Math Mismatch:** To hit $500,000 gross revenue after Steam's 30% fee, you need ~$714,000 in sales. At a standard $20 price point, this requires ~35,700 units. Achieving this with only a 5% demo conversion implies you need 714,000 demo downloads—a volume rarely achieved without significant existing IP or viral luck.
3.  **Cash Flow Timing:** Steam's 30-day payment hold plus chargeback reserves means revenue from the October launch won't clear until late November. If the $250,000 budget is strictly capped for development through October, you risk a liquidity crunch before the first royalty check arrives.

**Critical Flaws in Numbers & Assumptions**

*   **The Conversion Fallacy:** A 5% conversion from a *free* demo to a *paid* purchase is exceptionally aggressive for a new space-sim IP. Industry benchmarks for unrelated new IPs often hover between 1–3%. Basing your entire ROI on 5% creates a fragile financial model.
*   **Hidden Fee Blindspot:** You noted "hidden platform fees" as an unknown, yet they are standard. Beyond Steam's 30% cut, factor in ~5–8% for payment processing, taxes (VAT/GST), and potential refund rates (typically 5–10% in the first month). These combined can erode another 15% of your gross, slashing your projected net revenue significantly below the $500,000 target.
*   **Market Growth vs. Share:** A growing tide lifts all boats, but it also brings more sharks. A 15% YoY market growth likely means increased competition for the same user attention, potentially inflating your ad costs and lowering your actual conversion rate.

*   **Trigger:** Demo conversion rate **below 2.5%** after two weeks of live data (by 15 June 2025).
