**Three Biggest Failure Modes:**

1.  **Fatal Arithmetic Error (Revenue vs. Spend):** You projected $9,000 in revenue and an $8,000 ad spend, leaving a $1,000 margin. However, 200 members at $45/month for three months (June, July, August) generates **$27,000** in gross revenue ($45 × 200 × 3), not $9,000. If your revenue is actually only $9,000 in Q3, you are averaging only ~67 members per month, meaning you will fail to hit your 200-member goal by end-of-Q3. If you *do* hit 200 members quickly, your revenue is higher, but if the $9,000 figure was based on a slower ramp-up, your $8,000 ad spend represents nearly 90% of your early cash flow, leaving almost nothing for the **unpriced packaging and shipping**.
2.  **The "Unpriced Variable" Trap:** You explicitly stated packaging and shipping costs are unknown. In subscription boxes, these often consume 30–40% of the ticket price. At $45, if fulfillment costs $15/unit, your 200-member monthly cost is $3,000. Over Q3, that is $18,000. Combined with the $8,000 ad spend, your total costs are $26,000+. If your revenue calculation of $9,000 was the intended reality (slow uptake), you are losing money. If the $27,000 revenue is the reality, you break even only if fulfillment stays under $12.50/box. Launching without a fixed, verified fulfillment cost is gambling the $8,000 ad budget.
3.  **Conversion Delusion:** You assume 30% of existing customers (1,500 people) would sign up, yet you are only targeting 200. If your organic reach to existing customers is strong, you might not need the full $8,000 ad spend. Conversely, if you need paid ads to find *any* customers, the 30% organic assumption is false. Relying on a high organic conversion rate while simultaneously budgeting for heavy paid acquisition suggests a mismatch in channel strategy.
