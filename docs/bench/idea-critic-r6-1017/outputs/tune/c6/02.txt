**Recommendation: MODIFY (Pilot First)**
Do not approve the full $1.2M rollout yet. Execute a bounded pilot in **one** Metro branch with **three** loan officers and a capped technology spend of $250k. This tests the "instant-approve" adoption rate and underwriting capacity assumptions before committing to the remaining 80% of the budget. Proceed to full expansion only if the pilot achieves a net new loan volume of at least $360k (3% of total book) by end of Q3 2025 without requiring additional compliance headcount.

**Three Biggest Failure Modes:**
1.  **Revenue Math Error:** The projected $2M gain assumes the entire $12M book grows 15%, but new branches only generate *incremental* revenue. If the 15% growth target relies partly on existing branches, the new locations may fail to cover their specific $1.2M cost + 30% staff overhead, resulting in net loss despite total book growth.
2.  **Regulatory & Capacity Cliff:** The assumption that current underwriting/compliance can handle 30% more volume without new staff is a critical single point of failure. A 15% volume increase often triggers disproportionate regulatory reporting burdens or audit risks that existing staff cannot absorb, leading to processing bottlenecks or compliance violations.
3.  **Competitive Blind Spot:** If the competitor's "instant-approve" product has already captured the low-risk, high-speed segment (the most likely adopters), Harbor Trust's new branches may only attract higher-risk applicants who were rejected elsewhere, skewing the loan book's risk profile and increasing default rates beyond the flat-rate assumption.

**Abort Trigger:**
If net new loan volume attributed to the pilot branch is **<$360k** or if **any** additional compliance staff hiring becomes necessary to manage the pilot volume by September 30, 2025, immediately halt further branch openings and technology spending.

**Notes:**
The $2M revenue projection appears flawed as it applies the growth percentage to the existing base rather than isolating incremental gain from new assets.
Flat interest rate assumptions ignore potential margin compression if the "instant-approve" product forces a rate war to gain share.
