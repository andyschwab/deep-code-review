**Recommendation: Proceed with a "Go-If" Pilot, Not Full Launch**
Do not commit to the full $1.15M upfront spend (3 hires + inventory + licensing). Instead, execute a **capped pilot**: hire only **one** lead installer ($85k), secure licensing for **one** high-volume county ($50k estimated), and limit inventory to $150k. Proceed to the full three-hire rollout only if this pilot achieves **40 closed installs within 6 months** with a gross margin no lower than 35%.

**Main Failure Modes**
1.  **Margin Collapse from Labor Arbitrage:** Your model assumes Texas labor costs align with Arizona ($2.5k/kW vs. $2.0k/kW published rates) while ignoring your own observation of higher Dallas wages. If labor costs rise 20% above Arizona levels, your $8M revenue projection could see gross margin drop from 40% to below 25%, turning the projected $3.15M operating profit into a loss once fixed overhead is applied.
2.  **Permitting Bottlenecks Stalling Cash Flow:** Assuming a uniform permitting timeline is dangerous. If specific counties add 4–6 weeks of delay, your crew utilization drops. Three idle installers burning $255k/year in salary while waiting for permits will deplete cash reserves before the 250-install target is reached.
3.  **Incentive Dependency Risk:** Building the P&L on a 30% federal/state incentive remaining unchanged for three years is a single point of failure. Any legislative reduction to 10–15% immediately invalidates the $32k average contract price assumption, as customer adoption typically plummets when net costs rise.

**Kill Criteria (Stop-Loss Triggers)**
Stop or rethink the expansion immediately if either of the following occurs:
*   **Net Margin < 5% after Year 1:** If actual net profit (after all labor, inventory, and permitting costs) falls below 5% of revenue ($400k on $8M), the market density or cost structure cannot support the expansion model.
*   **Pilot Velocity Failure:** If the single-installer pilot fails to close **15 installs in the first 90 days** of operation, indicating that the sales cycle or permitting friction is twice as long as modeled.
