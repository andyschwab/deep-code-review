### Manual Review Strategy: Age-Bias Evaluation for Hiring System

**1. Construct a Stratified Test Corpus (The "Shadow" Batch)**
Since automated alerts are offline, you must manually construct a controlled dataset before processing real applications. Create 40–60 synthetic resumes that are identical in core technical competency, skills, and role relevance but vary systematically in age signals.
*   **Variables to Manipulate:** Graduation years (spanning 1985–2023), total years of experience (5 to 35 years), and early-career company names (to avoid era-specific bias).
*   **Control:** Ensure the "senior" and "junior" profiles solve the same technical problems with equivalent quality in the resume text.
*   **Segmentation:** Group these into pairs where only the age signal differs.

**2. Execute Blind Scoring and Gap Analysis**
Run this synthetic batch through your system. Have two reviewers independently record the output score/rank for each profile without knowing which variable was manipulated.
*   **Metric:** Calculate the average score delta between the "older" and "younger" versions of the same competency profile.
*   **Threshold:** Define a "fail" condition immediately. For example: "If the average score for candidates with >20 years experience is more than 10% lower than equivalent candidates with <10 years experience, the system fails."
*   **Qualitative Check:** Manually inspect the "reasoning" or "highlights" generated for older candidates. Look for hallucinated negatives (e.g., flagging "overqualified" or "culture fit" concerns not present in the input) or missing key skills that were successfully extracted from younger profiles.

**3. Implement a Human-in-the-Loop Gate (Irreversible Action Control)**
Ranking candidates for rejection is a high-stakes, hard-to-reverse action. Until the bias module is back online and statistical parity is proven:
*   **Freeze Auto-Rejection:** Do not allow the system to automatically reject any candidate flagged as "low score" if their profile contains age signals correlating to protected groups.
*   **Mandatory Human Review:** Route all scores in the bottom 30% and top 10% to a human recruiter for validation against the raw resume. The system should act as a *sorter*, not a *decider*, during this phase.
*   **Uncertainty Disclosure:** If the system displays confidence scores to internal recruiters, do not show raw percentages (e.g., "87% match"). Use defined coarse tiers (e.g., "High Match," "Potential Match," "Review Needed") to prevent over-reliance on uncalibrated numbers.

**4. Document Residual Risk (The Transparency Artifact)**
Create a temporary "System Limitation Card" for your internal team.
*   **State Clearly:** "Bias detection module offline; age-based fairness verified only via manual synthetic batch [Date]. Automated monitoring inactive."
*   **Owner:** Name the specific engineer or recruiter responsible for re-evaluating the system once the automated module returns.
*   **Status:** Mark any metric regarding "discrimination rate" as **UNVERIFIED** until the automated tool is active and running on live traffic. Do not invent a safety percentage.

**5. Legal and Compliance Routing**
Automated employment decision tools are heavily regulated (e.g., NYC Local Law 144, EU AI Act).
*   **Action:** Immediately route the question of "legal disclosure requirements for candidates" and "audit obligations" to legal counsel. Do not assume the manual review satisfies legal audit trails.
*   **Disclosure:** Prepare a candidate-facing notice stating that an automated tool is being used to assist in screening, pending legal confirmation on exact wording.

**Note:** This strategy measures harm via adversarial probing rather than certifying the system as "unbiased." The decision to ship rests with the product owner given the residual risk of the offline module.
