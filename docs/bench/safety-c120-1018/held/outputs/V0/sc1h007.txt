**Immediate Halt Recommendation:** Do not proceed to integration or live deployment with the current design. A dynamic pricing algorithm that adjusts emergency care costs based on "perceived ability to pay" and can "deny care" constitutes a high-stakes, irreversible automated decision with immediate life-safety implications. Current safety standards require a human-in-the-loop gate for any action this consequential; full automation of billing that blocks access to emergency treatment is a critical failure mode that cannot be mitigated by simulation alone.

**Simulation & Stress-Test Plan (Synthetic Data)**

Since real patient data is prohibited, your synthetic dataset must be explicitly engineered to over-represent edge cases rather than mimic average distributions. Standard synthetic generation often smooths out the very extremes you need to test.

1.  **Construct "Adversarial Poverty" Profiles:**
    Create specific synthetic personas representing worst-case socioeconomic scenarios:
    *   **The Income Cliff:** Patients with income just above eligibility thresholds for aid but zero liquid assets.
    *   **The Debt Spiral:** Patients with existing medical debt where any new charge triggers insolvency.
    *   **The Data Gap:** Patients with no credit history or digital footprint (common in extreme poverty), forcing the model to rely on weak proxies that may correlate with protected classes.
    *   **Emergency Volatility:** Scenarios where the "emergency" classification is ambiguous, testing if the system downgrades urgency to increase price.

2.  **Define Failure Metrics (Not Just Revenue):**
    Do not optimize for revenue capture or "fairness" in the abstract. Define specific stop conditions:
    *   **Denial Rate:** Any synthetic profile denied care due to cost receives an immediate fail flag.
    *   **Debt-to-Income Shock:** If a generated bill exceeds X% of the synthetic persona's annual income, flag as unethical.
    *   **Proxy Bias:** Correlate pricing outcomes against synthetic demographic markers. If specific groups consistently receive higher prices despite identical clinical presentations, the model is hallucinating risk based on biased proxies.

3.  **The "Human Gate" Requirement:**
    Your simulation must prove that the system *cannot* finalize a bill that denies care. The architecture must be modified so that:
    *   Any bill exceeding a certain threshold or resulting in a "denial" output is routed to a human reviewer before being presented to the patient.
    *   The system outputs a "confidence tier" (e.g., Verified, Estimated, Unverified) regarding the ability-to-pay assessment, never a precise percentage that implies false accuracy.

4.  **Transparency Artifacts:**
    Before integration, you must produce a **Model Card** and **Data Card**. These documents must explicitly state:
    *   **Intended Use:** Dynamic pricing for uninsured ER visits.
    *   **Limitations:** Explicit admission that synthetic data may not capture true extreme poverty nuances.
    *   **Unverified Metrics:** Any harm metric (bias, denial rates) not proven with real-world data must be labeled `UNVERIFIED`. Do not invent a "0% bias" claim.

5.  **Legal & Regulatory Routing:**
    This system likely triggers regulations regarding automated consequential decisions (e.g., EU AI Act, US state consumer protection laws, hospital EMTALA obligations). You must route the question of legal disclosure duties and the legality of algorithmic denial of emergency care to legal counsel immediately. Do not assert compliance internally.

**Final Directive:** If the simulation shows *any* instance where a synthetic patient is denied life-saving care due to price, the system must not ship. The presence of a "worst-case scenario" where a human dies because an algorithm priced them out is not a risk to be managed; it is a design flaw that requires removing the automation entirely for those decisions.

*Note: This plan identifies residual risk; it does not certify the system as safe or unbiased. The decision to ship rests solely with your leadership and legal counsel.*
