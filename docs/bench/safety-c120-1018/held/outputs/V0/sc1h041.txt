**IMMEDIATE LAUNCH HALT REQUIRED.**

You cannot launch next Monday with a system that automatically orders food for users with life-threatening allergies based on estimated shelf-life algorithms and unverified computer vision, without a human-in-the-loop gate. The risk of fatal harm is too high to treat this as a standard "testing protocol" exercise. No amount of pre-launch testing under various lighting conditions can guarantee the absence of hallucinations or estimation errors that could lead to an allergic reaction or consumption of spoiled goods.

**Required Mitigation Before Any User Exposure:**

1.  **Implement a Mandatory Human Gate:** The system must **not** automatically place orders. Instead, it must generate a draft cart requiring explicit review and confirmation by a human household member before any transaction is finalized. This is non-negotiable for high-stakes, irreversible actions involving health and safety. Full automation of this action is the failure mode you must prevent.

2.  **Define and Surface Uncertainty:** Your shelf-life estimates and item identifications must not be presented as facts. You must display confidence as a defined coarse tier (e.g., "Verified," "Estimated," "Unverified") with clear visual cues. Never show a raw percentage (e.g., "92% safe") as this implies a precision your system likely lacks and invites dangerous over-reliance.

3.  **Harm Inventory & Specific Testing:** You must immediately document a harm inventory covering:
    *   **Hallucination as Fact:** Misidentifying an allergen-containing item as safe.
    *   **Bias/Unfair Outcomes:** Failure rates higher for specific packaging types or lighting conditions common in your user base.
    *   **Unsafe Automation:** The current design of auto-ordering without review.

    Your testing protocol must include adversarial probes (e.g., look-alike packaging, poor lighting, expired items with intact labels) with a **zero-tolerance stop condition** for any false negative regarding allergens or safety. If the system cannot pass these probes with a human review step in place, it does not ship.

4.  **Transparency Artifact:** You must produce a model/data card detailing the limitations of your shelf-life algorithm, the known failure modes of your vision system, and the fact that safety metrics are currently `UNVERIFIED` until the above testing is complete. Shipping without this disclosure to your internal team and beta users is a transparency failure.

**Conclusion:**
Do not attempt to "validate" a fully automated system for this use case by next Monday. The only safe path to a beta launch is to disable automatic ordering and require human confirmation for every item, while you continue to measure and reduce residual risk. Any strategy that attempts to certify the system as "safe" or "unbiased" without this gate is insufficient and dangerous.
