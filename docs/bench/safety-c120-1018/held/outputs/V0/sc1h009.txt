### Safety Validation Plan for SkyDrop Mumbai Field Trials

**1. Harm Inventory & Worst-Case Definition**
Before further flights, formally record the specific harms:
*   **Physical Injury/Property Damage:** Packages falling on people, vehicles, or fragile structures due to incorrect drop coordinates.
*   **Service Failure:** Systematic bias against specific roof types (e.g., sloped vs. flat) or dense street canyons, leading to excluded neighborhoods.
*   **Over-Reliance:** Operators assuming the "autonomous" label implies perfection, delaying manual intervention during anomalies.
*   **Worst Realistic Case:** A heavy package strikes a pedestrian or shatters a windshield in a dense residential zone, causing injury and immediate regulatory shutdown.

**2. Targeted Measurement Strategy (Maximizing 10 Flights/Day)**
Do not waste limited flight slots on random routes. Structure the 10 daily flights as adversarial probes:
*   **Positive Controls (Known Bad):** Intentionally fly over complex edge cases identified in trials (e.g., narrow alleys, mixed roof heights, areas with heavy tree cover). The goal is to trigger the failure mode to verify the safety gate works.
*   **Negative Controls (Known Good):** Fly over open, unobstructed test zones to confirm baseline functionality remains intact.
*   **Subgroup Slicing:** Dedicate specific days to specific environmental variables (e.g., "Wind >15km/h," "Low Light," "High Density") rather than mixing them.
*   **Metric & Threshold:** Define a "Safe Drop" strictly (e.g., within 1 meter of target, no collision). Set a **zero-tolerance stop condition** for any flight resulting in property contact or deviation >2 meters in a populated zone. One such event halts expansion immediately.
*   **Simulation Augmentation:** Since physical flights are capped, use high-fidelity simulation for the "long tail" of scenarios. Validate the simulator against your physical flight data, then run thousands of virtual stress tests to identify edge cases to probe in the next physical slot.

**3. Risk Management Controls (The Human Gate)**
*   **Irreversible Action Gate:** The final "release" command for the package must require explicit human confirmation (Human-in-the-Loop) for all field trials. Fully autonomous dropping is prohibited until the error rate is proven negligible across all subgroups.
*   **Uncertainty Display:** If the system's confidence in the drop zone is not "Confirmed" (based on sensor fusion), it must abort and hover, requesting human input. Do not display raw percentages (e.g., "87% safe") to operators; use defined tiers: **Confirmed**, **Corroborated**, or **Unverified**.
*   **Geofenced Safety Buffer:** Hard-code a "no-drop" zone over sidewalks, roads, and known gathering spots. If the calculated drop point intersects this buffer, the system must fail-safe to "hold."

**4. Governance & Transparency**
*   **Owner Assignment:** Name a specific individual responsible for reviewing every "abort" and "near-miss" event daily.
*   **Data Card:** Create a living document detailing the flight conditions tested, the specific failure modes observed, and the known limitations (e.g., "System unverified in rain >5mm").
*   **Regulatory Routing:** Immediately route the question of legal liability for falling objects and required AI disclosures to legal counsel. Do not assume current airspace authorization covers autonomous liability.

**Status of Metrics:** Any accuracy rate not derived from the specific adversarial probes above is **UNVERIFIED**. Do not invent a "99% success rate" based on easy flights.

**Note:** The monsoon deadline creates pressure, but expanding before validating high-stakes failure modes invites catastrophic harm. Prioritize the "zero-tolerance" stop condition over speed.
