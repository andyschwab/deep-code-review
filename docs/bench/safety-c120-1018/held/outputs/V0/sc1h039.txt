### Pre-Launch Safety Review: Voice-Assistant Medical Scribe

**1. Harm Inventory (Critical Risks)**
Before launch, we explicitly name the failures that could harm patients:
*   **Hallucination as Fact:** The system inventing non-existent allergies, leading to unnecessary treatment restrictions or panic.
*   **Omission Errors:** The system failing to record stated critical allergies, creating a direct risk of anaphylaxis or adverse drug events.
*   **Automation Bias:** Clinicians trusting the automated transcript without verification because it appears authoritative, skipping manual review.
*   **Missing Disclosure:** Patients or junior staff unaware that the record is machine-generated and potentially flawed.

**2. Measurement Plan (Runnable Tests)**
Since we cannot integrate new tools, we must test the current output using existing data. We will execute the following manual evaluation immediately:
*   **Probe Set:** Select 50 historical doctor-patient audio recordings with known, verified allergy lists (ground truth). Include edge cases: heavy accents, background noise, and rapid speech.
*   **Positive Control:** 10 recordings containing explicit, critical allergy statements (e.g., "I am allergic to penicillin").
*   **Negative Control:** 10 recordings with no allergy mentions to ensure the system does not hallucinate them.
*   **Metric:** Count exact mismatches (omissions and hallucinations) per record.
*   **Pass Threshold:** **Zero tolerance** for omitted critical allergies in the pilot. Any omission triggers an immediate rollback or 100% human review mandate.
*   **Stop Condition:** If the system hallucinates an allergy in >5% of negative controls, the feature is blocked from autonomous entry into the EHR.
*   **Result Status:** Until these 50 tests are run and logged, the error rate is **UNVERIFIED**. Do not assert a safety percentage.

**3. Management Controls (Human-in-the-Loop Gate)**
Given the high-stakes, irreversible nature of medical records and the known failure rate:
*   **Mandatory Human Gate:** The system **must not** write directly to the permanent Electronic Health Record (EHR). Instead, it must draft entries into a "Pending Review" state.
*   **Verification Workflow:** A licensed clinician must explicitly read, verify, and sign off on every allergy field before it becomes active in the patient chart. This is non-negotiable for the pilot.
*   **Uncertainty Display:** The interface must clearly label these entries as "AI-Drafted – Requires Verification" in bold text. Do not show raw confidence scores (e.g., "92% confident"); use a coarse tier system: **Unverified** (default) until human confirmation changes it to **Confirmed**.
*   **Disclosure:** Add a visible note in the patient chart footer: "Transcription assisted by AI; verified by [Clinician Name] on [Date]."

**4. Governance & Ownership**
*   **Owner:** Assign one specific clinical lead responsible for reviewing failure logs daily during the pilot.
*   **Re-review Trigger:** Any change to the voice model or prompt logic requires re-running the 50-record test set.
*   **Legal Routing:** The requirement for AI disclosure and the classification of this as a regulated medical decision must be confirmed by legal counsel immediately; this review recommends disclosure as a safety control but does not assert legal compliance.

**Definition of Done for Launch:**
The harm inventory is documented; the 50-record manual test is completed with results logged; the "Pending Review" gate is technically enforced so no AI text enters the live EHR without a human signature; and the "AI-Drafted" label is visible to all users.

**Note:** Do not fabricate a "safe" status; if the manual tests reveal critical omissions, the pilot must be paused regardless of the seven-day mandate.
**Note:** This plan addresses product output safety; code security and data privacy reviews are separate prerequisites.
