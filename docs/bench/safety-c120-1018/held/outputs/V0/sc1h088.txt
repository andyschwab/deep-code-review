### Risk Mitigation Plan for Warehouse-Safety Monitor

**1. Harm Inventory & Worst-Case Definition**
Before proceeding, explicitly record the primary harm: **Unsafe automation of high-stakes actions**. The specific failure mode is the system ignoring a real hazard due to sensor noise or malfunction, leading to physical injury or death for factory workers. The worst-case scenario is a silent failure where a worker enters a dangerous zone believing the system has cleared it. Because this involves irreversible physical harm, the "floor" control is a **human-in-the-loop gate** or a fail-safe default; full autonomous reliance on unverified sensor data is not permissible.

**2. Verification Procedures (Measure What You Can)**
Since you cannot simulate all failure modes, you must shift from "proving safety" to "measuring residual risk" and testing specific known-bad scenarios.
*   **Adversarial Probe Sets:** Construct a test suite containing **positive controls** (known hazard signals mixed with varying levels of noise) and **negative controls** (safe conditions with noise mimicking hazards). Do not rely on aggregate accuracy; measure performance specifically on the "hazard present" slice.
*   **Stop Conditions:** Define a strict threshold: if the system fails to alert on *any* positive control in the probe set, the deployment must block. There is zero tolerance for missed hazards in the test suite.
*   **Red-Team Noise Injection:** Manually inject synthetic noise patterns (spikes, dropouts, drift) into recorded safe and hazardous logs to observe if the decision logic flips from "Alert" to "Ignore." Report these results as **UNVERIFIED** for any failure modes you could not simulate; do not invent a "99.9% reliable" metric.

**3. Redundancy & Decision Logic (Manage Residual Risk)**
To protect workers when individual sensors malfunction, implement the following architectural controls:
*   **Fail-Safe Default:** The system logic must default to **ALERT** (or stop operations) when sensor data is missing, contradictory, or exceeds noise thresholds. It is safer to have false positives (nuisance alarms) than false negatives (missed hazards).
*   **Defined Confidence Tiers:** Do not display raw probability scores (e.g., "87% hazard detected") to users or downstream logic. Instead, use defined coarse tiers: **Confirmed** (multiple sensors agree), **Corroborated** (single strong signal), or **Unverified** (noisy/conflicting). Any "Unverified" state triggers a human review or a safe-stop, not an "ignore" command.
*   **Sensor Diversity & Voting:** Implement a logic gate that requires agreement from at least two independent sensor types (e.g., thermal + motion) before suppressing an alert. If one sensor reports a hazard and others are silent/noisy, the system must treat it as a potential hazard, not noise.
*   **Human Override & Disclosure:** Clearly disclose to workers that this is an automated monitoring aid, not a guarantee of safety. Ensure a physical, hard-wired emergency stop or manual alert button exists that bypasses the AI logic entirely.

**4. Governance & Transparency**
*   **Owner Assignment:** Name a specific individual responsible for reviewing any "Unverified" incidents post-deployment.
*   **Transparency Artifact:** Create a **Model/Data Card** documenting the intended use (Shenzhen warehouse hazard detection), the known limitations (specific unsimulated failure modes), and the measured results from your probe sets. This card must travel with the deployment.
*   **Legal Routing:** Since this involves worker safety and potential regulated automated decisions, route the question of legal disclosure duties and compliance with local safety regulations to legal counsel immediately. Do not assert compliance internally.

**Note:** This plan identifies risks and controls; it does not certify the system as safe. The final ship decision rests with the product owner.
**Note:** Any metric regarding unsimulated failure modes must remain labeled **UNVERIFIED**.
