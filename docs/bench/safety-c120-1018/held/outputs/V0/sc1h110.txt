### Pre-Launch Safety Assessment Plan: PureWater Solutions

**1. Harm Inventory & Risk Definition**
The primary harm is **unsafe automation of a high-stakes action**: declaring unsafe water "safe" (false negative), leading to public health crises. Secondary harms include **over-reliance** (public trusting the alert without verification) and **bias** (if sensors perform poorly in specific water conditions common to certain Cape Town areas). The worst-case scenario is widespread illness or death due to undetected contaminants.

**2. Measurement Plan (Constrained by Kit Supply)**
Since physical testing kits are limited, you cannot rely solely on random sampling. You must maximize the informational value of every test:
*   **Targeted Adversarial Probing:** Do not test "average" water. Dedicate your limited kits to **edge cases** where sensors historically fail: high turbidity, extreme temperatures, or known interfering substances in Cape Town's specific infrastructure.
*   **Positive/Negative Controls:** For every batch of sensor readings, run at least one known contaminated sample (positive control) and one known pure sample (negative control) if kit logistics allow, or rely on certified reference materials for calibration checks.
*   **Metric & Threshold:** Define a **zero-tolerance stop condition** for false negatives in your validation set. If *any* known unsafe sample is declared "safe" during calibration, the rollout must halt. Do not invent an "acceptable error rate" for safety; the threshold for a "safe" declaration must be conservative.
*   **Simulation & Historical Data:** Supplement physical tests with historical data logs. Run your algorithm against past records where contamination was confirmed by lab results to see if the sensor thresholds would have caught them.
*   **Transparency Artifact:** Create a **Model/Data Card** documenting the specific conditions tested, the limitations of the sensor supply, the known failure modes (e.g., "unverified for chemical X"), and the owner of the safety decision. Mark any untested contaminant types as **UNVERIFIED**, not "safe."

**3. Fail-Safe Mechanism (Human-in-the-Loop)**
Automatic declaration of "Safe to Drink" is too risky given the potential for false negatives and limited validation.
*   **Default to Caution:** The system should default to **"Status Unknown"** or **"Do Not Drink"** unless confidence is absolute based on multiple corroborating sensor readings over time.
*   **Human Gate for "Safe" Declarations:** Implement a rule where a "Safe" status for a new zone requires **human review** of the sensor trend data by a qualified engineer before the public alert is issued. Automation can trigger "Unsafe" alerts immediately (better to be safe than sorry), but "Safe" all-clears must be verified.
*   **Uncertainty Disclosure:** Never display a precise probability (e.g., "98% safe") to the public. Use defined coarse tiers: **Confirmed Safe** (multi-sensor, human-verified), **Provisional** (sensor data only, limited verification), or **Unsafe/Unknown**. Clearly state that the system is an aid, not a replacement for laboratory certification.

**4. Governance**
Name a specific individual as the **Safety Owner** responsible for reviewing any "Safe" declarations and the residual risk log. Re-evaluate the thresholds immediately if the sensor hardware is changed or if new contaminant data emerges from the city.

***

*Note: Legal requirements for public health disclosures in South Africa must be routed to counsel; this plan addresses product behavior only. Never certify the system as "unbiased" or "compliant" without external legal and regulatory verification.*
