### Pre-Launch Validation Framework: Parisian Tenant Rights Chatbot

**1. Harm Inventory & Risk Mapping**
Before testing, formally record the specific risks to tenants:
*   **Hallucination as Fact:** The bot invents a legal clause or cites a repealed ordinance, leading a tenant to miss a deadline or waive a right.
*   **Outdated Advice (Latency Risk):** The bot applies old rent control caps or eviction timelines due to the two-week retraining lag, causing financial loss or unlawful eviction.
*   **Over-Reliance:** Tenants treat the bot's output as a binding legal judgment rather than preliminary guidance, skipping professional counsel in complex cases.
*   **Missing Disclosure:** Users are unaware they are interacting with an AI that may be outdated, creating a false sense of security.

**2. Measurement Plan (Runnable Eval Rows)**
Do not assert accuracy; measure it. Construct an evaluation suite with one test row per identified harm. Each row must include:
*   **Probe Set:** A mix of standard queries, adversarial prompts (e.g., "My landlord says I must leave tomorrow, is this legal?"), and edge cases involving recent ordinance changes.
*   **Controls:**
    *   *Positive Control:* Inputs where the correct legal answer is known and stable.
    *   *Negative Control:* Inputs where the law is ambiguous or recently changed, requiring a refusal.
*   **Metric:** Percentage of responses that are legally accurate *and* appropriately qualified.
*   **Pass Threshold:** Owner-defined. For high-stakes advice (eviction, money), propose **zero tolerance** for confident, incorrect assertions.
*   **Stop Condition:** If the bot provides confident, incorrect advice on >0% of high-stakes probes, launch is blocked.
*   **Reviewer:** A qualified legal expert must review all failures.

**3. Managing Residual Risk: The Human Gate & Refusal Logic**
*   **Mandatory Refusal Triggers:** Program the bot to explicitly refuse answering (and route to a human/resource list) when:
    *   The query involves active eviction proceedings or immediate financial liability.
    *   The relevant ordinance has changed within the last 30 days (exceeding your retraining latency).
    *   The model's internal confidence is below a "Confirmed" tier.
*   **Uncertainty Disclosure:** Never display raw confidence scores (e.g., "87% sure"). Instead, use defined coarse tiers with clear definitions:
    *   *Confirmed:* Based on stable, long-standing code.
    *   *Corroborated:* Matches multiple sources but requires verification.
    *   *Unverified/Outdated Risk:* Explicitly state, "Local laws change frequently; this information may be outdated. Consult a lawyer."
*   **Human-in-the-Loop:** For any output classified as high-stakes, require a human legal review before the advice is presented to the user, or design the flow to always hand off to a human partner for final validation in critical scenarios.

**4. Transparency Artifacts**
*   **Model Card:** Publish a versioned document detailing the bot's intended use (preliminary guidance only), limitations (two-week latency, not a substitute for counsel), and measured performance by legal topic.
*   **Data Card:** Document the source of the training data (specific Parisian codes), the cutoff date, and known gaps.

**5. Governance & Legal Routing**
*   **Owner:** Name a specific individual responsible for the bot's output safety.
*   **Legal Duty:** You asked about verifying against municipal codes. Whether your bot constitutes a regulated legal service requiring specific disclaimers or licensing under French law is a legal determination. **Route this immediately to counsel.** Do not assume the current disclaimer is sufficient.
*   **No Certification:** This framework measures risk; it does not certify the bot as "safe" or "unbiased." The decision to ship rests with your leadership based on the residual risk reported.

**Note:** Given your two-week latency, your "refusal" logic for recent changes is your primary safety control until the pipeline is accelerated.
