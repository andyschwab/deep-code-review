**Immediate Action Plan: Validate Tax Bot Accuracy & Mitigate Filing Risks**

**1. Halt Irreversible Automation (Human-in-the-Loop Gate)**
Do not enable automatic filing for all users yet. Filing a tax form is a high-stakes, irreversible action with legal and financial consequences. Given the known miscalculations in home-office deductions, you must implement a mandatory human review gate for any return involving complex deductions or flagged edge cases before submission. Automatic execution of potentially erroneous filings is the primary failure mode to prevent.

**2. Construct a Harm Inventory**
Before testing, explicitly document the specific risks your bot poses to Chicago entrepreneurs:
*   **Hallucination as Fact:** The bot presenting an unverified deduction rule as absolute truth.
*   **Bias/Unfair Outcomes:** Systematic under- or over-claiming for specific business types (e.g., sole proprietorships vs. LLCs) or neighborhoods.
*   **Over-reliance:** Users trusting the "minimized liability" output without understanding the audit risk.
*   **Unsafe Automation:** Filing incorrect forms that trigger IRS/IDOR audits or penalties without user confirmation.
*   **Missing Disclosure:** Users unaware that the filing decision was made by an AI, not a human accountant.

**3. Execute Targeted Output Evaluations (Measure, Don't Assert)**
You cannot certify the bot is "accurate" or "safe" without evidence. Run specific tests against your harm inventory:
*   **Adversarial Probe Set:** Create a dataset of 50+ known difficult home-office scenarios (mixed-use spaces, rental properties, recent code changes) where the correct deduction is known by a human expert.
*   **Positive/Negative Controls:** Include clear-cut cases (known good) and deliberately tricky cases (known bad) to ensure the bot distinguishes them.
*   **Metric & Threshold:** Define a pass/fail criterion. For high-stakes filing, propose zero tolerance for calculation errors in the test set. If the bot fails even one known case in the probe set, the stop condition triggers a block on auto-filing.
*   **Injection & Leakage Checks:** Test if malicious receipt images or prompt injections can alter the calculation logic or leak other users' data.

**4. Report Residual Risk (No Fabricated Metrics)**
Draft a transparency report for your team and users. Do not invent an "accuracy rate" (e.g., "99% accurate") if you haven't measured it across all scenarios. Label untested areas as **UNVERIFIED**. Explicitly state: "Home-office deductions for mixed-use properties remain UNVERIFIED pending further human review."

**5. Create a Model/Data Card**
Produce a simple, versioned document detailing:
*   **Intended Use:** Small-business tax deduction calculation for Chicago entities only.
*   **Limitations:** Known failure modes in complex home-office scenarios; not a substitute for a CPA.
*   **Data Provenance:** Source of the tax codes used (e.g., specific IRS/IDOR publications and dates).
*   **Owner:** Name the specific team member responsible for the output.

**6. Route Legal Questions**
Whether you are *legally required* to disclose AI usage in tax filings or if this tool constitutes a regulated "automated decision" under Illinois or federal law is a legal determination. Route this immediately to counsel; do not assert compliance yourself.

**Note:** Given your beta stage and limited budget, focus resources on the human-in-the-loop gate for high-risk filings and the adversarial testing of the specific home-office edge cases you already identified. Do not skip the transparency artifact; a simple model card is low-cost but critical for trust.
