### Pre-Launch Safety Checklist: Automated Claim Denial System

**1. Immediate Halt on Fully Autonomous High-Stakes Actions**
*   **Action:** Do not launch with "final decisions without human review."
*   **Requirement:** Implement a mandatory **Human-in-the-Loop (HITL) gate** for all denial decisions immediately. Given the tooling constraint where explanations cannot be generated in all supported languages, fully automated denials are unsafe and potentially deceptive. No claim should be rejected without a human verifying the logic and ensuring the customer receives a comprehensible reason in their language.

**2. Harm Inventory & Worst-Case Mapping**
*   **Action:** Document specific failure modes before UAT concludes.
*   **Required Entries:**
    *   **Bias/Unfair Outcomes:** Systematic denial rates higher for specific regions or languages due to data gaps.
    *   **Hallucination as Fact:** Denials based on fabricated policy clauses or non-existent evidence.
    *   **Missing Disclosure:** Customers unaware an AI made the initial assessment, especially where explanations are missing or garbled.
    *   **Unsafe Automation:** Irreversible financial loss caused by auto-rejection without a viable, immediate appeal path.
*   **Output:** A written record identifying who is affected by each harm and the worst realistic financial/emotional impact.

**3. Measurement Plan (Replace "Testing" with "Harm Evaluation")**
*   **Action:** Define runnable tests for each inventoried harm; do not rely on aggregate accuracy metrics.
*   **Requirements:**
    *   Create adversarial test sets for each supported language, specifically targeting the "explanation gap."
    *   Include **positive controls** (clearly valid claims) and **negative controls** (clearly invalid claims) for every language segment.
    *   **Stop Condition:** Define a specific failure threshold (e.g., *any* valid claim denied without a readable explanation) that automatically blocks the rollout of automation to that segment.
    *   **Status:** Mark any unmeasured metric (e.g., "bias rate for Language X") as **UNVERIFIED**. Do not invent safety percentages.

**4. Transparency & Disclosure Artifacts**
*   **Action:** Publish a Model Card and Data Sheet before launch.
*   **Content:**
    *   **Intended Use:** Clearly state the system is an assistive tool for reviewers, not a final decision-maker, due to current language limitations.
    *   **Limitations:** Explicitly list which languages lack readable explanation generation.
    *   **Data Provenance:** Document gaps in training data for international claims.
    *   **Performance:** Report evaluation results by language subgroup, not just global averages.
*   **Rule:** If a metric is not measured, label it **UNVERIFIED**. Never claim "0% error" or "unbiased."

**5. Governance & Legal Routing**
*   **Action:** Route regulatory questions to counsel immediately.
*   **Requirement:** Do not assert internally whether this system violates the EU AI Act, local insurance regulations, or consumer protection laws regarding automated decisions. Engage legal counsel to determine if "final decisions without human review" are legally permissible given the explanation failures.
*   **Ownership:** Name a specific individual responsible for reviewing denial errors and updating the harm inventory as the system scales.

**6. Ramp-Up Protocol**
*   **Action:** Reject the "50% volume by end of quarter" goal if safety gates are not met.
*   **Strategy:** Automation volume must be tied to the success of the HITL gate and the resolution of the explanation module. Start with low-stakes or internal-only processing. Only increase automation percentage when the error rate for specific language groups is verified as acceptable via the measurement plan above.

***

**Note:** This checklist treats the inability to explain denials in all languages as a critical safety failure, not just a UX bug.
**Note:** No AI system handling financial crises should operate without a verified, human-overrideable path for every single decision.
