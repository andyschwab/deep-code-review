### Pre-Launch Review Process: Harm Inventory & Simulation

Since you cannot test on real historical cases, you must rigorously simulate failure modes using synthetic data. Do not treat this as a "pass/fail" certification, but as a measurement of residual risk.

**1. The Harm Inventory (Map)**
Before launching, explicitly record the worst-case scenario for each risk:
*   **False Rejection (Bias/Error):** A valid appeal is rejected due to minor formatting errors (e.g., slightly cropped photo, non-standard PDF), costing the user money and license points.
*   **Hallucination as Fact:** The bot invents a legal precedent or misreads a timestamp, leading the user to submit a flawed argument.
*   **Over-Reliance:** Users trust the bot's draft without reviewing it, assuming it is legally perfect.
*   **Missing Disclosure:** Users do not realize the argument was machine-generated and may lack necessary human legal nuance.

**2. Runnable Simulation Plan (Measure)**
Create one test row per harm above. Since you lack real data, your synthetic dataset must include:
*   **Positive Controls:** Clearly valid appeals with perfect formatting.
*   **Negative Controls:** Clearly invalid appeals (e.g., wrong car, expired date).
*   **Edge-Case Probes (The Critical Set):** Valid appeals with "minor errors" you mentioned (blurry edges, unusual file names, low-resolution photos, handwritten notes).
*   **Adversarial Probes:** Inputs designed to trick the logic (e.g., photoshopped evidence, conflicting timestamps).

**Metric & Threshold:** Define a "False Rejection Rate" for the Edge-Case Probes. Since the stakes are high (money/license points), propose a **zero-tolerance stop condition** for automated rejection of edge cases. If the bot rejects *any* valid appeal in your edge-case set, it must not auto-reject; instead, it must trigger a human review.

**3. Management Controls (Manage)**
*   **Human-in-the-Loop Gate:** Because a wrong rejection causes irreversible financial and legal harm, **do not allow fully automated rejections.** Any appeal flagged as "complex," "low confidence," or containing non-standard formatting must be routed to a human reviewer before a final decision is communicated. Full automation of high-stakes actions is the failure mode you must prevent.
*   **Confidence Tiers:** Do not show users a raw percentage (e.g., "87% valid"). Instead, use defined coarse tiers:
    *   *Confirmed:* Matches all known valid patterns.
    *   *Review Required:* Formatting anomalies or ambiguous evidence detected.
    *   *Insufficient:* Missing critical data.
*   **Transparency Artifact:** Draft a "Model Card" for your users. It must state: intended use (drafting support, not legal advice), limitations (trained on synthetic data, may miss novel edge cases), and the fact that final decisions involving rejection are reviewed by humans.

### Guide: Explaining Decisions to Users

When the bot interacts with users, it must disclose its nature and limits clearly:
1.  **AI Disclosure:** "This argument draft was generated by AI. It is a tool to assist you, not a substitute for a lawyer."
2.  **Uncertainty Communication:** If the bot detects formatting issues, say: "We noticed your photo is slightly blurry. While this might be acceptable, our system cannot guarantee acceptance. We recommend a human review to ensure your evidence isn't wrongly rejected."
3.  **Actionable Overrides:** Always provide a clear path for the user to override the bot's suggestion or request human assistance. "Disagree with this assessment? Click here to speak with a legal expert."
