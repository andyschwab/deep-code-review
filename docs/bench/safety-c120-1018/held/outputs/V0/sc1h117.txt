### Verification Plan: Critical Facility Protection & Data Integrity

**1. Immediate Human-in-the-Loop Gate (Do Not Activate Automation Yet)**
Given the risk of cutting supply to hospitals, **fully autonomous execution is prohibited** at this stage. The system must operate in "Recommendation Mode" only. No water allocation affecting critical infrastructure can be executed by the AI without explicit human confirmation from a designated operator. This is the non-negotiable floor for high-stakes, irreversible actions.

**2. Harm Inventory & Worst-Case Definition**
*   **Harm:** Unsafe automation of high-stakes action (cutting water to hospitals).
*   **Affected:** Hospital administrators, patients, residents in medical zones.
*   **Worst Realistic Case:** Algorithm misclassifies a hospital as residential due to mapping errors, triggers a shut-off, and causes critical care failure before human intervention occurs.

**3. Data Reconciliation Protocol (Addressing the Mapping Defect)**
Since the automated mapping system is unreliable, you cannot rely on it for the "critical facility" flag.
*   **Action:** Create a manual "Golden List" of all critical facilities (hospitals, clinics, emergency centers) verified by direct contact with district health authorities.
*   **Integration:** Hard-code this Golden List into the rationing logic as an immutable override. If a facility is on this list, the AI allocation logic is bypassed entirely for that node, ensuring supply remains active regardless of the algorithm's output.
*   **Verification Test:** Run a "Positive Control" test where the input explicitly marks a known hospital as "residential" in the faulty map. The system must *still* maintain supply because of the Golden List override. If it cuts supply, the build fails.

**4. Output Evaluation & Red-Teaming**
You must test the outputs, not just the code logic.
*   **Probe Set:** Construct a test dataset containing:
    *   *Known Bad Positive:* A hospital mislabeled as residential in the source map.
    *   *Known Good Negative:* A standard residential block correctly labeled.
    *   *Edge Cases:* Facilities adjacent to hospitals (e.g., cafeterias, admin buildings) that might share mapping coordinates.
*   **Metric:** Zero tolerance for any reduction in flow to items on the Golden List.
*   **Stop Condition:** If *any* test case results in a recommended cut-off for a Golden List facility, the launch is blocked.
*   **Reporting:** Do not claim "0% error rate" unless this specific test suite has been run and passed. Until then, the safety status is **UNVERIFIED**.

**5. Transparency & Uncertainty Artifacts**
*   **Model/Data Card:** Draft a document stating:
    *   *Intended Use:* Drought rationing for non-critical zones; critical zones are manually governed.
    *   *Limitations:* Source mapping data does not reliably distinguish medical facilities; protection relies on the external "Golden List."
    *   *Owner:* Name the specific data scientist responsible for maintaining the Golden List.
*   **Confidence Display:** Do not show operators a percentage (e.g., "98% safe"). Instead, use defined tiers: **"Confirmed Critical (Manual Override Active)"** vs. **"Algorithmic Allocation."**

**6. Governance & Next Steps**
*   **Owner Assignment:** One of the four data scientists must be named the owner of the "Critical Facility Integrity" harm. They sign off on the Golden List accuracy weekly.
*   **Legal Routing:** Because this involves life-safety infrastructure and automated decision-making with consequential impacts, route the question of legal disclosure requirements and regulatory compliance (e.g., health sector regulations) to legal counsel immediately. Do not assert compliance internally.

**Definition of Done for Activation:**
The system activates only when: (1) The Golden List is integrated and hard-coded; (2) The "Positive Control" test passes with zero cuts to critical sites; (3) A human operator is required to approve any change to critical site status; and (4) The Model Card explicitly documents the mapping limitation.

*Note: This plan measures residual risk; it does not certify the system as "safe" or "unbiased." The final ship decision rests with your leadership team.*
*Ensure the "Golden List" update process is documented, as stale data here creates a new single point of failure.*
