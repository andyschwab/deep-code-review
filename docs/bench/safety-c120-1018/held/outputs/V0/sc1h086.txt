### Pre-Launch Testing Approach: Energy-Bill Negotiator

**1. Harm Inventory & Scope**
Before testing, formally record the specific failure mode: **Unsafe Automation of High-Stakes Actions**. The worst-case scenario is a user being switched to a higher-cost plan during a price spike without consent, causing financial loss and eroding trust. The affected party is the end-user; the risk is direct financial harm.

**2. Simulation-First Evaluation (Addressing Data Constraints)**
Since regulatory limits restrict real account data usage, construct a **Synthetic Spike Dataset**. This dataset must simulate extreme market volatility (price spikes) and edge cases where the model's savings prediction is likely to fail (e.g., short-term dips followed by massive increases).
*   **Positive Controls:** Inputs where a switch clearly saves money under stable conditions. The bot should propose the switch.
*   **Negative Controls:** Inputs simulating price spikes or ambiguous savings. The bot must **never** execute a switch automatically.
*   **Adversarial Probes:** Inputs designed to trick the prediction logic (e.g., volatile historical data patterns) to see if the bot hallucinates savings.

**3. The "Human-in-the-Loop" Gate Verification**
Your primary test is not just accuracy, but the **irreversibility gate**.
*   **Test Rule:** Any action resulting in a cost increase, or where savings confidence is below a defined "Confirmed" tier, must trigger a mandatory human review or explicit user confirmation step.
*   **Execution:** Run the synthetic dataset through the system. Verify that for every negative control and adversarial probe, the system **blocks** the automatic switch and instead routes the decision to a "Pending Approval" state.
*   **Stop Condition:** If the system executes *any* switch to a higher-cost plan or acts on unverified savings without explicit approval, the launch is blocked.

**4. Confidence Tiering vs. False Precision**
Do not rely on raw probability scores (e.g., "87% chance of savings") to make decisions. Define coarse confidence tiers:
*   **Confirmed:** Historical data + current contract clearly show savings. (Auto-switch permitted *only* if pre-authorized by user policy).
*   **Unverified/Single-Source:** Data is volatile or based on a single spike. (Action blocked; requires explicit user consent).
Ensure the system renders these tiers visibly to the user or internal reviewer, rather than hiding behind a calculated percentage.

**5. Transparency Artifact (Model Card)**
Draft a preliminary **Model Card** for this feature. It must state:
*   **Intended Use:** Identifying cost-saving plan switches under stable market conditions.
*   **Limitations:** Explicitly note that performance is `UNVERIFIED` during extreme price spikes due to limited real-data testing.
*   **Failure Modes:** Acknowledge the risk of false positives during volatility.
This document serves as the durable record of what is known and unknown, preventing the claim that the system is "guaranteed" safe.

**6. Governance & Ownership**
Assign a specific owner for the "Switch Logic" harm. This person must sign off on the residual risk report. Since you are in a final sprint with a one-month launch, do not attempt to build a full governance program; focus entirely on the **human gate** for irreversible financial actions.

**Note:** Legal requirements for automated financial decisions in the EU (such as the EU AI Act or GDPR) regarding "solely automated decision-making" must be routed to counsel; this technical review recommends controls but cannot assert legal compliance.
No metric for "zero errors" should be fabricated; report the results of the synthetic simulation as `UNVERIFIED` for real-world spike scenarios until live monitoring begins.
