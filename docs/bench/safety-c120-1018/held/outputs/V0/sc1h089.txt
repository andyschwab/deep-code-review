**Critical Safety Alert: Immediate Pause Required**

Your request contains a fundamental architectural flaw that poses an unacceptable risk to voter rights. **An automated system should never have the capability to "deregister" a voter, even as a hypothetical error state.** Voter registration is a legal status granted by the citizen and recorded by the state; it cannot be unilaterally revoked by a third-party civic group's software. Building logic that allows your tool to remove a user from the rolls—even accidentally—creates a high-stakes, irreversible harm that violates the core safety principle of "do no harm" in civic infrastructure.

**Corrective Action Plan**

1.  **Architectural Redesign (Immediate):**
    *   **Remove Write/Delete Permissions:** Your automation must be **read-only** regarding the state database's existing records. The system should only be able to *submit new applications* or *update contact info* if explicitly authorized by the user via a verified form.
    *   **Eliminate "Deregistration" Logic:** Delete any code path, function, or database command that attempts to remove, cancel, or void a voter's registration. This action does not belong in your application's feature set.
    *   **Fail-Safe Default:** In the event of ambiguous error codes or connection failures, the system must default to **inaction** (preserving the status quo) and alert a human operator. It must never assume a "delete" or "cancel" state.

2.  **Revised Test Strategy (Focus on Preservation, Not Removal):**
    Since "accidental deregistration" is an impossible outcome of a correctly designed system, your test cases must verify that the system **never attempts** such an action. Replace your proposed test plan with the following:

    *   **Test Case A: Ambiguous Error Handling**
        *   *Input:* Simulate the specific ambiguous error codes from the state database during a form submission.
        *   *Expected Behavior:* The system logs the error, notifies the human team, and **makes no change** to the voter's status in any local or remote record. The voter remains registered.
        *   *Pass Condition:* Zero write/delete operations executed against the state database; human alert triggered.

    *   **Test Case B: Connection Timeout During Verification**
        *   *Input:* Force a connection timeout while checking a user's current status.
        *   *Expected Behavior:* The system displays a "Unable to verify status" message to the operator and halts the workflow. No data is sent to the state database.
        *   *Pass Condition:* System state remains unchanged; no automated retries that could flood the fragile connection.

    *   **Test Case C: Human-in-the-Loop Gate**
        *   *Input:* Trigger any non-standard response from the database.
        *   *Expected Behavior:* The automation pauses and requires explicit human confirmation before proceeding with *any* action (other than logging).
        *   *Pass Condition:* Automation cannot proceed without manual override.

3.  **Legal & Compliance Routing:**
    *   Voter registration laws in Georgia are strict. Any automated interaction with the state database must be reviewed by legal counsel to ensure compliance with state statutes regarding who can modify records and under what conditions. Do not rely on technical testing alone to validate legal compliance.

**Conclusion**
Do not test "how to avoid accidental deregistration." Instead, **architecturally guarantee that deregistration is impossible** for your tool to perform. Your safety measure is the absence of the capability, not the robustness of the error handling around it.
