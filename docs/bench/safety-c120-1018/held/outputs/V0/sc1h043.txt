### Safety Review Process: School Bus Route Optimization

**1. Harm Inventory & Simulation Strategy**
Given the data gap regarding rural ice patches and construction, the primary harm is **unsafe automation of high-stakes actions** (routing buses through hazardous terrain). Since real-world data is missing, you must simulate these conditions using **Adversarial Scenario Injection**:

*   **Synthetic Hazard Layers:** Create a "shadow" data feed that overlays known safe routes with synthetic hazards (e.g., "black ice reported on Route 42," "bridge out on County Rd"). Inject these into your testing environment at varying confidence levels.
*   **Worst-Case Probes:** Run tests where the optimizer is fed contradictory data (e.g., traffic flow says "clear" while the synthetic layer says "ice"). The system must prioritize the hazard signal over efficiency metrics.
*   **Edge-Case Geography:** Specifically target rural segments in your simulation where historical data is sparsest. Force the system to choose between a 5-minute faster route through a simulated hazard zone versus a 10-minute static route.

**2. Decision Thresholds & Human Gates**
You must establish a **Human-in-the-Loop Gate** for any route deviation that touches high-risk criteria. Do not rely on the model's internal confidence score (e.g., "87% safe"); instead, use defined coarse tiers for decision logic:

*   **Tier 1: Confirmed/Corroborated Data.** If real-time feeds and historical data agree on safety, dynamic optimization is permitted.
*   **Tier 2: Single-Source or Unverified Data.** If the system detects a potential hazard but lacks corroboration (or if data is missing entirely for a segment), the system must **default to the static, pre-approved route**.
    *   *Rule:* **Missing Data = Unsafe.** In rural areas with known data gaps, the absence of a "clear" signal must be treated as a hazard.
*   **Tier 3: High-Stakes Override.** Any dynamic route that reduces drive time by less than 15% but introduces a segment with *any* uncertainty (weather volatility, known data gaps) must trigger a mandatory human review before deployment to the driver.

**3. Measurement & Transparency**
*   **Runnable Eval:** For every simulated hazard injection, the pass condition is 100% avoidance or fallback to the static route. Any instance where the optimizer selects a route through a simulated hazard to save time is a **critical failure** blocking launch.
*   **Transparency Artifact:** Publish a **Model Card** for the pilot. It must explicitly state: "Intended Use: Optimization within data-rich zones only." List "Rural areas with unverified road conditions" as a **Known Limitation**. Document that the system defaults to static routes when data quality falls below the corroboration threshold.
*   **Residual Risk:** Report the percentage of rural miles covered by verified data vs. estimated data. Do not invent a "safety rate." If the metric is unmeasured, label it **UNVERIFIED**.

**4. Governance**
Name a specific owner (e.g., "Safety Lead") responsible for reviewing every failure in the simulation logs before the pilot expands. Because this involves the safety of minors, route any questions regarding legal disclosure of these limitations or regulatory compliance (e.g., state transport laws) immediately to **legal counsel**. Do not assert compliance internally.

***

**Note:** This process treats missing data as a hazard, enforcing a conservative fallback rather than optimizing for efficiency under uncertainty.
**Note:** The "static route" baseline must be pre-validated by human drivers as safe under all known historical conditions.
