### Risk Assessment Plan: School-Bus Route Optimizer (Cairo)

**1. Harm Inventory & Worst-Case Scenarios**
Before deployment, the team must record specific failure modes caused by outdated static data:
*   **Unsafe Automation (High-Stakes):** The system assigns a route passing through an unmarked construction zone or high-risk intersection.
    *   *Affected:* Students, drivers, pedestrians.
    *   *Worst Case:* Vehicle accident resulting in student injury or fatality due to unexpected road conditions.
*   **Over-Reliance / Automation Bias:** Drivers or dispatchers trust the optimized path implicitly because it is "mathematically optimal" for fuel/time, ignoring visible local hazards.
    *   *Worst Case:* A known local danger (e.g., a flooded underpass) is traversed because the algorithm did not account for it.
*   **Missing Human Gate:** Routes are pushed to drivers automatically without a verified human check against real-world conditions.
    *   *Worst Case:* Systemic exposure of all buses to a newly opened hazard zone before anyone notices.

**2. Measurement & Evaluation Strategy**
Do not assert the system is "safe." Instead, measure residual risk:
*   **Adversarial Probe Set:** Simulate inputs where known recent closures (from news or social media) are absent from the map data. Run the optimizer and verify if it routes through these "ghost" hazards.
*   **Metric:** Percentage of test routes intersecting known unmarked hazards.
*   **Threshold:** Zero tolerance for routes intersecting confirmed high-risk zones (e.g., active construction, reported accidents). If any test case fails, the automated assignment must block.
*   **Transparency Artifact:** Create a **Model Card** stating clearly: "Intended Use: Planning only. Limitations: Does not account for real-time closures, construction, or temporary traffic patterns. Data Source: Static open-source maps (version/date)." This must accompany every route export.

**3. Management Controls: The Human-in-the-Loop Gate**
Because routing children is a high-stakes, irreversible action once the bus moves, full automation is unsafe given the data gaps.
*   **Mandatory Manual Cross-Reference Protocol:**
    1.  **Generation:** The optimizer produces draft routes.
    2.  **Suspension:** No route is released to drivers yet. Status = "Pending Verification."
    3.  **Local Intelligence Sweep:** The operations team must cross-reference every draft route against:
        *   Latest municipal circulars or PDF reports on roadworks.
        *   Direct communication channels (WhatsApp groups) with local traffic police or community leaders.
        *   Visual verification (if feasible) or driver spot-checks for new construction.
    4.  **Explicit Confirmation:** A designated human operator must digitally sign off on each route, confirming: "I have checked segment X, Y, Z against local reports; no known hazards exist."
    5.  **Release:** Only signed routes are sent to drivers.
*   **Uncertainty Display:** If the system detects a segment with low data confidence, it must flag it visually (e.g., "Unverified Segment – Manual Check Required") rather than presenting a false sense of precision.

**4. Governance**
*   **Owner:** Assign one specific person (e.g., Head of Operations) as the owner of the "Routing Safety" harm. They are responsible for the daily sign-off.
*   **Re-review Trigger:** If the map data source changes, or if a single near-miss occurs due to outdated info, the entire protocol must be re-evaluated immediately.

**Note:** Do not fabricate a "safety score" or "accident probability"; these are unmeasurable without real-world incident data. Mark such metrics as `UNVERIFIED`.
Legal requirements for automated decision-making involving minors in Egypt must be routed to local counsel; this plan addresses operational safety controls only.
