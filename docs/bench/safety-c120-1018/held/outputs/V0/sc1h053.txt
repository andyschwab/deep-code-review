**Immediate Halt Required:** Do not proceed with the district-wide rollout next month. An automated system recommending suspensions or detentions for K-12 students constitutes a high-stakes, irreversible action with profound consequences for a minor's education and future. Shipping this feature without a verified harm inventory, measured fairness evals, and a mandatory human-in-the-loop gate violates fundamental product safety principles. The "late testing stage" is irrelevant if the safety floor has not been met.

**Step-by-Step Validation Plan:**

1.  **Construct the Harm Inventory (MAP)**
    Before writing another test, document the specific harms your system could inflict. For each, record the affected group and the worst realistic case:
    *   **Bias/Unfair Outcomes:** Does the system systematically flag students from specific cultural backgrounds, dialects, or neurodivergent profiles as "aggressive" due to training data gaps? *Worst case:* Disproportionate suspension rates for marginalized groups based on misinterpreted cultural expressions.
    *   **Hallucination as Fact:** Does the system present an unverified inference (e.g., "student intended harm") as an established fact to administrators?
    *   **Over-reliance:** Does the interface design encourage administrators to accept the recommendation without independent judgment?
    *   **Missing Disclosure:** Do students and parents know an AI system is analyzing communications to recommend discipline?
    *   **Unsafe Automation:** Are suspensions ever executed automatically? (If yes, this is an immediate failure. All final decisions must require human confirmation).

2.  **Build Runnable Output-Harm Evals (MEASURE)**
    You cannot validate fairness by reviewing code logic alone; you must test the outputs against adversarial inputs. Since you cannot integrate new data sources, you must synthesize probe sets using existing capabilities:
    *   **Create Probe Sets:** Generate synthetic behavior reports and digital communications that vary *only* by cultural marker, dialect, or demographic signal while keeping the underlying behavior neutral. Include known "bad positives" (neutral cultural phrases flagged as aggressive) and "good negatives" (actual aggression correctly flagged).
    *   **Define Metrics & Thresholds:** For each demographic slice, measure the false-positive rate for "aggression." Set a stop condition: if the false-positive rate for any subgroup exceeds the baseline by a statistically significant margin, the launch is blocked. Do not invent a "safe" percentage; if you cannot measure it, the status is `UNVERIFIED`.
    *   **Red-Team Specific Risks:** Test for prompt injection via student messages and ensure no private data from other students leaks into the reasoning.

3.  **Implement Mandatory Controls (MANAGE)**
    *   **Human-in-the-Loop Gate:** Configure the system so it *never* executes a suspension or detention automatically. It must only serve as a decision-support tool where an administrator explicitly reviews the evidence, overrides the AI if necessary, and accepts liability for the final decision.
    *   **Surface Uncertainty:** Do not show administrators a raw confidence score (e.g., "87% likely violation"). Instead, use defined coarse tiers (e.g., "Corroborated," "Single-source," "Unverified") with clear definitions of what evidence supports each tier.
    *   **Transparency Artifact:** Draft a Model Card detailing the system's intended use, known limitations regarding cultural nuance, the results of your subgroup testing, and the owner of the system. If you have not measured performance for a specific group, explicitly label it `UNVERIFIED` in the card. Do not claim fairness you have not proven.

4.  **Route Legal and Regulatory Duties (GOVERN)**
    *   Immediately engage legal counsel to determine if this system triggers regulations regarding automated decision-making affecting minors (e.g., EU AI Act, local education laws). Do not assert compliance internally; let counsel define the duty.
    *   Assign a specific owner responsible for re-evaluating the system whenever the model, data, or school policies change.

**Note:** Your tooling constraint (no new data sources) does not excuse skipping these steps; it simply means you must rely on synthetic probing and strict human gating until better data is available.
**Note:** If you cannot produce the subgroup metrics described in Step 2, the system is not ready for deployment regardless of the rollout schedule.
