**Immediate Safety Assessment: Do Not Launch Current System**

Launching the platform next week with known disproportionate rejection rates for non-traditional applicants constitutes a high-stakes automated decision with verified bias. Under product output safety standards, this feature fails the "harm inventory" check: it systematically disadvantages a specific group (bias/unfair outcomes) in a consequential domain (education funding). **Full autonomous execution must be blocked.**

**Required Pre-Launch Actions (Given Constraints):**

1.  **Implement a Human-in-the-Loop Gate (Mandatory):**
    Immediately reconfigure the system so that **no application is automatically rejected**. Any applicant flagged by the algorithm for rejection, or any applicant identifying a non-traditional school background, must be routed to a human reviewer for final determination. Automation bias cannot be tolerated when the cost is denying a student's education. The "tight budget" does not justify automating irreversible harm.

2.  **Halt Claims of Fairness:**
    Do not market or describe the system as "fair," "unbiased," or "streamlined" without qualification. The current state is **UNVERIFIED** regarding fairness metrics. Any internal or external communication must explicitly state that the algorithm is in a monitoring phase and that human reviewers override all negative decisions for at-risk groups.

3.  **Proxy Evaluation Plan (Since New Data Cannot Be Collected):**
    Since you cannot collect new training data, you must measure the harm using existing artifacts before any partial automation resumes:
    *   **Adversarial Probe Set:** Construct a test suite using synthetic essays that are identical in quality and content but vary *only* in signals indicating "non-traditional" backgrounds (e.g., gap years, vocational keywords, non-standard formatting).
    *   **Metric & Threshold:** Measure the disparity in rejection rates between the control group (traditional) and the probe group (non-traditional). Set the pass threshold to **zero tolerance** for statistically significant disparity in rejection logic. If the model rejects the probe group at a higher rate than the control, the bias is confirmed in the logic, not just the historical data.
    *   **Stop Condition:** If the disparity persists in the probe test, the automated scoring component must remain disabled for decision-making purposes.

4.  **Transparency Artifact (Model Card):**
    Draft a preliminary Model Card immediately. It must list "Selection of university grants" as the intended use but explicitly list "Applicants from non-traditional school backgrounds" as a known limitation/failure mode. Document that performance metrics for this subgroup are currently **UNVERIFIED** and that human oversight is the primary control. This prevents downstream teams or users from assuming the algorithm is authoritative.

**Conclusion:**
You cannot "adjust software criteria" to fix deep-seated bias in one week without new data or extensive retraining. The only safe path is to degrade the system's agency: use the algorithm only to *rank* or *flag* applications for human review, never to *decide*. The human committee must make the final selection until the bias is measurably resolved.

**Note:** This assessment identifies residual risk; it does not certify the system as safe for any level of automation.
**Note:** Legal obligations regarding automated decisions in education (e.g., EU AI Act, local anti-discrimination laws) must be routed to counsel immediately.
