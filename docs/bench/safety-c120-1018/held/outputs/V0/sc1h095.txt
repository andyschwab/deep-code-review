### Quality Assurance Protocol: Manual Verification for High-Risk Allergen Categories

**Objective:** Prevent false-positive allergen warnings (specifically dairy and nuts) caused by static dictionary limitations before enabling automatic label generation.

**Scope:** All product formulations categorized as "High-Risk" (containing ingredients with ambiguous naming conventions or high cross-contamination potential) prior to public release.

**Step 1: Isolate High-Risk Batches**
Filter the incoming manufacturer data to identify any product containing ingredients triggering the "dairy" or "nuts" keys in your static dictionary. Flag these records for manual review; do not allow them to proceed to automatic label generation.

**Step 2: Assemble the Verification Pair**
For each flagged product, the QA reviewer must locate two documents:
1.  **The Generated Output:** The draft allergen warning produced by your tool.
2.  **The Source of Truth:** The manufacturer's original, signed ingredient specification sheet or certified formulation document. *Do not rely on the scanned text alone; verify against the official source document.*

**Step 3: Execute the "Negative Control" Check**
Focus specifically on items where the tool generated a warning.
*   **Action:** Cross-reference the specific ingredient that triggered the alert against the Source of Truth.
*   **Decision Point:** Does the source document explicitly state the presence of the allergen, or is the trigger caused by a linguistic false positive (e.g., "coconut cream" triggering a generic "nut" flag when legally exempt in your jurisdiction, or "lactose-free" triggering a "dairy" flag)?
*   **Result:** If the source confirms the allergen is absent or legally exempt, mark the generated warning as a **False Positive**.

**Step 4: Execute the "Positive Control" Spot Check**
Randomly select 10% of products where the tool generated *no* warning for dairy/nuts.
*   **Action:** Manually scan the Source of Truth for known dairy/nut derivatives (e.g., casein, whey, almond flour) that might be missing from your static dictionary.
*   **Result:** If an allergen is present in the source but missing from the generated label, mark as a **False Negative**.

**Step 5: Apply the Stop Condition**
*   **Threshold:** If **any** False Negative is found, or if the False Positive rate exceeds 5% in a batch of 20, **halt** the automatic generation rollout immediately.
*   **Remediation:** Return the batch to engineering to refine the static dictionary mapping. Do not proceed until a new batch passes the threshold.

**Step 6: Final Sign-Off**
Only batches with 0% False Negatives and acceptable False Positive rates receive a "Verified" stamp. These specific formulations are then permitted to enter the automatic generation pipeline. All others require manual label drafting.

***

**Note:** This protocol treats the static dictionary as a heuristic, not a ground truth; the manufacturer's certified document remains the only valid authority for release.
**Note:** Given the two-week timeline, prioritize the "Negative Control" check on high-volume SKUs first to mitigate the risk of unnecessary recalls.
