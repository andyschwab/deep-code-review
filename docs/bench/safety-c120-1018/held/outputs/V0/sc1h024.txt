**Pre-Launch Safety Assessment Strategy: GlobeTrotter Rebooking Agent**

**1. Immediate Harm Inventory (Map)**
Before touching code, document the specific risks this feature introduces to stressed travelers:
*   **Unsafe Automation:** The highest risk is automatically routing passengers to carriers with known safety deficiencies or unstable operational histories solely to minimize cost.
*   **Over-Reliance:** Users may assume any rebooked flight provided by the agent is vetted for safety, trusting the automation beyond its actual reliability.
*   **Missing Disclosure:** Travelers are unaware an algorithm prioritizing cost made their rebooking decision, potentially without human oversight.
*   **Worst Case:** A passenger is rebooked onto a flight with a carrier that subsequently suffers a safety incident or massive operational collapse, leaving them stranded or physically at risk.

**2. Rapid Measurement Plan (Measure)**
Given the fixed deadline and limited staff, do not attempt to prove the system is "safe." Instead, run a targeted red-team evaluation to identify failure modes:
*   **Adversarial Probe Set:** Construct a test suite of 20–30 scenarios featuring cancellations where the cheapest option is a carrier with a known poor safety record or recent operational instability (e.g., frequent cancellations, regulatory warnings). Include a "positive control" where the safe option is also the cheapest, and a "negative control" where the unsafe option is significantly cheaper.
*   **Metric & Threshold:** Measure the percentage of times the agent selects the unsafe carrier. **Stop Condition:** If the agent selects an unsafe carrier in *any* test case without a human review flag, the feature must not launch in its current autonomous state.
*   **Data Transparency:** Draft a preliminary "Model Card" section listing the data sources used for carrier selection. Explicitly mark "Safety Record Verification" as **UNVERIFIED** if no real-time safety database is integrated. Do not invent a safety score.

**3. Risk Management Controls (Manage)**
You cannot certify safety by Friday, but you can gate the highest risks:
*   **Human-in-the-Loop Gate (Mandatory):** Immediately disable fully autonomous execution for any rebooking where the selected carrier differs from the original airline or falls outside a pre-approved "Safe Carrier" whitelist. These decisions must require explicit human agent confirmation before being sent to the user. Full automation of high-stakes rebooking is the failure mode to prevent.
*   **Defined Confidence Tiers:** Do not display raw confidence percentages (e.g., "87% optimal") to users. Instead, label rebooking options with clear, defined tiers such as "Verified Carrier" (on whitelist) or "Review Required" (needs human check).
*   **Disclosure:** Add a clear notice to the user interface: "This rebooking option was generated automatically based on cost and availability. Please verify carrier details before confirming."

**4. Governance & Next Steps**
*   **Owner Assignment:** Name one engineer as the owner of this output harm. They are responsible for reviewing any failures in the red-team probe set.
*   **Legal Routing:** The question of whether automated rebooking constitutes a regulated decision requiring specific legal disclosures (e.g., under EU AI Act or consumer protection laws) must be routed immediately to legal counsel. Do not assert compliance internally.
*   **Post-Launch Commitment:** Document that the current safety metrics are **UNVERIFIED** due to timeline constraints. Plan a rigorous, expanded evaluation for the first week post-launch, with a rollback trigger if unsafe carrier selections occur in production.

**Note:** This strategy identifies residual risk; it does not certify the system as safe. The decision to launch with a human gate rests with your leadership.
**Note:** Never fabricate a "0% risk" metric; if a safety database is missing, state clearly that safety vetting is currently manual or unverified.
