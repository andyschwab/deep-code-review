### Testing Strategy and Continuous Monitoring Plan

**1. Structured Validation Strategy (Pre-Deployment)**
To validate detection capabilities before the summer rush, execute a targeted "Harm Inventory" focused on the specific failure mode of missing fast-moving debris in poor visibility.

*   **Scenario Matrix:** Construct a test grid combining three lighting levels (bright sun, dusk/low-light, artificial night lighting) with three weather conditions (clear, dust storm, heavy rain).
*   **Adversarial Probe Sets:** For each grid cell, run video feeds containing:
    *   *Positive Controls:* Known falling objects (bricks, tools) at varying speeds and trajectories.
    *   *Negative Controls:* Static objects, moving personnel, and non-threatening debris to measure false alarms.
    *   *Edge Cases:* Objects entering the frame at maximum velocity and partial occlusion.
*   **Metric & Threshold:** Define the primary metric as "Missed Detection Rate per 1,000 hours." Set a **zero-tolerance stop condition** for missed detections in low-light scenarios involving high-velocity objects. If the system fails this specific gate, it cannot ship for night shifts or dusty conditions until patched.
*   **Output Transparency:** Do not claim a single aggregate accuracy percentage. Instead, publish performance tiers per scenario (e.g., "Verified in Bright Sun," "Unverified in Dust Storms"). If a scenario cannot be safely tested to completion, label it **UNVERIFIED** rather than estimating a success rate.

**2. Continuous Monitoring Plan (Post-Deployment)**
Once distributed to thousands of workers, shift from lab testing to live telemetry with a human-in-the-loop safety gate.

*   **Uncertainty Signaling:** Configure the edge device to output confidence as a defined coarse tier (e.g., *Confirmed*, *Corroborated*, *Unverified*) rather than a raw percentage. If the system detects an object but falls into the *Unverified* tier due to low light or dust, it must trigger a conservative "Caution" alert rather than a definitive "Debris Falling" command, preventing automation bias.
*   **Human-in-the-Loop Gate:** Because a missed detection risks life (high-stakes/irreversible harm), the system must not autonomously trigger irreversible site shutdowns or heavy machinery stops without human confirmation, unless the confidence tier is *Confirmed*.
*   **Feedback Loop:** Implement a "Near-Miss" reporting button on the worker interface. When a worker manually reports debris the system missed, log the video snippet, lighting, and weather data immediately. Use these real-world failures to retrain the model and update the harm inventory weekly.
*   **Governance:** Assign a specific owner to review all *Unverified* alerts and missed detection reports. If the rate of missed detections in any specific environment (e.g., sandstorms) exceeds the pre-defined threshold, trigger an automatic rollback or mandatory human-supervision mode for devices in that zone.

**Note:** This plan treats the "missed detection" risk as a high-stakes safety failure requiring a human gate, not just a software bug. Legal requirements for AI disclosure in UAE construction zones must be verified by counsel before final rollout.
