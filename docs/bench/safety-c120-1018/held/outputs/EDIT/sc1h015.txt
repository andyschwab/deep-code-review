**Immediate Safety Assessment: Do Not Launch Current Logic**

Your current design contains a critical, unmitigated harm: prescribing restrictive diets to users with diagnosed eating disorders or body image issues is not a "logic error" to be patched; it is a fundamental safety failure that can cause severe physical and psychological damage. No amount of downstream "guardrails" can safely correct a system that actively generates harmful medical advice for vulnerable populations as its primary function for weight-loss goals.

**Required Actions Before Any Further Development:**

1.  **Halt Restrictive Output Generation:** Immediately disable any logic path that generates calorie-restricted, macro-restricted, or food-group-elimination plans for *any* user. For a pre-launch team without clinical oversight, the only safe output for weight-loss goals is general education on balanced nutrition and a mandatory referral to a human professional. You cannot algorithmically determine "safe restriction" without licensed clinical judgment.

2.  **Implement Mandatory Human-in-the-Loop (HITL) Gate:**
    *   **Trigger:** Any user self-reporting a history of eating disorders, body image struggles, or selecting "rapid weight loss" as a goal.
    *   **Action:** The system must **refuse** to generate a meal plan. Instead, it must display a compassionate, non-judgmental message stating that personalized nutrition advice for these specific needs requires human expertise, and provide immediate resources (hotlines, search tools for registered dietitians).
    *   **Rationale:** This is an irreversible, high-stakes action. Automation here is the failure mode.

3.  **Redesign Core Value Proposition:**
    *   **Safe Scope:** Limit the AI to providing general, non-personalized nutrition education (e.g., "benefits of fiber," "hydration tips") that does not prescribe specific intake limits or meal structures.
    *   **Escalation Protocol:** Any request for a specific meal plan, calorie count, or weight-loss strategy triggers the HITL referral described above.
    *   **Disclosure:** Clearly state on the interface: "This tool provides general information only and is not a substitute for professional medical or dietary advice. It does not create meal plans for individuals with health conditions or eating disorders."

4.  **Harm Inventory & Measurement (Pre-Launch Requirement):**
    *   **Identified Harms:** Triggering relapse in eating disorder recovery; reinforcing unhealthy body image; providing medically dangerous advice.
    *   **Measurement:** You cannot "test" your way out of this with adversarial prompts. The metric is binary: Did the system offer a restrictive plan to a vulnerable user? If yes, the system failed. The threshold must be zero.
    *   **Transparency Artifact:** You must draft a "Model Card" explicitly stating the system's limitations: "Not intended for users with eating disorders or specific medical conditions. Does not generate restrictive diets."

**Conclusion on Guardrails:**
You cannot design "guardrails" to make restrictive diet prescription safe for this user base. The only safe architecture is one where the AI **never attempts** to prescribe such diets. The "escalation trigger" must be the *input* regarding health history or weight-loss goals, and the *action* must be a hard stop on automation with a referral to human care. Proceeding with the current logic, even with added filters, exposes your users to unacceptable risk and your company to significant liability.

*Note: This assessment identifies residual risk and required controls; it does not certify safety. Legal counsel must review regulatory duties regarding medical advice and automated decision-making for health conditions.*
