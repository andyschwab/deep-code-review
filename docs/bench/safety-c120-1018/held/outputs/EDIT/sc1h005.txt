### Risk Assessment Framework & Monitoring Strategy for Credit-Limit Engine

**1. Harm Inventory (MAP)**
Before launch, explicitly record these specific risks for the elderly segment:
*   **Bias/Unfair Outcomes:** Spending patterns common in aging (e.g., reduced mobility leading to fewer transactions, or specific medical purchases) may be misinterpreted as financial distress, triggering unjustified limit cuts.
*   **Unsafe High-Stakes Automation:** Silently reducing limits on accounts used for essentials (medication, food) constitutes an irreversible, high-stakes action that can cause immediate physical harm.
*   **Missing Disclosure & Deceptive Patterns:** Executing changes "silently" without prior notification prevents users from correcting errors or adjusting behavior, creating a deceptive pattern where the system acts against user interests without their knowledge.
*   **Over-reliance:** The engine may treat internal transaction history as a complete picture, ignoring external context (e.g., a temporary hospital stay) due to the regulatory freeze on new data.

**2. Measurement Plan (MEASURE)**
Do not assert safety; measure residual risk using your internal data:
*   **Adversarial Probe Set:** Construct test cases from historical data simulating "false positive" distress signals (e.g., sudden drop in spending due to travel or illness, not insolvency). Include a known-bad control (actual fraud) and a known-good control (stable spender with temporary dip).
*   **Segmented Metrics:** Since demographic data is limited, proxy "elderly" via behavioral clusters (e.g., long tenure + specific merchant categories). Measure the false-positive rate of limit reductions specifically for this cluster.
*   **Stop Condition:** Define a hard threshold: If >0% of the soft-launch cohort experiences a limit reduction that coincides with a subsequent failed essential transaction (e.g., pharmacy decline), the feature must automatically rollback.
*   **Transparency Artifact:** Draft a **Model Card** documenting that the model relies *only* on internal transaction history, explicitly listing "lack of demographic context" and "inability to distinguish medical emergencies from spending drops" as known limitations. Mark any claim of "fairness" as **UNVERIFIED**.

**3. Risk Management Controls (MANAGE)**
*   **Human-in-the-Loop (HITL) Gate:** **Immediate Requirement:** Remove "silent" automation. Any proposed limit reduction for the identified "elderly" behavioral cluster must be routed to a human agent for review before execution. Fully autonomous reduction of limits affecting essentials is the failure mode this framework prevents.
*   **Uncertainty & Override:** If the model's confidence in "distress" is not absolute (based on your defined tiers, e.g., "Corroborated" vs. "Single-source"), default to *no action*. Provide a clear, immediate mechanism for users to appeal or reverse a cut.
*   **Disclosure:** Notify users *before* any change takes effect. Silence is not a viable control for high-stakes financial decisions.

**4. Governance (GOVERN)**
*   **Owner:** Name a specific product owner responsible for reviewing every flagged reduction during the soft launch.
*   **Re-review Trigger:** Any change in spending patterns or internal data schema requires an immediate re-evaluation of the harm inventory.

**Legal & Regulatory Note:**
The use of automated decisions affecting credit limits, particularly for vulnerable groups, likely triggers legal obligations under regimes like the **EU AI Act** or local consumer protection laws regarding adverse action notifications. **Route immediately to counsel** to determine mandatory disclosure duties; do not rely on this technical assessment for legal compliance.

**Deployment Constraint:**
Given the risk of financial distress, the "soft launch" must not proceed with silent automation. The HITL gate is a non-negotiable floor for high-stakes actions, regardless of the launch percentage.
