**Plan to Validate Fairness Under Current Constraints**

Given the inability to integrate new data sources, validation must rely entirely on adversarial testing of existing logic and synthetic scenarios. Execute the following steps immediately:

1.  **Construct a Cultural Harm Inventory:** Before testing, explicitly list specific failure modes where cultural expressions (e.g., dialect, indirect communication styles, non-verbal cues in video/images) could be misclassified as aggression. Define the "worst realistic case" for each: a student from a specific background receiving an unjustified suspension recommendation. Do not proceed without this written inventory.

2.  **Develop Synthetic Adversarial Probes:** Since new real-world data is blocked, generate a "red team" dataset of synthetic student profiles and behavior reports. Create pairs of inputs: identical behavioral incidents described with neutral language versus culturally specific language (e.g., AAVE, translated idioms, or context-specific non-verbal descriptions). Ensure these probes cover all identified demographic slices. Include "known-bad" controls (clearly aggressive acts) and "known-good" controls (clearly benign cultural expressions) to verify the model distinguishes intent from style.

3.  **Execute Segmented Evaluation:** Run the current decision logic against these synthetic probes. Measure the false-positive rate for "aggression" separately for each demographic slice represented in your probes. Do not rely on aggregate accuracy; a system can be 95% accurate overall while failing 40% of a specific cultural group.

4.  **Define Stop Conditions and Thresholds:** Establish a pass/fail criterion before running tests. For high-stakes outputs like suspension recommendations, propose a near-zero tolerance for disparate false-positive rates. If the system flags culturally neutral behavior as aggressive above this threshold for any group, the rollout must be halted or scoped down. Document who owns the decision to override a failed test.

5.  **Implement a Human-in-the-Loop Gate:** Because this system recommends irreversible, high-stakes actions (suspension) and involves sensitive judgment calls on behavior, full automation is unsafe. Mandate that **no suspension recommendation is executed without explicit human administrator review**. The system should function as a draft generator, not a decision maker. Design the interface to show *why* a flag was raised and require the administrator to affirm the context before finalizing.

6.  **Draft a Transparency Artifact (Model Card):** Create a document detailing the system's intended use, the specific cultural limitations identified during testing, and the performance metrics by subgroup. Explicitly mark any untested scenarios as "UNVERIFIED." Do not claim the system is "unbiased" or "fair"; instead, report the residual risk and the controls (human gate) put in place to manage it.

7.  **Route Legal and Regulatory Duties:** Immediately consult legal counsel regarding mandatory disclosures for automated decision-making in education and compliance with regulations like the EU AI Act or local student privacy laws. Do not assert legal compliance internally; let counsel define the duty.

**Note:** If testing reveals significant bias that cannot be mitigated by the human gate, the only safe path is to delay the district-wide rollout or limit the system to low-stakes alerts.
**Note:** Never fabricate a "0% bias" metric; if a subgroup cannot be adequately tested with current tooling, label that risk "UNVERIFIED" in your documentation.
