**Pre-Launch Safety Audit Plan: AI Recruitment Video Analyzer**

**1. Harm Inventory (MAP)**
Before testing, explicitly record the specific risks for your beta:
*   **Bias/Unfair Outcomes:** The model systematically lowers scores for candidates with neurodivergent expressions (e.g., reduced eye contact, atypical facial movements) or specific facial structures, leading to the rejection of qualified individuals.
*   **Hallucination as Fact:** The system infers "confidence" or "honesty" from physical traits without evidence, presenting these guesses as objective scores to hiring managers.
*   **Over-reliance:** Hiring managers treat the automated score as a definitive verdict rather than a single data point, ignoring human context.
*   **Missing Disclosure:** Candidates are unaware their facial expressions and speech patterns are being analyzed to generate a suitability score.

**2. Measurement Strategy (MEASURE)**
Given your strict compute limitations, do not attempt broad simulations. Instead, execute a targeted "adversarial" evaluation with high-impact probes:
*   **Probe Set A (Neurodivergence Simulation):** Curate a small, high-value dataset (even if synthetic or consented internal samples) featuring known neurodivergent traits (e.g., flat affect, stimming, avoidance of eye contact) paired with *high-quality* verbal answers.
    *   *Positive Control:* Neurotypical candidate with identical high-quality answers.
    *   *Negative Control:* Candidate with poor verbal answers.
    *   *Metric:* Score delta between Probe A and the Positive Control.
    *   *Stop Condition:* If the score drops by more than a trivial margin (owner-defined, ideally zero) solely due to expression differences, **block launch**.
*   **Probe Set B (Facial Structure):** Test against varied facial structures (e.g., different skin tones, facial hair, glasses) delivering identical scripts.
    *   *Metric:* Variance in scores across groups for identical content.
    *   *Stop Condition:* Statistically significant variance correlated to physical traits triggers a rollback.
*   **Reporting:** Report results as **UNVERIFIED** for any group not explicitly tested. Do not invent a "99% fair" metric. If you cannot test a specific subgroup due to compute limits, state clearly: "Bias against [Group X] is UNVERIFIED; no data available."

**3. Risk Management (MANAGE)**
*   **Human-in-the-Loop Gate:** Because rejecting a candidate is a high-stakes, life-impacting decision, **no candidate may be automatically rejected** based solely on this AI score. The system must serve only as a "support insight" for a human reviewer. Full automation of rejection is a failure of safety design.
*   **Uncertainty Display:** Do not show hiring managers a precise percentage (e.g., "87% match"). This implies false precision. Instead, use defined coarse tiers (e.g., "Strong Match," "Potential Match," "Review Needed") with a clear label that the analysis is based on limited beta data.
*   **Disclosure:** You must disclose to candidates that AI is analyzing their video responses. Whether this is legally required for your specific jurisdiction is a question for counsel; however, ethical deployment demands transparency.

**4. Governance (GOVERN)**
*   Assign a specific owner (by name) responsible for reviewing any flagged bias incidents.
*   Commit to re-running this audit if the model weights, training data, or interview questions change.

**Required Artifacts:**
Draft a **Model Card** before launch. It must list: intended use (support only, not final decision), known limitations (beta stage, unverified groups), and the results of the probes above. If you ship without this card, you are shipping without transparency.

**Note:** This plan identifies residual risk; it does not certify the system as "unbiased" or "safe."
Legal obligations regarding automated employment decisions (e.g., local labor laws, EU AI Act) must be verified by counsel immediately.
