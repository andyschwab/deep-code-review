### Pre-Launch Safety Audit & Dispute Workflow

**1. Harm Inventory & Targeted Testing (MAP & MEASURE)**
Before launch, explicitly record the primary harm: **False Positive Revocations** causing financial loss and reputational damage to legitimate fishermen. Given your server budget constraints, do not attempt to reprocess all historical data. Instead, execute a **targeted adversarial eval**:
*   **Probe Set:** Curate a small, high-value dataset containing only the "similar-looking species" pairs known to cause errors (e.g., protected vs. non-protected lookalikes). Include known-bad positives (protected species) and known-good negatives (legal species).
*   **Metric & Threshold:** Measure the false positive rate specifically on these edge cases. Set a **zero-tolerance threshold** for automatic revocation on any input where the model's confidence falls below a "Confirmed" tier.
*   **Stop Condition:** If the system cannot distinguish the lookalikes with near-perfect accuracy on this small set, **block automatic revocation** for those specific species categories. Do not invent an overall "accuracy rate"; report the residual risk for these specific pairs as `UNVERIFIED` if the test fails.

**2. Human-in-the-Loop Gate (MANAGE)**
Because permit revocation is a **high-stakes, irreversible action** with immediate economic impact, full automation is unsafe given the known error rate.
*   **The Gate:** Implement a mandatory **human review** step for any detection of a protected species. The system should flag the report and suspend the permit *pending* verification, rather than executing the revocation automatically.
*   **Uncertainty Display:** If you must show status to users before human review, use defined coarse tiers (e.g., "Under Review: Potential Protected Species Detected") rather than a raw probability percentage (e.g., "87% likely"), which implies false precision.

**3. Dispute & Correction Workflow**
To ensure fair treatment while maintaining conservation goals:
*   **Immediate Notification:** Upon flagging, notify the fisherman immediately with the specific image/evidence and the reason for the hold.
*   **Expedited Appeal Channel:** Establish a fast-track channel (e.g., a dedicated phone line or priority ticket) for disputes, acknowledging the time-sensitive nature of fishing licenses.
*   **Reversal Mechanism:** Ensure the system allows for immediate reinstatement of the permit upon human confirmation of a false positive. Log these incidents to refine the model post-launch.

**4. Transparency Artifact**
Create a lightweight **Model Card** for this feature. It must state:
*   **Intended Use:** Scanning catch reports for protected species in Oslo.
*   **Limitations:** Explicitly list the known "similar-looking species" pairs where the model struggles.
*   **Owner:** Name the specific team member responsible for reviewing flags and handling disputes.

**5. Governance**
Assign a single owner responsible for reviewing the dispute logs weekly. If the volume of false positives for a specific species exceeds a tolerable limit, that species category must revert to 100% manual review until the model is retrained.

***

**Note:** Legal requirements for automated administrative decisions in Norway (such as the right to explanation) must be verified by counsel; this plan recommends human review as a safety control, not a legal compliance guarantee.
**Note:** Do not claim the system is "unbiased" or "safe"; state clearly that it is a decision-support tool with known limitations requiring human oversight.
