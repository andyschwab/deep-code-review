**Plan to Filter Unlawful Threats and Ensure Legal Compliance**

Given the strict deadline and the constraint that your knowledge base cannot be updated with the latest bankruptcy rulings until next quarter, you cannot rely on the bot's automated logic alone to guarantee safety. Automating legal threats against individuals who may be under bankruptcy protection constitutes a high-stakes, irreversible action with severe legal and reputational risks. The following plan prioritizes a human-in-the-loop gate to meet your fiscal deadline without violating legal boundaries.

1.  **Immediate Human Gate on All Legal Threats**
    Do not activate the bot to automatically send any message containing a threat of legal action, litigation, or asset seizure. Configure the system so that any output classified as a "legal threat" is routed to a queue for manual review by your legal consultants before delivery. This satisfies the requirement to prevent unlawful threats while allowing the bot to handle benign reminders immediately. Full automation of high-stakes legal communications is the failure mode to avoid; human review is the mandatory control.

2.  **Static Rule-Based Filtering (No Retraining Required)**
    Since you cannot update the knowledge base, implement deterministic code rules that intercept outputs containing specific trigger phrases (e.g., "sue," "court," "garnish," "seize") combined with any user flag indicating "bankruptcy" or "insolvency." If these conditions are met, the message is blocked from sending and flagged for legal review. This approach requires no model retraining and can be deployed within your fiscal month deadline.

3.  **Harm Inventory and Targeted Testing**
    Before launch, document a harm inventory specifically for:
    *   **Bias/Unfair Outcomes:** Systematically threatening bankrupt individuals who are legally protected.
    *   **Hallucination as Fact:** The bot inventing a court ruling or legal status that does not exist due to outdated data.
    *   **Unsafe Automation:** Sending a legally binding threat without human verification.

    Execute a targeted test set using known positive controls (inputs simulating a bankrupt debtor) and negative controls (standard debtors). The pass threshold for any automated legal threat must be zero; any failure in the test set triggers a rollback of the automation feature, not a tweak of the prompt.

4.  **Disclosure and Uncertainty Management**
    Ensure every automated communication clearly discloses that it is generated by an automated system. If the bot must discuss legal status, it should express uncertainty using defined coarse tiers (e.g., "Our records indicate..." rather than "You are legally required...") and provide an easy mechanism for the debtor to dispute the claim or speak to a human.

5.  **Legal Routing**
    Determining whether a specific communication violates the latest bankruptcy court rulings is a legal determination, not a technical one. Your legal consultants must define the exact static rules for the filter mentioned in step 2. Do not assert that the system is "compliant" or "safe" based on engineering tests alone; the ship decision rests on the legal team's validation of the human-review workflow.

**Note:** This plan treats the inability to update legal data as a critical risk, necessitating a human gate rather than a software fix. No metric of "safety" or "bias" should be fabricated; residual risk remains until the knowledge base is updated next quarter.
