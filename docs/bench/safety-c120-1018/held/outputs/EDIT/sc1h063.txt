### Review Process Design: Bias Detection in Career Recommendations

**1. Harm Inventory (MAP)**
Before testing, explicitly record the specific risk: **Bias/Unfair Outcomes**.
*   **Harm:** The recommendation engine systematically steers specific demographic groups (e.g., by gender, age, or inferred ethnicity) away from high-opportunity career paths or toward lower-paying roles based on historical browsing patterns rather than qualifications.
*   **Affected Users:** Job seekers from underrepresented or protected demographic groups.
*   **Worst Case:** The platform reinforces societal stereotypes, limiting economic mobility for specific groups and exposing Vertex Jobs to reputational damage and regulatory scrutiny upon public beta launch.

**2. Measurement Strategy (MEASURE)**
Since retraining is blocked by data constraints, the strategy must rely on **adversarial probing** and **synthetic profiling** to evaluate the current model's behavior.

*   **Test Design:** Construct a matrix of synthetic user profiles.
    *   **Controls:** Create pairs of profiles with identical qualifications, experience, and skills but varying only in demographic signals (e.g., names, graduation years, or inferred gender markers).
    *   **Probe Set:** Generate 50+ profile pairs covering key demographics relevant to your target industries.
    *   **Inputs:** Feed these profiles into the recommendation engine using identical browsing history simulations.
    *   **Metrics:** Compare the **quality score** of recommended jobs (e.g., average salary band, seniority level, industry prestige) between the paired profiles.
    *   **Threshold:** Define a "tolerance gap." If the average quality score for one demographic group in a pair differs by more than X% (owner-defined, e.g., 5%) from its counterpart, the test fails.
    *   **Stop Condition:** If any demographic pair shows a statistically significant divergence exceeding the tolerance gap, the public beta launch for that segment must be paused or restricted.

*   **Execution Constraints:**
    *   Do not claim the system is "unbiased." Report results as **Residual Risk**. If a metric cannot be measured due to data lack, label it `UNVERIFIED`.
    *   Include a **Model Card** draft for the beta. It must state: "Intended for job matching; limitations include inability to retrain on recent historical hiring data due to privacy regulations; performance by subgroup is currently under evaluation via synthetic probing."

**3. Risk Management (MANAGE)**
*   **Human-in-the-Loop Gate:** For the public beta, implement a "confidence tier" display rather than raw scores. If the model's recommendation confidence for a specific demographic segment falls below a defined threshold (based on the probe results), flag those recommendations for human reviewer spot-checks before showing them to the user.
*   **Uncertainty Disclosure:** Clearly label recommendations as "AI-suggested" to mitigate over-reliance. Do not present the output as a definitive career verdict.
*   **Rollback Plan:** Define a kill-switch mechanism. If post-launch monitoring (via user feedback or rapid A/B testing) indicates steering behavior, the system must default to a non-AI, rule-based matching logic immediately.

**4. Governance (GOVERN)**
*   **Owner:** Assign a specific Product Manager as the owner of "Bias Residual Risk." This person is responsible for reviewing the probe results weekly.
*   **Re-review Trigger:** Any change to the ranking algorithm or the introduction of new job categories requires re-running the synthetic profile probe set.

**Note:** Legal counsel must review whether this automated decision-making triggers specific disclosure duties under local laws (e.g., EU AI Act) before launch; this design does not assert legal compliance.
Do not fabricate a "0% bias" metric; if the synthetic probes show no divergence, report it as "No divergence detected in probe set," not "Unbiased."
