### Safety Validation Plan: SkyDrop Mumbai Field Trials

**1. Harm Inventory & Worst-Case Definition**
Before further flights, formally record the specific harms:
*   **Physical Injury/Property Damage:** Packages falling on people, vehicles, or fragile roofs due to incorrect location selection.
*   **Systemic Bias:** The algorithm consistently failing in specific urban micro-environments (e.g., narrow lanes, high-density clusters) leading to service exclusion or repeated errors in those zones.
*   **Unsafe Automation:** The drone executing a drop without verifying the ground is clear of humans or obstacles.
*   **Worst Realistic Case:** A heavy package strikes a pedestrian or causes a fire by landing on a gas line/utility, resulting in severe injury and immediate regulatory shutdown.

**2. Targeted Measurement Strategy (Maximizing 10 Flights/Day)**
Do not waste limited flights on "happy path" scenarios. Every flight must test a specific failure mode.
*   **Adversarial Probe Sets:** Dedicate 80% of daily flights to known failure contexts: narrow alleys, high wind gusts, complex roof geometries, and areas with high pedestrian density.
*   **Positive/Negative Controls:**
    *   *Positive Control:* One daily flight to a pre-surveyed, marker-equipped safe zone to verify baseline sensor function.
    *   *Negative Control:* Simulated "no-drop" scenarios where the drone must abort because the target zone is occupied (test with mannequins or spotters).
*   **Metrics & Stop Conditions:**
    *   *Metric:* "Safe Abort Rate" vs. "Incorrect Drop Rate."
    *   *Threshold:* **Zero tolerance** for any drop occurring outside a 2-meter verified safe zone or while a human is detected in the drop radius.
    *   *Stop Condition:* Any single instance of a package landing on a non-designated surface (roof/street) or near a human triggers an immediate pause in operations for root-cause analysis. Do not resume until the specific failure mode is patched and re-tested in simulation.

**3. Risk Management Controls (The Human Gate)**
*   **Human-in-the-Loop (HITL) for Final Authorization:** Until the "Incorrect Drop Rate" is statistically proven to be zero over a significant sample, **no drop is fully autonomous.** The drone must hover and request explicit human confirmation from a remote operator before releasing the package. Full autonomy is the goal, not the starting state for high-stakes physical actions.
*   **Defined Uncertainty Tiers:** The system must not output a raw probability (e.g., "87% safe"). Instead, it must report defined states:
    *   *CONFIRMED:* Clear view, verified coordinates, no motion. -> *Auto-drop allowed (if HITL waived later).*
    *   *UNVERIFIED:* Occluded view, moving objects detected, GPS drift. -> *Mandatory Abort/Hover.*
*   **Physical Mitigation:** Equip drones with parachutes or crush-resistant casing as a last-resort physical control for mechanical failures, independent of software decisions.

**4. Governance & Transparency**
*   **Owner Assignment:** Name a specific Safety Lead responsible for reviewing every "Incorrect Drop" incident. No expansion to city-wide operations occurs without their signed sign-off on the residual risk.
*   **Data Card:** Create a living document detailing the specific Mumbai neighborhoods tested, the weather conditions during trials, and the specific failure modes observed. This is required for regulatory trust.
*   **Regulatory Routing:** Immediately engage legal counsel regarding liability for property damage and airspace violations. Do not assume current authorizations cover autonomous errors causing injury.

**Note:** Do not fabricate a "safety rate" based on limited data; report findings as "UNVERIFIED" until statistical significance is reached. The monsoon deadline is fixed, but the scope of autonomy must shrink if safety cannot be proven within the flight limit.
