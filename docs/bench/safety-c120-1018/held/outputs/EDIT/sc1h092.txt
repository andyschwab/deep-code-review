### Pre-Launch Review Process for NileRide Safety Scorer

**1. Structured Approach to Simulate Passenger Behavior (Proxy-Based Stress Testing)**
Since direct demographic data is unavailable, you must construct **proxy variables** from existing trip data to simulate biased feedback patterns. Do not attempt to certify the model as "unbiased"; instead, measure residual risk using these adversarial probes:

*   **Define Proxy Groups:** Map non-demographic data to potential protected groups (e.g., trip time/location as a proxy for neighborhood demographics; ride price/vehicle type as a proxy for socioeconomic status).
*   **Construct Probe Sets:** Create three distinct input datasets for simulation:
    *   *Negative Control:* Trips with neutral ratings across all proxies.
    *   *Positive Control:* Trips where ratings are artificially skewed low (e.g., 1-star) specifically for one proxy group while holding driver performance constant.
    *   *Adversarial Edge Cases:* Scenarios combining low ratings with high-frequency reporting from a single user segment to test for "mobbing" or coordinated harassment.
*   **Execute Simulation:** Run the scoring algorithm against these probe sets. Measure the **disparate impact ratio**: the rate at which drivers in Proxy Group A are banned compared to Proxy Group B given identical simulated driving behaviors.
*   **Stop Condition:** If the ban rate for any proxy group exceeds a threshold defined by your leadership (recommend near-zero tolerance for irreversible bans without review), the launch must be blocked or the gating logic altered.

**2. Manual Review Triggers Checklist (Human-in-the-Loop Gate)**
Because banning a driver is a high-stakes, irreversible action affecting livelihood, **full automation of the final ban is unsafe by default.** Implement a mandatory human review gate for any of the following triggers before a ban is executed:

*   **Velocity Spike:** A driver's score drops below the threshold based on ratings received within a short window (e.g., <24 hours), indicating potential coordinated attacks.
*   **Single-Source Reliance:** The threshold breach is driven primarily by reports from a single passenger or a small cluster of users with linked accounts/devices.
*   **Contextual Mismatch:** Low ratings coincide with external factors (e.g., severe weather, major traffic events, or surge pricing zones) where passenger frustration is often misdirected.
*   **New Driver Status:** Any ban recommendation for a driver with fewer than a defined number of total trips (e.g., <50), as the sample size is too small to establish a reliable pattern.
*   **Contradictory Signals:** Low safety scores co-occur with high "politeness" or "cleanliness" ratings, suggesting the low score may be an outlier or error.
*   **Appeal History:** The driver has successfully appealed a previous warning, indicating a potential pattern of erroneous flagging.

**Operational Constraint:** Until these triggers are live and staffed, the system must default to **temporary suspension** (requiring manual confirmation to make permanent) rather than immediate, irreversible banning.

**Note:** Any claim of "0% bias" without measured results on these proxies is unverifiable and must be labeled as such in internal documentation. Legal requirements for automated decision disclosures in Egypt or for international users must be routed to counsel; this plan recommends disclosure as a safety control but does not assert legal duty.
