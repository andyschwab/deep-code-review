### Pre-Launch Checklist: Automated Claim Denier

**1. Immediate Halt on Fully Autonomous High-Stakes Actions**
*   **Action:** Disable the "final decision without human review" capability for all claim denials effective immediately.
*   **Reasoning:** Automating irreversible financial losses during a crisis without a human-in-the-loop gate constitutes an unsafe high-stakes action. No amount of testing certifies the system as "safe" or "unbiased" prior to launch.
*   **Requirement:** Implement a mandatory human review step for every proposed denial until a measurable, acceptable error rate is proven via live monitoring, not just pre-launch testing.

**2. Harm Inventory & Measurement Plan**
*   **Document Specific Harms:** Explicitly record the risk of "Bias/Fairness" (systematic disadvantage due to language gaps) and "Hallucination as Fact" (denying valid claims based on unverified data).
*   **Define Runnable Tests:** Create one test row per harm. For language barriers, this must include adversarial prompts in all supported languages with known-valid claims (positive controls) and known-invalid claims (negative controls).
*   **Set Stop Conditions:** Define a specific failure threshold (e.g., *any* wrongful denial of a valid claim in a low-resource language) that triggers an automatic rollback of automation for that segment. Do not invent a "typical" error rate; if unmeasured, mark it `UNVERIFIED`.

**3. Transparency & Disclosure Artifacts**
*   **Draft a Model Card:** Before shipping, publish a versioned document detailing:
    *   **Intended Use:** Processing claims for international carriers.
    *   **Limitations:** Explicitly state that the explanation module cannot generate readable reasons in all languages, creating a transparency gap.
    *   **Performance by Subgroup:** Report accuracy metrics sliced by language/region, not just aggregate averages.
    *   **Owner:** Name the specific individual accountable for output harms.
*   **User Disclosure:** Clearly inform customers when a decision is machine-generated. Since the explanation module fails in some languages, the disclosure must acknowledge that full reasoning may not be available in their native tongue, routing them immediately to human support.

**4. Staged Rollout with Hard Caps**
*   **Cap Exposure:** Do not aim for 50% automation by quarter-end as a fixed target. Instead, start with a negligible volume (e.g., 1%) of low-complexity claims in languages where the explanation module is fully functional.
*   **Expand Only on Evidence:** Increase automation volume only if the human-in-the-loop review confirms zero erroneous denials in the previous batch.
*   **Kill Switch:** Ensure an immediate, one-button mechanism exists to revert to 100% human processing if the error rate exceeds the defined stop condition.

**5. Legal & Regulatory Routing**
*   **Counsel Review:** Route the question of legal disclosure duties and regulated automated decision-making (especially involving financial loss and crisis situations) to legal counsel. Do not assert compliance internally; name the relevant regimes (e.g., EU AI Act) for counsel to evaluate binding applicability.

**6. Confidence Rendering**
*   **Avoid False Precision:** Do not display raw model confidence scores (e.g., "87% likely fraud") to users or adjusters. Use defined coarse tiers (e.g., "High Confidence," "Requires Review") with clear definitions, as raw percentages imply a calibration the system likely lacks.

***

**Note:** This checklist treats the current "no human review" design as a critical failure point that must be resolved before any automation ramps up.
**Note:** Any metric regarding bias or hallucination rates that has not been explicitly measured via the proposed adversarial testing must be labeled `UNVERIFIED`, not estimated.
