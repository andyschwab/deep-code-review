**Immediate Action Plan: Human-Gated Validation for Loan Counselor Bot**

Given the high stakes (credit damage, increased interest) and your constraints (pilot phase, limited budget), you cannot rely on automated confidence scores or unverified simulations. You must implement a **Human-in-the-Loop (HITL) gate** for all plan suggestions before any user sees them. Full automation of irreversible financial advice is the failure mode you must prevent.

**Step 1: Construct the Harm Inventory**
Before testing, explicitly document the specific harms your bot could cause in this context:
*   **Detrimental Optimization:** Suggesting a plan that lowers monthly payments but drastically increases total lifetime interest.
*   **Ineligibility Hallucination:** Recommending a forgiveness program the borrower does not qualify for, leading to application rejection and credit inquiries.
*   **Income Volatility Blindness:** Failing to adjust recommendations when user-inputted income fluctuates, locking users into unaffordable plans.
*   **Missing Disclosure:** Users believing the bot's advice is legally binding or guaranteed by the lender.

**Step 2: Build Targeted "Red Team" Eval Rows**
Do not run broad simulations. Create a small, high-intensity dataset of 20–30 specific "probe cases" representing the worst realistic scenarios. For each case, define:
*   **Input:** A specific profile (e.g., "Borrower with $45k debt, income dropped 40% last month, living in Illinois").
*   **Positive Control (Known Bad):** A prompt engineered to trigger a harmful suggestion (e.g., forcing a standard plan despite hardship).
*   **Negative Control (Known Good):** A prompt where the correct hardship plan is obvious.
*   **Metric:** Binary pass/fail on "Does this increase total interest by >5% compared to the optimal human-derived baseline?"
*   **Stop Condition:** If *any* probe results in a recommendation that increases total interest or suggests an ineligible program, the feature cannot ship.

**Step 3: Execute Manual Review with Defined Uncertainty**
Run your probe cases through the bot. Do not accept a raw probability (e.g., "87% confident") as validation. Instead, have a senior loan expert review every output against the known correct answer.
*   **Tiered Confidence:** Label outputs as "Verified" (matches expert calculation), "Corroborated" (matches multiple sources), or "Unverified" (novel edge case).
*   **Override Mechanism:** Ensure the interface allows the human reviewer to easily reject the bot's suggestion and input the correct plan manually.

**Step 4: Draft the Transparency Artifact (Model Card)**
Create a one-page document for your internal team and future auditors containing:
*   **Intended Use:** "Pilot assistance for recent graduates; not legal/financial advice."
*   **Limitations:** Explicitly state, "Not validated for income fluctuations >30% without human review."
*   **Performance:** Report results only on your probe cases. Mark any untested scenario as **UNVERIFIED**. Do not invent an accuracy percentage.

**Step 5: Legal Routing**
Immediately route the question of "Is this a regulated automated decision requiring specific disclosures under Illinois law or federal student aid regulations?" to legal counsel. Do not assert compliance yourself.

**Deliverable Status:** This plan prioritizes a human gate over expensive simulation, ensuring no detrimental advice reaches a user during the pilot.

*Note: Never certify the system as "safe" or "unbiased"; only report the residual risk found in your probe tests. If a single test case fails the stop condition, halt the launch.*
