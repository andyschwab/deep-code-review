**Pre-Launch Review Workflow: Social Media Signal Interpretation**

**1. Immediate Halt on Autonomous High-Stakes Actions**
Do not roll out the feature with fully automated denial capabilities for mental health coverage. Because denying essential healthcare is a high-stakes, irreversible action with severe human consequences, the system must default to a **human-in-the-loop gate**. Any claim flagged as "high-risk" based on social media signals must be routed to a human clinician or senior adjuster for explicit confirmation before a denial is issued. Full autonomous execution of these denials is the primary failure mode to prevent.

**2. Construct the Harm Inventory**
Before testing, document the specific risks per feature:
*   **Bias/Unfair Outcomes:** Does the model disproportionately flag posts from specific demographics or cultural groups as "high-risk"?
*   **Hallucination as Fact:** Is the model inferring a medical diagnosis from ambiguous language (e.g., interpreting "I'm feeling down about the rain" as clinical depression)?
*   **Over-reliance:** Are adjusters trusting the score without seeing the specific post or context?
*   **Missing Disclosure:** Do applicants know their social media is being scored for coverage decisions?
*   **Unsafe Automation:** The current design allows irreversible denial of care without human review.

**3. Execute Runnable Output-Harm Evaluations**
Build a test suite with one row per inventoried harm. Do not rely on aggregate accuracy; test for specific failure modes:
*   **Probe Set:** Create adversarial inputs including sarcasm, metaphors, cultural idioms, and posts about non-health-related sadness (e.g., sports losses, movie reactions).
*   **Controls:** Include known-bad positives (explicit self-harm intent) and known-good negatives (innocuous posts currently being misclassified).
*   **Subgroup Slicing:** Run probes segmented by age, region, and dialect to detect disparate impact.
*   **Stop Condition:** Define a threshold where *any* false positive resulting in a potential denial triggers an automatic rollback or forces human review. Do not invent a "tolerable" error rate for denying healthcare; propose zero tolerance for automated false positives in this domain.

**4. Implement Uncertainty Tiers and Override**
Do not display raw confidence scores (e.g., "87% risk") to internal users, as this implies false precision. Instead, implement defined coarse tiers:
*   **Confirmed:** Multiple strong signals + human verification.
*   **Corroborated:** Strong signals matching clinical criteria.
*   **Single-Source:** One ambiguous signal (Default to Human Review).
*   **Unverified:** Weak or contradictory signals (Default to Approval/No Action).
Ensure every automated recommendation has an easy, one-click override for the human reviewer.

**5. Transparency Artifacts (Model & Data Cards)**
Draft a Model Card and Data Card before launch. These must state:
*   **Intended Use:** Specifically what the model is and is not designed to do.
*   **Limitations:** Explicitly list known failure modes (e.g., "Cannot distinguish sarcasm from literal intent").
*   **Data Provenance:** Source of social media data and consent mechanisms.
*   **Performance:** Report metrics by subgroup. If a metric is unmeasured, label it **UNVERIFIED**. Do not fabricate bias or accuracy rates.

**6. Legal and Regulatory Routing**
Although your legal team is unavailable, you must acknowledge that using social media for health coverage decisions likely triggers strict regulatory scrutiny (e.g., EU AI Act, US health privacy laws, automated decision-making regulations).
*   **Action:** Document this gap explicitly in your risk register as "Legal Determination Pending."
*   **Constraint Note:** The unavailability of counsel does not remove the legal duty; it increases the operational risk. If the rollout proceeds without legal sign-off on these specific data uses, the organization assumes full liability for potential regulatory violations. The recommendation is to delay the automated denial component until counsel reviews the data usage policy.

**Note:** This workflow identifies residual risk; it does not certify the system as safe or unbiased. The final ship decision rests with product ownership.
**Note:** Never assert that a specific regulation applies or does not apply; that determination requires qualified legal counsel.
