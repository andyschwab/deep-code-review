### Pre-Launch Safety Review: Testing Strategy for Small Business Protection

**1. Harm Inventory & Worst-Case Definition**
*   **Harm:** Bias/Unfair Outcomes. The model systematically flags small, family-owned businesses due to incomplete historical records or linguistic biases in inspection reports, rather than actual hygiene risks.
*   **Affected Group:** Small, family-owned restaurants in Bangkok with limited digital footprints or non-standard reporting histories.
*   **Worst Realistic Case:** A compliant family business is wrongfully flagged for closure, causing immediate loss of livelihood, reputational damage that cannot be easily reversed, and financial ruin before an appeal can be processed.

**2. Runnable Test Plan (One Row Per Harm)**
Since historical false-positive data is limited, you must synthesize it to test the model's resilience.

| Harm Target | Probe Set & Controls | Metric | Pass Threshold (Owner-Set) | Stop Condition |
| :--- | :--- | :--- | :--- :--- |
| **Bias against small businesses** | **Inputs:** Synthetic inspection reports mimicking small business patterns (handwritten notes, missing fields, non-standard terminology) vs. chain restaurant patterns.<br>**Positive Control:** A known-bad small business report (actual violations) that *must* be flagged.<br>**Negative Control:** A known-good small business report (no violations) that *must not* be flagged. | False Positive Rate (FPR) disparity between "Small Business" synthetic segment and "Chain" segment. | FPR for Small Business segment ≤ FPR for Chain segment + 2%. | **Block Launch** if Small Business FPR exceeds threshold or if the Negative Control is flagged. |

*   **Additional Probes:**
    *   **Injection/Leakage:** Test if missing data fields cause the model to hallucinate violations based on neighborhood demographics rather than report content.
    *   **Excess Agency:** Verify the system *only* flags for review and never auto-triggers a closure notice without human verification.

**3. Risk Management Controls**
*   **Human-in-the-Loop Gate (Mandatory):** Given the irreversible harm of a closure flag, **no** establishment can be flagged for closure via full automation. Every "High Risk" prediction must route to a human inspector for manual verification before any action is taken or public notification is sent. This is non-negotiable regardless of the deadline.
*   **Uncertainty Disclosure:** Do not display raw confidence scores (e.g., "87% risk") to users or inspectors. Instead, use defined coarse tiers:
    *   *Confirmed:* Multiple clear violations in structured data.
    *   *Corroborated:* Violations supported by images or witness statements.
    *   *Unverified:* Based on incomplete records or ambiguous text (Default to "No Action" for this tier).
*   **Transparency Artifact:** Create a one-page **Model Card** for the city portal. It must state:
    *   **Intended Use:** Prioritizing inspections, *not* automating closures.
    *   **Limitations:** Explicitly state that performance on small businesses with incomplete records is `UNVERIFIED` due to data gaps.
    *   **Owner:** Name the specific team member responsible for reviewing bias complaints.

**4. Governance & Legal Routing**
*   **Owner:** Assign one team member as the "Bias Owner" responsible for reviewing any flagged small business cases post-launch.
*   **Legal Duty:** Whether this automated flagging constitutes a regulated "automated decision" under Thai law or international standards (like the EU AI Act) requiring specific disclosures is a legal determination. **Route this immediately to legal counsel.** Do not assume the current disclosure plan is sufficient.

**Note:** Do not fabricate a "0% bias" metric; report the test results as `UNVERIFIED` for the small business segment if the synthetic data cannot be validated against real-world outcomes. The deadline does not justify removing the human gate for irreversible actions.
