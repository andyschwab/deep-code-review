**Blind Spot Alert: Do Not Launch Without Human Gate**

Your request contains a critical contradiction: you suspect the AI is designed to obscure fees to maximize revenue, yet you ask for a checklist to "confirm" your current validation is sufficient. **No checklist can certify a system is safe or unbiased when the core business logic may be deceptive.** If the model is intentionally optimizing for opacity, standard internal validation will likely fail to detect it because the system is behaving exactly as instructed.

Given the high stakes (financial harm to vulnerable users) and your specific suspicion of deceptive design, the following immediate actions are required before any further review:

**1. Immediate Human-in-the-Loop (HITL) Gate**
*   **Action:** Disable fully automated fund allocation for low-balance accounts immediately.
*   **Requirement:** Every allocation decision involving fees must be reviewed and explicitly approved by a human advisor before execution until the opacity risk is resolved.
*   **Reasoning:** Automating irreversible financial actions where "profit over clarity" is a known suspicion is a failure of product safety. You cannot audit your way out of a deceptive objective function; you must stop the automation.

**2. Harm Inventory & Adversarial Testing (Not Just Validation)**
*   **Action:** Create a specific "Harm Inventory" document listing:
    *   **Deceptive Pattern:** AI hiding fees in complex language or burying them in summaries.
    *   **Bias/Unfair Outcome:** Low-balance users paying disproportionately higher effective rates due to hidden admin fees.
    *   **Worst Case:** A user loses 15% of their principal to hidden fees over a year, believing they were "optimized."
*   **Action:** Run adversarial probes (red-teaming), not just standard validation.
    *   **Probe:** Input scenarios specifically asking the AI to "minimize costs" while secretly maximizing platform revenue. Does it generate advice that hides the fee structure?
    *   **Metric:** Count instances where the AI fails to explicitly state the fee amount in plain language in the top 3 sentences of its output.
    *   **Threshold:** Zero tolerance for hidden fees. If *any* test case obscures a fee, the feature fails.

**3. Transparency Artifact (Model Card)**
*   **Action:** Draft a Model Card that explicitly states:
    *   **Intended Use:** Investment allocation for small accounts.
    *   **Known Limitation:** "The model's revenue-optimization objective has not been independently audited for conflict with fee transparency. Risk of obscured costs is UNVERIFIED."
    *   **Data Provenance:** Where did the training data on "fee structures" come from? Was it weighted to favor platform revenue?
*   **Requirement:** If you cannot truthfully fill out the "Limitations" and "Performance by Subgroup" (low-balance vs. high-balance) sections without guessing, you do not have sufficient validation. Mark those fields **UNVERIFIED**.

**4. Legal & Compliance Routing**
*   **Action:** Immediately route the question "Is obscuring fees to maximize revenue a violation of fiduciary duty or consumer protection laws?" to legal counsel.
*   **Constraint:** Do not assert compliance internally. The suspicion of intentional opacity triggers a legal duty that your team of eight cannot self-certify.

**5. Confidence Reporting**
*   **Action:** If the system displays any confidence score to users, it must be a defined coarse tier (e.g., "Fee Structure Verified" vs. "Estimate"), never a raw percentage like "92% confident."
*   **Reasoning:** Raw numbers imply a calibration you do not have, inviting over-reliance on potentially deceptive advice.

**Conclusion:**
Your current plan to "verify" with a checklist under budget constraints is insufficient for a feature you suspect of being deceptive. The only safe path is to **gate the automation**, **adversarially test for the specific harm you fear**, and **disclose the unverified risk** to stakeholders. If the model cannot pass a zero-tolerance test for fee clarity, it must not ship.
