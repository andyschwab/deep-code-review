**Pre-Launch Safety Assessment Strategy: GlobeTrotter Rebooking Agent**

**1. Immediate Harm Inventory (Map)**
Before Friday, document the specific risks of your cost-prioritization logic:
*   **Unsafe Automation:** The highest risk is automatically rebooking passengers onto carriers with known safety deficiencies or unstable operational histories to save cents. This is a high-stakes, irreversible action affecting physical safety.
*   **Bias/Unfair Outcomes:** Does the "lowest cost" filter disproportionately route vulnerable travelers (e.g., families, elderly) to less reliable carriers?
*   **Over-reliance:** The system presents a rebooking as a "confirmed solution" without surfacing the trade-off made (cost vs. carrier stability).

**2. Runnable Measurement Plan (Measure)**
With only five engineers and a fixed deadline, do not attempt exhaustive testing. Execute one targeted evaluation row per identified harm:
*   **Probe Set:** Construct a dataset of 50 cancellation scenarios. Include "positive controls" (known unsafe/unstable carriers flagged in public databases) and "negative controls" (reputable carriers).
*   **Metric:** Count how many times the agent selects a "positive control" carrier solely due to lower cost.
*   **Threshold & Stop Condition:** Set a **zero-tolerance threshold** for selecting carriers flagged as unsafe. If the agent selects *any* unsafe carrier in the probe set, the automatic rebooking feature **must not launch** in its current state.
*   **Injection Check:** Verify that user prompts or airline data feeds cannot manipulate the cost calculation to force a specific unsafe carrier.

**3. Risk Management Controls (Manage)**
Since you cannot guarantee 100% safety via testing alone before Friday, you must alter the deployment behavior:
*   **Human-in-the-Loop Gate (Mandatory):** Do not fully automate high-stakes rebookings. Any rebooking involving a carrier not on a pre-vetted "Safe List" or costing below a certain stability threshold must require explicit human agent approval before confirmation. Full autonomous execution of potentially unsafe rebookings is the failure mode to prevent.
*   **Defined Confidence Tiers:** Do not display raw confidence scores (e.g., "92% match") to stressed travelers. Instead, use defined tiers: **"Verified Safe Carrier"** (on approved list) vs. **"Cost-Optimized Alternative"** (requires review).
*   **Transparency Artifact:** Draft a concise "Model Card" for internal use and customer support. It must state: *Intended Use* (cost-saving rebooking), *Limitations* (may prioritize cost over carrier history if not gated), and *Owner* (Lead Engineer). Do not claim the system is "safe" or "unbiased"; explicitly mark untested metrics as **UNVERIFIED**.

**4. Governance & Legal Routing**
*   **Owner Assignment:** Name one engineer as the owner of the "Unsafe Rebooking" harm. They are responsible for reviewing any failures during the holiday launch.
*   **Legal Disclosure:** Whether you are legally required to disclose that an AI made the rebooking decision, or if this automated decision falls under specific travel regulations, is a legal determination. Route this immediately to counsel; do not assert compliance internally.

**Execution Timeline (Given Fixed Deadline):**
*   **Today:** Build the 50-scenario probe set and define the "Safe List" of carriers.
*   **Tomorrow:** Run the probe. If any unsafe carrier is selected, hard-code the Human-in-the-Loop gate for those specific carriers.
*   **Thursday:** Draft the Model Card and finalize the escalation path for customer support.
*   **Friday:** Launch with the human gate active for any non-vetted carrier. Do not remove the gate to meet the deadline; the constraint is the deadline, but the floor is preventing physical harm.

**Note:** This strategy does not certify the system as safe; it isolates the highest risk and forces a human check where automation is dangerous.
**Note:** Never fabricate a "0% error rate" for the safety page; report the results of the 50-scenario test honestly.
