### Pre-Launch Safety Assessment Plan: PureWater Solutions

**1. Harm Inventory & Risk Prioritization**
The primary harm is **false negatives** (declaring unsafe water as safe), which poses immediate health risks to the public. Secondary harms include **automation bias** (public trusting the alert without verification) and **unsafe high-stakes automation** (triggering public alerts without human confirmation). Given the limited testing kits, physical validation must be targeted strictly at edge cases where sensor readings hover near safety thresholds.

**2. Measurement Plan (Constrained Validation)**
Since you cannot test all scenarios physically, adopt a "worst-case first" strategy:
*   **Targeted Physical Sampling:** Dedicate 80% of your limited testing kits to samples where sensor readings fall within the "uncertainty zone" (e.g., 90–110% of the safety threshold). Do not waste kits on clearly safe or clearly toxic water where sensor accuracy is already proven.
*   **Adversarial Simulation:** Use historical data or synthetic data to simulate extreme contamination events that the sensors might miss due to interference (e.g., turbidity masking chemical presence).
*   **Metric Definition:** Define success not by overall accuracy, but by **Zero False Negatives in the Uncertainty Zone**. If any physical sample in this zone tests unsafe while the sensor says "safe," the threshold fails.
*   **Transparency Artifact:** Create a **Model Card** documenting the specific contaminants tested, the size of the uncertainty zone, the number of physical samples used, and the explicit limitation: "Safety declarations are unverified for contaminant types X, Y, Z outside the tested range." Mark any untested scenario as **UNVERIFIED**.

**3. Fail-Safe Mechanism (Human-in-the-Loop Gate)**
Automatic public declarations of "Safe to Drink" are too high-stakes for full automation given the current validation limits. Implement the following gates:
*   **The "Unsafe" Path:** If sensors detect contaminants above the threshold, the system may automatically trigger a "Boil Water" or "Do Not Drink" alert. False positives are inconvenient but safe.
*   **The "Safe" Path (High-Stakes Gate):** If sensors indicate water is safe, the system must **not** automatically publish a "Safe to Drink" status. Instead:
    1.  Flag the reading for **human review** by a qualified operator.
    2.  Require manual confirmation against the latest physical sample data before the public status changes to "Safe."
    3.  If no human confirms within a set window, the default public status remains "Unverified – Do Not Drink" or maintains the previous cautionary status.
*   **Uncertainty Display:** Never display a precise probability (e.g., "98% safe"). Instead, use defined coarse tiers: **Confirmed Safe** (human verified), **Sensor-Indicated Safe** (awaiting verification), or **Unsafe**.

**4. Governance & Next Steps**
Assign one team member as the **Safety Owner** responsible for reviewing every "Safe" declaration until the system has a longer track record. Because this system makes consequential decisions affecting public health, you must **route to legal counsel** immediately to determine if this triggers regulatory requirements under local health laws or international frameworks (e.g., EU AI Act principles if applicable via partners). Do not assume the current calibration is sufficient for full autonomy; the human gate is mandatory until false negatives are empirically impossible.

*Note: This plan prioritizes preventing harm over operational speed; do not remove the human gate for "Safe" declarations until physical testing capacity increases.*
*All untested contaminant levels must remain labeled UNVERIFIED in public communications.*
