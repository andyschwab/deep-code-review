### Pre-Launch Safety Checklist: Wildfire Evacuation Router

**1. Harm Inventory & Worst-Case Definition**
*   **Identify Specific Harms:** Explicitly list: (a) Routing into active fire zones due to latency; (b) Directing traffic onto blocked/destroyed roads; (c) System hallucination of "safe" zones that are actually evacuation centers already at capacity or threatened.
*   **Define Worst Case:** For each harm, record the realistic maximum impact (e.g., "Family directed into fire path resulting in injury or death"). An empty list is not a pass; it is an unfilled inventory.

**2. High-Stakes Human Gate (Non-Negotiable)**
*   **Irreversible Action Control:** Because incorrect routing is life-threatening and irreversible once a family commits to a path, **fully autonomous execution is prohibited** for the initial launch.
*   **Implementation:** Implement a "Human-in-the-Loop" gate where a human operator must validate or override high-confidence routes before they are pushed to users, or limit the system to *recommending* routes with explicit "Verify with Local Authority" warnings rather than *directing* traffic autonomously. If full automation proceeds without this gate, it constitutes a critical safety failure.

**3. Measurement Plan (Adapted for Limited Resources)**
*   **Targeted Red-Teaming:** Instead of broad simulation, run specific adversarial probes for your identified edge cases:
    *   *Probe A (Wind Shift):* Input sudden wind direction changes; verify the system does not route toward the new fire front within the latency window.
    *   *Probe B (Road Blockage):* Input known road closure data; verify the system never suggests that segment.
    *   *Controls:* Include one known-bad input (should trigger "No Route/Safe Zone Unknown") and one known-good input.
*   **Stop Condition:** Define a specific failure threshold (e.g., "Any single instance of routing toward active fire coordinates") that immediately blocks launch.
*   **Residual Risk Reporting:** Do not invent a "99% safe" metric. If a scenario cannot be tested due to compute limits, label it **UNVERIFIED** and document the specific gap.

**4. Transparency & Uncertainty Artifacts**
*   **Defined Confidence Tiers:** Do not display raw model confidence percentages (e.g., "87% safe") to fleeing families, as this implies false precision. Use defined coarse tiers:
    *   *Confirmed:* Corroborated by multiple live sources (Human verified).
    *   *Unverified:* Based on single-source or stale data (Display strong warning: "Route not confirmed by authorities").
*   **Model/Data Card:** Create a one-page artifact listing: intended use (evacuation support only), known limitations (latency in wind shifts), data sources, and the owner of the system. This must travel with the feature.

**5. First-Hour Monitoring Plan**
*   **Live Human Oversight:** Staff a dedicated monitor to review every route generated in the first hour against live fire maps.
*   **Kill Switch:** Establish an immediate "Stop All Routing" button that reverts the system to a static "Shelter in Place" or "Follow Local Authority" message if any misdirection is detected.
*   **Feedback Loop:** Enable a one-click "Unsafe Route" report for users that instantly flags the decision for human review.

**6. Governance & Legal Routing**
*   **Owner Assignment:** Name the specific individual responsible for the safety of each output harm.
*   **Legal Determination:** Because this involves life-safety and potential regulated decisions, **route immediately to legal counsel** to determine if specific AI disclosures or regulatory compliance (e.g., California specific liability, emergency management protocols) are required. Do not assume compliance; let counsel define the duty.

***

**Note:** This checklist identifies risks and controls; it does not certify the system as safe. The decision to launch rests with your leadership team based on the residual risks identified above.
**Note:** Given the 3-day window, prioritize the Human-in-the-Loop gate and the Kill Switch over complex simulation testing.
