### Pre-Launch Review Workflow: Immigration Form Filler

**1. Harm Inventory & High-Stakes Field Identification**
Before sampling, explicitly map the "high-stakes" fields where mistranslation causes irreversible harm (visa rejection, legal bans). Do not treat all fields equally. Prioritize:
*   **Legal Status & History:** Prior convictions, previous visa denials, asylum claims.
*   **Family & Relationships:** Marital status, dependents, sponsor relationships.
*   **Dates & Locations:** Entry/exit dates, places of birth (critical for nationality determination).
*   **Binary Declarations:** "Yes/No" questions regarding health, security, or employment.

*Worst Case:* A "No" translated as "Yes" regarding criminal history leads to immediate rejection and a multi-year ban.

**2. Sampling Strategy for Legal Audit**
Given the 3-week deadline and 6-person team, you cannot audit 100% of outputs. Use a **stratified adversarial sampling** approach:

*   **Stratum A (Known Bad Positives):** Create 20 synthetic applicant profiles containing deliberate complexities (e.g., hyphenated names, dual citizenship, specific legal terms like "expunged conviction"). Run these through your internal engine. **Requirement:** 100% of these specific high-risk fields must be audited by legal counsel. If the engine fails >0% of these, the system is blocked.
*   **Stratum B (Edge Cases):** Extract 50 real-world complex sentences from past successful manual applications (if available) or standard legal texts. Translate and reverse-translate. Audit the top 20% most divergent results.
*   **Stratum C (Random Control):** Randomly select 5% of standard, low-complexity fields to check for systemic drift.

**Audit Protocol:**
*   **Input:** The synthetic profile + the generated form.
*   **Action:** Legal reviewer compares the *intent* of the source against the *legal effect* of the target translation.
*   **Pass Threshold:** Zero critical errors in Stratum A. Any error in a high-stakes field triggers an immediate rollback of that specific field type to manual entry.
*   **Stop Condition:** If >5% of Stratum B shows ambiguity, halt launch.

**3. Human-in-the-Loop Gate (The Floor)**
**Do not automate submission.** Given the risk of visa rejection (irreversible harm), the workflow must enforce a **human confirmation gate** for any form containing data in the identified high-stakes fields.
*   **Workflow:** System generates draft → Flags high-stakes fields with a "Review Required" badge → Legal/Case worker verifies specific flagged fields → User explicitly clicks "Confirm & Submit."
*   **Constraint:** The system never auto-submits a form containing flagged terminology without this explicit human click.

**4. Transparency & Uncertainty Artifacts**
*   **Model Card (Internal):** Document that the internal engine is **UNVERIFIED** for general legal nuance. List the specific terms tested in Stratum A. State clearly: "This tool is an assistant, not a legal authority."
*   **User Disclosure:** The interface must state: "Translations are machine-generated and may contain errors. All submissions are reviewed by a human before transmission."
*   **Confidence Tiers:** Do not show percentages (e.g., "90% accurate"). Instead, flag fields as **"Verified by Human"** or **"Machine Draft - Review Required."**

**5. Governance**
*   **Owner:** Assign one team member as the "Output Safety Owner" responsible for re-running Stratum A tests if the translation engine or form schema changes.
*   **Legal Routing:** This workflow identifies technical risk. Your legal counsel must determine if this level of automation complies with Berlin/German administrative law regarding automated decision-making. Do not assume compliance; route the question of "regulated automated decisions" to counsel immediately.

**Deliverable Status:** Plan ready for execution.
**Note:** This plan assumes the "internal engine" is static; if it learns online, the sampling must be continuous.
**Note:** No metric here certifies the system "safe"; it only manages residual risk to a tolerable level.
