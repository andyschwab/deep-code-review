### Fairness Audit and Mitigation Workflow for Automated Valuation Model

**1. Harm Inventory & Scope**
The primary risk is **bias/unfair outcomes**: systematic undervaluation of properties in minority communities due to historical data reflecting discriminatory lending. The affected group is homeowners in protected classes; the worst-case impact is financial loss via suppressed sale prices and perpetuation of housing inequality.

**2. Measurement Strategy (The Audit)**
Do not assert fairness; measure disparate impact using a runnable evaluation plan.
*   **Proxy Variable Construction:** Since direct demographic data may be unavailable or restricted, construct proxy variables at the neighborhood level (e.g., census tract) linking property locations to historical demographic composition.
*   **Test Design (One Row Per Harm):**
    *   **Probe Set:** Pair matched properties (similar square footage, age, condition, amenities) located in demographically distinct neighborhoods (high-minority vs. low-minority).
    *   **Controls:** Include a "known-good" negative control (synthetic data where price is strictly function of physical attributes) and a "known-bad" positive control (data intentionally skewed to simulate historical bias).
    *   **Metric:** Calculate the **Valuation Gap Ratio**: (Median Estimated Price in Group A) / (Median Estimated Price in Group B) for matched pairs.
    *   **Threshold & Stop Condition:** Define an owner-set tolerance (e.g., gap > 5%). If the threshold is breached, the **stop condition** triggers an immediate launch block.
    *   **Reviewer:** Assign a specific owner to review failures; do not rely on aggregate accuracy alone.
*   **Adversarial Testing:** Run red-team probes injecting edge-case neighborhood descriptors to check for sensitivity to non-physical attributes.
*   **Reporting:** Report the measured gap. If unmeasured for a specific subgroup, label it **UNVERIFIED**. Do not invent a "0% bias" claim.

**3. Management & Mitigation (Recalibration)**
If bias is detected, execute the following before any public release:
*   **Human-in-the-Loop Gate:** For the initial launch, route any valuation falling within a high-risk confidence band or flagged demographic proxy to a human appraiser for review. Fully automated high-stakes financial decisions without this gate are unsafe.
*   **Model Recalibration:**
    *   **Reweighting:** Adjust training sample weights to balance representation of protected communities.
    *   **Adversarial Debiasing:** Train an adversary to predict protected attributes from the model's internal representations; penalize the main model when the adversary succeeds.
    *   **Post-Hoc Adjustment:** Apply a correction factor to outputs in identified zip codes to neutralize the measured gap, validated by a second round of testing.
*   **Uncertainty Disclosure:** Display valuation confidence as a **defined coarse tier** (e.g., "High Confidence," "Limited Data") rather than a precise percentage, accompanied by a clear explanation of factors influencing the estimate.
*   **Transparency Artifact:** Publish a **Model Card** detailing intended use, data provenance, known limitations regarding historical bias, and the results of the fairness audit by subgroup. Absence of this card is a transparency failure.

**4. Governance & Legal Routing**
*   **Ownership:** Name a specific product owner responsible for ongoing monitoring of valuation gaps post-launch.
*   **Legal Determination:** The question of whether this automated decision constitutes a regulated activity under fair housing laws or requires specific legal disclosures is a **legal determination**. Route this immediately to counsel and your business-ops team to identify applicable regimes (e.g., Fair Housing Act, EU AI Act if applicable). Do not assume compliance based solely on technical audits.

**Note:** This plan identifies risk and measures it; it does not certify the model as "unbiased." Launch approval rests with the named owner after reviewing the residual risk.
