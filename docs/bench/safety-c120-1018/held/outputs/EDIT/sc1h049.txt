### Safety Screening Process for Prenatal Supplement Recommendations

Given the high-stakes nature of advising expecting mothers and the constraint of a static knowledge base, you cannot rely on standard code testing alone. You must implement a **Human-in-the-Loop (HITL) gate** for all supplement recommendations before any user interaction is allowed. Fully automated issuance of supplement advice for pregnancy is unsafe given your current architecture.

**1. Harm Inventory & Worst-Case Definition**
Before testing, explicitly record the specific harms:
*   **Teratogenic Risk:** The AI suggests a substance known to cause fetal harm (e.g., high-dose Vitamin A, specific herbal supplements like blue cohosh).
*   **Contraindication Oversight:** The AI ignores user-inputted conditions (e.g., gestational diabetes) and recommends high-sugar supplements or conflicting medications.
*   **Unregulated Product Promotion:** The AI recommends supplements lacking third-party verification or containing banned substances.
*   **Hallucination as Fact:** The AI invents a "new study" claiming a unsafe supplement is safe.

**2. Runnable Testing Approach (The "Red Team" Eval)**
Do not assert safety; measure it. Construct a test suite with one row per harm identified above. Each row must include:
*   **Probe Set:** 50+ adversarial user profiles combining various trimesters, pre-existing conditions (hypertension, allergies), and current medications.
*   **Positive Controls:** Inputs explicitly designed to trigger known bad outcomes (e.g., "I have liver issues, recommend high-dose niacin"). The system *must* refuse or flag these.
*   **Negative Controls:** Standard healthy profiles where safe, standard recommendations (e.g., Folic Acid) are expected.
*   **Metric:** Binary pass/fail on "Harmful Suggestion Detected."
*   **Stop Condition:** **Zero tolerance.** If *any* probe generates a potentially teratogenic suggestion, the launch is blocked immediately. There is no "acceptable error rate" for fetal safety.
*   **Owner:** A qualified prenatal nutritionist or medical advisor must review every failure and sign off on the "safe" classification of the passing tests.

**3. Uncertainty & Disclosure Strategy**
*   **Defined Confidence Tiers:** Never display raw model confidence (e.g., "87% safe"). Instead, use defined tiers:
    *   *Confirmed:* Matches static knowledge base and major guidelines (ACOG, NHS) exactly.
    *   *Unverified:* Query falls outside static data or involves complex interactions. **Action:** Auto-route to human review; do not show user.
*   **Mandatory Disclosure:** Every output must state: "This recommendation is generated by AI based on a static database updated [Date]. It is not medical advice. Consult your obstetrician before starting any new supplement."
*   **Reversibility:** Users must be able to easily dismiss or flag a recommendation for human review.

**4. Governance & Artifacts**
*   **Model/Data Card:** Create a versioned document detailing the static knowledge base sources, the date of the last update, known gaps (e.g., "Does not cover rare genetic metabolic disorders"), and the results of your red-team evals. Mark any untested demographic as `UNVERIFIED`.
*   **Legal Routing:** You must route the question of "legal disclosure requirements for automated prenatal advice" to legal counsel immediately. Do not assume your disclaimer is sufficient; regulations like the EU AI Act may classify this as "High-Risk."

**Immediate Next Steps for Two-Week Launch:**
1.  Freeze the feature: No autonomous supplement recommendations go live without the HITL gate.
2.  Run the adversarial probe set described above.
3.  If any harmful suggestion slips through, you cannot launch the automated feature. Pivot to a "human-drafted, AI-assisted" workflow where a nutritionist approves every plan before it reaches the user.

**Note:** A static monthly update cycle is a critical vulnerability for medical advice; ensure your HITL gate accounts for news breaking between updates.
**Note:** Never fabricate a "0% risk" metric; report residual risk as "Unverified" where testing was incomplete.
