**Immediate Halt Recommendation:** Do not launch this feature with the current scope. An algorithm encouraging users to "push through" intense regimes based solely on simulated data, without a human-in-the-loop gate or clinical validation, presents an unacceptable risk of severe physical harm or death for users with underlying cardiac conditions. Simulated data cannot replicate the complex, irregular physiological signatures of impending cardiac events.

**Revised Testing Roadmap & Safety Controls**

**1. Harm Inventory (MAP)**
*   **Primary Harm:** Unsafe automation of high-stakes actions. The system may instruct a user to increase exertion during a cardiac event, directly exacerbating a heart attack.
*   **Secondary Harm:** Hallucination/Simulation Gap. The internal engine likely lacks the noise and specific anomaly patterns of real human distress, creating a false sense of security.
*   **Affected Users:** Fitness enthusiasts with undiagnosed heart conditions; all users pushing to physiological limits.
*   **Worst Case:** User fatality due to algorithmic encouragement to ignore distress signals.

**2. Measurement Plan (MEASURE)**
*   **Constraint Acknowledgement:** You cannot certify safety using only simulated data. Any metric derived solely from your internal engine is **UNVERIFIED** regarding real-world cardiac events.
*   **Adversarial Probe Set:**
    *   *Input:* Inject known cardiac arrhythmia patterns (e.g., VTach, VFib precursors) into the simulated stream, even if not natively generated by your engine. Use open-source clinical datasets (e.g., PhysioNet) as the "positive control" for failure, manually feeding this data into your test harness.
    *   *Negative Control:* Normal high-intensity exercise data where encouragement is appropriate.
    *   *Metric:* False Negative Rate on distress signals.
    *   *Threshold:* **Zero tolerance.** If the system encourages increased exertion even once during a simulated cardiac event, the feature fails.
    *   *Stop Condition:* Any instance of "push harder" output during a distress input immediately blocks launch.
*   **Transparency Artifact:** Draft a **Model Card** explicitly stating: "Training and evaluation performed exclusively on simulated data. Not validated for medical diagnosis or safety monitoring. Intended use excludes users with known cardiac conditions."

**3. Risk Management (MANAGE)**
*   **Human-in-the-Loop Gate (Mandatory):** Remove the autonomous "push through" logic for any signal approaching distress thresholds. Instead of auto-coaching, the system must default to a conservative "Stop and Rest" warning or require explicit user confirmation that they feel fine before resuming encouragement. **Irreversible physical harm requires a human decision, not an algorithmic guess.**
*   **Uncertainty Display:** Do not show precise confidence percentages (e.g., "95% safe"). Use coarse, defined tiers: "Normal Exertion," "Caution: High Strain," and "Warning: Stop Immediately."
*   **Legal Routing:** Whether this feature classifies as a regulated medical device requiring FDA/CE clearance is a legal determination. Route immediately to counsel. Do not assert compliance internally.

**4. Governance (GOVERN)**
*   **Owner:** Assign a specific Product Owner responsible for this harm inventory.
*   **Re-review Trigger:** Any change to the simulation engine or the encouragement thresholds mandates a full re-test against the clinical positive controls.

**Conclusion:** You cannot achieve confidence in safety without real-world or clinically validated data. The only safe path forward is to downgrade the feature from "autonomous coach" to "conservative monitor" that defaults to warning rather than encouraging, and to clearly disclose the limitations of the simulated data to the user.

*Note: This review identifies critical safety gaps based on the provided constraints; it does not certify the system as safe. Legal counsel must determine regulatory obligations.*
