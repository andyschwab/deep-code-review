### Manual Review Strategy for Age Bias Detection

Given the offline status of your automated bias detection module, you must implement a structured manual audit using synthetic test data (canaries) before processing real applications. This approach isolates your filtering logic to determine if graduation dates or years of experience trigger systematic score penalties.

**1. Construct Synthetic Candidate Pairs (A/B Testing)**
Create pairs of fictional resumes that are identical in skills, education quality, project relevance, and formatting, differing *only* in age indicators.
*   **Pair A (Younger):** Graduated 2021, 3 years of experience.
*   **Pair B (Older):** Graduated 1995, 29 years of experience.
*   **Control:** Ensure both list the same current technical stack (e.g., Python, React) to prevent the system from penalizing "outdated" skills rather than age itself.
*   **Volume:** Generate at least 20 such pairs across different technical roles (e.g., Frontend, DevOps, Data Science) to ensure statistical relevance.

**2. Execute Blind Scoring Runs**
Process these synthetic profiles through your system in isolated batches. Do not mix them with real data yet.
*   Record the raw score, ranking position, and any automated rejection reasons for each candidate.
*   **Key Metric:** Calculate the "Age Delta" for each pair (Score of Younger Candidate minus Score of Older Candidate).
*   If the system is neutral, the delta should hover near zero. A consistent positive delta indicates a bias against older workers.

**3. Analyze Specific Triggers**
If a discrepancy is found, drill down into the specific fields causing the variance:
*   **Graduation Date:** Run a test where the graduation year is removed entirely. If the score equalizes, the date field is a direct negative weight.
*   **Years of Experience:** Test if the system caps the value of experience (e.g., treating 15 years the same as 5) or actively penalizes exceeding a threshold (e.g., >20 years).
*   **Keyword Correlation:** Check if the system associates older graduation dates with outdated keywords not explicitly present in the resume text (hallucination bias).

**4. Establish a Human-in-the-Loop Threshold**
Until the automated module is restored, mandate a manual review for any candidate whose score falls within a "borderline" range (e.g., the bottom 20% of qualified applicants). Specifically flag any rejection where the primary driver appears to be tenure length or graduation era rather than skill mismatch.

**5. Documentation and Remediation**
Log all findings in a bias audit register. If the manual review confirms systematic disadvantage:
*   Immediately disable the specific weighting factors for "Graduation Year" and "Total Years of Experience" in the configuration.
*   Retest with the synthetic pairs to confirm the delta has normalized.
*   Do not activate the system for client use until the Age Delta across all test pairs is statistically insignificant.

This manual "red team" approach allows you to validate fairness constraints empirically without relying on the unavailable automated alerts.

*Note: Ensure all synthetic data is clearly marked in your database to prevent accidental inclusion in live client reports.*
*Once the bias module is back online, re-run these same synthetic pairs to calibrate the automated alerts.*
