### Pre-Launch Bias Review Process for NileRide Safety Scorer

**1. Structured Simulation Approach**
Since diverse demographic datasets are unavailable, you must construct synthetic passenger behavior profiles to stress-test the scoring algorithm. Create distinct "passenger personas" that represent extreme but plausible behavioral patterns rather than demographic groups directly. These personas should include:
*   **The "Strict Perfectionist":** Consistently rates 4 stars for flawless trips, reserving 5 stars for impossible perfection.
*   **The "Mood-Volatile Rider":** Ratings fluctuate wildly based on external factors (traffic, weather) unrelated to driver performance.
*   **The "Retaliation Prone":** Systematically rates 1 star following necessary policy enforcements (e.g., refusing extra stops).
*   **The "New User Anomaly":** Exhibits erratic rating patterns typical of users unfamiliar with the interface.

Run the scoring model using trip logs where the *only* variable is the assignment of these personas to identical driver performance records. Track how quickly a driver reaches the ban threshold under each persona's feedback stream compared to a "neutral" baseline. Specifically, measure the "time-to-ban" variance. If a driver is banned significantly faster under the "Strict Perfectionist" persona than the "Neutral" persona despite identical driving metrics, your model lacks sufficient noise tolerance or outlier rejection.

**2. Manual Review Trigger Checklist**
Implement an automated "hold" status instead of immediate permanent bans when any of the following conditions are met. These cases require human intervention before the driver is deactivated:
*   **Velocity Spike:** The driver receives three or more low ratings (≤2 stars) within a 24-hour window. This suggests a coordinated attack or a specific localized event rather than a pattern of poor performance.
*   **Outlier Deviation:** A single rating drops the driver's average by more than 15% compared to their 30-day rolling average.
*   **Text-Score Mismatch:** Natural language processing flags positive keywords (e.g., "safe," "polite," "clean") in written feedback, yet the numerical rating is critical (≤2 stars).
*   **Route/Time Clustering:** Low ratings originate exclusively from a single geographic zone or a specific time block (e.g., Friday nights in District X), indicating potential regional bias or event-based anomalies.
*   **New Driver Penalty:** The ban threshold is reached within the driver's first 20 trips, where statistical variance is naturally highest.
*   **Reciprocal Disputes:** The low rating coincides with a formal dispute filed by the driver regarding that specific trip.

**3. Data Aggregation Safeguards**
Before finalizing the model, apply a "trimming" logic to the input data. Automatically discard the top and bottom 5% of ratings for any driver with more than 50 trips before calculating the final score. This reduces the impact of statistical outliers without needing external demographic data. Additionally, ensure the model weights recent trips higher but maintains a "floor" based on long-term history so that a sudden burst of biased feedback cannot immediately overwrite months of verified safe operation.

**Note:** Ensure your engineering team documents the specific thresholds used for these triggers to allow for rapid adjustment post-launch. This simulation process should be repeated weekly until the holiday rush begins to catch any regression in model behavior.
