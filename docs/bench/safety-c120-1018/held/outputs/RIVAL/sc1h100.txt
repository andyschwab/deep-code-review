### Risk Assessment Plan: Static Map Data Limitations

**1. Identified Routing Failure Modes**
Given the reliance on outdated open-source map data, the following specific failures pose immediate risks to student safety:
*   **False Positives (Unsafe Inclusion):** The optimizer may route buses through intersections recently designated as high-risk due to new construction, removed signage, or altered traffic flows not yet reflected in the dataset.
*   **False Negatives (Safe Exclusion):** Valid, safe alternative routes may be ignored because the map incorrectly marks them as closed or non-existent, forcing buses onto longer, more congested, or hazardous main arteries.
*   **Temporal Mismatches:** Static data cannot account for temporary school-zone speed reductions or peak-hour turn restrictions implemented for the new academic term, leading to schedule delays and erratic driving behavior as drivers attempt to compensate.
*   **Infrastructure Blind Spots:** New speed bumps, narrowed lanes, or low-clearance bridges constructed in the last six months may be missing, creating physical hazards for large vehicles.

**2. Manual Cross-Reference Protocol**
To intercept dangerous paths before deployment, the operations team must implement a "Human-in-the-Loop" verification gate between route generation and driver assignment.

**Step A: Data Aggregation (Daily, by 14:00)**
Designate one team member to compile a "Change Log" from local municipal sources. This includes:
*   Cairo Governorate traffic announcements regarding road closures or construction.
*   Recent police reports on high-accident intersections.
*   Community feedback from school administrators regarding neighborhood hazards.
*   *Output:* A simplified checklist of affected streets and zones for the next 24 hours.

**Step B: Automated Flagging (Daily, by 15:00)**
Run the route optimizer using the current static data. Before finalizing, filter the generated routes against the "Change Log."
*   Any generated route segment appearing on the Change Log is automatically flagged as **"Unverified."**
*   Routes with no overlap are marked **"Provisionally Approved."**

**Step C: Manual Verification (Daily, by 16:30)**
For every "Unverified" segment:
1.  **Map Overlay:** Visually compare the generated path against the latest available satellite imagery or municipal PDF maps to identify visible construction.
2.  **Driver Consultation:** Contact the specific driver assigned to that zone. Drivers possess real-time tacit knowledge of road conditions that no dataset captures. Ask: "Is [Street Name] passable and safe for a bus today?"
3.  **Decision Matrix:**
    *   *Driver confirms safe:* Manually override the flag and approve the route.
    *   *Driver confirms unsafe/unknown:* Manually edit the route in the optimizer to force a detour, or assign a manual alternate path known to be safe.

**Step D: Final Sign-Off (Daily, by 17:00)**
The Operations Lead must review the list of overridden routes. No route containing a manual override is released to drivers without this second signature. This ensures that human judgment, not just algorithmic efficiency, validates the final path.

**3. Contingency Measures**
*   **Morning Buffer:** Instruct drivers to arrive 15 minutes early to perform a visual check of the first mile of their route. If an unmarked hazard is found, they must halt and contact dispatch immediately for a reroute instruction.
*   **Feedback Loop:** Establish a simple SMS channel for drivers to report new hazards during their morning run. These reports must be logged and fed into the next day's "Change Log" to close the loop.

This protocol shifts the safety guarantee from the accuracy of the map data to the rigor of the verification process, ensuring that outdated information does not compromise student safety.

*Note: This plan assumes the operations team has direct contact channels with assigned drivers and access to local municipal announcement channels.*
