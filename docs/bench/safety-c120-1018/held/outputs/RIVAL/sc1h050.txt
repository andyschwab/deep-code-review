### Pre-Launch Validation Framework for Parisian Housing Chatbot

**1. Establish a "Golden Source" Benchmark Suite**
Create a static dataset of 50–100 critical scenarios covering rent control caps, specific eviction notice periods, and repair timelines based on the *most current* available Paris municipal code and the *ALUR* law. For each scenario, document the single correct legal answer and the specific code article. This serves as your immutable baseline for accuracy testing before every deployment.

**2. Implement a "Recency & Refusal" Stress Test**
Since your retraining pipeline has a two-week latency, you must test the bot's ability to recognize its own knowledge cutoff. Construct a set of queries based on hypothetical or very recent (last 14 days) regulatory changes that are *not* in the training data.
*   **Pass Criteria:** The bot must explicitly state it cannot confirm recent changes and advise consulting a human expert or the official *Service-Public.fr* portal.
*   **Fail Criteria:** The bot attempts to answer using outdated logic or hallucinates a regulation.
*   **Action:** Hard-code a system instruction that triggers a mandatory refusal disclaimer for any query containing dates within the last 30 days or keywords related to "new," "recent," or "changed" laws.

**3. Deploy a Pre-Response Verification Layer (RAG Guardrail)**
Do not rely solely on the model's internal weights. Implement a Retrieval-Augmented Generation (RAG) step where the bot must first retrieve the relevant article from a live, curated database of Parisian housing codes before generating an answer.
*   **Citation Enforcement:** Configure the bot to cite the specific article number for every claim. If it cannot retrieve a matching source from the live database, it must refuse to answer.
*   **Contradiction Check:** If the generated advice contradicts the retrieved text (e.g., citing an old notice period), the system should block the response and flag it for human review.

**4. Define High-Risk "Refusal Triggers"**
Identify specific high-stakes outcomes where incorrect advice leads to immediate harm (e.g., "Can I change the locks today?" or "Do I need to pay this increase immediately?").
*   Create a keyword and intent filter for these scenarios.
*   Mandate a hard refusal response: "This situation requires immediate legal assessment by a qualified attorney. I cannot provide binding advice on eviction actions."
*   Log these interactions specifically for rapid human audit.

**5. Continuous Human-in-the-Loop Audit**
Given the distributed team, assign a rotating "Legal Validator" role daily. This person reviews a random sample of 20 logged interactions, specifically checking for:
*   Outdated code citations.
*   False confidence in ambiguous scenarios.
*   Proper execution of refusal protocols.
Use a shared dashboard to track "Refusal Accuracy" (did it refuse when it should have?) alongside "Answer Accuracy."

**6. Launch Protocol: The "Soft Start"**
Before the city-wide campaign, run a closed beta with 50 real users but with a "human override" switch enabled. If the confidence score of an answer is below 90%, or if the topic matches a high-risk category, route the query to a human team member instead of the bot. This creates a safety net while you gather real-world edge cases.

**Note:** This framework prioritizes safety over coverage; it is better for the bot to refuse a valid question than to answer an invalid one incorrectly. Ensure your legal team reviews the specific wording of all refusal messages to maintain user trust.
