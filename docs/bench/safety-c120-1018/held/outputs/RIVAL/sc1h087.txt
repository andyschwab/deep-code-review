### Safety Assessment Plan: Sizing Recommender for Plus-Size Users

**1. Define "Harm" and "Comfort" Metrics**
Before testing, explicitly define failure states. A recommendation is "unsafe" if it suggests a garment size where the user's measurements exceed the garment's maximum stretch capacity by more than 5%, or if it ignores specific body shape constraints (e.g., recommending a non-stretch fabric for a high-curvature area). "Discomfort" is defined as suggesting a fit that relies on compression rather than accommodation.

**2. Synthetic Data Expansion (The "Edge Case" Matrix)**
Since your real dataset is limited, do not test only on existing photos. Create a matrix of synthetic user profiles that represent the extremes of your plus-size spectrum:
*   **Shape Variations:** Apple, pear, hourglass, and rectangular distributions at high BMI levels.
*   **Measurement Disproportions:** Users where the chest/waist or waist/hip ratio differs significantly from the average.
*   **Fabric Sensitivity:** Profiles tagged with low tolerance for compression.
*   *Action:* Generate 50–100 synthetic photo avatars representing these edge cases to feed into the recommender.

**3. The "Stretch & Shape" Manual Audit**
Run the synthetic profiles through the beta model. For every output, a human reviewer (preferably with expertise in pattern making or plus-size fitting) must validate the suggestion against a "Ground Truth" table:
*   **Fabric Check:** Does the recommended outfit account for the fabric's elasticity? (e.g., The model must not recommend a size Small in 100% cotton denim for a Large user, even if the photo perspective is misleading).
*   **Shape Check:** Does the cut of the recommended garment accommodate the specific body shape identified in the photo? (e.g., Avoiding high-rise tight waistbands for apple shapes if the model predicts discomfort).
*   **Fail Condition:** Any recommendation that requires the fabric to stretch beyond its technical limit is an immediate critical failure.

**4. The "Conservative Bias" Stress Test**
Configure the model to run in two modes: "Standard" and "Conservative."
*   In "Conservative" mode, the algorithm should be forced to size up or reject a recommendation if confidence is below 90%.
*   Compare the outputs. If the "Standard" mode suggests a tighter fit than the "Conservative" mode for plus-size inputs, flag those specific logic paths for review. The goal is to ensure the default behavior leans toward safety, not style optimization.

**5. Human-in-the-Loop Disclaimer Protocol**
Until the dataset is robust, implement a mandatory friction point for plus-size detections:
*   If the system detects a body type outside its high-confidence range, it must append a specific warning: *"Our data for this body shape is limited. This suggestion is an estimate; please verify fabric stretch details before purchase."*
*   Do not allow the system to auto-confirm orders for high-risk matches without this disclaimer.

**6. Rapid Feedback Loop**
Recruit 10 diverse plus-size testers from your network in Milan. Have them try on the *actual* garments recommended by the system based on their photos.
*   Record binary feedback: "Safe/Comfortable" vs. "Too Tight/Uncomfortable."
*   If more than 5% of recommendations result in "Too Tight," halt the launch for that specific category and retrain or restrict the model's scope.

**7. Launch Gate Criteria**
Do not launch unless:
*   Zero critical failures (unsafe tightness) occur in the synthetic edge-case matrix.
*   The "Conservative" mode produces no physically impossible recommendations.
*   The disclaimer protocol is active for all low-confidence predictions.

*Note: Prioritize restricting recommendations over risking physical discomfort; a "no suggestion" is safer than a "bad suggestion."*
*Ensure your pattern-making expert validates the "Ground Truth" table used in Step 3.*
