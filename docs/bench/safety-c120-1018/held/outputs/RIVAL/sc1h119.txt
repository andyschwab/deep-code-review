### Rollout Strategy: Himalaya SafeTrek Beta

**1. Mandatory User Warnings (Data Latency & Uncertainty)**
Given the reliance on cached data in low-connectivity zones, every user interface screen must display a persistent, high-visibility status banner. Do not bury this in settings.
*   **Visual Indicator:** Use a color-coded "Data Freshness" badge (e.g., Green: <1 hr, Yellow: 1–4 hrs, Red: >4 hrs).
*   **Explicit Warning Text:** "Warning: This map uses cached data last updated [Timestamp]. Terrain conditions may have changed. Do not rely solely on this app for life-safety decisions."
*   **Offline Mode Alert:** When connectivity is lost, trigger a modal pop-up: "You are now offline. Risk assessments are based on historical data and may not reflect current landslide activity."

**2. Conservative Safety Posture (Default Conditions)**
To mitigate the risk of missing new hazards (false negatives), the system must default to a "Conservative Safety Mode" under the following specific conditions:
*   **Stale Data Threshold:** If cached data is older than 4 hours, automatically reclassify all "Low Risk" zones as "Moderate/Unknown Risk."
*   **Recent Seismic or Weather Events:** If the last known data precedes a recorded earthquake (>2.5 magnitude) or heavy rainfall event (>50mm/hr) in the region, flag the entire affected area as "High Risk" regardless of previous low-risk predictions.
*   **Confidence Score Drop:** If the prediction model's confidence score falls below 60% due to missing sensor inputs, default the output to "Caution Advised" rather than "Safe."
*   **User-Reported Anomalies:** If any user in the vicinity reports a landslide or debris flow via the app, immediately elevate the risk level for that grid sector and its immediate neighbors to "High Risk" for all users, even if their cached data suggests otherwise.

**3. Beta Rollout Execution Plan**
*   **Phase 1: Internal Field Validation (Weeks 1–2):** The five-engineer team must deploy to distinct geographic zones with varying connectivity. Engineers will physically verify "High Risk" flags against ground truth to tune the false-positive rate, ensuring panic is minimized while maintaining safety.
*   **Phase 2: Controlled Community Pilot (Weeks 3–6):** Release to a closed group of 50 local guides and experienced hikers. Require mandatory acknowledgment of the data latency warning upon first launch. Collect feedback specifically on whether the "Conservative Mode" triggers too frequently or causes route paralysis.
*   **Phase 3: Public Beta with "Safe Mode" Lock:** For the general public release, lock the system so that users cannot disable the conservative defaults. Provide a clear "Report Hazard" button to crowdsource real-time updates that bypass the cache latency for critical alerts.

**4. Incident Response Protocol**
Establish a rapid-response channel where field reports of false negatives (missed risks) trigger an immediate manual override of the cached data for that region, pushing a high-priority update packet to any device that reconnects, however briefly.

*Note: This strategy prioritizes false positives (unnecessary caution) over false negatives (missed danger) to align with life-safety ethics.*
*Ensure all beta testers sign a waiver acknowledging the experimental nature of the cached data.*
