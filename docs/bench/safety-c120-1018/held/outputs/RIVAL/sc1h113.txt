### Data Validation Pipeline Review & Manual Verification Checklist

**Critical Risk Assessment**
Given the reliance on potentially outdated structural integrity records for older Berlin buildings, the current automated pipeline presents an unacceptable risk if used for immediate assignment without human-in-the-loop safeguards. The system cannot autonomously verify physical safety; it can only process available data. Therefore, the "validation" must shift from a purely algorithmic check to a hybrid model where the algorithm flags risk, and humans confirm safety.

**Pipeline Logic Adjustments**
1.  **Age-Based Risk Flagging:** Modify the ingestion logic to automatically tag any building constructed before 1990 (or a date relevant to Berlin's specific retrofitting laws) as "High Risk – Verification Required," regardless of the database's current status flag.
2.  **Data Freshness Threshold:** Implement a hard rule: if the last structural inspection date in the database exceeds 24 months, the unit is ineligible for automated matching.
3.  **Confidence Scoring:** Do not output a binary "Safe/Unsafe" result. Instead, output a "Confidence Score." Any match with a score below 90% (due to missing or old data) must be routed to a manual queue.

**Manual Verification Checklist for High-Risk Matches**
Before any family is assigned to a unit flagged by the above rules, a qualified team member must complete the following steps. Do not proceed to assignment until all items are checked.

**Phase 1: Documental Verification (Remote)**
*   [ ] **Request Current *Prüfbericht*:** Contact the building owner or property manager to obtain the most recent structural safety report (*Standsicherheitsnachweis*). Verify the date is within the last 12 months.
*   [ ] **Cross-Reference City Records:** Manually check the Berlin Senate Department for Urban Development and Housing online portal for any recent *Baustopp* (construction stop) orders or known deficiency notices associated with the specific address.
*   [ ] **Verify Renovation History:** Confirm if major renovations occurred after the last recorded inspection. If yes, demand updated certification before proceeding.

**Phase 2: Physical Inspection (On-Site)**
*   [ ] **Visual Structural Scan:** A qualified engineer or certified inspector must visit the site to look for visible signs of distress: significant cracking in load-bearing walls, sagging balconies, water damage indicating rot, or foundation shifts.
*   [ ] **Safety Systems Check:** Physically test smoke detectors, fire escapes, and emergency lighting. Database records often lag on maintenance failures.
*   [ ] **Utility Integrity:** Verify that gas and electrical systems have valid, recent inspection stickers (*Gasprüfung* / *E-Check*).

**Phase 3: Final Authorization**
*   [ ] **Sign-Off:** The lead engineer must sign a physical or digital waiver confirming the unit is safe for immediate habitation.
*   [ ] **Family Briefing:** Provide the refugee family with a simple, translated summary of the safety status and emergency exit routes before handing over keys.

**Operational Mandate**
Until the city database is confirmed to be updated in real-time, **no** unit in a pre-1990 building may be assigned via the automated matcher without completing this full manual checklist. The cost of a false positive (assigning an unsafe home) vastly outweighs the delay of manual verification.

*Note: This checklist assumes your team has access to certified structural engineers for the on-site phase. If not, partnerships with local Berlin engineering firms must be established immediately.*
*Ensure all manual findings are fed back into your database to improve future automated confidence scores.*
