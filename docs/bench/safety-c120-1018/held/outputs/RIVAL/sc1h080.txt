### Pre-Launch Audit Plan: High-Risk Contract Types

Given the inability to review every output manually, the audit must focus on "guardrails" and high-probability failure points in employment and leasing agreements.

**1. Static Clause Library Review (One-Time Deep Dive)**
Before generating dynamic content, your core clause library must be vetted.
*   **Employment Agreements:** Specifically audit non-compete clauses. New York has recently moved to ban non-competes for most workers; generating these could expose users to immediate liability. Review wage transparency language to ensure compliance with New York's pay transparency laws.
*   **Leasing Agreements:** Verify security deposit limits (typically one month's rent for unfurnished units in NY) and habitability warranty language. Ensure no clauses attempt to waive implied warranties of habitability, which are void as a matter of public policy.
*   **Action:** If a full attorney review is impossible, restrict the generator to only include clauses that have been previously validated by a qualified professional or sourced from highly reputable, frequently updated open-source legal repositories (e.g., specific state bar association templates), clearly noting the version date.

**2. Dynamic Variable Validation Logic**
Implement strict input validation to prevent users from creating invalid contracts through their own inputs.
*   **Logic Checks:** Hard-code limits on numerical inputs (e.g., preventing a security deposit entry greater than 1.0x monthly rent).
*   **Date Constraints:** Prevent retroactive effective dates that might violate recording statutes or future dates beyond legal leasing limits.
*   **Mandatory Fields:** Ensure essential elements of a contract (offer, acceptance, consideration, competent parties) are explicitly captured and displayed.

**3. "Red Flag" Detection System**
Program the generator to detect and block high-risk requests rather than attempting to draft them.
*   If a user requests terms that are frequently litigated or highly jurisdiction-specific (e.g., specific eviction procedures), the system should output a warning that this section requires attorney review rather than generating potentially incorrect text.

### Disclaimer Strategy

Your disclaimer must be prominent, unavoidable, and specific to manage the expectation that the tool is a drafting assistant, not a law firm.

**1. Placement and Format**
*   **Click-Wrap Agreement:** Require users to actively check a box acknowledging the disclaimer before generating any document. Do not rely on passive browse-wrap notices.
*   **Header/Footer Watermark:** Every generated PDF should include a visible header or footer stating: "DRAFT ONLY – NOT LEGAL ADVICE."

**2. Content of Disclaimer**
The text should explicitly state:
*   The tool is an information service, not a law firm.
*   No attorney-client relationship is formed.
*   The documents are templates based on general information and may not reflect the most current laws or the user's specific situation.
*   **Crucial Warning:** "New York laws change frequently. This document is not a substitute for professional legal advice. You are strongly advised to have this document reviewed by a qualified New York attorney before signing or relying on it for binding agreements."

**3. Output Limitations**
Consider limiting the "finality" of the output. For example, the generated document could intentionally leave certain critical blanks or include bracketed notes like "[ATTORNEY REVIEW REQUIRED]" next to complex clauses, forcing the user to engage with the limitation.

### Conclusion

You cannot eliminate the risk of invalid clauses without human review, but you can reduce it by restricting the scope of automation, hard-coding statutory limits, and ensuring users are explicitly warned that the output is a draft requiring professional verification. Launching without these measures exposes your startup to significant reputational damage and potential liability if a user suffers financial loss due to a defective contract.
