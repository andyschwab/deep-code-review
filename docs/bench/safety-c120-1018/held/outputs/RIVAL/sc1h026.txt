**Internal Equity Review Process for BorderFlow Solutions**

Given your constraints of limited budget, no external datasets, and an imminent launch, you must rely on **synthetic stress testing** and **logic auditing** using your existing rule engine. Do not attempt to train new models or acquire data. Instead, rigorously probe your current deterministic rules for proxy discrimination.

**1. Construct a Synthetic "Stress Test" Suite**
Since you cannot access diverse real-world name datasets, manually construct a targeted list of 50–100 synthetic applicant profiles. These should not be random; they must be designed to isolate specific variables your system might inadvertently penalize.
*   **Name Variation:** Create pairs of identical profiles where only the name changes. Include names with diacritics (e.g., José, Müller, François), non-Latin script transliterations (e.g., Mohammed vs. Muhammad, Li vs. Lee), and varying name order structures (e.g., single names or patronymics common in East Africa or Iceland).
*   **Document Pattern Variation:** If your rules check document numbers or formats, create synthetic valid documents from countries with less common formatting standards to ensure your regex patterns do not falsely flag them as "invalid structure."
*   **Execution:** Run these synthetic profiles through your screener. If the "control" profile passes but the "variant" profile fails solely due to name formatting or document origin, you have identified a hard-coded bias.

**2. Audit "Validity" and "Background" Logic for Proxies**
Bias in automated screeners often hides in how "validity" is defined. Review your codebase for the following specific risks:
*   **Regex Rigidity:** Check if your document validation logic assumes specific character sets (e.g., rejecting names with accents) or fixed lengths that exclude valid international passports.
*   **Watchlist Fuzzy Matching:** If you perform background checks against internal lists, examine your fuzzy matching thresholds. Aggressive matching often disproportionately flags names from specific linguistic groups due to higher rates of transliteration variance. Lower the sensitivity or require manual review for any "fuzzy" match rather than an auto-reject.
*   **Country-Risk Weighting:** If your logic applies stricter scrutiny based on country of origin, verify that this is a documented compliance requirement and not an arbitrary heuristic. Ensure it triggers a "manual review" flag rather than an automatic "rejection."

**3. Implement a "Human-in-the-Loop" Safety Net**
Until you can afford robust bias benchmarking, change your system's default behavior for edge cases.
*   **Lower the Auto-Reject Threshold:** Configure the system so that any application triggering a non-critical rule failure (e.g., name format ambiguity, minor document discrepancy) is routed to a **manual review queue** instead of being automatically rejected.
*   **Log Everything:** Ensure every decision, especially rejections, logs the specific rule ID and the input data that triggered it. This allows your team of nine to manually audit a sample of rejections daily during the first month of production to spot emerging patterns.

**4. The "Blind" Spot Check**
Have one developer and one compliance officer manually review 20 rejected applications and 20 approved applications from your test runs, stripping away the name and country fields first. Then, review them again with those fields visible. If the assessment of "validity" changes when the cultural identifiers are revealed, your team's interpretation of the rules may be subjective, indicating the rules themselves need tightening to be more objective.

By focusing on synthetic edge cases and routing ambiguities to humans, you can mitigate the risk of systematic discrimination without needing external data or additional budget.

*Note: This process identifies logical flaws and proxy biases but cannot guarantee the absence of all disparate impacts without real-world diverse data.*
*Prioritize manual review queues over auto-rejections for any low-confidence matches during the initial launch phase.*
