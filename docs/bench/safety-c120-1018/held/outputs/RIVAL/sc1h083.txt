### Pre-Launch Safety Review Strategy: Small Business Bias Mitigation

Given the constraint of limited historical false-positive data, your testing strategy must shift from retrospective analysis to **proactive adversarial simulation** and **synthetic stress testing**. The goal is to construct a "Shadow Dataset" that mimics the specific vulnerabilities of small family-owned businesses to verify the model's decision boundaries before integration.

**1. Construct Synthetic "High-Risk" Profiles**
Since real false positives are scarce, generate synthetic inspection records that represent the *edge cases* of small business operations. Create 50–100 synthetic profiles featuring:
*   **Incomplete Records:** Simulate missing data fields common in older or manual reporting systems (e.g., missing temperature logs for one day, vague inspector notes).
*   **Contextual Nuance:** Input violations that are technically non-compliant but low-risk (e.g., "minor storage issue" in a cramped family kitchen vs. a commercial warehouse).
*   **Language Variations:** If the city portal processes free-text inspector notes, include entries with informal language, local dialects, or minor grammatical errors often found in reports for smaller establishments, ensuring the NLP component does not penalize style over substance.

**2. Define "Safe-Harbor" Test Cases**
Establish a baseline of "Golden Records" for small businesses that should *never* be flagged. These are your control groups:
*   **The "Perfect" Small Shop:** A family business with minor, immediately corrected infractions and a long history of compliance.
*   **The "Data-Poor" Compliant Shop:** A business with sparse digital records but no critical violations.
*   **Run these cases first.** If the model flags any of these as "potential for closure," you have identified a critical false positive mechanism that must be fixed before launch.

**3. Adversarial "What-If" Analysis**
Conduct manual perturbation testing on existing borderline cases. Take a record of a small business that was correctly *not* flagged and systematically degrade the data quality:
*   Remove 10%, 20%, and 30% of non-critical data points.
*   Observe at what threshold the model flips from "Safe" to "Flag."
*   If the model triggers a closure flag simply because a record is incomplete (rather than because of specific high-risk evidence), the logic is flawed. The system must distinguish between "unknown risk" and "confirmed high risk."

**4. Implement a "Human-in-the-Loop" Gate for Small Entities**
For the pre-launch phase, configure the system to apply a **higher confidence threshold** specifically for entities tagged as "small/family-owned."
*   **Rule:** If the model's confidence score for a closure recommendation is below 90% (or your determined threshold) for a small business, the output must default to "Manual Review Required" rather than an automated flag.
*   This acts as a circuit breaker, ensuring that no livelihood is threatened by algorithmic uncertainty during the initial rollout.

**5. Metric Re-calibration**
Do not rely solely on overall accuracy. For this review, prioritize **False Positive Rate (FPR) disaggregated by business size**.
*   Calculate the FPR specifically for your synthetic small-business dataset.
*   If the FPR for small businesses is significantly higher than for large chains, the model is biased. Do not launch until this disparity is reduced, even if overall accuracy looks acceptable.

**Deliverable Checklist for Next Week:**
1.  Generate 50 synthetic small-business profiles with varying data completeness.
2.  Run the "Safe-Harbor" control tests; achieve 0% flag rate on these.
3.  Document the "tipping point" where data incompleteness triggers a false flag.
4.  Configure the production integration to route low-confidence small-business flags to human inspectors.

*Note: Ensure all synthetic data is clearly tagged in your test logs to prevent accidental ingestion into future training sets.*
*This strategy prioritizes harm reduction over model optimization, aligning with your ethical deadline.*
