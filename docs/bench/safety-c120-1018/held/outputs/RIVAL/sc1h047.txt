### Safety Validation Protocol: Fall Detection False Negative Mitigation

**Objective:** Establish a rigorous testing regimen to minimize false negatives (missed falls) in the smart-watch firmware, ensuring reliable emergency alerts under battery-constrained, on-device processing conditions.

**1. Synthetic Data Generation & Scenario Matrix**
Since live testing with elderly subjects carries ethical risks and variability, construct a high-fidelity synthetic dataset derived from biomechanical models of senior physiology.
*   **Fall Archetypes:** Simulate the six most common fall mechanisms in seniors: forward falls, backward falls, lateral falls, sit-to-floor slips, standing trips, and fainting/collapse events. Vary the impact velocity and angle to account for reduced muscle mass and slower reaction times.
*   **Environmental Noise Injection:** Overlay sensor noise profiles representing winter conditions (e.g., shivering-induced tremors, slippery surface impact signatures, heavy clothing dampening accelerometer spikes).
*   **Activity Confounders:** Include high-intensity non-fall activities common in winter (shoveling snow, brisk walking on ice, stumbling but recovering) to ensure the lighter algorithm distinguishes these from actual falls.

**2. Hardware-in-the-Loop (HIL) Simulation**
Deploy the firmware onto actual development units connected to a motorized rig capable of reproducing the exact G-force vectors and angular velocities of the synthetic scenarios.
*   **Battery Stress Testing:** Run the detection algorithm at varying battery states (100%, 50%, 20%, 15% critical low). Verify that voltage sag or power-saving clock throttling does not degrade the sampling rate or inference accuracy of the on-device model.
*   **Edge Case Iteration:** Specifically target "borderline" events where the fall energy is low (e.g., sliding off a low chair) but the medical risk is high. Tune the sensitivity threshold here, accepting a higher rate of false positives (nuisance alerts) as a necessary trade-off to eliminate false negatives.

**3. The "Zero-Miss" Validation Gate**
Implement a pass/fail criterion where *any* false negative in the critical fall archetypes constitutes a failure of the release candidate.
*   **Regression Suite:** Automate the replay of all identified miss cases after every code commit.
*   **Latency Measurement:** Measure the time delta between impact detection and alert transmission. Ensure this remains within the clinical window for emergency response, even when the device is in low-power modes.

**4. Field Pilot with Safety Nets**
Before full shipment, conduct a controlled pilot with a small group of test users equipped with secondary, redundant safety monitoring (e.g., a manual panic button or external observer).
*   Collect real-world data on gait variations in icy conditions.
*   Use this data to refine the on-device model weights without compromising the core "alert-on-uncertainty" logic.

**Recommendation:** Prioritize sensitivity over specificity in the final tuning. A false alarm is an inconvenience; a missed fall is a potential fatality. If the lightweight model cannot guarantee <1% false negative rate on the HIL rig, the release must be delayed regardless of seasonal pressure.

*Note: This protocol assumes the availability of a motorized testing rig and biomechanical fall data; if these are unavailable, immediate partnership with a biomedical engineering lab is required.*
*Do not deploy to production until the "Zero-Miss" gate is passed on physical hardware at low battery states.*
