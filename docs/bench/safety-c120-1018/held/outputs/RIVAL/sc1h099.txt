### Ethical Guardrails for Funeral-Cost Estimator

**1. Core Pricing Principles**
*   **Compassion Over Commerce:** Explicitly disable all "dynamic surge" logic triggered by high demand, time-of-day peaks, or inventory scarcity. The algorithm must treat funeral services as essential humanitarian aid, not retail commodities.
*   **Transparent Baseline:** Establish a fixed "Standard Care Price" for each package based strictly on actual operational costs plus a modest, fixed margin. This baseline must be displayed prominently before any optional add-ons.

**2. Mandatory Caps and Rules**
*   **Absolute Price Ceiling:** Implement a hard cap where the final quote cannot exceed 110% of the Standard Care Price, regardless of any remaining algorithmic variables.
*   **Zero Surge Multiplier:** Force the demand-multiplier variable in your code to `1.0` at all times. Remove any code paths that allow this value to rise above 1.0.
*   **Inventory Decoupling:** Ensure that low inventory of specific caskets or slots does not trigger price increases for alternative options. If an item is unavailable, the system should suggest the nearest equivalent at the same price tier, not a higher one.
*   **Cooling-Off Period:** Program the estimator to hold a generated quote constant for 24 hours once viewed by a user, preventing price changes if they return to the site while grieving.

**3. Immediate Implementation Steps**
*   **Code Freeze on Logic:** Isolate the pricing function. Comment out or delete any lines referencing `demand_factor`, `urgency_score`, or `scarcity_premium`.
*   **Hardcoded Overrides:** Insert a post-calculation check: `if (final_price > max_cap) { final_price = max_cap; }`.
*   **Audit Log:** Enable logging that records the "theoretical dynamic price" versus the "capped ethical price" for every request. This creates an audit trail proving the caps are active.

**4. Validation Testing Procedure**
Since you cannot run live network tests yet, perform the following offline validation using your historical data:

*   **Step A: Data Preparation:** Extract a dataset of historical requests including timestamps, demand levels, and inventory states from your retail algorithm's logs.
*   **Step B: Shadow Simulation:** Run this historical data through your *new* capped algorithm in a local sandbox environment.
*   **Step C: Comparative Analysis:** Generate a report comparing the original retail outputs against the new ethical outputs.
    *   *Pass Criterion:* There must be zero instances where the new price exceeds the defined cap.
    *   *Stress Test:* Specifically filter for historical "peak demand" periods. Verify that prices during these spikes remain identical to low-demand periods.
*   **Step D: Manual Review:** Have two team members manually review 50 random quotes from the simulation, specifically looking for any edge cases where "add-ons" might inadvertently bypass the base cap.

**Note:** Given the sensitivity of your user base, consider adding a human review step for any quote that exceeds a certain threshold before it is presented to the family.
Ensure your legal team reviews these caps to confirm they align with local consumer protection laws regarding essential services.
