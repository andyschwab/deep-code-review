To audit your health insurance chatbot for bias against high-cost procedures and ensure patient safety overrides cost-saving objectives, implement the following evaluation framework:

**1. Establish Independent Clinical Ground Truth**
Before testing the bot, curate a "Golden Dataset" of 50–100 clinical scenarios derived strictly from independent medical guidelines (e.g., USPSTF, CDC, or specialty society guidelines), not your internal training data.
*   **Composition:** Include cases where high-cost procedures (e.g., MRIs, specialist referrals, genetic testing) are explicitly recommended, cases where they are contraindicated, and ambiguous "gray area" cases.
*   **Labeling:** Tag each scenario with the correct clinical action (Approve, Deny, Refer) and the specific guideline citation. This dataset serves as the unbiased benchmark for all audits.

**2. Design Targeted Adversarial Test Cases**
Create specific prompts designed to trigger the bot's cost-saving biases. Do not rely on random queries; construct inputs that pit clinical necessity against financial friction.
*   **High-Cost Triggers:** Scenarios involving expensive diagnostics (e.g., "I have persistent neurological symptoms; do I need an MRI?") where guidelines support the test but costs are high.
*   **Preventative Care Pressure:** Queries about preventative screenings for high-risk demographics where early detection is critical but often deferred in cost-cutting models.
*   **Symptom Escalation:** Multi-turn conversations where a user describes worsening symptoms over time, testing if the bot delays necessary escalation to save immediate costs.
*   **Explicit Cost Conflict:** Prompts where the user mentions financial hardship or asks, "Is there a cheaper option?" to see if the bot voluntarily downgrades care against medical advice.

**3. Define Quantitative Safety Metrics**
Move beyond general accuracy to measure specific safety failures. Calculate the following rates against your Golden Dataset:
*   **False Denial Rate (Critical):** The percentage of cases where the bot discourages or denies a procedure that is medically indicated by independent standards. This must be near zero.
*   **Downgrade Frequency:** How often the bot suggests a lower-cost alternative (e.g., generic drug, primary care visit) when the guideline mandates a specific high-cost intervention.
*   **Guideline Citation Accuracy:** The percentage of times the bot's reasoning aligns with the correct independent guideline versus internal cost policies.
*   **Risk Stratification Error:** Instances where high-risk patients are incorrectly triaged to low-acuity pathways.

**4. Implement a "Safety Override" Audit Loop**
Integrate a mandatory human-in-the-loop review for specific triggers before the bot's logic is finalized.
*   **Automated Flagging:** Configure your evaluation harness to automatically flag any response that deviates from the Golden Dataset's recommendation on high-cost items.
*   **Clinical Review Board:** Have licensed clinicians review all flagged "False Denials" and "Downgrades." Their verdict determines if the model is prioritizing savings over health.
*   **Regression Testing:** Before every deployment, re-run the Golden Dataset. Any increase in the False Denial Rate blocks the release, regardless of operational cost projections.

**5. Stress Test Reasoning Transparency**
Require the bot to output its reasoning chain. Audit these chains for "cost-first" language (e.g., "usually not covered," "try conservative treatment first") appearing before clinical assessment. If the reasoning cites cost policies before clinical guidelines in high-risk scenarios, the model is biased.

By anchoring your evaluation to external medical standards rather than internal historical data, you can mathematically prove whether the bot is safe for member exposure.

Note: This framework requires access to current clinical guidelines and licensed medical professionals for the review board. Do not deploy until the False Denial Rate on high-cost procedures is validated at 0%.
