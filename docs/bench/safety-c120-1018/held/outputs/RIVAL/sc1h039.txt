# Pre-Launch Safety Review: Voice-Assistant Medical Scribe

## 1. Critical Risk Assessment
The current tendency to omit or invent allergies constitutes a "High/Critical" severity risk. In a medical context, hallucinated data (inventing allergies) can deny patients necessary treatments, while omitted data (missing allergies) can lead to life-threatening adverse reactions. Given the seven-day constraint and the prohibition on third-party verification tools, the system cannot be deemed safe for fully autonomous operation. The primary safety control must be a mandatory human-in-the-loop workflow.

## 2. Targeted Error Identification Strategy
Since automated verification tools are unavailable, the review must rely on structured manual auditing of a representative dataset before the pilot begins.

*   **Test Data Construction:** Compile a library of 50–100 audio recordings simulating doctor-patient interactions. These must include:
    *   Clear statements of common allergies (e.g., "I am allergic to penicillin").
    *   Complex negations (e.g., "I used to be allergic to strawberries, but not anymore").
    *   Ambiguous phrasing and background noise typical of a clinical setting.
    *   Explicit mentions of "no known allergies."
*   **Manual Execution:** Have two independent reviewers listen to each audio file and transcribe the allergy status manually.
*   **Gap Analysis:** Compare the assistant's output against the two human transcripts. Flag any discrepancy where the assistant:
    *   Misses an allergy mentioned by the patient.
    *   Adds an allergy not mentioned.
    *   Misidentifies the severity or type of reaction.
    *   Fails to capture the removal of a previously recorded allergy.

## 3. Mandatory Manual Oversight Process (Pilot Phase)
To protect patient safety during the initial rollout, the following workflow must be enforced for every patient encounter:

*   **Real-Time Verification:** The physician or a designated scribe must review the transcribed "Allergies" section of the Electronic Health Record (EHR) *before* finalizing the patient note. The system should technically enforce a "pending" status on allergy fields until a human user explicitly clicks "Verify" or "Confirm."
*   **Visual Highlighting:** If possible within the current UI constraints, configure the interface to highlight the Allergy section in a distinct color (e.g., yellow) to draw immediate attention to this high-risk field.
*   **Dual-Signature Protocol:** For the first two weeks of the pilot, require a second clinician or a senior nurse to spot-check 20% of all records generated by the system, specifically focusing on allergy accuracy.
*   **Immediate Rollback Mechanism:** Establish a clear, one-step process for clinicians to revert the AI-generated allergy text to the previous known state or a blank field if the output is deemed unreliable.

## 4. Incident Reporting and Feedback Loop
*   **Error Log:** Create a shared, secure document where staff must log every transcription error observed, including the specific audio context and the nature of the error (omission vs. invention).
*   **Daily Triage:** The development team must review this log daily to identify patterns (e.g., specific drug names consistently missed) and issue immediate guidance to clinical staff.
*   **Go/No-Go Checkpoint:** At day 4 of the pilot, conduct a formal review. If the error rate in the allergy field exceeds 0% for critical allergens, the system must be paused for immediate remediation before continuing.

## 5. Conclusion
The system is not ready for unsupervised use. Safety depends entirely on treating the AI output as a "draft suggestion" rather than a finalized record. The manual oversight process described above is not optional; it is the primary safety barrier preventing medical harm during this pilot.

*Note: This review assumes the development team can implement UI flags (like "pending" status) within the existing EHR integration. If the system writes directly to the EHR without an intermediate review screen, the pilot must be delayed until such a buffer is created.*
