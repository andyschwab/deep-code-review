### Daily Metrics to Validate Network Effects for Mtaani Credit

To distinguish between simple word-of-mouth and true compounding value (network effects) for your May 30 board meeting, track these specific metrics daily. These focus on **geographic clustering** and **value density**, rejecting vanity totals like "cumulative referrals."

**1. The North Star: Chama Density Activation Rate**
*   **Definition:** The percentage of new borrowers in a specific geographic cluster (e.g., a specific estate or chama group) who complete a loan cycle *after* 3+ other members from that same cluster have already transacted.
*   **Why it matters:** If this rate rises as cluster size grows, value is compounding locally. If it stays flat regardless of cluster size, you have linear word-of-mouth, not network effects.
*   **Target Question:** Does a user in a "dense" chama convert faster or borrow larger amounts than a user in a "sparse" one?

**2. The Viral Coefficient (k) by Cluster, Not Global Average**
*   **Definition:** Average number of successful referred borrowers generated by one existing borrower, calculated **per geographic cluster**.
*   **Why it matters:** A global average of 0.18 hides pockets of virality. You need to identify if specific chamas have $k > 1$ (viral growth) while others are dead.
*   **Action:** Plot $k$ against the total active users in that cluster. A positive correlation confirms network effects; a flat line indicates the incentive design is the bottleneck, not the market fit.

**3. Time-to-Second-Loan by Referral Source (Retention Proxy)**
*   **Definition:** The median days between first and second loan for users acquired via referrals within a high-density cluster vs. paid ads.
*   **Why it matters:** Network effects should lower risk and increase trust, leading to faster repeat usage. If referred users in dense clusters do not retain better than ad users, the "trust" assumption is unproven.

**4. Organic Acquisition Ratio Trend (The Sustainability Metric)**
*   **Definition:** (New borrowers from referrals) / (Total new borrowers) tracked weekly per cluster.
*   **Why it matters:** Investors need to see this ratio rising in mature clusters without increased ad spend. A stagnant 18% globally suggests the loop is broken; a rising trend in specific areas proves scalability.

**5. Average Loan Size vs. Cluster Maturity**
*   **Definition:** Average loan amount requested by users in clusters with <5 members vs. >20 members.
*   **Why it matters:** True network effects in lending often manifest as increased credit confidence. If loan sizes grow with cluster density, the network is creating tangible economic value.

**Implementation Rules for Your Data Team:**
*   **Identifier Seam:** Ensure every user is tagged with a stable, pseudonymous `chama_id` or `geo_cluster_id` to track cross-session behavior without exposing PII. Route raw personal data to your privacy compliance protocol.
*   **No Benchmarks:** Do not compare these numbers to industry averages. Compare today's cluster density metrics against your own baseline from March 12.
*   **Event Taxonomy:** Only instrument events that answer: "Did this user join because a neighbor did?" and "Did they borrow more because their group is active?" Ignore pageviews or app opens.
