**Executive Summary: Checkout Experiment (March 10–24)**

**Recommendation:** Do not roll out permanently. Extend the experiment with a pre-committed guardrail threshold to quantify long-term risk before any decision.

**Analysis of Conflicting Signals**
The variant delivered a nominal +38% lift in conversion (4.2% to 5.8%), projecting ~$12k/day incremental revenue. However, the concurrent 15% spike in refund requests indicates the "gain" is partially driven by confusion-induced purchases rather than genuine value. This creates a net-revenue risk: if refund processing costs, chargeback fees, and lost lifetime value (LTV) from churn exceed the immediate margin, the variant is profitability-negative despite the higher top-line conversion.

Your assumption regarding trust erosion is a valid **guardrail failure**. In rigorous experimentation, a win on the primary metric (conversion) is voided if a pre-defined guardrail (refund rate, support ticket volume, or NPS) breaches a safety threshold. The current data shows the guardrail has already tripped. Furthermore, reading this result after only two weeks with aggressive UI patterns introduces a "novelty effect" bias; users may react impulsively to countdown timers initially, but retention often collapses once the pattern is recognized as manipulative.

**Decision Framework for the Ethics Board**
To defend a decision next Tuesday, we must move from "feeling" to measured trade-offs using the following steps:

1.  **Recompute Net Revenue:** Immediately calculate `(Variant Revenue - Refund Value - Processing Costs)` vs. Control. If the net margin is lower or statistically indistinguishable, the decision is **Stop**.
2.  **Define the Guardrail Tripwire:** Establish a hard limit for refunds. For example: "If refund requests exceed 5% of transactions (or a 10% relative increase over baseline), the test terminates regardless of conversion lift." The current 15% spike likely exceeds any reasonable risk tolerance for a risk-averse organization.
3.  **Extend for Lagging Indicators:** A 14-day window is insufficient to measure churn or LTV. Extend the test for 30 additional days *without* peeking at daily significance. Monitor cohort retention for users acquired during the test period. If Week-2 retention drops in the variant, the "revenue" is borrowed from the future.
4.  **Ethical & Regulatory Audit:** Document the "confusion" mechanism. If pre-selected boxes or timers violate dark-pattern guidelines in your operating jurisdictions, the regulatory fine risk outweighs $12k/day. The ethics board requires evidence that you measured harm, not just hope.
