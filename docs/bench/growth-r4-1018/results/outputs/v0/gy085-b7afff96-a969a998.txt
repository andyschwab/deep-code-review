# Event Unification Strategy: 'Luz' to 'Lumina' Transition

**Audience:** Data Science Team, Regional Marketing Lead (Lisbon)
**Objective:** Unify fragmented event streams for accurate Q4 reporting and YoY growth analysis without losing historical context.

### Step 1: Implement a Logical Alias Layer (Do Not Rewrite History)
Do not alter raw historical data. Instead, create a transformation layer in your data warehouse (e.g., a SQL view or dbt model) that normalizes event names at query time. Map both `luz_add_to_cart` and `lumina_add_to_cart` to a canonical name, such as `unified_add_to_cart`.
*   **Action:** Add a metadata column `event_source_version` ('legacy_luz' vs 'new_lumina') to every unified row. This preserves the ability to audit data integrity and isolate legacy client behavior if needed.

### Step 2: Validate Data Integrity Before Aggregation
Before trusting the unified stream for Q4 reports, verify that the aliasing logic does not double-count or drop events during the transition overlap period (post-Sept 10).
*   **Action:** Run a reconciliation query comparing the sum of raw `luz_*` + `lumina_*` events against the count of `unified_*` events for the period of Sept 10–Present. Any discrepancy indicates a logging error or mapping failure that must be fixed before reporting.

### Step 3: Define the "Legacy Support" Cutoff Protocol
You cannot arbitrarily stop supporting old event names based solely on app version adoption if those clients still generate revenue. The cutoff must be driven by **event volume**, not just install base, to protect the 12% revenue stream from Portugal.
*   **Trigger Condition:** Monitor the daily share of `unified_add_to_cart` events originating from `event_source_version = 'legacy_luz'`.
*   **Cutoff Rule:** When the legacy event volume drops below **1% of total daily events** for 14 consecutive days *after* the 90% app adoption milestone is reached, declare the "End of Life" for the `luz_` prefix.
*   **Post-Cutoff Action:** After this date, flag any remaining `luz_` events as anomalies (likely stuck processes or bots) rather than valid user traffic. Do not merge them into the primary metric unless explicitly investigated.

### Step 4: Adjust Year-Over-Year (YoY) Comparisons
Comparing Q4 2024 (mixed naming) to Q4 2023 (pure `luz_` naming) requires explicit annotation to prevent misinterpretation of "growth" that is actually just schema noise.
*   **Action:** In all Q4 dashboards, include a footnote stating: *"Q4 2024 metrics include unified events from both 'Luz' and 'Lumina' schemas. Historical Q4 2023 data reflects 'Luz' schema only. Growth rates are calculated on the unified logical layer."*
*   **Verification:** Ensure the Data Science team runs a cohort analysis separating users who updated pre-Sept 10 vs. post-Sept 10 to confirm the rebranding did not negatively impact retention independent of the naming change.

### Step 5: Finalize Q4 Reporting Governance
*   **Immediate:** Deploy the unified view for the Marketing Lead to use in draft reports.
*   **Weekly:** Review the legacy event volume percentage.
*   **Decision Point:** Once the cutoff rule (Step 3) is met, formally deprecate the `luz_` alias in documentation and update data dictionaries to reflect `lumina_` as the sole source of truth moving forward.

**Note:** This strategy relies on your internal event volume data to set the cutoff; no external benchmark for "acceptable legacy traffic" exists. If the 90% adoption target is met but legacy event volume remains high, investigate whether high-value users are disproportionately failing to update before enforcing the cutoff.
