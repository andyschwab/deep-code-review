**Executive Summary: KoiNest Algorithm Experiment (Oct 3–21)**

**The Trade-off: Detection Volume vs. Trust Capital**
The experiment delivered a 22% increase in anomaly detection but incurred an 8% false positive rate on healthy tanks. While the higher detection volume suggests improved technical sensitivity, the 8% error rate has directly triggered cancellation threats from three major cooperative farms. In aquaculture, where farmer livelihoods depend on precise interventions, a false alarm is not a neutral data point; it is an active erosion of trust. The current data indicates that the marginal value of the extra 22% detection is being negated by the reputational damage caused by the 8% false alarms.

**Revenue and Relationship Implications**
The threat of subscription cancellations from large cooperatives represents an immediate revenue risk that outweighs the potential long-term gains of earlier detection. These cooperatives likely constitute a significant portion of Monthly Recurring Revenue (MRR). If the algorithm causes "unnecessary farmer panic," it increases the operational burden on the farmers (checking healthy fish) and reduces their confidence in the platform's reliability. Continuing a full rollout under these conditions risks converting a product feature into a churn driver. The ethical commitment to farmer livelihoods requires that the tool reduces uncertainty, not creates it; an 8% false alarm rate violates this principle by introducing costly, stressful noise into their operations.

**Strategic Options for Q4 Roadmap**

1.  **Immediate Full Rollout (High Risk):** Proceeding with the current model across all users prioritizes the 22% detection lift but ignores the active churn signal. Given the explicit threats from key accounts, this path likely results in immediate revenue loss and brand damage that will be difficult to repair. The assumption that "earlier detection always outweighs false positives" is disproven by the market reaction in this specific context.

2.  **Immediate Rollback (Safety First):** Reverting to the previous model stabilizes current customer relationships and halts the churn threat. This preserves revenue stability and trust but forfeits the 22% detection improvement. This is the safest path for short-term financial health and aligns strictly with the ethical mandate to avoid harming farmer operations, though it delays technical advancement.

3.  **Segmented Rollout / Targeted Deployment (Balanced Approach):** Instead of a binary go/no-go, restrict the new model to a specific segment where the cost of a false positive is lower or where users have explicitly opted into "beta" testing with higher tolerance for errors. Alternatively, deploy the model only in regions or farm types where the 8% false positive rate has not historically led to churn. This allows KoiNest to continue gathering data on the 22% lift without exposing the entire revenue base to the 8% failure rate. This approach requires clear communication to the affected cooperatives that their feedback directly shaped a more cautious deployment strategy.

**Recommendation for Board Decision**
The data suggests that the current "one-size-fits-all" deployment is unsustainable due to the high cost of false positives in this specific user base. The immediate priority must be revenue stability and trust preservation. A segmented rollout allows the company to honor its ethical commitment to farmers while continuing to refine the algorithm, whereas a full rollout jeopardizes both. The board should consider authorizing a segmented deployment with strict monitoring of churn metrics as the primary guardrail, rather than detection volume alone.

*Note: This analysis relies solely on the provided experiment figures and stated customer reactions; no external benchmarks were applied.*
