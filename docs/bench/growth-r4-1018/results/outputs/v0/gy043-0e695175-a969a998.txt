**Executive Summary for the Editorial Board: Homepage Layout Update (Sept 20)**

The current data presents a classic tension between engagement depth and inventory volume. While time-on-site has risen 12%, the 5% drop in pages-per-session poses a direct risk to ad revenue, which relies on page views. Without long-term retention data, we cannot yet validate the editorial hypothesis that "deeper engagement equals loyalty." Therefore, the immediate decision must rely on **revenue neutrality** and **cohort behavior** rather than assumed brand benefits.

**Critical Segmentation Required Before Decision**
To determine if this trade-off is acceptable, you must segment the current data by **Visitor Status (New vs. Returning)** and **Device Type**.
*   **Returning Visitors:** If the drop in pages-per-session is concentrated here, the new layout may be disrupting habitual reading paths, risking immediate churn. If returning users are staying longer but viewing fewer pages, calculate the **Revenue Per Session (RPS)**. If RPS is flat or up despite fewer pages (e.g., due to higher-value video ad units), the trade-off is financially safe.
*   **New Visitors:** If new users show higher time-on-site but low page depth, they may be consuming a single video and leaving (a "dead end"). This indicates poor activation into the broader site.
*   **The Unknown (Retention):** Since long-term retention is `UNVERIFIED`, do not claim the layout improves loyalty. Treat the "brand loyalty" argument as a hypothesis requiring a 4-week cohort study, not a fact for tomorrow's decision.

**Recommended Narrative & Decision Framework**
Frame the narrative around **"Quality of Inventory vs. Quantity of Impressions."** Do not present the 12% time increase as an unconditional win. Instead, present it as a shift in consumption mode that requires a monetization adjustment.

**Immediate Action Plan (This Week)**
1.  **Calculate Revenue Per Session (RPS) by Cohort:** Compare RPS for the week prior to Sept 20 against the week after, split by New/Returning users.
    *   *Decision Rule:* If RPS for Returning Users drops >2%, the layout change is costing money; prepare a revert plan or a hybrid layout. If RPS is stable or up, proceed with caution.
2.  **Analyze Exit Rates on Video Pages:** Determine if users are leaving the site immediately after the video ends.
    *   *Decision Rule:* If exit rates on video pages are >15% higher than text articles, the layout is a "leaky bucket." Implement immediate "next story" recommendations below the video player.
3.  **Define the Retention Experiment:** Commit to a 4-week analysis of weekly returning users for the Sept 20 cohort vs. the Sept 13 cohort.
    *   *Metric:* % of users returning in Week 2 and Week 4.
    *   *Guardrail:* Total daily ad impressions must not fall more than 3% below the pre-change baseline trend.

**Conclusion**
Do not declare victory on engagement yet. The 5% drop in pages is a tangible revenue risk that outweighs the theoretical benefit of loyalty until proven otherwise. Recommend maintaining the new layout **only if** Returning Visitor RPS remains neutral. If RPS declines, revert to the text-prioritized layout for returning users while keeping the video layout for new user acquisition tests.
