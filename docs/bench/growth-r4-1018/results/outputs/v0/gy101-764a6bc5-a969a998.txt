### Growth Analysis: FrostBite Boxes Pilot (Copenhagen)

**Status of Claims:**
Your 68% retention vs. 55% assumption is a valid **internal baseline comparison**; this is the only benchmark we can use. The drop in Average Order Value (AOV) from 420 DKK to 315 DKK is **UNVERIFIED** as a churn signal because the current data does not distinguish between "skipping a week" (retention intact, revenue delayed) and "downsizing/canceling" (revenue lost). Without instrumenting the specific user action behind the revenue drop, no definitive causal claim can be made.

**Diagnostic Metrics to Isolate:**
To separate seasonal adjustment from churn before the board meeting, you must instrument and query these three specific events immediately. Do not look at aggregate revenue alone.

1.  **"Subscription Status Change" Event:**
    *   **Question:** Are users formally pausing or canceling, or just modifying box size?
    *   **Properties:** `action_type` (pause, cancel, downsize, skip_week), `user_id` (pseudonymous), `timestamp`.
    *   **Reading Rule:** If the volume of `skip_week` events rises while `cancel` events remain flat, the AOV drop is a **seasonal adjustment** (cash flow timing issue, not value loss). If `downsize` or `cancel` spikes, it is a **churn signal**.

2.  **"Delivery Window Selection" Event:**
    *   **Question:** Are users pushing delivery dates further out (indicating lower urgency/consumption)?
    *   **Properties:** `scheduled_date`, `days_from_now`, `user_id`.
    *   **Reading Rule:** A shift in `days_from_now` correlating with the AOV drop confirms the "summer hiatus" behavior pattern. This validates holding marketing spend but adjusting cash flow forecasts.

3.  **"Core Action Completion" (Meal Consumption Proxy):**
    *   **Question:** Are they still eating the product?
    *   **Properties:** `login_to_recipe_page`, `reorder_click`, `user_id`.
    *   **Reading Rule:** High engagement here + low AOV = **Seasonal/Logistical friction** (they want it but are spacing it out). Low engagement + low AOV = **Product-Market Fit leak** (they are losing interest).

**How to Read the Experiment/Data:**
Do not treat the AOV drop as a single metric. Segment your retained cohort (the 68%) by **behavioral intent**:
*   **Segment A (The Skippers):** Users who triggered `skip_week` but returned within 14 days. Their Lifetime Value (LTV) is preserved; the revenue dip is an accounting artifact.
*   **Segment B (The Downsizers):** Users who changed box size without skipping. This indicates price sensitivity or waste avoidance.
*   **Segment C (The Silent Drop-offs):** Users with no event activity for 21+ days. This is true churn masked by the "retained" status if your definition of retention is merely "not canceled."

**Recommendation for the Board (Aarhus vs. Pause):**
You cannot make a binary "Expand vs. Pause" decision until you segment the 315 DKV AOV.
*   **If Segment A dominates:** **Pause expansion** to Aarhus. Do not pour acquisition spend (Aarhus launch) into a cohort that is naturally cycling down for summer. Optimize cash flow instead.
*   **If Segment B or C dominates:** **Pause marketing entirely.** The core value proposition is eroding before the seasonal hiatus even begins. Fix the box sizing or value perception in Copenhagen first.
