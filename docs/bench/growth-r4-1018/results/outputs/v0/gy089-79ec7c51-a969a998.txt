### Executive Summary: UI Change Impact Analysis (July 22 – Aug 1)

**Current Status:** The "Add to Basket" (ATB) button enlargement has successfully increased intent signals (+3.5% ATB) but failed to translate into revenue growth (Conversion Rate flat at 2.1%). This indicates a **leak downstream of the basket**, confirming your hypothesis that the change may be reducing friction for low-intent users ("window shoppers") without addressing actual purchase barriers.

**Verdict:** Do not declare this a win yet. The metric lift is real but vanity-adjacent because it decoupled from revenue. Keep the design only if downstream metrics improve; otherwise, revert or iterate.

---

### Hypotheses for the Discrepancy

1.  **The "Window Shopper" Inflation (Primary Hypothesis)**
    The larger button lowers the cognitive cost of clicking, encouraging users to add items they do not intend to buy (e.g., to check total cost including shipping later). This inflates the top of the funnel while the **Basket-to-Purchase** conversion rate likely *dropped*, offsetting the ATB gain.
    *   *Diagnostic:* Segment "Add to Basket" events by subsequent "Checkout Started" events. If the ratio of `Checkout Started / ATB` has decreased, the new clicks are low-quality.

2.  **Downstream Friction Dominance**
    The UI change addressed a micro-friction (button size) but ignored macro-frictions (shipping costs, payment options, account creation). Users are now entering the checkout flow in higher volumes but abandoning at the same high rate due to these unchanged barriers.
    *   *Diagnostic:* Compare abandonment rates at specific checkout steps (Shipping, Payment) pre- and post-July 22. If these rates are unchanged or higher, the bottleneck is not the PDP.

3.  **Mobile vs. Desktop Mix Shift**
    If the button change was more prominent or effective on mobile (where intent is often lower and connectivity slower), the traffic mix might have shifted toward a device with historically lower conversion rates, masking gains on desktop.
    *   *Diagnostic:* Break down ATB and Conversion Rate by device type. A surge in mobile ATB with flat mobile revenue confirms a device-specific intent issue.

### Proposed Experiments & Actions (Before Aug 15)

To isolate the cause and decide on the design, execute the following immediately:

**1. Cohort Analysis: "Intent Quality" Check (No new code required)**
*   **Action:** Query your analytics for two cohorts: Pre-July 22 and Post-July 22.
*   **Metric:** Calculate `Purchase Rate per ATB Session` (Purchases / Sessions with ATB).
*   **Decision Rule:** If this rate dropped significantly (>2%), the button is attracting low-intent users. **Action:** Revert the button size or add a "Quick View" modal that requires more intent before adding.

**2. A/B Test: The "Commitment" Variant**
*   **Design:** Run a test against the current large button.
    *   *Variant A (Control):* Current large button.
    *   *Variant B (Treatment):* Large button + immediate inline feedback (e.g., "Added! View Basket" with a link) OR a small friction step (e.g., color change on hover that requires a deliberate pause).
*   **Goal:** Determine if slightly increasing the "cost" of the click filters out window shoppers while retaining genuine buyers.
*   **Pre-commitment:** Run for 14 days or until 5,000 basket additions are recorded. Primary metric: `Revenue per Visitor`. Guardrail: `ATB Rate` (ensure it doesn't crash).

**3. Funnel Deep-Dive: Shipping Shock Test**
*   **Action:** Instrument or highlight the exact point where users drop off after adding to the basket.
*   **Hypothesis Test:** If >60% drop off at the "Shipping Calculation" step, the problem is not the button; it's the shipping strategy.
*   **Next Step:** If confirmed, pivot the UX team to test "Free Shipping Threshold" messaging on the PDP rather than iterating on the button further.
