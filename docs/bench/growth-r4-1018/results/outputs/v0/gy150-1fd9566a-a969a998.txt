**Recommendation: Adopt a "Trip-Cycle" Cohort Model for Retention**

Do not use a standard 30-day rolling window; it will falsely classify your successful users as churned the moment they return home. For LinguaGo, a "retained user" must be defined relative to their specific travel context, not a calendar date.

**1. Redefine the Metric: "Trip Completion" vs. "Churn"**
Shift your North Star from "Daily Active Users" to **"Trips Successfully Supported."**
*   **Active Phase:** A user is "active" only during their declared or detected trip window (e.g., arrival in Thailand to departure).
*   **Success Criteria:** A user is "retained" if they complete their trip with consistent usage (e.g., opened app ≥3 times/week while in-country).
*   **Churn Definition:** A user is only "churned" if they stop using the app *while still traveling*. Dropping usage after leaving the country is a **successful completion**, not a failure.

**2. The Measurement Framework: Event-Based Trip Cohorts**
Instrument two specific events to replace generic daily tracking:
*   `trip_start_detected`: Triggered by location change or manual itinerary import.
*   `trip_end_detected`: Triggered by location exit or 48 hours of inactivity post-arrival home.

**Cohort Logic:** Group users by `trip_start_date`. Measure retention as the percentage of users who remain active *until* `trip_end_detected`.
*   *Result:* You will likely see retention hover near 60–80% *during* the trip, then drop to 0% upon departure. This flatline is your success metric, proving the product solved the immediate need.

**3. Justifying the Re-Engagement Budget (The Marketing Case)**
Your Head of Marketing needs to know if past users represent future revenue. Since organic daily retention is naturally zero post-trip, you cannot measure "future value" via passive usage. You must measure **Reactivation Potential**.

*   **The Experiment:** Run a small-scale A/B test before full budget approval.
    *   **Control:** No email.
    *   **Treatment:** Send a "Plan Your Next Adventure" email featuring a new language pair (e.g., "Going to Japan next? Try LinguaGo for Japanese") 3 months after their `trip_end_detected`.
    *   **Primary Metric:** `reactivation_rate` (User opens app or starts a new trip plan within 7 days of email).
    *   **Guardrail Metric:** `unsubscribe_rate` (Ensure you aren't annoying users who are done with travel).

**Decision Rule for the Campaign:**
If the `reactivation_rate` exceeds your email cost per user (e.g., if 2% of users start a new trip plan and the lifetime value of that trip > campaign cost), approve the budget. If the rate is near zero, the drop-off is permanent, and the budget should be redirected to acquiring new travelers during peak season rather than re-engaging old ones.
