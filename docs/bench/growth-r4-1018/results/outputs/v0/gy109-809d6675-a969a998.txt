**Step 1: Construct a Synthetic Control or Difference-in-Differences (DiD)**
You cannot compare "before vs. after" in isolation due to your noted confounders (seasonality, email campaign). Instead, use a **Difference-in-Differences** approach:
*   **Treatment Group:** Visitors to the Pricing Page (exposed to videos).
*   **Control Group:** Identify a comparable page *not* featuring the new videos but subject to the same external forces (e.g., the "Features" or "Integrations" page) during the same three-week window.
*   **Analysis:** Calculate the change in conversion rate for both pages. If the Pricing Page rose by 1.3% while the Control Page remained flat (or rose by only 0.2% due to the email campaign), the differential lift is attributable to the videos. If both rose equally, the lift is likely the email campaign or seasonality.

**Step 2: Time-Series Interrupted Series Analysis**
If no suitable control page exists, perform an **Interrupted Time Series** analysis using your historical data (at least 6–12 months prior).
*   Model the pre-August 22nd trend and seasonality.
*   Project the expected conversion rate for the post-launch period *had the videos not existed*.
*   Compare the actual 4.8% against this counterfactual baseline. This isolates the "intervention effect" from organic trends.

**Step 3: Segment by Industry Vertical**
To determine if the effect varies by vertical, you must instrument the **referrer source** or **account domain** associated with the demo request.
*   Query your analytics for the conversion rate of visitors from known enterprise domains (matching the testimonial industries) vs. others.
*   **Reading Rule:** If the lift is concentrated entirely within the industries featured in the testimonials, confidence in the "trust" hypothesis increases. If the lift is uniform across all unrelated verticals, the driver is likely a general site-wide factor (like the email campaign).
