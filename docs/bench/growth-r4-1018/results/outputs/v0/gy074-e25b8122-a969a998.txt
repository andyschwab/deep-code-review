**Evaluation Framework: Gamification Expansion Decision**

**Executive Summary**
The 22-minute increase in daily time spent is a vanity metric in isolation; it indicates higher resource consumption but fails to confirm increased customer value. The simultaneous 15% rise in support tickets (battery drain, crashes) acts as a critical guardrail violation. Expanding these features based solely on engagement time risks scaling technical debt and accelerating churn among users with older devices. The decision to expand must be withheld until the correlation between "time spent" and "lifetime value (LTV)" is verified against the cost of instability.

**Diagnostic Analysis: The Value vs. Cost Seam**
1.  **Reframe the North Star:** Shift the primary success metric from "Average Time Spent" to "Retention of Core Action" or "Revenue Per User." If the 22 extra minutes consist of users struggling with crashes or draining batteries without completing levels or making purchases, the feature is destroying value, not creating it.
2.  **Segment the Instability:** The hypothesis that older devices are driving the 15% support spike must be tested immediately. Segment the crash and battery data by device model and OS version. If the negative signals are concentrated in the bottom 40% of device performance, the feature may be viable for high-end users but requires optimization or exclusion for others.
3.  **The Burnout Question:** To address the unknown variable of burnout, analyze retention cohorts for Week 2 and Week 4 of the test. Compare the test group against the control group. If the test group shows a steeper drop-off in Day-7 or Day-14 retention despite higher Day-1 engagement, the gamification is accelerating churn.

**Decision Matrix for Leadership**
Do not expand to all game modes yet. Instead, choose one of the following paths based on immediate data queries:

*   **Path A: Conditional Rollout (If LTV is up AND crashes are device-specific)**
    *   *Action:* Enable features only for devices meeting a minimum performance threshold.
    *   *Trigger:* Data confirms no retention drop-off in high-end segment; LTV per user increases by >5%.
*   **Path B: Optimization Sprint (If LTV is flat AND crashes are widespread)**
    *   *Action:* Halt expansion. Engineering focuses on reducing the battery/cpu cost of the streak/leaderboard logic.
    *   *Trigger:* Crash rate in the test group exceeds the control group by >5% across all device tiers.
*   **Path C: Termination (If Retention is down)**
    *   *Action:* Remove features. The engagement is "toxic"—users are stuck or frustrated, not delighted.
    *   *Trigger:* Week-2 retention in the test group is lower than the control group.

**This-Week Steps**
1.  **Query:** Run a cohort analysis comparing Day-7 retention and Revenue Per User between the test and control groups, segmented by device age.
2.  **Define Guardrail:** Set a hard stop threshold (e.g., if crash rate >2% above baseline, auto-disable feature) before any further testing.
3.  **Instrument:** Add an event tracking "session end reason" (completed level vs. crash/force close) to distinguish between healthy engagement and failure loops.

*Note: The 22-minute gain is unverified as positive value until linked to retention or revenue; treat it as a cost driver until proven otherwise.*
*Ensure all user segmentation uses pseudonymous IDs to comply with privacy standards while analyzing device-specific performance.*
