**Recommendation: Optimize for Task Completion Rate**

For CaseMaster, **Task Completion Rate** is the correct North Star metric; **Total Engagement Time** is a vanity metric in this context that risks optimizing for friction.

**Rationale**
Your goal is reducing legal research costs. In a professional utility like CaseMaster, value is delivered when a solicitor finds the necessary case law and exits to do their actual job (drafting, advising).
*   **Engagement Time:** Your Q1 data (45 mins/session) combined with support tickets about search struggles indicates that high time-on-site likely correlates with confusion, not depth. Increasing this metric would increase client costs, jeopardizing December renewals.
*   **Task Completion:** This directly measures efficiency. If a user finds the specific case law they sought, the task is complete. Rising completion rates with stable or falling time-per-session proves you are reducing research costs.

**Interpreting Metric Conflicts**
When these metrics diverge during your A/B test, apply the following decision rules:

1.  **Completion Rate ↑ | Engagement Time ↓ (The Ideal Win)**
    *   **Interpretation:** Users are finding answers faster. Friction is removed.
    *   **Action:** **Ship immediately.** This directly validates the "efficiency gain" promise required for enterprise renewals.

2.  **Completion Rate ↓ | Engagement Time ↑ (The Danger Zone)**
    *   **Interpretation:** You have increased friction. Users are stuck, looping through search results, or failing to find what they need.
    *   **Action:** **Stop the test.** This confirms the hypothesis that longer sessions equal confusion. Do not ship; this threatens contract renewals.

3.  **Completion Rate ↑ | Engagement Time ↑ (The Nuanced Case)**
    *   **Interpretation:** Users are finding more relevant cases (higher completion) but are choosing to read deeper because the content is better surfaced.
    *   **Action:** **Investigate qualitative data.** Check if the *extra* time is spent on high-value actions (e.g., downloading judgments, citing cases) vs. low-value actions (e.g., refining search queries). If the extra time is voluntary deep work, consider shipping. If it is navigation overhead, reject.

4.  **Completion Rate ↓ | Engagement Time ↓ (The Failure)**
    *   **Interpretation:** Users are giving up quickly. They cannot find anything and leave.
    *   **Action:** **Stop the test.** This is a catastrophic failure of utility.

**A/B Test Design Constraints (October 10 Launch)**
To ensure the read is valid for the December reviews:
*   **Primary Metric:** Task Completion Rate (defined as "Successful search resulting in case view/download within 3 clicks").
*   **Guardrail Metric:** Average Session Duration. Ensure it does not spike significantly (indicating new confusion) while completion rises.
*   **Pre-commitment:** Define your sample size and test duration before launch. Do not stop the test early if significance appears on Day 2 ("peeking"), as this inflates false positives. Read the results once at the pre-determined end date.
*   **Instrumentation:** Ensure you are tracking the specific event "Search Success" tied to a pseudonymous user ID to measure retention of this efficiency over time, not just one-off usage.
