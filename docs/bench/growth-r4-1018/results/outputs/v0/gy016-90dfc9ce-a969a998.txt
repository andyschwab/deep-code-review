### Proposed Activation Framework for ScholarWrite

**1. Redefine the North Star: From Frequency to "Value Realization"**
Discard the current "3 documents in 7 days" metric. For academic researchers, frequency is vanity; depth is value. Your hypothesis that editing duration correlates with subscription is strong. The new North Star for activation should be **"Completion of a Deep Edit Session."**

*   **Definition:** A user enters a "Deep Edit Session" when they spend >20 minutes continuously interacting with a single document (typing, accepting AI suggestions, or restructuring) within their first 14 days.
*   **Why:** This signals the user has moved past novelty and is trusting the AI with substantive work—the precise moment "aha" occurs for long-form writers.

**2. The Testing Framework: Cohort Correlation Analysis**
Do not guess the threshold; derive it from your existing data using a retrospective cohort analysis.

*   **Step A: Segment the February 1st Cohort.**
    Group users into three behavioral buckets based on their first 14 days:
    1.  *High Frequency/Low Depth:* >3 docs, <15 mins avg per doc.
    2.  *Low Frequency/High Depth:* 1–2 docs, >30 mins avg per doc.
    3.  *Mixed/Other:* All remaining patterns.
*   **Step B: Map to Retention.**
    Calculate the Month-2 retention rate and Trial-to-Paid conversion rate for each bucket.
    *   *Expected Outcome:* Bucket 2 will show significantly higher retention. If Bucket 1 has high churn, your current metric is actively misleading the team.
*   **Step C: Identify the Threshold.**
    Run a correlation sweep on "time spent" vs. "retention." Find the inflection point where retention probability jumps (e.g., is it 18 minutes? 25 minutes?). Set this specific time delta as your new activation criterion.

**3. Operationalizing for Customer Success**
Shift intervention triggers from "count-based" to "behavior-based":
*   **Old Trigger:** User has created 1 doc in 5 days (Risk of low volume).
*   **New Trigger:** User has created a doc but has *not* initiated a Deep Edit Session (>20 mins) by Day 10.
*   **Action:** CS intervenes not to ask for more documents, but to guide the user on *how* to use AI for deep editing (e.g., "Have you tried using ScholarWrite to restructure your literature review section?").

**4. Validation Experiment (Next 30 Days)**
*   **Hypothesis:** Users who hit "Deep Edit Session" in Week 1 will have a 20% higher Month-2 retention than those who only hit "3 Documents."
*   **Method:** Track the new metric alongside the old one. Do not change the product yet; simply measure which metric predicts the future better.
*   **Decision Rule:** If the correlation coefficient of "Deep Edit" to retention is >0.3 higher than "3 Docs," officially adopt "Deep Edit" as the company North Star for activation.
