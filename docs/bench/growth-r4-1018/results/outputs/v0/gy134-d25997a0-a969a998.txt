**Executive Summary: CloudVault Pricing Experiment (Weeks 1–6)**

**Current Status:** The experiment shows a **15% increase in subscriber volume** but an **8% decline in Monthly Recurring Revenue (MRR)** versus the control. This indicates immediate revenue cannibalization: users who previously paid the higher monthly rate are switching to the discounted annual plan.

**Key Risks:**
1.  **Immediate Cash Flow Deficit:** The 8% MRR drop is a verified, realized loss. The assumption that Lifetime Value (LTV) will recover this gap relies entirely on unproven churn reduction for the new annual cohort.
2.  **Unverified LTV Hypothesis:** Because the annual plan is new, there is **no historical data** to confirm lower churn. Projecting future retention to justify current losses is currently `UNVERIFIED`.
3.  **Cannibalization Without Compensation:** If annual churn does not drop significantly below monthly churn, the discount represents pure margin erosion with no long-term upside.

**Decision Framework & Break-Even Model:**
Do not extend the test based on "hope" for lower churn. Calculate the specific churn differential required to break even using your current numbers:
*   **The Math:** Let $R_m$ be monthly revenue and $R_a$ be the effective monthly revenue of the annual plan (total annual price / 12).
*   **The Tripwire:** The annual cohort's monthly churn rate ($C_a$) must be low enough such that the expected lifetime revenue exceeds the monthly cohort's expected lifetime revenue ($R_m / C_m$).
*   **Action:** If the required $C_a$ is historically unrealistic (e.g., requires <1% monthly churn when your baseline is 5%), the pricing tier is financially unsustainable regardless of volume gains.

**Recommendation for Q4 Budget:**
**Pause the broad rollout.** Continue the experiment only if you can define a **guardrail metric** (e.g., "MRR drop cannot exceed 5%") and a **pre-committed duration** sufficient to measure one full churn cycle of the monthly cohort. Do not declare a win based on signup volume alone; volume is a vanity metric here if it drives revenue down.

**This Week's Steps:**
1.  **Compute the Break-Even Churn:** Calculate the exact monthly churn rate the annual cohort must achieve to offset the 8% MRR loss. Present this single number to the CFO as the "hurdle rate."
2.  **Define the Stop Rule:** If the annual cohort's observed churn after 90 days does not trend toward this hurdle rate, terminate the annual discount.
3.  **Instrument the Cohort:** Ensure your analytics explicitly tag these users as "Annual-Discount-Cannibalized" vs. "New-Incremental" to separate users who switched from those who would not have bought at all.

*Note: All LTV projections remain `UNVERIFIED` until actual churn data is collected; do not budget against assumed retention improvements.*
*The 8% MRR drop is a factual baseline; any justification must exceed this verified loss.*
