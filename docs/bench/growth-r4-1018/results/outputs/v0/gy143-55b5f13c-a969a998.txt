**Evaluation: Dragon Quest Chronicles – South Korea Bundle Test**

**Conflicting Signals**
The experiment presents a high-reward, high-risk profile. The treatment group achieved a **15% increase in Revenue Per Daily Active User (RPDAU)**, indicating strong initial demand for the 9,900 KRW bundle. However, this gain is immediately threatened by a **4x spike in refund rates** (rising from 2% to 8%). While the net revenue impact remains positive in the short term (assuming the 15% gross lift outweighs the 6% absolute refund increase), the volatility suggests a critical friction point or user error rather than sustainable value capture.

**Risk Profile & Hypothesis Validation**
Your assumption that "accidental purchases by younger players" drives the refunds is plausible but currently **UNVERIFIED**. In the South Korean mobile market, strict regulations regarding minor gaming expenditures and "cooling-off" periods often mandate easy refund mechanisms. If the spike is indeed accidental purchases by minors, the current flow lacks sufficient friction (e.g., confirmation steps, biometric verification) to prevent errors. If the spike is due to buyer's remorse or confusion over the "bundle" value, the product messaging is flawed. Relying on the hope that refunds will "stabilize over time" without intervention is a dangerous strategy; refund behavior in gaming rarely self-corrects unless the root cause (UI confusion or lack of friction) is addressed.

**Signals to Monitor (The Decision Framework)**
To decide between a global launch or killing the feature, monitor these specific signals over the next 7 days, segmented by **user age** (if available) and **device type**:

1.  **Net Revenue Per User (NRPU):** Calculate `Gross Revenue - Refunds`. If NRPU in the test group drops below the control group, the feature is value-destructive regardless of the gross lift.
2.  **Refund Reason Codes:** Analyze the specific reasons cited in refund requests (e.g., "Unauthorized purchase" vs. "Item not as described"). A dominance of "Unauthorized" confirms the minor/accidental hypothesis.
3.  **Time-to-Refund Distribution:** Plot the time elapsed between purchase and refund request. A cluster within minutes suggests accidental clicks/UI flaws; a cluster days later suggests dissatisfaction or parental intervention after reviewing bank statements.
4.  **Repeat Purchase Rate (Post-Refund):** Track if users who refunded attempt to buy again. If they do not, the experience was damaging; if they repurchase the bundle correctly, it confirms the "accident" theory.

**Recommendation for the Regional Director**
Do not approve a global rollout based on current data. The 8% refund rate indicates a broken purchase confirmation step for a significant user segment. The immediate action is to **extend the test duration** with a modified variant that introduces a "hard friction" step (e.g., a swipe-to-confirm or secondary PIN entry) specifically for the bundle purchase.

**Decision Tripwire:**
*   **Launch:** Proceed only if the modified variant maintains >10% gross lift while reducing refund rates to <3%.
*   **Kill:** If the "hard friction" variant causes gross revenue to drop below the control group, the market sensitivity to price/complexity is too high for this bundle structure.

**Required Context:**
We need immediate access to the breakdown of refund reason codes and the demographic split (age/device) of the refunding users to confirm the "accidental purchase" hypothesis before designing the friction fix.
