**To:** VP of Customer Success, PeopleFirst
**Subject:** Feature Adoption Measurement & Intervention Plan for AI Performance Review Module

**1. The North Star Metric**
Reject "administrators enabled" (vanity) and "time spent" (noise). Adopt **"Weekly Active Managers Completing a Draft Review"** as your North Star. This metric rises only when the core value (AI-assisted review creation) is realized. "Reviews completed" is a lagging outcome; "time spent" can indicate confusion. The act of a manager initiating and drafting using the AI is the leading indicator of retention.

**2. Leading Indicators vs. Vanity Metrics**
Distinguish clearly between setup and engagement to guide your intervention plan:

*   **Vanity (Ignore for Adoption Health):**
    *   *% of Admins Enabled:* You are already at 80%; this measures sales success, not product value.
    *   *Total Logins:* A manager clicking in and out without action inflates this number.
    *   *Global Time Spent:* Aggregated time hides whether users are stuck or flowing.

*   **Leading Indicators (Track These):**
    *   *Activation Rate:* % of managers who, within 7 days of feature enablement, generate at least one AI-suggested comment or draft section. This validates the "aha" moment.
    *   *Draft-to-Submit Ratio:* Measures if the AI output is usable. A high draft count with low submission suggests the AI content requires too much editing.
    *   *Manager Initiation %:* The percentage of total managers who start the process. This is your primary lever for the intervention plan.

**3. Diagnostic Reading (Bottom-Up)**
Do not pour resources into more training (acquisition/activation) until you verify retention of the core action.
*   **Hypothesis Check:** You assume low interaction is due to training. However, if managers *start* a review (high initiation) but *abandon* it before drafting (low activation), the issue is UX or AI quality, not training. If they never start, it is an awareness/training issue.
*   **Data Gap:** The current "12% interaction" is `UNVERIFIED` without a defined event taxonomy. We do not know if "interaction" means a click or a completed draft.

**4. Event Taxonomy for Intervention**
Instrument only these three events to answer specific questions for your client interventions:
*   `review_started` (Property: `manager_id`, `department`): *Question:* Are managers aware of the feature?
*   `ai_suggestion_accepted` (Property: `suggestion_type`, `edit_distance`): *Question:* Is the AI providing value, or is it being deleted?
*   `review_submitted` (Property: `time_to_complete`): *Question:* Did the process finish?

**5. This-Week Steps**
1.  **Define the Baseline Query:** Run a cohort analysis on the Feb 1st release grouping by client. Calculate the % of managers who triggered `review_started` vs. `ai_suggestion_accepted`. If the drop-off is between start and AI acceptance, the problem is utility, not training.
2.  **Targeted Intervention Pilot:** Select two clients with high "enabled" but low "started" rates. Deploy a targeted "Manager Kickoff" guide (not general training) and measure the lift in `review_started` over 14 days.
3.  **Guardrail Check:** Monitor support ticket volume tagged "AI Review" for these clients. If usage rises but tickets spike, the feature is confusing, not just unknown.

*Note: Ensure `manager_id` is pseudonymous in analytics to comply with privacy standards; route PII handling to your compliance team. Do not benchmark these rates against external industry data; compare strictly against your own Week 1–4 trend.*
