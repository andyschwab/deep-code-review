**Executive Summary: Do Not Roll Out Based on Current Data**

The observed 25% lift in click-through rate (CTR) is **statistically unverified** as a measure of design success because the experiment violated the fundamental requirement of randomization. The treatment group's 40% over-representation of the 18–24 demographic introduces a severe **confounding variable**. Since this demographic naturally interacts differently with visual content than your core buyer persona (35–50), the result is a mixture of design effect and demographic bias. We cannot distinguish whether the lift comes from the new navigation or simply from having more young users in the test group.

**Recommended Analysis Path**

To rescue the insight without invalidating the effort, execute the following two steps immediately:

1.  **Segmented Re-analysis (Primary Recommendation)**
    Stop looking at the aggregate number. Slice the data by age cohort and compare Treatment vs. Control *within* each group:
    *   Calculate the lift for users aged 35–50 only.
    *   Calculate the lift for users aged 18–24 only.
    *   **Decision Rule:** If the 35–50 cohort shows no lift (or negative lift) while the 18–24 cohort drives the aggregate win, the design **fails** for your core buyers. Do not roll out. If both cohorts show similar positive lift, the design likely works universally, and the aggregate number was just noisy.

2.  **Statistical Adjustment (Secondary Option)**
    If sample sizes within segments are too small for reliable conclusions, apply **post-stratification weighting** or a **logistic regression model** with "Age Group" as a covariate. This mathematically adjusts the treatment effect to simulate what the result would have been if the age distribution were balanced. However, given the 40% imbalance, this adjustment will likely widen the confidence intervals significantly, potentially rendering the result statistically insignificant.

**Final Call for the Product Director**

*   **If the 35–50 segment lift is <5% or negative:** **Stop.** The design appeals to non-buyers and may alienate your core revenue source. Return to UX research focused on the 35–50 persona.
*   **If the 35–50 segment lift matches the aggregate (~25%):** **Proceed with caution.** The design works, but you must re-run a properly randomized A/B test with strict audience balancing before a global launch to confirm the magnitude of the win.
*   **Current Status:** The current "25% win" is **UNVERIFIED** for business value. Treat it as a hypothesis generator, not a green light.
