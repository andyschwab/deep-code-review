### Executive Summary: Pricing Experiment Verdict

To determine if the bundle offer is financially sustainable, we must move beyond conversion rates and average order value (AOV) to calculate the **Revenue Per Visitor (RPV)** and, critically, the **Contribution Margin Per Visitor**. The 8% conversion lift and 12% AOV drop are contradictory only until weighted by volume and cost.

**1. The Calculation Framework**
Do not compare conversion rates to AOV directly. Instead, calculate the net revenue impact per 1,000 visitors for both groups using your actual traffic data:
*   **Control RPV:** (Control Conversion Rate) × (Control AOV: $55)
*   **Treatment RPV:** (Treatment Conversion Rate) × (Treatment AOV: $48)

If the Treatment RPV is higher, top-line revenue increases. However, for the CFO, revenue is insufficient. You must apply the **gross margin percentage** to each scenario. If the bundle has a lower margin percentage than individual books (due to discounting), the profit gap widens.
*   **Verdict Formula:** (Treatment Visitors × Treatment Conv. Rate × Treatment AOV × Treatment Margin %) vs. (Control Visitors × Control Conv. Rate × Control AOV × Control Margin %).
*   **Break-even Analysis:** Calculate the exact conversion lift required to offset the 12% AOV drop given your specific margin structure. If the actual 8% lift is below this break-even point, the experiment reduces total profit.

**2. Required Data Breakdowns**
Request the following specific segments to validate the "cannibalization" hypothesis:
*   **Cannibalization Rate:** What percentage of Treatment converters previously purchased full-price individual books in the last 90 days? If >40% are existing high-value customers downgrading, the long-term risk is severe.
*   **New vs. Returning Split:** Did the conversion lift come entirely from new customers (incremental growth) or existing ones (substitution)? A lift driven by new users justifies lower AOV; a lift driven by existing users suggests value destruction.
*   **Attach Rate:** For those who bought the bundle, did they add any full-price items to the cart? If the attach rate is zero, the bundle is replacing higher-margin sales entirely.

**3. Lifetime Value (LTV) vs. Immediate Revenue**
For a fiscal year decision, **immediate contribution margin is the primary gate**, but LTV is the secondary constraint.
*   **Immediate:** If the experiment loses money on the first transaction (negative contribution margin), no amount of future LTV justifies it unless you have verified data showing these specific bundle buyers retain at a significantly higher rate than control users.
*   **LTV Context:** Only prioritize LTV if you have historical cohort data proving that customers acquired via discounts have a positive net present value over 12 months. Without that specific baseline, assume the lower AOV sets a lower anchor for future spending. Do not speculate on LTV improvements without evidence.

**Recommendation for the CFO:**
Do not make the offer permanent yet. The 8% lift is likely insufficient to overcome a 12% value drop unless the margin on the bundle is near-equivalent to individual books. Request the **margin-adjusted RPV** calculation immediately. If the result is negative, kill the test. If positive, run a targeted test only on *new* visitors to prevent further cannibalization of existing high-value customers.

**Note:** Ensure the experiment ran for a full business cycle (including weekends) to avoid day-of-week bias in the 3-week window.
**Note:** Verify that the "control" group was not exposed to other concurrent promotions that might have skewed the baseline AOV.
