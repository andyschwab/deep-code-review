### Pricing Trade-off Model: Short-Term Signals to Long-Term Profitability

To determine if the 15% price increase yields long-term profitability, you must model the **Net Revenue Impact** over a 12-month horizon, explicitly weighing the immediate revenue lift against the accelerated churn. Do not rely on the 5% total revenue increase observed in week six as a proxy for success; this is a lagging indicator that masks the bleeding of high-value cohorts.

**1. Recompute the Break-Even Churn Threshold**
Your data shows a cancellation spike from 2% to 8% (a 4x increase). Before forecasting, calculate the exact churn rate at which the price hike becomes neutral.
*   **Formula:** $New Price \times (1 - New Churn Rate) = Old Price \times (1 - Old Churn Rate)$
*   **Application:** If the old price is $P$ and old churn is 2%, retention is 98%. The new price is $1.15P$.
*   **Break-Even Retention:** $0.98 / 1.15 = 85.2\%$. This implies a break-even churn of **14.8%**.
*   **Current Status:** Your current churn (8%) is *below* the theoretical break-even (14.8%) for a single period. However, this simple math fails to account for **Lifetime Value (LTV) compression**. If the 8% churn rate persists or accelerates (common with price shocks), the cumulative loss of future months outweighs the immediate 15% bump.

**2. Model the Cohort Decay (The "Leaky Bucket")**
Do not aggregate all users. Segment the treatment group into those who stayed vs. those who left immediately.
*   **The Leavers:** Calculate the LTV of the 8% who canceled. Did they leave in month 1 or month 2? If they left immediately, you lost 100% of their future value.
*   **The Stayers:** Monitor the *secondary churn* of the remaining 92%. Price-sensitive users who don't cancel immediately often exhibit "silent churn" (downgrading usage, not renewing next year). If the stayers' churn rate drifts from 2% to 4% over the next quarter, the 15% premium evaporates.
*   **Action:** Project two curves: (A) Revenue with old price/stable churn, and (B) Revenue with new price/elevated churn. The intersection point where Curve B drops below Curve A is your **profitability horizon**. If this occurs before month 12, the hike destroys value.

**3. Guardrail Metrics & Decision Rules**
The 5% revenue lift is the primary metric, but it is insufficient without guardrails.
*   **Guardrail 1: Net Dollar Retention (NDR).** If NDR drops below 100% in the treatment cohort, the expansion revenue from price hikes is failing to offset contraction/churn.
*   **Guardrail 2: Payback Period.** Did the extra revenue collected in months 1–2 cover the Customer Acquisition Cost (CAC) of the users who just left? If not, you are burning cash to buy short-term GAAP revenue.

**This-Week Steps:**
1.  **Run the Cohort Decay Query:** Isolate the treatment group users active on March 1st. Calculate their specific churn rate for April (post-hike) compared to the control group. If the delta exceeds 4%, flag the LTV model as **UNVERIFIED** until a 90-day trend is established.
2.  **Define the Stop Tripwire:** Set a hard limit: If the treatment group's churn exceeds **10%** in any rolling 30-day window, revert the pricing for new signups immediately. This prevents compounding LTV loss while you analyze the existing base.
3.  **Present the Scenario Analysis:** Show the CFO two numbers: the "Best Case" (churn stabilizes at 8%) and the "Failure Case" (churn creeps to 12%). Recommend no permanent change until the 90-day cohort data confirms the churn curve has flattened.

*Note: The 5% revenue lift is a short-term accounting win; do not declare victory until the 90-day retention curve flattens. All LTV projections remain `UNVERIFIED` without confirmed churn stabilization.*
