**Subject:** URGENT: Search Bar Test Interpretation & Decision Path

**Current Status: UNVERIFIED**
We cannot declare this test a success or failure yet. While task speed improved (+2.5s), the 18% drop in clicks is ambiguous without **task completion rates**. Reduced clicks could signal efficiency (users finding things faster) or failure (users giving up before finding secondary links). Without completion data, we risk rolling out a change that increases bounce rates for our 2M monthly users.

**Possible Explanations**
1.  **Efficiency Gain (Hypothesis A):** The central search bar resolves queries faster, eliminating the need for navigational clicking. Users complete tasks with fewer steps.
2.  **Discovery Failure (Hypothesis B):** Users cannot locate secondary links previously accessed via the old menu. They leave the session early because they assume features are missing, artificially lowering click counts and potentially hurting retention.
3.  **Scope Narrowing (Hypothesis C):** Users are solving immediate needs but ignoring broader site exploration, which may impact long-term engagement or cross-sell metrics.

**Required Data Before Default Rollout**
To resolve this before Friday's sprint planning, we must gather the following immediately:

*   **Quantitative (Priority 1): Task Completion Rate.**
    *   *Query:* Compare the % of sessions where a user successfully reaches a target feature page (not just searches) between Control and Variant.
    *   *Why:* If completion rates dropped, the speed gain is irrelevant; users are getting lost. If completion rates held steady or rose, the click drop confirms efficiency.
    *   *Status:* **UNVERIFIED** (Requires immediate analytics query).
*   **Quantitative (Priority 2): Exit Rate on Search Results Page.**
    *   *Query:* Did users exit immediately after viewing search results more often in the Variant?
    *   *Why:* High exit rates suggest the search results didn't satisfy the intent, or users couldn't find "next steps."
*   **Qualitative (Rapid Turnaround): Session Replay & 5 User Interviews.**
    *   *Action:* Watch 20 replays of Variant sessions where users performed <3 clicks. Conduct 5 rapid interviews asking users to find a secondary feature.
    *   *Why:* To visually confirm if users are hovering/confused (looking for links) or moving decisively.

**Recommendation & Next Steps**
**Do not make this the default layout yet.** The risk of breaking discovery for 2M users outweighs the potential efficiency gain until completion rates are verified.

**This Week's Actions:**
1.  **Analytics Team:** Run the **Task Completion Rate** cohort analysis for Aug 1–15 by EOD tomorrow. This is the single metric that decides the outcome.
2.  **Design Team:** Conduct 5 rapid usability tests (remote) focusing on "finding secondary features" using the new layout. Report findings by Thursday noon.
3.  **Decision Gate:** If completion rates are neutral/positive AND qualitative data shows no confusion, we ship in the next sprint. If completion rates dropped >2%, we revert and iterate on secondary navigation visibility.

**Note:** This interpretation relies strictly on SwiftNav's internal baseline; no external benchmarks for "good" click reduction were applied.
