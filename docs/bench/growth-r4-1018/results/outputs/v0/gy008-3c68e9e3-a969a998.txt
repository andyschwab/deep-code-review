### Critical Success Metrics for Sahara Date Co. Drone Pilot

To prove operational stability and safety to the Operations Director and Municipal Council, track these four daily metrics. These distinguish a viable logistics model from a novelty experiment.

**1. On-Time Delivery Rate (OTD) by Cause**
*   **Definition:** Percentage of deliveries arriving within the 45-minute window.
*   **Required Breakdown:** You must tag every delay as either **Weather-Induced** (wind, heat) or **System-Induced** (battery degradation, mechanical fault).
*   **Why it matters:** A low OTD due to weather suggests a seasonal limitation; a low OTD due to system issues indicates the fleet is not ready for expansion. The Council needs to see that system-induced delays are near zero.
*   **Target:** >95% OTD overall, with <2% attributed to system failures.

**2. Spoilage Reduction vs. Cost Per Drop**
*   **Definition:** Compare the percentage of damaged/spoiled dates per drone drop against your historical trucking baseline, plotted against the fully loaded cost per drop (energy + maintenance + pilot oversight).
*   **Why it matters:** Faster transit is only valuable if it reduces spoilage enough to offset the higher operational cost of drones. If spoilage drops by 10% but cost per drop rises by 50%, the model is a novelty, not a business.
*   **Action:** Calculate the "Break-Even Spoilage Rate"—the specific reduction in waste required to make the drone cost-neutral compared to trucks.

**3. Battery Degradation Impact on Range Safety**
*   **Definition:** Average battery health percentage at launch vs. successful completion rate for flights over 30km.
*   **Why it matters:** Oman's heat accelerates battery wear. You must prove that battery degradation does not create a safety risk (e.g., forced landings) as the pilot progresses from Week 1 to Week 6.
*   **Safety Proof:** Show the Municipal Council a trend line proving that no flight was aborted or endangered due to unexpected battery drain.

**4. The "Go/No-Go" Stability Threshold**
*   **Definition:** A rolling 7-day average of successful completions without a single safety-critical incident (forced landing, near-miss, or payload loss).
*   **Why it matters:** Regulatory approval depends on consistency, not peak performance. One major incident can halt the program.
*   **Decision Rule:** If the 7-day rolling average drops below 98% success due to system errors, the pilot pauses for root-cause analysis before seeking expansion.

**Reading the Data:**
Do not compare your numbers to industry benchmarks you do not have. Compare Week 2 performance to Week 1, and May performance to June. If spoilage decreases but system-induced delays increase as batteries age, the model is currently unstable. If weather causes >15% of delays, the service level agreement (SLA) with hotels must include weather clauses.

3.  Pre-commit to a **fixed sample size** of 500 deliveries before analyzing success rates; do not declare victory after the first 50 perfect flights.
