### Executive Summary: Recommendation Engine Experiment (Jan 10 – Feb 21)

**Verdict: Do not ship. The "win" in listening time is likely noise driven by friction, not value.**

The conflict between +14 minutes listening time and a spike in skip rate (22% → 39%) indicates the new engine is creating **friction**, not **discovery**. Users are spending more time not because they are enjoying obscure baroque pieces, but because they are working harder to find something playable.

**The Diagnosis**
Your assumption—that deeper exploration naturally increases time without frustration—is contradicted by the data. A 17-point jump in skips suggests the recommendations are failing the "aha" moment. The increased duration is almost certainly composed of:
1.  **Search Time:** Users skipping multiple bad recommendations before finding a known track.
2.  **Abandonment Latency:** Users letting tracks play partially before deciding to skip, rather than instant skips.
3.  **Interface Friction:** If users cannot easily "save for later," they may skip immediately rather than curating a list, inflating the skip rate while they hunt for immediate gratification.

Without a pre-committed experimental design (sample size, duration, and primary metric defined *before* Jan 10), this six-week read is **UNVERIFIED** as a causal win. We do not know if the 14-minute gain is statistically significant or if it varies by user cohort (e.g., new vs. power users). Furthermore, we lack a guardrail metric (such as "sessions per week" or "churn rate") to ensure this frustration isn't driving users away entirely over the long term.

**Decision Framework for Founder & Curator**
*   **If the goal is Retention:** High skip rates are a leading indicator of churn. Users who cannot find value quickly will stop opening the app. The current trajectory risks the core business.
*   **If the goal is Catalog Depth:** The engine is failing to bridge the gap between "obscure" and "enjoyable." It is serving academic content without the contextual scaffolding users need to appreciate it.

**Recommended Actions (This Week)**
1.  **Segment the Skip Rate:** Query the analytics to separate skips occurring within the first 10 seconds (rejection of content) vs. skips after 30+ seconds (interface/search friction). This distinguishes "bad music" from "bad UX."
2.  **Instrument the "Save" Intent:** Add a single event tracking clicks on any "add to playlist" or "bookmark" interaction during recommendation sessions. If this rate is near zero, the interface hypothesis is confirmed; users have no path to retain value.
3.  **Pause and Redesign:** Halt the full rollout. Run a qualitative study with 10 users observing their reaction to the new recommendations. Adjust the logic to blend 80% familiar styles with 20% obscure picks, rather than 100% deep cuts.

**Final Call:** Treat the 14-minute increase as a warning sign of inefficiency, not engagement. Fix the skip rate before optimizing for duration.
