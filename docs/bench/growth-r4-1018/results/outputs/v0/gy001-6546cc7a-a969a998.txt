**Executive Summary: Pricing Experiment Sustainability Analysis**
**To:** CFO and Board of Directors, FrostByte Climbing Gyms
**From:** Head of Retention
**Date:** [Current Date]
**Subject:** Decision Framework for Nationwide Pricing Rollout

The March 12–April 12 experiment presents a critical trade-off: a 15% price increase drove an 8.4% churn rate (vs. 3.9% baseline) while increasing free walk-in traffic by 12%. To determine if this is sustainable, we must isolate three specific behavioral metrics that directly answer whether the revenue gain offsets the Lifetime Value (LTV) lost.

**1. Net Revenue Per Location (The Primary Metric)**
We must calculate the immediate net revenue impact, not just the headline churn.
*   **Calculation:** (Remaining Premium Members × New Price) + (Estimated Conversion of Walk-ins to Paid) vs. (Original Member Count × Old Price).
*   **Decision Rule:** If the net revenue per location is negative or flat after accounting for the increased operational cost of the 12% surge in free users (wear on ice walls/saunas), the model fails immediately. The "exclusivity" hypothesis is invalid if the core revenue engine shrinks faster than the free traffic can monetize.

**2. Free-to-Paid Conversion Velocity (The Activation Metric)**
The rise in free visitors is only valuable if they convert. Currently, this is an unverified vanity metric.
*   **Action:** Isolate the cohort of new free visitors during the test window. Track how many purchased a day pass or upgraded to premium within 14 days of their first visit.
*   **Decision Rule:** If the conversion rate of this new traffic is below our historical baseline for walk-ins, the "curiosity" spike is temporary noise. We are trading high-LTV members for low-intent browsers. Do not roll out unless the conversion rate of this specific cohort exceeds the churn loss ratio.

**3. Cohort Retention of "Stayers" (The Sustainability Metric)**
We must verify if the remaining premium members are stable or if churn will compound.
*   **Action:** Segment the members who *did not* cancel during the test. Are their visit frequencies dropping? A price hike often causes surviving members to reduce usage before eventually cancelling.
*   **Decision Rule:** If the average weekly visits of the remaining premium cohort dropped by >10% during the test, the 8.4% churn is likely the leading edge of a larger collapse. This indicates the price point has broken the value proposition, not just filtered the user base.

**Recommendation for Tuesday's Decision**
Do not roll out nationwide based on current data. The 8.4% churn represents a verified loss of high-value LTV, while the 12% traffic increase is an unverified gain until conversion rates are proven.

**Immediate Next Steps (This Week):**
1.  **Run the Net Revenue Query:** Aggregate March 12–April 12 revenue by location, subtracting the estimated marginal cost of the extra free users. Present this single number to the board.
2.  **Tag the Walk-in Cohort:** Instrument a specific event tag for "Post-Hike Free Visitor" to track their conversion over the next 30 days. Without this, the traffic spike is `UNVERIFIED` value.
3.  **Set the Tripwire:** If the "Stayer" visit frequency drops further in the first two weeks of April, trigger an automatic revert to the legacy pricing model to prevent brand erosion.

The data suggests we are currently bleeding value. The board must see the *net* dollar impact, not just the percentage shifts, before approving a nationwide change.

*Note: All figures above are derived strictly from the provided test window data; long-term LTV projections require the conversion tracking outlined in step 2.*
*No external benchmarks were used; success is defined solely by FrostByte's own baseline performance.*
