**Executive Summary: Crypto Payment Rollout (April 1–14)**

**Verdict: Do Not Expand Yet.** The rollout is currently **net-negative** and fails the profitability guardrail required for CFO approval. While the hypothesis of higher basket size was confirmed, the unmitigated chargeback rate destroys the margin gain. Expansion to 50% of traffic without resolving the fraud leak would scale losses, not revenue.

**Financial Reconciliation**
*   **Gross Lift:** The Average Order Value (AOV) increased from $85 to $145, a **70% lift** (significantly exceeding the 10% target).
*   **The Leak:** A 2.5% chargeback rate on crypto transactions is catastrophic compared to standard payment methods (typically <0.1%).
*   **Net Reality:** On every $10,000 of crypto GMV, you are losing **$250** directly to chargebacks. Unless your net margin on the remaining 97.5% of orders exceeds 2.5% *plus* the cost of goods sold and processing fees, this segment is operating at a loss. The "success" of higher AOV is an illusion if the revenue is subsequently reversed.

**Decision Logic**
The experiment violated the pre-commitment rule by introducing an unmonitored guardrail (chargebacks) post-hoc. A win on a primary metric (AOV) that causes a regression in a critical guardrail (fraud loss) is a failed test. The current data indicates a specific fraud vector targeting the new payment method, likely due to lower friction or irreversible settlement characteristics exploited by bad actors.

**Required Fraud Metrics Before 50% Expansion**
Do not expand traffic until the following metrics are instrumented, baselined, and shown to be within acceptable risk limits defined by the Risk Compliance team:

1.  **Net Revenue per Visitor (NRV):** Recalculate NRV specifically for the crypto cohort after deducting chargebacks and fraud investigation costs. This must exceed the control group's NRV.
2.  **Chargeback-to-Transaction Ratio by Cohort:** Segment chargebacks by user tenure (new vs. returning) and device fingerprint. If >80% of chargebacks come from new accounts, the vector is likely account creation fraud.
3.  **Time-to-Chargeback Latency:** Measure the days between transaction and dispute. Crypto chargebacks often have different windows than credit cards; understanding this lag is vital for cash flow forecasting.
4.  **False Positive Decline Rate:** As you implement stricter fraud rules to curb the 2.5% rate, monitor how many legitimate high-value crypto users are being blocked.

**This-Week Action Plan**
1.  **Pause Expansion:** Formally halt the plan to scale to 50% traffic. Maintain the 15% cap or reduce it if fraud volume is accelerating.
2.  **Instrument the "Net" Metric:** Update the dashboard to display "Net AOV" (AOV minus expected chargeback loss) as the primary success metric, replacing gross AOV.
3.  **Root Cause Analysis:** Isolate the 2.5% chargeback segment by IP, device ID, and user age. If the fraud is concentrated in a specific pattern (e.g., new users on mobile), implement a targeted rule to block that specific subset rather than banning the payment method entirely.

**Note:** The 2.5% chargeback figure is treated as a hard cost; do not model recovery unless historical data proves a >50% representment win rate for this specific payment type.
**Note:** Ensure all user identifiers used for fraud cohorting are pseudonymized and routed to privacy compliance protocols before sharing with external auditors.
