**Executive Summary: Pricing Experiment Analysis (ArcticLine Series, Q3)**

**Current Outcome:** The 12% discount drove a 28% volume increase in the South (415 units vs. 324 control) but collapsed gross margin from 34% to 21%. The promotion is currently value-destructive; the volume gain did not compensate for the margin erosion.

**Breakdown of Conflicting Signals**
The divergence between volume and profit indicates a shift in customer mix and terms, validating your assumption.
1.  **Volume vs. Price Elasticity:** A 12% price cut yielding 28% volume growth suggests high short-term elasticity. However, elasticity is not profit. The revenue increase was insufficient to cover the deeper discount and the associated cost of extended warranties demanded by the new customer segment.
2.  **Margin Erosion Drivers:** The drop from 34% to 21% implies that for every unit sold, the net contribution decreased significantly. This confirms the influx of lower-LTV customers who require higher service costs (warranties), effectively subsidizing low-quality sales with high-quality margin.
3.  **The 8% Hypothesis:** While an 8% discount might preserve more margin per unit, it risks failing to trigger the volume threshold required to offset fixed costs if the customer base remains price-sensitive. Without a controlled test of the 8% tier, assuming it balances volume and margin is speculative.

**Recommendation: Do Not Roll Out Permanently**
Revert to standard pricing immediately for Q4. The current data proves that discounting the ArcticLine series attracts unprofitable demand. A permanent rollout based on these results would institutionalize margin leakage.

**Decision Framework for Leadership**
To resolve the debate between the CFO and Sales Directors, apply the following logic before considering any future promotion:
*   **The Break-Even Rule:** Any future discount must be pre-calculated to ensure the *incremental* volume covers the *lost* margin per unit plus the variable cost of any added terms (e.g., warranties). If the math does not hold at the planning stage, the test should not run.
*   **Segment Isolation:** The "South" region aggregate masked the quality of sales. Future tests must isolate customer tiers (e.g., enterprise vs. SMB) to prevent high-margin accounts from receiving unnecessary discounts while chasing low-margin volume.
*   **Guardrail Metrics:** Volume cannot be the primary success metric. The primary metric must be **Total Contribution Margin**. If volume rises but total contribution falls, the experiment is a failure regardless of market share gains.

**Immediate Next Steps**
1.  **Halt the Promotion:** End the 12% discount effective immediately to stop margin bleed.
2.  **Model the 8% Scenario:** Calculate the specific volume lift required at an 8% discount to maintain the original 34% margin target, accounting for warranty costs. If the required volume exceeds historical capacity or market size, reject the 8% option.
3.  **Design a Guardrailed Test:** If a Q4 test is mandatory, limit it to a specific customer segment (e.g., new logos only) with a hard cap on warranty terms, measuring total contribution margin rather than unit volume.

**Note:** This analysis relies on the aggregate figures provided; precise break-even points require the exact unit cost and warranty expense data. No external benchmarks were used; all conclusions derive from your internal baseline performance.
