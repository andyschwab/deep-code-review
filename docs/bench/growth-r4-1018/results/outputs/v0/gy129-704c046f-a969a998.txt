**Diagnostic Breakdown: Algorithm B Impact vs. Inventory Confounder**

**Executive Summary**
The 25% increase in click-through rate (CTR) coupled with an 8% drop in purchase volume indicates a severe degradation in conversion efficiency. While the hypothesis of "curiosity clicks" is plausible, the concurrent inventory shortage creates a confounding variable that renders the current aggregate data `UNVERIFIED` for causal attribution. We cannot determine if the revenue drop is due to the algorithm recommending out-of-stock items or the supply chain failure alone without segmenting the data.

**Required Data Segmentation (The Disentanglement Plan)**
To isolate the true impact, recompute the metrics using the following three specific cuts. Do not rely on aggregate averages.

1.  **In-Stock vs. Out-of-Stock Cohort Analysis**
    *   **Action:** Filter transactions to include *only* products that remained fully in-stock throughout the test window.
    *   **Reading Rule:**
        *   If purchase volume for *in-stock* items drops significantly (e.g., >5%) under Algorithm B: The algorithm is failing to match user intent, validating the "frustration" hypothesis. **Decision:** Halt rollout; iterate on model relevance.
        *   If purchase volume for *in-stock* items remains flat or grows: The revenue drop is entirely driven by the inventory shortage (users clicking items that were unavailable). **Decision:** Investigate supply chain; the algorithm's CTR lift may be valid for discovery.

2.  **Click-to-Purchase Conversion by Category**
    *   **Action:** Compare conversion rates (Purchases / Clicks) for the "top-selling categories" (affected by shortage) versus "long-tail categories" (unaffected).
    *   **Reading Rule:**
        *   If conversion drops only in top-selling categories: The shortage is the primary driver.
        *   If conversion drops uniformly across all categories: Algorithm B is systematically degrading purchase intent regardless of inventory status.

3.  **Zero-Result and "Out of Stock" Click Events**
    *   **Action:** Measure the rate at which clicks on Algorithm B recommendations led to an "Out of Stock" landing page or a zero-result search.
    *   **Reading Rule:** A high rate here confirms the algorithm is aggressively surfacing unavailable inventory, artificially inflating CTR while blocking revenue. This is a model training error (not penalizing out-of-stock items), not just a supply chain issue.

**Immediate Next Steps (This Week)**
1.  **Query the Data:** Run the "In-Stock Only" conversion analysis immediately. This is the single metric that decides the path forward.
2.  **Guardrail Check:** Verify if Algorithm B increased clicks on low-margin or clearance items (which might explain high CTR but low revenue impact), ensuring the primary metric remains "Gross Profit per Session," not just volume.
3.  **Decision Tripwire:** If the "In-Stock" conversion rate for Algorithm B is lower than the baseline by more than 2%, stop the experiment and revert to the baseline algorithm before the holiday rush. If the rate is neutral or positive, the issue is purely inventory availability.

**Note:** Do not attribute the 8% drop to the algorithm until the "In-Stock" cohort is isolated; doing so risks fixing the wrong problem.
**Note:** Ensure the user identifier used for these cohorts is pseudonymous and stable across sessions to accurately track the funnel.
