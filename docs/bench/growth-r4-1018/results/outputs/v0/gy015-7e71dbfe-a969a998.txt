### Analysis Plan: Mobile Filter Friction vs. Intent Segmentation

**Objective:** Determine if the 2.7% conversion gap (Desktop 4.2% vs. Mobile 1.5%) and the negative filter correlation on mobile are caused by interface friction or differing user intent.

**1. Segment by Intent Proxy (The "Browsing" Hypothesis)**
Before analyzing the interface, isolate intent to see if mobile users are fundamentally different.
*   **Action:** Create two cohorts for both Desktop and Mobile: "High Intent" (users with items in cart or specific search queries) vs. "Low Intent" (generic browsing, high page depth without cart adds).
*   **Reading Rule:** Compare conversion rates *within* intent cohorts across devices.
    *   *If* Mobile High Intent converts similarly to Desktop High Intent, the gap is driven by **intent mix** (more browsers on mobile).
    *   *If* Mobile High Intent still lags significantly behind Desktop High Intent, the gap is **interface friction**.

**2. Funnel Drop-off Mapping (The "Friction" Hypothesis)**
Pinpoint exactly where the filter interaction diverges.
*   **Action:** Map the step-by-step drop-off for users who click "Filter" vs. those who do not, specifically on mobile. Focus on the steps immediately following filter application: "View Filtered Results" → "Click Product" → "Add to Cart."
*   **Reading Rule:** Look for a spike in exit rate immediately after applying a filter on mobile.
    *   *If* users drop off *during* the filter selection process (e.g., opening the drawer, selecting options), this indicates **usability complexity** (cognitive overload).
    *   *If* users apply filters successfully but drop off on the *results page* (e.g., slow load, poor layout of filtered items), this indicates **rendering or performance friction**.

**3. The "Filter Success" Cohort Analysis**
Test the assumption that filters hurt mobile conversion.
*   **Action:** Compare the conversion rate of mobile users who *successfully* apply a filter and view results against those who attempt but abandon the filter action mid-stream.
*   **Reading Rule:**
    *   *If* completers convert higher than abandoners, the issue is **interaction cost** (the effort to filter is too high, causing abandonment before value is realized).
    *   *If* completers convert *lower* than non-filter users (as your data suggests), investigate the **quality of results**. Are the filters returning irrelevant items on mobile due to sorting logic differences?

**4. Temporal & Contextual Control**
Rule out external variables since the Jan 20th launch.
*   **Action:** Overlay the conversion trend with device-specific traffic sources and time-of-day patterns.
*   **Reading Rule:** If the drop coincides with a shift in mobile traffic sources (e.g., more social referral traffic which is typically lower intent) rather than the filter launch date specifically, the cause is **mix shift**, not the feature.

**Decision Matrix for Q2 Roadmap:**
*   **Fix UI/UX:** If High Intent mobile users drop off during filter selection or if drop-offs spike immediately post-filter application. *Action: Simplify mobile filter drawer, reduce options, improve touch targets.*
*   **Fix Logic/Relevance:** If users apply filters but don't click results, or if filtered results convert poorly compared to unfiltered. *Action: Audit mobile sorting algorithms and result rendering.*
*   **Accept Behavioral Difference:** If High Intent mobile users convert at parity with desktop once intent is controlled. *Action: Optimize for browsing engagement rather than forcing desktop-style conversion flows.*
