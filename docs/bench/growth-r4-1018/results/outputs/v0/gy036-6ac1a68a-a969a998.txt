### Analysis Plan: Diagnosing the Conversion/Retention Divergence

**Objective:** Determine if the simplified onboarding flow (Sept 1) is creating a "false positive" activation where users convert without encountering the core value complexity, leading to early churn.

**1. Cohort Definitions**
Compare two distinct groups to isolate the change impact:
*   **Control Cohort:** Users who started trials between Aug 1–Aug 31 (Pre-simplified flow).
*   **Treatment Cohort:** Users who started trials between Sept 1–Sept 30 (Post-simplified flow).
*   *Segmentation:* Split both cohorts by "Converted" vs. "Did Not Convert" to analyze behavior differences specifically among those who paid.

**2. Behavioral Patterns to Investigate (The "Hidden Complexity" Hypothesis)**
Instrument and query the following events during the **Trial Period** only. The goal is to find actions that correlate with *long-term* retention but were potentially skipped in the new flow.

*   **The "Complexity Encounter" Rate:**
    *   *Question:* Did the user attempt or complete a high-friction core action (e.g., complex integration, advanced report generation) *before* paying?
    *   *Hypothesis:* The old flow forced this encounter (filtering out unready users), while the new flow allows conversion without it.
    *   *Metric:* % of converted users who completed [Core Complex Action] prior to payment. Expect this to be significantly lower in the Treatment Cohort.

*   **Time-to-Value vs. Time-to-Payment:**
    *   *Question:* How quickly did users pay relative to their first meaningful value event?
    *   *Hypothesis:* The new flow accelerates payment before value realization.
    *   *Metric:* Average days from Trial Start → Payment vs. Trial Start → [First Value Event]. A widening gap in the Treatment Cohort indicates "premature conversion."

*   **Feature Breadth During Trial:**
    *   *Question:* Are new converters using a narrower set of features?
    *   *Hypothesis:* Simplified onboarding guides users down a "happy path" that ignores essential but complex features needed for long-term stickiness.
    *   *Metric:* Average number of distinct feature categories used during the trial.

**3. Decision Matrix for the Head of Growth**
Each analysis outcome triggers a specific action:

*   **Scenario A: Treatment cohort skipped "Complexity Encounter" entirely.**
    *   *Diagnosis:* The flow is hiding necessary setup. Users are buying a product they haven't configured to succeed.
    *   *Action:* **Do not revert.** Instead, insert a mandatory "complexity checkpoint" or guided setup task *post-conversion* but *before* day 7 to force value realization early.

*   **Scenario B: Treatment cohort has similar behavior but lower retention.**
    *   *Diagnosis:* The issue is not behavioral skipping; the simplified flow may be attracting a fundamentally different audience (qualification issue) or the product experience post-pay is broken for new users.
    *   *Action:* **Tweak qualification criteria.** Add friction back to the trial start (e.g., require company size or use-case selection) to filter out low-intent leads before they enter the funnel.

*   **Scenario C: "Complexity Encounter" users in the Treatment Cohort still churn at high rates.**
    *   *Diagnosis:* The product complexity itself is the problem, not the flow. The old flow just hid this churn until later.
    *   *Action:* **Product Fix.** Invest in simplifying the core complex action itself rather than adjusting the funnel.

**This Week's Steps:**
1.  Query the % of Sept 1+ converters who completed [Core Complex Action] before payment compared to August.
2.  If the rate dropped by >20%, design an A/B test inserting a "setup milestone" email sequence for new payers to drive that specific action.
3.  If the rate is unchanged, prepare a proposal to add a qualifying question to the trial sign-up form to test audience quality.

*Note: All metrics must be calculated using your internal analytics data; no external benchmarks are applied. Ensure user IDs are pseudonymized for this analysis.*
