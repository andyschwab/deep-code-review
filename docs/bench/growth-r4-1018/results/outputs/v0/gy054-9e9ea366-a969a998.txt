**Recommendation: Adopt "Weekly Active Translators" (WAT) as the North Star Metric**

Define **Weekly Active Translators** as users who perform at least one successful translation action on three or more distinct days within a rolling seven-day window. Do not use "Pages Translated per Session" (depth) or "Daily Active Users" (raw frequency) as your primary metric.

**Supporting Logic for the Strategy Review**

1.  **Retention Correlation:** Your own data indicates that users translating fewer pages but returning daily possess higher long-term value. Depth (pages/session) is a vanity metric in this context; it captures single-session bursts (e.g., a student translating a whole document once) that do not predict habit formation. Frequency of return is the leading indicator of habit. By requiring activity on *three distinct days*, WAT filters out one-off users while capturing the "daily returner" cohort you identified as valuable, without being as noisy as raw DAU.
2.  **Revenue Alignment:** Premium tier conversion in utility plugins typically follows a "freemium habit" model. Users pay when the tool becomes indispensable to their daily workflow, not when they process high volumes in a single sitting. WAT directly measures the stickiness required to trigger upgrade consideration. If WAT rises, the pool of users ready for conversion expands; if only depth rises, you are optimizing for heavy but churn-prone usage.
3.  **Acquisition Efficiency:** Prioritizing WAT aligns marketing spend with quality. If you optimize for raw installs or single-session depth, acquisition costs will rise while retention leaks. Optimizing for WAT forces the product and marketing teams to focus on onboarding flows that drive *second and third day* usage, which your retention curves prove is the actual growth lever.

**Input Metrics to Move the North Star**
To influence WAT, track these two inputs:
*   **Day-2 Return Rate:** The percentage of new installs who return to translate on the second day. This validates the initial "aha" moment.
*   **Notification/Trigger Click-Through:** The rate at which users engage with context-aware prompts (e.g., "Translate this page?") on subsequent visits.
