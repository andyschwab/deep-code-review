**Executive Summary**
The current pilot data is **UNVERIFIED** for predicting network-wide success. While a 20% reduction in triage time is observed, the 8-point drop in patient satisfaction represents a critical guardrail failure. Extrapolating results from 3 urban hospitals to 40 predominantly rural facilities introduces high risk of demographic mismatch and behavioral variance. The hypothesis that speed gains stem from skipping non-essential questions suggests a potential compromise in care quality or patient rapport, which may be exacerbated in rural settings where patient-provider relationships often differ from urban centers.

**Key Risks in Extrapolation**

1.  **Demographic & Contextual Mismatch (Urban vs. Rural)**
    The pilot sample (100% urban) does not represent the target population (majority rural). Rural hospitals often face different patient acuity mixes, connectivity constraints, and distinct cultural expectations regarding physician interaction time. A workflow optimized for high-volume urban throughput may fail or cause deeper satisfaction drops in rural contexts where "speed" might be perceived as "rushed care."

2.  **Sample Size & Statistical Volatility**
    With only 150 doctors across 3 sites, the data lacks the power to detect variance in rare but critical events (e.g., mis-triage due to skipped questions). The 8-point satisfaction drop could be noise, a localized cultural reaction, or a systemic flaw; the current N is insufficient to distinguish these without deeper cohort analysis.

3.  **Behavioral Mechanism Uncertainty**
    The assumption that doctors are "skipping non-essential questions" is **UNVERIFIED**. Without event-level instrumentation tracking which fields are skipped versus completed, we cannot confirm if the speed gain is efficient workflow or dangerous corner-cutting. If the latter, scaling this behavior network-wide amplifies clinical risk.

4.  **Learning Curve vs. Fundamental Flaw**
    It is unknown if the satisfaction dip is transient (doctors learning the tool) or structural (patients inherently dislike the new interaction model). The pilot duration (starting August 1st) may be too short to observe stabilization.

**Critical Questions to Answer Before Rollout**

To move from **UNVERIFIED** assumptions to data-driven decisions, the following must be instrumented and measured:

*   **Event Taxonomy Question:** *Which specific triage steps are being skipped or shortened?*
    *   **Action:** Instrument events for "question skipped," "section auto-completed," and "time-per-question." Correlate these directly with individual patient satisfaction scores.
*   **Retention/Adoption Question:** *Does patient satisfaction recover over time (cohort analysis), or does it degrade?*
    *   **Action:** Segment satisfaction scores by "weeks since pilot start" for both doctors and patients. If the gap widens or stays flat after week 4, it is a fundamental flaw, not a learning curve.
*   **Generalizability Question:** *How does the "speed vs. satisfaction" trade-off perform in a rural context?*
    *   **Action:** Run a targeted A/B test or limited pilot in 2–3 rural hospitals *before* full rollout. Use the urban data only as a baseline, not a benchmark.
*   **Guardrail Question:** *Is the 20% speed gain correlated with increased re-triage or downstream errors?*
    *   **Action:** Measure "return to triage within 24 hours" or "escalation rate" as a primary guardrail metric. If speed increases while errors rise, the North Star (efficiency) is vanity.
