### Recommended KPIs for Clinical Advisory Board

To satisfy the clinical advisory board and bridge the gap between engagement and medical value, replace the vanity metric of "daily app opens" with the following three outcome-oriented indicators. These metrics move from raw sensor data to actionable health insights.

**1. Clinical Activation Rate (The "Aha" Moment)**
*   **Definition:** The percentage of users who complete a valid 30-day baseline measurement and successfully generate a shareable HRV trend report.
*   **Why it matters:** Daily opens are vanity; they do not prove the device works. This metric proves the system captures enough high-quality data to produce a clinical signal. It directly addresses the hypothesis gap regarding report generation.
*   **Instrumentation Question:** "Do users retain the device long enough to generate a statistically significant 30-day trend?"
*   **Action Rule:** If this rate is low despite high daily opens, the friction lies in data continuity or report generation logic, not user interest. Fix the data pipeline before scaling acquisition.

**2. Provider Engagement Loop (The Validation Signal)**
*   **Definition:** The percentage of generated reports that are explicitly shared with a healthcare provider (via PDF export, secure link, or EHR integration) within 48 hours of generation.
*   **Why it matters:** This validates the core hypothesis that the data is actionable enough for patients to involve their doctors. Without this step, the device remains a wellness toy rather than a medical tool.
*   **Instrumentation Question:** "Does the data compel the user to seek professional review?"
*   **Action Rule:** If users generate reports but never share them, the clinical relevance is unproven. The next step is qualitative interviews with non-sharers to understand if the report format is unclear or if the perceived value is too low.

**3. Physiological Response Cohort (The Outcome Proxy)**
*   **Definition:** The percentage of users showing a statistically significant improvement (e.g., >5% increase in RMSSD or decrease in resting heart rate) after 60 days, segmented by adherence level.
*   **Why it matters:** You assumed resting heart rate is the signal, but for elderly demographics, HRV complexity (RMSSD) is often more sensitive to autonomic nervous system changes than raw heart rate. This metric moves from "average change" (which hides non-responders) to "responder rate."
*   **Instrumentation Question:** "Is the intervention causing a measurable physiological shift in a meaningful portion of the cohort?"
*   **Action Rule:** Do not assert a benchmark for "good" improvement. Compare the 60-day cohort against their own 30-day baseline. If the responder rate is flat across high-adherence users, the underlying health hypothesis may be flawed, requiring a pivot in the clinical claim before the June launch.
