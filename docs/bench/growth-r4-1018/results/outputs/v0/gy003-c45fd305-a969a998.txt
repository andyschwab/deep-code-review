**Recommendation: Prioritize the Restaurant Selection-to-Menu Stage**

Allocate the upcoming quarter's engineering budget exclusively to optimizing the drop-off between restaurant selection and menu viewing. While the payment stage has a lower abandonment rate (10%), the sheer volume of loss at the earlier stage (60%) represents a significantly larger revenue opportunity. Fixing the checkout flow addresses a leak in a bucket that is already mostly empty; optimizing the selection screen fills the bucket.

**Rationale for the Regional Director**
With 5,000 daily orders currently completing the funnel, we can model the impact based on your provided drop-off rates.
*   **Current State:** If 5,000 orders reach payment (90% retention of those who viewed the menu), approximately 5,555 users viewed a menu. If only 5,555 users viewed a menu after a 60% drop-off from selection, roughly 13,888 users selected a restaurant daily.
*   **The Opportunity:** A modest 10% relative improvement in the selection-to-menu conversion (reducing drop-off from 60% to 54%) would yield ~750 additional daily users viewing menus. Even if only 10% of those convert to orders, that is ~75 extra daily orders.
*   **Comparison:** To match this gain by fixing payment, you would need to reduce the 10% payment abandonment to near zero (an unrealistic 100% relative improvement) to capture the remaining ~555 lost users, and even then, the ceiling is lower than the selection stage.

The hypothesis that "menu loading time" or "overwhelming choice" causes the friction must be validated before coding. Do not assume the cause; instrument to find it.

**Key Performance Indicators (KPIs) to Monitor**
1.  **Primary North Star Input:** *Selection-to-Menu Conversion Rate.* This is the specific lever we are pulling.
2.  **Guardrail Metric:** *Time-to-Interactive (Menu).* Ensure that attempts to simplify the list do not degrade technical performance.
3.  **Downstream Validation:** *Menu-to-Order Conversion Rate.* Verify that the users we successfully push to the menu page are still high-quality and not bouncing immediately after.

**This Week's Action Plan**
1.  **Instrument the "Why":** Add events to the restaurant list capturing *scroll depth*, *time spent*, and *filter usage* before the drop-off. This distinguishes between "overwhelmed" (high scroll, no click) and "technical friction" (immediate exit).
2.  **Run a Segmented Cohort Analysis:** Compare the drop-off rate for users on high-end vs. low-end devices and 4G vs. Wi-Fi. If the drop-off correlates strongly with device speed, your hypothesis about loading time is correct. If it is uniform, the issue is likely choice overload.
3.  **Design the Test:** Prepare an A/B test for next month where Variant A reduces the visible list to 10 curated options with a "Load More" button (testing choice overload) and Variant B implements aggressive lazy-loading and skeleton screens (testing performance). Pre-commit to a sample size of 20,000 exposures per variant to detect a 2% lift with statistical significance.
