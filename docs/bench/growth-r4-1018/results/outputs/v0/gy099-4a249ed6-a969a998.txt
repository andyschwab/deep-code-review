**Decision: Stop this specific test variant; do not extend duration.**

The 0.2% difference over a 16-day period with ~950 monthly visitors indicates the test lacked the statistical power to detect anything but a massive, unrealistic lift. Extending the duration to chase significance on such a tiny delta is a waste of resources and risks "peeking" bias without a pre-committed sequential design. The flat result suggests the hypothesis (artisan stories vs. product grids) did not move the needle for this audience, or the metric used was too distal from value.

**Interpretation of Signals**
Do not look for "noise" in the aggregate click-through rate (CTR). Instead, segment the existing data to rule out masking effects before killing the direction entirely:
1.  **New vs. Returning Visitors:** Did the story-driven layout confuse returning buyers looking for specific goods, while resonating with new users? If new user engagement rose but overall CTR stayed flat, the hypothesis has merit for acquisition, not retention.
2.  **Device Split:** If mobile traffic dominates and the layout broke the mobile experience (e.g., long text blocks pushing products below the fold), the failure is UX, not content strategy.
3.  **Downstream Behavior:** Did CTR stay flat but *time on page* or *add-to-cart* rates increase? If users engaged with the stories but didn't click the hero button, the "engagement" hypothesis might be right, but the Call-to-Action (CTA) placement was wrong.
