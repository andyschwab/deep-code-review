**Deliverable: Top Three Metrics for EcoStat Firmware Update (Board Briefing)**

To distinguish between passive ownership and active value realization for the Series A evaluation, track these three metrics. They prioritize **retention of value** over vanity volume, reading the funnel from the bottom up.

1.  **Weekly Active Schedulers (The North Star)**
    *   **Definition:** The count of unique devices where a user *modifies* or *confirms* an automated temperature schedule at least once per week.
    *   **Why this matters:** This rejects "static temperature" usage. A device running a static setpoint is a thermostat; a device with an active, evolving schedule is a *smart* energy partner. This metric rises only when the user derives the core promised value (automation).
    *   **Board Signal:** High numbers prove "stickiness" and product-market fit. Low numbers indicate users are ignoring the smart features, signaling a risk for long-term satisfaction despite hardware sales.
    *   **Instrumentation Requirement:** You must instrument the specific event `schedule_modified` or `schedule_confirmed` with a pseudonymous device ID. Without this, any claim of engagement is `UNVERIFIED`.

2.  **Schedule Retention Cohort (Week 4)**
    *   **Definition:** The percentage of users who activated a schedule in Week 1 who are still actively using (modifying or relying on) that schedule in Week 4.
    *   **Why this matters:** Acquisition (1,200 units sold) is vanity if the feature is abandoned. This measures whether the "aha moment" of automation sticks. It isolates the leak: do users try the feature and quit?
    *   **Board Signal:** This is your true health indicator. If Week 4 retention is low, pouring money into more sales (Series A) will only increase return rates later. Fix retention before scaling acquisition.
    *   **Instrumentation Requirement:** Track the `first_schedule_created` event to establish the cohort start date. Compare the user's own baseline from the January soft launch against post-June update cohorts.

3.  **Cloud Sync Success Rate (Reliability Guardrail)**
    *   **Definition:** The ratio of successful cloud handshakes to total connection attempts per device per day.
    *   **Why this matters:** You cannot distinguish "running in the background" from "broken connectivity" without this. If a user isn't scheduling, is it apathy or a firmware bug preventing cloud logic? This separates product behavior from system health.
    *   **Board Signal:** Demonstrates hardware/firmware reliability. A high drop-off here invalidates the other two metrics (you can't schedule if you can't connect).
    *   **Instrumentation Requirement:** Log `cloud_sync_attempt` and `cloud_sync_result` (success/fail). Note: Use a stable, pseudonymous device ID for cohorting, but route any raw MAC addresses or PII to your privacy compliance protocol; do not log PII in analytics events.

**Critical Implementation Notes for the Board:**
*   **No Benchmarks:** We will not compare these numbers to industry averages (which are often fabricated or irrelevant to hardware). Success is defined by an *upward trend* in our own Week 4 retention and Weekly Active Schedulers compared to the pre-June baseline.
*   **This Week's Step:** Before the June update ships, define the exact event names (`schedule_modified`, `cloud_sync_attempt`) in your telemetry pipeline. If these events are not logging by Day 1 post-update, the Series A narrative rests on `UNVERIFIED` assumptions. Do not guess the numbers; instrument the question.

*Note: These metrics assume the firmware update includes the necessary telemetry hooks. If the current build does not log `schedule_modified`, the value for Metric 1 will be zero until patched.*
