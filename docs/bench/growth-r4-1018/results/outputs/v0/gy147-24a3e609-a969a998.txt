### Framework for Evaluating User Health: The "Safe Value" North Star

**North Star Metric:** Weekly Active Users placing ≥1 verified bet within responsible gambling limits.
*Why:* This rejects vanity metrics (raw signups or session length) and ties growth directly to realized value (a placed bet) while hard-coding compliance (verified limits) as a prerequisite for success.

**The Tension: Engagement vs. Guardrails**
Your data presents a classic conflict between growth optics and behavioral reality. The 40% spike in Daily Active Users (DAU) paired with a drop in bets per user (4.2 → 2.8) creates two competing narratives:
1.  **Growth View:** New users are "warming up" (healthy activation lag).
2.  **Compliance/Risk View:** Friction in the checkout flow or odds display is preventing conversion, or users are hitting hidden responsible gambling limits prematurely.

You cannot resolve this by averaging metrics. High session length is a **vanity metric** here; in a regulated market, a long session without a bet often indicates confusion or failed compliance checks, not engagement. Conversely, aggressive bet volume targets can inadvertently push users toward risky behavior, violating your regulatory mandate.

**The Measurement Method (Bottom-Up)**
Do not pour more acquisition into this mix until you diagnose the retention of the core action (placing a bet).

1.  **Segment by Cohort, Not Aggregate:** Separate the May 10–12 traffic into "New Users" vs. "Returning Users."
    *   If the drop to 2.8 bets/user is isolated to **New Users**, your hypothesis about caution may hold.
    *   If **Returning Users** also dropped frequency, you have a platform friction issue or a market-specific event anomaly (e.g., odds were uncompetitive during finals).
2.  **Instrument the "Compliance Seam":** You must instrument specific events that answer named questions:
    *   *Event:* `limit_check_triggered` → *Question:* "Are users dropping off because they hit deposit/loss limits?"
    *   *Event:* `bet_slip_abandoned_post_calc` → *Question:* "Is the friction in the odds calculation or the final confirmation?"
    *   *Event:* `responsible_gambling_warning_viewed` → *Question:* "Does seeing a warning correlate with session termination?"
3.  **Define Guardrails:** Your primary growth metric (Bets Placed) must have a hard guardrail: **Incidence of Limit Breaches**. If bets go up but limit breaches rise, the "growth" is unsustainable and legally risky.

**Reading the Data**
Compare these figures only against your own baseline from the previous major sporting event, not industry averages (which are unavailable and likely irrelevant to your specific regulatory jurisdiction). If the "cautious new user" theory is true, you should see a cohort curve where bets-per-user rises in Week 2. If it stays flat, the drop is friction, not caution.

**This Week's Steps**
1.  **Query:** Run a cohort analysis splitting May 10–12 users by "New" vs. "Returning" to isolate where the 2.8 bets/user average is dragging down.
2.  **Instrument:** Add a single event `checkout_flow_exit_reason` capturing whether the exit was due to "limit reached," "odds change," or "technical error."
3.  **Decide:** If >15% of new users exit at the limit check, pause acquisition spend and review the limit UI clarity before the next major event.

*Note: Do not fabricate a "good" retention rate; use your pre-finals baseline as the only valid comparison. Ensure all user identifiers used for cohort tracking are pseudonymized and routed to your privacy compliance protocol.*
