**Comparative Analysis: Recommendation Engine Primary Metric for Sonido**

**Context:** Podcast discovery app launching in Argentina; Beta data (3,000 users) shows short-form clips drive +40% start frequency but -10 min session duration. Decision needed by Oct 20 for engineering roadmap. Audience: Founder and Head of Content.

### Option A: Optimize for Episode Start Frequency
*Focus: Discovery breadth and trial behavior.*

**Pros:**
*   **Aligns with "Discovery" Promise:** Directly measures the engine's ability to surface new content, reducing search friction for users overwhelmed by choice.
*   **Captures Early Funnel Momentum:** High frequency indicates successful activation and the "aha" moment of finding something new quickly.
*   **Supports Short-Form Strategy:** Validates the beta finding that clips drive engagement, potentially lowering the barrier to entry for new podcast listeners in the LATAM market.

**Cons:**
*   **Vanity Risk:** As seen in beta, this metric can rise while actual value delivery falls. Users may click but not listen, inflating numbers without building habit.
*   **Misaligned with Retention:** Starting an episode does not prove value consumption. If users abandon episodes quickly, long-term subscription retention will likely suffer despite high start counts.
*   **Content Quality Dilution:** May incentivize the engine to promote clickbaity or low-quality short clips over deep-dive content that builds loyal subscribers.

### Option B: Optimize for Total Listening Duration
*Focus: Value depth and content resonance.*

**Pros:**
*   **Stronger Retention Predictor:** Time spent is a proxy for value received. Users who listen longer are statistically more likely to perceive the product as useful and convert to paid subscriptions.
*   **Filters Noise:** Automatically deprioritizes content that users start but immediately skip, ensuring the recommendation engine learns from actual consumption, not just clicks.
*   **Sustainable Business Model:** Aligns engineering efforts with content quality and user satisfaction, which are critical for subscription revenue in a competitive market.

**Cons:**
*   **Slower Feedback Loop:** Duration metrics take longer to accumulate than start events, potentially slowing down iteration cycles for the recommendation algorithm.
*   **Barrier to Entry:** Might suppress the visibility of short-form content that serves as an effective onboarding tool for new users unfamiliar with podcast formats.
*   **Context Blindness:** Does not distinguish between one user listening for 2 hours vs. two users listening for 1 hour; needs normalization (e.g., duration per session) to be actionable.

### Recommendation
**Select Total Listening Duration (normalized per active user) as the North Star Metric.**

While start frequency measures *activity*, duration measures *value*. Your beta data already reveals the trap: a 40% lift in starts coupled with a 10-minute drop in duration suggests you are optimizing for "clicks" rather than "listening." In a subscription model, retention is driven by users finding content they love enough to stick with, not just content they are curious enough to click. Frequency is a useful input metric to monitor activation, but it should not be the primary optimization target if it cannibalizes depth.

**Next Steps (This Week):**
1.  **Define the Metric:** Formalize "Weekly Listening Duration per Active User" as the North Star, excluding background idle time to ensure accuracy.
2.  **Instrument the Question:** Tag the "episode_start" and "playback_complete/skip" events to calculate the "Completion Rate" as a guardrail metric; if duration rises but completion falls, investigate content mismatch.
3.  **Validate the Hypothesis:** Run a cohort analysis on the beta data correlating Week 1 listening duration against Week 4 retention to confirm duration is the leading indicator for your specific Argentine user base before finalizing the roadmap.
