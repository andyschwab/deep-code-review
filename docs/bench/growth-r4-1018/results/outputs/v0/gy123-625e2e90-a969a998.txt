**Strategic Brief: Refining Churn Prediction for Resource Efficiency and Enterprise Coverage**

**Current State & Risk**
The current model (threshold 0.6) achieves 85% recall but generates excessive false positives (40% of active users), draining support capacity. Simply raising the threshold to 0.8 risks reducing false positives at the cost of missing subtle churn signals from high-value enterprise clients, whose behavioral baselines differ from standard users.

**Recommended Approach: Segmented Thresholds & Behavioral Signal Expansion**
Do not apply a global threshold change. Instead, implement a **segmented modeling strategy** that treats enterprise and standard users as distinct populations with different risk profiles and cost functions.

**1. Immediate Action: Split the Population**
*   **Segment A (Enterprise/High-LTV):** Maintain a lower sensitivity threshold (e.g., 0.5–0.6) to preserve early warning coverage. The cost of a false negative (losing an enterprise client) vastly outweighs the cost of a false positive (a unnecessary check-in call).
*   **Segment B (Standard/SMB):** Increase the confidence threshold (e.g., to 0.75–0.8). For this segment, the cost of support outreach is high relative to individual account value, and false positives are the primary inefficiency.

**2. Analyze Additional Behavioral Signals (Enterprise Specific)**
To reduce false positives in the enterprise segment without raising the threshold, engineer features that distinguish "at-risk" behavior from "normal enterprise variance":
*   **Multi-User Engagement Depth:** Unlike single-user SMBs, enterprise churn often precedes a drop in *unique* active users across the account, even if total login counts remain stable. Track the ratio of active seats to licensed seats.
*   **Feature Adoption Breadth:** Enterprise clients often have "power users" masking overall stagnation. Measure the percentage of the account utilizing core modules vs. a single user driving all activity. A narrowing of feature usage is a stronger signal than total volume.
*   **Support Ticket Sentiment & Type:** Differentiate between "how-to" questions (activation/growth) and "friction/complaint" tickets. A spike in friction tickets from multiple users is a higher-fidelity churn signal than volume alone.
*   **Admin Activity:** Monitor admin-level actions (billing updates, user provisioning/de-provisioning). Sudden cessation of admin activity is a leading indicator for enterprise churn that standard models miss.

**3. Validation & Guardrails**
*   **Retrospective Simulation:** Before deploying, run the proposed segmented thresholds against historical data from January 10 to present. Quantify the reduction in false positives for Segment B and verify that known churned enterprise clients in Segment A would still have been flagged.
*   **Define Success Metrics:**
    *   *Efficiency:* Reduction in support hours spent on false positive outreach (Target: >30% reduction).
    *   *Coverage:* Recall rate specifically for Enterprise churn (Target: Maintain ≥85%).
    *   *Precision:* Improvement in precision for the Standard segment.

**Next Steps (This Week)**
1.  **Data Engineering:** Tag all historical events with an `account_tier` property to enable segmented analysis.
2.  **Analysis:** Run a cohort analysis comparing behavioral features of "False Positive" enterprise accounts vs. "True Positive" churned enterprise accounts to isolate the differentiating signals listed above.
3.  **Decision Gate:** Present the simulated impact of segmented thresholds to leadership by Friday, showing the projected hours saved vs. the risk exposure for enterprise clients.

**Note:** Do not adjust the global threshold until the segmented simulation confirms enterprise coverage remains intact. Unverified assumptions about enterprise behavior must be tested against actual churned enterprise logs.
