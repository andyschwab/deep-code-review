**Executive Summary: Social Sharing Feature Test (FitTrack)**

**Verdict:** The quantitative dataset is **compromised and unsalvageable** for statistical inference regarding user interest or feature viability. No causal claims can be made from this run.

**Reasoning:**
The experiment failed the fundamental requirement of randomization integrity. A 5% crash rate concentrated on older iOS devices introduces severe **selection bias**. The remaining 95% of the sample is no longer representative of the total population; it systematically excludes users with older hardware. Any observed difference in engagement between the control and treatment groups is now confounded by device capability rather than the feature itself. Attempting to "adjust" for this statistically is impossible without the exact demographic breakdown of the crashed sessions, which is missing. Furthermore, stopping the test after four hours based on an interim look at the data constitutes **peeking**, which invalidates standard significance thresholds even if the sample were unbiased.

**Recommended Action Plan:**

1.  **Quantitative Stance:** Declare the current A/B results `UNVERIFIED`. Do not calculate lift, conversion rates, or p-values from this window. Presenting these numbers to stakeholders risks making product decisions based on noise and bias.
2.  **Qualitative Salvage:** While the *numbers* are broken, the *behavior* of the 5% who crashed offers immediate directional insight.
    *   **Action:** Immediately pull session logs and crash reports for the affected iOS users.
    *   **Goal:** Determine if users attempted to engage with the social sharing feature before the crash. If logs show high intent (e.g., button clicks) followed immediately by crashes, this signals strong latent demand that was thwarted by technical debt, not a lack of feature interest.
    *   **Supplement:** If contact info is available and consent allows, survey a small sample of the crashed users asking: "What were you trying to do when the app closed?"
3.  **Next Experiment Design:**
    *   **Fix First:** Resolve the stability bug. A feature that crashes 5% of the base is a net negative regardless of engagement potential.
    *   **Re-run with Pre-commitment:** Relaunch the test only after the fix. Pre-commit to a sample size and duration calculated to detect your Minimum Detectable Effect (MDE).
    *   **Guardrails:** Set a hard automatic stop trigger if crash rates exceed 0.1% in the treatment group.
    *   **Segmentation:** Ensure device type (OS version, model year) is captured as a covariate to allow for stratified analysis post-test.

**Conclusion for VP of Product:**
The feature concept's viability remains an open question. This test measured our technical debt, not user desire. We cannot approve engineering resources for feature iteration until the stability baseline is restored. The immediate investment should be in fixing the crash, followed by a rigorous, full-duration re-test.

**Note:** The "5% crash" metric is a critical guardrail failure; any future test must treat stability as a primary success criterion, not just an operational detail.
**Note:** Do not attempt to impute missing data for the crashed users; the bias is structural, not random.
