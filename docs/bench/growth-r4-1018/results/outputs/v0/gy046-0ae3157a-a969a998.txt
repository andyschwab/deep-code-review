**Executive Summary: Search Algorithm Rollout Decision**

Do not proceed with a company-wide rollout based on current data. The contradiction between a 15% rise in "successful searches" and a 10% rise in "cannot find document" tickets indicates a critical flaw in your success metric, not a mixed signal. The behavioral definition of success (click + no immediate bounce) is being gamed by the new algorithm's likely bias toward newer documents. Users are clicking fresh results, failing to find the specific legacy information they need, and then leaving the search session to file a support ticket rather than clicking "back." The behavioral data measures engagement, not value delivery; the support tickets measure actual failure. Trust the support tickets as the leading indicator of business risk, especially for engineering where legacy documentation is often critical for maintenance and compliance.

**Required Qualitative Feedback to Resolve Contradiction**
Before the August 30th decision, you must validate the source of the support tickets to determine if this is a "legacy doc hiding" issue or general confusion. Execute these three actions this week:

1.  **Ticket Triage & Correlation:** Manually sample 50 recent "cannot find document" tickets. Cross-reference the user IDs and timestamps against the A/B test logs. Specifically, check if these users performed a search during the test window and if the top results served were "new" documents unrelated to their likely intent. If >40% of sampled tickets correlate with users who clicked new docs but failed their task, the algorithm is actively hiding critical assets.
2.  **Targeted User Interviews:** Identify 5 employees who filed tickets during the test period. Conduct 15-minute interviews asking: "What specific legacy document were you trying to find?" and "Did the search results show you relevant older docs?" This confirms whether the pain point is visibility of legacy content or general usability.
3.  **Query Log Analysis for "Zero-Result" Intent:** Query your search logs for high-frequency terms associated with known legacy systems (e.g., "mainframe," "v1 API," "deprecated"). Compare the click-through rate (CTR) on legacy documents for these terms between the control and test groups. If CTR on legacy docs dropped significantly in the test group while overall clicks rose, you have confirmed the cannibalization hypothesis.

**Decision Framework**
*   **Stop the rollout** if the triage confirms users cannot find specific, critical legacy documents they previously could. The cost of engineering downtime outweighs the gain in "successful" clicks on new content.
*   **Proceed with modification** only if the tickets are unrelated to legacy docs (e.g., general confusion about UI changes). In this case, re-weight the ranking algorithm to boost authority/age for specific query classes before expanding.

The North Star for internal tools is "time to correct information," not "clicks." Until you prove the new algorithm preserves access to critical legacy knowledge, the 15% lift is vanity masking a 10% increase in operational friction.

*Do not calculate a "net positive" by averaging the percentages; the 10% failure rate on critical tasks is a binary blocker for engineering tools.*
