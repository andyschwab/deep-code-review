### Top 3 Events to Instrument for Wishlist Launch

Instruct your contractor to record only these three specific actions. Do not track every click on the heart icon; tracking "clicks" creates noise without answering if the feature works. We only care about successful outcomes and their value.

**1. `wishlist_add_success`**
*   **What to record:** The moment an item is successfully saved to a user's list.
*   **Data to include:** A stable User ID (pseudonymous, not email), the Product ID, and the timestamp.
*   **Why:** This measures **Activation**. It tells us if users find the feature useful enough to use it. If this number is zero, the button is broken or hidden.

**2. `wishlist_purchase_conversion`**
*   **What to record:** A purchase event where at least one item in the cart was previously on that user's wishlist.
*   **Data to include:** The Order ID, Total Order Value, and a list of Product IDs that matched the wishlist.
*   **Why:** This measures **Value**. It directly tests your assumption that wishlists lead to sales. Without linking the purchase back to the wishlist, you cannot prove the feature generates revenue.

**3. `wishlist_remove_item`**
*   **What to record:** When a user manually deletes an item from their list.
*   **Data to include:** User ID and Product ID.
*   **Why:** This measures **Intent Change**. If users add items and then immediately remove them, the "Add" button might be misleading, or users are using the list as a temporary "maybe" bin rather than a desire list. This helps distinguish between "saving for later" and "cleaning up."

---

### How to Read the Data (Simple Instructions)

Once the data is flowing, ignore total pageviews or raw click counts. Look at these two calculations to decide if the feature is working:

1.  **The Conversion Rate:** Divide the number of `wishlist_purchase_conversion` events by the total `wishlist_add_success` events over a 7-day window.
    *   *Decision Rule:* If fewer than 10% of added items are bought within 7 days, your assumption that "wishlists equal future sales" is weak. You may need to add email reminders for wishlist items instead of waiting passively.
2.  **The Churn Signal:** Compare the count of `wishlist_remove_item` to `wishlist_add_success` within 24 hours.
    *   *Decision Rule:* If more than 30% of added items are removed within a day, users are likely treating the wishlist as a cart substitute or are confused by the UI. Investigate the user flow immediately.

**Note on Identity:** Ensure your developer uses a consistent, anonymous User ID for these events so you can track the same person from "Add" to "Purchase" across different days. Do not use raw email addresses in logs to protect customer privacy.
