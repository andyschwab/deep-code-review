### Executive Decision Framework: StreamLine Paywall Experiment

**Recommendation: DO NOT ROLL OUT. Revert to control immediately.**

The current data indicates a compound negative impact on revenue that cannot be justified by unverified long-term hypotheses. The experiment violates the prime directive of retaining existing value before optimizing acquisition quality.

#### 1. The Revenue Reality (Short-Term Verification)
We must reject the assumption that "stricter gates filter low-intent users" without data proving higher eventual conversion. Currently, the math is definitive:
*   **Acquisition:** +25% free sign-ups (Vanity Metric: rising regardless of value).
*   **Conversion:** -12% paid conversions (Actionable Metric: real revenue loss).
*   **Retention:** +8% churn among *existing* paid users (Critical Failure).

The rise in existing user churn is the deciding factor. Moving the gate for *new* users should not alter the experience for *existing* loyal customers unless the implementation was flawed. An 8% increase in churn among paying users suggests the new flow introduced friction or confusion that affected the broader user base, or that the "stricter" environment degraded the perceived value for everyone. This is an immediate revenue leak that outweighs any potential future gain from unproven user quality.

#### 2. The "Quality" Hypothesis is UNVERIFIED
You stated there is no data on the long-term Lifetime Value (LTV) of the new free users. Therefore, the claim that these users are "higher quality" is **UNVERIFIED**.
*   We cannot trade a known 12% drop in immediate conversion and an 8% spike in churn for a hypothetical future benefit.
*   Without a pre-committed measurement plan tracking these specific cohorts over 3–6 months, asserting they will convert better is speculation, not analysis.

#### 3. Decision Logic for Leadership
| Signal | Interpretation | Action Implication |
| :--- | :--- | :--- |
| **Paid Conversion -12%** | The friction is too high for ready-to-buy users. | Reject rollout. |

#### 4. This Week's Steps
1.  **Revert the Change:** Restore the original registration flow by Thursday to stop the 8% churn bleed among existing payers. This protects the current revenue baseline.
2.  **Instrument the Cohort:** Before re-testing, define the specific event taxonomy to track "Free User → Paid Conversion" over 90 days for any future variant. Do not run another test until you can measure the *actual* LTV, not just the sign-up count.
3.  **Root Cause Analysis:** Investigate why existing paid users were exposed to or affected by the new gate. This should have been impossible; if it occurred, it is a product bug or segmentation failure, not a user behavior trend.

**Final Verdict:** The experiment failed its guardrails (existing user retention) and its primary metric (paid conversion). The "quality" argument is unsupported by data. Revert now, fix the segmentation logic, and design a new test that isolates new users entirely while pre-committing to a 90-day LTV readout.
