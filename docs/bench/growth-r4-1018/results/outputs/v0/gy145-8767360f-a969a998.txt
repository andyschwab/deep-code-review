**Subject:** Evaluation of March Pricing Experiment Sustainability (CA Market)

**Executive Summary**
The observed 5% revenue increase coupled with a 22% rise in Customer Acquisition Cost (CAC) presents a high-risk signal of a **pull-forward effect** rather than sustainable value perception. The hypothesis that higher prices signaled quality is **UNVERIFIED** without controlled attribution data; the concurrent CAC spike suggests we may be paying significantly more to acquire customers who are simply converting earlier than they otherwise would have. Without correcting for this timing shift, Q2 forecasts will likely be inflated.

**Key Factors for Distinguishing Value vs. Temporal Anomalies**

**1. The Pull-Forward Litmus Test (April Cohort Analysis)**
To determine if revenue was borrowed from the future, isolate the **April 1–15 new booking volume** against the historical baseline.
*   **The Signal:** If April new bookings drop below the seasonal average by an amount roughly equal to the March surplus, the experiment successfully pulled forward demand but created no new value. This confirms a "slump" scenario.
*   **The Action:** Compare the *conversion rate* of April traffic to March traffic. If traffic volume is stable but conversion collapses, the price point has exceeded market willingness-to-pay for *new* logos, confirming the March surge was purely anticipatory.

**2. CAC Efficiency and Payback Period**
A 22% increase in CAC is a critical guardrail failure unless Lifetime Value (LTV) increased proportionally.
*   **The Calculation:** Recalculate the payback period using the new $18 ARPU (Average Revenue Per User) and the new CAC.
*   **The Verdict:** If the payback period has extended (e.g., from 6 months to 9 months) due to the CAC spike outpacing the price hike, the "value signal" hypothesis is financially false. You are buying revenue at a worse margin, not attracting higher-quality leads organically.

**3. Cohort Retention vs. One-Time Spike**
Value perception is proven by retention, not initial conversion.
*   **The Metric:** Track the **Day-30 and Day-60 retention** of the March "upgraded before hike" cohort versus a control group (if available) or historical cohorts at the $15 price point.
*   **The Risk:** If these users churn at a higher rate in Q2, the price increase acted as a barrier to long-term fit, attracting only those sensitive to deadline pressure rather than product value.

**4. Attribution of the "Quality Signal"**
The assumption that price drove quality perception is a confounder unless isolated.
*   **The Check:** Did marketing spend or messaging change in March? If the CAC rose because ad auctions became more competitive (unrelated to your price), the correlation between price and "quality perception" is spurious.
*   **Requirement:** Segment acquisition sources. If the CAC rise is uniform across all channels, it suggests market-wide pressure. If it is isolated to direct/organic, only then does the price-change hypothesis hold weight.

**Recommended Immediate Steps for Q2 Forecasting**

1.  **Adjust Q2 Top-Line Downward:** Model a "correction factor" for April/May based on the estimated volume of deals pulled into March. Assume a 10–15% dip in new bookings for April as a conservative baseline until actuals stabilize.
2.  **Freeze Broad Acquisition Spend:** Halt scaling of paid channels until the new CAC stabilizes. The current 22% premium erodes the margin gain from the price hike.
3.  **Implement a Guardrail Metric:** Define a "Net New ARR" metric that subtracts estimated pull-forward volume from gross new sales for the next 60 days to prevent false positive reporting.

**Conclusion**
Current data indicates a temporal anomaly (pull-forward) exacerbated by inefficient acquisition spending. Treat the March revenue surplus as non-recurring for Q2 forecasting purposes. Validate the "value signal" hypothesis only if April retention rates exceed historical baselines despite the higher price point.
