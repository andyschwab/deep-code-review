# RemoteSync Async Video Launch: Minimal Instrumentation Plan

To validate demand and identify friction without overburdening your two engineers, implement exactly these four events. This covers the full funnel from intent to success while capturing the specific permission failures you fear.

### Non-Negotiable Events (Implement Before Dec 1)

| Event Name | Trigger Point | Critical Properties | Why It Matters |
| :--- | :--- | :--- | : |
| `video_record_started` | User clicks "Record" button | `browser_type`, `os_version` | Establishes the top-of-funnel baseline. Without this, you cannot calculate conversion rates or drop-off. |
| `video_permission_denied` | Browser blocks mic/camera access | `device_type`, `error_code` | Directly answers your fear about invisible failures. If this spikes, you know to improve your permission UI, not your core product. |
| `video_recording_abandoned` | User stops recording before sending (<5 sec) | `duration_seconds`, `step_failed` | Distinguishes between technical failures and users changing their mind. Helps identify if the recording UI is confusing. |
| `video_sent` | Successful upload and share | `duration_seconds`, `recipient_count` | Your core success metric. Validates demand and measures engagement depth. |

### What to Defer Until Post-Launch

Do not build tracking for these yet; they add complexity without immediate decision-making value:
*   **Playback Analytics:** (`video_viewed`, `watch_time`). Wait until you have senders. Knowing someone watched doesn't help you fix the *sending* flow, which is your current bottleneck.
*   **Feature Discovery:** (`feature_hovered`, `tooltip_clicked`). Use simple heatmaps (e.g., Microsoft Clarity, free tier) instead of custom events to see if users find the button.
*   **Edit Actions:** (`video_trimmed`, `filter_applied`). These are secondary behaviors. First, prove users can send a raw video.

### Manual Data Verification Strategy

With no QA resources, use this "sanity check" protocol before launch:

1.  **The "Five-Click" Test:** Have the founder and both engineers manually trigger each event once in a staging environment. Check your analytics real-time view (GA4 DebugView or equivalent) immediately after each action. If the event doesn't appear within 60 seconds, the implementation is broken.
2.  **The "Zero-Base" Reconciliation:** On launch day, note the exact count of `video_sent` events in your dashboard at 5:00 PM. Manually count the actual videos generated in your database/backend logs for that same day. The numbers must match exactly. If analytics shows 10 sends but your database has 8, your tracking is firing prematurely or duplicating.
3.  **Permission Simulation:** Intentionally deny browser permissions on two different browsers (e.g., Chrome and Safari) during testing. Verify that `video_permission_denied` fires specifically in those instances and *not* when permissions are granted.

By focusing strictly on the "Attempt → Block → Abandon → Success" flow, you gain 90% of the necessary insight with 10% of the engineering effort.

*Note: Ensure your engineers use a consistent naming convention (lowercase, underscores) as listed above to avoid data fragmentation.*
*Do not attempt to track "partial uploads" yet; network interruptions are noise until you have volume.*
