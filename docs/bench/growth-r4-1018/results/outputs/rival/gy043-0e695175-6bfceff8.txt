**Executive Summary: Homepage Layout Update Assessment**

The September 20th layout update has successfully shifted user behavior from breadth (pages-per-session) to depth (time-on-site). While the editorial hypothesis of "deeper engagement" is supported by the 12% increase in time-on-site, the 5% decline in pages-per-session presents an immediate risk to ad revenue models dependent on impression volume. Without long-term retention data, the decision rests on whether the *quality* of the current engagement offsets the *quantity* loss.

**Immediate Diagnostic Priorities**

To determine if this trade-off is acceptable, you must isolate whether the drop in page views is a universal behavior change or concentrated in specific user cohorts. The aggregate 5% decrease likely masks divergent behaviors between new and returning visitors.

1.  **Segment by User Type (New vs. Returning):**
    *   **Hypothesis:** New visitors may be overwhelmed by video-heavy layouts, leading to higher bounce rates or single-page sessions, while returning visitors might be consuming longer video content but navigating less.
    *   **Action:** Compare pages-per-session and time-on-site for these two groups separately. If new users show a >10% drop in pages, the layout may be hindering audience acquisition. If returning users show stable page views but higher time, the layout is successfully deepening loyalty for the core audience.

2.  **Analyze Exit Rates on Video vs. Text Articles:**
    *   Determine if users are leaving the site immediately after watching a video on the homepage or if they are clicking into a video article and staying there (increasing time) but not navigating further.
    *   Check if the "next story" recommendation logic is functioning correctly below video players.

3.  **Revenue Per Session (RPS) Correlation:**
    *   Since ad revenue depends on page views, calculate the net impact: Has the increased time-on-site led to higher video ad completion rates or CPMs that offset the lower page count? If RPS has dropped proportionally to page views, the revenue risk is real. If RPS is stable, the "fewer but deeper" model is financially viable.

**Framing the Narrative for the Editorial Board**

Present the data not as a conflict, but as a strategic pivot with measurable risks and opportunities:

*   **The Opportunity:** The update is achieving its primary goal of deepening engagement. Users are spending more time with content, which typically correlates with higher brand affinity and subscription potential.
*   **The Risk:** The current ad model is volume-dependent. A sustained 5% drop in page views could equal a 5% revenue drop unless video ad monetization is more efficient than display ads.
*   **The Unknown:** We lack data on long-term retention. Does deeper engagement today lead to higher return rates next month? Or does the lack of content variety (fewer headlines) cause fatigue?

**Recommendation for Tomorrow's Meeting**

Propose a **30-day hybrid evaluation period** rather than an immediate revert. Commit to monitoring the "New vs. Returning" segmentation daily. If new user acquisition drops significantly, consider a tailored layout for first-time visitors. If returning user retention improves over the next month, the trade-off is justified. Request immediate access to preliminary video ad completion rates to assess if the revenue per minute engaged has increased enough to counter the page view loss.
