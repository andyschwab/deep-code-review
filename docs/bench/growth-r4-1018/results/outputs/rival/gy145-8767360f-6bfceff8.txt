# Pricing Experiment Evaluation: CloudVault Canada (March 1–30)

## Executive Summary
The March test increasing Enterprise tier pricing from $15 to $18/user/month generated a **5% total revenue lift** but coincided with a **22% rise in Customer Acquisition Cost (CAC)**. While the revenue gain suggests improved value perception among larger organizations, the concurrent CAC spike and potential for demand pull-forward create significant uncertainty for Q2 forecasting. The following framework distinguishes sustainable value gains from temporal anomalies.

## Key Factors for Evaluation

### 1. Cohort Analysis: New vs. Existing Customers
Separate revenue sources to identify the driver of the 5% lift.
*   **Existing Upgrades:** If the majority of revenue came from current customers upgrading *before* the hike to lock in lower rates, this is pure **pull-forward**. This revenue is borrowed from April/May and predicts a Q2 slump.
*   **New Sales:** If new logos signed at the $18 price point with stable conversion rates, this indicates **genuine value perception**.
*   **Action:** Segment March revenue by "Legacy Upgrade" vs. "New Contract @ $18." Only the latter is sustainable.

### 2. Conversion Rate vs. Average Contract Value (ACV)
Analyze the trade-off between price and volume.
*   **The Signal:** Did the close rate for *new* enterprise deals drop significantly? A stable close rate at a higher price confirms the "signaling hypothesis" (higher price = higher perceived quality).
*   **The Risk:** If conversion rates dropped >15% while ACV rose, the net gain may be fragile. The 22% CAC increase suggests sales teams are working harder or paying more for media to find buyers willing to accept the new price.
*   **Action:** Calculate the **Efficiency Ratio**: (Revenue Lift %) / (CAC Increase %). A ratio below 1.0 (0.23 in this case) indicates the growth is currently inefficient and potentially unsustainable without optimization.

### 3. Leading Indicators for April (The "Slump" Test)
Monitor these weekly metrics in April to detect the pull-forward hangover:
*   **Pipeline Velocity:** Are deals taking longer to close now that the urgency to "beat the hike" is gone?
*   **Lead Volume:** Has inbound interest from the target enterprise segment dropped compared to the February baseline?
*   **Churn/Renewal Sentiment:** Are existing customers at renewal expressing shock or resistance to the $18 rate?

### 4. CAC Decomposition
The 22% CAC rise requires immediate diagnosis before scaling:
*   **Media Efficiency:** Did ad costs rise, or did conversion rates on landing pages drop (requiring more spend per lead)?
*   **Sales Cycle:** Did the sales cycle lengthen, increasing the cost of sales per deal?
*   **Hypothesis Check:** If the price increase truly signals quality, CAC should theoretically stabilize or drop as qualification improves. A sustained rise suggests the market is resisting the new price point.

## Recommendation for Q2 Forecasting
Do not extrapolate the March 5% lift linearly into Q2. Instead, model three scenarios:
1.  **Conservative (Pull-Forward Dominant):** Assume March's excess revenue is deducted from April/May. Forecast flat revenue with elevated CAC.
2.  **Base Case (Partial Sustainability):** Assume 50% of the lift is permanent value capture; the rest is temporal.
3.  **Optimistic (Value Signaling Valid):** Assume the lift is fully sustainable, provided CAC efficiency improves by 10% in Q2.

**Immediate Next Step:** Isolate March revenue by customer type (new vs. expansion). If >60% of the lift came from existing customers rushing to upgrade, treat the Q2 forecast as neutral-to-negative until new logo acquisition stabilizes at the $18 price point.

*Note: Ensure attribution windows for CAC match the sales cycle length to avoid misaligning spend and results.*
