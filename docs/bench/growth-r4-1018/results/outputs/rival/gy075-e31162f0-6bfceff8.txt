### Recommendation: Revert to Original Pricing Layout Immediately

Based on the divergent metrics from your April 10–24 test, you should revert to the original pricing page layout before presenting to leadership next Tuesday. While an 8% increase in click-through rate (CTR) to checkout appears positive, the 20% drop in qualified enterprise leads indicates a critical failure in your high-value sales funnel. In enterprise SaaS, the lifetime value (LTV) of a single qualified lead often exceeds the revenue of hundreds of self-serve signups. The data suggests the $499 anchor price successfully nudged lower-intent traffic but simultaneously signaled "too expensive" or "misaligned" to your core enterprise buyers, causing them to disengage before entering the sales process.

The risk of permanent brand damage is real if this perception solidifies. Enterprise buyers operate on long consideration cycles; a 20% drop in just two weeks suggests an immediate rejection of your value proposition at the top of the funnel. Continuing the test to see if the effect is "temporary" risks losing deals that may never return, as prospects likely categorized CloudScale as out-of-budget based on that first impression.

### Key Factors for Your Executive Presentation

When presenting to the VP of Sales and CRO, frame your analysis around these four factors:

1.  **Revenue Quality vs. Quantity:** Highlight that the 8% CTR lift likely consists of lower-quality leads (SMBs or individuals) who will churn quickly or require high support costs, whereas the 20% drop represents high-contract-value opportunities. Calculate the potential revenue loss: if the average enterprise deal is $50k/year, losing 20% of those leads is far more costly than gaining extra $299/month self-serve accounts.
2.  **Funnel Disconnection:** Explain that the pricing page is serving two distinct audiences with conflicting needs. The anchor price optimized for the self-serve path but created friction for the high-touch path. The metric divergence proves that what works for self-serve conversion does not work for enterprise lead generation.
3.  **Brand Positioning Signal:** Acknowledge your hypothesis that the anchor price shifted perception. In enterprise sales, trust and fit are paramount. A price anchor that feels arbitrary or inflated can erode trust before a conversation even starts. Reverting signals that you listen to market feedback and prioritize enterprise partnerships.
4.  **Next Steps for Experimentation:** Propose a segmented approach rather than a one-size-fits-all page. Suggest future tests that dynamically show different pricing structures based on traffic source (e.g., organic search vs. targeted account-based marketing links) or company size data, ensuring enterprise buyers never see an alienating anchor price.

**Action Plan:** Revert the change today to stop the bleed on enterprise leads. Use the next two weeks to gather qualitative data from sales reps on specific prospect objections regarding price perception to inform a more nuanced, segmented testing strategy for Q3.
