**Recommendation: Feature Net Revenue Retention (NRR)**

For your Q3 board deck, you should feature **Net Revenue Retention (NRR)** as your primary health metric. In the logistics sector, where investors are wary of churn, NRR demonstrates that your expansion revenue outweighs your losses, proving the underlying value of your platform despite client departures.

Based on the figures provided:
*   **Starting MRR Context:** While the exact starting MRR is not explicitly stated, we know you lost €45k and gained €68k in expansion.
*   **The Narrative:** Your expansion (€68k) exceeds your churn (€45k) by **€23k**. This indicates that even if you acquired zero new logos in this period, your revenue base from existing customers grew.
*   **Investor Impact:** Presenting Gross Retention alone highlights the loss of 12 clients, which may trigger alarm regarding market fit or service reliability. Presenting NRR shifts the focus to the **net positive momentum** driven by your top accounts, validating your "land and expand" strategy.

**Critical Data Gaps to Resolve Before Finalizing**

To ensure the NRR calculation is defensible under board scrutiny, you must resolve these three specific data gaps regarding the lost clients:

1.  **Timing of Churn vs. Expansion:** You must confirm whether the €45k loss and €68k expansion occurred in the same observation window. If the €45k churn happened in June but the €68k expansion is recognized in August, combining them in a single six-month metric may artificially inflate current health. Ensure both figures represent closed, realized revenue within the exact same six-month period.
2.  **Reason for Churn (Concentration Risk):** You noted losing 12 clients. The board will immediately ask if these were small accounts or if any were mid-tier. If the 12 lost clients were all small firms, your Gross Revenue Retention is likely very high, which strengthens the NRR story. If one or two were significant accounts, you need a qualitative explanation prepared (e.g., "client went bankrupt" vs. "switched to competitor") to prove the churn is not systemic.
3.  **Validity of the "Stable Expansion" Assumption:** You are assuming top-five account expansions remain stable despite the seasonal Baltic dip. This is a forecast, not a realized fact. For a board deck reporting on the *last* six months, only include realized expansion. If you include projected Q3 expansion in this historical metric, you risk missing your guidance if the seasonal dip hits harder than expected. Separate historical NRR (past 6 months) from forward-looking guidance (Q3 projections).
