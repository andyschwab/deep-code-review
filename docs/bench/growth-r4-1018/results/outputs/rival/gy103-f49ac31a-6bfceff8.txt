# WriteWise Adoption & Habit Formation Measurement Plan

## 1. Defining "Value Realized" for the Philippine Market
For WriteWise, a login is not value. Given the hypothesis that users fail to see immediate worth in premium grammar suggestions, "Value Realized" must be defined by **output creation** and **feature reliance**, not just access.

**Primary Habit Metric: The "Polished Output" Event**
Count a user as having realized value only when they:
1.  Input text >50 words (indicating genuine writing intent).
2.  Accept at least one AI suggestion (grammar, tone, or translation).
3.  Export or copy the final text (signaling the task is complete).

**Secondary Habit Metric: Local Language Reliance**
To isolate the impact of local features, track the **"Tagalog-English Switch"**:
*   Trigger: User toggles between English and Tagalog/Cebuano modes or accepts a suggestion specific to code-switching (common in PH professional/student contexts).
*   Why: If retention correlates higher with this event than with standard grammar fixes, the local language feature is the differentiator, not the core editing tool.

## 2. Core Adoption Metrics (Beyond Logins)
Replace "Daily Active Users" with these leading indicators of habit formation:

| Metric | Definition | Target Signal |
| :--- | :--- | :--- |
| **Activation Rate** | % of downloads completing a "Polished Output" within 24 hours of install. | <20% indicates onboarding failure; >40% suggests strong initial fit. |
| **Time-to-Value (TTV)** | Average time from install to first "Polished Output." | If TTV > 3 days, the 7-day trial is too short to demonstrate worth. |
| **Suggestion Acceptance Rate** | % of offered suggestions accepted by the user. | Low rates suggest suggestions are irrelevant or too aggressive. |
| **Retention Cohort Stickiness** | DAU/MAU ratio specifically for users who hit "Value Realized" in Week 1 vs. those who didn't. | Isolates whether the *aha moment* drives retention. |

## 3. Measurement Plan: Isolating Local vs. Core Features
To determine if the pivot should be pricing or product focus, implement the following tracking events immediately:

**Event Schema:**
*   `suggestion_viewed`: Properties `{type: "grammar" | "local_translation" | "tone", language_context: "en" | "tl" | "ceb"}`
*   `suggestion_accepted`: Properties `{type, language_context, time_to_accept}`
*   `export_completed`: Properties `{word_count, suggestions_accepted_count}`

**Analysis Logic:**
1.  **Segment A (Core Users):** Users who only accept standard grammar/tone suggestions.
2.  **Segment B (Local Users):** Users who accept at least one local language/translation suggestion.
3.  **Comparison:** Compare Day-7 retention and Trial-to-Paid conversion between Segment A and Segment B.

**Hypothesis Validation:**
*   If Segment B has 2x higher retention but low conversion: The *product* works, but the *pricing* blocks them (Pivot Pricing).
*   If Segment A has higher retention than B: The local features are distracting; double down on core grammar (Pivot Product Focus).
*   If both segments have low retention and low "Polished Output" rates: The onboarding flow fails to guide users to the *first* suggestion acceptance (Fix Onboarding).

## 4. Immediate Next Steps for Founding Team
1.  **Instrument Tracking:** Deploy events for `suggestion_accepted` and `export_completed` with language properties within 48 hours.
2.  **Cohort Analysis:** Run a retroactive analysis on the 15,000 downloads using existing logs to estimate current "Polished Output" rates.
3.  **Onboarding Tweak:** If TTV is high, introduce a forced interactive tutorial that guarantees one successful suggestion acceptance within 2 minutes of opening the app.

This plan shifts the focus from vanity metrics (downloads) to behavioral economics (habit loops), providing the data needed to decide between a monetization pivot or a product refinement before the funding round.

*Note: Ensure all data collection complies with the Philippines' Data Privacy Act of 2012, specifically regarding user content processing.*
*Recommendation: Prioritize fixing the "Time-to-Value" before altering pricing models; users rarely pay for tools they haven't successfully used once.*
