# BuzzHub Infinite Scroll: Engagement vs. Infrastructure Cost Analysis Framework

## Executive Summary
The infinite scroll feature has successfully driven engagement (+50% DAU, +50% time spent) but at a disproportionate infrastructure cost (+200%). This framework evaluates whether the unit economics of this engagement are sustainable or if the feature threatens overall profitability.

## 1. Unit Economics Analysis

**Calculate Cost Per Active User (CPU) Before and After:**
- **Pre-launch CPU:** Total Infrastructure Cost ÷ Daily Active Users
- **Post-launch CPU:** New Total Infrastructure Cost ÷ New Daily Active Users
- **Threshold Check:** If Post-launch CPU > (Ad Revenue Per User × Margin Target), the feature is currently unprofitable at scale.

**Calculate Marginal Cost of Engagement:**
- Determine the cost of the *additional* 50% engagement: (New Cost - Old Cost) ÷ (New DAU - Old DAU).
- Compare this marginal cost directly against the marginal revenue generated by those specific additional users and session time.

## 2. Revenue Correlation Audit

Engagement does not automatically equal revenue. Verify the correlation:
- **Ad Impression Lift:** Did ad impressions increase by 50% (matching time spent), or less due to viewability issues/fill rates?
- **CPM Impact:** Did the quality of inventory change? (e.g., infinite scroll sometimes lowers CPMs if ad density dilutes value).
- **Revenue Per Session:** Calculate (Total Ad Revenue ÷ Total Sessions) pre- and post-launch. If this metric dropped, the feature is cannibalizing value.

## 3. Infrastructure Efficiency Diagnostics (Engineering Focus)

Isolate the 200% cost driver to determine if it is a fixable inefficiency or a fundamental flaw:
- **Data Fetching:** Is the app over-fetching data for content users never scroll to?
- **Caching Strategy:** Are repeated views of the same content hitting the database instead of a cache?
- **Media Loading:** Are high-resolution images/videos loading instantly for content far down the scroll queue?
- **Idle Connections:** Are user sessions keeping expensive connections open longer than necessary?

## 4. Sustainability Modeling

Project the trajectory under three scenarios:
- **Status Quo:** If DAU grows 5% monthly with current CPU, when does infrastructure cost exceed total gross margin?
- **Optimized:** If engineering reduces the cost spike from 200% to 75% (via caching/query optimization), does the model become profitable?
- **Capped:** If scroll depth is limited (e.g., max 50 items per session), what is the projected drop in engagement vs. the savings in compute?

## 5. Decision Matrix

| Scenario | Condition | Recommendation |
| :--- | :--- | :--- |
| **Profitable Growth** | Marginal Revenue > Marginal Cost | **Scale:** Invest in capacity; optimize margins later. |
| **Fixable Inefficiency** | Marginal Cost > Revenue, but diagnostics show waste | **Optimize:** Engineering sprint to fix caching/fetching; re-evaluate in 2 weeks. |
| **Fundamental Flaw** | Marginal Cost > Revenue, even with optimizations | **Limit/Revert:** Cap scroll depth immediately or revert to protect cash flow. |

## Immediate Next Steps
1.  **Finance:** Calculate current Marginal Revenue Per User (MRPU) vs. Marginal Infrastructure Cost Per User.
2.  **Engineering:** Profile the top 3 cost drivers of the infinite scroll endpoint within 48 hours.
3.  **Product:** Implement a temporary cap on scroll depth if the marginal cost exceeds MRPU by >20%.

*Note: This analysis relies on current week-one data; a 30-day cohort analysis is required to confirm if this engagement spike retains or churns.*
*Do not revert the feature entirely until the "Fixable Inefficiency" check is complete, as the engagement lift is significant.*
