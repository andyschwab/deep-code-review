**Recommended Key Performance Indicators for AlpinePulse Clinical Advisory Board**

To satisfy investor demands for outcome-based proof and address clinical skepticism, AlpinePulse must shift from measuring "app usage" to measuring "physiological response" and "clinical integration." The following three KPIs bridge raw sensor data to actionable health insights:

**1. Clinically Significant Resting Heart Rate (RHR) Reduction Rate**
*Definition:* The percentage of active users (wearing the device ≥18 hours/day for 20+ days) who demonstrate a statistically significant decrease (≥5 bpm) in their 7-day rolling average resting heart rate between Day 1 and Day 30.
*Rationale:* While your assumption that lower RHR signals better health is generally correct for cardiovascular fitness, a raw average is insufficient for a clinical board. This metric isolates the signal from noise by enforcing adherence thresholds and looking for a specific magnitude of change (5 bpm) rather than any fluctuation. It directly answers the investor question: "Is the device associated with measurable physiological improvement?" If the 65% daily open rate does not correlate with this metric, engagement is merely vanity.

**2. Clinical Report Sharing Frequency (CRSF)**
*Definition:* The percentage of users who export or share a generated health summary with a healthcare provider within the first 45 days of device activation.
*Rationale:* This validates your core hypothesis regarding doctor-patient integration. Without this data point, the device remains a consumer gadget rather than a health tool. Tracking this specifically measures the "actionable" nature of your insights. If users open the app daily but never share data, the loop between monitoring and medical intervention is broken. This metric serves as a proxy for clinical utility and trust in the device's data accuracy.

**3. HRV Trend Stability Score**
*Definition:* The ratio of users showing improved Heart Rate Variability (HRV) coherence (reduced variance in weekly averages) over a 30-day period, specifically segmented by those who report following app-guided breathing or relaxation exercises.
*Rationale:* For an elderly demographic, HRV is often a more sensitive marker of autonomic nervous system health and stress resilience than RHR alone. RHR can be heavily influenced by medication (common in this demographic), potentially masking the device's impact. HRV stability offers a more nuanced view of "health outcomes" related to stress management and recovery. Correlating this with specific in-app actions proves that the *insights* drive the *outcome*, satisfying the requirement for a path from data to action.

**Implementation Note:** Ensure your analysis excludes users with <80% wear time compliance to prevent skewed clinical data. Present these metrics to the board with confidence intervals to acknowledge statistical rigor.
