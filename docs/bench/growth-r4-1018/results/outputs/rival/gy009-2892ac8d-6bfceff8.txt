To confirm the severity of the UI issue for power users and guide the design lead's decision, analyze these specific behavioral metrics immediately:

**1. Task Completion Time & Click Depth (Primary Indicators)**
Compare the average time and number of clicks required for power users to complete their top 5 most frequent workflows (e.g., "create complex report," "assign dependencies") in the new UI versus the 30 days prior to launch.
*   **What to look for:** A statistically significant increase in either metric confirms your hypothesis that the layout hides efficiency. If click depth has increased by >20% for core tasks, the "adaptation over time" theory is unlikely to hold; this is a structural friction point that will drive churn.

**2. Feature Discovery & Utilization Rate**
Track the percentage of power users who access advanced features (custom fields, automation rules, Gantt charts) at least once per session.
*   **What to look for:** A sharp drop in utilization rates for specific high-value features indicates they are effectively "hidden" or too difficult to locate. If usage of revenue-generating features drops below 80% of pre-launch levels, the risk to the 40% revenue segment is critical.

**3. Error Rate & "Rage Click" Frequency**
Measure the rate of failed actions (e.g., clicking a button that doesn't respond, form validation errors due to UI confusion) and rapid, repeated clicks on the same element within a short timeframe.
*   **What to look for:** Spikes in these metrics pinpoint exactly which interface elements are causing frustration. High error rates on specific screens provide the design lead with immediate targets for hotfixes rather than a full revert.

**4. Support Ticket Correlation**
Quantify the volume of support tickets tagged as "UI confusion" or "can't find feature" originating from accounts identified as power users.
*   **What to look for:** A correlation coefficient >0.7 between the launch date and ticket volume from this segment validates that anecdotal complaints are systemic.

**Recommendation for the Design Lead:**
Do not wait for end-of-month churn data. If Task Completion Time and Feature Utilization show >15% degradation, the data supports an immediate intervention. The choice isn't binary (revert all vs. do nothing); the data will likely support a targeted "Classic Mode" toggle for specific high-friction workflows while retaining the new UI for general navigation. This preserves the redesign investment for new users while protecting the revenue base.

*Note: Ensure your analysis window excludes the first 48 hours post-launch to filter out "novelty exploration" noise. Segment strictly by the defined 5% revenue-generating cohort to avoid diluting the signal with general user adaptation data.*
