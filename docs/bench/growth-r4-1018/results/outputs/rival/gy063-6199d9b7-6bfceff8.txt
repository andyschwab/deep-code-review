### Recommended Metrics Framework for PeoplePulse

To present a stable growth narrative that accounts for seasonality while reflecting true customer value, I recommend shifting from raw volume metrics to **intensity-based** and **cohort-relative** measurements. This approach decouples "health" from "calendar month."

#### 1. Primary North Star Metric
**Metric:** **Weighted Engagement Intensity (WEI)**

**Definition:** Instead of counting *how many* users logged in (MAU), this metric measures the *density* of value-driving actions relative to the customer's baseline, heavily weighted toward your hypothesized value driver: feedback depth.

**Formula:**
$$ \text{WEI} = \sum (\text{Feedback Events}_i \times \text{Depth Score}_i) \div \text{Active Contracts} $$

*   **Depth Score:** A weighted value assigned to feedback actions (e.g., 1 point for a rating, 3 points for a text comment, 5 points for a multi-question review completion).
*   **Normalization:** By dividing by *Active Contracts* rather than *Total Seats* or looking at raw totals, you measure the average value extraction per paying entity, regardless of how many individual employees log in during off-peak months.

**Why this works for the CFO:**
*   **Seasonality Proof:** If usage drops 60% in December but the *depth* of the remaining interactions stays high (or the average per contract remains stable), the metric remains flat rather than crashing. It proves customers are still deriving core value even with lower frequency.
*   **Investor Narrative:** It shifts the story from "volatile user count" to "consistent value density." It demonstrates that even in quiet months, customers are engaging deeply enough to justify renewal.
*   **Validation Path:** While you lack historical correlation data, you can immediately begin tracking WEI alongside renewals. Given your hypothesis, high WEI scores in Q4 should strongly predict Q1 renewals, providing the missing correlation within one cycle.

#### 2. Supporting Guardrail Metrics

**Guardrail A: Cohort-Relative Activity Index**
*   **Definition:** Current month's activity expressed as a percentage of that specific cohort's own peak month (typically September).
*   **Formula:** $(\text{Current Month WEI} \div \text{Cohort's Peak Month WEI}) \times 100$
*   **Purpose:** This prevents penalizing the product team for natural cycles. If the index stays above a certain threshold (e.g., 40%) in December, it is considered "healthy," whereas a raw drop would look like failure. It sets realistic, dynamic expectations based on each customer's historical rhythm rather than an arbitrary linear growth target.

**Guardrail B: Critical Feature Adoption Rate (CFAR)**
*   **Definition:** The percentage of active contracts that utilize at least one "high-depth" feature (e.g., submitting a full review cycle) within a rolling 90-day window.
*   **Purpose:** This acts as a churn predictor. Even if daily logins vanish in December, as long as the contract completed a critical depth event in the last quarter, they are healthy. This smooths out monthly volatility by using a quarterly lookback, ensuring that a quiet month doesn't trigger false alarms about customer health.

### Implementation Note for the Board Presentation
When presenting to the board, frame the transition as moving from "Vanity Volume" (MAU) to "Value Density" (WEI). Explicitly state that raw MAU is a lagging indicator of seasonality, whereas WEI is a leading indicator of renewal intent. This allows the CFO to show stable or growing lines on the graph even during the December dip, provided the *depth* of engagement per contract holds steady.

*Note: Begin correlating WEI scores with renewal outcomes immediately; within 6–9 months, you will have the statistical proof to cement this as your official reporting standard.*
