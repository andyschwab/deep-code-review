### Daily Network Effect Validation Dashboard for Mtaani Credit

To distinguish between simple word-of-mouth and true compounding network effects before your May 30 board meeting, track these four specific metric clusters daily. These move beyond vanity counts to measure whether value actually increases as density grows in specific *chamas* or geographic clusters.

#### 1. The Viral Coefficient by Cluster (K-Factor)
Do not track a global referral rate. Calculate the K-factor separately for each geographic cluster or *chama*.
*   **Formula:** (Average invites sent per user in Cluster X) × (Conversion rate of those invites within Cluster X).
*   **The Signal:** If K > 1.0 in specific clusters, you have organic viral growth in those pockets. If K < 1.0 globally but > 1.0 in specific *chamas*, your product works virally only in high-trust environments, validating your core assumption.
*   **Daily Action:** Track K-factor trends per cluster. A rising K-factor in a cluster as its user count grows proves compounding value.

#### 2. Density-Adjusted Conversion Rate
This tests your hypothesis that "trust spreads faster within local chamas."
*   **Formula:** Conversion rate of referral invites sent into a cluster vs. the current number of existing borrowers in that specific cluster.
*   **The Signal:** Plot conversion rate (Y-axis) against existing user density (X-axis) for each cluster.
    *   **Flat/Declining Line:** No network effect; referrals are just individual word-of-mouth.
    *   **Upward Slope:** True network effect. As more people join a *chama*, the likelihood of new members joining increases because the platform becomes more valuable/trusted within that group.
*   **Daily Action:** Update this scatter plot daily. Look for the "tipping point" density where conversion rates spike.

#### 3. Cohort-Based Retention Differential
Network effects must improve retention, not just acquisition.
*   **Formula:** Compare Day-30 retention of borrowers who joined via referral in high-density clusters (>50 users) vs. low-density clusters (<10 users) vs. paid ads.
*   **The Signal:** If referred users in high-density clusters retain significantly better (e.g., 20% higher) than referred users in sparse clusters, the *network* is driving value, not just the incentive. If all referred users retain equally regardless of cluster size, you have successful marketing, not network effects.
*   **Daily Action:** Track rolling 7-day retention deltas between these cohorts.

#### 4. Organic-to-Paid Ratio by Geography
Investors need to see capital efficiency improving as the network grows.
*   **Formula:** (New borrowers from referrals in Cluster X) / (New borrowers from paid ads in Cluster X).
*   **The Signal:** In a true network effect model, this ratio should increase over time in mature clusters without increased ad spend. If the ratio stays flat despite growing user counts, the "viral loop" is broken, and you are relying on paid acquisition to seed every new user.
*   **Daily Action:** Monitor this ratio weekly per cluster. A rising trend indicates decreasing CAC and sustainable organic growth.

**Implementation Note:** Ensure your tracking attributes every new user to a specific *chama* or geographic cluster ID at signup; without this segmentation, global averages will hide local viral successes.

**Board Narrative:** Present these metrics to show *where* network effects exist (validating the *chama* hypothesis) rather than arguing they exist globally. If K > 1 in specific clusters, your strategy is to double down on seeding those high-trust networks rather than broad paid ads.
