**Risk Assessment: MediConnect Triage Module Rollout**

**Executive Summary**
Extrapolating the Ohio pilot results to the full 40-hospital network carries significant risk due to critical sample size limitations and demographic misalignment. The observed trade-off (20% speed gain vs. 8-point satisfaction drop) may worsen in rural settings where patient-provider relationships often drive satisfaction metrics more heavily than efficiency.

**Key Risks in Extrapolation**

1.  **Demographic Mismatch (Urban vs. Rural)**
    The pilot skewed urban, while the target network is predominantly rural. Rural patients often prioritize thoroughness and personal interaction over speed. The hypothesis that doctors are "skipping non-essential questions" may be perceived differently in rural contexts, potentially viewed as dismissive rather than efficient, exacerbating the satisfaction decline beyond the observed 8 points.

2.  **Sample Size & Statistical Power**
    With only 150 doctors across three hospitals, the data lacks the power to detect variance in sub-groups. We cannot determine if the satisfaction drop is consistent across all specialties or driven by a specific subset (e.g., emergency vs. primary care). A sample this small cannot reliably predict behavior across 40 diverse facilities.

3.  **The "Learning Curve" vs. "Fundamental Flaw" Ambiguity**
    The pilot duration (starting August 1st) may be insufficient to distinguish between temporary friction (doctors learning the tool) and a permanent workflow defect. If the satisfaction dip is due to doctors awkwardly navigating the new interface, it may resolve. If it is due to the tool structurally preventing relationship-building conversation, it will persist or worsen at scale.

4.  **Behavioral Scaling Risk**
    The assumption that speed gains come from skipping questions is unverified. If the tool *encourages* skipping via UI design, this behavior will scale linearly. If doctors are manually bypassing the tool to save time, the rollout to less tech-savvy rural staff might result in *lower* adoption and *no* speed gain, retaining the status quo without the efficiency benefit.

**Critical Questions to Answer Before Rollout**

To satisfy the CMO and Risk Compliance, the following must be resolved:

*   **Demographic Validity:** Does the correlation between "triage speed" and "patient satisfaction" hold in rural pilot sites? We need a controlled test in at least one rural hospital to validate the urban findings.
*   **Root Cause of Satisfaction Drop:** Is the 8-point drop driven by specific question categories being skipped? We need session-level data mapping exactly which fields are omitted during "fast" triages.
*   **Temporal Analysis:** Is the satisfaction score trending upward as doctors gain proficiency? We need a week-over-week breakdown of satisfaction scores since August 1st to identify a learning curve trajectory.
*   **Patient Segment Impact:** Did the satisfaction drop occur evenly across all patient demographics, or was it concentrated among elderly patients or those with complex conditions who require more dialogue?
*   **Workflow Integrity:** Are doctors skipping questions because the module allows it, or because the module is too slow? We need to distinguish between tool-enabled efficiency and workflow workarounds.

**Recommendation**
Do not approve the full rollout until a rural pilot is conducted and the root cause of the satisfaction dip is quantified. The current data suggests a high probability of reputational risk in the core rural market.

*Note: This assessment relies solely on the provided pilot metrics; no external benchmarking was performed.*
