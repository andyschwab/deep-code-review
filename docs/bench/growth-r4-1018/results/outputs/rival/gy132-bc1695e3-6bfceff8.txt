### Proposed Retention Definitions for StreamFiesta

Given your Friday release cadence and the distinct "Monday Sign-up" vs. "Binge-then-Wait" behaviors, standard DAU/WAU will indeed obscure true engagement. Below are three tailored definitions designed to satisfy regional directors' need for clear MoM growth while respecting your content rhythm.

#### Option 1: The "Episode Cycle" Retention (Recommended)
**Definition:** A user is "Retained" in a given week if they stream **any** content within a 10-day window starting from the Friday release date (Friday through the following Sunday).
*   **Time Window:** Rolling 10-day cycles anchored to release day.
*   **Action:** Any play >30 seconds.
*   **Pros:** Directly aligns with your product value proposition (new episodes). It captures the "Weekend Warrior" who waits for Friday without penalizing them for Monday/Tuesday inactivity. It smooths out the "binge then disappear" group by counting them as retained in the week they binged, and challenging them to return for the *next* cycle.
*   **Cons:** Slightly non-standard compared to generic SaaS metrics; requires custom SQL logic rather than out-of-the-box dashboarding.

#### Option 2: The "Monthly Content Consumer" (Executive Reporting)
**Definition:** A user is "Retained" in a calendar month if they stream content in **at least two distinct weekly release cycles** within that month.
*   **Time Window:** Calendar Month (for MoM reporting).
*   **Action:** Streaming in ≥2 separate Friday-to-Sunday windows.
*   **Pros:** Perfectly tailored for your Q3 goal of showing "Month-over-Month Growth." It filters out one-off binge users who never return, proving true habit formation. It inherently accounts for the sporadic nature of your users by demanding recurrence across your specific drop dates.
*   **Cons:** Lags in real-time visibility (you can't calculate this until the month ends); may show lower absolute percentages than weekly metrics, requiring education for stakeholders.

#### Option 3: The "Anchor Day" Cohort Retention
**Definition:** Measure retention based on the user's specific "First Stream Day" relative to the release cycle, comparing like-to-like behavior.
*   **Time Window:** 4-week rolling cohorts based on signup day (e.g., "Monday Sign-ups" vs. "Friday Sign-ups").
*   **Action:** Return visit within the *same* relative window of the following release cycle (e.g., Did the Monday signer return the following Monday-Sunday?).
*   **Pros:** Isolates the "Monday Sign-up" drop-off issue specifically, allowing you to prove to directors that the 40% drop-off is a timing mismatch, not churn. It prevents the "noisy" daily data from contaminating the weekly trend.
*   **Cons:** Complex to present; creates multiple retention curves rather than a single "North Star" number, which might confuse directors looking for a simple growth chart.

### Recommendation for Q3 Reporting
Present **Option 2 (Monthly Content Consumer)** as your primary KPI to the regional directors. It directly answers the pressure for MoM growth and proves users are building a habit around your episodic drops. Use **Option 1** internally for product teams to optimize weekly engagement tactics.

*Note: Ensure your analytics tool tracks "release cycle ID" as a property on every stream event to facilitate these custom windows.*
*Avoid using "Last 30 Days" active users, as this will double-count binge sessions and dilute the impact of your Friday releases.*
