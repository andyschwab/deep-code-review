# Comparative Analysis: Recommendation Engine North Star Metric for Sonido

## Executive Summary
For a podcast discovery app entering the Latin American market, **Total Duration of Listening Time** is the superior north star metric over Frequency of Episodes Started. While start frequency signals successful discovery, listening duration is a stronger proxy for content resonance and long-term retention, which are critical for subscription viability in price-sensitive markets like Argentina.

## Option A: Optimizing for Episode Start Frequency

**Pros:**
*   **Validates Discovery Efficiency:** A 40% lift in starts confirms the engine is effective at surfacing relevant options and reducing search friction.
*   **Encourages Exploration:** Incentivizes users to sample diverse content, preventing "filter bubbles" where users only hear familiar voices.
*   **Immediate Gratification:** High start rates create a perception of abundance and activity, potentially boosting early-day engagement metrics.

**Cons:**
*   **Vulnerability to Clickbait:** Optimizing for starts rewards sensational titles and short-form clips that users abandon quickly, degrading content quality over time.
*   **Misaligned with Value:** In podcasting, value is delivered *during* consumption, not at the click. A start without listen time generates no ad revenue or subscriber loyalty.
*   **The "Churn" Risk:** As seen in your beta, a 10-minute drop in session duration suggests users are sampling but not bonding. In subscription models, "sampling" without "sticking" leads to high churn after the trial period.

## Option B: Optimizing for Total Listening Duration

**Pros:**
*   **Stronger Retention Predictor:** Time spent is the most reliable correlate for subscription renewal. Users who listen longer are forming habits and emotional connections with hosts.
*   **Quality Alignment:** Forces the algorithm to prioritize content that actually holds attention, naturally filtering out low-quality or misleading recommendations.
*   **Monetization Readiness:** For future ad-insertion or premium tiers, inventory is defined by listening time, not start counts.

**Cons:**
*   **Discovery Friction:** May cause the engine to over-recommend long, safe content (e.g., 2-hour interviews) while suppressing niche or new creators that require a "warm-up" period.
*   **Barrier to Entry:** New users might feel overwhelmed if the app pushes long-form content before establishing trust, potentially lowering initial activation rates.

## Strategic Recommendation for the Latin American Market

**Choose Total Listening Duration as the North Star, with Start Frequency as a Guardrail.**

In the Latin American market, where data costs can be a concern and competition for attention is fierce, users are unlikely to pay for a service that wastes their time with content they do not finish. Your beta data provides a clear warning: the 40% increase in starts came at the cost of 10 minutes of session depth. This indicates that while users are *curious*, they are not *satisfied*.

Optimizing for starts alone will build a "clicky" user base that churns rapidly once the novelty of discovery fades. Optimizing for duration builds a "loyal" user base that finds value deep in the catalog.

**Implementation Strategy:**
1.  **Primary Goal:** Maximize Total Listening Duration per user per week.
2.  **Guardrail Metric:** Maintain Episode Start Frequency within 15% of the current baseline. This ensures the engine does not become too conservative and stops introducing new content.
3.  **Segmentation:** Analyze duration by content type. If short-form clips drive starts but long-form drives retention, the engine should use short clips as "trailers" to funnel users into longer episodes, rather than optimizing for the clip completion itself.

By prioritizing duration, Sonido aligns engineering efforts with the ultimate business goal: sustainable subscription retention.
