# GreenRoute Impact Measurement Framework

To satisfy the CSO's requirement for concrete numbers proving behavioral influence, you must move beyond binary "viewed/didn't view" metrics. You need a dual-layer framework measuring **Awareness (Exposure)** and **Impact (Delta)**.

### 1. Core Metric: The "Green Delta" (Impact)
This quantifies the actual emission reduction attributable to user decisions, addressing your concern about users viewing data but choosing dirty routes.

*   **Definition:** The difference between the emissions of the *selected* route and the *greenest available* route at the moment of decision.
*   **Formula:** `Selected Route CO2e - Greenest Option CO2e = Green Delta`
*   **Interpretation:**
    *   **Delta = 0:** User chose the optimal green route (Maximum Impact).
    *   **Delta > 0:** User accepted a carbon penalty for speed/cost.
    *   **Trend Analysis:** Track the *average* Green Delta per company over time. A decreasing average Delta indicates behavioral shift, even if users don't always pick the greenest option.
*   **Reporting Stat:** "Average Carbon Penalty Accepted per Shipment." If this drops from 15% to 8% over Q4, you have proof of behavioral change.

### 2. Secondary Metric: "Green Intent Rate" (Awareness)
This measures how often the feature interrupts the default "fastest route" workflow.

*   **Definition:** The percentage of planning sessions where the user views the sustainability tab or sorts by "lowest emissions" before confirming a route.
*   **Formula:** `(Sessions with Green Tab View / Total Planning Sessions) * 100`
*   **Segmentation:** Crucial for your friction hypothesis. Segment this by:
    *   **High-Friction Users:** High view rate + High Green Delta (They look, care, but can't commit due to operational constraints).
    *   **Converted Users:** High view rate + Low Green Delta (They look and act).
    *   **Ignorant Users:** Low view rate + High Green Delta (Feature adoption failure).

### 3. The "Friction vs. Value" Ratio
To address your 10% increase in planning time, correlate time spent with the Green Delta.

*   **Metric:** `Seconds Spent in Planning / Green Delta Achieved`
*   **Goal:** Prove that the extra 10% time yields a disproportionate reduction in emissions. If users spend 30 extra seconds to save 5kg of CO2, that is an efficiency win. If they spend 30 seconds and save 0kg (Delta remains high), the feature is pure friction.

### Implementation Plan for Priya's Report

**A. Baseline Establishment (Oct 1–31 Data)**
Calculate the counterfactual: "If every user had chosen the greenest route shown, what would total emissions be?" Compare this theoretical minimum to actual emissions to establish the "Maximum Addressable Impact."

**B. Cohort Analysis**
Group your 500 freight companies by adoption behavior:
1.  **Green Champions:** Top 20% lowest average Green Delta.
2.  **Aware but Constrained:** High tab views, high Delta. (Target for product education/customer success).
3.  **Non-Adopters:** Zero tab views.

**C. The Executive Narrative**
For the annual statement, report three specific numbers:
1.  **Total Avoided Emissions:** Sum of (Greenest Option - Selected Route) for all trips where Selected < Greenest is impossible, so rather: Sum of (Baseline Average Route - Selected Route) where Baseline is pre-feature behavior, OR simply report the aggregate `Green Delta` avoided compared to a control group if available. *Correction:* Report **"Total Carbon Saved vs. Pre-Feature Baseline"** or **"Total Carbon Saved vs. Greenest-Option Potential."** The latter shows the gap; the former shows progress.
2.  **Behavioral Shift:** "% of shipments where the Green Delta was <5% (near-optimal choice)."
3.  **Engagement Efficiency:** "Average planning time increase (10%) resulted in a X% reduction in carbon intensity per mile."

**Recommendation:** Do not just measure "Tab Views." A user can view the tab and ignore the data. Measure the **correlation** between viewing the tab and the **magnitude of the Green Delta**. If viewing the tab reduces the Delta by 20% compared to non-viewers, you have causal proof of influence.

*Note: Ensure your data pipeline captures the "Greenest Option" metrics at the exact timestamp of the query, as traffic and weather change optimal routes dynamically.*
*This framework shifts the narrative from "feature usage" to "carbon outcome," directly addressing the CSO's need for environmental impact validation.*
