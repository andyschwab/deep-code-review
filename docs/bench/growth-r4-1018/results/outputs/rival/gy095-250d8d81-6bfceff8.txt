**Recommended Leading Indicator: "Day-2 Collaboration Depth Score"**

To replace your lagging storage volume metric, track the **percentage of new accounts that complete three distinct file-sharing actions within their first 48 hours**.

While your hypothesis suggests "frequency of sharing" is key, raw frequency can be misleading (e.g., one user rapidly sharing 50 files to themselves vs. a user sharing 3 files to three different external partners). The latter signals true product stickiness and integration into the customer's workflow. Therefore, the specific action to isolate is **successful external shares** (files shared with an email domain different from the uploader's).

**Why this predicts 12-month retention:**
1.  **Workflow Integration:** A user sharing files externally has moved beyond "testing" to "operating." They have invited colleagues or clients into the ecosystem, creating switching costs.
2.  **Early Signal:** This behavior typically happens before the 50GB threshold is reached. If a customer hasn't shared a file by Day 2, they are likely treating CloudVault as a passive backup drive, which correlates with high churn in month 3–4.
3.  **Actionable Window:** Identifying these users on Day 3 gives your Customer Success team 87 days before the average renewal decision point to intervene, compared to waiting for the 50GB milestone which may occur too late.

**Implementation for Monday Executive Reviews:**
Report this as a weekly cohort metric: *"Of the accounts opened last week, X% achieved 'Collaboration Depth' (3+ external shares) by Day 2."*

*   **Green Zone:** >40% adoption. Revenue health is stable.
*   **Yellow Zone:** 25–40% adoption. Investigate onboarding friction.
*   **Red Zone:** <25% adoption. High risk of churn in Q3/Q4; trigger immediate outreach protocols for non-collaborating accounts.

This metric shifts your focus from "how much space they use" to "how deeply they are embedded," providing the early warning system your leadership team requires.

*Ensure your tracking distinguishes between internal and external shares to avoid inflating the metric with self-sharing behavior.*
