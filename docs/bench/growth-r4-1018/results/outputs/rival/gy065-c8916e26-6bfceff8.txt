### Executive Decision Framework: StreamLine Paywall Experiment

**Recommendation: Revert to Control Immediately.**
Do not roll out the change. The simultaneous drop in paid conversions (-12%) and rise in existing user churn (+8%) indicates immediate, quantifiable revenue destruction that cannot be justified by unproven long-term hypotheses.

#### 1. The Financial Reality (Short-Term Impact)
The data shows a "double penalty" on revenue:
*   **Acquisition Loss:** A 12% drop in new paid subscriptions directly reduces top-line growth.
*   **Retention Crisis:** An 8% churn increase among *existing* loyal users is a critical failure. These users have already validated the product value; frustrating them suggests the new flow creates friction that outweighs the content value, risking brand reputation and lifetime value (LTV).
*   **The "Quality" Fallacy:** While free sign-ups rose 25%, this likely represents "false positives"—users intrigued by the content but unwilling to commit, now captured as low-intent free users. There is no evidence yet that these users convert to paid at a higher rate than the control group.

#### 2. The Analysis Framework for Leadership
To weigh short-term loss against potential long-term gain, use this **Break-Even Quality Model**. Do not make a decision until you can populate these variables:

**A. Calculate the Revenue Hole**
$$ \text{Daily Revenue Loss} = (\text{Lost New Subs} \times \text{ARPU}) + (\text{Churned Existing Subs} \times \text{LTV}_{\text{remaining}}) $$
*This is your known, immediate cost.*

**B. Determine the Required Lift**
To justify the change, the new cohort of free users must generate enough future revenue to cover the Daily Revenue Loss. Calculate the **Required Conversion Rate (RCR)** for the new free users:
$$ \text{RCR} = \frac{\text{Daily Revenue Loss} + \text{Current Free-to-Paid Revenue}}{\text{New Free User Volume} \times \text{ARPU}} $$
*If the historical free-to-paid conversion rate is lower than this RCR, the experiment fails mathematically.*

**C. The Time-Value Discount**
Revenue lost today is worth more than revenue gained in 6 months. Apply a discount rate to future conversions. If the payback period exceeds 90 days, the cash flow impact may be unacceptable for a growth-stage company.

#### 3. Strategic Risks
*   **Alienating the Base:** Existing users churning due to UI/UX friction is a product failure, not a segmentation success. It suggests the new gate interrupts the core value proposition.
*   **Data Latency:** You explicitly stated LTV data is missing. Making a permanent decision without this data is speculation. In the absence of data showing higher quality, you must assume the new users are of equal or lower quality than the control.

#### 4. Action Plan
1.  **Revert Immediately:** Stop the bleeding on existing user churn. Restore the experience for loyal customers.
2.  **Isolate the Variable:** The increase in free sign-ups is a positive signal for *top-of-funnel interest*, but the conversion mechanism is broken. Consider A/B testing the *messaging* of the paywall rather than the *timing*, or test the earlier gate only on **new traffic** (excluding existing users) to prevent alienating the base.
3.  **Run a Long-Term Cohort Study:** Re-launch the experiment to a small, isolated segment of *new* users only. Track them for 60–90 days to measure actual LTV before considering a broader rollout.

**Conclusion:** The current evidence points to a net negative outcome. The risk of compounding churn among loyal users outweighs the unverified potential of higher-quality free users. Revert, learn, and re-test with stricter guardrails.

*Note: Ensure your analytics tracking distinguishes between "churn due to friction" vs. "natural churn" in future tests to isolate the specific cause of the 8% rise.*
