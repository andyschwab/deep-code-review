### Diagnostic Plan: Isolating Algorithm Impact vs. Inventory Constraints

To determine whether the 8% purchase drop stems from Algorithm B's relevance issues or the concurrent inventory shortage, you must segment the data by **product availability** and **user intent**. Do not rely on aggregate totals, as they conflate the two variables. Execute the following four analyses immediately:

**1. The "In-Stock Only" Control Test**
Filter the dataset to include *only* transactions and clicks for SKUs that remained fully in-stock throughout the entire test window for both the control (Algorithm A) and variant (Algorithm B) groups.
*   **If purchases drop in this subset:** The algorithm is the culprit. Users are clicking but failing to convert even when inventory is available, confirming your team's hypothesis about low relevance and user frustration.
*   **If purchases remain stable in this subset:** The algorithm is likely fine; the aggregate drop is almost entirely driven by the inventory shortage affecting out-of-stock items.

**2. Conversion Rate by Inventory Status**
Create a 2x2 matrix comparing Conversion Rate (CVR) across these dimensions:
*   **Rows:** Algorithm A (Control) vs. Algorithm B (Variant)
*   **Columns:** In-Stock Items vs. Out-of-Stock/Low-Stock Items
*   **Analysis:** If Algorithm B's CVR collapses specifically in the "Out-of-Stock" column while holding steady in the "In-Stock" column, the inventory shortage is the primary driver. If CVR drops across both columns for Algorithm B, the recommendation logic is failing to surface buyable intent.

**3. Click-to-Purchase Funnel Depth**
Analyze the "Click-to-Cart" and "Cart-to-Purchase" rates separately.
*   **High Click-to-Cart but Low Cart-to-Purchase:** Suggests users find items interesting (validating the +25% CTR) but abandon at the end due to stockouts (inventory issue).
*   **Low Click-to-Cart:** Suggests the post-click experience is disappointing (e.g., irrelevant details, poor landing pages), indicating an algorithm relevance issue regardless of stock levels.

**4. Category-Level Correlation**
Map the 8% revenue decline against the specific categories experiencing shortages.
*   Calculate the revenue loss attributable *only* to the missing volume in shortage categories using last year's baseline or pre-test trends.
*   If the calculated loss from missing inventory accounts for >90% of the total 8% drop, the algorithm is likely innocent. If the unexplained gap remains significant after accounting for missing stock, the algorithm is suppressing conversion efficiency.

**Recommendation for Stakeholders**
Present the "In-Stock Only" metric as your primary decision gate. If Algorithm B drives equal or higher revenue on available inventory despite the overall drop, proceed with model iteration to refine relevance signals, while treating the supply chain issue as a parallel operational fix. If the algorithm underperforms even on in-stock items, pause the rollout to prevent revenue erosion during the holiday rush, regardless of inventory status.
