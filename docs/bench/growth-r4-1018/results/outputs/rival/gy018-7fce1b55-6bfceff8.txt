To model the long-term profitability of your pricing test, you must construct a cohort-based Lifetime Value (LTV) comparison that explicitly accounts for the "churn shock" observed in month one. Do not extrapolate the 5% revenue lift linearly; the 8% cancellation rate indicates a structural shift in your customer base composition that will compound over time.

**1. Define the Observation Windows and Cohorts**
Separate your data into two distinct cohorts based on the test start date: Control (old price) and Treatment (new price). Your analysis must cover a 12-month horizon. Crucially, distinguish between the "initial churn shock" (Month 1) and the "steady-state churn" (Months 2–12). The 8% churn in the treatment group likely includes price-sensitive users who would have churned eventually but did so immediately due to the hike. You must determine if churn returns to the baseline 2% in Month 2 or remains elevated. If it remains elevated, the 5% revenue gain is likely temporary.

**2. Calculate Adjusted Monthly Recurring Revenue (MRR) per Cohort**
Compute the net MRR for each month $t$ (where $t=1$ to $12$) using the following logic:
*   **Survivors:** Calculate the remaining user count by applying the specific churn rate for that month to the previous month's survivors. Do not use an average churn rate; apply the 8% specifically to Month 1, then apply the observed Month 2+ rate thereafter.
*   **Revenue per User:** Apply the correct price point ($P_{old}$ vs. $P_{new}$) to the surviving users.
*   **Net MRR:** $Survivors_t \times Price$.

**3. Model the 12-Month LTV Delta**
Sum the Net MRR for all 12 months for both cohorts to get the total 12-month value per original signup.
*   **Formula:** $LTV_{12} = \sum_{t=1}^{12} (Survivors_t \times Price_t)$
*   **Comparison:** Subtract $LTV_{12}(Control)$ from $LTV_{12}(Treatment)$.

If the result is negative, the price increase destroys value despite the short-term revenue bump. If positive, the increase is viable only if the steady-state churn (Months 2–12) does not creep up.

**4. Sensitivity Analysis for the CFO**
Since you only have one month of churn data, you must present a scenario matrix rather than a single number. Create three scenarios for Months 2–12 churn in the Treatment group:
*   **Optimistic:** Churn reverts to 2% (the spike was purely elastic demand clearing).
*   **Base Case:** Churn stabilizes at 4% (partial elasticity).
*   **Pessimistic:** Churn remains at 8% (permanent brand/price mismatch).

Present the LTV delta for all three. This highlights the break-even churn rate required to justify the hike.

**5. Recommendation Framework**
Do not recommend a permanent change based solely on the 5% revenue lift. That figure ignores the cost of acquiring replacements for the 6% incremental churn (8% - 2%). If your Customer Acquisition Cost (CAC) is high, replacing these users will erase the revenue gain. Recommend a permanent increase only if the "Base Case" scenario shows a positive LTV delta *and* your sales team confirms they can refill the churned volume without increasing CAC. If the "Base Case" is negative, recommend reverting to the control price and testing a value-add bundle instead of a raw price hike.
