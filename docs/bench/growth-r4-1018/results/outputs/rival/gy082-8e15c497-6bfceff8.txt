# Experiment Analysis: New Dashboard Layout (Oct 5 End Date)

## Executive Summary
The new dashboard layout presents a classic usability trade-off: it successfully activates casual users but actively impedes power users. The data indicates a **segmentation failure**, not a holistic win. Rolling out globally risks churning high-value users, while reverting sacrifices significant activation potential. The recommended path is a **segmented experience** (e.g., "Advanced Mode" toggle or role-based default), though this requires immediate qualitative validation before committing Q4 roadmap resources.

## Divergence Analysis
Your hypothesis is strongly supported by the quantitative split. The 8% aggregate increase in engagement time masks a bifurcated user experience:
*   **Casual Users (+22% feature usage):** The simplified interface likely reduced cognitive load, making core features more discoverable. This confirms the layout solves the "novice paralysis" problem.
*   **Power Users (-15% feature usage):** For daily experts, "simplification" often equates to "increased friction." Hiding advanced tools behind extra clicks or removing dense information density forces inefficient workflows. The drop in usage suggests they cannot find or access their essential tools as quickly as before.

## Revenue Impact Assessment
To decide between global rollout, reversion, or segmentation, we must weigh the value of the bottom 40% against the top 5%.

*   **Risk (Top 5% Power Users):** This segment typically drives disproportionate revenue (often 40-60% of ARR in B2B/SaaS) and provides critical product feedback. A 15% drop in feature usage is a leading indicator of churn. If even 10% of this group churns due to frustration, the revenue loss could exceed the lifetime value of thousands of newly activated casual users. Furthermore, power users often influence team-wide purchasing decisions; their dissatisfaction poses an expansion risk.
*   **Opportunity (Bottom 40% Casual Users):** A 22% increase in feature usage correlates strongly with retention and eventual upsell. Activating this group expands the total addressable market for premium features and reduces early-stage churn. However, the immediate revenue realization from this group is slower compared to the immediate risk of losing established contracts.

**Verdict:** The immediate revenue risk of alienating the top 5% outweighs the long-term gain of activating the bottom 40% *if* forced into a binary choice. A segmented approach captures the upside while mitigating the downside.

## Recommended Next Steps

### 1. Immediate Measurement (Qualitative Gap Fill)
Before committing to a toggle switch, validate *why* power users are disengaging:
*   **Targeted User Interviews:** Recruit 5-7 daily users who showed decreased usage. Conduct screen-share sessions observing their workflow on the new layout. Ask specifically where they feel friction.
*   **Session Replay Analysis:** Use tools like PostHog or FullStory to watch recordings of power user sessions. Look for "rage clicks," rapid back-and-forth navigation, or abandonment of specific advanced tasks.
*   **In-App Micro-Survey:** Trigger a one-question survey for users with >10 logins/week: "What is the one thing missing from your ideal dashboard?"

### 2. Strategic Decision Framework
*   **Do Not Roll Out Globally Yet:** The risk to high-value accounts is too high without mitigation.
*   **Do Not Fully Revert:** You would lose the proven gains for the majority of your user base.
*   **Propose a "Phased Segmentation" for Q4:**
    *   **Short-term:** Implement a user-setting toggle ("Classic View" vs. "New View") allowing power users to self-select out immediately. This stops the bleeding while you build a permanent solution.
    *   **Medium-term:** Develop role-based defaults (auto-detect power users based on login frequency/feature depth) so the interface adapts without manual configuration.

## Conclusion
The data confirms that one size does not fit all. The revenue protection of your top 5% is paramount, but the activation of the bottom 40% is essential for scale. Proceed with a **segmented rollout strategy**. Invest the necessary Q4 resources to build the toggle or role-based logic; the cost of development is lower than the cost of churning your most valuable customers.

*Note: Ensure your analytics tracking distinguishes between "feature hidden" and "feature ignored" to confirm if power users are unable to find tools or choosing not to use them.*
