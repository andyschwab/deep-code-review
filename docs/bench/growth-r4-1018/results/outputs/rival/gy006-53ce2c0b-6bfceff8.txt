**Recommendation: Discard the results entirely and do not roll out the change.**

The dataset is statistically invalid for decision-making. Stopping a test early due to external shocks (server outage) introduces severe selection bias that cannot be corrected post-hoc. The observed 4% uplift is likely an artifact of the outage rather than a true effect of your variant.

**Why the Data Is Unsalvageable:**

1.  **Selection Bias:** You confirmed the outage disproportionately affected mobile users (50% of traffic). If the variant performed differently on mobile vs. desktop (which is highly probable in travel booking), the removal of a non-random segment of mobile users skews the aggregate result. The remaining 70% of traffic is no longer representative of your Australian user base.
2.  **The Peeking Problem:** You stopped the test before reaching the pre-determined sample size required for statistical significance. Analyzing results at an arbitrary stopping point dramatically increases the false positive rate. A 4% lift observed after only 4 days (approx. 8,000 users) when the test was planned for 14 days suggests the result is noise, not signal.
3.  **Confounding Variables:** The server outage itself alters user behavior. Users who managed to book during an outage may be more persistent or technically savvy than the average user, creating a "survivorship bias" that inflates conversion rates independently of your design change.

**Specific Checks to Confirm Bias (for Engineering Validation):**

While the data cannot be used to validate the variant, run these three checks to demonstrate the corruption to your Head of Engineering:

*   **Segment Split Analysis:** Isolate the data by device type (Mobile vs. Desktop). Compare the conversion rates of the variant vs. control *within* each segment. If the mobile segment shows a wildly different lift (or drop) compared to desktop, or if the mobile sample size is critically depleted, the aggregate 4% lift is mathematically meaningless.
*   **Time-Series Interruption Check:** Plot hourly conversion rates for both variants. Look for a sharp divergence coinciding exactly with the start of the server outage. If the gap between variant and control widens only after the outage began, the uplift is caused by the infrastructure failure, not the product change.
*   **Traffic Composition Audit:** Compare the ratio of Mobile/Desktop traffic in the test period against a stable baseline period (e.g., the week prior). If the test period has a significantly lower percentage of mobile traffic than the baseline, your sample is structurally broken.

**Next Steps:**

1.  **Do Not Roll Out:** Rolling out based on this data risks deploying a change that may actually decrease bookings once traffic normalizes.
2.  **Stabilize Infrastructure:** Address the server resource constraints identified by the outage. Running a new test on unstable infrastructure will yield equally useless results.
3.  **Restart the Test:** Re-launch the A/B test once stability is confirmed. Reset the sample size calculation based on your current traffic volumes and commit to the full duration (or until significance is reached via a proper sequential testing method) regardless of interim results.

Given the holiday season approach, prioritize the infrastructure fix immediately. A delayed but valid test is infinitely more valuable than a fast, wrong decision that could cost revenue during your peak season.
