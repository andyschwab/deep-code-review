### Recommendation: Prioritize "Task Completion Rate" and "Search Refinement Frequency" Over Dwell Time

Do not roll out globally yet. The conflicting signals (higher CTR, lower dwell time) indicate the new algorithm is successfully attracting clicks via potentially misleading titles or superficial relevance, but failing to satisfy user intent. Dwell time is a poor proxy for satisfaction in this context because it cannot distinguish between "found the answer instantly" and "gave up immediately."

To prove genuine utility to engineering leadership, shift your primary success metrics from engagement proxies to outcome-based measurements:

**1. Primary Metric: Task Completion Rate (TCR)**
*   **Definition:** The percentage of search sessions that end with a positive user signal, such as clicking "Was this helpful?", copying text from the page, or downloading an attached document.
*   **Why:** This directly measures if the user solved their problem. If CTR is up but TCR is flat or down, the new ranking is generating "clickbait" results that waste user time.
*   **Target:** TCR must remain stable or increase. If CTR rises by 50% but TCR drops, the update is a failure regardless of traffic volume.

**2. Secondary Metric: Search Refinement Frequency (Pogo-sticking)**
*   **Definition:** The rate at which users return to the search results page within 30 seconds of clicking a result to click a *different* link or modify their query.
*   **Why:** This isolates "misleading titles." A user who clicks, spends 10 seconds, returns, and clicks result #2 indicates the first result was irrelevant. High refinement frequency confirms your hypothesis that users are bouncing due to poor relevance.
*   **Target:** This rate should decrease. An increase proves the top results are failing to match the query intent.

**3. Guardrail Metric: Zero-Result Rate & Query Abandonment**
*   **Definition:** Sessions where users click no results or leave the knowledge base entirely immediately after the search.
*   **Why:** If users find the results untrustworthy, they may stop using search altogether.
*   **Target:** Must not increase.

### How to Interpret the Current Data

*   **The "Efficiency" Hypothesis (Optimistic):** If *Task Completion Rate* is stable or higher, and *Refinement Frequency* is lower, then the drop in dwell time is positive. It means users are finding precise answers faster. The algorithm is working.
*   **The "Misleading Title" Hypothesis (Pessimistic):** If *Task Completion Rate* has dropped and *Refinement Frequency* has spiked, the algorithm is prioritizing high-click-through titles over actual relevance. The 90-second dwell time represents users realizing the page is wrong and leaving. The algorithm is broken.

### Immediate Action Plan

Before the global rollout, instrument these three specific events in your analytics stack:
1.  `search_result_clicked` (with position and query)
2.  `search_results_page_viewed` (to detect returns/bounces)
3.  `content_interaction` (copy, download, or "helpful" vote)

Run a qualitative audit on the top 20 most frequent queries. Manually review the new #1 result versus the old #1 result. If the new winners have high CTR but low actual relevance in manual review, revert the deployment. Do not rely on aggregate time-on-page data to make this decision; it is too ambiguous for high-stakes infrastructure changes.

*Note: Ensure your tracking window captures the return-to-search event within a 60-second window to accurately flag bounces.*
*Note: Segment this data by user role; engineering queries may legitimately have shorter dwell times than HR policy queries.*
