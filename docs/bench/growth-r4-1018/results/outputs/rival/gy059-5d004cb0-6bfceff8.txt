# Re-Engagement Diagnostic Framework: FitTrack Dormant User Campaign

## 1. Funnel Bottleneck Analysis
Map the user journey to isolate where drop-off occurs. Do not assume the email is the sole culprit; the friction may exist post-click.

| Funnel Stage | Metric | Diagnostic Question | Data Source |
| :--- | :--- | :--- | :|
| **Delivery** | Bounce/Spam Rate | Did 30k emails actually hit the inbox? | ESP Logs |
| **Engagement** | Open Rate (22%) | Is the subject line working, or is this bot traffic? | ESP Logs |
| **Intent** | Click-Through Rate (1.5%) | **Primary Bottleneck:** Does the offer/messaging fail to persuade? | ESP/Web Analytics |
| **Access** | App Launch Rate | Did clickers successfully open the app? (Check for deep link breaks) | Mobile Attribution |
| **Conversion** | Offer Redemption | Did they see the "Free Week" modal? Did it error out? | Product Analytics |
| **Retention** | Day-3/7 Activity | Did they return and use the app, or churn immediately? | Product Analytics |

**Immediate Hypothesis:** Since opens are decent (22%) but clicks are halved (1.5%), the disconnect is likely between the *expectation* set by the subject line and the *value proposition* in the email body, or the technical execution of the deep link.

## 2. Critical Segmentation Strategy
Stop analyzing the 30,000 users as a monolith. Dormancy is not a single state. Slice the data by these dimensions to find hidden winners or specific failure points:

*   **Dormancy Duration:** Split into 60–90 days vs. 90+ days. Users away for 6 months may have deleted the app or forgotten their password, making a "free week" irrelevant compared to a "reset my account" prompt.
*   **Pre-Churn Behavior:**
    *   *High Engagers:* Previously used app 5+ times/week. (Likely burnout or life event).
    *   *Low Engagers:* Never formed a habit. (Likely product-market fit issue; re-engagement may be futile).
    *   *Feature Specific:* Did they stop after a specific feature update or paywall encounter?
*   **Subscription Tier:** Free vs. Expired Premium. Expired premium users might resent a "free week" offer if they feel the paid version wasn't worth it previously.
*   **Acquisition Channel:** Organic vs. Paid Ads. Paid users often have lower lifetime value and higher churn; they may require a different incentive structure.
*   **Device/OS:** Is the CTR low specifically on iOS or Android? This could indicate a broken deep link or OS-specific rendering issue.

## 3. Content & Offer Audit
*   **Message-Offer Alignment:** Does the email copy explicitly explain *how* to claim the free week? If the CTA says "Come Back" but the offer is "Free Premium," the cognitive load is too high.
*   **Friction Check:** Does clicking the link require a login? If passwords are forgotten, the friction kills the conversion. Consider magic links for this specific campaign.
*   **Offer Relevance:** For users who never paid, "Free Premium" is high value. For those who canceled, it may signal "low quality." Test a "We miss you" content angle vs. a "Financial Incentive" angle.

## 4. Technical Validation Checklist
Before refining creative, rule out breakage:
*   **Deep Link Integrity:** Verify that the email links open the specific app screen (e.g., the premium upgrade modal) rather than just the home screen.
*   **Load Time:** Ensure the landing page loads instantly on mobile data.
*   **Tracking:** Confirm that the "click" event in your ESP matches the "session start" in your analytics. A mismatch suggests link redirection issues.

**Next Steps:** Run the segmentation analysis immediately. If a specific segment (e.g., "60-90 day high engagers") shows >3% CTR, isolate them for the next wave and suppress the underperforming segments to protect sender reputation.

*Note: Ensure your "Free Week" logic doesn't accidentally trigger for users who are already on a trial or have technical account flags.*
*Prioritize fixing deep links before rewriting copy; technical friction is the most common cause of sudden CTR drops.*
