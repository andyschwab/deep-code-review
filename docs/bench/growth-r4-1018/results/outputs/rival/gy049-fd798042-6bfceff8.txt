**Verdict: Preliminary Failure on Core Mission; Do Not Roll Out Globally**

Based strictly on the data provided, the experiment indicates a **failure** relative to HabitHero's core mission of "building daily routines," despite the superficial success in login frequency.

The conflict in your metrics reveals a classic "engagement trap." The 25% increase in daily logins paired with a 40-second drop in session duration suggests the gamification elements (badges, leaderboards) have successfully shifted user motivation from **intrinsic** (building a habit) to **extrinsic** (claiming a reward). Users are treating the app as a transactional vending machine for points rather than a tool for routine formation.

For a habit-building product, **frequency without depth is often a leading indicator of churn, not retention.** If the average session is now too short to complete a meaningful routine activity, the "login" event has become a vanity metric. You are measuring the success of the *game*, not the success of the *habit*. The hypothesis that users are "logging in just to claim rewards and then leaving" is strongly supported by the inverse relationship between your volume and duration metrics.

Rolling this out to 2 million users based on login counts alone risks inflating your top-line engagement numbers while silently eroding the actual value users derive from the product. Once the novelty of the badges wears off, the extrinsic motivation will vanish. Because the intrinsic habit loop was not strengthened (evidenced by the shallower engagement), there is no underlying behavior to sustain the users, likely leading to a sharp drop in retention once the gamification novelty expires.

**On Measuring True Habit Formation**
Since session duration is no longer a reliable proxy, you must decouple "time spent" from "value created." To evaluate true habit formation without relying on specific retention models, you need to identify the **"Action of Value"**—the specific interaction within the app that correlates most strongly with a user returning organically after 30+ days.

If a user logs in, claims a badge, and leaves in 10 seconds, they have not performed the Action of Value. Your analysis must shift from measuring *presence* (logins, time) to measuring *completion* of the core routine. If the data shows that users who spend less time are also failing to complete their core routine tasks, the gamification is actively cannibalizing your product's utility. The trade-off is not positive; you are exchanging deep, sustainable usage for shallow, fragile visits.

**Recommendation:** Halt the global rollout. The current configuration optimizes for the wrong behavior.
