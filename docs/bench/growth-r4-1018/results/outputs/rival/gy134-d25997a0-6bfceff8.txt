# Executive Summary: CloudVault Pricing Experiment Analysis

**Recommendation:** Pause the broad rollout immediately. Proceed only with a targeted, segmented test while modeling long-term LTV scenarios. The current 8% MRR decline indicates severe cannibalization that upfront discounts cannot justify without proven churn reduction.

## Current Situation
The six-week A/B test introduced a discounted annual plan alongside the standard monthly subscription. Results show:
- **New subscribers:** +15% (positive volume signal)
- **Monthly Recurring Revenue (MRR):** -8% (negative revenue signal)
- **Primary issue:** Existing monthly-intent customers are downgrading to the cheaper annual option, cannibalizing higher-margin revenue.

## Key Risks

**1. Revenue Hole Without Churn Payback**
The 8% MRR drop requires substantial churn improvement to break even. If monthly churn is currently 5% monthly, the annual plan customers would need churn below 2% monthly to compensate for the upfront discount within 12 months. Without historical annual plan data, this is unproven.

**2. Customer Segment Cannibalization**
The experiment attracted price-sensitive customers who would have paid full monthly rates. This suggests poor segment targeting rather than genuine new market expansion. The 15% subscriber increase may represent mostly downgraded customers, not incremental growth.

**3. Cash Flow Impact**
While annual plans provide upfront cash, the 8% MRR reduction affects valuation metrics and quarterly revenue recognition. Board expectations for Q4 revenue targets are now at risk.

**4. Pricing Perception Damage**
Once customers anchor on discounted annual pricing, reverting to monthly-only or raising annual rates becomes difficult. This could permanently compress margins.

## Break-Even Modeling Framework

To justify the annual plan discount, calculate the required churn reduction:

**Break-even formula:**
- Monthly plan: $100/month × 12 months × (1 - monthly churn rate)^12
- Annual plan: $1,000 upfront (equivalent to ~$83/month) × 12 months × (1 - annual churn rate)

**Required annual churn rate** = Where annual plan LTV equals monthly plan LTV over 24-36 months.

*Example:* If monthly churn is 5% (≈46% annual retention), annual plan customers need <25% annual churn to break even within 18 months. This requires validation before scaling.

## Potential Long-Term Outcomes

**Optimistic Scenario (20% probability):**
Annual customers demonstrate 60%+ lower churn than monthly cohorts. The 8% MRR dip recovers within 9 months as reduced attrition compounds. LTV increases 25%+ over 24 months.

**Base Case Scenario (50% probability):**
Moderate churn improvement (30-40% reduction) partially offsets revenue loss. MRR stabilizes but doesn't fully recover for 12-15 months. Net LTV gain is marginal (5-10%).

**Pessimistic Scenario (30% probability):**
Churn reduction is minimal (<20%) because price-sensitive customers remain price-sensitive regardless of billing frequency. The 8% MRR loss becomes permanent, and LTV actually decreases due to lower ARPU.

## Immediate Actions Required

1. **Segment the data:** Separate true incremental customers from cannibalized monthly-intent users. The 15% increase likely includes both.

2. **Model multiple scenarios:** Present CFO with 12/24/36-month projections showing MRR recovery timelines at different churn reduction levels (20%, 40%, 60%).

3. **Test targeted rollout:** Restrict annual plan to specific segments (enterprise, specific use cases) where churn is predictably lower, rather than blanket availability.

4. **Adjust discount depth:** Consider reducing the annual discount from current levels to improve unit economics while testing price elasticity.

5. **Establish monitoring cadence:** Track cohort-level churn monthly for the next quarter to validate assumptions before Q4 budget commitment.

## Board Messaging
Frame this as disciplined experimentation revealing critical unit economics before scale. The experiment succeeded in identifying cannibalization risk early—better now than after full rollout. Request approval for a refined, segmented test with clear churn milestones that must be met before Q4 expansion.
