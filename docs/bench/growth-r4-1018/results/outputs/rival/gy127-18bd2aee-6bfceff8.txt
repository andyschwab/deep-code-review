**To:** CFO, Sales Director
**From:** Growth Lead
**Date:** February 21
**Subject:** Pricing Experiment Results: Emergency Thermal Kits (Jan 5 – Feb 20)

**Executive Summary**
The pricing experiment shifting the premium anchor from 4,500 CAD to 5,200 CAD successfully increased overall Average Revenue Per User (ARPU) by 14%. However, this gain came at the cost of an 8% decline in Standard Tier conversion volume. While the net financial impact for the test period is positive, the data suggests we are trading volume for margin, potentially alienating price-sensitive core customers.

**Key Findings**
*   **Revenue Lift:** The 14% increase in ARPU indicates that the higher anchor successfully pulled some customers toward higher-priced options or increased the perceived value of the remaining tiers.
*   **Volume Erosion:** The 8% drop in Standard Tier conversions contradicts the assumption that our institutional buyers are entirely price-insensitive. This segment appears to include volume-driven accounts that are sensitive to relative price positioning.
*   **Net Impact:** Assuming the Standard Tier represents the bulk of volume, an 8% volume loss against a 14% ARPU gain suggests a net positive revenue outcome *for the test period*, provided the margin on the Standard Tier is not significantly higher than the upsell mix.

**Risks & Unknowns**
1.  **Churn Latency:** The six-month churn risk is unquantified. Institutional buyers may accept the price initially for emergency needs but re-evaluate during renewal cycles, leading to delayed churn that this 45-day test cannot detect.
2.  **Core Customer Alienation:** The drop in Standard Tier volume suggests we may be pricing out smaller research outposts or individual researchers who form the base of our market presence. Losing these customers could reduce long-term market share and referenceability.
3.  **Sustainability:** The 14% ARPU lift may be a short-term "shock" effect. Without a longer observation window, we cannot confirm if this behavior stabilizes or reverts.

**Recommendations**
*   **Do Not Roll Out Globally Yet.** The risk of delayed churn and volume erosion in core segments is too high without further validation.
*   **Segment Analysis:** Immediately break down the conversion drop by customer type (Institutional vs. Individual). If the 8% drop is entirely driven by individuals, the strategy may still be viable for institutional channels.
*   **Monitor Leading Churn Indicators:** Track support tickets, renewal inquiries, and procurement cycle times for customers acquired during the test period compared to the prior cohort.
*   **Extended Test:** Run the pricing for an additional 60 days specifically targeting institutional buyers to isolate price sensitivity from seasonal demand fluctuations.

**Decision Required**
Approve a segmented analysis and extended monitoring period before authorizing global implementation.

***

*Note: This analysis relies solely on the provided aggregate metrics; granular cohort data is required to validate churn assumptions.*
*The 14% ARPU gain must be weighed against the lifetime value loss of the 8% of customers who did not convert.*
