To calculate CanvasFlow's true virality coefficient (K-factor) and isolate organic growth from paid influence, you must aggregate the following specific data points from your fragmented databases into a unified user-level dataset keyed by `user_id` and `session_id`.

### 1. Required Data Points for Aggregation

**From Database A (User & Account Context):**
*   `user_id`: Unique identifier.
*   `account_id`: To group users into teams.
*   `registration_date`: To filter for the post-launch window (March 15–Present).
*   `acquisition_source`: Raw UTM parameters (`utm_source`, `utm_medium`, `utm_campaign`) and `referrer_url` at sign-up.
*   `inviter_id`: The ID of the user who sent the invite link (if captured at sign-up).

**From Database B (Session & Collaboration Activity):**
*   `session_id`: Unique session identifier.
*   `timestamp`: Start and end time.
*   `action_type`: Specifically filter for "invite_sent," "link_copied," "board_shared," and "collaborator_joined."
*   `active_members_count`: Snapshot of team size during the session.
*   `device_type` & `location`: To help filter bot traffic or anomalies.

### 2. Data Cleaning & Segmentation Logic

Before calculation, apply these filters to ensure data integrity:
*   **Time Window:** Restrict all data to March 15 through the day before your board meeting (April 29).
*   **Exclude Paid Traffic:** Filter out any new user where `utm_medium` matches `cpc`, `ppc`, `paid_social`, or `affiliate`. Retain only users with `organic`, `referral`, `direct`, or empty UTM values.
*   **Define "Active Team":** Flag accounts where `active_members_count` > 3 at any point in the observation window.

### 3. The Calculation Structure

Do not use a simple aggregate ratio. You must calculate the coefficient based on **cohorts** to account for the time lag between invitation and conversion.

**Step A: Define the Cohort**
Group existing users (inviters) by their registration week (e.g., Week of March 15, Week of March 22). This ensures you are comparing inviters who have had equal time to generate referrals.

**Step B: Calculate the Numerator (Valid Invites)**
Count the number of *new, qualified* sign-ups generated by each cohort.
*   *Condition:* New user must have `acquisition_source` = "referral" AND `inviter_id` matches a user in the cohort.
*   *Validation:* Exclude sign-ups that did not result in a "collaborator_joined" event within 7 days (filters link sharing without collaboration).

**Step C: Calculate the Denominator (Eligible Inviters)**
Count the number of users in the cohort who performed at least one sharing action ("invite_sent" or "link_copied").
*   *Critical Note:* Do not use total sign-ups as the denominator. Only users who actually attempted to share can generate viral growth.

**Step D: The Formula**
$$K = \frac{\sum (\text{Valid New Users per Cohort})}{\sum (\text{Active Inviters per Cohort})}$$

**Step E: Isolate the "Team Size" Hypothesis**
Run the calculation twice:
1.  **Global K:** All active inviters.
2.  **Segmented K:** Filter denominator to only include inviters belonging to accounts with >3 active members.

If *Segmented K* is significantly higher (>1.0) while *Global K* is low (<0.5), your hypothesis is confirmed: large teams drive the loop, and small teams are diluting the metric.

### 4. Strategic Recommendation Framework

Present the results to your VP of Growth using this decision matrix:
*   **If K > 1.0 for Large Teams:** Double down on incentives targeted specifically at accounts hitting the 3-user threshold. Do not broaden incentives to all users.
*   **If K < 0.5 across all segments:** The loop is broken. Sharing is happening, but conversion/collaboration is not. Pivot budget to direct sales outreach immediately; do not scale incentives.
*   **If Paid Traffic Contamination > 15%:** Your current attribution is flawed. Delay the decision until UTM hygiene is fixed, as you cannot trust the organic baseline.

Ensure the final report explicitly states the observation window dates and the exclusion criteria for paid traffic to satisfy board scrutiny regarding data validity.

*Note: Since session data is fragmented, verify that `user_id` formats match across both databases before joining; mismatches here will artificially deflate your denominator.*
*Do not include pending invitations (sent but not accepted) in the numerator; they represent potential, not realized virality.*
