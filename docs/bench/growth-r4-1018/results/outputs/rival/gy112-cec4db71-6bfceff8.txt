**Recommendation: Adopt "Verified Yield per Active Cycle" as the Primary North Star Metric**

For GreenStack's Q3 roadmap and sensor hardware justification, the primary KPI must be **Verified Yield per Active Cycle** (defined as *harvestable biomass meeting quality standards* divided by *completed growing cycles*), rather than System Uptime.

While System Uptime is a critical operational guardrail, it is a lagging indicator of infrastructure reliability, not a leading indicator of customer success. Your February churn data provides definitive proof: clients with 99.8% uptime left due to poor harvest quality. This decoupling demonstrates that uptime is a necessary baseline condition, but it does not drive retention or value. Customers pay for crops, not for lights that stay on.

**Trade-off Analysis: Yield vs. Uptime**

Focusing on **System Uptime** as the North Star creates a dangerous misalignment between engineering incentives and agronomic reality.
*   **The Risk:** Engineering teams will optimize for stability above all else, potentially refusing necessary environmental fluctuations (e.g., stress-testing temperature or humidity) that agronomy requires to maximize flavor, density, or nutrient profiles.
*   **The Blind Spot:** As seen in February, you can achieve perfect uptime while delivering a failed crop. This metric hides the "silent failures" where the system runs perfectly but the biological output is unsellable.
*   **Seasonal Context:** Uptime is static; it does not account for seasonal variations in light intensity or crop types. A system can be up 100% in winter but produce 40% less yield due to natural light variance. Uptime alone fails to capture this efficiency drop.

Focusing on **Verified Yield per Active Cycle** aligns the entire organization toward the customer's economic outcome.
*   **The Alignment:** This forces Engineering to build sensors and controls that specifically protect *biological output*, not just machine status. It validates the budget for new sensor hardware by tying it directly to the quality and quantity of the harvest.
*   **The Trade-off:** This metric introduces variability. Yield fluctuates based on crop type, season, and grower skill. It is noisier than uptime. However, this noise is the signal you need to improve the product.
*   **Seasonal Context:** This metric naturally absorbs seasonal variations. If winter yields drop, the metric reflects it, prompting investigations into whether the system needs to compensate (e.g., supplemental lighting algorithms) rather than masking the issue with a green "99.9% Uptime" dashboard.

**Implementation Strategy for the May 12 Session**

To secure the sensor hardware budget, frame the new sensors not as "monitoring tools" but as "yield assurance devices." The roadmap should explicitly state that these sensors will close the loop between environmental data and harvest quality, directly moving the North Star.

**Operational Guardrail:** Do not discard Uptime entirely. Demote it to a **Threshold Guardrail Metric**. Set a strict minimum (e.g., 99.5%). If uptime falls below this, it triggers an immediate incident response, but it does not become the primary focus of the quarterly strategy. This ensures operational efficiency is maintained without distracting from the ultimate goal of customer value.

By choosing Verified Yield, you align Engineering and Agronomy around the only outcome that prevents churn: a successful harvest.
