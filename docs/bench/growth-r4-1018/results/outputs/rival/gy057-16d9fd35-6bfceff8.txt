### Executive Summary: Baroque Recommendation Engine Test
**Status:** Inconclusive / High Risk
**Recommendation:** Do not launch globally. Pause for qualitative diagnosis.

The six-week experiment presents a classic "engagement trap." While daily listening time increased by **14 minutes**, the skip rate for recommended tracks nearly doubled (**22% → 39%**). This divergence indicates that the algorithm is successfully capturing attention but failing to deliver satisfaction.

#### Interpreting the Conflict
The data suggests users are not "engaging" in the traditional sense; they are **hunting**.

1.  **The "Hunting" Hypothesis:** The 14-minute increase likely represents time spent skipping through multiple obscure tracks to find one tolerable piece, rather than sustained listening. In classical music, where movements can be long, a user might skip three 5-minute tracks before finding a fourth they enjoy. The time accumulates, but the experience is frustrating.
2.  **The "Academic" Mismatch:** Your assumption that "deeper catalog exploration equals engagement" appears flawed for this segment. While subscribers may *say* they want obscure baroque, their behavior (high skip rate) proves the suggestions are too niche, too academic, or contextually wrong.
3.  **Interface Ambiguity:** The unknown variable is the "save" friction. If users cannot easily bookmark a track to explore later, they may skip it immediately to avoid losing their place, inflating the skip rate artificially. However, a 17-point jump is too large to blame solely on UI; the core relevance logic is the primary suspect.

#### Strategic Implications for Funding
For your upcoming funding round, launching a feature that doubles frustration metrics (skips) while inflating vanity metrics (time) is dangerous. Investors will view the high skip rate as a signal of poor product-market fit or a broken recommendation engine.

*   **Risk of Launch:** Churn risk increases if users feel the service no longer understands their taste.
*   **Opportunity:** The 14-minute lift proves there is *latent demand* for exploration, but the current delivery mechanism is broken.

#### Immediate Next Steps
Do not adjust the logic blindly yet. You must distinguish between "bad suggestions" and "bad UI."

1.  **Qualitative Sprint (1 Week):** Conduct 5–10 user interviews with participants from the test group. Ask specifically: "Did you skip because you disliked the piece, or because you wanted to save it for later?"
2.  **Metric Refinement:** Calculate a "Completion Rate" for recommended tracks. If the 14-minute gain comes from many short listens (skips), the feature is failing. If it comes from fewer, longer listens despite the skip rate, the feature has potential.
3.  **UI Test:** Before retraining the model, add a prominent "Save for Later" or "Explore Composer" button to the recommendation card. Re-run a micro-test to see if the skip rate drops when users have an alternative to the skip button.

**Decision:** Kill the global rollout. Pivot to a "Discovery Mode" optional feature while you validate whether the skips are due to taste mismatch or interface limitations.
