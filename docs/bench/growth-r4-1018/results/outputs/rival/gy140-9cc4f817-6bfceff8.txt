# NewsDaily Push Notification Default Change: Go/No-Go Assessment

## Executive Recommendation: **NO-GO (Revert Immediately)**

The current trajectory indicates a critical trust erosion that outweighs short-term engagement gains. While the 30% increase in session starts is statistically significant, the complaint volume (200+ formal complaints in a single cycle) signals a "dark pattern" perception that threatens long-term brand equity and retention.

## Quantitative vs. Qualitative Weighting Framework

To make this decision rigorous for the CEO and Head of Brand, apply the following **Trust-Adjusted Value (TAV)** calculation rather than looking at engagement in isolation:

### 1. The Engagement Illusion
*   **Gain:** +30% session starts driven by 85% opt-in rate (vs. 20% baseline).
*   **Risk:** This is "forced engagement," not "valued engagement." Users opening the app to disable notifications or out of confusion do not represent genuine retention.
*   **Metric to Watch:** Monitor **Day-7 Retention** and **Uninstall Rate** over the next 14 days. If uninstalls rise by >5% above baseline, the LTV loss from churned users will exceed the LTV gain from increased session frequency.

### 2. The Complaint Velocity Threshold
*   **Current State:** 200+ complaints is an anomaly. In mobile apps, a spike of this magnitude usually precedes a wave of 1-star app store reviews, which directly impacts organic acquisition (conversion rate drops).
*   **Threshold Rule:** If complaint volume exceeds 0.1% of the affected user base within 48 hours, the feature is considered "hostile."
*   **Action:** Calculate the ratio: `Complaints / Total Users Affected`. If this exceeds 0.05%, the reputational cost (support hours + brand damage) exceeds the ad/impression revenue from the extra sessions.

### 3. The Trust Tax
*   **Qualitative Signal:** Users feel violated, not served. The assumption that "they will appreciate it once they customize" is flawed; users who feel tricked rarely return to customize—they leave.
*   **Brand Impact:** Press cycles amplify "privacy violation" narratives. Reverting *before* the press cycle turns a "product misstep" into a "responsive customer care" story.

## Decision Matrix

| Scenario | Action | Rationale |
| :--- | :--- | :--- |
| **High Engagement + Low Complaints** | **GO** | Valid growth hack. |
| **High Engagement + High Complaints** | **NO-GO** | **Current State.** Short-term gain bought with long-term trust. |
| **Low Engagement + High Complaints** | **NO-GO** | Immediate failure. |
| **Low Engagement + Low Complaints** | **ITERATE** | Neutral impact, test different copy. |

## Immediate Next Steps

1.  **Revert Default:** Change the default back to "Opt-In" immediately. Frame this internally as a "trust preservation" move, not a retreat.
2.  **Proactive Communication:** Email the 85% who were opted in: *"We heard you. We changed our default settings too aggressively. You are still opted in, but here is a one-click way to adjust preferences or opt-out."* This turns a complaint into a trust-building moment.
3.  **A/B Test a Middle Ground:** Instead of a hard default, test an **interstitial modal** on the first launch explaining the value of breaking news with a clear "Yes/No" choice. This respects agency while highlighting value.
4.  **Monitor Cohorts:** Isolate the "Forced Opt-In" cohort. If their Day-30 retention is lower than the historical "Voluntary Opt-In" cohort, the data proves the hypothesis wrong.

**Conclusion:** Do not wait for the uninstall data to confirm the trend. The volume of qualitative feedback is a leading indicator of churn. Prioritize brand trust over inflated session metrics.

*Note: This assessment assumes the 200 complaints represent a statistically significant sample of silent dissatisfaction. Ensure support tickets are tagged by "unwanted notifications" to track the exact decay rate.*
*Reverting now allows you to control the narrative before external press picks up the story.*
