# StockPop Pilot Measurement Plan: 48-Hour Early Warning System

To address the three-week operational window and the goal of minimizing stockouts during the peak weekend (Dec 15–17), this plan prioritizes **velocity** and **variance** over absolute volume. Since foot traffic is unknown, we must normalize all metrics against the store's own early performance to detect misalignment relative to its specific context, rather than an arbitrary corporate average.

### Core Philosophy: Relative Velocity, Not Absolute Targets
Do not measure success against the projected "500 transactions/day" initially. A location with naturally low foot traffic might hit 500 transactions only by running out of stock too fast, while a high-traffic location might process 800 and still have dangerous overstock. Instead, measure how quickly specific SKUs move relative to the store's opening baseline.

### The 48-Hour Leading Indicators
Regional directors and logistics teams should monitor these three specific ratios daily. Any deviation triggers an immediate review for stock reallocation.

**1. The "Day-2 Decay" Ratio**
*   **Definition:** Compare the transaction velocity of Day 2 to Day 1 for top-tier SKUs (the top 20% of items by expected volume).
*   **The Warning Sign:** If Day 2 velocity drops by more than 40% compared to Day 1, it indicates either immediate stockout risk (if inventory is low) or a severe product-market mismatch (if inventory is high).
*   **Why it works:** In a pop-up, curiosity drives Day 1. Day 2 reflects actual demand. A sharp decay suggests the initial rush consumed the "easy" sales, and the remaining inventory is not moving fast enough to clear before the peak weekend.

**2. The Stock-to-Sale Coverage Hours**
*   **Definition:** Calculate `(Current On-Hand Inventory / Average Hourly Sales Rate)` for critical categories.
*   **The Warning Sign:** If coverage hours fall below 36 hours for any top-tier SKU before December 14th, flag for emergency transfer. Conversely, if coverage exceeds 120 hours (5 days) after 48 hours of operation, the item is dead weight and should be pulled or discounted immediately to free up space.
*   **Why it works:** This converts unknown foot traffic into a time-based runway. It tells you exactly when a stockout will happen if trends continue, allowing logistics to intervene before the Dec 15–17 peak.

**3. The "Empty Shelf" Frequency**
*   **Definition:** The number of times a top-tier SKU hits zero inventory and is restocked within a single operating day.
*   **The Warning Sign:** More than one restock event per day for a single SKU indicates demand is outpacing the replenishment cycle.
*   **Why it works:** This is a direct proxy for lost sales due to stockouts. In a three-week window, you cannot afford days where shelves are empty during prime hours. This metric highlights supply chain lag immediately.

### Implementation for Rapid Reallocation
To account for unknown foot traffic, establish a **Store-Specific Baseline** at the end of Day 1. All subsequent alerts are triggered by deviations from *that specific store's* Day 1 performance, not a network average.

*   **Green Zone:** Velocity is stable (±20% of Day 1); Coverage hours are between 48–96 hours.
*   **Amber Zone:** Velocity decay >30% OR Coverage hours <48 or >100. Action: Prepare transfer orders.
*   **Red Zone:** Velocity decay >50%, Coverage hours <36, or multiple restocks/day. Action: Execute immediate inter-store transfer or flash promotion.

By focusing on the *rate of change* and *time-to-stockout* rather than total revenue, your team can identify failing locations within 48 hours and redistribute inventory to maximize availability for the critical December 15–17 peak weekend.

*Note: Ensure POS systems timestamp every transaction to calculate hourly rates accurately. Do not wait for end-of-day reports; use real-time dashboards for the "Coverage Hours" metric.*
