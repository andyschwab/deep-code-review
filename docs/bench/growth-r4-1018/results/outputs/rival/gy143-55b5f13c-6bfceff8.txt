**Experiment Evaluation: Dragon Quest Chronicles (South Korea)**

**Conflicting Signals**
The test presents a high-reward, high-risk profile. The **15% increase in revenue per daily active user (ARPDAU)** indicates strong initial demand and effective pricing for the 9,900 KRW bundle. However, the **4x spike in refund rates (2% to 8%)** severely undermines this gain. If the refund rate stabilizes at 8%, the *net* revenue lift may be negligible or negative once processing fees and potential platform penalties are accounted for. The core conflict is between top-line growth velocity and transactional integrity.

**Critical Monitoring Signals**
To decide between a global launch or feature termination, monitor these specific metrics over the next 14 days:

1.  **Refund Rate Trajectory:** Plot the daily refund rate. A "stabilization" hypothesis requires the rate to trend downward toward baseline (2-3%) as users familiarize themselves with the flow. If the rate remains flat at 6-8%, the issue is structural, not behavioral.
2.  **Time-to-Refund Distribution:** Analyze the timestamp delta between purchase and refund request.
    *   *Signal for Accidental Purchase:* High volume of refunds occurring within <5 minutes.
    *   *Signal for Value Mismatch:* Refunds occurring hours or days later suggest the product did not meet expectations.
3.  **Device & Account Context:** Segment refunds by device type (shared family tablet vs. personal phone) and account age. If refunds concentrate on devices with multiple user profiles or new accounts, this supports the "younger player" hypothesis.
4.  **Net Revenue Per User (NRPU):** Recalculate the primary success metric as `(Gross Revenue - Refunded Amount) / DAU`. If NRPU is not statistically higher than the control group, the feature fails regardless of gross volume.

**Required Context for Decision**
Before approving a wider rollout, the following context is essential:
*   **Platform Policy Risk:** Google Play and Apple App Store monitor refund ratios. Sustained rates >5% can trigger manual reviews, loss of featured placement, or account suspension. Confirm the threshold for penalties in the South Korean region.
*   **Friction Analysis:** Does the current purchase flow lack a confirmation step specific to high-value bundles? In markets with high family-device sharing, a "swipe-to-buy" mechanic without a secondary confirmation (e.g., biometric or password re-entry) is a known driver of accidental spend.
*   **Customer Support Volume:** Qualitative data is needed. Are support tickets citing "child made purchase" or "didn't receive items"? The former validates the accident theory; the latter indicates a technical bug.

**Recommendation Path**
*   **Launch Globally:** Only if the refund rate trends below 4% within two weeks AND net revenue remains significantly positive.
*   **Kill Feature:** If the refund rate holds above 6% or if platform policy risks outweigh the marginal net revenue gain.
*   **Iterate (Most Likely):** Implement a mandatory confirmation step for this specific price point in Korea and re-test. The data suggests the *offer* is valid, but the *friction* is insufficient for this demographic.

*Note: Do not extrapolate the 15% gross lift to global markets without adjusting for regional purchasing power and refund behaviors.*
*Ensure legal compliance regarding minor protection laws in South Korea before any further rollout.*
