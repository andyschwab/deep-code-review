# Strategic Brief: Refining FlowBank Churn Prediction Model

**To:** Head of Data Science, Customer Success Director
**From:** Retention Strategy Lead
**Date:** October 26, 2023
**Subject:** Balancing Precision and Recall in Churn Prediction

### Current Challenge
Our current model (threshold 0.6) achieves high recall (85% detection) but suffers from low precision, flagging 40% of active users as high-risk. This creates significant operational inefficiency for Customer Success (CS) while risking "alert fatigue." Simply raising the threshold to 0.8 risks missing subtle churn signals from high-value enterprise clients, whose usage patterns differ fundamentally from standard users.

### Recommended Strategy: Segmented Thresholding & Feature Enrichment

Rather than applying a global threshold adjustment, we recommend a **segmented approach** combined with **feature enrichment** to distinguish between "noise" and "signal."

#### 1. Implement Segment-Specific Thresholds
Enterprise clients and standard users exhibit different baseline behaviors. A single threshold forces a trade-off that harms one segment to help the other.
*   **Action:** Split the model output by customer tier (Enterprise vs. SMB/Standard).
*   **Proposal:**
    *   **Standard Users:** Raise threshold to **0.75–0.8**. This segment generates high volume; reducing false positives here yields immediate CS efficiency gains with acceptable risk, as their churn drivers are often price or immediate friction.
    *   **Enterprise Clients:** Maintain or lower threshold to **0.55**, but require **secondary validation**. Enterprise churn is high-cost; missing a signal is more expensive than a false alarm. However, false alarms here damage relationships.

#### 2. Integrate "Health Score" Behavioral Signals
The current model likely relies heavily on negative signals (login drop-off, support tickets). We must integrate positive engagement metrics to filter out "happy but inactive" users who are falsely flagged.
*   **Depth of Usage:** Analyze feature adoption breadth. A user logging in less but using *critical* core features deeply is likely stable.
*   **Stakeholder Expansion:** For Enterprise, track *new* user invitations or seat expansions within the account. Churning accounts rarely expand.
*   **Support Sentiment:** Differentiate between "high ticket volume" (often a sign of active implementation) and "negative sentiment/frustration" in ticket text. Current logic may treat volume alone as risk.
*   **Executive Alignment:** Track engagement from key decision-makers (not just end-users). If the CIO logs in monthly, the account is likely safe even if daily users are quiet.

#### 3. Adopt a Tiered Intervention Protocol
Align model output with CS resource allocation to prevent waste.
*   **Tier 1 (High Confidence > 0.8):** Immediate senior CS outreach.
*   **Tier 2 (Medium Confidence 0.6–0.8):**
    *   *Enterprise:* Personalized check-in by Account Manager.
    *   *Standard:* Automated nurture campaign or in-app survey to gauge sentiment before human intervention.
*   **Tier 3 (Low Confidence < 0.6):** Monitor only; no outreach.

### Expected Outcomes
*   **Efficiency:** Reducing false positives among standard users by raising their specific threshold will cut unnecessary outreach volume by an estimated 25–30%.
*   **Coverage:** Maintaining lower thresholds for Enterprise, validated by positive behavioral signals, ensures we capture subtle churn risks without blinding the team to noise.
*   **Relationship Health:** Preventing unnecessary "save calls" to happy enterprise clients preserves trust and reduces annoyance.

### Next Steps
1.  **Data Science:** Re-run historical data with segmented thresholds to quantify the precision/recall trade-off per tier.
2.  **CS Leadership:** Define the "positive health signals" specific to our top 20 enterprise accounts for feature engineering.
3.  **Pilot:** Launch the tiered intervention protocol for 30 days before full rollout.

---
*This approach shifts the goal from a single "perfect score" to a dynamic decision framework aligned with customer value.*
