To isolate the causal impact of the video testimonials and rule out seasonal noise or the email campaign, you must move beyond simple before-and-after comparisons. Since the videos were launched globally on August 22nd without a native A/B test, you need to construct a quasi-experimental design using historical data and segmentation.

**Step 1: Construct a Synthetic Control or Interrupted Time Series**
Do not compare the three weeks post-launch to the three weeks pre-launch in isolation. Instead, perform an Interrupted Time Series (ITS) analysis.
*   **Data Collection:** Gather daily pricing page CTR data for at least 12 weeks prior to August 22nd and the 3 weeks post-launch.
*   **Baseline Modeling:** Fit a trend line to the pre-launch data that accounts for day-of-week seasonality and any existing growth trends.
*   **Counterfactual Projection:** Extend this trend line into the post-launch period to establish what CTR *would* have been had the videos not been added.
*   **Impact Calculation:** The causal lift is the difference between the actual observed CTR (4.8%) and the projected counterfactual CTR. If the actual data points fall consistently outside the 95% confidence interval of your projection, you have statistical evidence of an intervention effect.

**Step 2: Isolate the Email Campaign Confounder**
To rule out the email campaign as the primary driver:
*   **Segment by Traffic Source:** Filter your analytics to compare CTR lifts across "Organic/Direct" traffic versus "Email" traffic.
*   **Logic:** If the lift is driven solely by the email campaign, you will see a spike in CTR only within the Email segment, while Organic/Direct remains flat. If the video is the driver, you should see a sustained lift across *all* traffic sources, including organic visitors who never received the email.
*   **Temporal Check:** If the email campaign was a short burst but the CTR lift has remained steady for three weeks, this supports the video hypothesis (which is always present) over the email hypothesis (which is transient).

**Step 3: Validate by Industry Vertical**
To determine if the effect varies by industry:
*   **Firmographic Segmentation:** Use your analytics tool's custom dimensions (or reverse-IP lookup data if available) to segment visitors by industry vertical.
*   **Relevance Matrix:** Map the three featured enterprise clients to their respective industries.
*   **Comparative Analysis:** Compare the CTR delta for industries *featured* in the videos versus industries *not featured*.
    *   *Hypothesis:* Industries seeing their peers in the videos should show a significantly higher CTR lift than unrelated industries.
    *   *Validation:* If non-featured industries show the same lift as featured ones, the driver may be general social proof rather than specific peer validation. If only featured industries lifted, the content is highly targeted but may have limited broader appeal.

**Step 4: Calculate ROI for the CMO**
Present the findings using a conservative attribution model:
*   **Incremental Conversions:** (Actual Conversions – Projected Counterfactual Conversions) × Average Deal Value.
*   **Cost Basis:** Total production cost of the three videos.
*   **Projection:** If the "Featured Industry" lift is 2x higher than the "Non-Featured" lift, recommend producing videos only for top-tier verticals first, rather than a blanket production of ten random feature videos.

**Recommendation for Future Rigor**
For the next ten videos, do not launch globally. Use a holdout test: show the new video to 50% of traffic and the old static page to 50%. This provides immediate, undeniable causality without needing complex statistical reconstruction.
