**Recommended North Star Metric: Active Enterprise Retention Rate (AERR)**

**Definition:** The percentage of enterprise clients who purchase verified carbon offsets in at least two distinct quarters within a rolling 12-month window.

**Rationale for Selection**
While total volume (50,000 tons) demonstrates scale, it incentivizes sales to close large, one-off deals with low-quality projects, ignoring your concern for permanence and quality. Pure retention based on a 14-month contract is too lagging for quarterly product decisions. AERR bridges this gap by measuring *sustained value delivery* rather than just initial acquisition or long-term contract status. It forces the organization to prove that the product delivers enough ongoing value to trigger a second purchase within a year, regardless of the initial contract length.

**Evaluation of Candidate Metrics**

| Metric | Pros | Cons | Behavioral Influence |
| :--- | :--- | :--- | :--- |
| **Total Tons Offset** | Simple to track; shows immediate market scale. | Ignores project quality/permanence; encourages "bulk dumping" of cheap credits. | **Sales:** Pushes largest possible one-time deals.<br>**Product:** Prioritizes checkout speed over verification tools. |
| **Number of Active Buyers** | Tracks market penetration; good for top-of-funnel health. | Does not distinguish between a trial user and a committed partner; ignores depth of engagement. | **Sales:** Focuses on closing new logos at any cost.<br>**Product:** Optimizes onboarding friction, neglects long-term utility. |
| **Project Diversity Score** | Directly aligns with quality/permanence goals; encourages portfolio building. | Hard to define objectively; may incentivize selling unnecessary variety rather than solving client needs. | **Sales:** May oversell complex bundles to check boxes.<br>**Product:** Builds catalog features, potentially ignoring core purchasing flow. |
| **Repeat Purchase Rate (Quarterly)** | Measures immediate satisfaction; short feedback loop. | Can be volatile; might encourage small, frequent transactions that don't reflect strategic enterprise partnerships. | **Sales:** Nudges clients to buy frequently but potentially in smaller, less strategic amounts.<br>**Product:** Focuses on transactional ease over strategic planning tools. |
| **Active Enterprise Retention Rate (Recommended)** | Balances frequency and commitment; filters out one-off buyers; actionable within 3-6 months. | Requires defining "active" clearly; slightly more complex to calculate than raw volume. | **Sales:** Incentivizes landing clients with genuine long-term needs.<br>**Product:** Prioritizes features that prove value early enough to trigger a Q2/Q3 purchase (e.g., impact reporting, portfolio analytics). |

**Implementation Impact**
Adopting AERR will shift Engineering's focus from merely processing transactions to building tools that help clients *understand* and *report* on their offset impact quickly. If clients can see the value of their initial purchase through robust dashboards and verification data, they are more likely to return within the year. Simultaneously, Sales will be discouraged from signing "one-and-done" deals that look good on volume charts but fail to generate recurring revenue, aligning their incentives with the long-term health required for Series B growth.

*Note: Ensure your data pipeline can attribute purchases to specific client IDs across quarters to calculate this accurately.*
*This metric should be reviewed monthly to catch drops in repeat behavior before the 12-month window closes.*
