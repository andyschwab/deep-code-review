# Framework: Efficiency-Value Alignment for CaseMaster

**Recommendation:** Optimize for **Task Completion Rate (TCR)** supported by **Time-to-Completion**, not Total Engagement Time.

For legal research tools sold on "efficiency gains," longer sessions often indicate friction, not value. Your support tickets confirming users struggle to find case law validate that the current 45-minute average likely represents wasted billable hours, not deep engagement. Enterprise clients renew based on reduced research costs; therefore, your success criteria must measure speed and accuracy, not duration.

### Metric Definitions & Alignment

| Metric | What It Measures | Alignment with "Reducing Costs" | Risk if Optimized Alone |
| :--- | :--- | :--- | : |
| **Task Completion Rate** | % of sessions where a user finds specific case law and exports/saves it. | **High.** Directly measures successful research outcomes. | Users might settle for irrelevant cases just to "complete" the task quickly. |
| **Total Engagement Time** | Duration from login to logout. | **Low/Negative.** In this context, likely measures confusion and dead-ends. | Incentivizes confusing UI that traps users, increasing their billable waste. |
| **Time-to-Completion** | Time elapsed between search start and successful export/save. | **Critical.** The true efficiency metric. | Could encourage superficial research if not paired with quality checks. |

### Interpreting Conflicting Signals

When running your A/B test, you will likely encounter scenarios where these metrics diverge. Use this decision matrix to interpret results:

**Scenario A: Engagement Time ⬇️ | Task Completion Rate ⬆️**
*   **Interpretation:** **Ideal Outcome.** Users are finding what they need faster. Friction is removed.
*   **Action:** Declare victory. This directly supports the "efficiency gain" narrative for December renewals.

**Scenario B: Engagement Time ⬆️ | Task Completion Rate ⬇️**
*   **Interpretation:** **Critical Failure.** Users are spending more time but failing more often. This confirms the "friction" hypothesis.
*   **Action:** Reject the variant immediately. This increases client costs and churn risk.

**Scenario C: Engagement Time ⬆️ | Task Completion Rate ➡️ (Flat)**
*   **Interpretation:** **Hidden Friction.** Users are eventually succeeding, but the path is harder. They may be reading more irrelevant results or navigating poor information architecture.
*   **Action:** Investigate "Time-to-Completion." If the time per successful task has increased, treat this as a negative result. Do not be fooled by stable completion rates; efficiency has degraded.

**Scenario D: Engagement Time ⬇️ | Task Completion Rate ⬇️**
*   **Interpretation:** **Abandonment.** Users are giving up faster. The tool feels broken or results are poor.
*   **Action:** Critical failure. Investigate search relevance and zero-result pages.

### Implementation for October 10 A/B Test

To satisfy enterprise renewal requirements, structure your test success criteria as follows:

1.  **Primary Metric:** **Task Completion Rate** (defined as: Search Initiated → Case Viewed → Export/Save Action).
2.  **Secondary Metric:** **Median Time-to-Completion** for successful tasks.
3.  **Guardrail Metric:** **Support Ticket Volume** related to "search difficulty" during the test window.
4.  **Reporting Narrative:** "Our new dashboard reduces average research time per case by X% while maintaining/improving success rates, directly lowering your firm's billable waste."

Do not report "Total Session Time" as a success metric to clients. Instead, frame the *reduction* in session time for specific tasks as the core value proposition.

*Note: Ensure your tracking plan distinguishes between "idle time" (user thinking) and "active struggle" (rapid clicking, filtering, reformulating searches) to refine the Time-to-Completion metric further.*
*This framework assumes your definition of "Task Completion" includes a verifiable action like exporting or saving; if not, implement this tracking before October 10.*
