### Executive Summary: Crypto Payment Rollout Evaluation (April 1–14)

**Recommendation: Do Not Expand Yet.**
While the crypto segment demonstrates a compelling **70% increase in Average Order Value (AOV)** ($145 vs. $85), the **2.5% chargeback rate** represents a critical financial risk that currently outweighs the revenue benefit. Expanding to 50% of traffic without resolving this variable exposes the company to unsustainable losses and potential compliance penalties. Approval for full launch should be contingent on reducing the net fraud loss below the gross margin threshold.

#### 1. Financial Impact Analysis
The "success" of this test cannot be determined by AOV alone; it must be evaluated on **Net Revenue per Order**.

*   **Gross Uplift:** The crypto cohort generates an additional **$60 per order** ($145 - $85).
*   **Fraud Cost:** At a 2.5% chargeback rate, you lose the entire order value ($145) on 1 out of every 40 transactions, plus associated fees.
    *   *Expected Fraud Loss per Order:* $145 × 2.5% = **$3.63**.
*   **The Hidden Variable:** Chargebacks often carry additional administrative fees ($15–$25 per incident) and operational costs. If we assume a $20 fee per chargeback:
    *   *Additional Fee Loss per Order:* $20 × 2.5% = **$0.50**.
    *   *Total Expected Loss per Order:* **$4.13**.

**Verdict:** If your gross margin on a standard $85 order is less than **$64.13** (the $60 uplift + $4.13 fraud cost), this payment method is currently **profit-negative** despite the higher basket size. For high-margin digital goods, this may still be profitable; for low-margin physical goods, this is likely a net loss.

#### 2. Critical Fraud Metrics to Monitor Before Expansion
Before expanding to 50% of traffic, the Risk Compliance team must establish baselines for the following metrics over a 30-day observation window:

1.  **Net Revenue Retention Rate:** Calculate `(Total Crypto Revenue - Chargebacks - Fees) / Total Crypto Revenue`. This must exceed the retention rate of traditional payment methods.
2.  **Chargeback Reason Code Distribution:** Analyze whether chargebacks are "Friendly Fraud" (customer claims they didn't authorize) or "True Fraud" (stolen credentials). Crypto transactions are irreversible; if the majority are "True Fraud," your current verification stack is insufficient.
3.  **Time-to-Chargeback Latency:** Measure the days between transaction and dispute. Crypto chargebacks often arrive faster than credit card disputes. If the latency is <7 days, you can implement real-time blocking rules; if >45 days, you face prolonged capital exposure.
4.  **First-Party Fraud Rate:** Track users who make a small successful crypto purchase followed immediately by a large one that is charged back. This indicates account testing.

#### 3. Strategic Next Steps for CFO Approval
To secure approval by month-end, propose a **"Controlled Scale-Up"** rather than a full 50% expansion:

*   **Implement Velocity Limits:** Cap crypto transactions at $200/day per user until the chargeback rate drops below 1.0%.
*   **Require Enhanced Verification:** Mandate 3D Secure or additional ID verification for crypto payments exceeding $100.
*   **Run a Cohort Analysis:** Isolate the 15% test group by user tenure. If the chargebacks are concentrated among new accounts (<30 days old), restrict crypto payments to verified, tenured users only.

**Conclusion:** The high AOV proves demand exists, but the 2.5% chargeback rate is a "stop ship" signal for a broad rollout. Proceed only after implementing friction controls that reduce the effective chargeback rate to <1.0% without collapsing the conversion rate.

*Note: This analysis assumes a standard chargeback fee structure; actual impact depends on your specific merchant agreement terms.*
*Immediate action required: Freeze crypto limits for new users until reason codes are analyzed.*
