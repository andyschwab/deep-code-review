# FrostByte Climbing Gyms: Pricing Experiment Impact Analysis
**Period:** March 12 – April 12 | **Scope:** 14 Locations, Iceland

## Executive Summary
The 15% price increase for premium members generated immediate revenue per user but triggered a churn rate (8.4%) more than double the historical baseline (3.9%). While free walk-in traffic increased by 12%, this low-value volume does not mathematically offset the loss of high-LTV premium subscribers in the short term. The hypothesis that price hikes drive exclusivity and curiosity is unsupported by the current data; the primary effect is premium erosion.

## Critical Behavioral Metrics for Isolation

To determine long-term sustainability, the following specific metrics must be isolated immediately:

### 1. Net Revenue Per Location (NRPL) Delta
Do not look at top-line revenue. Calculate the net change per location:
*(Remaining Premium Members × New Price) + (Estimated Walk-in Conversion Value) – (Lost Premium Members × Historical LTV)*.
If this figure is negative, the model destroys value regardless of traffic spikes.

### 2. Churn Cohort Tenure
Segment the 8.4% churn by membership length.
*   **<6 months:** Likely price-sensitive trial users (expected loss).
*   **>2 years:** Core community pillars. Losing these members damages community density, which indirectly reduces retention for remaining users. If long-tenure churn is >4%, the brand equity damage is severe.

### 3. Walk-in to Premium Conversion Rate
The 12% rise in free visitors is a vanity metric unless they convert.
*   Track the percentage of new free visitors who purchase a day pass or upgrade to premium within 14 days.
*   **Benchmark:** If conversion is <5%, the "curiosity" hypothesis is false; you are merely attracting non-paying loiterers who increase operational costs (sauna/ice wall wear-and-tear) without revenue contribution.

### 4. Utilization Density vs. Capacity
Analyze peak-hour capacity usage.
*   Did the exodus of premium members free up enough capacity to improve the experience for remaining payers?
*   Conversely, did the influx of free users congest facilities during peak times, degrading the product for those still paying the higher fee? (Check member satisfaction scores or app complaints during the test window).

### 5. Win-Back Elasticity
Measure the response of the 268 members who cancelled (8.4% of 3,200) to a single "return at old price" offer sent 7 days post-cancellation.
*   **>40% return:** Demand is highly elastic; the price hike was the sole driver of churn. Reverting is safe.
*   **<15% return:** Churn may be correlated with other factors (seasonality, competitor activity), though price remains the primary suspect.

## Strategic Recommendation for Board Decision

**Do not roll out nationwide.** The current data indicates a negative unit economics shift. The revenue gain from the 15% hike on remaining members is mathematically unlikely to cover the Lifetime Value (LTV) loss of nearly 270 high-value customers in a single month, especially given the low monetization potential of the increased free traffic.

**Immediate Action:** Revert pricing for existing members to stop the bleeding of long-tenure cohorts. Consider a phased approach where new sign-ups face the higher price while grandfathering current members, allowing you to test acquisition elasticity without destroying retention.
