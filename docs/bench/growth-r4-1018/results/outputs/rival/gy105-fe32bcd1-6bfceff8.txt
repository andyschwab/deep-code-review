### Diagnostic Plan: Loan Approval Funnel Drop-Off

**1. Quantitative Segmentation Strategy**
To isolate whether the friction stems from the *number* of documents or *file constraints*, segment your funnel data (Nov 5–Present) against the baseline (Oct 1–Nov 4) using these specific dimensions:

*   **Document Count Completion:** Compare conversion rates for users who uploaded 1 ID vs. 2 IDs. If drop-off spikes immediately after the first upload, the *requirement* for a second ID is the blocker.
*   **File Attribute Failure:** Segment by file size and format. Calculate the abandonment rate for uploads >2MB vs. <2MB, and non-PDF/JPG formats vs. standard formats. A high drop-off on large files indicates a technical constraint issue rather than a policy friction issue.
*   **Device & Connection Context:** Segment by device type (Mobile vs. Desktop) and estimated connection speed (if available via latency metrics). Since your market is Southeast Asia, mobile users on 3G/4G may struggle disproportionately with multiple uploads compared to desktop users on Wi-Fi.
*   **Time-to-Abandon Clustering:** Analyze the timestamp delta between "Upload Start" and "Session End."
    *   *< 30 seconds:* Likely immediate rejection of the requirement (policy friction).
    *   *~6 minutes (current avg):* Likely struggle with file selection, resizing, or upload timeouts (technical friction).
*   **Error Event Correlation:** Filter users who triggered specific error events (e.g., `upload_failed`, `file_too_large`, `timeout`) versus those who simply navigated away without error triggers.

**2. Step-by-Step Diagnosis**
1.  **Reconstruct the Micro-Funnel:** Break the "Document Upload" stage into discrete steps: *Prompt Viewed → File Selected → Upload Initiated → Upload Complete (ID 1) → Prompt Viewed (ID 2) → File Selected → Upload Complete (ID 2).* Identify exactly which micro-step holds the 6-minute delay.
2.  **Cohort Comparison:** Run the segments above for the post-launch cohort vs. the pre-launch baseline. Look for statistically significant divergences specifically in the mobile segment and large-file segment.
3.  **Technical Audit:** Cross-reference abandonment timestamps with server logs for upload timeouts or 4xx/5xx errors during the same window.

**3. Required Qualitative Data**
Quantitative data reveals *where* they leave; qualitative data reveals *why*. Immediately deploy:
*   **Exit-Intent Survey:** Trigger a one-question popup at the moment of abandonment on the upload page: "What stopped you from completing your application?" (Options: "Too many documents," "File too big," "Don't have ID ready," "Taking too long").
*   **Session Replay Analysis:** Watch 20–30 recordings of abandoned sessions. Look for "rage clicks," repeated file selection attempts, or users switching tabs (likely to resize photos).
*   **Support Ticket Tagging:** Review customer support chats from Nov 5 onward. Tag and count inquiries related to "upload issues," "file size," or "ID requirements."

**4. Recommended Action Plan Structure**
For your CLO presentation, structure your findings as:
*   **The Evidence:** Present the specific segment (e.g., "Mobile users uploading files >2MB") where drop-off is 80%+.
*   **The Root Cause:** Define if it is policy (2 IDs required) or technical (file limits/mobile UX).
*   **The Fix:** Propose A/B testing a single-ID flow for low-risk segments or implementing client-side image compression before upload.
*   **The Projection:** Estimate the recovery in conversion rate (e.g., "Restoring mobile conversion to 15% yields +X funded loans/day").

Ensure all metrics compare equal observation windows and exclude pending applications still within the processing window to avoid skewing the drop-off rates.
