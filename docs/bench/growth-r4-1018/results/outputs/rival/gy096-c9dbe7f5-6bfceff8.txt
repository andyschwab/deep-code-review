To measure true virality for FilterTurk, you must move beyond a single viral coefficient (K-factor) and implement a multi-touch attribution model that distinguishes between direct invites and passive exposure. Your current K-factor of 0.8 likely underreports growth because it ignores the "passive viewer" funnel—users who see edited photos on Instagram but do not click a tracked link.

**1. Redefine Your Viral Events and Properties**
You need to track two distinct invitation flows with specific properties in your analytics tool (e.g., GA4, Mixpanel):
*   **Direct Invite:** Triggered when a user shares a link via DM.
    *   *Properties:* `share_method` (whatsapp, imessage), `recipient_count`, `unique_link_id`.
    *   *Attribution:* Use deep links with unique tokens. If the recipient installs and opens the app within 7 days, credit the inviter.
*   **Passive Exposure (The Missing Link):** Triggered when a user exports an image with a visible watermark or "Edited with FilterTurk" badge.
    *   *Tracking Mechanism:* Since you cannot track views on Instagram directly, create a branded hashtag (e.g., #FilterTurkCollab) and a unique landing page URL (filterturk.com/insta) mentioned in the watermark or bio.
    *   *Proxy Metric:* Track installs where the `referrer` is "instagram.com" combined with organic search volume for your branded hashtag or app name spikes correlating with high-volume user posts.

**2. Calculate Two Separate Coefficients**
Do not blend these into one number. Calculate:
*   **Direct K-Factor:** (Invites Sent × Conversion Rate of Direct Links). This measures your explicit referral loop.
*   **Passive Virality Rate:** (Organic Installs attributed to Social Referrals / Total Active Users posting to Social). This estimates the "billboard effect" of your watermarked images.

**3. Implement a Cohort-Based Analysis**
Instead of a global average, segment by user cohort (e.g., users who joined in Week 1 vs. Week 4). Track how many *new* users each cohort generates over 30 days. This reveals if newer users are becoming more or less viral over time, indicating if your product changes are working.

**4. Attribution Model for the Marketing Director**
For the March 20 decision, present a "Total Viral Impact" dashboard:
*   **Explicit Growth:** Users from tracked direct links.
*   **Implicit Growth:** Users arriving via organic social search or direct traffic within 24 hours of a spike in user-generated content (UGC) volume.
*   **Recommendation Logic:** If *Direct K-Factor* < 1.0 but *Total Viral Impact* shows strong organic lift, the product mechanics are working, but the "call to action" on shared images is weak. In this case, do not cut organic efforts; instead, optimize the watermark to include a stronger hook (e.g., "Tap link in bio to try this filter"). If both metrics are low, then influencer budget is required to jumpstart the network effect.
