# Fjord Green Beta Success Metrics: Oslo & Bergen Launch

To satisfy investor scrutiny by June 1, success must be defined not by volume (downloads) but by validated behavioral change and retained impact. The following metrics balance your engagement hypothesis with actual carbon reduction, tailored to the Norwegian market's high trust in data and strong cultural emphasis on *dugnad* (collective effort) and tangible results.

### 1. Primary North Star: Verified Weekly Active Reducers (WAR)
**Definition:** The count of unique users who log at least three distinct sustainable actions *and* achieve a minimum verified carbon reduction of 5kg CO2e within a rolling 7-day window.
**Why this matters:** This metric directly validates your core hypothesis while filtering out "vanity logging." In the Norwegian context, where greenwashing skepticism is high, combining frequency with a calculated impact threshold proves that users are not just gamifying the app but actually reducing their footprint. It shifts the narrative from "we have users" to "we are driving measurable climate action."

### 2. Retention Cohort: The "Habit-Formed" User Rate
**Definition:** The percentage of users from the initial Oslo/Bergen cohort who maintain "WAR" status (see above) for 8 consecutive weeks post-onboarding.
**Why this matters:** Your hypothesis suggests 6-month retention follows 3 actions/week. However, waiting 6 months for investor data is too slow. An 8-week leading indicator confirms the habit loop is closing. If users sustain this for two months, the probability of 6-month retention is statistically high. This demonstrates to investors that the product creates lasting behavioral change, not just a novelty spike.

### 3. Impact Efficiency: Average CO2e Reduced Per Active User
**Definition:** The total verified carbon reduction (kg CO2e) divided by the number of Weekly Active Reducers, tracked weekly.
**Why this matters:** This distinguishes between high-frequency/low-impact logging (e.g., turning off lights) and low-frequency/high-impact actions (e.g., switching heating sources or dietary changes). Norwegian households have high emissions related to heating and transport; this metric ensures your app encourages high-value actions rather than just frequent, trivial ones. It answers the investor question: "Is the quality of action improving?"

### 4. Community Multiplier: Household Action Density
**Definition:** The average number of distinct users engaging in "WAR" behaviors within the same household IP range or invited family group.
**Why this matters:** Sustainability in Norway is often a household-level decision, not an individual one. A single user logging actions is good; a whole household adopting the app indicates deep market penetration and higher switching costs. This metric signals to investors that Fjord Green is becoming a household utility rather than a personal gadget, increasing Lifetime Value (LTV).

### 5. Data Trust Score: Verification Completion Rate
**Definition:** The percentage of logged actions where the user provides optional proof (receipt upload, photo, or connected smart meter data) versus self-reported entries.
**Why this matters:** For a seed round, data integrity is critical. High verification rates validate your backend carbon calculations and protect against inflated metrics. It demonstrates to investors that your impact numbers are audit-ready and defensible, a crucial differentiator in the climate-tech sector where accuracy is increasingly regulated.

**Note:** Do not report "Total Actions Logged" or "Total Downloads" as primary success metrics; these are easily inflated and do not correlate with your retention hypothesis.
**Note:** All carbon calculations must explicitly reference Norwegian emission factors (e.g., specific grid mix for electricity vs. heating oil) to maintain local credibility.
