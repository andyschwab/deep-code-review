### Analysis Structure: Push Notification Timing Test

**1. Immediate Recommendation: Do Not Roll Out Globally**
Given the 12% order drop in Surabaya, a global rollout risks significant revenue loss in a key secondary market. The aggregate 4% lift is entirely driven by Jakarta; applying this change universally effectively subsidizes Jakarta's gains with Surabaya's losses. Treat the Surabaya result as a critical guardrail failure, not a statistical anomaly, until proven otherwise.

**2. Diagnostic Framework**
To validate your hypothesis regarding cultural lunch timing and distinguish between "annoyance" (temporary) vs. "misalignment" (structural), structure your analysis as follows:

*   **Hypothesis Validation (Meal Timing):**
    *   **Action:** Cross-reference order timestamps (not just notification clicks) for both cities over the last 90 days.
    *   **Look for:** The natural peak of organic lunch orders. If Surabaya's organic peak is consistently later than 11:00 AM (e.g., 12:00–1:00 PM), the 11:00 AM notification is arriving before hunger triggers, leading to dismissal or opt-outs.
    *   **Metric:** Time-to-order after notification. If Jakarta users order within 15 mins but Surabaya users take 90+ mins (or don't order at all), the timing mismatch is confirmed.

*   **Annoyance vs. Missed Opportunity:**
    *   **Action:** Analyze unsubscribe rates and "notification disabled" events post-test for Surabaya vs. Jakarta.
    *   **Interpretation:** A spike in disables indicates *annoyance* (long-term damage). Flat disable rates but lower conversion suggests *misalignment* (missed window, but no brand harm).
    *   **Lag Effect:** Check if Surabaya users who didn't convert at 11:00 AM ordered later organically. If total daily orders per user in Surabaya remained flat, the test merely shifted timing. If total daily orders dropped, you lost demand.

*   **Segment Granularity:**
    *   Break down Surabaya results by user type (new vs. loyal) and order frequency. Loyal users may have entrenched habits resistant to change, while new users might be more flexible. If loyal users drove the drop, the risk of churn is higher.

**3. Decision Matrix for the Director**

| Scenario | Data Signal | Recommendation |
| :--- | :--- | :--- |
| **Cultural Misalignment** | Surabaya organic peak > 11:30 AM; low unsubscribe rate. | **Segmented Rollout:** Adopt 11:00 AM for Jakarta; retain 12:30 PM (or test 12:00 PM) for Surabaya. |
| **User Annoyance** | Surge in notification disables/uninstalls in Surabaya. | **Revert Immediately:** Halt test in Surabaya. The timing is perceived as spam. |
| **Temporary Anomaly** | No significant difference in organic peaks or disables; drop within confidence interval noise. | **Extend Test:** Run for 2 more weeks to confirm statistical significance before deciding. |

1.  **Pull Organic Baselines:** Generate the hourly order distribution curve for both cities (pre-test).
