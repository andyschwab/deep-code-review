# Experimental Design: 30-Day Trial Extension (Seasonal Adjustment)

## Hypothesis
Because post-holiday travel planning requires a longer consideration window, extending the free trial from 14 to 30 days will increase the paid conversion rate by at least 20% relative to the control, overcoming the typical 15% seasonal dip observed in January and February.

## Methodology: Stratified Randomized Controlled Trial with Seasonal Baseline

To isolate the trial length impact from seasonal noise, we will run a **parallel A/B test** throughout the entire experimental window, rather than comparing this year's results to last year's historical data.

### 1. Experimental Structure
*   **Control Group (A):** 14-day trial (Current Standard).
*   **Variant Group (B):** 30-day trial (New Treatment).
*   **Allocation:** 50/50 split of all new signups.
*   **Duration:** February 1 – February 28 (28 days).
    *   *Note:* Users signing up on Feb 28 in Variant B will remain in the trial through March 30. Analysis must wait until the full 30-day observation window closes for the final cohort (approx. April 1).

### 2. Isolating Seasonal Noise
Historical year-over-year (YoY) comparison is invalid here because macroeconomic factors or product changes may differ from last year. Instead, we rely on **internal concurrent controls**:
*   Since both Group A and Group B experience the exact same external seasonal conditions (weather, post-holiday sentiment, market volatility) simultaneously, the "seasonal noise" cancels out when calculating the **lift** (Variant Rate / Control Rate).
*   **Metric Calculation:** Do not compare Feb 2024 Variant vs. Feb 2023 Control. Compare Feb 2024 Variant vs. Feb 2024 Control.

### 3. Primary & Guardrail Metrics
*   **Primary Metric:** Paid Conversion Rate (Users converting to paid / Total signups) measured at the end of their specific trial window.
*   **Secondary Metric:** Time-to-convert (to see if users convert faster within the extended window).
*   **Guardrail Metrics:**
    *   Support ticket volume per user (does a longer trial increase confusion?).
    *   Server/Infra cost per user (does 30-day access significantly spike costs?).

### 4. Sample Size & Power Considerations
Given the expected 15% baseline drop in conversion rates during this period, the absolute number of conversions will be lower, requiring a larger sample size to detect significance.
*   **Action:** The analytics team must calculate sample size using the **expected seasonal baseline** (e.g., if normal conversion is 10%, use 8.5% for the calculation), not the annual average.
*   **Risk:** If traffic volume is too low to reach significance within the month, the test must run longer or be deemed inconclusive. Do not stop early based on interim results.

### 5. Analysis Plan
*   **Cohort Locking:** Analyze by signup date cohort. Do not mix users who signed up in Jan vs. Feb.
*   **Statistical Test:** Two-proportion z-test comparing conversion rates between A and B.
*   **Segmentation:** Analyze separately by device (mobile vs. desktop) and acquisition channel, as seasonal effects may vary by source.

## Implementation Checklist
1.  **Randomization:** Ensure user assignment is sticky (users keep their assigned trial length even if they return later).
2.  **Tracking:** Tag events with `trial_length_assigned` (14 vs 30) and `trial_start_date`.
3.  **Wait Period:** Define the analysis date as March 31 (for 14-day) and April 1 (for 30-day) to ensure all cohorts have completed their windows.

This design ensures that any difference in conversion is attributed to the trial length, not the season, as both groups suffer the "slump" equally.

*Ensure the analytics team sets up automated alerts if the guardrail metrics spike >10% in the variant group.*
*Do not extend the trial for existing users currently in a 14-day window to avoid data contamination.*
