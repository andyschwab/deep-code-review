# BetStream User Health Framework: Balancing Growth and Compliance

To resolve the tension between your Head of Growth's acquisition goals and your Compliance Officer's risk mandates, you must shift from a single "engagement" metric to a **Dual-Axis Health Score**. This framework evaluates users simultaneously on **Commercial Value** and **Behavioral Safety**, treating them as co-primary indicators rather than trade-offs.

### 1. The Core Tension: Volume vs. Velocity
Your data from the May 10–12 finals illustrates the classic conflict:
*   **Growth Perspective:** A 40% spike in Daily Active Users (DAU) indicates successful acquisition and brand relevance during peak events. The drop in bets per user (4.2 → 2.8) is interpreted as "onboarding friction" or "cautious new user behavior."
*   **Compliance Perspective:** A sudden surge in observers who do not transact can signal "problem gambling precursors" (chasing losses without committing) or "market manipulation observation." Conversely, high velocity (many bets in short time) triggers responsible gambling alerts.

The risk lies in optimizing for **Session Length** alone. In sports betting, a long session with zero bets could mean a user is confused (bad UX) OR a user is exhibiting dangerous "near-miss" fixation (regulatory risk). Optimizing purely for **Bet Volume** ignores users who are betting impulsively beyond their means.

### 2. The Dual-Axis Measurement Model
Replace "Average Bets Per User" with a segmented matrix. Plot every user daily into one of four quadrants based on **Transaction Velocity** (Bets/Session) and **Observation Ratio** (Time on Odds Screen / Time in Checkout).

| Quadrant | Profile | Growth Interpretation | Compliance Interpretation | Action |
| :--- | :--- | :--- | :--- :--- |
| **High Velocity / Low Observation** | The "Whale" or "Chaser" | High LTV, efficient conversion. | **Critical Risk.** Potential loss of control. | Trigger responsible gambling checks; limit stakes. |
| **Low Velocity / High Observation** | The "Cautious Newbie" | Friction in checkout? Education needed. | **Healthy.** Deliberate decision-making. | Nurture with educational content; reduce checkout steps. |
| **High Velocity / High Observation** | The "Strategic Player" | Highly engaged, analyzing markets. | **Monitor.** Could be professional or arbitrage. | Standard monitoring; offer VIP limits. |
| **Low Velocity / Low Observation** | The "Drop-off" | Churn risk; poor UX. | **Low Risk.** | Re-engagement campaigns or offboarding. |

**Application to May 10–12 Data:**
Your drop from 4.2 to 2.8 bets/user likely represents a massive influx of users falling into the **"Cautious Newbie"** quadrant. If their **Observation Ratio** is high (spending time viewing odds) but **Checkout Abandonment** is low (they eventually bet), this confirms your hypothesis of healthy caution. If **Checkout Abandonment** is high, it indicates technical friction or regulatory blockers (e.g., failed identity checks) preventing conversion.

### 3. Redefining "Healthy Engagement"
For the quarterly review, report on **Safe Active Rate** instead of just DAU.
*   **Formula:** `(Users with 1+ Bets AND No Responsible Gambling Triggers) / Total DAU`
*   **Why it works:** This metric satisfies Growth by tracking monetized users and satisfies Compliance by explicitly excluding risky behavior from the "success" count.

**Recommendation:** Do not attempt to raise the average bets per user back to 4.2 immediately. Instead, measure the **Conversion Rate of Observers**. If the 40% DAU spike converts to paid users at a stable rate over 7 days, the lower immediate frequency is a feature of the event audience, not a bug. If conversion stalls, investigate the checkout flow for regulatory friction points (e.g., excessive identity verification steps during high-traffic windows).

*Note: Ensure all event tracking captures "odds view" timestamps separately from "bet placement" to calculate the Observation Ratio accurately.*
*This framework requires no new tools, only a restructuring of how existing event data is aggregated and reported.*
