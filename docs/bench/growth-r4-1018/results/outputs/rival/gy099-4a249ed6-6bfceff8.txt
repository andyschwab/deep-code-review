### Interpretation and Next Steps for Claddagh Crafts A/B Test

**Verdict: Do not extend this test; pivot the hypothesis.**

With 950 monthly visitors, your two-week test likely generated fewer than 700 unique users per variant. To detect a realistic 10% lift on a standard e-commerce click-through rate (approx. 3%), you would need roughly 12,000 users per variant. Your sample size was insufficient to detect anything but a massive, unrealistic 50%+ swing. Therefore, the "statistically insignificant" result is expected noise, not proof that the design failed. However, extending the duration is not the solution; waiting another month for significance on such low traffic delays learning.

**How to Interpret the Null Result**
The flat data indicates that for your specific audience, the *visual* change (hero image + button color) was not a strong enough lever to override existing browsing habits. It does not prove that "artisan stories" lack value, only that this specific execution did not create enough friction or motivation to change behavior. The hypothesis likely failed because it tested a cosmetic variable (layout/color) against a deep psychological driver (emotional resonance).

**Signals to Investigate Before Pivoting Strategy**
Before abandoning the "artisan story" direction entirely, check these qualitative signals in your existing analytics or session recordings:
1.  **Scroll Depth:** Did users on the variant scroll further down to find stories if they weren't in the hero? If yes, the placement was the issue, not the content.
2.  **Time on Page:** Did the variant increase dwell time even if clicks didn't move? Higher engagement without immediate conversion often precedes brand loyalty in handmade goods.
3.  **Mobile vs. Desktop:** With low traffic, a split device audience can mask wins. Check if mobile users (likely higher traffic) reacted differently than desktop users.

**Recommended Next Steps for Your Team**

1.  **Stop the Test Immediately:** Do not run it longer. The opportunity cost of waiting for statistical significance on 950 visitors/month is too high.
2.  **Shift from A/B to Qualitative Validation:** Since you cannot rely on quantitative significance soon, validate the "emotional resonance" hypothesis qualitatively.
    *   Add a one-question poll to the site: "What matters most when buying wool goods: (A) Seeing the maker's story, (B) Price/Shipping, (C) Product variety?"
    *   Conduct 5 user interviews with recent visitors to ask directly if the hero image influenced their trust.
3.  **Redesign the Hypothesis for High Impact:** If you must A/B test again, test a variable with a higher potential lift that requires less traffic to detect.
    *   *New Hypothesis:* "Because trust is the primary barrier for handmade goods, placing a specific 'Meet the Maker' video thumbnail directly on the product card (not just the homepage) will increase add-to-cart rates by 15%."
    *   This moves the value proposition closer to the point of purchase, increasing the likelihood of a measurable effect.
4.  **Prepare for the April 10 Review:** Present the test as a "learning iteration" that proved visual tweaks alone are insufficient for your traffic volume. Recommend a Q2 strategy focused on high-impact content placement validated by direct user feedback rather than broad homepage A/B tests.
