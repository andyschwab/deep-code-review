# Mobile Funnel Dissection Plan: Interface Friction vs. Behavioral Intent

To distinguish between a broken interface and different user intent, structure your analysis around three specific investigative pillars. Do not rely on aggregate conversion rates alone; you must isolate behavior *within* the funnel stages.

### 1. The "Intent-Controlled" Cohort Analysis
Before blaming the UI, verify if mobile users are actually trying to buy. Segment your mobile traffic into two distinct cohorts based on entry behavior:
*   **High-Intent Mobile:** Users who land directly on a Product Detail Page (PDP) or use site search with specific commercial keywords (e.g., "buy," "price," model numbers).
*   **Browse-Intent Mobile:** Users who land on category pages, blog posts, or use broad navigation.

**The Test:** Compare the conversion rates of *High-Intent Mobile* users against *High-Intent Desktop* users.
*   **Finding A:** If High-Intent Mobile still converts at ~1.5% while High-Intent Desktop is at ~4.2%, **intent is not the culprit**. The drop-off is likely friction.
*   **Finding B:** If High-Intent Mobile converts at 3.8% (close to desktop), but your overall mobile average is dragged down by Browse-Intent users, the issue is **audience mix**, not the filter UI.

### 2. The "Filter Engagement" Micro-Funnel
Since the filter launch (Jan 20) correlates with the drop, isolate the exact moment of failure for users who *do* engage with filters. Create a step-by-step funnel specifically for the "Filter Used" segment on mobile:
1.  View Category Page
2.  Open Filter Drawer/Modal
3.  Select ≥1 Filter Option
4.  View Filtered Results
5.  Click Product (Add-to-Cart or PDP view)
6.  Checkout Initiation

**The Diagnostic:**
*   **Drop-off at Step 2 or 3:** Users open the filter but don't select anything or close it immediately. This indicates **usability friction** (hard to tap, confusing layout, slow load).
*   **Drop-off at Step 4 or 5:** Users successfully filter but don't click products. This suggests the **filter logic is too aggressive** (zero results, hiding relevant items) or the resulting grid is poorly rendered on mobile.
*   **Comparison:** Run this same micro-funnel for Desktop. If Mobile drops 40% at "Select Option" while Desktop drops 5%, you have confirmed **interface complexity**.

### 3. Temporal and Device-Specific Context
Analyze *when* and *how* mobile users convert to rule out situational factors.
*   **Time-of-Day/Day-of-Week:** Do mobile conversions spike during commute hours (high distraction) vs. evening (high focus)? If conversion is low during "focus times," it supports the UI friction hypothesis.
*   **Connection Speed:** Correlate filter usage with page load times. If the filter script heavy on mobile data, users may abandon due to latency, not cognitive overload.
*   **Return Visitor Rate:** Check if mobile users who *fail* to convert return later on Desktop to buy. If 30% of "mobile abandoners" convert on desktop within 48 hours, the mobile experience is a **research tool**, not a broken store.

### Recommended Output for Stakeholders
Present your findings in a "Friction vs. Intent" matrix:
*   **Quadrant 1 (High Friction, Low Intent):** Fix UI *and* adjust marketing targeting.
*   **Quadrant 2 (High Friction, High Intent):** **Critical Priority.** The UI is actively blocking sales from ready buyers. Immediate UX remediation required on the filter component.
*   **Quadrant 3 (Low Friction, Low Intent):** Accept the metric; optimize for engagement/newsletter signup instead of immediate purchase.
*   **Quadrant 4 (Low Friction, High Intent):** Status quo; investigate other bottlenecks (e.g., checkout form).

**Immediate Action:** Implement event tracking on the specific filter interactions (open, select, apply, clear) if not already granular enough to support Step 2 above.

*Note: Ensure your "filter usage" definition excludes accidental taps on mobile, which can skew the "engaged" population with low-intent users.*
