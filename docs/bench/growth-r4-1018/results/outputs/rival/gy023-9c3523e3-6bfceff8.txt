**Interpretation for David: The "Passive Retention" Paradox**

The data indicates a critical decoupling of **habit formation** from **value delivery**. While a 40% return rate suggests successful top-of-funnel engagement (likely driven by the new social feed), the 77% collapse in weekly workouts (3.5 to 0.8) signals that these users are transitioning from "active participants" to "passive observers."

**Behavioral Patterns to Investigate Immediately**

To determine if this retention is sustainable or a leading indicator of churn, we must segment user behavior into three specific patterns:

1.  **The "Scroll-Only" Cohort:** Isolate users who trigger `app_open` and `feed_view` events but have zero `workout_started` events for 14+ consecutive days. If this group comprises >60% of your returning users, the social feature is cannibalizing core utility. These users are deriving value from content consumption, not fitness outcomes, making them highly vulnerable to competitors with better content algorithms.
2.  **The "Friction" Cohort:** Analyze users who open the app, view the home screen, and exit within 10 seconds without scrolling or starting a workout. A spike here suggests the social feed has increased UI complexity, obscuring the primary "Start Workout" CTA. This supports the hypothesis that users want to work out but cannot easily find the tool.
3.  **The "Hybrid" Validator:** Identify the minority (<10%) who maintain high workout counts *and* high social usage. If their retention is significantly higher than "workout-only" users, social features are a valid retention lever. If their workout frequency also trends downward over time, social features are a distraction, not a complement.

**Strategic Implications**

*   **If Pattern 1 dominates:** Retention without action is **not sustainable** for a fitness app. Users who do not achieve fitness outcomes (tracked workouts) will eventually churn when novelty fades or when they realize they aren't progressing. Do not pivot the roadmap to social; instead, treat social as a re-engagement channel to drive users back to tracking.
*   **If Pattern 2 dominates:** The issue is **onboarding and UX**, not feature value. The roadmap should prioritize simplifying the path to the first workout action, potentially gating social features until a user completes their first tracked session.
*   **Recommendation:** Do not assume social features justify the roadmap pivot yet. The drop from 3.5 to 0.8 workouts suggests the core value proposition is eroding. Prioritize an experiment where the "Start Workout" button is made immutable and prominent for returning users, measuring if workout completion rates recover without sacrificing return rates.

**Next Step:** Request engineering to run a cohort analysis comparing `feed_view_count` vs. `workout_completed_count` for January signups over the last 8 weeks to quantify the correlation.

*Note: This analysis assumes event tracking is accurately distinguishing between screen views and active tool usage.*
