### Executive Recommendation: Pause Expansion, Diagnose Before Cutting Spend

Do not expand to Aarhus yet, and do not pause marketing entirely. The data indicates a **composition shift**, not necessarily a failure of product-market fit. Your 68% retention (vs. 55% target) proves the core value proposition holds; however, the 25% drop in Average Order Value (AOV) suggests subscribers are actively modifying their usage patterns rather than abandoning the service. Expanding now scales a revenue model that is currently leaking value per user. Pausing marketing entirely risks losing the critical mass needed to cover your fixed cold-chain logistics costs.

**Immediate Action:** Freeze expansion capital. Reallocate resources to a 10-day "Behavioral Diagnostic" sprint to isolate the cause of the AOV drop before the board meeting.

### How to Isolate the Signal: The "Box State" Matrix

To distinguish between downsizing (optimization) and skipping (churn signal), you must stop looking at aggregate revenue and segment your active cohort into four mutually exclusive states based on weekly behavior. Calculate the percentage of your 340 retained subscribers (68% of 500) falling into these buckets for Weeks 1–6:

1.  **Full Continuity:** Subscribers ordering the same box size every week.
2.  **Active Downsizers:** Subscribers who remain active every week but switched from a larger box to a smaller one (e.g., Family to Single).
3.  **Skip-Pattern Users:** Subscribers who order intermittently (e.g., Week 1, skip Week 2, order Week 3).
4.  **Soft Churn:** Subscribers who have not ordered in 14+ days but haven't formally cancelled.

**Interpretation Logic:**
*   **If Bucket 2 (Downsizers) > 60% of the variance:** This is a **pricing/packaging mismatch**, not a churn signal. Customers love the reindeer meat but find the initial commitment too large for their consumption rate. *Recommendation:* Introduce a "Summer Saver" smaller box tier immediately. Retention is strong; you just need to right-size the product.
*   **If Bucket 3 (Skippers) > 60% of the variance:** This is a **frequency/logistics issue**. Copenhagen urbanites may lack freezer space or are treating this as an occasional luxury rather than a staple. *Recommendation:* Test a "bi-weekly subscription" plan. Do not expand to Aarhus until you stabilize the delivery cadence, as irregular drops increase your cold-chain cost per unit.
*   **If Bucket 4 (Soft Churn) is growing:** Only then is this a true retention failure. Given your 68% hard retention, this is unlikely to be the primary driver.

### The Logistics Reality Check

Your cold-chain model makes "skipping" more dangerous than "downsizing." A downsizer still generates a delivery route density that helps cover fixed costs. A skipper creates gaps in route density, spiking your cost-per-delivery.

**Board Narrative:**
Present the 68% retention as validation of the *product* (reindeer meat demand is real). Frame the AOV drop as a *packaging optimization opportunity*. Request approval to launch a "Flexible Summer Plan" (smaller boxes or bi-weekly delivery) for the existing Copenhagen cohort. Commit to expanding to Aarhus only after this new plan stabilizes the blended AOV above 350 DKK for two consecutive weeks.

**Note:** Ensure your dashboard tracks "active subscription status" separately from "weekly order volume" to avoid conflating skipped weeks with cancellations.
**Note:** Use the 10-day diagnostic to survey the "Active Downsizers" specifically; their feedback will dictate the new packaging tiers.
