# Evaluation Framework: Gamification Feature Expansion

## Executive Summary
The experiment presents a classic "hollow engagement" risk: a significant lift in usage time (+22 mins/day) coupled with degraded technical performance (+15% support tickets). The core decision is not whether users are playing more, but whether this additional playtime is sustainable and profitable given the infrastructure strain.

## 1. Diagnose the Engagement Quality
Before projecting revenue, determine the nature of the increased time spent.
*   **Session Structure:** Did the 22-minute increase come from longer individual sessions or more frequent logins? Longer sessions on older devices correlate strongly with the reported battery drain.
*   **Retention Cohorts:** Segment the 200,000 players by device age. If the engagement lift is concentrated on newer devices while older devices show high crash rates, the feature is fragmenting the user base.
*   **Burnout Signals:** Analyze Day-7 and Day-14 retention for the test group versus control. If retention drops despite higher daily time, the gamification is accelerating burnout rather than building habit.

## 2. Quantify the Operational Cost
Translate the 15% rise in support tickets into a tangible cost to offset against potential gains.
*   **Ticket Volume vs. User Base:** Calculate the absolute number of additional tickets. A 15% rise on a low baseline may be manageable; a rise on a high baseline could overwhelm support.
*   **Device Correlation:** Map crash reports specifically to the "daily streak" and "leaderboard" triggers. Confirm if the instability is caused by server load (as hypothesized) or client-side rendering issues on legacy hardware.
*   **Churn Attribution:** Cross-reference users who submitted tickets with those who churned within 48 hours. If ticket submission predicts immediate churn, the feature is actively driving users away.

## 3. Resolve the LTV Unknown
Since Lifetime Value (LTV) cannot be observed in a three-week window, use proxy metrics to model the outcome.
*   **Monetization Density:** Did revenue per daily active user (ARPDAU) increase, decrease, or stay flat? If time spent rose 22 minutes but ARPDAU remained static, the additional engagement is non-monetizable "filler" content.
*   **Ad Load Capacity:** If the game is ad-supported, did the extra time allow for more ad impressions without depressing fill rates or eCPM?
*   **Subscription Conversion:** Did the streaks/leaderboards drive any uptick in premium conversions? If not, the features may only be retaining low-value users.

## Recommendation Structure for Leadership
Present the findings to the Head of Growth and Game Design Director using a "Conditional Expansion" framework rather than a binary Go/No-Go:

1.  **If** retention curves hold steady and ARPDAU increases: **Expand**, but mandate a technical sprint to optimize battery usage for older devices first.
2.  **If** retention drops or ARPDAU is flat: **Pause Expansion**. The feature is cannibalizing long-term health for short-term vanity metrics.
3.  **If** crashes are isolated to devices >3 years old: **Expand with Device Gating**. Enable features only for devices meeting specific performance thresholds, preserving the experience for the majority while protecting the legacy segment.

## Critical Missing Data
To finalize the decision, you must isolate the churn rate of the "crashed" cohort versus the "engaged" cohort. Without knowing if the frustrated users are the same ones playing longer, you cannot determine if the feature is net-positive.
