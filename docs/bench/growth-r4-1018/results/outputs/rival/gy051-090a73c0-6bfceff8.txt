### Executive Summary: A/B Test Divergence Analysis

**Recommendation:** Do not roll out the simplified form globally. Implement a **segmented experience**: deploy the simplified variant exclusively to **new users**, while retaining the original detailed form for **returning logistics managers**.

**Rationale:** The data indicates a fundamental difference in user intent and task complexity. New users (18.4% conversion on simplified) likely face friction from cognitive overload; removing fields reduces anxiety and accelerates the initial commitment. Conversely, returning managers (9.1% conversion on simplified) presumably require the "extra fields" you hypothesized for complex operational needs, such as multi-stop routing, specific temperature zoning, or compliance documentation. For this segment, "simplification" equates to "incomplete functionality," forcing them to abandon the flow or contact support to complete complex bookings, thereby depressing conversion.

### Immediate Next Steps: Metrics to Track

To validate this segmentation strategy before full engineering implementation, track the following specific metrics over a 14-day observation window:

1.  **Task Completion Rate by User Type:**
    *   *Definition:* Percentage of users who successfully submit a booking *and* receive a confirmation ID.
    *   *Segmentation:* Split strictly by `user_type` (New vs. Returning) and `form_variant`.
    *   *Goal:* Confirm that returning users on the original form complete complex bookings at a higher rate than on the simplified version.

2.  **Form Abandonment Point Heatmap:**
    *   *Definition:* The specific field where users drop off.
    *   *Segmentation:* Returning users on the *simplified* variant.
    *   *Goal:* Identify if returning users are abandoning because required fields for their specific use case (e.g., "Secondary Drop-off Location") are missing entirely, causing confusion before submission.

3.  **Support Ticket Volume per 100 Bookings:**
    *   *Definition:* Number of support inquiries related to "booking errors" or "missing options" normalized by total booking attempts.
    *   *Segmentation:* Returning users on the simplified variant.
    *   *Goal:* Quantify the operational cost of the simplified form. If this spikes, the form is failing to capture necessary logistics data, shifting the burden to your operations team.

4.  **Average Booking Complexity Score:**
    *   *Definition:* A derived metric counting the number of distinct stops, temperature zones, or special handling flags per successful booking.
    *   *Segmentation:* Compare averages between New and Returning users.
    *   *Goal:* Statistically prove that returning users attempt significantly more complex transactions that the simplified form cannot accommodate.

5.  **Time-to-Complete (Median):**
    *   *Definition:* Time elapsed from landing on the form to successful submission.
    *   *Segmentation:* Returning users on Original vs. Simplified.
    *   *Goal:* Ensure the original form isn't unnecessarily slowing down experts. If experts are faster on the original form despite more fields, it confirms the extra fields are necessary tools, not friction.
