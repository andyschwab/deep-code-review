**Executive Summary**
The A/B test (Oct 5–Nov 2) presents a classic volume-vs-engagement trade-off. Group A (4 emails/week) generated **€4,200 more revenue** than Group B (2 emails/week), despite Group B achieving a **9% higher open rate**. While reduced frequency successfully improved engagement metrics (suggesting better sender reputation), the four-week window was insufficient to determine if Group B's lower revenue is a permanent loss or a delayed conversion cycle.

**Interpretation of Conflicting Signals**
Your assumption that reduced frequency hurts top-of-funnel awareness is likely correct for the short term. With only two touchpoints weekly, Group B had fewer opportunities to convert within the 28-day window. The higher open rate in Group B indicates that when we *do* email them, they are more attentive, but the total volume of conversions suffered.

However, the risk of declaring Group A the definitive winner prematurely is high. Artisanal baking kits are considered goods with a considered purchase cycle. If Group B customers are simply delaying purchases rather than abandoning them, switching to the high-frequency model (Group A) for Q4 could inflate short-term revenue while increasing unsubscribe rates and spam complaints post-holiday, damaging our 2025 deliverability.

**Critical Missing Metrics**
Before finalizing the Q4 budget, we must analyze these three specific data points immediately:
1.  **Unsubscribe & Spam Complaint Rates:** If Group A's unsubscribe rate is significantly higher (e.g., >0.5% difference), the €4,200 gain is "expensive revenue" that will cost more to replace in Q1 via acquisition spend.
2.  **Repeat Purchase Rate:** Did Group A drive more *new* customer conversions, or did they simply cannibalize sales by emailing existing buyers too aggressively?
3.  **7-Day Post-Test Conversion:** We need to track both groups for one additional week without new emails. If Group B's revenue spikes as delayed purchases materialize, the lifetime value (LTV) gap may narrow significantly.

**Recommendation for Q4 Budget**
Do not fully commit the Q4 budget to a single frequency strategy yet.
*   **Immediate Action:** Adopt a **hybrid approach** for the holiday season. Segment the list: apply Group A's frequency (4x/week) only to customers who have purchased in the last 90 days (high intent), and maintain Group B's frequency (2x/week) for older segments to protect domain reputation during high-volume holiday traffic.
*   **Budget Allocation:** Reserve 15% of the ESP budget for potential reputation recovery tools or list cleaning services in Q1, anticipating that the high-frequency push may impact deliverability.
*   **Final Decision:** Delay the permanent frequency policy decision until January 15, once we have analyzed the post-holiday churn and LTV of both cohorts.

The €4,200 gain is real, but not necessarily sustainable if it comes at the cost of long-term list health.
