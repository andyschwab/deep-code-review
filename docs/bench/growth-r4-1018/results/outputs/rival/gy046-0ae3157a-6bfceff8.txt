**Executive Summary: Wiki Search Algorithm Rollout Recommendation**

**Decision:** Do not proceed with a company-wide rollout by August 30th. The current data presents a critical contradiction between behavioral efficiency and operational success that poses a high risk to engineering productivity if scaled.

**Analysis of Mixed Signals**
The 15% increase in "successful searches" (clicks without immediate bounce) is a vanity metric in this specific context. While it indicates users are clicking results they don't immediately reject, it does not confirm they found the *correct* legacy documentation. The algorithm likely favors newer, syntactically similar documents that satisfy the click-but-not-resolve pattern.

The 10% rise in "cannot find document" support tickets is the leading indicator of failure. In an internal knowledge base, legacy documentation often contains critical compliance, security, or architectural constraints that newer docs omit. If the new algorithm buries these assets, engineers may click irrelevant newer docs (inflating the success rate) before eventually giving up and filing a ticket. The behavioral data measures engagement; the support tickets measure task completion. You must trust the self-reported failure (tickets) over the proxy metric (clicks) when the cost of error involves critical infrastructure knowledge.

**Required Qualitative Feedback & Next Steps**
To resolve this contradiction before a final decision, you must validate the source of the support tickets immediately. Execute the following three actions:

1.  **Ticket Content Audit:** Manually review the 10% increase in support tickets. Categorize them by whether the user explicitly requested a legacy document version or described a specific outdated process. If >50% cite specific legacy topics, the algorithm is actively suppressing critical info.
2.  **Failed Session Sampling:** Isolate the session logs of users who filed tickets. Analyze their search queries and click paths. Did they click a top result (the "successful" search) and still fail? This confirms the "false positive" click theory.
3.  **Targeted User Interviews:** Interview 10 engineers who filed tickets. Ask specifically: "Did you see a result that looked relevant but lacked the specific legacy detail you needed?"

**Recommendation**
Pause the rollout. Refine the algorithm to boost "critical legacy" tags or adjust recency weighting. Re-run a targeted test only on queries known to require legacy docs. A company-wide rollout based on current metrics risks a significant drop in engineering velocity due to information retrieval failures.

*Note: The August 30th deadline allows approximately two weeks for this qualitative audit and a micro-test refinement.*
*Ensure the "success" definition is updated to include "time-to-resolution" or "ticket avoidance" rather than just click retention.*
