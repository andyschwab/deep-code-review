### Proposed Activation Framework for ScholarWrite

**Hypothesis:** For academic researchers using ScholarWrite, "depth of engagement" (time spent editing, citation integration, section structuring) is a stronger predictor of long-term retention and subscription renewal than "frequency of use" (number of documents generated).

**Recommended North Star Metric:** **"Deep Session Completion"**
Define a user as activated when they complete **one** session lasting >20 minutes that includes at least **two** of the following high-value actions:
1.  Inserting or formatting citations.
2.  Using the AI to rewrite or expand a specific section (not just generating a new doc).
3.  Exporting or saving a document with >1,000 words.

This shifts the focus from "trying the tool" to "doing real work" with the tool, aligning with the long-form nature of academic writing.

#### Testing Framework: Cohort-Based Activation Validation

To validate this new definition against your current "3 documents in 7 days" metric, execute the following four-step analysis using your existing historical data (specifically the February 1st cohort and subsequent months):

**1. Retrospective Correlation Analysis**
Segment your historical users into four groups based on their first-week behavior:
*   **Group A (Current Standard):** 3+ docs, <20 mins total editing time.
*   **Group B (Deep Engagers):** 1-2 docs, >30 mins total editing time.
*   **Group C (Hybrid):** 3+ docs AND >30 mins editing.
*   **Group D (Low Activity):** <3 docs AND <20 mins editing.

Calculate the **Day-30 Retention Rate** and **Trial-to-Paid Conversion Rate** for each group. If your hypothesis holds, Group B and C should show significantly higher conversion rates than Group A, despite lower document counts. This quantifies the "activation gap" in your current model.

**2. Define the "Magic Moment" Threshold**
Within the "Deep Engagers" group, perform a threshold analysis to pinpoint the exact tipping point. Plot editing time (x-axis) against conversion rate (y-axis). Look for the inflection point where the conversion curve steepens.
*   *Example Finding:* You may discover that users who spend **22 minutes** editing and insert **one citation** convert at 45%, whereas those at 18 minutes convert at only 12%. This specific combination becomes your precise activation criteria.

**3. Prospective A/B Test on Onboarding Triggers**
Once the new criteria are statistically validated, run an operational test with your Customer Success team:
*   **Control Group:** CS intervenes when a user hits "3 documents."
*   **Test Group:** CS intervenes when a user hits "1 Deep Session" (as defined above) OR if they fail to hit it by Day 4.
*   **Measurement:** Compare the Day-60 retention and support ticket volume between groups. The goal is to see if intervening based on *depth* prevents churn more effectively than intervening based on *frequency*.

**4. Implementation & Monitoring**
Update your analytics dashboard to track "Activated Users" using the new definition alongside the old one for a transition period. Create an automated alert for the CS team when a trial user completes a "Deep Session" (success signal) or reaches Day 5 without one (risk signal).

By shifting your definition to reflect the actual workflow of a researcher (deep, iterative editing) rather than a generic SaaS metric (document count), you will align your success metrics with genuine user value, allowing for more timely and relevant customer support interventions.

*Note: Ensure your event tracking captures "session duration" and specific feature interactions (like citation clicks) accurately; if these events are not currently logged, engineering must instrument them before running the retrospective analysis.*
