4.  **Cancellation Reason Code Distribution**: Segment the 18% cancellation rate by stated reason (e.g., "driver no-show," "rider safety concern," "price disagreement"). If >50% cite safety during rain, this is a policy issue, not a pricing one.

### Data Segmentation Strategy

To isolate weather impacts from driver availability for Ravi's operational decision, segment your existing dataset using the following matrix:

**Step 1: Weather Overlay**
Tag every trip request with local weather data for that specific hour in Ikeja and Yaba. Create two buckets:
*   **Dry Conditions**: No precipitation recorded.
*   **Wet Conditions**: Any precipitation recorded.

**Step 2: Time-of-Day Cross-Section**
Within each weather bucket, split data into:
*   **Rush Hour**: 7–9 AM and 5–7 PM.
*   **Off-Peak**: All other operating hours.

**Step 3: Driver Supply Check**
For each of the four resulting segments (Dry/Rush, Dry/Off-Peak, Wet/Rush, Wet/Off-Peak), calculate:
*   **Request-to-Driver Ratio**: Number of ride requests divided by active drivers online.
*   **Cancellation Source**: Percentage of cancellations initiated by the *driver* vs. the *rider*.

**Interpretation Guide for Ravi:**
*   **Hire More Drivers If**: The "Wet/Rush" segment shows a high Request-to-Driver ratio but low driver-initiated cancellations. This indicates unmet demand due to supply shortage.
*   **Pause Expansion If**: The "Wet/Rush" segment shows high *rider*-initiated cancellations citing safety, regardless of driver availability. This suggests the product is unsafe for Lagos rain conditions without further iteration (e.g., rain gear mandates, speed limits).
*   **Pricing Issue If**: Cancellations spike in "Dry/Off-Peak" segments with low request volumes, suggesting the 850 Naira price point is too high for non-urgent trips.
