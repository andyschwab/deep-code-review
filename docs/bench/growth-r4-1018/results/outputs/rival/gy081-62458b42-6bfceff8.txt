**Executive Summary: Checkout Experiment (March 10–24)**

**Recommendation:** Do not roll out the variant permanently. Immediately halt the experiment and initiate a "Trust Impact Assessment" before considering any modified re-test.

**Reasoning:**
While the variant delivered a statistically significant lift in conversion rate (+38%, from 4.2% to 5.8%) and estimated daily revenue (+$12k), the concurrent 15% spike in refund requests indicates a critical failure in user comprehension and consent. This pattern suggests the use of "dark patterns"—specifically aggressive countdown timers (inducing false urgency) and pre-selected add-ons (exploiting inertia)—which drove short-term transactions at the expense of customer intent.

For a risk-averse organization, the financial gain is illusory when netted against the downstream costs:
1.  **Unit Economics:** The 15% increase in refunds directly erodes the $12k revenue gain. If the average order value of refunded items exceeds the margin on the incremental sales, the variant is already net-negative.
2.  **Long-Term Liability:** High refund rates correlated with "confusion" are a leading indicator of chargebacks and regulatory scrutiny (e.g., FTC guidelines on negative option billing). The cost of defending against regulatory action or reputational damage far outweighs two weeks of inflated revenue.
3.  **Brand Equity:** Converting users who immediately regret their purchase destroys lifetime value (LTV). These users are unlikely to return and may actively dissuade others, creating a churn debt that will manifest in Q3/Q4.

**Decision Framework for the Ethics Board:**
To defend this decision, we propose evaluating the experiment against three criteria rather than conversion rate alone:

*   **Intent Verification:** Did the user knowingly purchase the add-on? Pre-selected boxes fail this test by design. A sustainable checkout requires explicit opt-in.
*   **Net Revenue Retention:** Does the revenue hold after 30 days? We must model the P&L including the observed refund rate. If (Gross Revenue - Refunds) < Control Group Net Revenue, the test is a failure regardless of top-line spikes.
*   **Support Load Ratio:** Did the increase in support tickets regarding "confusion" exceed the threshold of operational efficiency? A 15% spike in refunds implies a much larger volume of frustrated users who did not formally request a refund but will not return.

**Next Steps:**
1.  Revert to control immediately to stop brand erosion.
2.  Calculate the net revenue impact after accounting for the specific refund volume observed.
3.  Design a follow-up hypothesis that tests urgency or bundling *without* deceptive UI elements (e.g., explicit opt-in bundles with clear value props) to determine if revenue lift can be achieved ethically.

The pressure to hit Q2 targets should not justify tactics that compromise Q3 stability and legal standing.
