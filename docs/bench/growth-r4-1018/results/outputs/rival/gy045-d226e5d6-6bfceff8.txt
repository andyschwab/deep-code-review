**Recommendation: Optimize for Gross Merchandise Value (GMV)**

For SecondHand Stockholm's 2025 strategy, you must prioritize **Gross Merchandise Value (GMV)** over total transaction volume. While volume grew 30% (50k to 65k units), the 15.5% drop in Average Order Value (450 SEK to 380 SEK) indicates a degradation in marketplace quality that threatens long-term liquidity.

**The Argument for Investors**

1.  **Seller Liquidity is the Bottleneck:** In a two-sided marketplace, supply constrains growth more than demand. Your current trajectory suggests you are attracting low-value inventory or forcing price compression. If sellers cannot achieve fair market value, they will churn. Without data on seller churn yet, GMV acts as your leading indicator: stable or growing GMV per active seller proves sellers are succeeding. Optimizing for volume alone incentivizes cheap items that clog the feed without generating sufficient revenue to cover operational costs or satisfy sellers.
2.  **Revenue Alignment:** Take rates are typically percentage-based. A 65,000-unit month at 380 SEK yields 24.7M SEK in GMV. A hypothetical 55,000-unit month at 450 SEK yields 24.75M SEK in GMV—but likely with lower support costs and higher seller satisfaction. Volume growth without GMV growth is "vanity scaling"; it increases operational load without increasing top-line revenue potential.
3.  **Seasonal Resilience:** Sweden's fashion market is heavily influenced by Q4 winter coat sales, which naturally carry higher price points. If you optimize for volume, your algorithm may suppress these high-value, lower-frequency items in favor of cheap summer accessories to hit unit targets, causing you to miss the critical Q4 revenue spike. GMV alignment ensures your platform highlights high-value seasonal inventory when it matters most.
4.  **Market Saturation Signal:** In a saturated Nordic market, acquiring new buyers is expensive. Retaining high-value sellers is cheaper and more sustainable. A focus on GMV signals to investors that you are building a premium, liquid marketplace rather than a discount bin. It forces product decisions that improve match quality for expensive items, which drives trust and repeat usage.

**Implementation Guardrails**
Do not abandon volume entirely; treat it as a guardrail metric. Set a minimum threshold for monthly transactions to ensure liquidity, but make GMV the primary success metric for bonuses, roadmap prioritization, and investor reporting. Until you have seller churn data, monitor the *ratio* of GMV to Active Sellers weekly. If this ratio drops while volume rises, your strategy is failing.

**Note:** Frame the AOV drop not as a failure, but as a strategic pivot point where you chose to validate demand elasticity, and now must pivot to value retention.
**Note:** Explicitly state in the plan that Q4 GMV targets will be weighted 2x against Q2/Q3 to account for seasonal coat sales.
