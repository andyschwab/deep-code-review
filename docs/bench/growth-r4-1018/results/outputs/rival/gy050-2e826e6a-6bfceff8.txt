# Diagnostic Plan: MFA Setup Drop-Off Analysis

**Objective:** Distinguish between device access friction and technical SMS delivery failures to inform CISO and CS VP decisions by April 25th.

## 1. Immediate Data Extraction (Technical Logs)
Deploy these queries against your identity provider and SMS gateway logs for the April 10–12 window:

*   **SMS Delivery Status Codes:** Filter for non-delivered messages. Categorize failures by:
    *   *Carrier Rejections* (invalid number, blocked short code) → Indicates data quality or carrier blocking.
    *   *Timeouts/Network Errors* → Correlate with geographic regions (match phone number country codes to your known client locations). If >15% of failures in specific regions match your "spotty coverage" hypothesis, this is a technical bottleneck.
*   **Session Abandonment Points:** Track the exact step where users drop off:
    *   *Drop-off before entering phone number:* Suggests policy confusion or lack of device access awareness.
    *   *Drop-off after requesting code but before entry:* Strong indicator of SMS non-receipt (technical) or inability to retrieve code (device access).
    *   *Drop-off after multiple failed code entries:* Indicates usability issues or time-sync problems, not necessarily device access.
*   **Time-to-Complete Analysis:** Calculate the median time between "code requested" and "code entered."
    *   *Normal:* <2 minutes.
    *   *Delayed:* >5 minutes suggests users are waiting for access to personal devices (e.g., waiting until lunch break or commute).

## 2. Rapid User Feedback Mechanisms
Implement these low-friction collection methods immediately:

*   **Exit-Intent Micro-Survey:** Trigger a single-question popup when users abandon the MFA setup flow:
    *   *"What's preventing you from completing MFA setup right now?"*
    *   Options: "No access to personal phone," "Didn't receive code," "Too complicated," "Will do later."
    *   Keep it optional to avoid adding friction.
*   **Targeted Admin Outreach:** Customer Success should contact 20–30 admins from the 700 who dropped off, prioritizing:
    *   10 from regions with known coverage issues.
    *   10 from industries with strict device policies (finance, healthcare, government).
    *   10 random samples.
    *   Ask: "Did you receive the SMS? If not, did you try alternative methods? Do you have access to your personal mobile during work hours?"
*   **Support Ticket Tagging:** Create a temporary tag "MFA-Setup-Issue" for all incoming tickets. Analyze volume and content for patterns (e.g., "code not arriving" vs. "can't use personal phone at work").

## 3. Segmentation Analysis
Cross-reference drop-off rates by:
*   **Geography:** Compare completion rates across countries/regions. If regions with poor coverage show significantly higher drop-off (>45% vs. 30% baseline), technical delivery is a primary driver.
*   **Industry Vertical:** If finance/healthcare/government show higher drop-off than tech/retail, device access policies are likely the culprit.
*   **Company Size:** Smaller companies may have more flexible device policies than large enterprises.

## 4. Success Metrics for Diagnosis
By April 24th, you should have:
*   **Failure Attribution:** % of drop-offs caused by SMS non-delivery vs. user abandonment.
*   **Geographic Correlation:** Clear map of whether coverage issues align with drop-off spikes.
*   **Device Access Signal:** Qualitative data from surveys/outreach on personal device availability.
*   **Recommendation:** Based on findings, propose either (a) alternative MFA methods (email, authenticator app, hardware tokens) for device-restricted users, or (b) SMS provider optimization for affected regions.

---
*Present findings with clear visualizations: a funnel showing drop-off points, a geographic heat map of failures, and direct quotes from user outreach.*
*If SMS delivery rates are >95% but drop-off remains high, the issue is almost certainly device access policy, not technical failure.*
