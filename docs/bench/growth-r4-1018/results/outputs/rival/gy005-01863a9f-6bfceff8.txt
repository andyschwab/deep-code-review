To determine the net financial impact, you must calculate the **Revenue Per Visitor (RPV)** for both the control and variant groups, then layer in your margin costs. Do not rely on conversion rate or Average Order Value (AOV) in isolation, as they are currently telling conflicting stories.

**Step 1: Calculate Revenue Per Visitor (RPV)**
RPV is the definitive metric for this trade-off because it combines traffic, conversion, and value into a single efficiency number.
*   **Formula:** `Conversion Rate × Average Order Value = RPV`
*   **Control Group:** If your baseline conversion was $X$% and AOV was $55, your Control RPV was $0.X \times 55$.
*   **Variant Group:** Your new conversion is $1.08X$% (an 8% lift) and AOV is $48 (a 12% drop). Your Variant RPV is $1.08X \times 48$.

**The Verdict Math:**
Multiply the factors: $1.08 \text{ (conversion lift)} \times 0.88 \text{ (remaining AOV after 12% drop)} = 0.9504$.
Your variant RPV is approximately **95%** of your control RPV. This indicates a **~5% decrease in revenue efficiency per visitor**. Unless the bundle has significantly lower fulfillment costs than individual books, this experiment likely reduced total top-line revenue.

**Step 2: Factor in Margins for Profit**
Since your CFO cares about the bottom line, you must adjust for Cost of Goods Sold (COGS).
*   **Formula:** `(RPV × Gross Margin %) - Fixed Costs per Visitor = Profit Per Visitor`
*   If the bundle consists of existing inventory with no extra cost, the margin percentage might be similar, meaning the 5% revenue drop translates directly to a 5% profit drop.
*   However, if the bundle clears slow-moving stock or reduces shipping costs (one box vs. two), the margin % on the $48 bundle could be higher than on the $55 mixed cart. You need the specific margin % for the bundle vs. the control average to finalize the profit calculation.

**Required Data Breakdowns**
To explain the "why" behind the numbers and address the cannibalization fear, request these three specific segments:
1.  **Cannibalization Rate:** What percentage of bundle buyers previously purchased 2+ full-price books in a single session? This quantifies exactly how much high-value behavior was displaced.
2.  **New Customer Acquisition:** Did the bundle attract entirely new buyers who otherwise wouldn't have purchased? If 100% of the conversion lift came from new customers, the cannibalization fear is mitigated, even if RPV is slightly lower.
3.  **Unit Economics:** Compare the COGS and shipping cost per unit for the bundle vs. the average control order. If the bundle saves $4 in shipping, the profit impact might be neutral despite the revenue drop.

**LTV vs. Immediate Revenue**
For a CFO evaluating a permanent pricing change for the current fiscal year, prioritize **immediate contribution margin**. Lifetime Value (LTV) is too uncertain for a pricing verdict unless you have historical data proving that bundle buyers retain at a significantly higher rate than single-book buyers. Without that proof, assume LTV is neutral. If the experiment loses money on day one, you cannot justify it based on speculative future retention.

**Recommendation:** Based on the provided figures, the math suggests a net negative impact on revenue efficiency (~5% drop).
