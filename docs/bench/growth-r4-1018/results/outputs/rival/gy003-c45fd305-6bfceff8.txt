**Recommendation: Prioritize the Restaurant Selection-to-Menu Funnel Stage**

Allocate engineering resources immediately to optimize the transition between restaurant selection and menu viewing, rather than the checkout flow.

**Rationale Based on Volume Impact**
With 5,000 daily orders representing the final 40% of users who pass the selection stage, your total daily funnel entry is approximately 12,500 users. Currently, 7,500 users (60%) drop off before viewing a menu. In contrast, only ~550 users abandon at payment (10% of the 5,500 who reach checkout).

Even a conservative 10% relative improvement in the selection-to-menu conversion rate would recover ~750 additional orders daily. To achieve the same volume gain by fixing checkout, you would need to reduce abandonment from 10% to nearly 0%—a mathematical impossibility given inevitable friction like declined cards or address errors. The "overwhelming choices" hypothesis is statistically more probable to yield significant revenue lifts than micro-optimizing an already high-converting payment stage.

**Primary Hypothesis to Test**
"We believe that reducing cognitive load on the restaurant list (via better filtering, curated collections, or limiting initial display count) will increase the rate of users viewing a menu by 15%, because the current drop-off suggests decision paralysis rather than technical latency."

**Key Performance Indicators (KPIs) for Q3**

1.  **Primary Metric:** *Menu View Rate* (Users who view a menu / Users who land on restaurant list). This directly measures the friction point.
2.  **Secondary Metric:** *Time-to-Menu*. Distinguish between users leaving immediately (overwhelmed) vs. users waiting too long (technical lag).
3.  **Guardrail Metric:** *Average Order Value (AOV)*. Ensure that guiding users to specific restaurants doesn't inadvertently push them toward lower-priced options.
4.  **North Star:** *Daily Completed Orders*. The ultimate validation of revenue impact.

**Expected Outcome**
If the hypothesis holds, a 15% lift in menu views could conservatively translate to a 10-12% increase in total completed orders by Q3 end, adding ~500-600 daily orders. This volume impact dwarfs any potential gain from checkout optimization, where the ceiling for improvement is mathematically capped by the small absolute number of abandonments.

**Note:** Validate the "choice overload" vs. "loading time" theory by segmenting drop-off rates by device type and connection speed before engineering begins.
Ensure tracking events distinguish between "list load," "scroll depth," and "restaurant click" to isolate the exact friction moment.
