### Evaluation Framework: Quality vs. Quantity Trade-off

To determine if the 22% conversion lift justifies the 10% support spike, you must quantify the **Net Economic Impact** and **Compliance Risk Exposure** before making the change permanent. Do not rely on conversion rate alone; the "friction" you removed was likely serving as a quality gate and trust signal.

**1. Calculate the Net Economic Value per Variant**
Compare the profit generated by each version, subtracting the cost of errors and support.
*   **Formula:** `(Completed Checkouts × Avg Order Value × Margin %) - (Support Tickets × Cost Per Ticket) - (Error Rate × Cost of Correction/Churn)`
*   **Action:** If the variant's additional revenue is less than the increased operational cost of support and data correction, the test is a financial loss despite the higher volume.
*   **Key Metric:** **Profit Per Visitor (PPV)**. If PPV is lower on the variant, the conversion gain is an illusion created by low-quality submissions.

**2. Quantify Compliance and Data Integrity Risk**
For your legal officer, "privacy concerns" are not just sentiment; they are regulatory liabilities.
*   **Data Accuracy Cost:** Calculate the percentage of orders requiring manual intervention due to incorrect data (e.g., wrong shipping addresses, invalid emails). Multiply this by the fully loaded cost of fixing the error post-purchase.
*   **Regulatory Exposure:** If users are confused about data collection because explanatory fields were removed, this may violate transparency requirements (e.g., GDPR, CCPA). The risk is not just current tickets, but potential fines or audit failures.
*   **Action:** Classify the 10% support spike. Are these "how-to" questions (fixable with UI copy) or "trust/legal" objections (requiring structural changes)? If >50% are trust-based, the variant fails the compliance check regardless of revenue.

**3. Segment the "False Positive" Conversions**
The 22% increase likely includes users who abandoned the process *later* in the funnel or churned immediately due to frustration.
*   **Cohort Analysis:** Compare the Day-7 retention and refund rates of the Control vs. Variant groups. If the variant group has a significantly higher refund or churn rate, the initial conversion was a "false positive."
*   **Support Ticket Correlation:** Track if the users submitting support tickets are the same ones converting. High-value customers expressing privacy concerns represent a disproportionate risk compared to low-value conversions.

**Recommendation for Stakeholders**
*   **For Legal:** Do not launch permanently until the "privacy concern" tickets are reduced to baseline levels via non-field UI elements (e.g., trust badges, micro-copy explaining *why* data is needed without requiring extra input fields).
*   **For Customer Success:** Implement a "Cost of Poor Quality" model. If the support team cannot absorb the 10% increase without hiring or degrading service for existing customers, the variant is operationally unsustainable.

**Immediate Next Step:** Run a **hybrid variant** that keeps the 4-field structure but reintroduces the *trust signals* (explanatory text, security badges, data usage summaries) that were implicitly lost when fields were removed. Test if this recovers trust without reintroducing friction.

*Note: Ensure your analytics track "successful fulfillment" as the primary metric, not just "form submission," to capture downstream errors.*
*Do not extend the test duration to wait for significance if guardrail metrics (support volume) are already breached; stop and iterate.*
