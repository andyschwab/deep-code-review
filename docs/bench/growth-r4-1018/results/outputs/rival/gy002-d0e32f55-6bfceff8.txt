### Tracking Plan for Acme Goods Wishlist Feature

**Goal:** Verify if wishlist usage drives purchases within 7 days without overwhelming your limited storage.

**Recommendation on Clicks:** Do **not** track every click on the heart icon. Clicks often include accidental taps or users changing their minds. Tracking them creates "noise" that makes your data harder to read and wastes storage. Only record the action when the system successfully saves the item. This ensures every record represents a genuine user intent.

#### Top 3 Events to Instrument

Instruct your developer to send these three specific data records. Keep the names exactly as written below for consistency.

| Event Name | When to Fire (Trigger) | Data to Include (Properties) |
| :--- | :--- | :--- |
| `wishlist_added` | Immediately after the item is successfully saved to the user's list. | `product_id`, `product_name`, `price`, `user_id` (or email if logged out) |
| `wishlist_removed` | When a user manually deletes an item from their list. | `product_id`, `user_id` |
| `purchase_from_wishlist` | When a transaction is completed containing an item currently or previously on that user's wishlist. | `order_id`, `total_value`, `product_ids_in_order`, `user_id` |

*Note to Developer:* Ensure `user_id` is consistent across sessions so you can link the wishlist action to the future purchase. If users shop without logging in, use a persistent browser cookie ID.

---

#### How to Read the Data (Simple Analysis)

Once the feature launches, export your logs to a spreadsheet once a week. You do not need complex software; you only need to answer two questions.

**1. Is the feature being used?**
Count the total `wishlist_added` events for the week.
*   **Calculation:** Divide this number by your total site visitors (or product page views).
*   **Success Signal:** If more than 2–5% of engaged users add an item, the feature is visible and useful. If near zero, the button may be hard to find.

**2. Does it drive sales? (The 7-Day Test)**
This verifies your assumption that wishlists lead to orders.
*   **Step A:** List every `wishlist_added` event with its date and `user_id`.
*   **Step B:** Look for a `purchase_from_wishlist` event from the *same* `user_id` occurring within 7 days of the add date.
*   **Step C:** Calculate the **Conversion Rate**: (Number of users who bought within 7 days) ÷ (Total number of users who added an item).
*   **Success Signal:** If this rate is higher than your standard site conversion rate (typically 1–3% for e-commerce), the wishlist is successfully recovering potential lost sales.

**Decision Rule:**
If the conversion rate from wishlist users is **higher** than your average order rate, the feature is working. Keep it and consider emailing users about their wishlist items after 3 days of inactivity. If the rate is **lower**, the feature may be acting as a "parking lot" where users save items but never intend to buy, requiring a change in strategy.
