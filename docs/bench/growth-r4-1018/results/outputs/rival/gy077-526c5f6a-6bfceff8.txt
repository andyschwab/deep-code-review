# Recommendation: Do Not Ship Yet

Based on the data provided, you should **not ship this tooltip to all 300 clients by Friday**. The divergence between your engagement metric (+14% click-through) and your support metric (+450% ticket volume) indicates a critical usability failure that threatens your long-term retention and operational capacity.

### Interpreting the Divergence

Your hypothesis assumed that explicit guidance would reduce errors. The data proves the opposite: the tooltip is creating confusion, not clarity.

1.  **The "Success" Metric is Misleading:** The 14% increase in clicks on the main booking button does not necessarily mean successful bookings or satisfied users. In the context of a 450% spike in support tickets, these clicks likely represent "rage clicks" or confused users frantically trying to dismiss the tooltip or find the function they expected. You are measuring activity, not value.
2.  **The Support Spike is a Leading Indicator of Churn:** An increase from 2 to 11 tickets per week in a niche B2B environment is severe. For ice hotel managers, time is their most scarce resource during the booking season. If your software requires them to contact support to complete basic tasks, you are introducing friction into their core workflow. In a small market like Iceland, reputation spreads quickly; frustrating your early adopters now will damage retention and referrals later.
3.  **Operational Unsustainability:** Your team of 12 cannot absorb a sustained 5.5x increase in support volume. If this pattern holds across all 300 clients, your support queue will become unmanageable, diverting engineering and product resources away from growth work to fire-fighting. This violates the core principle of growth: scalable acquisition and retention.

### The Decision Framework

You are facing a trade-off between a short-term engagement lift and long-term product health.

*   **If you ship now:** You prioritize the 14% click lift but accept a broken onboarding experience. You risk overwhelming your support team and alienating your entire customer base during their peak season. The "confusion" is not a minor cost; it is a blocker.
*   **If you hold back:** You preserve customer trust and team capacity. You acknowledge that the current implementation fails the "guardrail metric" test (support tickets).

### Immediate Next Steps

1.  **Halt the Rollout:** Inform the founders and head of design that the guardrail metric (support tickets) has failed catastrophically. Shipping now would be negligent given the known risk to client success.
2.  **Qualitative Investigation:** Immediately review the 11 support tickets. What exactly are users confused about? Is the tooltip obscuring critical information? Is the language unclear? Is it triggering at the wrong time?
3.  **Iterate, Don't Abandon:** The insight that users need guidance is likely still valid, but the *execution* is flawed. Redesign the tooltip based on the specific feedback from the 11 tickets.
4.  **Re-test with a Longer Window:** Run a new test for at least 2–3 weeks. Monitor not just click-through rates, but actual booking completion rates and support ticket volume. Ensure the support metric remains stable before considering a full rollout.

**Conclusion:** The data suggests you are damaging the user experience. Do not trade long-term retention for a vanity metric. Fix the confusion first.
