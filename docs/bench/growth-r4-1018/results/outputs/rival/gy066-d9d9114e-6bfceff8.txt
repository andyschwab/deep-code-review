**Executive Summary: FitTrack Social Sharing Test (Truncated)**

**Recommendation:** Treat the quantitative dataset as **compromised for performance measurement** but **valid for directional interest signals**. Do not calculate lift, conversion rates, or statistical significance from the 3,000 participants. The 5% crash rate on iOS introduces severe selection bias; users with older devices (who crashed) are systematically excluded from engagement metrics, likely skewing results toward power users with newer hardware. Any calculated "win" is statistically uninterpretable.

**Salvageable Insights & Statistical Approach**
While we cannot measure *how well* the feature works, we can assess *if the concept resonates* with the stable cohort using these methods:

1.  **Descriptive Only, No Inference:** Report raw counts and percentages of engagement (e.g., "X% of stable users clicked 'Share'") without confidence intervals or p-values. Explicitly label these as "observations from a non-representative sample."
2.  **Cohort Segmentation (Proxy Analysis):** Since exact device data for crashed sessions is unknown, segment the *stable* 95% by available proxies (e.g., iOS version, app version, tenure). If engagement is consistent across iOS 15, 16, and 17 within the stable group, it suggests the *concept* holds across generations, despite the crash bug. If engagement drops sharply on older iOS versions (even among those who didn't crash), the feature may inherently struggle with that demographic.
3.  **Qualitative Triage:** Prioritize session replays and crash logs for the 5% failure group. Did they attempt to share before crashing? If crash logs show the error triggers immediately upon accessing the share menu, the intent data is lost. If the crash happens post-share, the engagement signal is valid for those users.

**Required Additional Data Collection**
To validate viability before engineering allocation, gather:

*   **Crash Forensics:** Correlate crash logs with device models and iOS versions. Determine if the bug is fixable via a hotfix or requires a full release.
*   **User Feedback:** Deploy an in-app survey to the stable cohort asking: "How likely are you to use this feature once stable?" (Net Promoter Score style). This bypasses behavioral bias.
*   **Competitive/Market Benchmark:** Compare observed engagement rates (even if biased) against industry benchmarks for social sharing in health apps. If the biased rate is drastically below benchmark, the concept may be weak regardless of the bug.

**Conclusion for VP of Product**
The feature concept remains **unproven, not disproven**. The data confirms the feature is usable for users on stable devices, but we cannot quantify its impact on retention or growth yet. Recommend proceeding with the engineering fix *only if* qualitative feedback indicates high user demand. Do not re-launch the A/B test until the crash is resolved and a new power analysis confirms the required sample size for a valid test.

*Note: Do not present the 4-hour data as "early results" to stakeholders; frame it strictly as a "technical validation test" that identified a critical blocker.*
