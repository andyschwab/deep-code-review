**Subject:** Operational Efficiency Measurement Framework (Beta Phase)

**North Star Metric: Confirmed Deliveries Completed**
Reject "registered volunteers," "pageviews," or "messages sent." These are vanity metrics that rise regardless of actual aid delivery. Your single North Star is the count of supply deliveries where a donor and recipient were successfully matched and the goods were physically transferred. This is the only number that proves survival impact to donors.

**Input Metrics (The Drivers)**
To move the North Star, track exactly three input metrics that answer specific operational questions:
1.  **Match Rate (%):** Of users who post a need or offer, what percentage achieve a match? (Answers: Is the marketplace liquid enough?)
2.  **Time-to-Confirmation (Hours):** Time elapsed between a post and a confirmed match. (Answers: Is the matching logic fast enough for disaster response?)
3.  **Sync Success Rate (%):** The percentage of "confirmed delivery" events successfully uploaded from rural zones. (Answers: Is our offline-first architecture working?)

**Event Taxonomy: Instrument Only What Answers a Question**
Do not instrument "browsing" or generic clicks. Given sporadic connectivity, every byte counts. Implement only these three events, each tied to a decision:

*   **`delivery_confirmed`** (Primary Event)
    *   *Question:* Did a successful coordination occur?
    *   *Properties:* `delivery_id`, `timestamp_local`, `offline_synced` (boolean), `photo_uploaded` (boolean).
    *   *Decision Rule:* If `photo_uploaded` is false but `delivery_confirmed` is true, accept the text confirmation as valid but flag for manual spot-check. Do not block the metric on photo upload due to bandwidth constraints.
*   **`match_created`**
    *   *Question:* Are we connecting people, even if delivery lags?
    *   *Properties:* `time_since_post`, `channel_type` (sms/web/app).
    *   *Decision Rule:* If `match_created` is high but `delivery_confirmed` is low, the bottleneck is physical logistics, not software matching.
*   **`sync_attempt`**
    *   *Question:* How much data are we losing to connectivity?
    *   *Properties:* `payload_size`, `success` (boolean), `retry_count`.
    *   *Decision Rule:* If success < 80%, prioritize engineering resources to compression/offline queueing over new features.

**Reading the Funnel: Retention of Utility First**
Do not focus on acquiring more volunteers until the current 1,200 are successfully delivering. Read the funnel bottom-up:
1.  **Retention/Completion:** Of the matches made, how many result in `delivery_confirmed`? If this leaks, adding volunteers creates noise, not aid.
2.  **Activation:** Do new volunteers complete at least one `delivery_confirmed` within 48 hours of signing up?
3.  **Acquisition:** Only scale recruitment once the above two hold true.

**Experiment Rigor & Baselines**
*   **No Fabricated Benchmarks:** We cannot assert what a "good" match rate is for disaster relief. Compare this week's performance strictly against last week's baseline.
*   **Connectivity Unknowns:** Treat any metric without a corresponding `sync_attempt` success flag as `UNVERIFIED`. Do not estimate rural activity; instrument the sync failure explicitly to quantify the blind spot.
*   **Donor Reporting:** For next week's report, present the trend of `delivery_confirmed` against the goal of 500. If the current trajectory (e.g., 20/week) misses the April 1 target, state the gap clearly rather than inflating with vanity sign-up numbers.

2.  **Query:** Run a daily cohort analysis showing `match_created` vs. `delivery_confirmed` to identify the drop-off point.
3.  **Decide:** If `sync_attempt` failure > 20%, halt feature work and dedicate all engineering capacity to offline synchronization reliability immediately.
