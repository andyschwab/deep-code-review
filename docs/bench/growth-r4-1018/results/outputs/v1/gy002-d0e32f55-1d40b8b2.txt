### Top 3 Events to Instrument for Wishlist Launch

Provide these exact instructions to your development contractor. Do not track every click; track only completed actions that answer specific business questions.

**1. Event Name: `wishlist_add_success`**
*   **When to fire:** Only when an item is successfully saved to the user's list after clicking the heart icon. Do not fire on the click itself, only on the confirmed save.
*   **Data to include:** `user_id` (stable ID to track return visits), `product_id`, and `timestamp`.
*   **Question this answers:** "Are users finding enough value to actually save items?" This measures activation, not just interest.

**2. Event Name: `wishlist_view`**
*   **When to fire:** When a user opens their wishlist page.
*   **Data to include:** `user_id` and `item_count` (number of items currently in the list).
*   **Question this answers:** "Do users return to review their saved items?" High adds but low views indicate the feature is a dead end; users save but never come back.

**3. Event Name: `purchase_from_wishlist`**
*   **When to fire:** When a checkout is completed for an item that was present in the user's wishlist within the last 7 days.
*   **Data to include:** `user_id`, `product_id`, `order_value`, and `days_since_wishlisted` (calculated as checkout time minus wishlist time).
*   **Question this answers:** "Does the wishlist actually drive revenue?" This validates your assumption about the 7-day purchase window.

### How to Read the Data

Since you lack a dedicated data team, perform these three manual checks weekly using your logs:

1.  **Calculate the Conversion Rate:** Divide the count of `purchase_from_wishlist` events by the count of `wishlist_add_success` events for the same week.
    *   *Decision Rule:* If this rate is below 5% after four weeks, the feature is not driving sales. Investigate if users are viewing the list (`wishlist_view`) but not buying. If they view but don't buy, the barrier may be price or shipping, not the feature itself.
2.  **Verify the 7-Day Assumption:** Look at the `days_since_wishlisted` property in your purchase events.
    *   *Decision Rule:* If the majority of purchases happen after 14 days instead of 7, update your marketing emails to target users at the two-week mark instead of one. If most purchases happen within 24 hours, the "wishlist" is acting more like a "cart hold," and you should consider sending immediate abandonment reminders.
3.  **Check Retention, Not Just Volume:** Ignore the total number of adds if the `wishlist_view` count is flat.
    *   *Decision Rule:* If `wishlist_add_success` is high but `wishlist_view` is low, users are saving items impulsively but have no intent to return. This is a vanity metric. You must improve the reminder mechanism (email or notification) before celebrating the number of adds.

**Implementation Note:** Ensure your `user_id` remains consistent across sessions so you can link the initial save to the future purchase. Without a stable ID, you cannot measure the 7-day correlation.

**Storage Note:** By firing only on success and view (not every hover or click), you will keep log volume minimal while capturing 100% of the data needed to make a go/no-go decision on the feature.
