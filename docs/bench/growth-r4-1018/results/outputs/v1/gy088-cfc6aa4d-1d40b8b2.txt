**Recommendation: Adopt "Monthly Active Enterprise Clients" as the North Star Metric**

For a Series A climate tech platform with enterprise sales cycles exceeding four months, **Monthly Active Enterprise Clients (MAEC)** is the superior North Star. While "Total Carbon Offset" sounds impactful, it is a vanity metric in this context: it can be inflated by one-off transactions or pilot programs that do not guarantee recurring revenue. Investors at Series A prioritize **retention and repeatability** over cumulative volume. A rising count of *active* paying clients proves product-market fit and predictable recurring revenue (ARR), which directly correlates with valuation. "Total Carbon Offset" should remain a secondary impact metric, not your primary growth driver.

**Comparative Analysis for the Board**

| Metric | Pros | Cons & Investor Risks |
| :--- | :--- | :--- |
| **Total Carbon Offset** | • Demonstrates immediate environmental mission alignment.<br>• Easy to aggregate and display as a large cumulative number. | • **Vanity Risk:** Can rise while churn increases (e.g., losing clients but offsetting more via one-offs).<br>• **No Revenue Signal:** Does not prove clients are sticking around or expanding contracts.<br>• **Manipulable:** Can be gamed by subsidizing offsets without customer commitment. |
| **Monthly Active Enterprise Clients** | • **Retention Proxy:** Directly measures if high-value clients derive ongoing value.<br>• **Revenue Correlation:** Strongly ties to Net Revenue Retention (NRR), a key Series A diligence metric.<br>• **Sustainability:** Forces focus on product stickiness rather than one-time sales. | • **Lagging Indicator:** Moves slowly due to long sales cycles.<br>• **Definition Sensitivity:** Requires a strict definition of "Active" to avoid counting dormant accounts. |

**Tracking Leading Indicators (Given 4+ Month Sales Cycles)**

Since MAEC moves slowly, you must instrument **activation events** that predict a client becoming "Active" before the first renewal. Do not track "everything"; instrument only these three events that answer specific questions:

1.  **Event:** `integration_completed`
    *   **Question:** Did the client successfully connect their data sources? (Without this, churn is near 100%).
    *   **Property:** `time_to_integrate` (days).
2.  **Event:** `first_offset_batch_verified`
    *   **Question:** Did they move from setup to actual value delivery?
    *   **Property:** `batch_size_tonnes`.
3.  **Event:** `dashboard_login_weekly`
    *   **Question:** Is the client engaging operationally, not just administratively?
    *   **Property:** `user_role` (to distinguish champions from billing contacts).

**This Week's Steps**
1.  **Define "Active":** By Tuesday, finalize the strict criteria for an "Active Enterprise Client" (e.g., "logged in + verified >1 tonne in last 30 days") and apply it to your historical data to establish your true baseline.
2.  **Instrument the Seam:** Ensure your analytics pipeline captures a stable, pseudonymous `enterprise_account_id` to track these accounts across sessions, distinct from individual user IDs.
3.  **Baseline Check:** Run a cohort analysis on clients who integrated 6 months ago; if their `dashboard_login_weekly` rate is below 40%, your retention leak is in adoption, not acquisition. Fix this before pouring money into new sales.
