**Interpretation of Data Reliability: UNVERIFIED**

The reported 4% lift for the "green button + short FAQ" variant is **statistically unreliable** as a basis for isolating specific effects or rolling out changes globally. You ran a multivariate test (MVT) with three variables but are attempting to draw conclusions about individual drivers (button color) and interactions (masking effects) without providing the necessary factorial breakdown.

In a valid MVT, you must analyze the main effect of each variable (e.g., Green vs. Non-Green regardless of other changes) and the interaction effects (e.g., does Green only work when the FAQ is short?). Your statement that the coverage summary data is "inconclusive" while claiming a lift for a specific *combination* suggests you are looking at cell-level winners rather than main effects. This is a classic error: a winning combination in one cell does not prove which variable drove the win, especially with 45,000 visitors split across $2 \times 2 \times 2 = 8$ cells (approx. 5,600 per cell). At this sample size per cell, the power to detect interaction effects is extremely low unless the effect size is massive.

Furthermore, if you monitored the dashboard daily and stopped because "it hit 4%," the result is compromised by **peeking**, which inflates the false-positive rate well above 5%. Without confirmation that the sample size was pre-committed based on a Minimum Detectable Effect (MDE) calculation for *interaction* terms, the 4% figure is noise, not signal. Your assumption that button color "masked" the others is a hypothesis, not a finding; statistically, it is equally likely that the lift was a random fluctuation in that specific cell or driven by an unmeasured confounder (e.g., traffic source mix during those 20 days).
