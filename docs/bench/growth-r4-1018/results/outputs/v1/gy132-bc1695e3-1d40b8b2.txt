### Proposed Retention Definitions for StreamFiesta

Given your Friday release cadence and the observed "binge-then-disappear" behavior, standard Daily or Weekly Active Users (DAU/WAU) will indeed obscure true value. Below are three retention definitions tailored to your episodic model, designed to distinguish between engaged fans and churned users.

**Option 1: "Episode-Cycle Retention" (Recommended)**
*   **Definition:** A user is "retained" in a given week if they consume *any* content within a 5-day window centered on the release (Thursday through Monday).
*   **Pros:** Directly aligns with your product value proposition (the Friday drop). It captures the "weekend warrior" segment without penalizing them for Monday–Thursday inactivity. It filters out the noise of mid-week non-users who are still loyal.
*   **Cons:** Requires shifting your reporting cycle from calendar-month to "release-cycle" months (approx. 4.3 cycles per month), which may require explanation to directors accustomed to strict calendar boundaries.
*   **Best For:** Measuring the core health of the weekly release strategy.

**Option 2: "Content-Depth Cohort Retention"**
*   **Definition:** Segments users by behavior at signup. "Bingers" are retained if they return within 21 days of their last session; "Weekly Viewers" are retained using the Episode-Cycle definition above.
*   **Pros:** Acknowledges your data showing 25% of users binge and vanish. It prevents these users from falsely dragging down your weekly metrics, treating their 3-week absence as normal behavior rather than churn.
*   **Cons:** More complex to explain to regional directors; requires maintaining two parallel retention curves.
*   **Best For:** Internal product tuning and understanding if the "binge" group eventually converts to weekly viewers.

**Option 3: "Monthly Active Engaged" (MAE)**
*   **Definition:** A user is retained in a month if they watch content in at least *two* distinct weekly release cycles within that calendar month.
*   **Pros:** Satisfies the directors' demand for "month-over-month" growth while filtering out one-off signups who watched one episode and never returned. It smooths out the Friday spike.
*   **Cons:** Lags in signal; you won't know a user is churned until the month ends. Less useful for immediate weekly tactical changes.
*   **Best For:** High-level executive reporting for Q3 growth targets.

**Recommendation for Q3 Reporting:**
Adopt **Option 1 (Episode-Cycle Retention)** as your primary North Star input for internal decision-making, but report **Option 3 (MAE)** to the regional directors to satisfy the calendar-month requirement while ensuring the metric reflects actual repeated value.

**Immediate Next Steps:**
1.  **Instrumentation:** Verify your event taxonomy tracks `episode_watch_start` with a `user_id` and `timestamp` to enable the Thursday–Monday window query.
2.  **Baseline Query:** Run a historical query defining "retained" as watching in 2+ cycles last month; if this number is <20% of signups, the issue is content resonance, not metric definition.

*Note: Do not compare these rates to industry "standard" retention benchmarks; measure only against your own baseline trend as you refine the definition. Avoid peeking at daily fluctuations during the week; evaluate retention only after the Monday window closes.*
