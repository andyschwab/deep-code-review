**To: Chief Financial Officer**
**Subject: Verdict Framework for Bundle Offer Profitability**

The 8% conversion lift and 12% Average Order Value (AOV) drop create a revenue neutral point at a **~4.5% increase in total order volume**. If the experiment drove more than a 4.5% rise in total orders compared to the control baseline, top-line revenue increased; if less, it declined. However, revenue is not profit. To determine financial sustainability for the fiscal year, we must calculate the **Contribution Margin per Visitor**, not just top-line revenue.

**1. The Net Impact Calculation**
Do not rely on the aggregate "8% lift." Recalculate using the specific margins of the items sold.
*   **Formula:** `(Total Orders_Bundle × Margin_Bundle) + (Total Orders_Individual × Margin_Individual)` vs. `(Total Orders_Control × Margin_Control)`.
*   **The Cannibalization Check:** You correctly suspect cannibalization. We must isolate whether the bundle buyers would have purchased full-price items anyway. If the bundle margin is 40% and the individual book margin is 60%, an 8% volume increase may not offset the margin compression from users trading down.
*   **Break-Even Tripwire:** Calculate the specific conversion rate required to match the control group's total profit. If the observed conversion rate in the test group falls below this derived threshold, the offer destroys value regardless of volume growth.

**2. Required Data Breakdowns**
Request the following segments from analytics immediately to validate the behavior:
*   **Cohort by Purchase History:** Split results between "New Customers" and "Returning High-Spenders." If high-spenders are trading down to the bundle while new customers remain flat, the offer is eroding core revenue. If new customers are driving the volume lift with minimal impact on existing high-spenders, the risk is lower.
*   **Units Per Transaction (UPT):** Did the bundle increase the total number of books shipped? If UPT rose significantly, shipping costs may have eroded the margin further.
*   **Return/Refund Rates:** Compare return rates between bundle and control. Discounted bundles sometimes attract higher return rates or lower satisfaction, impacting net profit later.

**3. Lifetime Value (LTV) vs. Immediate Revenue**
For a fiscal year decision, **immediate contribution margin is the primary driver**, but LTV is the guardrail.
*   **Immediate:** If the bundle reduces margin per transaction without sufficient volume compensation, it hurts cash flow now. Do not bank on future LTV to fix a negative unit economics model today.
*   **LTV Context:** Only extend the horizon if data shows bundle buyers return at a significantly higher rate than control users within 90 days. Without evidence of higher retention, assume the transaction is standalone. Optimizing for LTV is invalid if the first transaction loses money and retention does not demonstrably improve.

**Verdict Path**
*   **Ship Permanent:** Only if `(Lifted Volume × Bundle Margin)` exceeds `(Control Volume × Control Margin)` AND high-value customer cannibalization is <5%.
*   **Stop:** If the break-even volume tripwire was not met or if returning high-spenders shifted disproportionately to the bundle.
*   **Extend/Test:** If results are mixed by cohort (e.g., profitable for new users, loss-making for existing), restrict the offer to new customers only and re-run for two weeks.

**This Week's Step:** Run the margin-adjusted profit calculation on the three-week test data using the formula above. If the net profit per visitor is negative, halt the rollout. If positive, segment by customer tenure to ensure core revenue isn't being cannibalized before presenting the final go/no-go.
