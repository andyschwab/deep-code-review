### Pilot Success Framework: VeriChain ID x Partner Bank

**North Star Metric: Verified Identities per Hour (VIPH)**
*Definition:* The count of unique users who successfully complete the full KYC flow and receive a verified status, normalized by hour.
*Rationale:* This metric rises only when both speed (throughput) and security (approval) are functioning. It rejects vanity metrics like "total sign-ups" or "system uptime," which can increase even if the product fails to verify anyone.

**The Balanced Scorecard (Input Metrics)**
To satisfy both technical velocity and compliance risk, track these three input metrics that drive the North Star. Do not weigh them with arbitrary percentages; instead, use them as guardrails for decision-making.

1.  **Median End-to-End Verification Time (Speed)**
    *   *Question:* Are we meeting the <10 minute value promise?
    *   *Target:* <10 minutes for the 50th percentile user.
    *   *Action:* If this slips >15 mins, investigate infrastructure latency or third-party API delays immediately.

2.  **False Positive Rate (Security)**
    *   *Question:* Are we rejecting legitimate users due to over-sensitive fraud rules?
    *   *Definition:* Percentage of users flagged for manual review who are subsequently approved by human agents.
    *   *Action:* If this exceeds the baseline established in your 500-user sandbox, loosen automated fraud thresholds. High false positives kill speed and user experience.

3.  **Fraud Capture Rate (Risk)**
    *   *Question:* Are we catching actual bad actors?
    *   *Definition:* Percentage of verified users later flagged as fraudulent (via bank feedback loops) vs. total attempts.
    *   *Action:* This is the primary guardrail for the Chief Risk Officer. If this rises above the sandbox baseline, tighten rules immediately, even if it impacts Speed.

**Weighing the Metrics: The Decision Matrix**
Do not average these numbers. Use this logic to resolve conflicts during the first month:
*   **Scenario A:** Speed is high (<10m), but False Positives are rising.
    *   *Verdict:* **Unstable Growth.** You are trading long-term trust for short-term throughput. *Action:* Pause marketing acquisition; tune fraud models.
*   **Scenario B:** Fraud Capture is perfect, but Speed is >48 hours.
    *   *Verdict:* **Product Failure.** The value proposition (replacing traditional KYC) is broken. *Action:* Escalate to engineering; bypass non-critical checks if safe.
*   **Scenario C:** Speed is good, Fraud Capture is good, but VIPH is low.
    *   *Verdict:* **Adoption/Activation Issue.** The tech works, but users drop off before finishing. *Action:* Analyze the drop-off funnel step (UX issue).

**Leading Indicators for Month 1**
Watch these weekly to predict end-of-month success:
1.  **Day-1 Retention of Verified Users:** Do verified users actually initiate a banking action within 24 hours? If not, the verification may be frictionless but useless.
2.  **Manual Review Queue Depth:** A rising queue indicates the automated system is failing to decide, threatening the <10 min promise.
3.  **Drop-off Rate at Document Upload:** The most common friction point in identity flows. A spike here indicates a technical regression or UX confusion.

**This Week's Steps**
1.  **Instrument the "Verification Complete" event** with properties for `duration_seconds`, `fraud_score`, and `manual_review_flag` to calculate VIPH and False Positive Rate immediately.
2.  **Establish the Sandbox Baseline:** Compute the exact False Positive Rate and Median Time from your 500 test users; set these as the "UNVERIFIED" threshold triggers for the pilot.
3.  **Define the Stop-Loss:** Agree with the CRO that if the Fraud Capture Rate exceeds the sandbox baseline by >0.5%, the pilot pauses for rule tuning regardless of speed gains.
