**Executive Summary: Social Sharing Feature Test (FitTrack)**

**Verdict:** The quantitative dataset is **compromised for causal inference** and cannot determine if the feature drives value. The 5% crash rate on iOS, concentrated on older devices, introduces severe selection bias. We cannot distinguish between "users disliked the feature" and "users were unable to use the feature." Consequently, the early engagement metrics are **UNVERIFIED** indicators of product viability.

**Statistical Approach: Salvage via Segmentation, Not Aggregation**
Do not calculate an overall lift or significance. The mixed population (crashed vs. stable) invalidates the control/treatment comparison. Instead, apply the following triage:
1.  **Isolate the Stable Cohort:** Filter the 3,000 participants to include *only* iOS users who did not experience a crash and all Android users (assuming no Android bugs).
2.  **Compare Baselines:** Check if the "Stable iOS" group matches the "Android/Control" group on pre-experiment metrics (e.g., historical session length). If they differ, the crash mechanism itself selected for a specific user type, rendering even the stable subset biased.
3.  **Descriptive Only:** Report engagement rates for the "Stable" group strictly as descriptive data ("X% of users who could access the feature clicked share"), not as a causal lift. Label this explicitly as "Uncorrected for Selection Bias."

**Qualitative Data Requirements (Immediate Action)**
Since the numbers are noisy, pivot to high-signal qualitative inputs to assess concept viability before engineering allocates resources to a fix:
*   **Crash Session Replay:** Review session recordings for the 5% crash cohort. Did users attempt to share before the crash? This confirms intent despite the failure.
*   **Targeted User Interviews:** Contact 5–10 users from the "Stable" treatment group who *did* engage. Ask: "What motivated you to share?" and "What would have stopped you?"
*   **Support Ticket Analysis:** Scan for immediate feedback regarding the share button visibility or confusion, independent of the crash.

**Decision Framework for the VP**
*   **If** stable users show high intent (e.g., >20% click-through on the share prompt) **AND** qualitative feedback confirms strong social motivation: **Proceed with Fix.** The concept is viable; the bug was the blocker.
*   **If** stable users ignore the feature **OR** qualitative feedback indicates low relevance: **Pause.** Do not fix the bug yet; the feature likely lacks value regardless of stability.
*   **Guardrail:** Do not re-launch until the crash rate is <0.1%. A 5% crash rate on a core flow is a critical reliability failure that outweighs any potential feature gain.
