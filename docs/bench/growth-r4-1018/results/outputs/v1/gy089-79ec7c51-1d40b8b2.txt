### Deep Dive: UI Change Impact Analysis (July 22 – Aug 1)

**Executive Summary**
The 3.5% lift in "Add to Basket" (ATB) actions coupled with a flat 2.1% purchase conversion rate indicates a **funnel leakage shift**, not a total failure. The UI change successfully reduced friction for *intent* but likely increased low-quality entries (window shoppers) or exposed downstream friction points that were previously masked by higher abandonment earlier in the flow. The metric divergence suggests the change altered *who* enters the funnel, not necessarily the final purchase capability for high-intent users.

#### Hypotheses for the Discrepancy

1.  **The "Window Shopper" Inflation (Mix Shift)**
    *   **Theory:** The prominent button encourages casual browsers to click ATB as a "save for later" mechanism without genuine purchase intent. This inflates the ATB denominator, diluting the conversion rate even if absolute purchase volume remains steady.
    *   **Indicator:** Check the ratio of "ATB → Checkout Start." If this dropped significantly while "Checkout Start → Purchase" remained stable, the issue is top-of-funnel quality, not downstream barriers.

2.  **Downstream Friction Exposure**
    *   **Theory:** By moving more users to the basket, you have amplified the impact of existing barriers (shipping costs, payment options). Previously, users dropped off before seeing these costs; now they see them later, creating a "shock" at the payment stage.
    *   **Indicator:** Analyze drop-off rates specifically at the "Shipping Calculation" and "Payment Method Selection" steps compared to the pre-July 22 baseline.

3.  **Mobile vs. Desktop Disparity**
    *   **Theory:** The button enlargement may have different usability effects across devices. If mobile traffic (often lower conversion) saw a disproportionate spike in ATB clicks due to easier tapping, it could skew the aggregate conversion rate downward.
    *   **Indicator:** Segment ATB lift and conversion rates by device. A high mobile ATB lift with flat mobile conversion confirms this hypothesis.

#### Proposed Experiments & Actions (Before Aug 15)

**Experiment A: The "Intent" Filter (A/B Test)**
*   **Design:** Run a variant where the enlarged button includes micro-copy clarifying intent (e.g., "Add to Basket – Free Returns" vs. just "Add to Basket").
*   **Goal:** Determine if clarifying value propositions at the click point filters out window shoppers, improving the quality of the ATB cohort.
*   **Metric:** Primary: ATB → Checkout Start rate. Guardrail: Overall Revenue per Visitor.

**Experiment B: Downstream Friction Isolation (Segmented Analysis)**
*   **Action:** Immediately segment the post-July 22 data by **New vs. Returning** users and **Device Type**.
*   **Hypothesis Test:** If returning users show a stable conversion rate while new users drop, the UI is attracting low-intent new traffic. If mobile shows a sharp drop in ATB → Checkout, the UI may be causing accidental clicks on smaller screens.
*   **Deliverable:** A segmented funnel report by Aug 5 to inform Q3 strategy.

**Experiment C: Shipping Cost Transparency Test**
*   **Design:** For users who click the enlarged ATB, trigger an immediate, non-intrusive tooltip or modal estimating shipping costs before they leave the PDP.
*   **Goal:** Mitigate "sticker shock" in the basket by bringing cost awareness earlier, validating if shipping is the true barrier.
*   **Metric:** Basket Abandonment Rate at the Shipping Step.

#### Immediate Next Steps
1.  **Segment the Data (By Aug 2):** Break down the July 22–Aug 1 data by device and user type (new/returning) to isolate where the conversion dilution is occurring.
2.  **Audit Drop-off Points (By Aug 5):** Compare step-by-step drop-off rates (ATB → Checkout → Payment) against the June baseline to pinpoint the exact friction layer.
3.  **Launch Experiment A (By Aug 8):** Begin the micro-copy A/B test to validate the "window shopper" hypothesis before the Q3 review.

*Note: All metrics must be compared against BritStyle's own June/July baseline; no external benchmarks are applied. Ensure user identifiers are pseudonymized for cohort tracking.*
