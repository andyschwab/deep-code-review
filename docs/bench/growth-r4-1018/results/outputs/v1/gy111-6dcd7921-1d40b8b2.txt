2.  **Invite Timestamp:** To cohort invites by week and measure latency.
3.  **Activation Event:** A boolean flag indicating if the invitee performed the "core action" (e.g., edited a board or joined a live session) within 7 days of signing up. Sign-ups alone are vanity; only activated users count toward true viral growth.
4.  **Traffic Source Tag:** A strict flag distinguishing "Referral Link" clicks from "Paid Campaign" clicks. If a user arrives via a referral link but also has a paid ad cookie, your attribution logic must prioritize the referral only if the referral click was the *last* non-direct touch before sign-up, or exclude them entirely to be conservative.
5.  **Team Size Snapshot:** The number of active members in the inviter's team at the moment of the invite, to test your hypothesis regarding teams with >3 members.

**Calculation Structure:**
Do not use cumulative totals. Calculate the **Viral Coefficient (k)** per weekly cohort:
$$k = (\text{Number of Invites Sent per User}) \times (\text{Conversion Rate of Those Invites to Activated Users})$$

*   **Numerator:** Count of *activated* users who signed up via a referral link in Week $X$.
*   **Denominator:** Count of *active* users in Week $X$ who were eligible to send invites.
*   **Isolation Logic:** Exclude any invitee who landed on a paid landing page variant or entered via a tracked paid campaign ID, even if they also used a referral link. This ensures the numerator reflects only organic pull.

**Testing the "Team Size" Hypothesis:**
Segment the calculation into two distinct cohorts:
1.  Inviters with $\le$ 3 active team members.
2.  Inviters with $> 3$ active team members.
Calculate $k$ separately for each. If $k_{>3}$ is significantly higher (e.g., $>0.5$) while $k_{\le3}$ is near zero, your assumption holds. If both are low, the loop is broken regardless of team size.

*   **If $0.5 \le k < 1.0$:** The loop contributes but doesn't sustain. Recommendation: Optimize the invite flow (e.g., reduce friction to the "core action") before increasing spend.
*   **If $k < 0.5$:** The loop is leaking. Recommendation: Do not scale incentives. Pivot resources to fixing activation or exploring direct sales.
