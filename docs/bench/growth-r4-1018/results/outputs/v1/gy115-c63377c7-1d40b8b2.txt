**Recommendation: Optimize for Task Completion Rate**

For CaseMaster, **Task Completion Rate** is the correct North Star metric; **Total Engagement Time** is a vanity metric in this context that risks optimizing for friction. Your hypothesis that "longer sessions indicate deeper research" contradicts your support data indicating users struggle to find case law. In legal research, value is derived from speed and precision (reducing billable hours spent searching), not duration. A solicitor finding a precedent in 5 minutes delivers higher value to their client than one taking 45 minutes due to navigation issues. Therefore, rising engagement time alongside falling completion rates signals increased confusion, not deeper usage.

**Framework for Interpreting Metric Conflicts**

When evaluating your A/B test results, apply this decision matrix based on the divergence between the two metrics:

1.  **Time Up, Completion Down (The "Friction" Trap):**
    *   **Diagnosis:** Users are lost. The interface is likely hiding search functions or presenting irrelevant results, forcing users to click repeatedly without success.
    *   **Action:** **Reject** the change immediately. This directly increases legal research costs for your clients, threatening your December renewals.
    *   **Business Impact:** High churn risk; clients cannot justify renewal if efficiency drops.

2.  **Time Down, Completion Up (The "Efficiency" Win):**
    *   **Diagnosis:** Users are finding what they need faster. This is the ideal state for a productivity tool.
    *   **Action:** **Ship** the change. This validates your value proposition of cost reduction.
    *   **Business Impact:** Strongest signal for enterprise renewals; demonstrates tangible ROI (time saved = money saved).

3.  **Time Up, Completion Up (The "Depth" Scenario):**
    *   **Diagnosis:** Users are successfully finding initial cases and then choosing to explore related jurisprudence deeper within the tool.
    *   **Action:** **Investigate** via cohort analysis. Verify if the extra time is voluntary exploration (high value) or forced reading of poorly summarized results. If voluntary, this is acceptable, but ensure it doesn't plateau into frustration.
    *   **Business Impact:** Positive, provided the "extra time" correlates with successful case outcomes, not just browsing.

4.  **Time Down, Completion Down (The "Abandonment" Risk):**
    *   **Diagnosis:** Users are giving up quickly. The tool may be failing to return *any* relevant results, causing immediate exit.
    *   **Action:** **Roll back** and prioritize search relevance algorithms over UI tweaks.

**Execution Plan for October 10 A/B Test**

To ensure the test yields a defensible result for your Head of Product and December reviews:

*   **Primary Metric:** Task Completion Rate (defined as: User executes a search and clicks/view a specific case law document within 3 minutes).
*   **Guardrail Metric:** Average Session Duration. Monitor to ensure efficiency gains do not come at the cost of users missing critical context (though unlikely given current friction).
*   **Instrumentation:** Define the event `search_task_completed` with properties `search_duration_ms` and `result_clicked`. Do not instrument generic page views.
*   **Decision Rule:** Pre-commit to a sample size based on your Q1 traffic to detect a 5% lift in completion rate. Read the result **once** at the end of the test period. Do not peek at daily data; a temporary dip in completion rate during early exposure is noise, not a failure.
*   **Reporting for Renewals:** Frame the success criteria around "Hours Saved per Research Task." If completion rate rises and time drops, calculate the aggregate time saved across the firm to present a dollar-value efficiency gain to clients.

**Note:** Do not fabricate a "good" completion rate benchmark; compare the test variant strictly against your Q1 baseline. If the data shows high variance, extend the test duration rather than stopping early for significance.
