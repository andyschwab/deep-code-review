### Measurement Plan: StockPop Pilot Early Warning System

**Audience:** Regional Directors and Supply Chain Logistics
**Objective:** Detect inventory misalignment within 48 hours of site opening to enable rapid reallocation before the Dec 15–17 peak.

#### 1. The North Star (Pilot Success)
**Metric:** **Peak Weekend Stockout Avoidance Rate**
*Definition:* The percentage of high-demand SKUs available at the start of the Dec 15–17 weekend across all 20 locations.
*Why:* Total revenue is a lagging outcome; for a 3-week pop-up, running out of stock during the single critical weekend is an irreversible failure. This metric focuses purely on readiness for the value moment.

#### 2. The 48-Hour Leading Indicators (The "Fail Fast" Signals)
Do not wait for monthly trends. Monitor these three inputs daily for the first two days to predict weekend stockouts.

| Indicator | The Question It Answers | Warning Threshold (Trigger Action) |
| :--- | :--- | :--- |
| **Hourly Sell-Through Velocity vs. Baseline** | Is demand at this specific site significantly higher or lower than the 500 txn/day expectation? | If actual velocity deviates >20% from the projected hourly curve by Hour 24, flag for immediate transfer. |
| **Empty Shelf Time (Top 20 SKUs)** | Are core items selling out faster than replenishment can occur? | If any Top 20 SKU is out of stock for >2 consecutive hours during peak operating times, trigger emergency restock. |
| **Conversion Drop-off at POS** | Are customers abandoning purchases due to missing items? | If transaction count plateaus while foot traffic (door counters) remains high, assume inventory friction. |

#### 3. Handling Unknown Foot Traffic
Since external foot traffic is unknown, do not try to measure the traffic itself. Instead, measure the **ratio of internal signals**:
*   **The Signal:** Compare *Transactions per Hour* against *Units Sold per Hour*.
*   **The Logic:** If units sold spike but transactions remain flat, basket size is growing (good). If transactions flatline while staff report crowds, you have a stockout. This isolates inventory performance from external foot traffic variables without needing a foot-traffic counter.

#### 4. Event Taxonomy (Instrument Only These)
To answer the questions above, instrument only these specific events in your system. Do not track "page views" or generic logs.

1.  **`sku_stock_status_change`**
    *   *Properties:* `sku_id`, `location_id`, `previous_quantity`, `new_quantity`, `timestamp`.
    *   *Question:* How quickly are we depleting specific SKUs at each site?
2.  **`pos_transaction_complete`**
    *   *Properties:* `location_id`, `timestamp`, `line_item_count`, `total_units`.
    *   *Question:* What is the real-time velocity of sales compared to the 500 txn/day baseline?
3.  **`stock_transfer_initiated`**
    *   *Properties:* `from_location`, `to_location`, `sku_list`, `reason_code` (e.g., "velocity_mismatch").
    *   *Question:* Are we successfully moving stock to where demand is proving highest?

#### 5. Decision Rules for Logistics Team
*   **Day 1 (24h mark):** Calculate actual sell-through velocity. If a site is >20% above baseline, pre-emptively pull stock from sites >20% below baseline. Do not wait for a stockout.
*   **Day 2 (48h mark):** Review "Empty Shelf Time." Any location with >2 hours of empty shelf time on Top 20 SKUs requires an immediate emergency transfer from the nearest surplus site.
*   **Dec 14 (Pre-Peak):** Freeze all non-critical transfers. Ensure 100% of Top 20 SKUs are stocked at 110% of predicted weekend demand based on Days 1–2 velocity.

*Note: All thresholds are derived from your stated 500 txn/day baseline; no external benchmarks were invented. If POS data cannot capture "empty shelf" time directly, use a manual "out-of-stock" scan event by store staff every 2 hours.*
