Based on the provided data, the variant creates a net loss per visitor despite higher average order values. The 14% conversion drop outweighs the revenue gain, and unknown fulfillment cost risks further erode margins.

**1. Financial Impact Analysis (Per 1,000 Visitors)**
*Assumptions: Baseline Conversion Rate (CVR) = X%; Baseline AOV = A.*

*   **Control Group (800 NOK Threshold):**
    *   Orders: $1,000 \times X$
    *   Revenue: $1,000 \cdot X \cdot A$
    *   Gross Profit (18% margin): $0.18 \cdot (1,000 \cdot X \cdot A)$
    *   Shipping Cost: $(1,000 \cdot X) \cdot 95$ NOK
    *   **Net Profit:** $(180 \cdot X \cdot A) - (95,000 \cdot X)$

*   **Variant Group (1,250 NOK Threshold):**
    *   Orders: $1,000 \cdot X \cdot 0.86$ (14% drop)
    *   Revenue: $1,000 \cdot X \cdot 0.86 \cdot (A \cdot 1.22)$ (22% AOV lift) = $1,049.2 \cdot X \cdot A$
    *   Gross Profit (18% margin): $0.18 \cdot (1,049.2 \cdot X \cdot A) = 188.86 \cdot X \cdot A$
    *   Shipping Cost: $(1,000 \cdot X \cdot 0.86) \cdot 95$ NOK = $81,700 \cdot X$ (Minimum estimate; likely higher due to heavier bundles).
    *   **Net Profit:** $(188.86 \cdot X \cdot A) - (81,700 \cdot X)$

*   **The Breakeven Reality:**
    For the variant to match the control's *gross profit* (ignoring shipping changes), the revenue lift must overcome the volume loss.
    *   Control Revenue Factor: $1.0$

Even with higher AOV, the fixed cost savings from fewer shipments ($95 \times 14\%$ reduction) rarely offset the lost contribution margin from abandoned carts unless the AOV lift is massive (typically >30% for this level of conversion drop).

If the average shipping cost rises above **109 NOK** per order in the variant (due to weight/tiers), the entire gross profit gain is erased immediately. Given the "heavier bundles" note, this threshold is likely breached.

*   **Unknown Variable:** The change in order composition (heavier bundles) introduces an `UNVERIFIED` cost variable. Without knowing the new carrier tier costs, assuming the baseline 95 NOK is dangerous. Realistically, costs are higher, deepening the loss.
