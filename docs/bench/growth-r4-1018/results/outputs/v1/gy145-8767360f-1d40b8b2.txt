**Subject:** Evaluation of March Pricing Experiment Sustainability (CA Market)

**Executive Summary & Immediate Call**
The reported 5% revenue lift is currently **UNVERIFIED** as a sustainable gain due to the high probability of a "pull-forward" effect (users upgrading early to avoid the hike) and a concurrent 22% rise in Acquisition Cost (CAC). Without isolating the cohort of users who upgraded *specifically* to beat the price increase versus those who upgraded due to perceived value, we cannot distinguish between borrowed revenue and genuine lift. **Decision:** Do not forecast the 5% lift into Q2. Treat April's baseline as the true test; if April's organic upgrade rate (excluding the pre-hike surge) drops below the pre-March baseline, the experiment resulted in a net loss once the 22% CAC increase is factored in.

**Key Factors to Distinguish Value Perception from Temporal Anomalies**

1.  **Cohort Separation (The "Pull-Forward" Test)**
    *   **Action:** Segment March upgrades into two distinct groups: (A) Users who upgraded between March 1–25 (anticipating the hike) and (B) Users who upgraded after the $18 price went live.
    *   **Reading Rule:** If Group A comprises >60% of the volume, the revenue spike is almost entirely temporal. Genuine value perception is only proven if Group B's conversion rate matches or exceeds the historical baseline at the higher price point.
    *   **Q2 Forecast Impact:** Subtract Group A's revenue from Q2 projections entirely; this is revenue stolen from April/May, not new growth.

2.  **Net Revenue Retention (NRR) vs. One-Time Spike**
    *   **Action:** Track the churn rate of the March upgraders in April and May.
    *   **Reading Rule:** If the churn rate for these users spikes in April (downgrades or cancellations), the price signal failed, and the "value" was merely urgency. Sustainable value requires the upgraded cohort to remain stable at the $18 tier.
    *   **Financial Guardrail:** Calculate the break-even point where the 22% higher CAC is offset by the increased Lifetime Value (LTV). If the upgraded users churn within 3 months, the increased CAC makes this pricing strategy unprofitable regardless of the top-line revenue bump.

3.  **Acquisition Funnel Leakage**
    *   **Action:** Compare the conversion rate of *new* enterprise trials in March (at $18) against the historical average at $15.
    *   **Reading Rule:** A 22% rise in CAC suggests the higher price is creating friction at the top of the funnel. If trial-to-paid conversion dropped by more than ~18% (roughly the price increase ratio), the volume loss will eventually erase the revenue gain.
    *   **Sustainability Check:** If new logo acquisition slows significantly in April while CAC remains elevated, the "signaling higher quality" hypothesis is invalid; the market is simply pricing out marginal customers.
