**Recommendation: Adopt "Weekly Active Translators" (WAT) as your North Star Metric.**

Define WAT as: *The count of unique users who complete at least one successful translation action within a 7-day rolling window.*

**Supporting Logic for Strategy Review**

1.  **Rejects Vanity, Captures Value:** "Active installs" (50k) is a stock metric that accumulates regardless of value delivery. "Pages per session" measures depth but penalizes your most loyal users who translate single, high-intent phrases daily. Your own retention data confirms that *frequency* (returning daily) correlates stronger with long-term value than *depth* (multiple pages). WAT captures the user who gets value repeatedly, which is the prerequisite for premium conversion.
2.  **Aligns Acquisition with Retention:** By optimizing for WAT, marketing shifts focus from raw install volume to acquiring users who actually integrate the plugin into their workflow. If you optimize for "pages per session," you risk over-investing in power users who are already saturated, while ignoring the "daily checker" who represents scalable growth.
3.  **Revenue Correlation:** For a plugin, premium conversion usually hinges on habit formation (e.g., needing translation every day) rather than single-session volume. A user translating 1 page/day for 30 days is a higher lifetime value candidate than one translating 30 pages in one day and churning. WAT directly measures this habit.

**Input Metrics to Move WAT**
To influence WAT, track these two inputs:
*   **Activation Rate:** % of new installs performing their *first* translation within 24 hours. (Fixes the leak where users install but never start).
*   **Day-2 Retention:** % of users returning to translate on the second day after install. (Validates the initial "aha" moment).

**This Week's Steps**
1.  **Query Validation:** Run a cohort analysis in your analytics tool comparing "Users with ≥3 sessions/week" vs. "Users with >5 pages/session" against your Q1 premium conversion rate. Expect the frequency cohort to show higher conversion; bring this chart to Wednesday's meeting.
2.  **Instrumentation Check:** Verify your event taxonomy tracks a unique `translation_completed` event with a stable, pseudonymous user ID. If you are currently only tracking "sessions" or "pageviews," you cannot calculate WAT accurately; flag this as a critical engineering ticket before the review.

*Note: Do not assert a specific "good" retention percentage benchmark; compare your Q2 WAT trend strictly against your Q1 baseline to measure improvement.*
