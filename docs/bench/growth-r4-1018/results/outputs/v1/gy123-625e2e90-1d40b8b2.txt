**Strategic Brief: Refining Churn Prediction for Resource Efficiency and Enterprise Coverage**

**Objective**
Balance support team efficiency with accurate detection of high-value enterprise churn by moving from a single global confidence threshold to a segmented, behavior-driven model.

**Current Diagnosis**
The current model (0.6 threshold) yields excessive false positives (40% of active users), draining support resources. Simply raising the threshold to 0.8 risks creating false negatives for enterprise clients, whose churn signals are often subtle and distinct from standard user patterns. The core issue is not the threshold value itself, but the lack of segmentation; a "one-size-fits-all" score cannot account for differing behavioral baselines between user cohorts.

**Recommended Strategy**

1.  **Segment-Specific Thresholds**
    Do not apply a global 0.8 threshold. Instead, calculate separate precision-recall curves for **Enterprise** vs. **Standard** segments.
    *   *Action:* Isolate historical data for enterprise clients. Determine the threshold that maximizes recall for this specific group, even if it lowers overall precision. For standard users, raise the threshold to reduce noise.
    *   *Result:* Support resources focus on high-probability standard users, while enterprise coverage remains high.

2.  **Introduce "Value-Weighted" Risk Scoring**
    Replace the binary "High/Low Risk" flag with a **Risk × Value** score.
    *   *Action:* Multiply the churn probability by the customer's Lifetime Value (LTV) or Annual Recurring Revenue (ARR).
    *   *Logic:* A 65% probability churn risk for a $50k ARR client warrants immediate outreach. A 65% risk for a $500 ARR client may not. This ensures that even if the model misses some subtle enterprise signals (lower confidence), the high value of the account keeps them in the outreach queue.

3.  **Refine Behavioral Signals for Enterprise Cohorts**
    Enterprise churn often precedes usage drops with specific "soft" signals that standard models miss. Analyze these specific events for the enterprise segment only:
    *   *Admin Activity:* Reduction in multi-factor authentication logins by admins or changes in billing contact details.
    *   *Integration Health:* Increased API error rates or decreased webhook delivery success (technical friction often precedes cancellation).
    *   *Seat Utilization:* Decline in *active* seats vs. *paid* seats (expansion contraction).
    *   *Support Sentiment:* Shift in support ticket tone (not just volume) detected via NLP on enterprise tickets.

4.  **Implement a "Human-in-the-Loop" Validation Phase**
    Before full deployment, run a two-week shadow test.
    *   *Action:* Apply the new segmented thresholds and value-weighted scoring to live data without triggering automatic outreach.
    *   *Measurement:* Have Customer Success manually review the "High Value/Medium Risk" bucket to verify if the subtle enterprise signals are being captured. Measure the reduction in false positives for standard users.

**Immediate Next Steps (This Week)**
*   **Data Science:** Generate separate precision-recall curves for Enterprise vs. Standard segments using January–present data. Identify the optimal threshold for each that maintains >90% enterprise recall.
*   **Customer Success:** Define the top 3 "soft signal" behaviors unique to your lost enterprise clients in the past year to feed into feature engineering.
*   **Joint Decision:** Agree on a pilot "Risk × Value" formula and validate it against last month's false positive list to estimate saved support hours.

**Note:** Do not adjust the global threshold to 0.8 without segmenting first; this will likely blindside the enterprise cohort. All metric baselines must be derived from FlowBank's internal historical data, not external industry averages.
