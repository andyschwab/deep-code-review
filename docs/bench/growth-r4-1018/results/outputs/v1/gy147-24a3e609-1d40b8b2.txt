### Framework for Evaluating User Health: The "Safe Value" Matrix

To resolve the tension between growth and compliance for BetStream, replace single-dimensional engagement metrics with a **Safe Value North Star**. This metric rises only when a user places a verified bet *within* their pre-defined responsible gambling limits. Vanity metrics like raw Daily Active Users (DAU) or total session length are rejected because they increase even when users are merely observing without transacting or when they are exhibiting risky behavior.

**1. The North Star and Input Metrics**
*   **North Star:** Weekly Active Bettors (users placing ≥1 settled bet within compliance limits).
*   **Input Metrics:**
    *   *Activation Rate:* % of new users placing a first bet within 24 hours of signup.
    *   *Safe Session Depth:* Average number of odds viewed per successful bet (filters "cautious observation" from "checkout friction").
    *   *Limit Utilization:* % of users hitting deposit/time limits (a guardrail, not a success metric).

**2. Diagnosing the May 10–12 Anomaly (Retention First)**
Your assumption that new users are "cautious" is `UNVERIFIED` without cohort segmentation. A drop from 4.2 to 2.8 bets/user during a high-stakes event could indicate two opposing realities:
*   **Healthy Observation:** New users (acquired via the finals spike) have a lower initial frequency but higher Day-7 retention.
*   **Checkout Friction:** High traffic caused latency in the betting slip or payment gateway, causing abandonment after odds selection.

**Action:** Segment the May 10–12 data by **User Tenure** (New vs. Returning).
*   If the drop is isolated to *New Users* while *Returning Users* maintain ~4.2 bets, your "cautious observer" hypothesis holds.
*   If *Returning Users* also dropped to ~2.8, this indicates systemic friction (e.g., latency, odds feed lag) that must be fixed before acquiring more traffic. Pouring acquisition spend into a funnel where existing users cannot complete bets is wasted capital.

**3. Reconciling Growth vs. Compliance**
The conflict arises because traditional growth metrics (Session Length, Bet Volume) can inadvertently incentivize risky behavior, while compliance metrics (Limits Set, Cool-offs) look like "friction" to growth teams.
*   **The Tension:** A rise in "Session Length" might mean deep engagement, or it might mean a user chasing losses. A rise in "Bets Per User" might mean revenue growth, or it might mean a breach of responsible play thresholds.
*   **The Resolution:** Treat compliance limits as **Guardrail Metrics**, not secondary goals. An A/B test on the checkout flow is only a "win" if it increases conversion *without* increasing the rate of limit breaches or self-exclusion requests. If conversion rises but limit breaches rise, the result is `UNVERIFIED` as a net positive; the product is growing unsafely.

**4. Event Taxonomy for Dual Audiences**
Instrument only events that answer specific questions for both stakeholders:
*   `bet_slip_viewed` (Property: `odds_count`, `time_on_slip`): Answers "Are they researching or stuck?"
*   `bet_placed_success` (Property: `user_tenure`, `limit_headroom`): Answers "Did they convert safely?"
*   `limit_warning_displayed`: Answers "Are we intervening effectively?"

Do not instrument "everything." Every event must tie to the "Safe Value" North Star or a specific compliance guardrail.
