**Diagnostic Breakdown: Algorithm B Impact vs. Inventory Confounder**

**Executive Summary**
The 25% lift in click-through rate (CTR) coupled with an 8% drop in purchase volume indicates a severe degradation in conversion efficiency. While the hypothesis of "curiosity clicks" is plausible, the concurrent inventory shortage creates a confounding variable that renders the current aggregate data `UNVERIFIED` for causal attribution. You cannot determine the true algorithm impact until you isolate traffic exposed to out-of-stock items.

**Immediate Diagnostic Steps (This Week)**

1.  **Segment by Inventory Status (The Primary Cut)**
    *   **Action:** Query your analytics to split users into two cohorts: those who viewed/searched for items **in stock** vs. those who viewed items **out of stock** during the test window.
    *   **Reading Rule:**
        *   If the purchase drop exists *only* in the "out of stock" cohort: The algorithm is likely fine; the issue is supply chain.
        *   If the purchase drop exists in the "in stock" cohort: The algorithm is recommending irrelevant items (low intent), validating the team's frustration hypothesis.
    *   **Metric:** Conversion Rate (Purchases / Sessions) per cohort, compared to the baseline period.

2.  **Analyze the "Click-to-Cart" Funnel**
    *   **Action:** Measure the drop-off between "Item Click" and "Add to Cart" specifically for Algorithm B vs. Baseline.
    *   **Reading Rule:** A sharp increase in drop-off at this specific stage for in-stock items confirms the "curiosity/frustration" theory. Users are clicking but rejecting the item upon seeing details/price/reviews.

3.  **Guardrail Check: Revenue Per Visitor (RPV)**
    *   **Action:** Calculate RPV for the test group.
    *   **Reading Rule:** Even if total volume dropped due to inventory, if RPV for *available* inventory is stable or up, the algorithm may be successfully upselling. If RPV is down, the algorithm is degrading value.

**Decision Framework for Leadership**

*   **Scenario A (Supply Chain Issue):** Purchase volume recovers in the "in stock" segment to baseline levels.
    *   **Decision:** Do not iterate the model. Escalate to supply chain/logistics to resolve stockouts before the holiday rush. Algorithm B can proceed to wider rollout once inventory stabilizes.
*   **Scenario B (Algorithm Failure):** Purchase volume and conversion rates remain depressed even for "in stock" items.
    *   **Decision:** Halt Algorithm B rollout immediately. The model optimizes for engagement (clicks) at the expense of intent (revenue). The ML team must retrain with a loss function weighted toward conversion or revenue, not just CTR.
*   **Scenario C (Mixed Signal):** Moderate drop in "in stock" conversion, severe drop in "out of stock."
    *   **Decision:** Iterate the model to deprioritize low-inventory categories (a business rule filter) while investigating the relevance gap.

**Required Data Artifacts**
To finalize this verdict, the ML team must produce a cohort analysis table showing:
1.  Session Count by Algorithm (A/B)
2.  % of Sessions interacting with Out-of-SKU items
3.  Conversion Rate (In-Stock Only)
4.  Revenue Per Visitor (In-Stock Only)

Without separating the inventory constraint from user behavior, any conclusion on the algorithm's quality is statistically invalid.

**Note:** Do not rely on aggregate "total purchase volume" for the final decision; it conflates two distinct failure modes. Ensure the "in-stock" filter uses real-time inventory snapshots matched to the exact session timestamp.
