### Executive Summary: Recommendation Engine Experiment (Jan 10 – Feb 21)

**Verdict: Do not ship. The feature is currently broken.**

The 14-minute increase in listening time is a **false positive** driven by friction, not value. The skip rate surge (22% → 39%) indicates the algorithm is failing to match user intent. Users are not "exploring deeper"; they are hunting for playable content amidst irrelevant suggestions. Shipping this now risks churning core subscribers who value curation quality over raw catalog size.

**The Conflict Decoded**
*   **Duration Up (+14m):** This is likely "wasted time." Users spend extra minutes skipping bad recommendations before finding music they actually want to hear. In classical streaming, where listeners often seek specific moods or composers, high friction inflates session time artificially.
*   **Skip Rate Up (+17pts):** This is the true signal of failure. A near-doubling of skips proves the "obscure baroque" logic is misaligned with user taste or context. The assumption that "deeper exploration = engagement" is invalid when the skip rate exceeds the activation threshold.

**Decision Framework for Founder & Curator**
We must distinguish between **Content Failure** (wrong pieces) and **UX Failure** (hard to save).

| If the cause is... | The Data Will Show | Immediate Action |
| :--- | :--- | :--- |
| **Content Mismatch** (Too academic) | High skips occur *immediately* (<10s) across all user segments. | **Pivot Logic:** Relax the "obscurity" constraint. Re-weight the algorithm to prioritize known favorites mixed with only 10% obscure tracks. |
| **UX Friction** (Hard to save) | Skips are delayed; users listen partially but don't "heart" or "add to library." Session time is high, but library growth is flat. | **Fix Interface:** Add a prominent "Save for Later" or "Explain this Choice" button directly on the player. |

**Financial & Strategic Impact**
For a niche service like SaffronBeats, retention is the primary revenue driver. A 39% skip rate suggests the feature actively degrades the core value proposition (trust in curation). Pushing this before the funding round signals a lack of product-market fit. It is safer to report "experiment inconclusive; refining algorithm" than to ship a feature that increases frustration.

**This Week's Steps**
1.  **Segment the Skips:** Query the analytics for *time-to-skip* on recommended tracks. If >60% of skips happen within the first 15 seconds, the issue is **Content Mismatch**. If skips happen after 30+ seconds without a "save" action, the issue is **UX/Discovery**.
2.  **Run a Guardrail Check:** Calculate the "Library Add Rate" (saves per session) for the test group vs. control. If adds are flat or down while time is up, confirm the "wasted time" hypothesis and halt the rollout immediately.
3.  **Qualitative Sanity Check:** Have the lead curator manually review the top 50 most-skipped "obscure" pieces suggested last week. If these tracks are genuinely esoteric (e.g., unknown composers vs. known baroque), the algorithm's definition of "obscure" is too aggressive.
