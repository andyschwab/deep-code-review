**Decision: Stop this specific test variant immediately; do not extend duration.**

With 950 monthly visitors, your traffic volume is too low to detect a 0.2% difference within a two-week window without exposing the experiment to severe "peeking" risks and false negatives. Extending the test to chase statistical significance on such a microscopic lift is a waste of engineering time and delays learning. The flat result indicates that swapping hero images and button colors does not move the needle for your audience. This is not necessarily a failure of the "artisan story" concept, but a confirmation that surface-level UI tweaks are not the bottleneck for Claddagh Crafts.

**Interpretation of the Null Result**
The lack of movement suggests the hypothesis—that emotional resonance via layout changes drives immediate clicks—is misaligned with user intent at the homepage stage. For a niche marketplace, users often arrive with specific intent (search) or need deeper proof of value before engaging. A 0.2% variance is effectively noise; treating it as a signal would be "peeking" without a pre-committed sample size large enough to support it. Your baseline traffic cannot support granular A/B testing on secondary metrics like click-through rate (CTR) unless the expected lift is massive (e.g., >20%).

**Signals to Verify Measurement vs. Interest**
Before pivoting strategy, verify these three signals in your existing data to rule out measurement error:
1.  **Bounce Rate by Variant:** Did the new layout increase bounces? If users left faster, the design may have confused them, masking any potential interest in the stories.
2.  **Scroll Depth:** Did users actually see the artisan stories? If the content is below the fold and scroll depth didn't change, the test measured visibility, not interest.
3.  **Downstream Conversion:** Did CTR stay flat but *purchase rate* or *time on site* improve? Sometimes "ugly" or dense content filters for higher-intent buyers. If revenue per visitor is flat, the hypothesis is truly dead for this execution.
