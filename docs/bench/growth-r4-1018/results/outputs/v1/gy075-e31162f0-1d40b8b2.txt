**Recommendation: Revert the anchor pricing immediately and initiate a segmented diagnostic test.**

The divergent metrics indicate a classic "vanity vs. value" failure. While the 8% increase in checkout clicks suggests the anchor successfully manipulated perceived value for lower-tier buyers, the 20% drop in qualified enterprise leads signals a severe breakdown in trust and positioning for your highest-value segment. In enterprise SaaS, the sales cycle relies on perceived partnership and stability; an artificially high anchor can signal desperation or misalignment with market reality, causing serious buyers to disengage before a conversation starts. The risk of permanently damaging brand positioning outweighs the marginal gain in self-serve conversion.

**Key Factors for Decision:**

1.  **Lifetime Value (LTV) Disparity:** An enterprise contract is likely worth 10–50x a self-serve subscription. Losing 20% of high-touch leads to gain 8% more low-tier clicks is almost certainly a net revenue loss. You must calculate the break-even point: how many additional $299 plans are required to offset the loss of a single enterprise deal? The answer will likely show the current test is destroying value.
2.  **Intent vs. Noise:** The increase in clicks may be "noise" from users who are price-sensitive and unlikely to convert to paid plans, whereas the drop in enterprise leads represents a loss of high-intent buyers. The anchor price may have filtered out the wrong cohort while repelling the right one.
3.  **Brand Perception Durability:** Enterprise buying committees discuss vendors internally. If the pricing page makes the product look "too expensive" or "gimmicky," that perception spreads through the market faster than it can be fixed by reverting the page. Delaying the revert increases the surface area of brand damage.
4.  **Attribution Lag:** The drop in leads is already visible, suggesting the impact is immediate. Waiting to see if it is "temporary" is a gamble with your primary revenue engine. The data already shows a negative correlation between the anchor and enterprise interest.

**This-Week Action Plan:**

*   **Revert Immediately:** Restore the original pricing layout today to stop the bleeding of enterprise leads. Treat the current state as a confirmed negative guardrail breach.
*   **Segmented Analysis:** Query your analytics to compare the *conversion rate* (not just click-through) of the $299 plan during the test period versus the baseline. If the 8% click lift did not result in an 8% revenue lift, the test failed on both fronts.
*   **Design a New Test:** Do not test global anchors against enterprise audiences. Design a future experiment that serves the anchor price *only* to traffic segments identified as SMB/self-serve (e.g., by company size or traffic source), while preserving the original layout for traffic matching enterprise profiles. Pre-commit to a sample size that detects a 5% change in enterprise lead volume before launching.

The data indicates the anchor price is acting as a repellent for your most valuable customers. Prioritize the integrity of the enterprise funnel over self-serve optimization until a segmented approach can be validated.
