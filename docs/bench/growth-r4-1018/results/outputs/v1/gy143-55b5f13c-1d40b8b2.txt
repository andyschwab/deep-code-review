**Evaluation: Dragon Quest Chronicles (South Korea) – Bundled Currency Experiment**

**Conflicting Signals**
The experiment presents a high-reward, high-risk profile. The treatment group achieved a **15% increase in Revenue Per Daily Active User (RPDAU)**, indicating strong initial demand or perceived value for the 9,900 KRW bundle. However, this gain is immediately threatened by a **4x spike in refund rates** (rising from 2% to 8%). While the net revenue impact remains positive in the short term (+7% approx., assuming refunds fully negate the grossed amount), the volatility suggests a breakdown in purchase intent verification rather than product dissatisfaction. If the hypothesis regarding "accidental purchases by younger players" is correct, the current net gain is unsustainable; chargebacks from payment providers often exceed simple refund rates and carry penalty fees that can erase the 15% top-line gain.

**Critical Monitoring Signals**
To decide between a global launch or killing the feature, monitor these three specific signals over the next 14 days:

1.  **Net Revenue Per User (NRPU) Trend:** Stop tracking gross RPDAU. Calculate NRPU (Gross Revenue minus Refunds minus Estimated Chargeback Fees). If NRPU trends downward as the refund rate stabilizes above 5%, the feature destroys value.
2.  **Refund Latency Distribution:** Plot the time-between-purchase-and-refund. Accidental purchases typically cluster within minutes or hours. If refunds are spreading out over days, the issue is likely buyer's remorse or value mismatch, invalidating the "accidental click" hypothesis.
3.  **Cohort Retention Post-Refund:** Compare the Day-7 retention of users who refunded against those who did not. If refunding users churn at a significantly higher rate, the friction is damaging long-term LTV, making the short-term revenue spike a "leaky bucket" scenario.

**Required Context for Decision**
Before approving a wider rollout, the following data points are mandatory to validate the risk profile:
*   **Payment Method Mix:** In South Korea, specific payment methods (e.g., carrier billing vs. credit cards) have different friction levels and dispute policies. Identify if the 8% refund rate is isolated to low-friction methods like carrier billing, which are common for younger demographics.
*   **Device Sharing Indicators:** Do we have telemetry indicating multiple user profiles or rapid session switching on devices generating refunds? Without this, the "younger player" assumption remains `UNVERIFIED`.
*   **Chargeback vs. In-App Refund Split:** Distinguish between soft refunds (in-app) and hard chargebacks (bank disputes). A rise in hard chargebacks is a critical guardrail violation that requires immediate cessation, regardless of gross revenue.

**Recommendation**
Do not launch globally yet. The 8% refund rate is a critical guardrail breach. Extend the test for 14 more days with a **pre-committed stop rule**: if the refund rate does not drop below 4% or if Net Revenue Per User turns negative, kill the feature. Simultaneously, instrument a "confirmation delay" or "swipe-to-confirm" interaction specifically for the 9,900 KRW bundle to test if friction reduces accidental purchases without hurting conversion.
