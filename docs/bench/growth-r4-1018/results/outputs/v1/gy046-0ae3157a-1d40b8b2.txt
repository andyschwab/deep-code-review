**Executive Summary: Search Algorithm Rollout Decision**

Do not proceed with a company-wide rollout based on current data. The contradiction between a 15% rise in "successful searches" and a 10% rise in "cannot find document" tickets indicates the new algorithm is optimizing for vanity metrics while degrading utility for critical tasks. The behavioral definition of success (click + no immediate bounce) is flawed; it captures "satisficing" behavior where users click a plausible-looking new document but fail to find the specific legacy information they actually needed, eventually leaving to file a ticket. In an internal knowledge base, legacy documentation often holds critical compliance or system architecture data; hiding these creates high-severity risk that raw click-through rates mask.

**Analysis of Mixed Signals**
Trust the support tickets over the current behavioral metric. The 15% increase likely reflects the algorithm successfully surfacing newer, perhaps more engaging but less critical content, causing users to click and linger briefly before realizing the content is irrelevant. This inflates the "success" rate while increasing frustration. The 10% ticket spike is a lagging indicator of actual task failure. If the test population (Engineering) relies heavily on newer docs, their aggregate "success" rate rises, masking the failure of specific sub-groups needing legacy data. Rolling this out company-wide will amplify support costs and operational risk.

**Required Qualitative Feedback & Immediate Actions**
To resolve this before the August 30th deadline, execute the following three steps immediately:

1.  **Ticket-to-Session Linking:** Manually sample 50 recent "cannot find document" tickets. Correlate the user ID and timestamp with their search session logs. Specifically, identify what query they ran and which "successful" click the new algorithm served them. This verifies if the "success" event was a false positive.
2.  **Targeted User Interviews:** Recruit 10 employees who filed tickets during the test period. Ask them to walk through their specific search task. Determine if the "newer document" bias actively hid the legacy file they required. This answers your unknown variable regarding whether the tickets stem from legacy doc invisibility or general confusion.
3.  **Redefine the North Star Metric:** Stop measuring "clicks without bounce." Define a new success metric: "Search to Resolution," verified by either (a) no follow-up search within 10 minutes or (b) no support ticket filed within 24 hours for that user. Re-run the analysis on the test data using this stricter definition; if the 15% gain disappears or turns negative, the algorithm is failing.

**Decision Path**
If the qualitative sampling confirms that ticket-filers were served irrelevant new docs, the algorithm requires a "legacy weight" adjustment before any broader testing. If the tickets are unrelated to search (e.g., permission issues), only then consider a limited rollout. Do not trust the aggregate 15% lift until the definition of "success" aligns with actual task completion.

*Note: The current "success" metric is a vanity metric that rewards engagement, not resolution. The support ticket volume is the leading indicator of business risk.*
