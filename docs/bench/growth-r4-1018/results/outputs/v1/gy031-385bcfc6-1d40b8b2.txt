**Executive Summary: Dynamic Routing Pilot Analysis**

The observed pattern—a sharp initial gain (14.5%) rapidly decaying to neutral (-2.1%) and finally negative performance (+1.8%)—is inconsistent with a stable algorithmic improvement and strongly indicates **behavioral adaptation** or **environmental mismatch**, not a statistical anomaly in the baseline. The initial "win" was likely a real but short-lived effect driven by optimal conditions or a "novelty halo," while the subsequent decay suggests the system failed under the specific stressors (blizzards) you hypothesized, leading to costly manual overrides.

**Diagnosis of Decay**
1.  **The "Honeymoon" Phase (Days 1–7):** The 14.5% reduction was likely real but fragile. Drivers may have followed the algorithm strictly during calm weather, or the algorithm optimized well for established ice conditions before the blizzard cycle began.
2.  **The Erosion (Days 8–10):** As conditions worsened, the algorithm's recommendations likely became suboptimal or unsafe. The drop to 2.1% suggests drivers began selectively ignoring routes, blending efficient algorithmic paths with manual corrections, diluting the net benefit.
3.  **The Reversal (Final Week):** The 1.8% increase confirms the system became a liability. If drivers overridden the system *after* committing to a poor algorithmic suggestion (e.g., driving into a drift then backtracking), fuel consumption would exceed the baseline due to "double-handling" the route. Alternatively, the algorithm may have routed trucks onto longer "safe" detours that consumed more fuel than experienced drivers would have risked on direct paths.

**Immediate Data Investigation Plan**
To isolate the cause before Tuesday, query your telemetry and driver logs for these three specific correlations:

*   **Override Frequency vs. Weather Severity:** Correlate the timestamp of every manual route deviation with local weather data (wind speed, visibility, snow accumulation). *Hypothesis confirmation:* If >60% of overrides occur during visibility <500m or wind >40km/h, the algorithm lacks a "blizzard mode."
*   **The "Double-Back" Metric:** Calculate the distance/fuel spent on segments where a truck deviated from the suggested path and then returned to it (or a parallel path) within 5km. *Hypothesis confirmation:* A spike in this metric during the final week proves the algorithm sent trucks into dead ends or unsafe zones requiring reversal.
*   **Driver Tenure Stratification:** Segment fuel efficiency by driver experience level. *Hypothesis confirmation:* If veteran drivers show a smaller penalty (or faster recovery) than new hires when overriding, the issue is trust/calibration; if all drivers show identical decay, the algorithm's core logic is flawed for dynamic ice conditions.

**Recommendation for COO Decision**
**Do not roll out to the full fleet of 200 vehicles yet.** The risk of increasing fuel costs across the entire operation during the critical spring thaw is too high given the negative trend in the pilot's final week.

**Actionable Next Step:**
Approve a **"Blizzard-Mode" Patch Sprint** instead of a full rollout. Restrict the software to "Advisory Only" (no auto-routing) for the next 14 days while engineering ingests the override data to tune the weather weighting parameters. Re-evaluate for a phased rollout (20 vehicles) only after the algorithm demonstrates neutral (0%) rather than negative (-1.8%) performance during the next weather event.

*Note: The initial 14.5% gain validates the concept's potential, but the rapid decay proves the current model cannot handle Nunavut's volatility without human-in-the-loop safeguards.*
