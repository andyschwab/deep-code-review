**Deliverable: Top Three Metrics for EcoStat Firmware Update (Board Briefing)**

To distinguish between passive ownership and active value realization for the Series A evaluation, track these three metrics. Each ties directly to your hypothesis that scheduling drives satisfaction and reduces returns.

**1. Weekly Active Schedulers (WAS)**
*   **Definition:** The count of unique devices where a user manually creates, edits, or confirms a temperature schedule at least once per week.
*   **Why it matters:** This validates your core hypothesis. A user setting a static temperature is merely operating a thermostat; a user engaging with the schedule is realizing the "smart" value proposition. Unlike cumulative sales (1,200 units), this metric rises only when the core value is delivered.
*   **Board Signal:** High WAS indicates product stickiness and differentiation from dumb thermostats. Low WAS suggests the "smart" features are friction-heavy or irrelevant, predicting higher churn or return rates despite hardware sales.
*   **Instrumentation Requirement:** Log an event `schedule_modified` with properties `{method: "manual" | "auto_adjusted", device_id}`. *Note: Distinguish manual engagement from automatic algorithmic tweaks to avoid vanity inflation.*

**2. Cloud-Dependent Feature Retention (Week 4)**
*   **Definition:** The percentage of users who, four weeks after activation, still utilize at least one feature requiring cloud connectivity (e.g., remote app adjustment, energy reporting, weather-based auto-schedule) within a 7-day window.
*   **Why it matters:** This separates "background runners" from engaged ecosystem users. If a device operates purely locally after week one, the user is not engaging with the software platform you are building valuation upon. It also serves as a proxy for hardware reliability regarding connectivity.
*   **Board Signal:** Demonstrates the durability of the software moat. A drop-off here indicates users are reverting to local-only mode, signaling potential connectivity friction or lack of perceived value in cloud features.
*   **Instrumentation Requirement:** Log `cloud_feature_used` with properties `{feature_name, connection_status}`. *Status: UNVERIFIED until the firmware update instruments the distinction between local-only operation and successful cloud handshakes.*

**3. Static-Mode Conversion Rate**
*   **Definition:** The percentage of active devices that have not triggered a schedule change or cloud interaction in 14 days, effectively running in "static hold" mode.
*   **Why it matters:** This is your negative indicator (guardrail). It directly identifies the "letting it run in the background" cohort you fear. A rising rate here correlates directly with your concern about reduced long-term satisfaction and potential returns.
*   **Board Signal:** Provides a concrete ceiling on your addressable engaged market. If 60% of units are in static mode, your Series A narrative must shift from "smart platform" to "reliable hardware," which commands a different valuation multiple.
*   **Instrumentation Requirement:** Derived from the absence of `schedule_modified` or `cloud_feature_used` events over a rolling 14-day window per `device_id`.

**Reading Rule for the Board:**
Do not compare these numbers to industry benchmarks (e.g., "SaaS retention is 90%"), as hardware usage patterns differ fundamentally. Compare the June post-update cohort against your January–May baseline. Success is defined as a **statistically significant increase in WAS** and a **decrease in Static-Mode Conversion** relative to your own prior data.
