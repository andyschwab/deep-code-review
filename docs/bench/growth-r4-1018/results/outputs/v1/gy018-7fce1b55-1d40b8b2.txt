### Pricing Trade-off Model: Short-Term Signals to Long-Term Profitability

To determine if the 15% price increase yields net positive value over a 12-month horizon, you must model the **Break-Even Churn Rate** against your observed **Actual Churn Rate**. Do not rely on the 5% total revenue lift from week one; that figure is contaminated by the "survivor bias" of early stayers and ignores the cliff of cancellations.

**1. Recompute the Unit Economics (The "Stayer" Math)**
First, isolate the revenue per surviving user.
*   **Old ARPU (Average Revenue Per User):** $X
*   **New ARPU:** $1.15X
*   **Old Monthly Churn:** 2%
*   **New Monthly Churn (Observed):** 8%

The immediate revenue impact per 1,000 users in Month 1:
*   **Control:** 1,000 users × $X × 98% retention = $980X revenue.
*   **Treatment:** 1,000 users × $1.15X × 92% retention = $1,058X revenue.
*   *Observation:* The 5% aggregate revenue lift you saw is mathematically consistent with this snapshot ($1,058 vs $980). However, this ignores the compounding erosion of the user base.

**2. The 12-Month Cohort Projection**
Project the cohort size over 12 months using the observed churn rates.
*   **Control Cohort (Month 12):** $1,000 \times (0.98)^{12} \approx 784$ users remaining.
*   **Treatment Cohort (Month 12):** $1,000 \times (0.92)^{12} \approx 367$ users remaining.

Now calculate cumulative revenue over 12 months (sum of monthly revenues):
*   **Control Total Revenue:** $\sum_{t=1}^{12} [1000 \times (0.98)^t \times X] \approx 10,560X$
*   **Treatment Total Revenue:** $\sum_{t=1}^{12} [1000 \times (0.92)^t \times 1.15X] \approx 9,340X$

**Verdict:** Under the current 8% churn rate, the pricing change results in an **~11% decrease** in total annual revenue compared to the control, despite the higher price point. The loss of lifetime value from leavers outweighs the premium paid by stayers.

**3. Determine the Break-Even Churn Threshold**
To justify the price hike, the new churn rate must be low enough that the extra 15% revenue compensates for lost volume.
*   Formula: $(1 + \text{Price Increase}) \times (1 - \text{New Churn})^t > (1 - \text{Old Churn})^t$
*   For a sustainable long-term model, the **New Monthly Churn must not exceed ~4.3%**.
*   Your observed 8% is nearly double this threshold. Unless the 8% was a one-time "shock" churn that will revert to <4.3% in Month 2, the strategy destroys value.

**4. Recommendation Logic for CFO/VP Sales**
*   **If 8% churn persists:** Do not roll out. The 5% short-term revenue gain is a "sugar high" that masks a 11% annual revenue contraction.
*   **If churn was a one-time shock:** Segment the data. Did cancellations come from a specific legacy cohort? If the *ongoing* monthly churn for new renewals is <4.3%, proceed with a phased rollout.
*   **Guardrail Metric:** Monitor "Net Revenue Retention" (NRR) weekly. If NRR drops below 100% for two consecutive weeks post-rollout, trigger an automatic price rollback.

If the 8% is uniform across all segments, halt the permanent rollout immediately; the math proves the price elasticity is too high for this tier.
