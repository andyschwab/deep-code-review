**Executive Summary: Do Not Roll Out Yet**

The reported 25% lift is **statistically unverified** as a causal effect of the design. Because the treatment group contained a 40% over-index of users aged 18–24 (a demographic with historically different engagement patterns than your core 35–50 buyers), the result is confounded. The observed lift is likely a **demographic artifact**, not a design win. We cannot distinguish whether the design works for everyone or simply appeals to the over-represented younger cohort without re-analysis.

**Immediate Analytical Actions**

To resolve this without discarding the data, execute the following three steps:

1.  **Stratified Analysis (The Trustworthy Subset)**
    Stop looking at the aggregate number. Split the data by age cohort immediately.
    *   **Action:** Calculate the lift *only* within the 35–50 age group for both treatment and control.
    *   **Decision Rule:** If the lift in the 35–50 segment is near zero or negative, the design fails for your core buyers regardless of the aggregate win. If the lift holds true within this specific cohort, the design may be valid for your target persona.

2.  **Statistical Adjustment (Post-Stratification Weighting)**
    If you must produce a single adjusted number, apply post-stratification weights to mirror the true population distribution of your site traffic.
    *   **Action:** Down-weight the 18–24 users in the treatment group and up-weight the 35–50 users so that the treatment group's demographic mix matches the control group (and your actual user base).
    *   **Result:** This generates an "adjusted lift" estimate. Treat this number as directional, not definitive, as weighting increases variance and reduces confidence.

3.  **Guardrail Check: Revenue Per Visitor (RPV)**
    Your hypothesis states younger users click more but buy less. Click-through rate (CTR) is a vanity metric here if it does not correlate to value.
    *   **Action:** Compare RPV, not just CTR, across the same stratified cohorts.
    *   **Risk:** It is highly probable that while CTR increased 25%, RPV decreased or remained flat due to the influx of non-buying younger users. A win on CTR with a loss on RPV is a failed experiment.

**Recommendation to Product Director & UX Team**

*   **Verdict:** **Extend/Re-test.** Do not launch globally based on the current aggregate data.
*   **The Fix:** If the stratified analysis shows no lift for the 35–50 cohort, the experiment effectively failed for the business goal. You must re-run the test with **proper randomization checks** enforced before launch to ensure demographic balance.
*   **If Re-running is Impossible:** Rely *only* on the unweighted results of the 35–50 subset. If that subset shows no significant improvement, the new design offers no proven value to your core buyers.
