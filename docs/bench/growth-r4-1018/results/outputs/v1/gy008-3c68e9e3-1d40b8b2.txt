### Critical Success Metrics for Sahara Date Co. Drone Pilot

To prove operational stability and safety to the Operations Director and Municipal Council, track these four daily metrics. These distinguish viable logistics from novelty by focusing on reliability and unit economics rather than raw volume.

**1. On-Time Delivery Rate (OTD) with Weather/Battery Stratification**
*   **Definition:** Percentage of deliveries arriving within the 45-minute target window.
*   **Why it proves stability:** Raw averages hide failure modes. You must segment this daily by **weather condition** (e.g., wind speed >20km/h, temperature >40°C) and **battery cycle count** (e.g., batteries with >50 cycles vs. new).
*   **Decision Rule:** If OTD drops below 95% in specific weather or with aged batteries, the model is not yet stable for regulatory approval. This directly addresses the municipal council's safety and reliability concerns.

**2. Spoilage-Adjusted Cost Per Drop**
*   **Definition:** (Total Daily Operational Cost + Value of Spoiled Dates) / Total Successful Deliveries.
*   **Why it proves viability:** Faster transit is only valuable if it reduces spoilage enough to offset higher drone operating costs. Compare this daily against your known trucking cost per drop.
*   **Decision Rule:** The drone cost must be lower than or equal to the truck baseline *including* the value of saved dates. If spoilage remains high despite speed, the hypothesis is false.

**3. Technical Abort Rate (Safety Guardrail)**
*   **Definition:** Percentage of flights aborted pre-delivery due to battery degradation alerts, sensor failures, or automatic weather grounding.
*   **Why it proves safety:** A completed delivery that nearly failed is a near-miss. High abort rates indicate the system is operating too close to its failure envelope, a critical red flag for airspace clearance.
*   **Decision Rule:** Any upward trend in aborts related to battery age requires immediate fleet grounding for that battery batch. This is your primary safety evidence for the council.

**4. Customer Satisfaction (CSAT) vs. Transit Time Correlation**
*   **Definition:** Daily average hotel satisfaction score plotted against actual transit time.
*   **Why it proves value:** Validates the assumption that 45 minutes drives satisfaction. If satisfaction is high regardless of minor delays, the "45-minute" target may be flexible; if it plummets at 50 minutes, the window is rigid.
*   **Decision Rule:** Use this to define the maximum allowable transit time for the service level agreement (SLA).

**Projection Method for End-of-Month Report**
Do not invent benchmarks. To project failure rates and costs for the council:
1.  Calculate the **current daily average** for the four metrics above using your first two weeks of data.
2.  Apply a **confidence interval** based on your daily variance. If your abort rate fluctuates wildly, your projection must show a wide range of possible outcomes, signaling risk.
3.  Explicitly state: "Based on [X] days of observed data, the projected failure rate is [Y]% with a confidence interval of [Z]%." If data is insufficient for a specific weather condition, label that projection **UNVERIFIED** and recommend extending the pilot through that season before expansion.
