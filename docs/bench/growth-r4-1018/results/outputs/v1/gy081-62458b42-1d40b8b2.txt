**Executive Summary: Checkout Experiment (March 10–24) Recommendation**

**Decision:** Do not roll out permanently. Extend the experiment for 14 days with a mandatory guardrail constraint, or pause the variant pending a revised design that removes deceptive patterns.

**Reasoning:**
The reported 38% lift in conversion (4.2% to 5.8%) is statistically significant but operationally `UNVERIFIED` as a net win due to the concurrent 15% spike in refund requests. The current read suffers from "peeking" bias if the decision is made before the pre-committed end date, and more critically, it lacks a defined guardrail metric for post-purchase friction. In a risk-averse framework, a revenue gain that immediately triggers a 15% increase in support costs and potential regulatory exposure (aggressive countdowns and pre-selected boxes often violate consumer protection standards) represents a negative expected value when long-term Customer Lifetime Value (LTV) and brand trust are factored in. The "win" is likely a false positive driven by confusion-induced purchases rather than genuine value creation.

**Framework for Final Decision:**
To defend this before the ethics board, we must re-evaluate the data using a **Primary + Guardrail** model:
1.  **Primary Metric:** Net Revenue (Gross Sales minus Refunds/Chargebacks). The current $12k/day gain is illusory if refund processing costs and lost future LTV exceed it.
2.  **Guardrail Metric:** Refund Rate % and Support Ticket Volume per 1,000 Orders. A variant that moves the primary metric up but breaches the guardrail threshold (e.g., refund rate > baseline + 2%) is an automatic fail, regardless of top-line revenue.
3.  **Ethical/Compliance Check:** Pre-selected add-ons and fake scarcity timers are high-risk for regulatory scrutiny. If the mechanism relies on user error rather than intent, it fails the "long-term trust" test required for sustainable growth.

**Immediate Next Steps (This Week):**
*   **Recompute Net Impact:** Calculate the exact dollar value of the 15% refund spike against the $12k revenue gain to determine the true daily net profit. If net profit is neutral or negative, kill the test immediately.
*   **Extend with Constraints:** If net profit remains positive, extend the test to a pre-committed sample size (calculated based on current traffic) but mandate that the variant be modified to remove pre-selected boxes. Re-run only the countdown timer element with full transparency to isolate the driver of conversion vs. confusion.
*   **Prepare Ethics Defense:** Document that the initial "win" was rejected specifically because the guardrail (refund rate) was breached, demonstrating proactive risk management rather than reactive damage control.

The immediate financial gain cannot justify a strategy that erodes the baseline trust required for retention. Prioritize the guardrail; if the variant cannot convert without confusing users, it is not a scalable growth lever.
