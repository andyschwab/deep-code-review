**Executive Summary: KoiNest Algorithm Experiment (Oct 3–21)**

**Current Status:** The experiment delivered a 22% increase in anomaly detection but incurred an 8% false positive rate on healthy tanks. This trade-off has directly threatened revenue stability, with three major cooperative farms initiating cancellation proceedings due to trust erosion.

**Trade-off Analysis: Detection Gain vs. Trust Cost**

1.  **The Value Proposition Failure:** Your core assumption—that earlier detection always outweighs occasional false positives—has been invalidated by user behavior. In aquaculture, a false alarm triggers immediate, costly, and stressful manual interventions by farmers. An 8% error rate means roughly 1 in 12 "all clear" days becomes a crisis event. For professional cooperatives, this noise degrades the tool from a "risk mitigator" to a "risk creator," directly attacking the livelihood security you promise to protect.
2.  **Revenue vs. Metric Optimization:** While the 22% lift in detected anomalies looks positive on a dashboard, it is currently destroying net revenue. The cost of losing three large cooperative subscriptions likely exceeds the marginal value of the additional anomalies detected across the entire base. In a B2B context, retention is the primary growth engine; acquiring new farms is impossible if current flagship clients are churning due to reliability issues.
3.  **Ethical & Brand Implications:** Your ethical commitment to farmer livelihoods is currently compromised. Frequent false alarms cause unnecessary stress and resource waste (labor, water testing, potential unnecessary treatments). Continuing a rollout that farmers explicitly identify as harmful violates the product's social contract and risks long-term brand damage in a tight-knit industry where reputation travels faster than software updates.

**Strategic Options for Q4 Roadmap**

*   **Option A: Immediate Full Rollback**
    *   *Implication:* Stops the bleeding immediately. Signals to the three threatening cooperatives that KoiNest prioritizes their operational stability over algorithmic experimentation.
    *   *Risk:* You lose the 22% detection gain entirely for the short term.
    *   *Outcome:* Stabilizes revenue and trust; resets the baseline to the previous reliable model.

*   **Option B: Segmented Rollout (High-Risk)**
    *   *Implication:* Restricting the new model to smaller, less sensitive farms while keeping cooperatives on the old model.
    *   *Risk:* This creates a two-tier system where your most valuable customers do not receive the "improved" product. It admits the product is not ready for prime time without fully owning the failure. It may not satisfy the cooperatives who already experienced the errors during the test.
    *   *Outcome:* Preserves some detection data but maintains a fragile relationship with key accounts.

**Recommended Path Forward**

Given the explicit threat of cancellation from your largest revenue sources, the practical implication is clear: **Trust is the bottleneck, not detection speed.** The 8% false alarm rate is a functional defect in a high-stakes environment, not a tolerable variance.

To secure Q4 revenue and honor your ethical commitment, the immediate action is to **rollback the algorithm to the previous stable version for all cooperative farms** while communicating transparently that the update caused unintended operational friction. Preserve the new model only for internal analysis or non-critical pilot segments where false alarms carry no financial penalty. The roadmap must shift from "maximizing detection counts" to "achieving precision thresholds that guarantee zero unnecessary farmer panic." Do not present the 22% gain as a win; present the resolution of the trust crisis as the Q4 priority.

**Note:** The 22% metric is currently a vanity number because it drives churn; focus the board discussion on Lifetime Value (LTV) protection.
**Note:** No statistical re-calculation is needed; the user cancellation intent is the definitive signal of product-market misfit for this specific release.
