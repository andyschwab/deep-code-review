### Proposed Activation Framework for ScholarWrite

**1. Redefine the North Star: From Frequency to Value Depth**
Reject the current "3 documents in 7 days" metric. For academic researchers, this is a vanity metric that rewards superficial usage while missing deep engagement. Based on your observation, the new North Star for activation should be **"Deep Edit Session"**: a single session where a user spends >20 minutes actively editing a document with AI assistance. This aligns with the user's core job-to-be-done (writing long-form papers) rather than an arbitrary frequency count.

**2. The Testing Framework: Cohort Correlation Analysis**
To validate this hypothesis against long-term retention without guessing benchmarks, execute the following three-step analysis on your existing data (February 1st cohort onwards):

*   **Step A: Segment by Behavior, Not Time.**
    Re-segment your historical users into three mutually exclusive groups based on their first 14 days:
    1.  *High Frequency/Low Depth:* ≥3 docs, but no session >20 mins.
    2.  *Low Frequency/High Depth:* 1–2 docs, with at least one session >20 mins.
    3.  *Low Engagement:* Neither of the above.
*   **Step B: Calculate Retention Lift.**
    Compare the Day-30 and Day-90 retention rates of Group 2 against Group 1. If Group 2's retention is significantly higher, your assumption is confirmed. Do not look for an industry "good" rate; look for the *delta* between these two behaviors within your own data.
*   **Step C: Identify the "Aha" Threshold.**
    Run a correlation sweep on edit duration. Test thresholds at 15, 20, 25, and 30 minutes. Select the specific minute mark where the correlation to subscription renewal peaks. This becomes your precise activation definition.

**3. Operationalizing for Customer Success**
Update the intervention trigger for your team immediately:
*   **Old Trigger:** User has <3 docs after 5 days.
*   **New Trigger:** User has created a document but has *no* session exceeding 15 minutes (the lower bound of your test range) after 3 days.
*   **Action:** CS intervenes not to push for *more* documents, but to guide the user into a *deeper* editing workflow on their existing draft.

**4. Experiment Design for Validation**
Before hard-coding this forever, run a "Shadow Metric" experiment for 30 days:
*   **Primary Metric:** Track "Deep Edit Session" (>20 mins) as the official activation rate.
*   **Guardrail Metric:** Monitor total document count to ensure depth isn't increasing at the cost of total usage volume.
*   **Decision Rule:** If the correlation between "Deep Edit" and Month-2 renewal is >1.5x stronger than the correlation for "3 Docs," permanently adopt the new definition. If the data is inconclusive, extend the observation window by two weeks before changing the CS playbook.
