**Recommendation: Prioritize Retention-Led Engagement Over Viral Acquisition**

For the next two quarters, MeetSync's single primary metric must be **Weekly Active Hosts Retaining Core Value**, defined specifically as the percentage of new hosts who schedule and complete at least three meetings within their first 30 days. Do not optimize for viral coefficient or raw sign-up volume yet.

**The Argument**
Your data reveals a critical disconnect: the 18% increase in meeting minutes proves the noise-cancellation feature successfully deepens value for active users, yet new host creation remains flat. This indicates that while your product works well once adopted, the barrier to entry (the network effect) prevents new organizations from switching. Pouring effort into viral mechanics now is inefficient because you cannot yet prove that a "sticky" host naturally invites others who then stay. If you optimize for viral coefficient before solving retention, you risk amplifying a leaky bucket—inviting more guests who sign up as hosts but fail to find enough value to return, wasting acquisition capital and diluting your brand promise.

By focusing on **Retention of New Hosts**, you validate that the core value proposition (superior audio quality leading to productive meetings) is strong enough to hold an organization. Only when a host consistently returns does the network effect begin to compound organically. The 18% lift in minutes suggests you are close to this threshold; the roadmap must ensure that every new host crosses it.

**Implications of Choosing Retention (Recommended)**
*   **Roadmap Focus:** Features will target the "first 30 days" journey. Expect investments in onboarding flows that force early success (e.g., one-click scheduling integrations, automated follow-ups for first meetings) rather than referral incentives.
*   **Resource Allocation:** Engineering effort shifts from broad acquisition hooks to stability and engagement triggers for existing accounts.
*   **Risk:** Top-line user growth numbers will appear stagnant in the short term, potentially frustrating stakeholders focused on market share. However, this builds a defensible foundation where churn is minimized before scale is attempted.

**Implications of Choosing Viral Coefficient (Alternative)**
*   **Roadmap Focus:** Features will prioritize frictionless guest-to-host conversion and sharing mechanics (e.g., "start your own meeting" prompts for guests).
*   **Resource Allocation:** Marketing and product teams chase volume, assuming quality will retain them later.
*   **Risk:** Without confirming that new hosts stay, you may inflate sign-up numbers while churn silently erodes your base. Given the flat host creation despite better audio, there is no evidence that easier sharing alone will overcome the switching barrier. This path risks high burn rate for low long-term yield.

**Decision Rule**
Commit to the retention metric for two quarters. If the percentage of new hosts completing three meetings in 30 days does not increase by at least 15% relative to your current baseline by the end of Q2, only then should you pivot to a viral-led strategy. Until then, treat acquisition as secondary to proving that MeetSync is indispensable once adopted.

*Note: All percentage targets above are relative to your internal baseline; no external industry benchmarks were used. Ensure your analytics track individual host IDs across sessions to verify the "three meetings" metric accurately.*
