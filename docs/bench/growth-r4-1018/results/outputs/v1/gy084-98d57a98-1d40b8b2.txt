**Subject:** URGENT: Search Bar Test Interpretation & Decision Path (Aug 1–15)

**Executive Summary**
The August 1–15 test moving the search bar to top-center yields conflicting signals: a **2.5s reduction in time-to-find** (positive) paired with an **18% drop in clicks/session** (ambiguous). We cannot currently classify this as a success or failure. The efficiency gain suggests improved discoverability for primary tasks, but the click drop creates a high risk that users are abandoning sessions early or failing to access secondary navigation. Without task completion rates, the "efficiency" hypothesis is `UNVERIFIED`.

**Possible Explanations**
1.  **True Efficiency (Best Case):** Users locate primary features faster and execute them with fewer clicks. The 18% drop represents eliminated friction.
2.  **Premature Abandonment (Worst Case):** The new layout obscures secondary links or confuses users, causing them to leave the site before completing their intent. The time savings reflect shorter sessions, not faster tasks.
3.  **Navigation Blindness:** Users find the primary search but lose context for surrounding features, reducing exploration and cross-selling opportunities.

**Critical Data Gaps**
Our current instrumentation answers "how fast" and "how many clicks," but not "did they succeed?" We lack:
*   **Task Completion Rate:** Did users finish their intended workflow?
*   **Exit Rate on Landing:** Did bounce rates increase for specific entry cohorts?
*   **Secondary Link Engagement:** Specific click-through rates on non-search navigation elements.

**Recommendations & Immediate Next Steps**
Do not roll out to 2M users yet. The risk of widespread abandonment outweighs the unproven efficiency gain. Execute the following before Friday's sprint planning:

1.  **Define & Query Completion (Quantitative):**
    *   *Action:* Immediately query the analytics platform for "Task Completion Rate" (defined as reaching the [Specific Success Page/Event]) segmented by test variant.
    *   *Decision Rule:* If completion rates dropped >2% in the test group, **revert immediately**. If stable or improved, proceed to step 2.
    *   *Metric:* Completion Rate %; Confidence Interval.

2.  **Analyze Exit Paths (Quantitative):**
    *   *Action:* Compare exit rates on the homepage and primary landing pages between variants.
    *   *Decision Rule:* If exit rates rose significantly (>5%) for the test group, the layout is causing drop-offs. **Do not ship.**

3.  **Rapid User Validation (Qualitative):**
    *   *Action:* Conduct 5 rapid remote usability sessions (30 mins each) focusing on users who exhibited low click counts in the test variant. Ask: "Did you find everything you needed?"
    *   *Goal:* Confirm if low clicks equal satisfaction or confusion.

**Decision Framework for Friday**
*   **Ship Default:** Only if Task Completion is flat/up AND Exit Rates are flat.
*   **Iterate Layout:** If Completion is flat but Secondary Link clicks are down (fix visibility, re-test).
*   **Revert:** If Task Completion is down OR Exit Rates are up.

**Required Artifact by Thursday EOD:** A dashboard view showing Task Completion % and Exit Rate % split by variant A/B. Without these figures, the 2.5s speed gain is vanity.

***

*Note: The 18% click reduction is treated as `UNVERIFIED` regarding user satisfaction until completion rates confirm intent was met. Do not rely on the time-saved metric alone as a proxy for value.*
