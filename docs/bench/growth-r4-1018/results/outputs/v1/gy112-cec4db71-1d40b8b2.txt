**Recommendation: Adopt "Verified Yield per Successful Cycle" as the North Star Metric**

For GreenStack's Q3 roadmap, the single primary KPI must be **Verified Yield per Successful Cycle** (defined as harvestable biomass meeting quality specifications divided by the number of initiated grow cycles).

**Why this over System Uptime:**
Your February churn data provides the definitive evidence: three major clients left despite 99.8% uptime because harvest quality failed. System uptime is a **vanity metric** in this context; it measures system availability, not customer value. A farm can be "up" 100% of the time while producing unusable crops due to subtle environmental drifts (humidity, light spectrum, nutrient balance) that uptime monitors do not catch. Focusing on uptime aligns engineering to keep servers running but decouples them from the agronomic reality that pays the bills.

**Verified Yield** forces alignment. It only rises when:
1.  The system is operational (Engineering's domain).
2.  The environment is optimized for biological output (Agronomy's domain).
3.  The output meets market quality standards (Customer Value).

If the system is up but the crop fails, the metric drops. If the crop is high quality but the system crashes mid-cycle, the metric drops. This creates the unified goal your CEO needs.

**Trade-offs and Seasonal Variations:**
*   **Focusing on Uptime:** Risks "false positives" where teams celebrate stability while customers churn due to poor quality. It ignores seasonal variations in crop difficulty; maintaining 99.9% uptime in winter is operationally different than summer, but uptime alone doesn't reflect the *value* delivered during those harder cycles.
*   **Focusing on Verified Yield:** Acknowledges seasonal variance naturally. If winter cycles inherently produce lower yields due to light conditions, the metric reflects this reality rather than masking it with high uptime scores. The trade-off is that this metric is noisier and requires robust data instrumentation to define "verified" (quality checks) accurately. It shifts the burden from simple binary monitoring (up/down) to complex outcome tracking.

**Operational Efficiency Guardrail:**
To ensure operational efficiency is not ignored while chasing yield, establish **Energy Cost per Unit of Verified Yield** as a strict guardrail metric. If Verified Yield rises but energy costs spike unsustainably, the initiative is paused. This prevents agronomy from maximizing output at the expense of unit economics.

This metric stops the debate by making customer value the only path to a "win" for both teams.
