**Executive Summary: CloudVault Pricing Experiment (Weeks 1–6)**

**Current Status:** The experiment shows a **15% increase in subscriber volume** but an **8% decline in Monthly Recurring Revenue (MRR)** versus the control. This indicates immediate revenue cannibalization: users who previously paid the higher monthly rate are switching to the discounted annual plan.

**Key Risks**
1.  **Immediate Cash Flow Deficit:** The 8% MRR drop is a verified, realized loss. The assumption that Lifetime Value (LTV) will recover this gap is currently **UNVERIFIED** because the annual plan lacks historical churn data.
2.  **Cannibalization Without Retention Gain:** If the annual subscribers do not exhibit significantly lower churn than the monthly cohort, the discount represents pure margin erosion with no long-term upside.
3.  **Premature Decisioning:** Six weeks is insufficient to measure the primary hypothesis (reduced attrition over 12 months). Declaring a "win" based on signup volume or a "loss" based solely on short-term MRR ignores the time-value of money and churn dynamics.

**Modeling the Break-Even Point**
To justify the upfront discount, the reduction in churn must mathematically offset the revenue loss. We cannot assert a specific break-even date without internal churn baselines, but the logic requires:
*   **The Cost:** The total discount given upfront per converted user.
*   **The Gain:** The marginal revenue retained from users who *would have churned* on a monthly plan but stay on the annual plan.
*   **The Threshold:** The annual cohort's churn rate must be low enough that the extended tenure generates more total revenue than the monthly cohort's higher rate over the same period. Until we observe at least one full annual cycle or a statistically significant divergence in month-3/month-6 retention curves, the LTV uplift remains a projection, not a fact.

**Recommendation for Q4 Budget Allocation**
**Decision: Continue with Guardrails (Do not pause, do not expand).**
Pausing now wastes the acquisition spend already incurred and forfeits the data needed to validate the LTV hypothesis. However, expanding spend is unjustified while MRR is negative.

**Required Actions for Next Week:**
1.  **Establish a Hard Guardrail:** Set a maximum allowable MRR decline (e.g., -10%). If the gap widens beyond this threshold in the next two weeks, trigger an automatic pause to prevent unchecked bleed.
2.  **Define the Read-Out Schedule:** Pre-commit to reviewing retention cohorts at **Day 90** and **Day 180**. Do not make a final "ship/kill" decision before the Day 90 read, as early churn noise is high.
3.  **Instrument the Specific Question:** Ensure analytics track "Plan Type" as a persistent property to compare **Month-3 Retention** between Annual vs. Monthly cohorts specifically. If Month-3 retention for annual users is not statistically higher than monthly users by the next review, the LTV hypothesis is failing.

**Conclusion:** The board should authorize continued operation under strict MRR guardrails. The budget allocation must account for a temporary revenue dip while purchasing the data necessary to prove or disprove the long-term retention benefit.
