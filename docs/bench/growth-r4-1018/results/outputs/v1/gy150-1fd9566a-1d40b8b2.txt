**Recommendation: Define Retention via "Trip-Cohorts" and "Return-Traveler Rate"**

Do not use a standard 30-day rolling retention metric; for LinguaGo, this will mathematically guarantee a "failure" reading because your product utility is inherently transient. A user who uses the app daily for 14 days in Thailand and then stops is a **success**, not a churned user. Measuring them against a 30-day window misclassifies completed value as lost retention.

**1. The New Definition of "Retained"**
Shift from time-based windows to **event-based cohorts tied to travel cycles**.
*   **Primary Metric:** "Trip Completion Rate." A user is "retained" if they remain active throughout their declared or inferred trip duration (e.g., Days 1–14 of their stay).
*   **Secondary Metric:** "Return-Traveler Rate." The percentage of users who reactivate the app for a *new* trip (either to Thailand again or a new location) within 12 months. This is your true long-term retention signal.

**2. The Measurement Model**
Implement a **Trip-Cohort Analysis** instead of standard monthly cohorts:
*   **Cohort Start:** Date of first use in a specific country (e.g., "Thailand Dec 2023 Arrivals").
*   **Active Window:** Days 0 to *Trip End Date* (estimated via last active day or flight data if available).
*   **Success Criteria:** High daily active usage *during* the window; expected drop to zero *after* the window.
*   **Re-engagement Signal:** A "null" period followed by a new "active" event >60 days later indicates a successful re-engagement, not a retention leak.

**3. Justification for Marketing Budget**
To justify the re-engagement email campaign, do not argue based on fixing "churn." Argue based on **Customer Lifetime Value (CLV) expansion**:
*   **Current State:** You capture value once per trip.
*   **Opportunity:** If 10% of past users travel again annually, a targeted campaign triggering 3 months before peak season (September/October) can reactivate this dormant base.
*   **The Pitch:** "Our 'churn' is actually 'trip completion.' The budget is not to fix a leaky bucket, but to refill it by converting one-time travelers into recurring multi-trip users."

**4. Immediate Actions for the Head of Marketing**
*   **Segment the Data:** Isolate users whose last active date was >45 days ago but who had >5 days of prior activity. Label this group "Completed Travelers," not "Churned."
*   **Test the Hypothesis:** Run a small A/B test on this "Completed" segment.
    *   *Control:* No email.
    *   *Treatment:* "Planning your next adventure? Get 20% off Premium for your next trip."
    *   *Success Metric:* Reactivation rate (new session start) within 30 days of email.
*   **Forecast:** If the test shows a 5% reactivation rate, multiply that by your total "Completed Traveler" base and the average revenue per trip to calculate the potential ROI of the full campaign.

**Note:** Ensure your event taxonomy tags every session with a `trip_id` or `location_context` property to automate this cohorting; without this tag, you cannot distinguish between a user who left the country and one who simply abandoned the app.
**Note:** Do not benchmark these rates against SaaS industry standards (e.g., "good retention is 40%"); compare only against LinguaGo's own historical trip-completion rates.
