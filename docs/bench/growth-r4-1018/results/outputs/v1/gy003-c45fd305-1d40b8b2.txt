**Recommendation: Prioritize the Restaurant Selection-to-Menu Transition**

**Decision:** Allocate the Q3 engineering budget exclusively to optimizing the drop-off between restaurant selection and menu viewing. Do not prioritize checkout flow optimization at this time.

**Rationale & Impact Projection**
Your data indicates a structural leak in the top of the funnel that renders checkout optimizations inefficient. With 5,000 daily orders and a 10% abandonment rate at payment, you are losing approximately **556 orders daily** at checkout (assuming the 10% applies to those who reach that stage). However, the 60% drop-off after restaurant selection suggests that for every 100 users who pick a restaurant, only 40 proceed to view a menu.

If we assume your current 5,000 daily orders represent the 40% who successfully pass the menu stage, your total daily "restaurant selectors" is roughly 12,500. This means you are losing **7,500 potential sessions daily** before a menu is even viewed. Even a conservative 10% relative improvement in this specific transition (reducing the drop-off from 60% to 54%) would recover **750 additional daily orders**. This single gain exceeds your total current daily loss at checkout (556 orders). Pouring resources into checkout now is optimizing a bucket with a hole in the bottom; you must plug the upstream leak first to make downstream fixes valuable.

**Addressing the Hypothesis**
While menu load time is a valid suspect, the "overwhelming choice" hypothesis must be tested simultaneously as it requires different solutions (curation vs. performance). The recommendation is to run A/B tests on the selection screen logic before touching payment infrastructure.

**Key Performance Indicators (KPIs) to Monitor**
To measure success, track these three metrics specifically for the **Restaurant Selection → Menu View** step:

1.  **Primary Metric:** *Menu View Rate* (Percentage of users who click a restaurant and successfully load the menu within 5 seconds).
    *   *Target:* Increase from current ~40% to 45% by end of Q3.
2.  **Guardrail Metric:** *Average Order Value (AOV)* of recovered users.
    *   *Reason:* Ensure that simplifying choices or speeding up loads does not attract low-intent users who browse but never order.
3.  **Diagnostic Metric:** *Time-to-Interactive (TTI)* vs. *Scroll Depth* on the restaurant list.
    *   *Reason:* If TTI is low but scroll depth is shallow, the issue is choice overload. If TTI is high, the issue is performance.

**Expected Outcome by End of Q3**
By shifting focus to the selection screen, you project an increase in total order volume from 5,000 to approximately **5,750 daily orders** (a 15% lift), assuming a modest 10-15% relative improvement in the conversion of the largest drop-off segment. This yields significantly higher revenue impact than maximizing the already efficient 90% checkout completion rate.
