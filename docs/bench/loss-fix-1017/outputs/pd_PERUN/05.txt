### Evidence Structure: Enterprise Satisfaction Split

**1. The Data Signal (Verified)**
*   **Segment A (<50 employees):** Satisfaction 89/100. Hypothesis: Simpler workflows match current feature set.
*   **Segment B (>500 employees):** Satisfaction 61/100. Hypothesis: Mismatch in complexity, features, or adoption.
*   **Business Context:** Segment B drives ~70% of revenue but represents a minority of the 3,200 total customers.
*   **Risk:** High revenue concentration in the lowest-satisfaction segment creates churn risk that outweighs the high scores of smaller, lower-revenue accounts.

**2. The Riskiest Assumption Gate**
*   **Assumption:** "Enterprise dissatisfaction is solvable via better support/onboarding rather than requiring fundamental product changes."
*   **Status:** Untested. Building a "better onboarding" program before validating the root cause is a potential waste of Q3 resources if the core product lacks enterprise-grade features (e.g., granular permissions, audit logs, cross-project dependencies).
*   **Required Test:** Qualitative discovery with 5–7 churned or at-risk enterprise accounts.
    *   *Method:* "Mom Test" interviews focusing on past specific behaviors, not future desires. Ask: "Walk me through the last time your team struggled to launch a project," not "Do you need more training?"
    *   *Kill Threshold:* If >3 of 5 interviews cite missing *capabilities* (e.g., "We couldn't enforce approval workflows") rather than *confusion* (e.g., "We didn't know where the button was"), the "support-only" hypothesis is disproven.

**3. Investigation Plan for Q3 Priorities**
Do not prioritize based on the assumption. Execute these three steps to generate the evidence needed for the executive case:

*   **Step A: Transactional Deep-Dive (Quantitative)**
    *   Cross-reference the 61/100 score with specific feature usage logs for enterprise tenants.
    *   *Question:* Are low-scorers using advanced features at lower rates than high-scorers? This distinguishes "unused features" from "missing features."
*   **Step B: The "Switch" Interviews (Qualitative)**
    *   Interview 5 enterprise admins who recently downgraded or cancelled.
    *   *Focus:* The "push" factors. What specific workflow broke? Did they switch to a competitor? If so, what specific capability did the competitor have that Solstice lacked?
*   **Step C: Support Ticket Analysis**
    *   Tag the last 200 enterprise support tickets as "How-to" (training gap) vs. "Can't do X" (feature gap).
    *   *Metric:* If >40% are "Can't do X," the investment must be product engineering, not customer success.
