**Recommendation: Do not build a native mobile app yet. Prioritize fixing the mobile web experience first.**

**Evidence & Analysis**
Your current data validates a **problem** (clunky mobile web causing a 22% drop in completion), but it does not validate the **solution** (a native app). The 38% mobile session rate indicates users are *trying* to use your product on phones, likely out of necessity, not necessarily that they desire a separate installable binary.

**The Riskiest Assumption**
Your critical assumption is: *"Mobile users want a native app enough to download it, rather than just wanting the current website to work better."*
This assumption is currently **untested**. Building a native app now risks spending 18 engineer-months (6 engineers × 3 months) on a solution users may not adopt, while the known friction (mobile web bugs) remains unresolved.

**Proposed Action Plan**
1.  **Fix the Known Leak (Immediate):** Dedicate engineering resources to resolving the specific mobile web friction points causing the 22% completion drop. This is the cheapest, fastest way to recover lost value from your existing 4,560 monthly mobile sessions.
2.  **Run a "Fake Door" Test (Before Native Build):** Before writing native code, test demand for an app.
    *   Add a prominent "Get the NoteStream App" button in your mobile web menu.
    *   **Metric:** Track the click-through rate (CTR).
    *   **Kill Threshold:** If fewer than 5–10% of mobile users click the button, you likely lack the demand to justify a native build. If they click, show a "Coming Soon" signup page to capture emails.
3.  **Interview Mobile Users:** Conduct 5–10 "Mom Test" interviews with users who visited via mobile but failed to complete a note. Ask about their past behaviors: *"When was the last time you downloaded a new app for note-taking?"* and *"What specifically stopped you from finishing your note today?"* Do not ask *"Would you use an app?"* (hypothetical); ask what they *have done* (behavioral).

**Decision Rule**
*   **Build Native Only If:** The fake-door test shows high intent (>10% CTR) AND interviews reveal specific needs that mobile web physically cannot meet (e.g., offline access, camera integration) that users explicitly say they pay for or switch apps for.
*   **Stick to Mobile Web If:** Users primarily complain about speed, layout, or bugs. These are web problems, not platform problems.

**Summary for Planning Meeting**
We have evidence of mobile *friction*, not mobile *app demand*. The highest ROI move is to fix the 22% completion drop on the web immediately. We will validate native app demand with a zero-code fake-door test before committing 3 months of engineering velocity.
