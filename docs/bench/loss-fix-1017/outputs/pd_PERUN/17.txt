**Recommendation: Do not choose a model yet. Run a "Riskiest-Assumption Gate" test first.**

Your current data is insufficient to decide between sales-led and self-serve because your sample size (5 customers) is too small and skewed (80% high-touch). Your core assumption—that mid-size companies *expect* sales relationships at this price point—is unverified. Building a sales team based on this assumption risks high fixed costs if the market is shifting toward self-serve. Conversely, investing heavily in product-led onboarding based on one outlier may waste resources if that customer was unique.

**The Decision Framework:**
You must validate the "Sales Expectation" assumption before committing capital.

**1. The Riskiest Assumption:**
"Companies with 50–500 employees will not adopt a $800/mo compliance tool without a direct sales relationship and white-glove onboarding."

**2. The Cheap Test (Behavioral, not Hypothetical):**
Do not ask potential customers what they *prefer* (they will lie to be polite). Instead, run a **Fake-Door / Concierge Test** on new inbound leads or a targeted list of 20 prospects.
*   **Variant A (Self-Serve Path):** Drive traffic to a landing page with clear pricing ($800/mo) and a "Start Free Trial" button that leads to an automated onboarding flow (no human contact offered).
*   **Variant B (Sales Path):** Drive traffic to a page with the same pricing but only a "Book a Demo" call-to-action.

**3. Metrics & Kill Thresholds (Define these numbers before starting):**
*   **Metric:** Conversion rate from landing page visit to active setup (credit card entered or demo booked).
*   **Proposed Kill Threshold:** If fewer than **2 out of 20** (10%) prospects in the Self-Serve path complete setup without human help, the assumption holds: the market currently demands sales. If **5 or more** (25%) succeed, the self-serve model is viable.
*   **Deadline:** Run this test over 14 days.

**4. Immediate Action Plan:**
*   **Pause Hiring:** Do not hire account executives yet.
*   **Pause Major Product Refactor:** Do not rebuild onboarding yet.
*   **Execute Test:** Direct your next 20 qualified leads to the two variants above. Track exactly how many drop off when human help is removed.
*   **Interview Drop-offs:** For those who abandon the self-serve flow, conduct a "Mom Test" interview asking about their *past* behavior with similar tools, not their feelings about your tool. Ask: "Tell me about the last compliance tool you bought. How did you buy it? Who was involved?"

**Decision Rule:**
*   **If Self-Serve fails the threshold:** Pursue a sales-led model. Your "hand-holding" is currently a feature, not a bug. Productize the onboarding checklist you already use manually.
*   **If Self-Serve passes the threshold:** Invest in automating your existing 12-hour onboarding process. This is your scalable engine.

**Note:** Your single self-serve customer is a data point, not a trend. Treat them as a case study to understand *why* they succeeded (e.g., specific industry, technical sophistication) rather than proof of a general market shift.
**Note:** Do not fabricate market trends; let the 20-prospect test generate the evidence needed for your strategy session.
