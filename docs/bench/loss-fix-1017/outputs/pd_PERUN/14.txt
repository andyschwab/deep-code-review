### Interview Plan: CodeForge Trial Conversion

**Objective:** Validate whether "unclear value perception" is the primary driver of the 70% trial drop-off, without exhausting your 87-user pool.

**1. Who to Interview (Stratified Sampling)**
Do not interview only the "vocal" users or those who just churned. To avoid bias, select a fixed sample of **8 users** divided into three specific groups based on behavior, not availability:

*   **Group A: The "Almost" Converts (3 users)**
    *   *Criteria:* Used the tool for 10–13 days, engaged with core features, but did not convert.
    *   *Why:* They saw the most value short of paying. Their reasoning reveals the final friction point.
*   **Group B: The Early Drop-offs (3 users)**
    *   *Criteria:* Signed up but stopped using the tool within 3 days.
    *   *Why:* They test your "value perception" hypothesis immediately. If they didn't understand the value, the problem is onboarding/messaging, not product gaps.
*   **Group C: The Converted Paid Users (2 users)**
    *   *Criteria:* Successfully converted within the last 30 days.
    *   *Why:* You need a baseline. What specific moment made *them* decide to pay? This contrasts against Groups A and B to isolate the missing trigger.

**Selection Method:** Use your analytics database to filter users matching these criteria. Randomly select the specific names from each filter bucket. Do not let Customer Success or Sales recommend names, as this introduces "availability bias."

**2. How Many & Fatigue Management**
*   **Total Interviews:** 8.
*   **Pool Impact:** This represents ~9% of your total user base, leaving 79 users untouched for future validation testing. This is sustainable.
*   **Cadence:** Schedule 2 per day over 4 days. Do not batch them all in one week if possible; spread them out to allow for reflection between sessions.

**3. Interview Focus (The "Mom Test" Approach)**
Do not ask "Did you find value?" or "Was the pricing clear?" (Users will lie to be nice). Instead, ask about past specific behaviors:
*   "Walk me through the last time you tried to solve [problem CodeForge solves] before using us."
*   "What specifically happened in your workflow on Day 2 that made you stop/open the tool?"
*   "When you decided to pay (or not), what was the specific conversation or metric you looked at?"

**4. Decision Rule**
*   **If 5+ of 8 users** cite confusion about *what* the tool does or *how* to get started: Your hypothesis (unclear value perception) is confirmed. Focus on onboarding/messaging fixes.
*   **If 5+ of 8 users** cite specific missing features or integration failures: Your hypothesis is false. The issue is product gaps, not perception.

**Note:** Record these sessions. Do not take notes only; you need exact quotes to show your team.
**Next Step:** Once these 8 are complete, stop interviewing and design a cheap experiment (e.g., a modified onboarding email sequence) to test the finding on the remaining 79 users.
