### Riskiest Assumption Gate & Testing Roadmap

Your hypothesis that **booking fee tolerance** is the riskiest assumption is plausible, but "risk" is a function of **Impact × Uncertainty**. An assumption that kills the business model if wrong (high impact) but is already clearly false based on your 47 bookings (low uncertainty) is not the *riskiest*—it's already validated or invalidated. The riskiest assumption is the one with high impact that you currently have **no behavioral evidence** for.

Based on your metrics (340 hosts, 90 chefs, only 47 bookings), the conversion rate is critically low (~14% of hosts book, ~52% of chefs get booked). This suggests a friction point earlier in the funnel than the fee. Before testing the fee, you must validate **desirability** and **viability** of the core exchange.

**Prioritized Testing Plan (Pre-Board Meeting)**

| Rank | Assumption | Why It's Risky (Impact/Uncertainty) | Cheapest Behavioral Test | Metric & Kill Threshold |
| :--- | :--- | :--- | :--- :--- |
| **1** | **Chefs want this type of gig (Viability)** | If chefs don't actively seek/manage these gigs, supply dries up, killing the marketplace regardless of host demand. You have 90 signups but don't know their intent vs. curiosity. | **Concierge Test:** Manually match 5 interested chefs with 5 hosts. Ask chefs to set a menu and price *before* matching. | **Metric:** % of signed-up chefs who complete a full profile and set a price.<br>**Kill Threshold:** If <20% of contacted chefs set a price within 48 hours, the supply model fails. |
| **2** | **Hosts care more about cuisine than price (Desirability)** | If price is the primary driver, your "private chef" value prop collapses into a commodity catering service, destroying margins. | **Fake Door Test:** On the landing page, show two identical chef profiles differing only by price and cuisine specificity. Track clicks. | **Metric:** Click-through rate (CTR) on high-price/specific-cuisine vs. low-price/generic.<br>**Kill Threshold:** If high-price option CTR is <30% of low-price option, price is the dominant factor. |
| **3** | **Hosts will pay a 15% booking fee (Monetization)** | Your hypothesis. If false, unit economics break. But if #1 or #2 fails, this doesn't matter. Test only after supply/demand fit is proven. | **Pre-sale Test:** During the concierge match, explicitly state the 15% fee in the contract before the host confirms. | **Metric:** % of matched hosts who sign and pay despite the fee.<br>**Kill Threshold:** If >40% of matched hosts drop off at the fee disclosure, the model is unviable. |

**Immediate Action:**
Do not build new features (photos, advanced scheduling) until **Assumption #1** is cleared. Your current bottleneck is likely supply activation or fundamental demand mismatch, not the fee structure.
