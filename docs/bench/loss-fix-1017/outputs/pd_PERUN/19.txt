**Recommendation: Run a "Fake-Door" Pricing Test Before Building**

Do not commit engineering time yet. Your riskiest assumption is not technical feasibility, but **willingness to pay**. The cheapest test to disconfirm this is a behavioral experiment where users attempt to purchase the feature before it exists.

**The Test Plan**
1.  **Setup (3 days):** Add an "AI Meeting Summaries" toggle in your settings for eligible accounts (e.g., teams with >4 recurring meetings).
2.  **The Trigger:** When a user enables it, present a modal: *"This feature requires an upgrade to the Pro Plan ($25/mo) or a $10/mo add-on."* Include a "Start Free Trial" or "Upgrade" button.
3.  **The Catch:** If they click to upgrade, show a message: *"We are rolling this out gradually. You've been added to the priority waitlist, and we'll notify you when your account is enabled."* **Do not charge them yet.**
4.  **Metric:** Track the **conversion rate** of eligible users who click the upgrade button.
5.  **Kill Threshold:** If fewer than **5%** of exposed eligible users attempt to upgrade within two weeks, the willingness to pay is likely too low to justify the $4,000/mo compute cost and 2-month dev cycle. Drop or pivot the idea. If >10% convert, proceed to a concierge pilot.

**Why this works:** It measures actual behavior (clicking to pay) rather than stated intent (saying they want it). It validates the price point specifically, addressing your uncertainty about whether this should be free or paid.

**Next Steps for Wednesday's Review**
*   **Decision Rule:** Agree on the specific conversion % that justifies the build.
*   **Scope Defense:** Explicitly mark "full automated pipeline" as a **non-goal** for now; the only goal is validating the price signal.

*Note: Do not fabricate interview quotes or market sizes to support this; the test results will provide the only evidence that matters.*
