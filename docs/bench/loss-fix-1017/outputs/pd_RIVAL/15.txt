**Recommendation: Do Not Invest in Full Localization Yet**

Based on the data provided, proceeding with a $90,000, four-month investment is too risky. The current evidence (14 requests out of 1,200 customers) represents only 1.2% of your base, which is insufficient to validate the assumption that this is the "tip of the iceberg." Before committing engineering resources, you must de-risk the **Value** and **Viability** assumptions through low-cost experiments.

**Immediate Action Plan:**

1.  **Validate Demand (Low Cost):** Contact the 14 requesting prospects directly. Do not ask if they *would* use a localized version; ask what specific barriers the English interface created for them and what revenue they lost because of it. Quantify the potential contract value of these 14 leads specifically.
2.  **Test Willingness to Pay (No Code):** Create a simple landing page or email campaign targeting similar retailers in Germany, Japan, and Brazil describing the localized features and multi-currency support with a "Join Waitlist" or "Pre-order" button. Measure conversion rates to see if interest extends beyond the initial 14.
3.  **Assess Opportunity Cost:** Calculate the potential revenue gain from deepening English-market features over the same four-month period. Compare this against the speculative revenue from the non-English markets.

**Decision Criteria for Future Investment:**
Only proceed with the $90,000 build if you can secure:
*   Signed Letters of Intent (LOIs) or pre-commitments from at least 5–7 of the 14 prospects covering a significant portion of the development cost.
*   Data showing that localization would increase conversion rates in these markets by a factor that yields ROI within 12 months.
*   Confirmation that delaying English-market enhancements will not cause churn among your existing 1,200 customers.

Until these conditions are met, the safest strategic move is to maintain the English-only product while running these validation tests.
