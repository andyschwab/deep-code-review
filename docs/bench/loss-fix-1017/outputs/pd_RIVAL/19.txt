### Recommendation: Pre-Sell Validation Experiment

Do not commit engineering time yet. Your core risk is **Viability** (willingness to pay), not Feasibility. Since you have direct access to 2,800 active users, run a "Fake Door" or Concierge test to validate revenue before building.

**Step 1: Segment & Recruit (Days 1-2)**
Filter your user base for teams with recurring meetings >45 minutes. Select 50–100 power users. Send a personal email from you (the Product Lead):
> "We're exploring a new AI Summary feature that automatically generates action items and transcripts for long meetings. It would be an add-on at $10/month. We aren't building it yet, but if we did, would you add it to your plan? Click here to reserve your spot."

**Step 2: The "Paywall" Test (Days 3-7)**
The link should lead to a landing page describing the benefit (not the tech). Include two buttons:
1.  **"Add to Plan for $10/mo"** (Leads to a checkout flow or a "Thank you, we'll charge you when launched" confirmation).
2.  **"Not interested right now."**

*Crucial:* Do not build the backend. If they click "Add," capture the intent. If you want stronger signal, ask for a refundable $1 deposit.

**Step 3: Measure & Decide (Day 8)**
Calculate the conversion rate.
*   **Success Metric:** If >5–8% of targeted users commit to paying, the $4,000/mo cost is likely covered by ~400 subscribers ($4,000 revenue), which is ~14% of your total base. This validates the business case.
*   **Failure Signal:** If <2% commit, the perceived value doesn't match the price point. Pivot to including a limited version in the base plan or shelve the feature.

**Why this works:**
This tests actual behavior (opening a wallet) rather than opinions ("Would you use this?"). It costs $0 in engineering time and provides concrete data for your CEO: "X% of users committed to paying $Y, projecting $Z in ARR."

**Alternative Low-Fi Test:**
If a landing page feels too heavy, offer a "Concierge" service to 10 customers: manually transcribe and summarize one meeting for them using existing tools, then ask if they'd pay $10/mo to automate it. Their reaction to the manual deliverable predicts their willingness to pay for the automated one.

Present these findings next Wednesday as a validated learning outcome, not a feature request.

*Note: Ensure your terms of service allow for testing pricing tiers before charging.*
*Focus on the "commitment" metric, not just click-through rates.*
