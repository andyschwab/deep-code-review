### Evidence Gathering Plan & Recommendation

**1. Immediate Evidence to Gather (Next 7 Days)**
*   **Cohort Analysis of Cancellations:** Segment the 34% spike by customer tenure. Specifically, isolate customers who renewed annually *before* March 14 versus those who are mid-cycle. If the spike is predominantly mid-cycle customers canceling early, your assumption about the pricing page triggering preemptive churn is likely correct.
*   **Exit Survey Data:** Deploy a mandatory one-question survey upon cancellation: "What is the primary reason for canceling today?" with options: "Price increase observed," "Found a cheaper alternative," "Lack of usage/value," or "Budget cuts." Look for the correlation between "Price increase observed" and the March 14 timeline.
*   **Competitive Win/Loss Data:** Consult Sales immediately. Have they lost recent deals to specific competitors citing lower prices? If sales are losing *new* logos to cheaper rivals, existing customers are likely aware of these alternatives.
*   **Support Ticket Sentiment:** Scan support logs for keywords like "price," "increase," "competitor," or "renewal cost" in the last 60 days.

**2. Strategic Recommendation: Wait vs. Adjust**
*   **Do not adjust pricing yet.** A reactive price rollback signals instability to the CFO and devalues the product.
*   **Wait for the data outlined above (7–10 days).**
    *   **Scenario A (Value Perception):** If exit surveys cite "lack of value" or "budget cuts" rather than direct price comparisons, the issue is not the competitor's price but your communicated value. *Action:* Launch a value-reinforcement campaign before renewal dates.
    *   **Scenario B (Competitor Switching):** If data confirms customers are leaving specifically for a lower-cost competitor, *then* consider a targeted retention offer (e.g., a locked-in rate for a 2-year extension) rather than a broad price cut.

Present the findings as a validated hypothesis. If the data confirms the "preemptive cancellation" theory, propose a "Retention Bridge" pilot: offer at-risk annual customers a one-time incentive to renew early at their current rate, measuring the reduction in churn against the cost of the discount. This shows the CFO a controlled experiment rather than a panic reaction.
