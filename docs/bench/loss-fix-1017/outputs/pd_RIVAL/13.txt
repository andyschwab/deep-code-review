### Recommendation: Do Not Revert Yet; Run a Targeted Diagnostic

**Decision:** Do not revert the free-tier limit change immediately. Two weeks of data is insufficient to distinguish between a sustained behavioral shift and a short-term adjustment period, especially given the confounding variable of the landing page redesign. Reverting now would invalidate the experiment and delay learning.

**Immediate Action Plan (Before Friday):**
1.  **Segment the Data:** Isolate users who hit the 200-query limit within their first week versus those who did not. If the activation drop is concentrated *only* among users who hit the cap, your assumption is likely correct. If activation dropped across *all* users (including those with <50 queries), the landing page redesign or another factor is the primary culprit.
2.  **Analyze the "Cliff":** Check the drop-off rate specifically at query #200 compared to the historical drop-off at #500. Did users stop entirely, or did they struggle to find value earlier?
3.  **Qualitative Pulse:** Quickly survey 5–10 recent churned users who hit the limit. Ask: "What prevented you from running more queries?" rather than "Was the limit too low?" to uncover if the issue is volume or relevance.

**Strategy:**
Present a "Hold and Measure" proposal to the growth team. Commit to monitoring the activation rate for two more weeks (through April 1) to see if the metric stabilizes as users adapt to the new constraint. If the segmented data shows the limit is the sole driver of churn for high-intent users, propose a targeted intervention (e.g., a one-time bonus query pack for users who hit the limit) rather than a full revert. This preserves the monetization hypothesis while mitigating the activation risk.

*Note: Ensure your analytics can distinguish between traffic sources to rule out the landing page redesign as the primary cause before finalizing your conclusion.*
