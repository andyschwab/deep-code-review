**Interpretation & Recommendation for FlowBoard Growth Team**

The February 2024 cohort anomaly (22% D60 retention vs. historic 38–42%) is almost certainly a **composition shift**, not a product degradation. Since onboarding, pricing, and core acquisition channels remained constant, the 15% signup spike from the February 8 launch likely diluted the cohort with users possessing lower inherent intent or different use cases than your organic baseline. Launch-driven traffic often includes "curiosity" users who lack the immediate pain point required for long-term workflow automation adoption, naturally resulting in a lower retention floor.

If this were a product bug or a broken onboarding step introduced in February, you would expect to see a similar degradation in March and April cohorts acquired through the same channels. If those subsequent cohorts have returned to the 38–42% range, the product is healthy, and the February dip is purely an artifact of that specific user mix.

**Action Plan:**
1.  **Do not allocate engineering resources** to "fix" retention for this specific cohort; the behavior is likely rational given their lower intent.
2.  **Segment the February cohort immediately.** Isolate the users who arrived via the Feb 8 launch event from those who arrived organically that month. You will likely find the organic subset retains at ~40%, while the launch subset retains at <10%, confirming the hypothesis.
3.  **Adjust forecasting.** Treat the February data as an outlier in LTV calculations. Do not let this single cohort skew your perception of overall product-market fit.
4.  **Future Launch Strategy:** Use this insight to design better qualification filters for future launch traffic. Consider delaying access or adding a "intent survey" step during high-volume spikes to ensure only high-fit users enter the active cohort.

**Conclusion:** This is an acquisition quality issue, not a product failure. No immediate product intervention is required, but marketing segmentation for future launches should be refined.
