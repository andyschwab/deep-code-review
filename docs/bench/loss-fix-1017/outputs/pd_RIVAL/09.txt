**Recommendation: Iterate on the Single-Page Design with a "Review-First" Variant**

Do not revert to the 3-page checkout, nor roll out the current single-page design globally. The 28% increase in add-to-cart rate indicates the single-page flow successfully reduces early friction, a critical metric for growth. However, the drop-off at the final step confirms your hypothesis: users feel rushed or lack trust due to insufficient order review before payment commitment.

The current single-page design likely compresses the "review" phase too aggressively. In a 3-page flow, the dedicated review page acts as a psychological "cooling-off" period where users verify details, building confidence to click "Pay." Removing this step entirely creates anxiety, causing hesitation exactly where you observed it.

**Proposed Next Test: The "Expanded Review" Single-Page Variant**

Run a follow-up A/B test comparing:
1.  **Control:** Current 3-page checkout.
2.  **Variant A (Previous):** The tested single-page design (collapsed review).
3.  **Variant B (New Hypothesis):** A modified single-page design where the order summary is prominently expanded and sticky above the "Pay" button, requiring an explicit "I have reviewed my order" checkbox before the payment button becomes active.

**Success Metrics & Signal Clarity:**
*   **Primary Metric:** Conversion Rate (Sessions to Purchases).
*   **Secondary Metric:** Add-to-Cart rate (to ensure we retain the early friction win).
*   **Guardrail Metric:** Time-on-page for the final step (to ensure users are actually reviewing, not just stuck).

**Why this gives a clearer signal:**
If Variant B outperforms both the Control and Variant A, it validates that the *structure* of a single page is superior, but the *execution* of the review step was the flaw. If Variant B performs similarly to the Control, it suggests that the multi-step mental model is deeply ingrained in your specific user base, and a full single-page collapse may never work regardless of tweaks. This isolates the variable (review visibility) rather than testing the entire flow architecture again.

**Executive Summary for CTO/Marketing:**
"We have validated that simplifying the early flow drives engagement (+28% add-to-cart), but our current single-page implementation sacrifices trust at the final moment. By testing a 'Review-First' single-page variant, we aim to capture the early efficiency gains without compromising final conversion confidence. This targeted iteration minimizes engineering rework while maximizing potential revenue uplift."
