### Prioritized Assumption Testing Roadmap for Bazaar

Based on your current metrics (47 bookings from 430 users) and the need to validate the core business model before the June 15 board meeting, here is the prioritization of your 8 assumptions using an Impact vs. Risk framework.

| Priority | Assumption | Category | Rationale | Recommended Experiment |
| :--- | :--- | :--- | :--- :--- |
| **1** | **Hosts will pay a 15% booking fee** | Viability | **Highest Risk/Impact.** If hosts won't pay this on top of ingredients/venue, revenue model collapses. Your intuition is likely correct; this is the "kill switch." | **Fake Door Test:** Create two landing pages for a specific chef/event. One shows "Total: $X" (fee included), one shows "$X + 15% Service Fee." Drive equal traffic. Measure conversion to "Request to Book." |
| **2** | **Chefs want this type of gig** | Value/Supply | Without supply, demand is irrelevant. If chefs prefer restaurant stability or find the logistics too heavy, the marketplace fails. | **Concierge Test:** Manually recruit 5 chefs for a specific weekend without building software. Ask them to commit to a date/menu. Measure drop-off rate during onboarding vs. actual completion. |
| **3** | **Hosts care more about cuisine than price** | Value | Dictates your sorting algorithm and marketing messaging. If price dominates, you are competing with catering, not experiences. | **A/B Search Test:** Modify search results for a user segment. Group A sorts by "Cuisine Match," Group B by "Lowest Price." Track click-through and booking rates. |
| **4** | **Weekday demand exists** | Market Size | Critical for chef utilization. If only weekends work, supply saturation happens fast, limiting growth. | **Targeted Email Campaign:** Send a "Weeknight Dinner Special" offer to 100 past hosts. Measure open rates and inquiry volume compared to weekend offers. |
| **5** | **Quality photos drive bookings** | Usability/Value | Important for conversion, but secondary to price/supply. Easy to fix if proven true. | **Listing Audit:** Compare booking rates of listings with professional photos vs. smartphone photos. Correlate directly with conversion data you already have. |
| **6** | **Chefs will set their own menus** | Operational | Affects scalability. If chefs need hand-holding, ops costs rise. | **Survey/Interview:** Ask the 90 signed-up chefs: "How long did it take you to finalize your first menu?" and "Would you use pre-set templates?" |
| **7** | **Hosts will book 2+ weeks in advance** | Operational | Impacts chef scheduling and cash flow. | **Data Analysis:** Review the 47 completed bookings. Calculate the average lead time. No new test needed yet; use existing data. |
| **8** | **Repeat bookings will drive growth** | Strategy | Long-term LTV driver, but irrelevant if initial acquisition fails. | **Cohort Analysis:** Track the 340 hosts. How many have booked twice? Defer active testing until single-booking unit economics are proven. |

**Immediate Next Steps:**
1.  **Run the Fake Door Test (Assumption #1) immediately.** This validates your biggest fear regarding the business model.
2.  **Conduct the Concierge Test (Assumption #2) in parallel.** Ensure you actually have a supply side willing to transact.

Present these two experiments as your "Phase 1 Validation" to the board. If either fails, the business model requires a pivot before scaling marketing or features.

*Note: Focus your board presentation on the "Kill Switch" assumptions (1 & 2). Validating these proves you understand where the real existential risks lie.*
