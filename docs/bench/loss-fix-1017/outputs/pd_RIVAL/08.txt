### Validation Plan: Advanced Version History Add-On

**Objective:** Validate willingness to pay and perceived value for advanced version control (branching/comparison) without engineering investment.

**1. Qualitative Discovery (Week 1)**
*   **Target:** The 3 accounts who mentioned iteration struggles + 5 other enterprise admins.
*   **Method:** 30-minute problem-focused interviews.
*   **Script Focus:** Ask about the *last time* they lost work or struggled to merge design versions. Quantify the cost of that error (hours lost, delayed launch).
*   **Key Question:** "Walk me through how you currently track major design changes. What tools or manual processes do you use outside of Lumen?"
*   **Goal:** Confirm the pain is acute enough to warrant a separate budget line item, not just a feature request.

**2. "Fake Door" Pricing Test (Week 2-3)**
*   **Setup:** Add a new tier option in the account settings or billing page labeled "Advanced Version History" at $15/seat/month.
*   **Mechanism:** When users click "Upgrade" or "Learn More," display a modal: *"This feature is coming soon. Join the waitlist to get early access and a founding-member discount."*
*   **Metrics:** Track Click-Through Rate (CTR) on the upgrade button and conversion to waitlist sign-up.
*   **Segmentation:** Show this only to teams with 10+ seats (your target persona) to avoid confusing free-tier users.

**3. Concierge Pre-Sale (Week 3-4)**
*   **Target:** Users who signed up for the waitlist or expressed high interest in interviews.
*   **Offer:** Manual implementation. Offer to manually manage their branching/comparison needs using internal tools for one month at the proposed price ($15/seat).
*   **Success Criteria:** Secure 3–5 paying pilot customers. If they won't pay for a manual service solving the exact problem, they won't pay for the automated feature.

**Decision Framework for Leadership:**
*   **Proceed:** If >5% of targeted admins click the upgrade button AND you secure 3 pilot customers.
*   **Pivot:** If clicks are high but pilots fail, the price point or packaging is wrong (re-test at $5/seat or bundle differently).
*   **Kill:** If CTR is <1% and interviewees describe the problem as "minor annoyance" rather than "critical risk."

This approach tests actual behavior (clicks and payments) rather than opinions, minimizing risk before engineering commitment.

*Note: Ensure legal review of the "fake door" copy to comply with consumer protection laws in your region.*
