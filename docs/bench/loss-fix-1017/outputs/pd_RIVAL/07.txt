# Waypoint Q2 Discovery Plan: Validating the Calendar Sync Hypothesis

**Objective**
Determine if lack of calendar sync is the primary driver of churn for the 8 customers who stopped using the app after month one, or if it is a secondary symptom of deeper value/engagement issues.

**Phase 1: Deep-Dive Interviews (Weeks 1-2)**
*Target:* Re-contact the 5 customers who explicitly mentioned calendar sync and the 8 who cited "stopped using."
*Goal:* Uncover the specific job-to-be-done that failed.
*Key Questions (The Mom Test approach):*
*   "Walk me through the last time you tried to schedule a team shift using Waypoint. What happened step-by-step?"
*   "You mentioned calendar sync was missing. Tell me about a specific instance where this caused a problem. What was the immediate consequence?"
*   "Before you canceled, what other methods did you try to solve the scheduling conflict? (e.g., spreadsheets, text messages, manual entry)"
*   "If we had launched calendar sync yesterday, would you still have canceled? Why or why not?" (Only ask this after establishing past behavior).
*   "What was the deciding factor between Waypoint and the cheaper alternative you found?"

**Phase 2: "Fake Door" Validation Experiment (Weeks 3-4)**
*Target:* Current active users who haven't synced calendars yet.
*Goal:* Measure actual demand vs. stated interest without building the full integration.
*Action:* Add a "Sync to Google/Outlook Calendar" button in the settings for a segment of users.
*Measurement:*
*   Click-through rate on the button.
*   Completion rate of the auth flow (if built lightly) or drop-off at a "Coming Soon" message.
*   Follow-up survey to clickers: "What specific problem were you hoping this would solve today?"

**Phase 3: Competitive Tear-Down (Week 5)**
*Action:* The Designer and Founder will sign up for the top 3 "cheaper alternatives" cited in churn interviews.
*Goal:* Map their onboarding flow and calendar features.
*Output:* A comparison matrix highlighting where competitors solve the "first month engagement" drop-off. Is it purely calendar sync, or is their onboarding simpler?

**Decision Criteria (Go/No-Go)**
*   **Build:** If >40% of re-interviewed churned users cite calendar sync as the *sole* blocker to renewal, AND the Fake Door experiment shows >15% click-through among active users.
*   **Pivot:** If users admit they stopped using the app because "scheduling wasn't a priority" or "the team didn't adopt the habit," regardless of sync features.
*   **Stop:** If the cheaper alternatives offer robust sync but users still churn due to price, indicating Waypoint's core value prop is not differentiated enough to command a premium.

**Roles**
*   **Founder:** Lead interviews, analyze competitive pricing.
*   **Product Designer:** Design Fake Door asset, map competitor UX flows.
*   **Co-Founder:** Review experiment data, assess technical feasibility of top 2 calendar integrations.

This plan shifts focus from "building a feature" to "validating the root cause of disengagement" before writing code.

*Note: Prioritize re-interviewing the 8 "stopped using" customers first; their churn reason is most ambiguous and critical to your hypothesis.*
