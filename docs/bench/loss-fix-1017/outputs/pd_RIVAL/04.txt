### Discovery Plan: Validating Enterprise Reporting Demand

**Objective:** Determine if low adoption at Meridian stems from misaligned report content or a lack of fundamental need, and assess if advanced reporting is a viable differentiator for new prospects.

**1. Retrospective Behavioral Interviews (Meridian Corp)**
*Target:* The 44 users who logged in once or never, plus the 3 power users.
*Goal:* Uncover the specific "Jobs to Be Done" without pitching the current dashboard.
*Key Questions:*
*   "Walk me through the last time you needed to analyze expense data for a stakeholder. What specific question were you trying to answer?"
*   "What tools or workarounds did you use to get that answer? (e.g., exporting to Excel, asking finance manually)"
*   "What was the most frustrating part of that process?"
*   "Show me the last report you built or requested. What decision did it inform?"
*Avoid:* Asking "Do you like the new dashboard?" or "Would you use more features?"

**2. Sales Call Shadowing & Prospect Interrogation**
*Target:* The two enterprise prospects currently in the pipeline.
*Goal:* Distinguish between "checkbox" requests and genuine pain.
*Action:* Listen for how they describe their current reporting failures.
*Key Probing:*
*   "You mentioned needing advanced reporting. Tell me about the last time your current solution failed to give you the data you needed."
*   "How much time does your team currently spend manually aggregating this data?"
*   "Have you tried to solve this with other tools? What happened?"

**3. Usage Data Deep Dive (Meridian)**
*Target:* Analytics on the 3 active users.
*Goal:* Identify patterns in successful usage.
*Actions:*
*   Which specific metrics or filters did the 3 active users engage with?
*   Did they export data immediately after viewing? (Indicates the dashboard is a view, not the final destination).
*   Correlate login times with known financial closing cycles.

**4. "Fake Door" or Concierge Test (Optional Validation)**
*Target:* A small segment of non-Meridian enterprise customers.
*Goal:* Test willingness to engage before building.
*Action:* Offer a "custom report build" service manually. If prospects request it but refuse a 15-minute discovery call to define requirements, the demand is likely low. If they eagerly define requirements, the pain is real.

**Success Criteria for Proceeding to Build:**
*   Identification of at least 3 recurring, high-stakes questions that current reports miss.
*   Evidence that prospects are currently spending significant manual effort (time/money) solving this externally.
*   Clear distinction between "nice to have" visibility and "must have" compliance/decision data.

**Timeline:** 2 Weeks (1 week for interviews/data, 1 week for synthesis).
**Output:** A "Reporting Opportunity Brief" detailing specific data gaps, required visualizations, and a go/no-go recommendation based on validated pain, not sales anecdotes.

This plan shifts focus from feature requests to underlying business problems, ensuring engineering resources are only committed to solving verified needs.
