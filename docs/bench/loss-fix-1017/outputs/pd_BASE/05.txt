### Interpretation and Investigation Plan for Solstice Q1 Data

**1. Interpret the Split: A Risky Revenue Concentration**
Your data reveals a critical misalignment: 70% of your revenue depends on a segment (enterprise) that is actively dissatisfied (61/100), while your highest satisfaction (89/100) comes from a minority revenue source. The gap (28 points) is too large to be noise; it indicates a structural mismatch, not just "teething pains." Your assumption that smaller teams have "simpler workflows" is likely only half the truth; the enterprise score suggests your current tool may actively *frustrate* complex workflows rather than just failing to support them.

**2. Immediate Investigation: The "Switch" and "Job" Analysis**
Do not build new features yet. You must distinguish between **missing capabilities** (viability/desirability) and **implementation failure** (usability/onboarding). Run the following targeted research immediately:

*   **Conduct 10 "Switch" Interviews with Enterprise Churn Risks:** Identify 10 enterprise accounts with low scores or recent support tickets. Ask specifically about the *moment* they realized Solstice wasn't working for a specific complex workflow.
    *   *Ask:* "Walk me through the last time you tried to manage a cross-departmental creative launch. What specific step forced you to leave Solstice and use email/Spreadsheets/Slack instead?"
    *   *Goal:* Determine if they left because the feature didn't exist (requires build) or because they couldn't configure the existing feature (requires onboarding/support).
*   **Analyze Support Ticket Tags by Segment:** Correlate the 61/100 score with support volume.
    *   *Hypothesis A:* If tickets are "How do I X?", the issue is onboarding.
    *   *Hypothesis B:* If tickets are "I wish I could Y" or "System limits reached," the issue is missing functionality.
*   **The "Very Disappointed" Filter:** Segment your survey responses. Among enterprise users, ask: "How would you feel if you could no longer use Solstice?" If <40% say "Very Disappointed," you do not have Product-Market Fit in enterprise, regardless of revenue. No amount of onboarding fixes a lack of fit.

**3. Recommendation for Q3 Investment Priorities**
Present two distinct paths to the executive team based on the research findings above. Do not propose a middle ground.

*   **Path A (The Support Play):** If research shows features exist but are unused/misconfigured, invest Q3 in a dedicated Enterprise Success team and guided onboarding flows. *Risk:* Low product change, high operational cost.
*   **Path B (The Product Pivot):** If research shows fundamental workflow gaps (e.g., lack of granular permissions, complex dependency mapping, or resource leveling), pause generic feature work. Dedicate Q3 to an "Enterprise Core" sprint targeting the top 3 workflow blockers identified in interviews. *Risk:* High engineering cost, delays for SMB users.

**Decision Rule:** If the top 3 enterprise complaints are identical across 8/10 interviews, proceed with Path B. If complaints are varied and relate to confusion, proceed with Path A. If interviews reveal they are already evaluating competitors with specific features you lack, Path B is mandatory to protect the 70% revenue base.

*Note: Do not rely on aggregate scores for the final decision; the specific behavioral evidence from the 10 interviews will dictate the ROI of your Q3 spend.*
*Ensure your "non-goals" for Q3 explicitly state what you will NOT build for the SMB segment if you choose Path B, to prevent scope creep.*
