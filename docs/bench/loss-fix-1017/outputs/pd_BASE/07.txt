# Waypoint Q2 Discovery Plan: Validating the Calendar Sync Hypothesis

## 1. The Riskiest Assumption Gate
**Assumption:** "Lack of calendar sync is the primary driver of churn for the 8 customers who stopped using the app, and fixing it will retain them despite cheaper alternatives."

**The Test:** A "Fake Door" or Concierge test on current at-risk users (not churned ones).
*   **Action:** Identify 10 active users who have *not* yet synced a calendar but are in their first month. Offer a manual "concierge" sync (you manually add their events) or a beta toggle for the feature.
*   **Metric:** Adoption rate of the sync offer and retention at Day 30 for those who accept vs. those who don't.
*   **Kill Threshold:** If fewer than **4 out of 10** offered users accept the sync solution, OR if those who accept still churn by Day 30 at the same rate as non-syncers, **do not build the automated integration.** The hypothesis is false; the root cause is likely value perception or onboarding, not the missing feature.
*   **Deadline:** 3 weeks.

## 2. Interview Re-Analysis (Structured Evidence)
Your current data is ambiguous because the interviews were semi-structured. Before building, re-contact the **5 customers** who explicitly mentioned calendar sync.
*   **Goal:** Distinguish between "nice to have" and "blocking."
*   **Key Question (Mom Test style):** "Walk me through the last time you tried to schedule a shift without the sync. What specifically did you do instead? How much time did that cost you?"
*   **Decision Rule:** If they cannot describe a specific, painful workaround they employed, the lack of sync is a rationalization for churn, not the cause. Do not build based on vague complaints.

## 3. Addressing the "Cheaper Alternative" Signal
You cannot validate price sensitivity without a price test, but you can validate *value*.
*   **Action:** Review the 14 customers who left for cheaper options. Did they use the calendar feature before leaving?
*   **Hypothesis Check:** If they *never* set up a calendar (if applicable) or churned before the "aha" moment, price was likely a proxy for "didn't see enough value to justify *any* cost."
*   **Next Step:** Do not lower prices yet. Focus discovery on whether the users who *stayed* are using calendar sync. If your retained power users don't use it, building it won't stop price-driven churn.

## 4. Immediate Non-Goals (Scope Defense)
*   **Building the full automated calendar integration:** This is a non-goal until the Concierge test passes the kill threshold.
*   **Price discounting campaigns:** A non-goal until we prove the product delivers unique value that cheaper tools do not.
*   **Re-onboarding the 68 lost customers:** A non-goal for now. Focus entirely on retaining the *current* cohort by solving the right problem.

## Summary for Co-Founder & Designer
We are not building the sync feature yet. We are spending the next 3 weeks running a manual test to prove that sync solves the retention problem. If the test fails the kill threshold, we pivot to investigating onboarding and value delivery, saving weeks of engineering time on a feature that won't move the needle.

**Note:** This plan prioritizes behavioral evidence (what users do) over stated intent (what they say in interviews).
**Note:** All "Kill Thresholds" must be agreed upon by the team before the test starts to prevent moving the goalposts.
