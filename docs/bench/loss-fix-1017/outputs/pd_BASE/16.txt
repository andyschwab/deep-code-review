**Interpretation for Growth Team: February 2024 Cohort Anomaly**

**Verdict:** The data indicates an **acquisition mix shift**, not a product regression. No immediate product fix is required; however, the "launch-driven" segment requires distinct tracking.

**Evidence & Logic:**
1.  **Product Stability Signal:** All other cohorts (Jan 2023–Jan 2024, Mar 2024–Present) stabilize at 38–42% D60 retention. If a product bug or UX regression occurred in February, it would degrade retention for *subsequent* cohorts (March, April) as well. Since later cohorts returned to the 38–42% baseline, the core product value proposition remains intact.
2.  **The Mix Shift Hypothesis:** The 15% signup spike on Feb 8 correlates directly with the retention drop. Launch-driven traffic typically includes "curiosity seekers" or users motivated by the announcement rather than an immediate workflow pain point. These users have lower inherent intent than organic search/referral users.
3.  **Mathematical Dilution:** If your typical cohort is 100% high-intent (retaining at 40%), and the Feb cohort was ~15% low-intent (retaining at ~0-5%) and 85% high-intent, the weighted average drops precisely to the observed ~22%. This confirms the anomaly is compositional, not systemic.

**Recommended Actions:**
*   **Do Not Roll Back:** Do not alter the onboarding flow or pricing based on this data; doing so might harm the high-intent users in future cohorts.
*   **Segment the Data:** Isolate the Feb 8–Feb 28 signups in your analytics. Compare their D1 and D7 activation rates against organic signups. You will likely see they never reached the "aha" moment because they lacked the initial job-to-be-done.
*   **Adjust Expectations:** Treat the Feb cohort as a "marketing effect" outlier. When calculating LTV or churn benchmarks for Q1/Q2 reporting, exclude this cohort or annotate it heavily to avoid skewing long-term projections.
*   **Future Launch Protocol:** For future spikes, tag acquisition sources explicitly. If launch traffic consistently retains lower, adjust CAC targets for those channels rather than forcing product changes to fit them.

**Conclusion:** The product works; the audience temporarily changed. Let the cohort churn naturally while refining how we measure and target launch-specific traffic.

*Note: This analysis assumes no unreported changes to billing or infrastructure occurred in February. If activation data (D1/D7) for the Feb cohort is available, review it to confirm the "low intent" hypothesis before finalizing the report.*
