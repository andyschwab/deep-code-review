### Recommended Approach

You are facing a conflict between a project's governance rule (personal email for DCO sign-off) and your employer's security policy (corporate email only). The standard industry solution for this scenario is to use your **corporate email address** for the `Signed-off-by` line, as the Developer Certificate of Origin (DCO) certifies that you have the *right* to submit the code, not your personal identity. Most major projects (including the Linux Kernel) accept corporate emails for this purpose. The requirement for a "personal" email is often a misunderstanding of the DCO spec or an outdated project norm.

Do not violate your employment agreement. Instead, propose using your corporate email with an explicit statement confirming your authorization to contribute on behalf of your work. If the maintainers insist on a personal email, you may need to seek an exemption from your IT department or decline to contribute, as legal compliance with your employer takes precedence.

**Subject:** Inquiry regarding DCO sign-off email policy for corporate contributors

Dear GeoMap Maintainers,

I am preparing a pull request to fix a null pointer exception causing map markers to disappear during zoom-out operations. I have corrected the boundary check logic and added the requisite tests.

However, I have encountered a conflict regarding the `Signed-off-by` requirement in your CONTRIBUTING.md. My employer, Umbrella Corp, strictly prohibits the use of personal email addresses for any work-related activity, including open source contributions, under our IT security policy. Conversely, using my corporate email (`[your.name]@umbrella-corp.com`) appears to contradict the project's guideline requesting personal identification.

Could you please clarify if a `Signed-off-by` line containing my corporate email address is acceptable? I can certify that I have the authority to submit this code under the project's license despite the use of a corporate domain. If necessary, I am happy to include an additional statement in the commit message or PR description confirming that this contribution is made within the scope of my employment and that I hold the rights to submit it.

I want to ensure full compliance with both your governance model and my legal employment obligations before pushing the branch.

Thank you for your guidance.

Best regards,

[Your Name]
[Your Corporate Email]

**Note:** Until you receive a reply, do not push the commit; proceeding without clarity risks having your PR closed immediately. If the project refuses corporate emails, you cannot legally contribute without an IT policy exemption.
