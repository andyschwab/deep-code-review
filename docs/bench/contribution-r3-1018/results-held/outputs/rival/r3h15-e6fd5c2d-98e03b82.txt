If no policy exists, email the maintainers directly (check the `CODEOWNERS` file or gemspec for emails) with the subject line: **"Private: Security Vulnerability Report - SecureAuth Session Hijacking."** Explicitly state that you have a fix ready in a stale PR but are withholding public escalation to coordinate responsibly. If there is a private security channel or email list I should use instead, please direct me there. If I hear no guidance on a private channel within 7 days, I will proceed with standard responsible disclosure timelines to protect users."

This creates a documented paper trail showing you acted in good faith without publicly shaming them yet.

Since this affects RiverBank's production environment, stop acting solely as an individual contributor. Ask your engineering manager or CTO to reach out. Maintainers often ignore individual PRs but respond quickly to business stakeholders whose infrastructure is at risk. A brief email from a company domain stating, "This vulnerability is actively impacting our production services; we need to coordinate a fix," carries significantly more weight than a GitHub comment.

**4. Prepare a Fork (The Last Resort)**
If 7–10 days pass with zero response after the security flag, you must protect your customers. Fork the gem, apply your fix, and publish it as a temporary internal gem (e.g., `secure_auth-riverbank`) or a clearly named community fork (e.g., `secure_auth-maintained`). Update your application to use this version. You can then inform the original maintainers: "We have deployed a patched fork to protect our users. We are happy to merge this back into the mainline whenever you are ready to review." This solves your immediate risk without requiring their permission.

**Do not** open a duplicate public issue detailing the exploit; this violates responsible disclosure norms and may alienate the maintainers further. Keep the exploit details private until a patch is widely available.
