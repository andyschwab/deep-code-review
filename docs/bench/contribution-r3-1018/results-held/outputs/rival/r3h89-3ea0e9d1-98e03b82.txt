The safest and most effective course of action is to **open a public issue** on the project's repository describing the typo without providing a code patch or claiming authorship of the fix.

Here is the recommended workflow to strictly adhere to Cybergdyne Systems' legal constraints:

1.  **Create a Neutral Issue:** Navigate to the project's "Issues" tab and select "New Issue." Choose the "Documentation" or "Bug Report" template if available.
2.  **Describe the Error Only:** In the description, state clearly: "There is a typographical error in [File Name/Section] where [incorrect text] appears. It should read [correct text]."
3.  **Avoid Legal Triggers:** Do not attach a diff, do not link to a fork, and do not use phrasing like "I propose changing..." or "My fix is..." Simply report the factual error in the documentation.
4.  **No Disclosure Required:** You do not need to mention your employer, your legal team, or the license incompatibility in the issue. Reporting a factual error in a public repository is generally considered community feedback, not a "contribution" of intellectual property, and does not trigger license assignment clauses.
5.  **Let Maintainers Act:** By opening the issue, you enable the maintainers or other contributors (who are not bound by your employer's restrictions) to see the error and submit the actual Pull Request. The fix will be merged under their authorship or the authorship of a third party, completely bypassing your company's prohibition on you submitting code.

**Regarding Anonymous Submission:**
Most GitHub/GitLab projects do not have a mechanism for "anonymous patches." Even if you emailed a patch to the maintainers, accepting it would still require them to attribute it to someone, and if they trace it back to you, it could create an audit trail linking you to the contribution. Opening a public issue as a standard user account is the industry-standard method for reporting errors without legally "contributing" code.

**Note:** Ensure you are logged into your personal GitHub account, not a corporate-managed one, when filing the issue to maintain separation between your employment and this community feedback.
