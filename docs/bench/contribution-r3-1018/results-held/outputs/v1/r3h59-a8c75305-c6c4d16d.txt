### Recommended Course of Action

Do not use a personal email address, as this violates your employment agreement. Do not use your corporate email if it explicitly breaches the project's written governance rule, as this may cause your contribution to be rejected or create legal ambiguity. The standard industry resolution for this conflict is to **contact the maintainers before submitting the patch** to request an exception or clarification. Most projects enforce the "personal email" rule to ensure the *individual* is liable for the code, not to forbid corporate addresses per se; they often accept a corporate email if the "Signed-off-by" line clearly certifies that you have the right to submit the code under the project's license.

**Action Plan:**
1.  **Hold the PR:** Do not open the Pull Request yet.
2.  **Send an Inquiry:** Email the maintainers or open a preliminary issue (if allowed) explaining the conflict.
3.  **Proposed Solution:** Offer to use your corporate email accompanied by an explicit statement in the commit message or a separate Contributor License Agreement (CLA) confirming you are authorized to contribute on behalf of your employer.
4.  **Fallback:** If they refuse corporate emails, ask if they accept contributions via a project-specific alias or if a project maintainer can apply your patch (as a "patch submission") while they handle the sign-off.

If a personal email is strictly mandatory with no exceptions, please advise on the preferred procedure for corporate contributors in my situation. I want to ensure full compliance with both your governance model and my employment obligations before submitting the patch.

Sincerely,
