### Recommended Course of Action

**Do not draft a public issue yet.** Your employer's policy forbids contribution without a formal security mechanism, and raising this publicly before confirming the maintainers' stance could inadvertently reveal your company's proprietary due diligence methodology or create an awkward dynamic if the project is hobbyist-maintained.

Instead, take these three steps:

1.  **Check for Hidden Documentation:** Before contacting anyone, verify if a `SECURITY.md` exists in the repository root, `.github/` directory, or if there is a security advisory tab enabled on the GitHub project page. Sometimes these are present but not linked in the main README.
2.  **Send a Private Inquiry:** If no documentation exists, send a direct, private message to the primary maintainer(s) via GitHub. Do not mention your company's specific policy constraints or "vendor risk assessments." Instead, frame it as a contributor wanting to ensure responsible disclosure.
    *   *Draft Message:* "Hello, I'm interested in contributing some performance optimizations to QueueManager. Before I begin, I wanted to ask if you have a preferred channel for reporting potential security vulnerabilities or a security policy in the works? I want to ensure I follow best practices if I encounter anything sensitive during my work."
3.  **Evaluate the Response:**
    *   **If they respond positively** (e.g., "We use GitHub Advisories" or "Email me at..."): You can then ask your legal/compliance team if this informal but clear channel satisfies the policy.
    *   **If they do not respond** or indicate they have no process: You must halt contribution. Contributing without this clearance violates your employer's rules. In this case, the only safe path is to fork the project for internal use at Acme Analytics, applying your optimizations locally without pushing upstream.

**Why this approach?**
It respects your employer's hard constraint (no contribution without security protocols) while avoiding the risk of publicly exposing your company's internal audit criteria. It gives the maintainers a chance to clarify their process privately, which is often sufficient for compliance teams to grant an exception, without forcing a public conversation about their project's maturity.

**Time-boxed Fallback:**
If you receive no reply within 10 business days, assume the answer is "no process exists" and proceed with the internal fork strategy. Do not send a follow-up public issue.
