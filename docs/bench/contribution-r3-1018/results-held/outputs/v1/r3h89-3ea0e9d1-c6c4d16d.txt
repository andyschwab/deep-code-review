The safest and most effective course of action is to **open a public issue** on the project's repository describing the typo's location and the correct text, without attaching a patch, linking to a fork, or claiming authorship of the fix.

Here is the recommended procedure to strictly adhere to Cybergdyne Systems' legal constraints:

1.  **Do Not Create a Fork or Branch:** Do not clone the repository to your local machine to make the edit, and do not push any code to a personal or company GitHub/GitLab account. Even creating a branch can sometimes be interpreted as creating a "derivative work" under strict legal interpretations of incompatible licenses.
2.  **File a Descriptive Issue:** Navigate to the project's issue tracker. Create a new issue titled "Documentation Typo in [File/Section Name]." In the body, state: "There is a typographical error in [specific file/line] where '[incorrect text]' appears. It should read '[correct text]'."
3.  **Avoid Attribution:** Do not sign the issue with a "Signed-off-by" line or explicitly state "I propose this fix." Simply present the factual error and the factual correction. You are reporting a bug in the documentation, not contributing code.
4.  **No Direct Contact Required:** You do not need to contact the maintainers privately unless the project specifically requests security vulnerabilities be reported that way (which a typo is not). Public issues are the standard mechanism for this.

**Why this works:**
Reporting a fact (a typo exists) and a standard correction (the spelling) is generally not considered creating a derivative work subject to the license's contamination clauses. You are acting as a user reporting an error, not a developer contributing a licensed modification. By letting a third party (the maintainer or another contributor) type the actual characters into the codebase and commit them, the chain of custody for the code change originates from someone not bound by your employer's restriction, effectively decoupling your legal risk from the fix.

**Note:** If the project has a specific "Documentation" or "Good First Issue" label, apply it to help maintainers prioritize.
**Note:** If you are unsure if *reporting* the error itself violates policy, consult your legal team with the draft issue text before posting.
