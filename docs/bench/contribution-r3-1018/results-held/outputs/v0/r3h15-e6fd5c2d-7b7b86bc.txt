### Strategy to Escalate Your SecureAuth Pull Request

**1. Verify the Security Disclosure Channel Immediately**
Do not open a new public issue linking to your PR yet. Since this involves a critical session hijacking vulnerability, the project likely has a private responsible disclosure process (e.g., a `SECURITY.md` file, a specific email alias like `security@secureauth.org`, or a HackerOne program).
*   **Action:** Check the repository root for `SECURITY.md`. If found, contact them there immediately, referencing your PR number but **do not** disclose specific details of the exploit or your production incident publicly.
*   **Why:** Publicly discussing an unpatched critical vulnerability can violate responsible disclosure norms and alert attackers before a fix is ready. Maintainers often prioritize private security reports over public PRs.

**2. The "Nudge" Protocol (If no security channel exists or after contacting them)**
If there is no private channel, or after you have notified them privately, proceed with a public but polite escalation that frames the silence as a potential oversight rather than negligence.
*   **Action:** Comment on your existing PR (do not close/reopen it yet). Use this template:
    > "Hi maintainers, following up on this critical session hijacking fix. I understand you are busy, but given the severity, could you confirm if this is being reviewed? If this PR is not the right format, please point me to the correct process. If I don't hear back in 5 business days, I will open a tracking issue to ensure visibility, unless advised otherwise."
*   **Timing:** Wait exactly 5 business days after this comment.

**3. The Escalation: Open a Tracking Issue**
If silence persists after the comment above:
*   **Action:** Open a new issue titled "Critical Security Fix: Session Hijacking Vulnerability (PR #XXX pending review)."
*   **Content:** Briefly describe the *class* of vulnerability (session hijacking) without revealing your specific production details or RiverBank's architecture. Link the PR. State clearly: "This fix is ready and tested; seeking review to protect downstream users."
*   **Why:** Issues are often triaged faster than PRs. This creates a public record of the risk without being aggressive.

**4. Community Channels**
If the issue is also ignored for one week:
*   **Action:** Check the project's `README` or website for a mailing list, Slack, or Discord. Post a concise summary: "Seeking security review for a critical session fix in SecureAuth. PR #XXX and Issue #YYY are open. No response in 2 months. Advice appreciated."
*   **Tone:** Frame it as "seeking advice on the process" rather than "complaining about ignore."

**Direct Answer to Your Uncertainty:**
Do **not** push harder on the PR alone; the maintainers may have missed it. The correct next step is to check for a private security contact first. If none exists, comment on the PR with a deadline, then open a tracking issue. This is standard procedure for critical fixes and will not label you a nuisance; it labels you as a responsible stakeholder.

**Fallback:**
If all channels fail after 3 weeks of escalation, consider publishing a detailed advisory (after a reasonable embargo period) to warn the community, explicitly stating that the maintainer was unresponsive. This is a last resort but necessary when customer safety is at risk.

*Note: Ensure all public text strips references to "RiverBank" and specific production logs to protect your company's privacy.*
*Remember: You are advocating for the safety of all users, not just your own.*
