You cannot contribute the code under the current project rules without resolving the intellectual property (IP) conflict. The Contributor License Agreement (CLA) requiring IP assignment is a hard gate; maintainers who state it is "non-negotiable" will not merge contributions from entities that cannot sign it. There is no technical workaround or alternative licensing arrangement that bypasses a mandatory CLA if the project governance enforces it strictly.

Your company does not necessarily need to become a "formal sponsor" unless the project explicitly offers sponsorship as a path to CLA exemption (which is rare). Instead, the resolution usually requires your company's legal team to engage directly with the project's governing foundation to negotiate a **Corporate Contributor License Agreement (CCLA)** or a specific IP waiver. Many foundations (like Apache, CNCF, or Eclipse) have standard CCLAs that allow companies to authorize contributions without transferring underlying IP ownership, satisfying both the project's legal needs and corporate policies. If this specific project lacks a CCLA option, your legal team must request one or seek a written exception for this specific contribution.

### Steps to Resolve and Communicate

1.  **Halt Technical Work:** Do not open a Pull Request yet. Submitting code before resolving the CLA issue creates legal ambiguity and wastes reviewer time.
2.  **Internal Legal Review:** Confirm with your legal team if they are willing to sign a CCLA (if available) or negotiate a limited license grant that stops short of full ownership assignment.
3.  **Draft Communication to Maintainers:** Send a polite inquiry to the project's steering committee or legal contact (not just the code maintainers) before opening an issue. Use the following template:

   **Subject:** Corporate Contribution Inquiry: CLA Conflict and Potential CCLA Path for OmniSoft

   > "Dear DataFlow Maintainers,
   >
   > Our team at OmniSoft has developed a verified performance optimization for the message queue handling that addresses a 30% throughput bottleneck under high load. We are eager to upstream this fix.
   >
   > However, we have encountered a blocker regarding the Contributor License Agreement (CLA). Our corporate policy strictly prohibits the assignment of intellectual property ownership to third parties, though we are fully willing to grant an irrevocable, perpetual license under the project's existing open-source terms.
   >
   > We understand the CLA is currently non-negotiable for individual contributors. Does the foundation support a Corporate Contributor License Agreement (CCLA) or an alternative mechanism that allows corporate entities to contribute without assigning IP ownership? If not, is there a process to discuss a potential exception or a path forward for enterprise contributors facing this specific legal constraint?
   >
   > We wish to resolve this compliantly and avoid stalling the contribution. Please advise on the correct legal contact or procedure.
   >
   > Sincerely,
   > [Your Name]"

4.  **Wait for Legal Clearance:** Do not proceed until you have written confirmation that your proposed arrangement is acceptable.
5.  **Generalize the Contribution (Once Cleared):** If legal clearance is granted, ensure the code patch is stripped of all internal identifiers (hostnames, paths, proprietary error messages) and generalized to rely only on synthetic data before submission.

### Direct Answer to Your Uncertainty
There is no way to contribute under a "different arrangement" unilaterally; the project's rules control the merge. Your company likely needs to engage legally to establish a CCLA or specific waiver, not necessarily become a financial sponsor. Precedent exists in major foundations where CCLAs solve this exact conflict, but it requires active legal negotiation, not a code change.

### Fallback
If the foundation refuses any alternative to the current CLA and your legal team cannot sign it, you must keep the fix internal. You may document the *general* nature of the performance issue (without revealing your specific implementation or proprietary data) as a public issue to alert the community, allowing volunteers to implement a similar fix independently.
