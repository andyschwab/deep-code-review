### Recommended Action Plan

You cannot and should not attempt to bypass either the project's governance or your employer's security policy. The "Signed-off-by" line in open source (based on the Developer Certificate of Origin) is a legal attestation that *you* have the right to submit the code, not strictly a requirement for a specific *type* of email domain. Most projects accept corporate emails provided the identity is clear.

**Step 1: Verify the Rule Nuance**
Re-read the GeoMap `CONTRIBUTING.md`. It likely says "signed-off-by" with an email, but rarely explicitly forbids corporate domains unless stated. If it strictly says "personal," this is an unusual constraint that likely stems from a desire to verify individual identity rather than corporate ownership.

**Step 2: The Inquiry Strategy**
Do not submit the PR yet. Open an issue or send a message to the maintainers (via their preferred channel, often a mailing list or discussion board) explaining the conflict. Frame it as a compliance conflict, not a refusal to follow rules.

**Step 3: Draft Inquiry to Maintainers**
Subject: Question regarding Signed-off-by policy and corporate email constraints

> "Hello GeoMap Maintainers,
>
> I have developed a fix for the marker disappearance bug during zoom-out (null pointer exception in boundary logic) and am ready to submit a PR.
>
> However, I have encountered a conflict between the project's 'Signed-off-by' requirement and my employer's security policy. My organization (Umbrella Corp) strictly prohibits the use of personal email addresses for any work-related output, including open source contributions. Consequently, I can only sign commits with my corporate email address.
>
> Could you confirm if a 'Signed-off-by' line using my corporate email, accompanied by a verification statement confirming my individual authority to submit this code, would satisfy the project's governance requirements?
>
> Example proposed line:
> `Signed-off-by: [My Name] <[my.name]@umbrella-corp.com>`
>
> I want to ensure full compliance with both your contribution guidelines and my employment agreement before proceeding. Thank you for your guidance."

**Step 4: Prepare the Commit (Pending Approval)**
Once they reply (most will say yes), format your commit as follows:

```text
fix: resolve marker disappearance on zoom-out boundary check

Corrected the boundary check logic that caused a null pointer exception
when markers were rendered outside the visible zoom threshold.
Added regression tests to verify marker persistence at extreme zoom levels.

Signed-off-by: [Your Name] <[your.name]@umbrella-corp.com>
```

**Direct Answer to Your Uncertainty**
You do not need to choose between violating your job or the project rules. The standard open source practice accepts corporate emails for the "Signed-off-by" line as long as *you* are the one signing it. The rule exists to establish a chain of custody, not to enforce personal email usage. If the maintainers insist on a personal email despite your explanation, you unfortunately cannot contribute this specific patch without violating your employment agreement; in that case, you must drop the contribution.

**Fallback**
If the maintainers do not respond within two weeks, do not submit the patch. Instead, document the fix internally at Umbrella Corp as a "local imprint" (a patch applied only to your fork) and note the governance blocker in your internal records. Do not attempt to forge a signature or use a personal email against policy.
