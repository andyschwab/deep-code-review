**Recommendation: Do not go full-scale yet.** Proceed only as a constrained pilot with a hard kill switch. The current plan relies on unverified customer behavior and assumes supply chain perfection, creating a high risk of stranded inventory and missed financial targets.

**Three Main Failure Modes:**
1.  **Adoption Collapse:** The 68% survey acceptance rate is likely inflated (social desirability bias). If actual tote adoption drops below 45%, the $0.50 disposal saving vanishes while the $12 tote cost becomes a sunk loss on unused units, extending break-even beyond 36 months.
2.  **Supply Chain Fragility:** The plan assumes 100% monthly delivery reliability for 10,000 pouches. Any single month of delay halts the summer line launch, costing far more in lost sales than the $7,500 material savings. There is no buffer stock or secondary supplier.
3.  **Hidden Reverse-Logistics Cost:** The model accounts for disposal savings but ignores the cost of retrieving, cleaning, and redistributing the reusable totes. If return rates mirror adoption (e.g., only 68% return), you incur $12 replacement costs per lost unit plus administrative overhead, potentially negating all disposal fee savings.

**Arithmetic & Logic Flaws:**
*   **Baseline Confusion:** You forecast a "22% waste reduction versus our current 12% baseline." This phrasing is ambiguous. If you reduce waste *by* 22% from a 12% baseline, your new waste rate is 9.36%. If you mean reducing waste *to* 22%, that is an increase in waste. Clarify if the target is a 22% *relative* improvement or an absolute percentage point drop.

You are already $4,500 in the hole before counting the $6,000/month tote expenditure (500 × $12). The math does not support a 12-month break-even unless the $12 tote is a one-time capital expense amortized over years, not a per-order variable cost. If it is per order, the program loses money every single month.
