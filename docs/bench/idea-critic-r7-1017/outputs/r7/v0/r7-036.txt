**Recommendation: Go-if**
Proceed with a restricted pilot **only if** you cap the initial launch at 50 spots/month and secure a written confirmation of the regulatory fee structure before writing any code. Do not launch the full 400-spot capacity or hire the second developer until the carrier contract is signed and the actual regulatory fee is verified.

**Three Biggest Failure Modes**
1.  **Regulatory & Compliance Collapse:** Your model assumes a $200/quarter flat fee, but you admitted this is unknown. If the regulator classifies user-uploaded audio as broadcast content requiring individual licensing or charges per-spot fees, your $500/month legal budget could explode to tens of thousands, instantly destroying unit economics.
2.  **Carrier Dependency Risk:** The entire engagement loop relies on an unsigned contract for a 20% SMS discount. If the carrier declines or delays, your customer acquisition cost (CAC) spikes, and without SMS alerts, listener engagement for time-sensitive ad slots may drop too low to fill even 100 spots, let alone 400.
3.  **Supply-Demand Mismatch:** You assume 400 filled spots based on 12,000 listeners, but there is no evidence that even 1% of listeners want to *create* ads rather than just consume them. If supply dries up at 50 spots, the marketplace feels empty, deterring the few advertisers who join, creating a death spiral before you reach the 250-spot break-even point.

**Kill Criterion**
Terminate the project immediately if, after 90 days of the capped pilot, the **net profit is below $0** (not $1,000) OR if the verified regulatory fee exceeds $1,000 per quarter.

*Note: Your current break-even calculation ignores the risk of the unsigned carrier contract; treat the SMS discount as $0 in your conservative modeling. The $8 CPM assumption is irrelevant since you are charging a flat $30 fee, not a programmatic rate.*
