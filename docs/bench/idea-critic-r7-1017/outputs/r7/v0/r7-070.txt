**Recommendation: Proceed with a "Go-If" conditional approval.**
Launch the program only if the third-party licensing is secured as a flat $80,000 fee. Do not proceed under a per-user royalty model, as it introduces uncapped variable costs that threaten the projected $1.2M revenue and could render the $50 subscription price unsustainable.

**Three Biggest Failure Risks:**
1.  **Licensing Model Collapse:** If forced into a per-user royalty, margins will erode faster than anticipated. With 770 users paying $50/month, even a small per-user fee compounds significantly against the fixed $520k development budget, potentially turning the projected profit into a loss within the first year.
2.  **Overestimated Opt-In Rate:** The plan assumes a 35% adoption rate (770 students). If actual adoption falls below 20% (440 students), revenue drops to ~$264k annually, failing to cover the $200k software and $150k content costs, let alone recoup the initial investment within two years.
3.  **Hardware Obsolescence & Support:** Allocating only $45k for 150 headsets ($300/unit) assumes current pricing holds for 12 months. If hardware costs rise or the selected units become obsolete before the 12-month development cycle ends, replacement costs could spike, while technical support for VR hardware may create hidden operational expenses not included in the budget.

**Pre-Agreed Kill Trigger:**
Terminate the project immediately if, at the 6-month development mark, the cumulative committed costs (software + content + licensing) exceed $300,000 without a signed licensing agreement confirming the flat $80,000 fee structure.

*Note: The 15% churn assumption is optimistic for a new VR format; stress-test revenue models with 25% churn. Ensure the $125k marketing budget specifically targets VR-ready users within the existing base to validate the 35% opt-in hypothesis early.*
