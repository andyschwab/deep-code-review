**Recommendation:** Proceed with a **conditional pilot expansion (Go-If)** rather than a full national rollout. Authorize the $3.5M license and training only for a defined 500km "Phase 1" zone, contingent on verifying that the false-positive rate remains below 5% and net savings exceed $4M in the first six months of this phase.

**Three Biggest Failure Modes:**
1.  **Technical (False Positives at Scale):** The model's 95% accuracy may hide a catastrophic false-positive rate when scaled beyond 120km. If the system flags healthy tracks as faulty too often, technicians will suffer "alert fatigue," ignoring real dangers or wasting thousands of man-hours verifying non-issues, effectively halting operations.
2.  **Financial (Arithmetic Overreach):** The projected $12M annual savings relies on the pilot's 15-hour reduction scaling linearly to the entire network. This assumes downtime causes are identical nationwide. If the national network suffers from systemic infrastructure decay rather than unpredictable faults, the AI cannot prevent the downtime, rendering the $4.7M upfront cost unrecoverable.
3.  **Operational (Training Bottleneck):** Training 150 technicians simultaneously while maintaining 24/7 rail operations creates a resource collision. If the training disrupts current maintenance schedules more than the AI saves, net downtime will increase in the short term, causing immediate operational backlash.

**Kill Criteria:**
Scrap the project immediately if, after the first year of Phase 1, the **net realized savings (actual downtime reduction minus operational costs) fall below $2.5 million**, or if the **false-positive alert rate exceeds 8%**, necessitating manual verification for more than 10% of all alerts.

**Hidden Risks & Arithmetic Slips:**
*   **Arithmetic Error:** Your math implies the pilot saved 15 hours *per year* on 120km, yet the pilot only ran for six months. Did you mean 15 hours saved *during* the six-month pilot (annualized to 30 hours), or is the 15-hour figure already an annualized projection? If the latter, the $12M national projection assumes the national network is exactly 48 times larger than the pilot (120km × 48 = 5,760km); verify this scale factor matches your actual track mileage.
*   **Hidden Risk:** The $800k/hour downtime cost likely includes reputational damage and regulatory fines which are non-linear. A localized failure in the AI causing a major accident could trigger liabilities far exceeding the "hourly" operational cost, a risk not captured in the linear savings model.
