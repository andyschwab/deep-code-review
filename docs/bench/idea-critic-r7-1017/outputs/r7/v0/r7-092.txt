**Recommendation: Do not proceed with the full 12-hectare switch yet; instead, run a 1-hectare pilot in the 2024 season.**

While the projected revenue gain (€9,600) is attractive, the current math shows a net loss of €9,600 (€19,200 added cost minus €9,600 added revenue) even if the yield targets are met. Furthermore, relying on a 15% yield lift and stable pest pressure without field validation exposes the vineyard to significant financial risk, especially given the uncertainty around EU organic certification changes which could render the product unusable mid-season. A single-hectare trial limits the financial exposure to approximately €1,600 while providing the data needed to validate the yield lift and regulatory compliance before committing the full €69,600.

**Three Main Failure Modes:**
1.  **Regulatory Rejection:** EU certification updates ban the active ingredient in Vineveil Naturals after purchase but before harvest, resulting in a total loss of the €69,600 investment and potential fines for non-compliant application.
2.  **Yield Disappointment:** The assumed 15% yield lift fails to materialize (e.g., actual lift is <5%), leaving the vineyard with higher input costs and no offsetting revenue, deepening the net loss beyond the projected €9,600 deficit.
3.  **Pest Pressure Spike:** Pest levels exceed the historical average of 0.9 kg/ha (e.g., rising above 1.5 kg/ha), overwhelming the organic treatment and causing crop damage that reduces both volume and quality below current baseline performance.

**Kill Criteria (Stop/Rethink Triggers):**
*   **Financial:** Projected net revenue shortfall for the block exceeds **€5,000** by the mid-season review (August 2024).
*   **Agronomic:** Pest pressure exceeds **1.2 kg/ha** despite treatment, or any official notice is received regarding the non-compliance of Vineveil's active ingredients under new EU rules.
*   **Timeline:** Full adoption is paused until the pilot demonstrates a net positive or break-even outcome for one full harvest cycle.

*Note: The current proposal contains a mathematical gap where costs outweigh benefits even under optimistic scenarios; the pilot is designed specifically to test the validity of the 15% yield assumption.*
