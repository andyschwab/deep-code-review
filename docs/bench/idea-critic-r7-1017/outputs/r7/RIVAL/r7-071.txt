**Recommendation: Do not proceed with the full pilot yet.**

The math does not support your assumption that a 7% premium will cover fees in year one. At £15 base cost (£12 materials + £3 labor), a 7% premium adds only £1.05 per bottle. Against a £0.25 blockchain fee, your net gain is £0.80 per bottle. On the 15,000-bottle pilot, gross premium revenue is £15,750, but blockchain fees total £3,750. While this leaves £12,000 to cover the £8,500 integration cost, it assumes **100% of customers pay the premium**. If only 60% of buyers value the label enough to pay extra (a common adoption curve), your revenue drops to £9,450, leaving you £4,050 short of breaking even on integration alone, ignoring operational overhead. Highland Dew's 5% uplift on 120,000 units works due to volume; your low-volume, high-cost structure makes you vulnerable to low adoption rates.

**Three Biggest Failure Modes:**
1.  **Low Scan/Trust Conversion:** Customers buy the bottle for the spirit, not the tech. If fewer than 40% scan the QR code, the perceived value of "provenance" never materializes, and retailers will refuse to stock a higher-priced SKU that doesn't move.
2.  **Premium Erosion:** The market treats the label as a commodity feature rather than a differentiator. Competitors copy the QR code without the blockchain backend, undercutting your price while offering a similar visual cue.
3.  **Integration Drag:** The £8,500 one-time fee is a floor, not a ceiling. Customizing ERP systems to handle per-bottle tokenization often reveals hidden labor costs that double the initial integration estimate, destroying your margin before the pilot ends.

**Kill Metric:**
Terminate the project immediately if **premium revenue per bottle averages below £0.60** over the first 5,000 units sold.

This threshold represents the point where the net margin (£0.60 premium - £0.25 fee = £0.35) is too thin to ever recover the £8,500 sunk cost within a reasonable timeframe, even if adoption improves later.

*Note: Run a "fake door" test first: label 500 bottles with a static QR code linking to a PDF certificate (cost: ~£0) to measure actual scan rates and willingness to pay before signing the blockchain contract.*
