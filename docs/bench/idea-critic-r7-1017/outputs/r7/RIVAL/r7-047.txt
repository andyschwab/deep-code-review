**Recommendation:** Proceed with the pilot launch on 15 July 2025, but strictly cap the initial hardware deployment to 20% of your projected user volume (approx. 1,200 users) to validate the conversion rate before committing the full 1,200,000 ARS hardware budget. Treat the 120,000 ARS/quarter data-compliance cost as a confirmed liability, not a potential risk, and deduct it immediately from your cash flow projections.

**How This Could Fail:**
*   **Conversion Delusion:** Your model assumes 5% of *all* virtual tour takers will buy a $30 kit. In immersive retail, conversion rates often hover below 1% unless the experience includes a direct, frictionless checkout inside the VR environment. If you only achieve 1% conversion, revenue drops to 60,000 ARS/month, leaving you 190,000 ARS/month in the hole against operating costs.
*   **The "Hidden" Maintenance Cliff:** Headsets in public kiosks suffer rapid degradation (hygiene wear, battery failure, lens scratching). If you lack an on-site technician, downtime will spike, destroying the user experience. Budget an immediate 15% contingency (180,000 ARS) for hardware replacement in year one.
*   **Software Licensing Trap:** The 350,000 ARS license covers two years, but VR platforms (iOS/Android/Standalone) update aggressively. If your software isn't cloud-updatable, you may face a costly forced migration or brick your devices within 18 months.

**Kill/Pivot Triggers:**
*   **KILL THE PROJECT:** If the Month 2 conversion rate is below **1.5%** (generating <90,000 ARS/month). At this level, you cannot cover the quarterly compliance tax and maintenance, let alone recoup capital.
*   **PIVOT:** If engagement (time spent in tour) is high (>3 minutes) but conversion is low. This indicates the experience is working but the offer is wrong. Pivot immediately to using the VR tour as a lead-gen tool for high-margin wine club subscriptions rather than one-off tasting kits.
