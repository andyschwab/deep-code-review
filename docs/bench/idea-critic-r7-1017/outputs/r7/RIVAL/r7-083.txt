**Recommendation: Do Not Proceed** in the current form. The proposal relies on unvalidated assumptions that create unacceptable financial risk. Specifically, the revenue model assumes a 100% conversion rate from "visitor" to "payer" within the VR environment, which is statistically impossible for a new medium. A realistic conversion rate (typically 2–5% in e-commerce) would reduce projected revenue from $2.4M to roughly $48k–$120k, making the $150k investment unrecoverable.

**Primary Failure Modes:**
1.  **Revenue Illusion:** The math treats all 200,000 projected users as paying customers. If only 5% buy, you lose $90k.
2.  **Hardware Friction:** Assuming 30% hardware penetration among existing web users is optimistic; VR headsets remain niche. If penetration is actually 5%, user acquisition costs will skyrocket.
3.  **Content Bottleneck:** Coordinating live demos from 12 master weavers introduces massive operational risk. If artisans cancel or technical latency ruins the "live" feel, the core value proposition collapses.
4.  **Hidden Costs:** The budget ignores high-fidelity 3D asset creation, server costs for low-latency multi-user streaming, and the significant marketing spend required to drive users to a VR-specific experience rather than your existing web store.

**Kill Trigger:**
Terminate the project immediately if, by **Month 4**, a functional prototype fails to demonstrate a **purchase conversion rate of at least 3%** in a closed beta of 500 users, or if total spend exceeds **$60,000** (40% of budget) without a verified, scalable pipeline for artisan onboarding.

**Note:** The 9-month timeline is likely aggressive given the complexity of synchronizing live video with 3D commerce; expect slippage. Consider pivoting to a lower-fidelity "360-degree video" MVP to test conversion before committing to full VR development.
