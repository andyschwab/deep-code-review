**Recommendation: Proceed with a Phased Pilot, Not Full Adoption**

Do not roll out Canvas Pulse gallery-wide yet. Instead, authorize a **three-month limited pilot** on 20% of inventory (specifically non-local or established international works) to validate local artist tolerance before risking community relations. The projected revenue uplift is mathematically sound, but the assumption that Cape Town artists will accept Durban's volatility terms is unverified and represents a critical single point of failure for the gallery's reputation.

**Arithmetic & Assumption Check**
Your top-line math holds: moving 1,340 units from R150 to R165 yields ~R20,100 extra monthly revenue (R241k/year), which comfortably covers the R45k integration cost in under two months. However, your model assumes foot traffic grows *simultaneously* with price increases. Historically, higher prices suppress volume unless demand is highly elastic; if the 8% traffic growth lags or fails to materialize, you risk raising prices without the volume buffer, potentially alienating your core local audience immediately.

**Top Three Failure Modes**

1.  **Artist Revolt & Supply Shock (Highest Risk):** If local artists perceive the 1.2% fee plus dynamic volatility as unfair compared to fixed pricing, they may withdraw work. Unlike the Durban hub, Cape Town's community is tightly knit; a coordinated boycott could reduce available inventory by >30%, destroying the algorithm's ability to optimize prices due to lack of product.
2.  **Revenue Dilution via Fee Stack:** The model nets R15 extra per ticket but ignores the compounding cost of the 1.2% transaction fee on the *higher* price point. At R165, the fee is R1.98. While small, if the promised 8% traffic boost requires heavy marketing spend not listed in your costs, the net margin could be negligible, making the R45k integration cost harder to justify.
3.  **Technical/Reputational Whiplash:** If the algorithm reacts too aggressively to low traffic by slashing prices, it devalues the brand perception of "community art." Conversely, if it spikes prices during a popular exhibition, visitors may feel gouged. Either extreme triggers negative word-of-mouth that suppresses the very foot traffic the algorithm relies on.

**Kill Switch Trigger**

Terminate the project immediately if **active artist participation drops below 85% of the current roster** within the first 60 days of the pilot.

*Reasoning:* You currently have a healthy roster supporting R1.2m revenue. Losing more than 15% of your suppliers indicates a breakdown in community trust that no amount of algorithmic efficiency can fix. Once participation dips this low, the inventory scarcity will force prices up artificially, creating a feedback loop that drives away visitors and validates the artists' fears, causing a total collapse of the model.
