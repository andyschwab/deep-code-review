**Recommendation: Proceed with a phased rollout, but delay the $30k marketing spend until latency on low-end Android devices is verified.**

The pilot math supports the investment, but your assumption that the 1.5% lift applies universally is the single point of failure. The basket size increase (ARS 45) is negligible (~2%), meaning the business case relies entirely on volume from the conversion lift. If the tool frustrates users on slower devices, you risk depressing the baseline conversion rate below the current 3%.

**Main Failure Modes & Hidden Risks:**
*   **The "Low-End" Cliff:** If load times exceed 3 seconds on entry-level Androids (common in broader markets vs. a flagship store), adoption will tank. Users won't wait; they will leave. This could drop overall conversion below 2.5%.
*   **Support Cost Underestimation:** $15k/month for support implies heavy human intervention. If the tool requires staff assistance to work, your margins evaporate. The hidden cost is the opportunity cost of staff time not spent selling.
*   **Marketing Waste:** Spending $30k to drive traffic to a broken or slow experience accelerates brand damage. You pay to acquire users who immediately churn.
*   **Currency Volatility:** Your costs are in USD, but revenue is in ARS. If the peso devalues significantly during the 4-month build, your real revenue drops while USD costs remain fixed.

**Kill/Rethink Triggers:**
1.  **Conversion < 3.2% after 60 days:** If the lift is less than 0.2% points, the ROI does not cover the $15k/month support + dev amortization.
2.  **Adoption < 10% of eligible users:** If fewer than 1 in 10 visitors try the tool, the friction is too high regardless of conversion rate.
3.  **Support tickets > 5% of users:** If more than 1 in 20 users need help, the UX is broken.

*Note: Verify the Android latency on a $200 device before signing the dev contract. Do not approve the marketing budget until Week 1 data confirms the tool works standalone.*
