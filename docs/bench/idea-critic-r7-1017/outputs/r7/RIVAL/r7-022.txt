**Recommendation: Modify**

Proceed with the pilot, but reject the current financial model. Your projection relies on a 5% conversion rate that is likely 2–3x higher than the industry average for new marketplace traffic (typically 1.5%–2.5%). Furthermore, comparing Silk Loom's 12% fee to GlobalMarket's 10% without adjusting for GlobalMarket's higher average order value creates a false dichotomy; a lower fee on lower-value goods may yield less profit than a higher fee on premium baskets. You must renegotiate the shipping/customs cost absorption or bake it into the price before launching, as the current $1,200 monthly estimate creates a hidden margin cliff.

**Three Most Likely Failure Points**

1.  **Conversion Rate Overestimation:** If the actual conversion rate lands at the industry standard of 2% instead of your projected 5%, you will gain only 60 sales, not 150. This drops incremental revenue to ~$1,620, which is barely enough to cover the $1,200 in new operational costs and the amortized integration fee, leaving almost no profit for the cooperative.
2.  **Margin Erosion from Hidden Fees:** The model treats the $0.25 per-item fee and the $1,200 shipping/customs handling as secondary. In reality, if these costs are not passed to the consumer, they directly slash your net margin. On a $30 item, the 12% commission ($3.60) + $0.25 fee + prorated shipping/customs could consume over 25% of revenue, turning "new sales" into a net loss compared to your boutique channel.
3.  **Channel Cannibalization:** The plan assumes the 150 new sales are entirely incremental. If Silk Loom's visitors are simply existing customers who would have bought in Fez (but now buy online), you incur the 12% commission and shipping costs on sales you would have made anyway at 0% commission, effectively paying to downgrade your own revenue.

**Specific Trigger to Stop or Change Course**

Establish a hard stop if the **net profit per unit on Silk Loom is less than $8.00** after three months, or if the **conversion rate remains below 2.5%** after spending $1,000 on targeted promotions within the platform. At that point, the channel is either operationally inefficient or fundamentally mismatched with your product, and you should pause new inventory allocation to Silk Loom immediately.
