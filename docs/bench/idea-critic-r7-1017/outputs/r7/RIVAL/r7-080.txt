**Main Failure Modes:**
*   **Technical:** The AI generates patterns that look distinct digitally but fail in physical weaving due to yarn tension or loom limitations, causing high waste rates that erase margin.
*   **Market:** Wholesale clients treat the AI designs as "generic" compared to your heritage hand-looms, refusing to place repeat orders beyond the initial novelty purchase.
*   **Financial:** The 15% contingency is insufficient if hardware integration requires custom retrofitting of older Porto mill machinery, blowing the budget before production starts.

**Kill Trigger:**
Terminate the project if **repeat order volume from wholesale clients is below 40% of initial trial orders** after six months. This indicates the designs are not sustaining commercial demand.

**Hidden Risk in Your Numbers:**
You assumed **zero ongoing licensing fees**. Most AI models require compute resources, maintenance, or periodic retraining costs that are rarely one-off. If this is an on-premise model, the "hidden" cost is the specialized engineering talent required to maintain it (likely €80k–€120k/year), which is not in your integration budget. If it's cloud-based, inference costs will scale with usage, directly eating into the margin of every meter of fabric sold.
