**Recommendation: Do not approve the full $1.2M investment at this time.** Instead, authorize a capped $150,000 pilot phase to validate core assumptions before committing to the full 12-month development cycle. The current proposal relies on unverified licensing costs and overly optimistic adoption rates that expose the organization to significant financial risk.

**Primary Failure Modes:**
1.  **Licensing Black Box:** Without quotes from the three partner museums, the 30% marketing allocation could be entirely consumed by content fees, destroying the projected 15% net margin.
2.  **Adoption Delusion:** The assumption that 70% of tourists will access or rent VR headsets is aggressive; friction in hardware distribution often reduces actual usage to <20% in similar heritage pilots.
3.  **Revenue Math:** Even at full capacity (45,000 visitors), $540,000 revenue against a $1.2M spend requires multiple years to break even, contradicting the "15% net margin" claim unless operating costs are near zero.

**Kill/Pivot Triggers:**
*   **Licensing Cost:** If total museum licensing fees exceed $250,000 annually.
*   **Pilot Adoption:** If a pre-launch test with 500 users shows less than 30% willingness to use/headset-rental conversion.
*   **Unit Economics:** If the cost to acquire and serve a single VR user exceeds $8.00 (leaving insufficient margin against the $12 ticket price).

**Note:** The Rivermouth comparison is dangerous without adjusting for population density and tourist demographics. Demand the museum licensing quotes before any further discussion.
