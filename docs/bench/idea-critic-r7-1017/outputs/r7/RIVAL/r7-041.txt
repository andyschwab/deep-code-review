**Recommendation: Proceed with a Conditional Pilot, Not Full Deployment**

Do not commit to the full 150 km rollout based on the current numbers. The financial model is critically fragile because it treats a recurring operational expense (OpEx) as a one-time capital expense (CapEx). Specifically, you have budgeted $700k for software and contingency, but the analytics license alone is $250k/year. Over the 12-month pilot, this consumes 36% of your remaining buffer immediately, leaving only $450k for integration, unknown corrosive maintenance, and contingency. Given the harsh environment, maintenance costs could easily exceed $150k annually, eroding your first-year ROI before the system stabilizes.

**Three Main Failure Modes**

1.  **OpEx Bankruptcy:** The model assumes the $2.5M covers the first year fully. In reality, the recurring $250k license plus unpriced corrosive maintenance creates an immediate cash-flow deficit. If maintenance exceeds $200k/year (a likely scenario in corrosive conditions), the project burns through its contingency within six months, forcing a halt before data maturity is reached.
2.  **Accuracy Decay in Situ:** The 95% accuracy figure is likely a lab or clean-pipe specification. In Athens' aging, corrosive infrastructure, background noise and sensor degradation could drop real-world accuracy below 80%. At that threshold, the cost of false positives (unnecessary excavation crews) may exceed the value of the water saved, turning the system into a net financial loss.
3.  **Installation Underestimation:** The $1,000/installation estimate assumes ideal access. If 20% of the 150 km requires traffic management, deep excavation, or specialized entry permits, installation costs could spike by $60k–$100k, further cannibalizing the software budget.

**Kill/Re-scope Trigger**

**Trigger:** If the **combined actual cost of software licensing and verified maintenance requirements exceeds $450,000** during the first six months of deployment.

*Action:* Immediately halt further sensor installation. Re-scope the project to a 50 km "high-loss" zone to validate the maintenance model and real-world accuracy before attempting the full 150 km network. Do not spend the remaining capital until the unit economics of the corrosive environment are proven.
