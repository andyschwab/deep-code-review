**Recommendation:** Proceed with a **conditional approval** request. The 20% throughput gain and $150k annual savings justify the $2.5M investment only if the waste-to-energy agreement is formalized and maintenance downtime is contractually capped. Do not seek full final authorization yet; instead, ask the CFO for approval to finalize vendor contracts *contingent* on two specific proofs: a signed zero-fee waste acceptance letter and a guaranteed maximum downtime clause.

**Three Biggest Failure Points:**
1.  **The "Verbal" Trap:** The waste-to-energy plant reneges on the free disposal, imposing fees that erase the projected $150k annual savings. Without a signed contract, this $0 cost assumption is a liability, not an asset.
2.  **Hidden Downtime Costs:** The vendor's vague "low single-digit" downtime estimate proves optimistic. If maintenance exceeds 5 days per quarter, the lost production (20% of daily volume) will outweigh the efficiency gains, turning the throughput increase into a net loss.
3.  **Performance Gap vs. Competitor:** Achieving only 85% waste reduction versus the competitor's 90% may seem acceptable, but if the remaining 15% incurs high disposal fees or regulatory penalties not modeled, the unit economics will fail to meet the ROI timeline.

**Halting/Redesign Trigger Metric:**
Initiate a mandatory redesign or halt if **actual waste reduction falls below 75%** OR **unscheduled downtime exceeds 4 days per quarter** during the first 12 months of operation. These thresholds provide a buffer below your 85% target and 20% gain while preventing bleed from operational drag.

*Note: Secure the waste-to-energy agreement in writing before any capital deployment; verbal agreements in supply chain logistics are historically unreliable during budget cycles.*
*Ensure the vendor contract includes a penalty clause for downtime exceeding the guaranteed limit to offset production losses.*
