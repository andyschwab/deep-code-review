**Recommendation:** Proceed with a **Phased Pilot Launch** limited to 50,000 users (6.25% of base) rather than the proposed full 15% rollout. Approve the $850k development budget only upon securing a fixed-cost licensing agreement below $75k. Do not commit to the full 120,000 subscriber conversion target until the pilot validates the 30% engagement lift assumption.

**Primary Failure Modes & Hidden Costs:**
*   **Unrealistic Conversion Assumption:** Converting 15% of existing users to a *new* paid tier in three months is historically anomalous. Competitor data from a music app does not transfer cleanly to language learning; if conversion lands at a more conservative 3%, revenue drops to $228k, failing to cover the $850k development + potential $150k licensing cost.
*   **Licensing Cost Variance:** The $50k–$150k range represents a 200% variance. At the $150k ceiling, your net revenue margin shrinks by 13%, potentially turning the project net-negative in the first six months if conversion lags.
*   **Churn Underestimation:** Gamification often increases initial engagement but can accelerate churn once novelty fades ("novelty decay"). If churn rises from 4% to 6% due to user fatigue with the game mechanics, the lifetime value (LTV) of the new subscribers collapses, invalidating the ARPU model.
*   **Hidden Integration Costs:** The budget lacks line items for QA testing on diverse Android/iOS versions prevalent in Seoul, customer support scaling for gamification bugs, or potential latency costs from real-time music streaming.

**Pre-Agreed Halt Trigger:**
Terminate the project immediately if, after 45 days of the pilot:
1.  **Conversion Rate** is below 4% of the pilot group, OR
2.  **Weekly Active Time** increase is less than 15% (half the projected boost), OR
3.  **Final Licensing Agreement** exceeds $90,000.

*Note: This approach protects the company from a $1M+ loss while preserving the option to scale if the unit economics prove valid.*
*The "competitor music app" metric should be treated as an upper-bound ceiling, not a baseline expectation.*
