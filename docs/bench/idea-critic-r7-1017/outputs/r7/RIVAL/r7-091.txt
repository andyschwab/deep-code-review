**Recommendation: NO-GO (Conditional Hold)**

Do not green-light the full $3.2M investment yet. While the unit cost reduction is attractive, the unquantified certification timeline and Skyforge's scrap rate data create a financial risk profile that exceeds acceptable limits for a full production ramp. Instead, authorize a **$400k Pilot Phase** to produce 500 blades specifically for certification testing and scrap rate validation. Proceed to full ramp-up only if scrap stays under 8% and preliminary regulatory feedback is received within 4 months.

**Three Biggest Failure Modes**

1.  **Certification Limbo:** The regulator takes 18+ months instead of 12, tying up capital while competitors with certified forged blades capture market share. The "9–12 month" estimate is a typical baseline, not a guarantee for novel additive processes.
2.  **Scrap Rate Explosion:** Process variability hits Skyforge's 15% scrap rate (or higher), erasing the 12% unit cost savings. At $1,050 per unit, a 15% scrap rate adds ~$157 to the effective cost of every good blade, making the new unit cost ~$1,067—more expensive than the current forged method.
3.  **Raw Material Volatility:** Metal powder prices rise even 10% in 2025. Unlike forging billets, additive powders are highly specialized; a price spike combined with high scrap rates creates a double-margin compression that the current model cannot absorb.

**Specific Stop/Re-evaluate Triggers**

*   **Scrap Rate:** Halt ramp-up if pilot scrap exceeds **8%** consistently over three production runs. (Break-even analysis suggests anything above 8% negates the cost advantage).
*   **Certification Timeline:** Re-evaluate strategy if no provisional regulatory feedback is received within **6 months**, or if full certification exceeds **14 months**.
*   **Material Costs:** Freeze hiring/expansion if metal powder spot prices increase by **>7%** above current contracts.

**Note:** The Skyforge benchmark is a warning, not a blueprint; their 10% savings were likely temporary until scrap stabilized.
