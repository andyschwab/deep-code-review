**Recommendation for Dr. Chow:**
Approve the pilot only under a "phased engagement" model that explicitly caps initial scope at 500 active users (rather than 1,500) and reallocates $40,000 of the budget immediately to a loaner tablet pool. This corrects the arithmetic error in your adoption estimate (15% of 70% is 1,260 students, not 1,500) and addresses the critical equity gap for the 3,600 students without smartphones. By starting smaller, you reduce the risk of high-visibility failure while validating whether the bot actually drives the targeted 10% reduction in crisis referrals before scaling to the full 1,260 projected users.

**Primary Failure Modes:**
1.  **Equity Backlash:** Launching without a device strategy excludes 30% of students, likely triggering parental complaints and board scrutiny that overshadows any clinical success.
2.  **Engagement Collapse:** If the actual weekly active rate falls below 8% (common for wellness bots), the cost-per-interaction will skyrocket to over $46, making the program fiscally indefensible compared to hiring two additional counselors.
3.  **Data Silos:** If the bot cannot integrate with existing student information systems to track referral outcomes automatically, you will be unable to prove the 10% reduction target, rendering the pilot's results anecdotal.

**Pre-agreed Stop/Pivot Trigger:**
Halt or pivot the project if, by **15 March 2025** (midpoint), the program fails to achieve **both** of the following:
*   A sustained weekly active user rate of **10%** among the enrolled cohort.
*   A verified **5% interim reduction** in crisis referrals compared to the same period in the prior year.

If either metric is missed, immediately pause new enrollments and redirect remaining funds to direct human intervention services.
