**Recommendation: Proceed with a Phased Pilot, Not Full Launch**

Do not commit the full $520,000 yet. Instead, approve a **$65,000, 3-month Pilot Phase** targeting 50 users. Use this phase to validate the core assumption that students will pay a premium for VR immersion and to lock down the licensing model. If the pilot achieves 80% retention and confirms the flat-fee licensing option, greenlight the full build. If not, the project is killed before the major capital outlay.

**Three Biggest Failure Modes**

1.  **The Licensing Trap (Hidden Cost):** You have not quantified the per-user royalty. If licensors demand a royalty >$5/user/month, your unit economics collapse, turning a projected profit into a loss on every active subscriber. This is the single biggest financial risk.
2.  **Hardware Friction & Support Burden:** Distributing 150 headsets creates a massive hidden operational cost. Broken hardware, shipping logistics, and user technical support will divert engineering resources from product development to IT helpdesk work, likely exceeding the $125k marketing budget in hidden labor costs.
3.  **Novelty Churn:** The 15% churn assumption is optimistic for hardware-dependent products. If users find the headset uncomfortable or the novelty wears off after two months, churn could spike to 40%, making the $1.2M revenue projection impossible regardless of content quality.

**Pre-Agreed Kill Trigger**

Kill the project immediately if **either** of the following occurs at the end of the 3-month Pilot:
*   **Enrollment:** Fewer than **40 out of 50** pilot users (80%) remain active subscribers after Month 2.
*   **Licensing:** The third-party licensors refuse a flat fee and demand a royalty structure that exceeds **$4.00 per user/month**.

*Note: This approach protects $455,000 of capital while testing the specific unknowns that threaten the business model.*
