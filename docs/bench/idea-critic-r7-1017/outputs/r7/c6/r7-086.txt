**Recommendation: Proceed with a conditional "Go-If" pilot, not a full rollout.**
Do not commit the full $1.2M capex yet. Instead, initiate a binding feasibility phase capped at $45k to resolve the two critical unknowns (interconnection fees and equipment discount scalability) before September 1, 2024. Only if these variables confirm the financial model should you proceed to the full install.

**Primary Failure Modes:**
*   **Financial:** The revenue model is overstated. Your calculation assumes 100% capacity factor (solar panels do not produce peak power 24/7/365). A realistic ~20% capacity factor reduces projected annual revenue from $360k to roughly $72k, drastically extending the payback period. Additionally, if the 10% equipment discount does not scale to 120 homes, capex could exceed the budget by >$50k.
*   **Regulatory:** The 30-day permit assumption is a single point of failure. Any delay pushes the install past the September 30, 2025 target, potentially missing tax credits or grant windows. If the utility revokes or reduces the 15¢/kWh net-metering rate before the contract is signed, the project becomes insolvent.
*   **Technical/Grid:** The local grid infrastructure may not support 600kW (120 homes × 5kW) of inbound power without costly upgrades, which the undisclosed interconnection fee might cover. If the fee exceeds your buffer, the project stalls.

**Kill Criteria (Stop/Pivot Triggers):**
1.  **Interconnection Fee:** If the utility confirms fees **> $50,000**, halt immediately. This erodes the contingency budget required for the discount risk.
2.  **Net-Metering Rate:** If the guaranteed rate is **< 12¢/kWh**, pivot or stop. Below this threshold, the revised realistic revenue (~$57k/year) cannot support the debt service on $1.2M capex.
3.  **Permit Timeline:** If city approval exceeds **45 days** from application, pause the rollout. This indicates a high risk of missing the 2025 deadline.
4.  **Equipment Pricing:** If the vendor confirms the 10% discount applies *only* to the 200-home tier and cannot be prorated, stop. You must re-bid the project to ensure the $1.2M budget covers the actual unit cost for 120 homes.

**Note:** Your current revenue math assumes continuous peak production; recalculate using a 20% capacity factor to avoid presenting inflated returns to the COO. Secure the interconnection fee quote in writing before any other expenditure.
