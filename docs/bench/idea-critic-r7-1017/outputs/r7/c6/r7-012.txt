**Recommendation: GO-IF**
Proceed only if a binding compliance cost estimate is secured below $25,000 and a pilot with 500 users validates wallet adoption exceeds 40% before committing the full $120,000 budget. Do not launch the full program in Q3 2025 without these gates.

**Three Main Failure Modes**
1.  **Regulatory Cost Overrun:** Your budget assumes development and design costs but leaves compliance as an unknown. If state digital-asset regulations classify these NFTs as securities or require extensive reporting, legal fees could easily exceed $50,000, blowing the budget before code is written.
2.  **Adoption Delusion:** You assume 70% of regulars (10,500 people) will download a wallet. Industry data suggests friction for non-crypto natives is extreme; if only 15% adopt (2,250 people), you will generate only ~$2,250 in extra annual revenue against a $120,000 spend, resulting in a massive net loss.
3.  **Revenue Math Error:** Your projection of 12,000 extra sales implies a 10% volume increase (12,000 / 120,000 total sales), yet you cite a 5% visit increase. Unless every returning customer doubles their purchase frequency or ticket size, the math does not support the $120k investment payback.

**Stop/Pivot Trigger**
 Halt full implementation if the pilot program (limited to 500 users) shows a wallet claim rate below 40% within the first 30 days, or if the initial legal compliance quote exceeds $25,000.

**Strongest Objection**
Maya Patel will likely ask: "Why are we forcing customers to navigate complex crypto wallets just to get a loyalty discount when a simple phone-number-based system achieves the same retention goal at 1% of the cost and zero regulatory risk?"

**Better Alternative**
Replace the NFT infrastructure with a traditional digital loyalty platform integrated directly into your existing POS. This captures the same data and drives repeat visits without the $120,000 development cost, the regulatory uncertainty, or the friction of requiring customers to manage private keys.
