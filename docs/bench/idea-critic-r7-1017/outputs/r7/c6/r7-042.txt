**Recommendation: Do not go full-scale yet.** Proceed only with a **3-month capped pilot** focused on validating the reusable tote acceptance rate and supplier reliability before committing to the full summer line rollout.

**Three Main Failure Modes & Hidden Costs:**
1.  **Revenue Erosion from Tote Refusal:** Your model assumes 68% acceptance, but if this drops to 50%, you must absorb the $12 tote cost for half your orders or lose the sale. At 500 orders/month, a 18% drop in acceptance (from 68% to 50%) adds **$1,080/month in unbudgeted hardware costs** ($12 × 90 extra units), wiping out your projected disposal fee savings ($250/month) and turning the pilot cash-flow negative immediately.
2.  **Supplier Concentration Risk:** Relying on a single supplier for 10,000 biodegradable pouches/month creates a single point of failure. Biodegradable materials often have longer lead times and higher defect rates than standard plastics. A single month's delay stops 500 orders, potentially costing far more in lost revenue and expedited shipping than the $7,500 material budget.
3.  **Flawed Break-Even Arithmetic:** Your break-even calculation appears optimistic. You project $0.50/order savings on disposal fees. At 500 orders/month, that is only **$250/month in savings**. Against a monthly material cost of roughly $7,500 (pouches) + $6,000 (totes for 500 units, even if reused, the initial outlay is high), the $250 savings cannot possibly cover the capital expenditure in 12 months unless the "savings" includes avoided purchase of *standard* packaging (which you didn't subtract from the $0.75 pouch cost). If the $0.75 is *incremental* cost over standard packaging, the math fails completely.

**Kill/Pivot Trigger:**
Terminate or pivot the program if, after 60 days:
*   **Tote Acceptance Rate < 60%:** This indicates the customer value proposition is weak, and the hardware subsidy is unsustainable.
*   **Supplier On-Time Delivery < 95%:** Any significant delay proves the supply chain cannot support the summer line volume.

**Arithmetic Flaw Identified:**
The break-even claim of 12 months is unsupported by the provided numbers.
*   **Monthly Savings:** 500 orders × $0.50 = $250.
*   **Monthly Material Cost:** (10,000 pouches × $0.75) + (500 totes × $12) = $7,500 + $6,000 = $13,500 (initial month).
*   Even ignoring the tote reuse cycle, the $250 monthly saving cannot recoup a $13,500 initial outlay in 12 months ($250 × 12 = $3,000). You are likely missing the comparison against the *current* packaging cost baseline in your net-savings figure.

*Note: Verify the definition of "$0.75 each"—is this the total cost or the incremental premium over your current packaging? The current math implies a massive net loss.*
