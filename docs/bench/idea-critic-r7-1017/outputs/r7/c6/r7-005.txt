**Recommendation: Proceed with a "Go-If" Pilot, Not Full Launch**
Approve the project only under a staged commitment: release a single-vineyard MVP (Minimum Viable Product) to test conversion rates before committing the full €250,000. The current plan relies on unverified assumptions about user behavior and hidden costs that threaten the break-even timeline. Do not authorize the full budget until the licensing fee is fixed and a pilot proves users actually convert from VR to physical visits.

**Three Biggest Failure Modes**
1.  **Conversion Delusion:** The assumption that 20% of VR users will visit physically is likely optimistic; if conversion is below 5%, direct sales drop to €18,000, making the €250,000 investment unrecoverable within the projected timeframe.
2.  **Cost Overrun from Unknowns:** The €30,000–€70,000 licensing variance and undefined marketing budget could inflate initial costs by 40%, pushing the break-even point well past Q2 2026 and straining the path to the €2.2M revenue target.
3.  **Regulatory Blockade:** If Italian regulatory clearance for VR content is delayed or denied, the €60,000 spent on 3-D scanning becomes a sunk cost with no deployable product.

**Stop/Pivot Trigger**
Halt or pivot the project immediately if the MVP pilot (limited to one vineyard) fails to achieve a **4% conversion rate** from download to on-site ticket purchase within the first six months, or if the total confirmed upfront costs (licensing + marketing) exceed **€200,000**.

*Note: The current financial model excludes the marketing spend required to hit 30,000 downloads, rendering the Q2 2026 break-even calculation incomplete. Immediate validation of the 3-D engine licensing fee is required before any funds are released.*
