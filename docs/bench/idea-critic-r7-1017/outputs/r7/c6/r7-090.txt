**Recommendation:** Proceed with a **conditional pilot ("Go-If")**, not a full commitment. Authorize the $2.5M spend only if the vendor signs a binding performance guarantee covering the "low single-digit" downtime ambiguity and confirms the waste-to-energy intake in writing. The current verbal agreement and vague maintenance figures represent unacceptable financial risk for a project of this scale.

**Three Biggest Failure Modes:**
1.  **Hidden Operational Costs:** The "zero-charge" grain disposal relies on a verbal agreement; if the plant imposes fees or rejects the volume, the projected $150k annual savings evaporates, turning the line into a net cost center.
2.  **Throughput Illusion:** If maintenance downtime exceeds 4% (approx. 15 days/year), the theoretical 20% throughput gain (10k to 12k bottles) vanishes, failing to justify the capital outlay against the competitor's 90% reduction benchmark.
3.  **Performance Gap:** Achieving only 70–80% waste reduction instead of the projected 85% fails to meet the strategic "zero-waste" branding promise, damaging credibility with stakeholders who expect parity with the competitor's 90% claim.

**Kill Criteria (Halt/Redesign Triggers):**
*   **Waste Reduction < 75%** during the first 90 days of operation (below this, the environmental ROI is insufficient).
*   **Unplanned Downtime > 6 days per quarter** (exceeding the "low single-digit" vendor implication).
*   **Verified Handling Fees > $20/ton** for spent grain (eroding >15% of projected savings).
