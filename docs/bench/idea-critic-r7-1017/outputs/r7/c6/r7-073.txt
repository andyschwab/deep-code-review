**Recommendation: Proceed with a staged "Go-If" pilot, not a full immediate rollout.**

Approve the US$85,000 development cost conditional on a 6-week limited launch in two stores (including the flagship) to validate performance on low-end Android devices. Do not commit to the remaining six stores or the US$30,000 marketing budget until the pilot confirms that latency does not suppress the conversion lift below 0.8 percentage points.

**Main Failure Modes & Hidden Risks:**
1.  **Device Fragmentation Risk:** The 1.5% lift was achieved in a controlled flagship environment; if low-end Android phones (common in the broader market) experience >3 seconds of load time, adoption may drop to near zero, rendering the conversion lift theoretical.
2.  **Margin Erosion:** The basket size increase (ARS 45) is only ~2%. If the US$15,000/month support cost is fixed regardless of usage, and adoption is low due to latency, the tool becomes a net loss leader immediately.
3.  **Timeline Slippage:** A 4-month timeline targeting July 2025 leaves no buffer for the necessary Android optimization; missing the peak seasonal window could delay ROI by a full year.
4.  **Hidden Cost:** The "marketing push" is listed as unbudgeted; without it, organic discovery of the AR feature may be too low to move the needle on overall store conversion, making the tech investment invisible.

**Kill Criteria:**
Terminate the full rollout if, after 3 months of the staged pilot:
*   The conversion lift averages **below 0.8 percentage points** (less than half the pilot result) across the test stores.
*   User session data shows **>40% abandonment** of the AR feature specifically on devices older than 3 years.
*   The net profit impact (incremental margin minus support costs) is **negative** for two consecutive months.

**Note:** Your current math assumes the ARS 45 basket lift applies to *all* visitors, but it likely only applies to *users who engage*; ensure your financial model distinguishes between "store conversion" and "feature engagement." Verify if the US$15,000/month support scales down if the rollout is paused, as fixed costs destroy the "staged" advantage.
