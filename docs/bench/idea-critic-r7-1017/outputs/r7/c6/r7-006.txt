**Recommendation:** Proceed with a **conditional pilot ("Go-If")** capped at **$185,000** for the first 90 days, explicitly excluding the loaner tablet program until engagement data validates the need. Do not commit the full $250,000 or the full six-month timeline upfront.

**Critical Failure Points:**
1.  **Arithmetic & Engagement Gap:** Your estimate of 1,500 weekly users (15% of the 70% with phones) implies 9,000 monthly interactions, but this assumes every user interacts only 6 times/month. If usage is lower, the cost-per-intervention skyrockets. More critically, ignoring the 30% without devices (3,600 students) creates an equity gap that could trigger community backlash and skew data, making the "10% reduction" target unachievable across the whole district.
2.  **Hidden Infrastructure Cost:** The budget lacks a line item for loaner tablets. Providing devices to even half of the 3,600 unconnected students would likely exceed the remaining $65,000 buffer, forcing a mid-pilot budget crisis or a reduction in software licensing/features.
3.  **False Comparative Baseline:** Matching a neighbor's 8% success to your 10% target ignores demographic differences. If your at-risk population faces barriers the neighbor's did not (e.g., language, device access), the bot may see high adoption but low efficacy, wasting funds on a tool that doesn't move the needle on crisis referrals.

**Pre-Agreed Kill Criteria (Stop/Pivot Triggers):**
*   **Stop Immediately:** If weekly active users fall below **800** (approx. 8% of connected students) by **March 1, 2025**, or if the cost per meaningful interaction exceeds **$45**.
*   **Pivot Strategy:** If engagement is high (>1,200 users) but crisis referrals do not drop by at least **4%** (half the target) by the 90-day mark, pause the expansion to loaner devices and reallocate funds to human-in-the-loop support rather than scaling the bot.

**Note:** Your current user math (1,500 users × 6 interactions = 9,000) is fragile; verify the assumed frequency of use per student before finalizing the vendor contract. The $250k budget is insufficient to cover both the software and a meaningful device loaner program without risking early termination.
