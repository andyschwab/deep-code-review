**Three Biggest Failure Modes:**
1.  **Technical (False Positives):** The model's 95% accuracy may hide a high false-positive rate at scale. If the system flags too many non-existent faults, technicians will suffer "alert fatigue," ignoring real warnings and increasing, rather than decreasing, downtime.
2.  **Financial (Overestimated Savings):** The $12M savings projection relies on linearly extrapolating 15 hours of saved downtime from a 120km pilot to the entire national network. This ignores that the Midwest corridor may have been an outlier; if the national average downtime reduction is only 5%, the ROI turns negative against the $4.7M upfront cost.
3.  **Operational (Training Bottleneck):** Training 150 technicians simultaneously by October 2025 risks operational disruption. If the training quality varies, inconsistent adoption across regions will create data silos, rendering the predictive model ineffective due to poor input data.

**Pre-agreed Kill Criterion:**
Scrap the project immediately if, after the first 12 months of Phase 1, the **net verified savings fall below $1.5 million** (covering only the annual storage and amortized license costs), or if the **false-positive rate exceeds 10%**, causing a measurable increase in technician response times.
