**Recommendation: Proceed with a conditional pilot (Go-If), not a full rollout.**

Launch the blockchain pilot on the 15,000-bottle single-cask release only if you secure a contractual cap on integration costs and a hard exit clause. Do not commit to the full 45,000-bottle annual production until the pilot proves that customers will pay the premium, not just scan the label. The math for a full rollout is fragile: at £0.25 per label, your annual variable cost is £11,250 plus £8,500 integration, totaling £19,750. To cover this solely via a 7% premium on a £15 base cost (£12+£3), you need roughly £282,000 in *additional* revenue. While a 7% uplift on 45,000 bottles theoretically generates ~£47,000 in extra margin (assuming the premium is pure profit, which it is not), this ignores the significant risk that the "premium" merely offsets marketing spend or fails to materialize on volume. The competitor's 5% uplift at 120,000 units does not guarantee your 7% at 45,000; scale dynamics differ.

**Three Biggest Failure Modes:**
1.  **Scan-Trust Gap:** Customers scan the QR code out of curiosity but do not trust the blockchain data enough to justify a price increase, resulting in high engagement metrics but zero revenue uplift.
2.  **Margin Erosion:** The 7% premium is absorbed by retailer margins or promotional discounting rather than reaching Glenforge's bottom line, leaving you with the blockchain fees but no net profit increase.
3.  **Integration Bloat:** The "one-time" £8,500 integration fee escalates due to legacy ERP incompatibilities, a common hidden cost in supply chain digitization, destroying the first-year ROI before a single bottle is scanned.

**Kill Criteria:**
Terminate the project immediately if, by 31 January 2026 (mid-pilot), the **net premium revenue** (price uplift minus blockchain fees) from the single-cask release is less than **£1,500**. This threshold ensures the project is generating actual profit, not just activity, and allows you to cut losses before the peak sales season ends.
