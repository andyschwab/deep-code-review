**Recommendation: Proceed with a "Go-If" Pilot, Not Full Launch**
Do not commit the full $520,000 yet. Approve a capped **$60,000, 3-month pilot** to validate the licensing model and actual user demand before building the full software suite. Proceed to full development **only if** the pilot secures a flat-fee licensing deal under $90,000 and achieves a 25% opt-in rate from a test group of 100 students.

**Three Biggest Failure Modes**
1.  **The Licensing Trap:** The "unknown" royalty model is a critical risk. If vendors demand per-user royalties instead of a flat fee, your marginal cost per student spikes, potentially destroying the $50/month margin entirely as you scale.
2.  **Opt-In Overestimation:** Assuming 35% (770 students) will adopt a new, hardware-dependent format is aggressive. If adoption mirrors typical ed-tech upsells (often <10%), you will have 150 headsets sitting idle while carrying depreciation costs, failing to reach the revenue needed to cover the $200k software investment.
3.  **Hardware Obsolescence & Support:** The $300/headset budget assumes current pricing and durability. VR hardware evolves rapidly; cheap units may lack the fidelity for language nuance or break under student use, creating hidden replacement costs and support bottlenecks not in the $125k marketing/ops budget.

**Pre-Agreed Kill Trigger**
Kill the project immediately if, by the end of Month 4 of the pilot:
*   **Enrollment:** Fewer than **40 students** (25% of the 160-student pilot target) remain active subscribers.
*   **Cost:** The finalized licensing agreement exceeds **$95,000** total or mandates a per-user royalty higher than **$5/student/month**.

*Note: The current revenue projection of $1.2M appears overstated; 770 users at $50/mo with 15% annual churn yields closer to $850k over two years, not $1.2M. Correcting this math is essential before presenting to the CEO.*
