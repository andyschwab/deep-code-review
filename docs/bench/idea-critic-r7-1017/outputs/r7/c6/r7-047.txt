**Recommendation:** Proceed with a **Go-If** pilot, conditional on capping the initial hardware spend at 40% of the budget (480,000 ARS) for a single kiosk and a limited web A/B test, rather than the full rollout.

**Rationale & Failure Modes:**
Your current plan relies on a conversion funnel that is likely overstated. Assuming 30% of website visitors (6,000 users) will voluntarily launch a heavy immersive experience is aggressive; industry benchmarks for optional web-based 3D content often hover below 5%. If participation is actually 5%, your revenue drops to 50,000 ARS/month, extending the payback period from 6 months to over 3 years, excluding the unknown maintenance and potential 480,000 ARS/year privacy compliance costs. The primary failure mode is **sunk cost in underutilized hardware**: buying 1,200,000 ARS of headsets before validating that guests actually want to wear them in a tasting room setting. Additionally, the "hidden" cost of staff time to sanitize and manage headsets during peak hours is unaccounted for and could degrade the core tasting experience.

**Kill Criteria:**
Terminate or pivot the project if, after 45 days of operation:
1.  **Adoption Rate:** Less than 8% of kiosk foot-traffic or 2% of web visitors initiate the tour.
2.  **Conversion:** The tour-to-purchase conversion rate is below 1.5% (generating <90,000 ARS/month).
3.  **Operational Friction:** Staff report that headset management delays regular tasting service by more than 10 minutes during peak hours.

If these thresholds are met, pivot immediately to a lower-fidelity 360° video tour accessible on personal devices to eliminate hardware and maintenance costs.

*Note: The projected 300,000 ARS monthly revenue assumes a frictionless user journey that rarely exists in new VR deployments; the proposed cap limits exposure while testing real demand.*
*Ensure the 120,000 ARS quarterly privacy cost is formally quoted by legal before any data collection begins, as this alone could negate pilot profitability.*
