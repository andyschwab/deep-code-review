There is no publicly available statistic detailing the specific percentage of Series A founders in Singapore who raised their pricing after securing their first enterprise customer in 2024.

This level of granular data does not exist for several reasons. First, pricing strategies are highly confidential operational details that startups rarely disclose in public filings or press releases. Second, venture capital databases (such as Crunchbase, PitchBook, or CB Insights) track funding rounds, valuations, and investor names, but they do not monitor real-time pricing adjustments relative to specific customer milestones. Third, 2024 is currently ongoing; comprehensive annual reports analyzing founder behavior for the full year will not be compiled and published by research firms until early 2025.

Using a fabricated or unverified number to open your talk carries significant risk. If an audience member—particularly an investor or fellow founder—asks for the source, and you cannot provide a reputable study, it may undermine your credibility immediately.

Instead of relying on a non-existent statistic, consider framing your opening around the well-established strategic principle behind the question. It is a common and recommended best practice globally, including in Singapore's mature startup ecosystem, for B2B SaaS companies to re-evaluate pricing upon landing their first enterprise client. This milestone often validates that the product solves a high-value problem, justifying a shift from early-adopter pricing to value-based enterprise tiers.

You might open your talk with a qualitative hook such as:
*"In Singapore's competitive 2024 landscape, a critical inflection point for Series A founders isn't just closing the first enterprise deal—it's having the courage to raise prices immediately afterward. While exact numbers are closely guarded secrets, the pattern is clear: founders who align pricing with enterprise value post-first-signature secure stronger unit economics for their growth phase."*

This approach allows you to address the trend accurately without risking your reputation on false data. If you absolutely require quantitative data, you would need to commission a private survey of Singaporean founders or wait for specialized regional reports (e.g., from Golden Gate Ventures, Sequoia SEA, or BCG) that may analyze pricing trends later in the year.
