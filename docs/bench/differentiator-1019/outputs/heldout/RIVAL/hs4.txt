This data is proprietary, held internally by vendors (like CrowdStrike, Palo Alto Networks, or local MSSPs) and never aggregated into a public benchmark by industry analysts (Gartner, IDC) at that level of geographic and vertical specificity.

However, for your forecast cell, you should not leave it blank or guess randomly. Based on general public sector procurement data and enterprise cybersecurity benchmarks in North America, you can use a **calculated estimate range**:

**Estimated Range: 270 – 450 days (9 – 15 months)**

### Reasoning for this Estimate:
1.  **Public Sector Procurement Laws:** Canadian municipalities must adhere to strict tendering processes (e.g., MERX, Biddingo). This mandates public RFPs, evaluation committees, and council approvals, which inherently add 3–6 months compared to private sector deals.
2.  **Budget Cycles:** Municipalities often operate on calendar-year or fiscal-year budgets with rigid approval windows. If a deal misses a Q3 council meeting, it often slips to the next fiscal year.
3.  **Enterprise Cybersecurity Complexity:** These deals involve CISOs, IT directors, legal teams, and often third-party auditors. The technical proof-of-concept (POC) phase alone can take 60–90 days.
4.  **Current Market Context:** Post-pandemic digitization and rising ransomware threats have accelerated some approvals, but economic caution has lengthened others. The average has stabilized around the 12-month mark for significant six-to-seven-figure contracts.
