# Likely objections, ranked by damage

**1. "You'll cannibalize our cheapest plan." (Claire, highest risk)**
Every $29 customer who drops to free costs $348 a year, and that loss is certain, while the upside is speculative. Your 4,200 customers are the only revenue you can count on.
*Bring:* For cheapest-plan customers, the distribution of forms in use and monthly submissions. Report the share under 3 forms and 100 submissions. Then give a downside table: if 5%, 10% or 20% of those customers downgrade, what is the annual revenue loss, and how many new paid conversions are needed to offset it? Also bring their tenure and churn history. Propose grandfathering or limit design as mitigation.

**2. "You don't know what a free user costs." (Ravi and Claire)**
This is your admitted gap, so expect it first. If free users cost more than 4% of them yield, the plan loses money at scale.
*Bring:* Hosting cost per submission and per stored form. Support tickets per trial user, versus paid users, from the last two quarters. Estimated spam and abuse signup rate, since free tiers attract bots. Then show breakeven: (4% × $29 × months paid, net of margin) must exceed cost per free user. Show the result at 2%, 4% and 6%.

**3. "4% is invented, and free users are lower-intent than trial users." (Claire)**
Trial converts at 11%, but trial users chose to evaluate paying. Free users have no deadline pressure. 4% within 6 months may be optimistic.
*Bring:* Published freemium conversion benchmarks, with sources, for comparable tools. Your own data on trial users who didn't convert but kept using, and on how many hit 3 forms or 100 submissions in 14 days. A sensitivity table, not a single point estimate.

**4. "Free will eat the trial." (Claire)**
Prospects who would have started a trial and converted at 11% may take free instead and never pay.
*Bring:* Monthly trial starts, trial-to-paid by signup source, and what share of current converters would have fit inside the free limits. Model net paid conversions with and without the free tier.

**5. "Are the limits right?" (Ravi)**
Too generous and nobody upgrades. Too tight and it's a worse trial with no word of mouth.
*Bring:* Usage distribution for trial and paying users across forms and submissions. Show where 3/100 sits as a percentile, and compare 5/200 and 2/50 alternatives.

**6. "Is top of funnel actually our problem?" (Ravi)**
If signups aren't the bottleneck, free solves the wrong problem.
*Bring:* Current signup volume and CAC by channel, the referral share of signups, and where the funnel drops hardest (visit to signup, or trial to paid). Consider showing "Powered by Acme" branding on free forms as a built-in referral channel and quantify it.

**7. "It's hard to take back." (Ravi)**
Removing a free tier later damages trust.
*Bring:* A reversible pilot proposal: a 60 to 90 day test on a subset of traffic or one region, with predefined kill criteria on downgrade rate, cost per free user and conversion. Include engineering effort estimates.

**Pre-empt in the pitch:** Lead with objections 1 and 2 and answer them with numbers. A pilot with kill criteria turns the unknowns into things the test will measure.

*Note: I've assumed the cheapest plan is $29 and that you can pull per-account usage; I didn't have any of your real figures.*
