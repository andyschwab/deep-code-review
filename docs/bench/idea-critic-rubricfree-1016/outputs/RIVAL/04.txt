## Build vs. buy: Harbor Outfitters analytics dashboard

**Assumption to replace:** I used $180k fully loaded cost per engineer, since you didn't give one. Swap in your real figure.

| | Buy (vendor) | Build (your estimate) |
|---|---|---|
| Year 1 | $96k | ~$120k build (8 engineer-months) + ~$30k maintenance + hosting/access control (unpriced) |
| Year 2+ | $96k/yr | ~$45k/yr maintenance + hosting (unpriced) |
| 3-year total | ~$288k | ~$255k + hosting/access control |
| Time to first value | Likely weeks (confirm with the vendor) | 4+ months |

On your numbers, build is only about 10% cheaper over three years, and the unpriced items alone could erase that. Build wins only if the estimate holds and needs stay stable.

**Where your build case is weakest**

1. **The ad hoc querying unknown is the whole decision.** If finance wants self-serve next year, you are building a query layer, semantic model and permissions, not 25 reports. That could double the effort. The vendor product may already include it, so you'd be comparing against capability you can't match.
2. **The 4-month figure is a point estimate with no contingency.** It's unclear whether it covers data modeling, testing, rollout and training, or only dashboard construction.
3. **Hosting and access control are unpriced.** Row-level security for margin data is slow, risky work, and it's the part that most often runs over.
4. **25% of one engineer for maintenance is probably low.** Source schema changes, data quality complaints, and support for 60 users usually land on the same person.
5. **Opportunity cost is missing.** Eight engineer-months is not free just because salaries are already paid. Say what else those engineers would ship.
6. **Key-person risk.** If one of the two builders leaves, maintenance falls apart.
7. **The vendor side is shaky too.** Price increases, seat growth beyond 60, and integration work are unquantified, so the "buy" column is also soft.

**What Marcus will most likely push back on**

- **"What happens if this runs 2x?"** At 8 months, build is ~$240k in year 1 versus $96k. Have this number ready.
- **"Why is the saving so small for this much risk?"** A ~$33k saving over three years doesn't pay for the variance.
- **"Who owns it in 18 months?"** He'll want a named owner and a budget line for maintenance.
- **"Why not buy now and revisit?"** This is his strongest argument. A 1-year vendor term is cheap insurance against both the ad hoc unknown and your estimate.
- **"What's the stop condition?"** He'll want milestones and a point where you cancel.

**What I'd do before the meeting**

- Get a written answer from finance on self-serve ad hoc needs.
- Price hosting and access control, and add a 30–50% contingency to the estimate.
- Ask the vendor about a 1-year term, seat flexibility and an exit clause.
- If you still favor build, propose a milestone-gated plan: 6-week prototype, go/no-go, capped spend.

Note: my cost figures are illustrative and depend on your loaded engineer cost. The vendor's capabilities and time to deploy are unverified.
