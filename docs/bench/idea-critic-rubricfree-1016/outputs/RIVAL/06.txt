**Short answer:** as framed ("each site covers its own daytime"), the proposal leaves a gap. The 7-hour offset cannot give both sites 08:00-20:00 local, and the fix moves pain around rather than removing it.

**The math:** Lisbon 08:00-20:00 is Denver 01:00-13:00. Denver 08:00-20:00 is Lisbon 15:00-03:00. That leaves Lisbon 03:00-08:00 (Denver 20:00-01:00) uncovered. Closing it means something like Lisbon 07:00-19:00 and Denver 12:00-00:00. Denver then works until midnight and Lisbon starts at 07:00, so neither site's "own daytime" holds.

**Risks and mitigations**

1. **Uncovered or midnight handoff window.** The Denver-to-Lisbon handoff lands at midnight Denver, when no one is fresh.
 *Mitigation:* make the written handoff doc (open incidents, flapping alerts, pending changes) mandatory and async. Alternatively, shift the boundaries to Lisbon 06:00-18:00 and Denver 11:00-23:00, and explicitly choose which site takes the early or late tail.

2. **Denver load is 50% higher per head.** Covering 14 shifts a week: Denver averages 1.75 shifts per person, Lisbon 1.17. On a weekly rotation, Denver is on every 4th week and Lisbon every 6th. If one Denver person is out, the other three average 2.33 shifts each.
 *Mitigation:* either rebalance (Lisbon covers more hours, such as 13h shifts or extra weekend coverage) or compensate the difference explicitly. Don't just assume 4 is enough. Show Tom a leave-adjusted load model.

3. **Night pages don't disappear.** 9 of 23 weekly pages are overnight today. They will now fall in Denver's 22:00-00:00 and Lisbon's 06:00-08:00 edges, plus any residual overlap gaps.
 *Mitigation:* get a pages-by-hour histogram from last quarter and set boundaries from the data, not from a symmetric split. Also cut the noisy alerts, since 23 a week is itself a burnout driver.

4. **Single point of failure per shift.** One person per 12 hours, and a long incident can cross the handoff.
 *Mitigation:* add a secondary or escalation tier, and a rule that an incident in progress stays with its owner for a defined overlap, then transfers explicitly.

5. **Stipend fairness and pay structure.** A flat weekly stipend would pay Denver the same for more frequent duty.
 *Mitigation:* pay per shift, with uplifts for off-hours (evenings, early mornings, weekends and holidays) and a separate per-page or time-in-lieu payment after a night page. The two countries' pay and holiday rules differ, so have HR and legal check each country's rules on rest periods and on-call pay.

6. **Daylight saving mismatch.** The EU and US change clocks on different dates, so the offset drops to 6 hours for roughly a week in late October (Oct 25-Nov 1 this year) and again in spring.
 *Mitigation:* define shifts in UTC, publish the changeover weeks in advance, and review the schedule twice a year.

7. **Expertise concentration.** Four Denver engineers may not all know every service.
 *Mitigation:* audit runbook coverage per service and cross-train before launch.

8. **Burnout of the two at-risk people.**
 *Mitigation:* keep them off the first rotation cycle or on lighter shifts, and run a 60-90 day pilot with defined success metrics: pages per person, after-hours pages, handoff incidents and a team survey.

*Note:* I'm assuming the offset is Lisbon ahead of Denver by 7 hours and that on-call is one primary per shift. If your roster has other roles, the load figures change.
