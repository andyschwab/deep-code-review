## The three questions Elena is most likely to ask

**1. "Why do we believe apps retain 1.5x better, and what does that retention gain actually earn us?"**
She will go straight to your central assumption. A competitor's remark at a conference is hearsay: unknown definition of retention, unknown product, unknown cohort, and a likely selection effect (people who install apps are already your most loyal users). Expect her to follow with: "What is the dollar value of 1.5x?" You need to connect it to revenue: retained users x conversion x price, against a 6-month, 4-person build plus ongoing maintenance of two platforms.

**2. "Why not a PWA or a better mobile web experience first?"**
72% of sessions are on mobile browsers, so the demand is for a good mobile experience, not necessarily a native one. She will ask what native gives you that a PWA, push via web, home-screen install, and a faster mobile site don't. Related: "Can we test the retention hypothesis cheaply before committing a team for six months?"

**3. "What do app store fees do to our margin, and what does the team cost us in what we're not building?"**
Store fees (typically 15-30%) apply to subscriptions started in-app. Only about 18% of new subscriptions start on mobile today, but that share would likely rise if apps succeed, and you may not be able to steer users to web checkout cleanly. She'll also ask about opportunity cost: what the 4 people would otherwise ship, and whether June 2027 is a hard date or a guess. Feature parity vs. subset will come up here, because parity pushes the timeline and cost.

## Where your numbers are too thin

- **The 1.5x retention figure.** Single secondhand source, no definition (D1? D30? subscriber churn?), no baseline from your own data. This is the weakest point and the whole case rests on it.
- **No baseline retention numbers.** You cite 310,000 MAU but not your current retention curves, churn, or conversion. Without them, 1.5x can't be turned into users or dollars.
- **Missing financial model.** There's no revenue upside, no build and ongoing maintenance cost, no payback period. "4 people x 6 months" is effort, not cost. Also unaccounted for: app review cycles, QA across devices, and two-platform upkeep after launch.
- **Fee impact is unquantified.** You have 18% mobile-originated new subscriptions but no subscription volume, price, or fee tier (the reduced small-business rate may apply to you). Compute the margin hit as a range, including a scenario where mobile share grows.
- **The 72% mobile figure cuts both ways.** It shows mobile matters, but not that those users want an app. You have no install-intent evidence (survey, waitlist, or push-opt-in data).
- **Scope undecided.** Parity vs. subset changes the cost and timeline, so the 6-month estimate isn't defensible until you choose. Recommend a subset (core planning, grocery list, saved plans) and say what's cut.
- **June 2027 date.** It's derived from the plan, not from a market or seasonal reason. Say why that date matters, or present it as a target.

**Before December 9:** pull your own cohort data on users who already add the site to their home screen or use push, and use it as an internal proxy for the app retention uplift. That gives you a defensible number in place of the conference claim.
