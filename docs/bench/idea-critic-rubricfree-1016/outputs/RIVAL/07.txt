## Toughest objections

**Aisha Bello (CTO)**

1. **"20% faster" is a feeling, not a measurement.** Self-reported PR speed is vulnerable to novelty bias and to people who volunteered for a pilot being enthusiasts. Faster PRs also don't mean faster delivery if review load, bugs, or rework went up.
2. **25 of 220 doesn't generalize.** You are extrapolating to every team, including embedded firmware, which wasn't in the pilot. Firmware work (C/C++, hardware constraints, long debug cycles, safety or certification concerns) is exactly where AI suggestions are least reliable.
3. **Quality and security risk.** What happens to defect rates, review burden on senior engineers, and AI-generated vulnerabilities? Nothing in the pilot addresses this.
4. **Why all 220, why January 5?** A company-wide rollout is hard to reverse and the date looks arbitrary. Why not a staged rollout with go/no-go gates?
5. **Cost and vendor risk.** $39 × 220 × 12 ≈ $103K/year. What's the expected return, what's the contract term, and what is the exit plan if Lanternworks changes pricing, quality, or terms? Did you compare alternatives?

**Henrik Sand (General Counsel)**

1. **The central legal question is unanswered.** Is code retained by the vendor? Is it used for training? Without that answer, approving a rollout means accepting unknown IP and confidentiality exposure.
2. **The pilot may already have been a problem.** Was proprietary code sent to Lanternworks during the pilot, and under what terms? If there was no agreement, we may have an existing exposure to assess.
3. **Contract terms.** Data processing agreement, retention and deletion rights, subprocessors, breach notification, indemnification for IP claims over generated code, and ownership of output.
4. **Customer and third-party obligations.** Do customer contracts, NDAs, or export or regulatory rules restrict where source code can go? Firmware may carry stricter constraints.
5. **Timeline pressure.** A fixed date with legal review of unknown duration puts legal in the position of being the blocker. He will likely refuse to commit to January 5.
6. **Open-source licensing.** Does the tool reproduce licensed code, and is there filtering or attribution?

## Evidence you're missing

- **Measured outcomes:** PR cycle time, review time, defect and revert rates, and rework, comparing pilot engineers against a baseline or matched control group.
- **Pilot representativeness:** who was in the 25 (seniority, languages, teams), how they were chosen, and how many dropped usage after week 2.
- **Firmware evidence:** a small separate pilot with that group, or an explicit carve-out from the rollout.
- **Security review:** results of scanning AI-assisted code, and a check of what the tool can access in your repositories.
- **Legal findings:** the vendor's data retention and training terms, a signed data agreement, and a legal review timeline.
- **Economics:** total cost (about $103K/year at list), the value of the time saved under conservative assumptions, contract length, volume discounts, and competing tools.
- **Adoption data:** actual usage telemetry, not only survey responses.

## Suggested reframe

Propose a phased plan: legal clearance first, then expand to teams with measured results, with firmware gated separately. Treat January 5 as a target contingent on legal sign-off, not a commitment.
