## Risks

**Value case**
1. **The 15% figure has no stated basis.** 15% of 900 is 135 tickets a month. The community manager alone costs about $9,200 a month, roughly $68 per deflected ticket before benefits and engineering time. If a ticket costs you less than that to handle, deflection doesn't pay for this.
2. **Two quarters is tight.** A February launch means the target lands around August, and forum answers only deflect tickets once they exist and are findable.
3. **Tickets may not be forum-shaped.** If many are account, billing or bug reports, a forum can't absorb them. You haven't said whether you've categorized them.

**Adoption (Sam's ghost-town objection)**
4. **Cold start.** Nothing in the proposal says why 48,000 weekly users would post instead of filing an issue or asking elsewhere.
5. **An empty or unanswered forum is worse than none.** Public unanswered threads signal neglect.

**Cost**
6. **10% of engineering time is the bigger bill and is unpriced.** Which engineers, what it displaces, and what happens when threads need real debugging are all unspecified.
7. **One community manager is a single point of failure.** Coverage across vacation, burnout and time zones is unaddressed, and so is the hiring lead time. The hire may not be in seat by February.

**Unknowns you named**
8. **Moderation load.** Spam, abuse and off-topic threads are unquantified, and you have no policy, tooling or escalation path.
9. **Competitive exposure.** A detailed changelog could show Brightline your direction. You have no policy on what's published versus withheld, or how far behind shipping it runs.

**Other**
10. **Public expectations.** Feature-request threads create implied commitments you may not want.
11. **Bundling.** The changelog is cheap and the forum is expensive, but the proposal ties them together, so Sam's doubts about the forum could sink both.
12. **No exit criteria.** Nothing says what failure looks like or when you'd stop.

## Blunt read

**Not ready for Sam yet.** The idea is plausible, but right now it asks him to fund a hire and 10% of engineering on one unsupported number, with both of your open questions unanswered, and he already doubts forums. He will likely say no or stall.

The gaps look fixable in a couple of weeks:

- **Split the proposal.** Propose the changelog now, with a written rule that it covers shipped work only, not roadmap, which also answers the Brightline concern. Treat the forum as a separate, staged decision.
- **Back the 15% with data.** Categorize a sample of recent tickets and estimate how many have public, reusable answers. Then price a ticket.
- **Pilot the forum.** Use a cheaper test, such as a Discord or GitHub Discussions space, or a contractor in place of the hire, with a 90-day target.
- **Set a kill criterion up front.** For example, "if fewer than X threads get answered within 24 hours or tickets are down less than Y by month 6, we shut it down." This addresses the ghost-town objection directly.
- **Cost the engineering time** in dollars and name who bears it.
- **Draft a one-page moderation plan** so the unknown becomes a bounded risk.

Note: I used only the numbers you gave; the per-ticket comparison needs your actual support cost.
