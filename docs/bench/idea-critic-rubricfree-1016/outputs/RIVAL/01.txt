# How Dana will likely attack the plan

**1. The dates don't work (biggest hole).** Nine months plus six weeks of parallel running ends in roughly October 2027 if you start now, and later if you start in January. Support ends March 2027. Her first question will be "what runs billing for the 7+ months in between?" Without an answer, the plan reads as a rewrite that arrives after the deadline.

**2. "Rewrite" is the only option on the table.** She'll ask why not a runtime upgrade, paid extended support, or an incremental (strangler) migration that moves pieces off the old runtime in priority order. You show no comparison, so she'll assume you haven't looked.

**3. The proration and refund unknowns are unbounded.** "Nobody knows" is fine as a risk, but a 180k-line rewrite that must reproduce undocumented money behavior has no stated way to find the differences. The six-week parallel run only catches cases that real traffic hits in that window. Annual renewals, rare refund paths, and month-end or leap-day effects may not occur.

**4. The staffing assumption is untested.** Ledger-team experience in the language doesn't mean they know billing domain logic. And who are the five? If they come from the ledger team, what does that team stop doing? If it's a mix, where are the others from? "No outside hiring" is stated as fact, but it's a guess.

**5. The estimate has no basis.** Is 5 engineers for 9 months derived from anything (a similar migration, a module-by-module sizing), or is it a gut number? Rewrites of this size usually slip, and she'll ask what happens at month 9 if you're 60% done.

**6. Cutover risk is thin.** Does the parallel run shadow traffic read-only, or can both services move money? What's the rollback path after cutover, and what's the customer impact of a proration bug?

## Fix before November 4

- **The timeline vs. March 2027.** Find out what extended support, if any, the vendor offers and what it costs, and bring a bridge plan. This is the one issue that can end the conversation.
- **A short alternatives comparison.** Rewrite vs. upgrade vs. incremental, even half a page. It shows you chose the rewrite rather than defaulting to it.
- **A way to bound the edge-case risk.** Propose characterization tests and a replay of historical production transactions through old and new logic, with a divergence report. A small spike on proration and refund before the meeting would be best. Even a sample of past refunds run both ways gives her evidence instead of a worry.
- **Named people.** Who the five are, where they come from, and what the ledger team's lead says about lending them.

## Acknowledge openly in the room

- **The edge-case count is unknown.** Say it plainly, then present the replay approach and propose a go/no-go checkpoint after the first phase.
- **The estimate is uncertain.** Offer a range and a re-estimate gate at a fixed point, such as month 3.
- **The no-hiring assumption is a hypothesis.** State what would trigger hiring (for example, ramp-up slower than planned) and who decides.
- **Domain knowledge is gone.** Nobody who wrote the code is left. Frame it as why the replay harness comes first.

Note: I can't verify vendor support terms or your team's capacity, so confirm both before the meeting.
