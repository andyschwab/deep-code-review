# The CRO's case against your proposal

**1. "You're trading $1.2M of known ARR for an unquantified bet."** Three of the five dropped items were discussed with enterprise customers worth about $1.2M. What ARR, pipeline or retention benefit do multi-state tax filing and the mobile timesheet app have? If you can't put a number next to them, the cut looks like risk with no stated upside.

**2. "Sales may have made commitments, and you don't know which."** If any are written (order forms, SOWs, RFP responses, emails promising delivery), this is contractual exposure, not just churn risk. Even verbal "it's on the roadmap" promises become broken promises the day we cut. You say you haven't confirmed either way, so you're asking the exec team to approve a cut before knowing the downside.

**3. "Your assumption that renewals are safe is untested."** Has anyone spoken to those customers? When do they renew? A customer who was told a feature was coming and then hears nothing is a churn risk, and enterprise customers talk to each other.

**4. "Two bets on 30 engineers is concentration risk."** Multi-state tax is regulated, detail-heavy work with a long tail of edge cases. If it slips, Q1 ships one thing or nothing. Why not cut to three or four items, or keep the customer-backed items at reduced scope?

**5. "Focus is not the same as cutting."** Why drop these five rather than sequence them into Q2? A deferral with a communicated date is a different customer conversation from "cancelled."

**6. "'None will be done well' is an opinion."** Which of the seven are failing, and why: staffing, scope, dependencies? If the problem is the plan's execution, cutting to two might not fix it.

**7. "The rollout is a mess."** Sales will hear about this before customers do, and they'll improvise the message. Who owns that communication?

# Where you have no good answer yet

- **Argument 2 (written vs. verbal commitments).** You've said you haven't confirmed it, so you have no answer. This is the most dangerous gap. Ask Sales Ops, Legal and the account executives to pull contracts, order forms, SOWs and email threads on the three accounts. You have until November 12.
- **Argument 3 (renewals).** You have no evidence for the assumption, and you haven't given me the renewal dates. Get them, and ideally talk to at least one of the three customers or their account owners.
- **Argument 1 (upside of the two bets).** I have no information on the revenue tied to either bet. If you have that data, this one is answerable.

You are probably better placed on arguments 4 and 6 if you can show delivery data (slipped dates, context-switching, quality issues) for the seven initiatives. Argument 5 is a choice you can make: a partial deferral of the customer-backed items might be the stronger proposal.
