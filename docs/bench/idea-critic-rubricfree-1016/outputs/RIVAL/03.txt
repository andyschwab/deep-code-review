# Brief: Moving from the release train to continuous deploys

**Reframe the pitch.** The pain is "we couldn't tell which change broke things." Continuous deploys fix that by making every deploy a single change, but cadence is only one way to get there. Priya will ask whether cheaper options would do. Argue that the proposal is the one that fixes attribution and also shrinks blast radius and recovery time.

## Weak points

1. **The coverage assumption is the load-bearing claim, and it's untested.** 68% line coverage measures what code runs, not what regressions get caught. Both rollbacks shipped through your current checks, so the tests missed at least two real failures. Find out what those were and whether better tests would have caught them.
2. **Rollbacks are rare, so the case rests on cost per incident.** Two rollbacks across roughly 240 changes is a low failure rate. Quantify what a day-long rollback cost (customer impact, engineer-days). If it's small, the ROI is thin.
3. **The risk moves, it doesn't vanish.** Under a train, a bad change meets a two-week bake. Under continuous deploys it hits production within minutes, so you need canary or staged rollout, automated rollback, and monitoring good enough to detect a bad change quickly. Say whether these exist today.
4. **Q1 2027 is tight.** About 5.5 months from today, including the holidays, for a cultural and tooling change across 14 engineers. Propose phases with exit criteria, not a single cutover date.
5. **Customer success is a real dependency, not a footnote.** Per-change shipping breaks their per-train briefing workflow unless it is redesigned first.

## Suggested shape

- **Phase 1:** Tag and deploy per change within the existing train, or move to weekly or twice-weekly trains. This gives bisectable releases quickly.
- **Phase 2:** Add feature flags and canary rollout, and close the test gaps from the two incidents.
- **Phase 3:** Go continuous for low-risk services first, expanding as metrics hold.
- **Customer success:** Use PR labels to auto-generate release notes, a weekly digest, and a pre-brief only for flagged customer-visible changes. Flags let you deploy now and announce later.

## Questions Priya will likely ask

1. What caused each of the two rollbacks, and would continuous deploys have prevented or shortened them?
2. What is the evidence that 68% coverage catches enough? What's the escape rate today?
3. Why not weekly trains, or per-change deploy tags with the train kept?
4. What's the rollback time and automated detection plan for a bad single deploy?
5. What do we do when a change needs coordination (migrations, multi-service)?
6. How will customer success and support be briefed, and have they agreed to the new process?
7. What are the success metrics (change failure rate, MTTR, lead time), and what result makes us stop or revert?
8. Who is on call for continuous deploys, and what is the load on 14 engineers?
9. What does the train give us today (predictability, compliance, audit) that we'd lose?
10. What's the cost and who owns the pipeline, flag, and monitoring work in Q4 and Q1?

**Note:** Before the meeting, get the incident post-mortems and the escape-rate data. Without them, the coverage question is the one that sinks the proposal.
