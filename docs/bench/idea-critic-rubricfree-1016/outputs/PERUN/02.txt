# Likely objections, ranked by damage

**1. Cannibalization of the $29 plan (Claire).** This is the most damaging. The revenue loss is certain and immediate, while the upside is speculative. Your floor is about $122K MRR (4,200 × $29), and any customer who fits inside 3 forms and 100 submissions can downgrade to $0.
- **Data to bring:** the share of current cheapest-plan customers whose forms and submissions already fit the free limits (the at-risk MRR), and what the paid plans gate besides those limits. Model downgrade rates of 2%, 5% and 10%. For scale, 5% of 4,200 is 210 customers, or about $6.1K MRR lost.

**2. The 4% upgrade assumption is unsupported (Ravi and Claire).** Free users are lower-intent than trial users, who convert at 11%. A permanent free tier may also pull away trial users who would have paid.
- **Data:** the usage of your 11% trial converters, to show what share would have stayed free under these limits. You also need an evidence-based source for 4%, such as comparable freemium form builders or your own free-like behavior (e.g., expired trials that stay active). Then show a break-even table: the upgrade rate needed at different costs per free user and downgrade rates.

**3. Unknown cost per free user (Ravi).** Without this number, the proposal can't be evaluated. Free tiers also attract spam, abuse and support load.
- **Data:** hosting cost per form and per submission, support tickets per trial user versus per paying customer, and an estimate of spam or abuse exposure. Even a rough range is better than none. Show that the cost at 10x signups is still bounded.

**4. Top-of-funnel isn't proven to be the bottleneck (Claire).** If the real constraint is conversion or activation, more signups won't help.
- **Data:** current trial signups by channel, CAC, trial-to-paid by channel, and how many customers come from referrals today. Also show whether free users would carry branding (e.g., "powered by") that drives word of mouth, and whether you have evidence that word of mouth is limited by the trial.

**5. It's hard to reverse (Ravi).** Once users have a permanent free tier, taking it away damages trust.
- **Data:** a proposed pilot (a cohort, a region, or a time-boxed test) with pre-committed kill criteria, such as downgrade rate above X% or cost per free user above $Y. Show that the limits can be tightened without breaking promises.

**6. Cheaper alternatives weren't considered (Ravi).** Options include a longer trial, a lower-priced entry plan, a free tier with a shorter expiry, or a free tier limited to a single form.
- **Data:** why each alternative loses, ideally with a rough model of each one against the same break-even table.

**7. Are the limits right? (Claire).** If 100 submissions is too generous, you cannibalize paid users. If it's too tight, free users never experience value and don't spread the product.
- **Data:** the distribution of forms and submissions per active customer and per trial user, to show where the limits fall relative to real usage.

**Suggested framing:** open with the downgrade risk and the break-even table, because Claire will go there first. Then propose a limited pilot rather than a permanent launch, since that answers objections 2, 3 and 5 together.

*Note: I haven't seen your data, so all figures beyond your inputs are illustrative. The 5% downgrade is a scenario, not a prediction.*
