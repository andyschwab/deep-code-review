## Stress test: follow-the-sun proposal

**Main finding:** two 12-hour daytimes can't cover 24 hours with a 7-hour offset. If "daytime" means 08:00-20:00 local, Lisbon's day is 01:00-13:00 Denver time. Together the two days cover 19 hours and leave a 5-hour hole, 20:00-01:00 Denver (03:00-08:00 Lisbon). Someone still takes off-hours pages, so the proposal reduces overnight work rather than removing it.

| # | Risk | Mitigation to propose |
|---|---|---|
| 1 | **The 5-hour hole.** Someone covers it, and the pitch of "each site covers its own daytime" is not literally true. | Pull the page timestamps from last quarter and count how many of the 9 weekly overnight pages fall in the hole. Then shift the boundaries (e.g. Lisbon 06:00-20:00, Denver 13:00-23:00 MT) so each site takes 2-3 off-hours hours. Say plainly that these hours get an uplift. |
| 2 | **Denver headcount.** A 50/50 split puts 7 shifts a week on 4 people (1.75 each) against 1.17 each for Lisbon's 6. One absence leaves 3 people. | Split by headcount (about 60/40), for example Lisbon ~14h and Denver ~10h. Alternatively, let Lisbon cover some Denver shifts. Set a minimum rotation size and a rule for what happens during leave. |
| 3 | **Burnout isn't addressed for the two at risk.** Twice-daily handoffs may mean more frequent but shorter disruptions, not fewer. | Check whether either person sits at a site that gets the busiest windows. Add a recovery rule: time off after a shift with more than N pages, and no back-to-back shifts. |
| 4 | **Handoffs.** You go from 1 handoff a week to 14. Incidents in progress at shift change get dropped or are unclear. | Add a 30-minute overlap, a written handoff template, and a rule that an incident ends or is explicitly transferred by name. Pilot for 4-6 weeks before committing. |
| 5 | **DST drift.** The US and Europe change clocks on different dates, so the gap is 6 hours for about a week this month (25 Oct-1 Nov) and for 3 weeks in March. | Define shifts in one fixed reference (UTC or one site's time), and publish the two transition weeks' schedules in advance. |
| 6 | **Stipend fairness.** Paying the same flat amount per shift hides the fact that shifts differ in off-hours exposure and page load. Lisbon and Denver may also be on different pay and legal frameworks. | Use a base rate per on-call hour, an off-hours uplift, and a per-page or time-in-lieu payment for overnight pages. Keep the rate structure identical across sites. Check Portuguese and Colorado rules with HR/legal before Tom sees numbers. |
| 7 | **No escalation backup.** A 12-hour solo shift with no secondary is a single point of failure, especially for a 4-person site. | Name a secondary or manager escalation per site, even if it only pages when the primary doesn't respond. |

**For Tom:** present this as a pilot with kill criteria. For example, revert if overnight pages per person don't fall, if any site exceeds a set shift load, or if handoff-related incidents rise.

**Notes:** I can't judge the stipend amounts without knowing the current rate. The page-time distribution (risk 1) is the most important number you don't yet have.
