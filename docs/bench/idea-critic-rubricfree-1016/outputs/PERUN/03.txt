# Stress test: moving to continuous deploys

**Strongest objection:** The pain you cite is slow diagnosis (2 rollbacks, about a day each, cause unknown), not the train itself. Continuous deploys fix attribution, but a cheaper fix might too. The proposal needs to show why the bigger change is required.

**Weak points**
1. **Coverage is not detection.** 68% line coverage says what code is executed, not how many regressions get caught. Your own data cuts the other way: roughly 240 changes last quarter, and 2 escaped badly enough to force full rollbacks. Under continuous deploy, each escape ships immediately with no train-wide soak. Evidence you don't have yet: how many escaped defects, including small ones, did tests miss last quarter?
2. **Thin sample.** Two rollbacks is too few to estimate risk either way. Count hotfixes and minor incidents too.
3. **Missing rollback and exposure mechanics.** Per-change deploys only help if you have fast automated rollback, canary or staged rollout, and feature flags for risky or customer-visible work. None are mentioned.
4. **Customer success is a dependency, not an open question.** Their per-train notes and support briefings assume a batch. Without a replacement (change labels, a weekly digest, flag-gated launches), support will meet changes unbriefed.
5. **Timeline.** About 5 months to Q1 2027 end, with no pilot or interim checkpoint.
6. **Cheaper alternatives not considered:** per-change revert/bisect tooling on the existing train, weekly or twice-weekly trains, or canarying each train. Any of these could cut the day-long diagnosis without the full switch.

**Suggested reframe:** Propose a pilot on one lower-risk service. Set go/no-go gates, and expand only if they hold.

**Kill or pause criteria (set numbers before you start)**
- Change-failure rate above the current train baseline for 2 consecutive months.
- Median time to restore above a target (e.g. under 30 min).
- Support reports unbriefed customer-facing changes more than N times per month.
- Test escapes show coverage isn't catching what matters, and no gap-closing plan exists.

**Questions Priya will likely ask**
1. What problem are we solving: rollback frequency or rollback diagnosis time? Why not fix diagnosis alone?
2. What is our current change-failure rate and time to restore, and what will they be afterward?
3. What evidence says 68% coverage catches enough? What escaped last quarter?
4. How does a bad change get stopped and reverted automatically, and how fast?
5. How do risky or customer-visible changes get hidden behind flags?
6. Who owns the on-call burden when deploys happen at any hour?
7. How will customer success write notes and brief support, and have they agreed to the new process?
8. Why did the train exist in the first place (three years ago), and which of those reasons still apply (audits, customer commitments, release coordination)?
9. What is the pilot scope, and what makes us stop?
10. Is Q1 2027 realistic given what must be built first?
11. What does it cost in engineering time, and what gets delayed?

**Before sending:** get Priya's original rationale for the train and customer success's view. Both will shape her reaction more than the technical case.
