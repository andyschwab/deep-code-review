## Weaknesses in the plan

1. **The hardware case could break the shutoff date.** If those devices call v1 and can't be updated remotely, a June 30, 2027 shutoff breaks a customer's deployed product, and a migration guide won't fix that. Neither of your fallbacks (shutting off anyway, or extending indefinitely) is acceptable, and the plan names no third option. You also don't yet know whether this is the $400K customer.

2. **The 6-month migration estimate is untested.** From Dec 1 to June 30 is about 7 months, so you have roughly one month of slack. Enterprise release cycles, change freezes (especially around year-end), and procurement or security reviews can eat that. As stated, "they can migrate within 6 months" is your assumption, not their commitment.

3. **There is a single hard date and no checkpoints.** Nothing says what you'll do if, say, 2 of 5 customers haven't migrated by March. Without a pre-agreed gate, the date either slips quietly or gets enforced against a customer who isn't ready.

4. **The economics are unstated.** 1.5 engineers is a real cost, but compare it with your own loaded cost against the revenue at risk. The $400K customer alone may be comparable, and you haven't given the combined revenue of the other four. If the deprecation causes one churn, the savings may be gone.

5. **Contract terms are unchecked.** Do any agreements promise v1 availability, minimum notice periods, or SLAs that a shutoff would breach? This could change the date outright.

6. **The public announcement may come first.** Customers should hear about this from their account manager before Dec 1, not from a public post. You have about six weeks now to do that.

7. **The long tail is unaccounted for.** "Most" of the 6% comes from five customers, so you have no plan for the rest. They need notice, usage data, and an owner.

8. **There is no technical mitigation.** You haven't considered a compatibility shim, a proxy that translates v1 calls to v2, or paid extended support for specific customers. For the hardware customer, a shim may be cheaper than 1.5 engineers.

## Questions for account managers

- For each of the 5 customers: what is their release cadence, are there freeze periods in 2027, and who owns the integration on their side?
- Which customer has v1 calls in deployed hardware? How many devices, what firmware, can they update over the air, and what is the replacement cycle?
- Is that customer the $400K one? What are the renewal dates and the total revenue at risk across all five?
- Do any contracts or SLAs commit us to v1 availability or specific notice periods?
- Has any customer already said they can't or won't migrate? What would they accept instead (paid extension, shim, credits)?
- Would they commit to a migration date in writing if we offer solutions-engineer support?
- Who are the customers in the remaining v1 traffic outside the top 5, and has anyone contacted them?
- Are there upcoming negotiations or renewals where a deprecation announcement would hurt?

Bring the decision as options with a gate (for example, a March go/no-go review) rather than a fixed date. Include a fallback for the hardware customer, and agree who talks to customers before Dec 1.
