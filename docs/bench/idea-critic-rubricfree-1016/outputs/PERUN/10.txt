## Risks

**Demand and adoption**
1. **Ghost town.** Sam has already raised this, and the proposal has no seeding plan or activity threshold. At launch the forum has no content, so there is nothing to deflect tickets with.
2. **Unsupported 15% deflection.** The figure is an assumption with no evidence behind it. Forums only deflect questions that are public and repeatable. Billing, account and bug tickets mostly won't move. You haven't broken down what the 900 tickets are.
3. **Slow payoff.** A February launch gives roughly two quarters to hit the target, which is about when a forum's searchable archive starts to matter.

**Cost**
4. **Weak economics on current numbers.** 15% of 900 is about 135 tickets a month. The community manager alone is about $9,200 a month, or roughly $68 per deflected ticket, before the engineering time. Without your cost per ticket, nobody can tell whether that is a good trade.
5. **Engineering time is unpriced and unbounded.** "About 10 percent" has no headcount or dollar figure. It also interrupts roadmap work, and if engineers end up answering what support answers now, the cost moves rather than shrinks.

**Operations**
6. **Unknown moderation load.** One person means no coverage for vacations, spam waves, abuse or heated threads, and it is a single point of failure.
7. **Unanswered threads are worse than no forum.** Public questions that go unanswered, or engineers making casual commitments in public, hurt the brand.

**Strategy**
8. **Competitive exposure from the changelog.** You don't know whether a detailed changelog helps Brightline. That is a decision you can make, not a mystery: set the level of detail deliberately.
9. **Bundling.** The changelog needs no hire and has different risks, but it is tied to the forum's cost and to Sam's skepticism.

**Decision quality**
10. **No stop condition.** There is no definition of failure, such as active users, response time or deflection by a date, and a hire is hard to reverse.
11. **Weak attribution.** Ticket volume may move for other reasons, such as releases or user growth, so you could not tell whether the forum caused the change.

## Blunt read

**Not ready for Sam. It needs more work first.** He has a stated objection, and the proposal doesn't answer it. It asks for a permanent salary against an assumed number, with the two biggest unknowns still open.

Before the meeting:
- **Split the changelog out.** It is cheap, low-risk and approvable alone. Decide the detail level you're comfortable showing Brightline, such as shipped features and fixes but no roadmap.
- **Break down the 900 tickets** by category and estimate what share a public forum could realistically answer. Re-derive the deflection target from that.
- **Put dollar figures on the engineering time** and your cost per ticket, then show the break-even.
- **Propose a limited pilot or a contract or part-time role** with pre-agreed kill criteria, for example minimum weekly active posters, median response time and deflected tickets by a set date.
- **Answer the ghost-town objection directly:** how you will seed content and who answers in the first 90 days.
- **Estimate moderation load** from comparable communities, and label it an assumption.

Presented this way, you're asking Sam to approve a bounded test rather than a bet.
