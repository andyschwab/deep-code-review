## The CRO's case against your proposal

**1. "$1.2M ARR is at risk, and you've assumed otherwise."**
Three of the five dropped items were discussed with enterprise customers worth about $1.2M ARR. Your claim that cutting them won't cost renewals is an assumption. You haven't tested it with those accounts, and these are the customers whose renewals matter most.

**2. "If sales made written commitments, we're in breach."**
If any commitment is in an order form, SOW, or email, dropping the item is a contractual problem, not a roadmap choice. Even verbal commitments get cited in renewal negotiations. You don't know which kind exists.

**3. "Your two bets don't protect existing revenue."**
Multi-state tax filing and a mobile timesheet app sound like growth and expansion bets. Neither is tied to the accounts that pay us today. You're trading known ARR for a forecast.

**4. "Concentration risk."**
Two bets means two points of failure. If multi-state tax filing slips, which is plausible given regulatory complexity, Q1 produces nothing.

**5. "Fewer initiatives won't fix execution."**
Thirty engineers on two bets doesn't guarantee they get done well. Can that many people work two projects in parallel without coordination overhead? You've argued that seven is too many, not that two is right.

**6. "Why this timing?"**
Cutting scope right before renewal season, with no customer conversations first, signals that product is making decisions without go-to-market input.

**7. "Cheaper alternatives exist."**
Descope the five instead of dropping them. Staff two at minimal levels. Sequence them into Q2. Why is the only option all or nothing?
