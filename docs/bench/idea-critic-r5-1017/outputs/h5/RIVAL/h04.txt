**Recommendation: Proceed with a Pilot, Not a Full Launch**

Do not commit to the full May 1 launch yet. The financial model is critically flawed because it ignores $1,350/month in editorial labor costs and relies on an unproven churn assumption. Instead, propose a "Founding Member" pilot: secure just **three** sponsors at $5,000/month for a three-month trial starting May 1. This caps your risk while validating the actual churn rate and sales cycle before scaling to ten sponsors.

**Three Biggest Failure Modes**

1.  **Profitability Erosion:** The plan nets $3,000/month per sponsor before editorial labor. Once you account for 30 hours of staff time at $45/hour ($1,350), the margin drops to $1,650. If any sponsor demands extra custom integrations or ad-hoc edits, this tier could become break-even or loss-making.
2.  **Sales Friction & Churn:** You are assuming 100% sell-through (10 sponsors) and 80% renewal without data. If the niche churn rate is higher than expected (e.g., 40% instead of 20%), you will face a revenue cliff in month 13 while carrying fixed editorial overhead.
3.  **False Competitive Benchmark:** Comparing your rate to regional TV is dangerous because the audiences differ. If your core demographic overlap is only 15%, sponsors paying a premium for *your* specific audience may feel the $5,000 price point is high relative to their actual conversion, leading to non-renewal.

**Kill Switch Metrics**

Terminate the tier immediately if either of the following occurs:
*   **Renewal Rate < 60%:** If fewer than 6 out of 10 sponsors (or 2 out of 3 in the pilot) renew after the initial term, the product-market fit is insufficient to sustain the dedicated editorial staff.
*   **Six-Month Revenue < $150,000:** If you fail to secure an average of 5 active sponsors per month during the first half of the year, the sales velocity is too slow to justify the operational complexity.
