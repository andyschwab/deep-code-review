**Recommendation: Proceed with a "Go/No-Go" gate at the compliance quote stage.**

The unit economics are strong: $3M revenue against $700k CapEx and $90k annual OpEx yields a payback period under 12 months, even conservatively. However, do not commit capital until you have written confirmation of the certification timeline and cost. Treat the equipment purchase as contingent on these two unknowns.

**Three Biggest Failure Modes:**

1.  **Regulatory Timeline Mismatch:** Healthcare certification often takes 3–6 months, not the weeks required for a July 1 start. If inspectors cannot sign off before the contract start date, you face immediate breach-of-contract penalties or lost revenue while fixed costs burn.
2.  **Liability Insurance Exclusion:** Standard commercial policies frequently exclude industrial healthcare laundering or demand exorbitant premiums for biohazard exposure. If your carrier quotes >$40k annually or refuses coverage, your 15% margin evaporates instantly.
3.  **Utilization Fragility:** Moving from 85% to 95% utilization leaves zero buffer for machine downtime. If one of the four new washers fails or requires maintenance, you cannot meet the 24/7 pickup SLA without expensive overtime, turning the profitable volume into a loss leader.

**Kill/Pause Triggers:**

*   **Certification Cost > $60,000** (Your current budget implies ~$50k allocated; anything higher breaks the CapEx model).
*   **Certification Timeline > 8 Weeks** (Anything longer jeopardizes the July 1 revenue start and cash flow projections).
*   **Insurance Premium > $35,000/year** (This threshold protects the 15% net margin target).

**Note:** Secure a "letter of intent" from the hospitals allowing a 30-day grace period for certification; this de-risks the timeline significantly. Do not order the washers until the insurance binder is in hand.
