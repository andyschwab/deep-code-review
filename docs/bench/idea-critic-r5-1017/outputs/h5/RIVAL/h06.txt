**Three Main Failure Modes:**
1.  **Conversion Collapse:** Online evening learners behave differently than summer-camp families. If conversion drops from 0.8% to 0.4% (common when moving from high-intent camp leads to cold online traffic), your cost per student doubles to $90, slashing projected profit by 45% and requiring $13,500 in marketing to hit 150 students.
2.  **Hidden Variable Costs:** The $2,200 integration fee is a one-time sunk cost, but you have no budget for ongoing bandwidth or tiered support. If 150 students stream video simultaneously, unmetered bandwidth overages or emergency server scaling could consume 20–30% of your revenue, turning a profitable cohort into a break-even experiment.
3.  **Churn Shock:** Evening learners often quit after the first month due to fatigue. If churn exceeds 15% after Month 1 (vs. a typical 5–8% for committed programs), your Month 2 revenue drops to $25,500 instead of the projected $30,000, making the three-month revenue target unachievable.

**Stop/Pivot Trigger:** Halt the rollout if **Day 30 enrollment is <100 students** OR **Day 14 churn exceeds 10%**. These thresholds indicate either a fatal acquisition channel mismatch or a product-market fit issue with the evening format.

*   **The Moderator Math:** You allocated $1,200 for a weekend moderator, but a 90-day program starting Oct 1 includes ~13 weekends. At $1,200 total, that’s ~$92/weekend, which is likely below market rate for a qualified language moderator, risking quality or requiring you to absorb extra costs mid-pilot.

More critically, cash flow will be lumpy: you’ll collect ~$30,000 in Month 1, but marketing costs ($6,750) and integration fees ($2,200) are due upfront, creating a working capital gap.
*   **Platform Overkill:** Paying for 5,000 concurrent user capacity when you expect 150 total students (likely <50 concurrent) is wasteful. Negotiate a tiered plan based on actual peak usage to save 40–60% on platform fees.
