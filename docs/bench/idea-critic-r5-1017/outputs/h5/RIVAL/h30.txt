**Recommendation: Do not launch the pilot in its current form.**

The financial model contains a critical error that obscures a massive risk exposure. You calculated the average ISA revenue as $6,750, but the math ($45k × 15% × 3 years) actually yields **$20,250** per student. While this suggests higher potential revenue, it relies on 100% of students earning $45k+ and 100% collection efficiency. If even 30% of students earn below the threshold or default, your cash flow collapses compared to the guaranteed $12,000 upfront. With 60% of the cohort on ISA, you are swapping $720,000 in guaranteed cash (60 students × $12k) for a risky receivable asset that may not materialize for years, potentially creating an immediate operational cash crunch.

**Primary Failure Modes:**
1.  **Salary Assumption Risk:** If the job market cools and average starting salaries drop to $35,000, revenue per ISA student falls to $15,750 (a 22% drop), drastically altering the unit economics.
2.  **Collection & Default Risk:** Unlike upfront tuition, ISAs require active collections. A 15% default rate (common in student lending) would erase nearly all the profit margin over the upfront model.
3.  **Regulatory Risk:** ISAs face increasing scrutiny regarding whether they constitute unregistered securities or predatory lending; a regulatory halt could freeze expected revenue mid-year.
4.  **Administrative Overhead:** Tracking income, verifying employment, and managing collections for 90 students requires legal and operational infrastructure you currently do not have, eating into the 15% share.

**Pre-agreed Stop/Pivot Trigger:**
Terminate the ISA pilot immediately if **net operating cash flow turns negative for any two consecutive months** within the first 12 months of the cohort's start date, or if **verified graduate employment income averages below $38,000** for the first graduating class. These triggers protect the academy's liquidity before the receivable gap becomes insurmountable.
