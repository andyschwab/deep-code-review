**How This Could Fail:**
1.  **Arithmetic Error:** Your revenue math is incorrect. 120 rooms × 70% = 84 units. At $2,200/month, gross potential is $2.21M/year, not $37M. Even at 100% occupancy, you only generate ~$2.2M annually. At 80% occupancy, revenue is **$1.77M**, not $37M. This means your project would take **12+ years just to recover the construction cost**, ignoring operating expenses, debt service, and the $1M+ fire safety gap. The deal is fundamentally insolvent as modeled.
2.  **Regulatory Black Box:** Proceeding without a fixed licensing fee is dangerous. If the city imposes a commercial-to-residential conversion tax or requires expensive sprinkler retrofits beyond your $1M estimate, margins could vanish entirely.
3.  **Market Mismatch:** Downtown hotel districts often lack the grocery, laundry, and community infrastructure required for co-living. If tenants churn because the location feels like a tourist trap rather than a neighborhood, occupancy will collapse.

Abort or pivot immediately if the **pilot property fails to sustain 65% occupancy for three consecutive months** after stabilization, or if the **municipal impact study reveals licensing fees exceeding $15,000 per unit**.
