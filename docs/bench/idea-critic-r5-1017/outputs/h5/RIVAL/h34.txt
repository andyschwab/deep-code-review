**Recommendation: Do not launch the full wholesale arm yet.** Instead, execute a 90-day pilot program exclusively with the Main Street flagship and two other partner cafés. The current plan carries excessive fixed costs ($65,000 upfront + $7,000 monthly burn) against unproven demand. Securing the flagship’s commitment is the critical path; without it, the volume assumptions collapse, and the break-even timeline extends indefinitely. Validate the production logistics and margin reality on a small scale before committing to the $50,000 marketing spend and long-term prep space lease.

**Three Biggest Failure Points:**
1.  **The Anchor Tenant Risk:** If the Main Street flagship declines the 6-month trial, you lose 12% of projected volume immediately, but more critically, you lose the social proof needed to sign the remaining 12 cafés. The sales cycle will stall, leaving you with high fixed costs and low revenue.
2.  **Margin Erosion via Waste:** The plan assumes <5% waste. In wholesale, order cancellations or forecasting errors happen. If waste hits 12% (common in early rollout), your 20% gross margin halves to 10%, doubling the time to break-even and consuming cash reserves rapidly.
3.  **Operational Bottleneck:** The signature croissant quality is your brand equity. A small dough-mixing machine and a new 600-sq-ft space may create a quality bottleneck. If wholesale production degrades the product consistency for your existing retail customers, you risk cannibalizing your core profitable business to subsidize an unproven wholesale channel.

**Stop/Pivot Trigger:**
Terminate or pivot the wholesale effort if **net cash flow from the wholesale division is negative for two consecutive months after the pilot phase**, or if the **Main Street flagship does not sign a binding agreement within 45 days**. Do not wait for quarterly results; monthly cash burn is the leading indicator of failure in this low-margin, high-volume model.
