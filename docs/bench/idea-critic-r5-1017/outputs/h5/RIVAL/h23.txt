**The Math Error**
You calculated $45,000 monthly revenue based on 10 groups at $5,000 each. However, 10 × $5,000 = **$50,000**, not $45,000. More importantly, your assumption of "10 groups per month" implies hosting a retreat almost every single Monday, Tuesday, and Wednesday (roughly 13 weeks × 3 days = 39 days available; 10 groups × 3 days = 30 days booked). This leaves almost no buffer for maintenance, setup, or sales cycles, and assumes 100% utilization of your mid-week inventory immediately upon launch.

A realistic conservative model looks like this:
*   **Conservative Volume:** 4 groups/month (1 per week).
*   **Revenue:** 4 × $5,000 = $20,000.
*   **Variable Costs:** Staffing/catering likely scale with headcount. If your $12,000 fixed cost estimate assumes 10 groups, it may only be $5,000 for 4 groups. Let's assume $6,000 for safety.
*   **Insurance:** Unknown variable (let's estimate $1,000/month prorated).
*   **Net Contribution:** $20,000 - $6,000 - $1,000 = **$13,000/month**.

While $13k is positive, it is far from the $30k+ net you implied ($45k - $14.5k).

**How This Plan Could Fail**
1.  **Lead Conversion Fallacy:** Wedding leads are typically individuals planning personal events. Their decision-making criteria, budget authority, and timeline differ vastly from corporate procurement officers. Assuming a 30% conversion rate without a dedicated B2B sales pitch or existing corporate relationships is highly optimistic.
2.  **Brand Dilution & Operational Drag:** Using the same staff and facilities for rowdy corporate retreats could degrade the "exclusive, romantic" atmosphere required for your high-margin weddings. If a wedding party arrives on Thursday to find remnants of a corporate beer pong tournament or exhausted staff, you risk cancelling future $12,000 bookings.
3.  **The Insurance Gap:** If the premium for corporate liability is significantly higher than estimated (common for alcohol-serving corporate events), it could erase margins on smaller groups.

**Kill/Pivot Trigger**
Establish a **First Quarter Net Contribution Threshold of $25,000 total** (approx. $8,300/month average).
*   **Why:** This accounts for a ramp-up period (e.g., 2 groups in Month 1, 4 in Month 2, 6 in Month 3).
*   **Action:** If the cumulative net profit after three months is below $25,000, **pivot** immediately to a "premium only" model (fewer groups, higher price point) or **kill** the program to refocus on wedding upsells. Do not wait for a full year; cash flow drag on a seasonal business is fatal.
