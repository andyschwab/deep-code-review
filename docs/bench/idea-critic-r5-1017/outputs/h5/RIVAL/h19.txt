**Recommendation: Proceed with a "Regulatory-First" Pilot**
Move forward immediately, but restrict the initial scope to a 90-day internal pilot using only currently available DOT data. Do not commit to the full $150k budget until the Q1 2027 privacy rules are finalized. The projected $150k annual savings (60% of $250k) against a $150k one-time build cost offers a compelling 12-month ROI, provided the regulatory environment does not invalidate your data access. The reduction in onboarding time (12 days to 5) unlocks operational capacity that outweighs the financial savings alone.

**Top Three Failure Modes**
1.  **Regulatory Data Lockout:** The Q1 2027 DOT privacy rule may restrict programmatic access to the very safety records your risk score relies on. If the rule requires explicit carrier consent for each data pull, your automated vetting flow breaks, forcing a revert to manual checks and eliminating the efficiency gain.
2.  **Churn Underestimation:** Your model assumes a static 5% quarterly churn. If the stricter vetting causes friction that pushes even 2% of marginal carriers to competitors, the cost of lost capacity and emergency spot-market sourcing could exceed the $250k subscription savings, making the tool net-negative on total logistics cost.
3.  **Integration Debt:** The prototype pulls from the TMS, but legacy TMS data is often unstructured. If engineering underestimates the cleaning required to normalize 180-day compliance lapses across different carrier formats, the $30k contingency will vanish in month two, ballooning the effective cost per saved dollar.

**Kill Trigger**
**Terminate the project if the total development spend exceeds $105,000 (70% of the total budget) before a fully automated, error-free risk score is generated for 100% of active carriers.** This specific threshold protects you from a "sunk cost" scenario where you spend the full $150k but still require manual intervention, leaving you with neither the old subscription nor a functional replacement.

*Note: Validate the TMS data cleanliness immediately; if more than 15% of carrier records require manual correction during the pilot, pause development to address data governance first.*
