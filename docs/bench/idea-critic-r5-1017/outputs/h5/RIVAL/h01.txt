**Recommendation: Proceed with a "Soft Launch" Pilot (First 3 Weeks Only)**
Do not commit to the full season yet. Run the first nine trips (Dec 15–Jan 4) as a controlled pilot. This validates the winter demand assumption and safety protocols before the heavy capital of the full season is deployed. If the pilot hits the financial and safety targets below, greenlight the remainder of the season.

**Main Ways This Could Fail**
1.  **Weather Volatility vs. Refund Policy:** Winter seas are unpredictable. If you cancel 30% of trips due to conditions but have sold non-refundable tickets or incurred fixed guide/fuel costs for prep, your margins evaporate instantly.
2.  **The "Spring Parity" Fallacy:** Assuming 70% occupancy (21 guests) is dangerous. Winter daylight is shorter, temperatures are lower, and the "whale watching" demographic often skews older or family-oriented, groups less likely to brave freezing conditions. A drop to 40% occupancy (12 guests) turns every trip into a loss leader.
3.  **Insurance Shock:** If the broker lands on the $5,000 end of the range (or adds exclusions for winter waves), your break-even point shifts dramatically, potentially making the venture unviable at current ticket prices.

**Pre-Agreed Stop/Pivot Condition**
**Trigger:** If the **average net profit per trip over the first 9 outings is below $1,200** OR if **weather cancellations exceed 25% of scheduled trips**.
**Action:** Immediately pause the schedule. Pivot to a "Weekend Only" model (Saturdays only) to reduce fixed guide/fuel burn, or delay the full launch until March 1st when conditions stabilize. Do not continue the 3x weekly cadence if these thresholds are breached.

**Hidden Costs & Flawed Assumptions**
*   **Flawed Assumption (Demand):** You are betting on "similar to spring." Winter tourism is fundamentally different; it is often driven by hardcore enthusiasts or tourists with rigid itineraries, not the casual local crowd of spring. Your 21-guest estimate is likely optimistic by 30–40%.
*   **Hidden Cost (Maintenance):** Saltwater + freezing temps + heavy use = accelerated wear on engines and hulls. Budget an extra 15% for immediate winter-specific maintenance (heater cores, winterized fluids, de-icing equipment) that isn't in your $45 fuel line.
*   **Hidden Cost (Liability):** The $15–$20 variable cost per person for gear/food doesn't account for the *replacement* cost of that gear when it gets damaged by harsh winter conditions or lost overboard in rougher seas.
*   **Cash Flow Gap:** The $15,000 upfront CAPEX (wetsuits/lockers) plus the potential $5,000 insurance premium creates a $20k hole before ticket #1 is sold. At ~$1,200 estimated net profit/trip, you need ~17 successful trips just to break even on startup costs. Ensure you have the cash runway to survive a slow January.
