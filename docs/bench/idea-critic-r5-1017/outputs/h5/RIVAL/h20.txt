**Recommendation: Do not proceed with the full $530k rollout yet.**

Your math is correct on the total outlay ($500k hardware + $30k training + $15k marketing = $530k), but your risk profile is fatal. You are betting $530,000 on an unverified assumption: that insurers will reimburse $250 for a 24-hour turnaround. If they don't, your addressable market collapses to cash-pay patients, likely insufficient to fill two new machines.

**Top 3 Failure Modes:**
1.  **Reimbursement Wall:** Major insurers refuse the $250 code, capping your volume at <10% of projections.
2.  **Cannibalization:** Existing 48-hour clients "upgrade" to 24-hour without paying the full premium, eroding your $60/unit baseline profit.
3.  **Operational Drag:** Rush logistics increase error rates (remakes), wiping out the $90 premium margin.

**Kill Switch Criteria:**
Terminate the project if, by Month 3:
*   **Volume:** Premium units do not exceed 35 units/month (10% of current base).
*   **Margin:** Net profit per premium unit (after remakes/logistics) falls below $45.

**Better Alternative: The "Soft Launch" Pilot**
Instead of buying two machines immediately, lease one CNC unit or negotiate a "try-before-you-buy" clause with the vendor for 90 days. Run a targeted pilot with 5 high-volume local dentists who explicitly agree to the $250 price point *before* installation. Validate the insurance reimbursement code with just 20 claims. This caps your downside at ~$45k (lease + minimal marketing) rather than risking $530k on a hypothesis.

*Note: Your 20% quarterly growth assumption is aggressive for a regulated medical device market; base your ROI model on flat adoption for the first 6 months to stress-test cash flow.*
