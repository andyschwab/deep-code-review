**Three Most Likely Failure Points**

1.  **Regulatory Blockade:** State boards of pharmacy may prohibit or severely restrict the courier transport of Schedule II–V controlled substances without expensive, time-consuming licensing upgrades. If compliance costs exceed $1.50 per order, the unit economics collapse.
2.  **Contractual Penalties:** Existing third-party delivery contracts likely contain exclusivity clauses or termination fees triggered by bringing logistics in-house. Unanticipated legal fees or buyout costs could spike the initial CapEx beyond the pilot budget.
3.  **Conversion Overestimation:** The 12% conversion rate assumes frictionless adoption. If actual conversion lands at 6% (a common variance in pharmacy behavioral shifts), revenue drops to $4M while fixed overhead remains, turning the pilot into a loss leader immediately.

**Pre-Agreed Kill Condition**

Terminate the pilot immediately if, for any four consecutive weeks, the **net contribution margin per order falls below $1.25**.

This threshold accounts for the base fulfillment cost ($6.00) plus a buffer for variable compliance and packaging overages. If the margin dips below this level, the volume required to break even becomes operationally unattainable for a same-day model, and continuing would only deepen losses without a clear path to profitability.
