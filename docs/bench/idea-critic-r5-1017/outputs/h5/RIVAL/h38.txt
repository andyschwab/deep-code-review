Your current payback model is too aggressive because it ignores the "unknown" grant variable and assumes zero battery degradation or maintenance premium for the new fleet.

**Main Failure Modes**

1.  **Grant Shortfall:** If you receive less than the expected 40% infrastructure subsidy ($84,000), your CapEx rises, extending the payback period beyond your 3-year target. Without the grant, the infrastructure ROI drops significantly.
2.  **Battery Degradation & Range Anxiety:** You calculated 132 miles daily with a 15% buffer. If battery capacity degrades by even 2% annually (typical for heavy transit use), those six busiest routes may become unreachable on a single charge within 18 months, forcing costly mid-day top-ups or route reductions.
3.  **Underestimated Maintenance:** Electric buses have lower fuel costs but often higher specialized maintenance costs early in their lifecycle (software updates, battery thermal management, technician retraining). Assuming diesel-level maintenance savings is risky; the $150k/bus saving could shrink to $90k if specialized labor rates are higher than anticipated.

**Pre-Agreed Kill Condition**

Terminate the full rollout if, after the 90-day pilot review, the **actualized payback period exceeds 48 months**.

This calculation must include:
*   Actual grant amount received.
*   Realized fuel savings (based on actual kWh vs. diesel gallon equivalent costs).
*   Any unplanned maintenance costs or downtime penalties incurred during the pilot.

If the math shows a 4-year return on investment instead of 3, the capital is better deployed elsewhere in the network where reliability and margins are proven.
