**Recommendation: Do not proceed with the July 1 launch under the current terms.**

The plan contains a critical financial blind spot: the complete exclusion of performance licensing fees. Streaming rights for recorded theatre are distinct from in-person performance rights and often carry significant royalties (typically 10–20% of gross revenue or a flat fee per stream). Without securing these costs, your $150,000 budget and profitability projections are fictitious. Additionally, your conversion assumption is dangerously optimistic; expecting 30% of in-person attendees to convert to a monthly digital subscription ignores the difference between occasional event-goers and habitual streamers. A more realistic conversion rate (3–5%) would yield only 240–400 subscribers, generating ~$4,000/month—far below the break-even point required to sustain production costs.

**Three Most Likely Failure Points:**

1.  **Licensing Cost Shock:** Once you approach rights holders (playwrights, composers, unions), the required royalties will likely exceed your remaining budget, forcing a price hike to ~$25/month (killing demand) or immediate cancellation.
2.  **Conversion Collapse:** Achieving only 5% conversion (400 subscribers) instead of 30% results in $48,000 annual revenue against $195,000 in upfront costs, creating an unsustainable burn rate within 90 days.
3.  **Bandwidth Underestimation:** If the 1080p stream attracts higher-than-expected traffic or longer view times, the $0.02/minute estimate may scale non-linearly due to CDN peak pricing, eroding margins on the few subscribers you do acquire.

**Pre-agreed Kill Condition:**

Terminate the project immediately if **Month 1 gross revenue is less than $15,000** (approx. 1,500 subscribers) OR if **confirmed licensing fees exceed 12% of projected gross revenue**.
