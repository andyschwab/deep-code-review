**Recommendation:** Proceed with the dine-in rollout for two screens, but condition the final construction start on securing a written timeline guarantee from the local health department regarding inspections. The projected unit economics are strong enough to absorb the identified staffing costs and potential minor delays, offering a clear path to profitability that aligns with the boutique cinema benchmark. However, the regulatory timeline is the only variable capable of destroying the project's ROI before it begins; do not break ground without clarity there.

**Three Biggest Failure Points:**
1.  **Regulatory Paralysis:** The health department delays inspection or licensing beyond the four-week buffer, causing construction costs to bleed into lost operational months without revenue.
2.  **Margin Erosion from Labor:** The $8k/month per screen staff cost ($16k total) combined with unaccounted licensing fees and food waste reduces the net profit margin below the breakeven threshold, turning the "incremental" revenue into a net loss.
3.  **Customer Experience Friction:** The complexity of dine-in service slows down turnover between the three daily shows, reducing actual occupancy below the modeled 70% and diluting the ticket price premium.

**Abort Trigger:**
Abort the project if the **average net profit per screen is below $5,000 after 90 days of full operation**. This metric accounts for the higher operational complexity and ensures the venture is not just generating revenue, but actually contributing to the bottom line after all new variable costs (labor, food cost, licensing) are absorbed.
