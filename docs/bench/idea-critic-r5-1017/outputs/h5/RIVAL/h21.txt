**Recommendation: Scale Back**

Do not proceed with the current $150k upfront investment. The unit economics are fundamentally broken for a pilot. You are projecting $3,240 in first-year revenue against a $150,000 cost. Even if your 12% quarterly growth holds perfectly, you will not recover the initial outlay within the pilot's lifetime, let alone generate profit. The math implies you are spending $46,296 to acquire a customer who pays you $1,080 in year one. This is not a pilot; it is a charity donation with extra steps.

**Immediate Action:** Slash the "Marketing" and "Hardware" upfront costs. A pilot of three farms should rely on manual installation and direct founder sales, not a $30k marketing budget or $90k in custom hardware inventory. Reduce the upfront cash burn to under $15k by using existing sensors and personal networks. Prove the *value* of the soil boost before industrializing the *delivery*.

**Primary Failure Modes**

1.  **Financial Catastrophe:** The most certain failure is running out of cash. At a $150k burn for $3k return, you need 46 farms just to break even on cash flow, ignoring the time value of money and operational overhead. If the regulatory approval slips by three months, your cash runway may evaporate before the first sensor is legally active.
2.  **Adoption Illusion:** You assume farms will "adopt within six months." Farmers are risk-averse; they trust neighbors, not demos. If Hawthorn, Riverbend, and Oakleaf do not see a measurable yield increase in the first 90 days, they will not buy the second round of kits. Your growth model relies on retention you haven't earned.
3.  **Technical/Regulatory Block:** The nitrogen sensor is your single point of failure. If regulatory approval takes six months instead of six weeks, the hardware sits idle, batteries die, and the "pilot" becomes a storage problem. Without the sensor data, the "Rapid Soil Boost" value prop is unproven.

**Kill Condition**

**Terminate the project immediately if cumulative revenue does not reach $10,000 by December 31, 2024.**

Your proposed $50k target is dangerously high for a scaled-back pilot and too low to validate the current $150k model. A $10k threshold forces you to secure actual purchase orders beyond the initial demo kits. If three partner farms won't commit to a second purchase or a subscription tier generating $10k total within six months of launch, the product-market fit does not exist, and further capital deployment is waste.
