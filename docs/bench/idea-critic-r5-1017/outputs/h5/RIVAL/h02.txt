**Recommendation: Do not launch as planned.** The current model contains a critical production impossibility and overly optimistic acquisition math that guarantees failure in Month 1. You are planning to ship 7,000 boxes against a hard capacity limit of 5,000, creating an immediate 2,000-order backlog you cannot fulfill. Furthermore, your marketing budget ($100k at $10 CPA) yields only 10,000 total customers over time, which contradicts the assumption of securing 7,000 subscribers instantly from a single quarter's traffic.

**Primary Failure Modes:**
1.  **Capacity Breach:** You cannot fulfill the launch volume. Shipping 7,000 units when capped at 5,000 will result in delayed shipments, immediate customer anger, and chargebacks before the service proves its value.
2.  **Acquisition Shortfall:** Converting 4% of cold traffic (8,000 subscribers) is industry-unheard-of (typical e-commerce conversion is 1–2%). Even at 2%, you only get 4,000 subscribers, leaving you at 60% of your revenue target.
3.  **Unit Economics Collapse:** If churn is higher than 5% monthly (common for subscriptions after the novelty wears off), and acquisition costs rise above $10, the $150k packaging budget burns before renewal revenue stabilizes.

**Specific Trigger to Stop or Pivot:**
Initiate a "Stop-Loss Pivot" if **either** of the following occurs by the end of Month 2:
*   **Fulfillment Ratio < 90%:** If you cannot ship 90% of orders within the promised window due to the 5,000-unit cap.
*   **Actual Churn > 8% Monthly:** If more than 8% of subscribers cancel in any single month after the initial 60-day window.

**Corrective Path:**
Reduce the launch target to **4,000 subscribers** (aligning with realistic 2% conversion and 80% of your capacity). Use the remaining capacity buffer to manage logistics friction. Reallocate the saved packaging budget to extend the marketing runway, allowing you to test acquisition costs without betting the pilot on a single month's performance. Validate the churn rate with this smaller cohort before scaling to the 5,000-unit hard limit.
