**Recommendation: NO-GO on the current plan; proceed only as a "GO-IF" pilot.**

Do not commit to the $620,000 purchase or the six-week shutdown yet. The proposal relies on a 20% revenue increase driven by an unverified assumption that market demand for 12-ton vessels has grown since 2022. Without validating this demand, the project risks becoming a high-cost asset with low utilization. Instead, initiate a targeted 30-day market validation sprint to confirm there are enough 8–12-ton vessels in your region seeking haul-out services. If, and only if, this data confirms a waiting list or signed letters of intent covering at least 40% of the projected new capacity, should you proceed to the board for funding.

**Three Biggest Failure Modes:**

1.  **Demand Illusion:** The 2022 survey is stale; if the local fleet composition hasn't shifted toward heavier vessels, the lift will sit underutilized. You would incur the full debt service and higher operating costs without the projected $420,000 revenue uplift, extending the payback period indefinitely.
2.  **Shutdown Revenue Cliff:** A six-week shutdown during the sailing season could cost significantly more than estimated. If you lose regular customers to competitors during this window, the one-time revenue loss might exceed $150,000, eroding the first year's cash flow needed to service the new debt.
3.  **Hidden Infrastructure Costs:** The proposal assumes only the lift cost. Upgrading power supply, reinforcing the concrete pad, or modifying drainage for a 12-ton unit often incurs uncapped civil engineering costs that can add 15–20% to the total project price, breaking the five-year financing model.

**Cancellation/Pivot Trigger:**

Cancel the purchase immediately if the 30-day market validation fails to secure signed expressions of interest from at least 15 distinct vessel owners (representing ~40% of the new capacity) willing to schedule haul-outs within the first six months of operation. If this threshold is not met, pivot to a strategic partnership model: rent time on a neighboring yard's heavy lift or subcontract heavy haul-outs until organic demand justifies the capital expenditure.
