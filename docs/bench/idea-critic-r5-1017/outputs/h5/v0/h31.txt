**Recommendation:** Proceed with a **conditional launch ("Go-If")** on November 1, 2024, but immediately reduce the initial fleet commitment from 150 to 100 trucks. This lowers the upfront equipment outlay from $2.5 million to $1.5 million, preserving capital while still covering the 45 targeted sites with a buffer. The full fleet of 150 should only be deployed if signed contracts exceed 60 sites by October 15.

**Three Most Likely Failure Modes:**
1.  **Revenue Shortfall via Price War:** If FrostLine Services executes a rumored 5% undercut, our average contract price could drop to ~$253,000. Combined with a potential loss of 10–15 sites due to aggressive competitor locking, revenue could fall to $9 million, failing to cover the fixed costs of a 150-truck fleet and prep expenses.
2.  **Weather-Dependent Demand Collapse:** The plan assumes 38 inches of snowfall. A mild winter (e.g., <20 inches) would trigger contract clauses allowing clients to reduce payments or cancel early, leaving us with idle, branded assets and no recurring revenue to justify the $30,000/unit prep cost.
3.  **Hidden Operational Scaling Costs:** The model accounts for truck prep but ignores the lag in hiring and training 150 qualified drivers. A labor shortage could force overtime pay or result in service failures during the first major storm, leading to immediate contract penalties and reputational damage that outweighs first-year revenue.

**Specific Kill/Pivot Trigger:**
Cancel the expansion to 150 trucks and pivot to a subcontractor model if **signed contracts by October 15 are fewer than 50 sites** OR if the **weighted average contract price falls below $240,000**. Additionally, if pre-season snowfall forecasts are revised downward by more than 30% before November 1, delay the launch entirely.
