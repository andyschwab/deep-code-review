**Recommendation:** Proceed with a **phased rollout** (10 stores) rather than an immediate all-store launch, conditional on securing a dynamic pricing mechanism for niche genres before the March 2025 target. The pilot's 18% margin is promising but likely inflated by the specific demographics of the downtown Seattle location; applying these figures universally without adjusting for regional variance risks significant capital erosion.

**Top Three Failure Modes:**
1.  **Financial (Inventory Obsolescence):** The assumption of stable $8 average trade-in value fails if niche genres (the "long tail") accumulate unsold. Unlike new books, used inventory depreciates; if turnover slows, carrying costs and eventual write-downs will obliterate the 18% margin, turning the $185k investment into a sunk cost for dead stock.
2.  **Operational (Labor Variance):** The pilot's labor cost ($2,340) may not scale linearly. Processing 1,200 customers/week in diverse locations could require more grading time per book than the Seattle pilot, especially if staff are less experienced. If processing time exceeds 4 minutes per book, labor costs will double, pushing the program into negative territory.
3.  **Brand (Quality Consistency):** Inconsistent grading standards across 42 locations could lead to customers receiving poor-quality "trade-in" books, damaging Inkwell's reputation for curation. A single viral complaint about moldy or damaged resold books could undermine trust in the entire brand, not just the used section.

**Kill Criteria (Stop/Redesign Triggers):**
*   **Net Margin < 12%** for two consecutive months post-launch in any phased cohort.
*   **Inventory Turnover Ratio < 0.6** (books sold vs. books acquired) after 90 days, indicating severe stagnation in niche categories.
*   **Customer Return/Complaint Rate > 5%** on resold items, signaling a breakdown in quality control.

*Note: The 18% margin projection assumes identical foot traffic conversion across all stores, which is statistically improbable given location diversity. The $185k budget leaves little room for error if the dynamic pricing model is not implemented prior to launch.*
