**Recommendation: Do not proceed to full launch yet; authorize only a capped pilot to validate demand and logistics.**

Your revenue math is correct ($30M), but the plan rests on an unverified assumption that 30% of existing customers will switch to flat-pack without evidence of apartment-renter demand. Furthermore, comparing your $130 production cost to a competitor's $150 price while ignoring their lower shipping fees creates a false margin advantage. You must validate the true landed cost and actual conversion rate before committing $4.5M in capital.

**Three Biggest Failure Modes:**

1.  **Demand Cannibalization without Growth:** If apartment renters do not value the flat-pack format enough to offset the loss of margin from existing customers switching from assembled units, you will incur the $4.5M capital cost without achieving the projected $30M net new revenue. The "switch" may simply erode your current high-margin sales.
2.  **Landed Cost Inversion:** Your $130 unit cost excludes logistics, waste disposal, and veneer supply risks. If shipping flat-packs (which often require more protective packaging or specific pallet configurations) plus waste fees pushes the total landed cost above $160, your gross margin collapses below the 18% ROI target, making the line less profitable than the current assembled model.
3.  **Supply Chain Fragility:** Relying on veneer sheets without a disruption contingency creates a single point of failure. If veneer supply halts, you cannot produce either assembled or flat-pack goods, but the fixed costs of the new CNC equipment remain, accelerating cash burn during downtime.

**Kill Criteria (Trigger Points):**

*   **Pilot Unit Cost:** If the fully loaded landed cost (production + shipping + waste) exceeds **$155 per unit** during the Q4 2025 pilot.
*   **Demand Volume:** If confirmed pre-orders or pilot conversion rates indicate annual demand will be **< 60,000 units** (50% of the projected 120k), failing to justify the capital outlay.
*   **Cannibalization Rate:** If more than **15%** of flat-pack sales come from existing customers who would have otherwise bought the higher-margin assembled unit, indicating net revenue erosion.
