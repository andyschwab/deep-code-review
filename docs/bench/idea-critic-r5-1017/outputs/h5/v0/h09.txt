**Recommendation:** Do not scale to the full city yet; extend the pilot for 90 days specifically to measure post-promotion adoption rates and battery degradation, as current data relies on unverified assumptions about user behavior after February 1, 2025, and unknown wear-and-tear costs.

**Three Biggest Failure Modes:**
1.  **Adoption Cliff:** Users revert to self-charging once the promotional discount ends, causing station utilization to drop below the break-even point of ~3 swaps/station/day.
2.  **Hidden Degradation Costs:** Frequent swapping accelerates battery wear beyond the assumed 800 cycles, increasing replacement costs and eroding the $18,600 projected revenue gain.
3.  **Revenue Math Error:** The model assumes a direct 1:1 translation of downtime reduction to ride volume; if rider demand is not constrained by charging time, the 12% ride increase will not materialize.

**Kill-Switch Trigger:** Halt expansion immediately if the net profit per station averages below $250/month (approx. $5,000 total for the current 20 stations) over any rolling 30-day period after the promotion ends.

**Note on Numbers:** Your revenue projection assumes every minute of saved downtime converts to a paid ride, which only holds true if demand currently exceeds supply during peak charging hours.
