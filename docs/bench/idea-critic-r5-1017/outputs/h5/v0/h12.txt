**Recommendation:** Do not launch the program in its current form; proceed only as a **conditional pilot** capped at 50 customers for Year 1, with a mandatory review before scaling to the full 150.

**Three Biggest Failure Modes:**
1.  **Revenue Math & Churn Compounding:** Your projection of $30M is mathematically incorrect and dangerously optimistic. $12,000 × 150 customers × 20 years equals **$36M**, not $30M. More critically, you assumed <5% churn but admitted having no post-warranty data. If churn is actually 8% (industry average for post-warranty), you lose over half your customer base by Year 10, slashing real revenue to ~$18M–$20M, potentially below the break-even point once costs rise.
2.  **Unmodeled Input Cost Escalation:** You explicitly excluded the 8% aluminum price increase. At $200k/year in added parts costs, this totals **$4M over 20 years** (undiscounted). Combined with the $2.4M in planned crew/software spend, your initial margin buffer is likely insufficient if energy prices or labor costs also rise, turning a "maintenance profit" into a loss leader.
3.  **Lifespan Mismatch Risk:** The plan relies on systems lasting 25 years to cover the "last mile." If the 2019–2024 installations suffer early degradation (e.g., inverter failures at year 12 rather than 18), the maintenance contract becomes a liability where repair costs exceed the annual $12k fee, especially as systems age out of efficient operation.

**Pre-agreed Kill Trigger:**
Terminate the program immediately if **Year 1 actual churn exceeds 6%** OR if **aluminum-driven parts costs exceed $210k in any single fiscal year**. These are hard financial tripwires that invalidate the core unit economics before long-term capital is committed.
