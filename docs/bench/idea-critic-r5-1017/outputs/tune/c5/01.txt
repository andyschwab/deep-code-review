**Recommendation:** Proceed with the 3-month pilot starting October 1, 2024, but explicitly condition the full rollout on achieving a **minimum net revenue contribution of $45,000 per month** by the end of the pilot, rather than relying on the theoretical $320,000 projection.

**Three Biggest Failure Modes:**
1.  **Cannibalization of High-Margin Revenue:** The $25/hour group rate may simply migrate existing $50–$80/hour private students into a cheaper bucket. If even 20% of group attendees are diverted from private sessions, the net revenue lift evaporates, and tutor hourly earnings drop, causing churn.
2.  **Overestimated Tutor Adoption & Capacity:** Your model assumes 70% adoption (280 tutors) immediately. In reality, managing 8 students simultaneously requires different pedagogical skills than 1-on-1 tutoring. If only 30% of tutors qualify or volunteer, volume drops to $137,000/month, failing to cover the $200,000 upfront investment within a reasonable payback period.
3.  **Hidden Technical & Operational Drag:** The estimate ignores the cost of scheduling complexity, no-show management for 8-person groups, and potential platform latency during live video scaling. A single technical failure in a paid group session damages brand trust significantly more than in a private lesson.

**Pre-Agreed Halt Trigger:**
Halt the rollout immediately if, by the end of Month 2 of the pilot, the **net new revenue** (total group revenue minus estimated cannibalized private revenue) averages less than **$15,000 per month** across the entire pilot cohort. This threshold ensures we cover at least the variable costs and a fraction of the fixed investment before committing to a full launch.

**Hidden Risks Missed:**
*   **Tutor Earnings Dilution:** If tutors fill groups but earn less per hour than private sessions, they will quietly deprioritize the feature, causing student dissatisfaction due to inconsistent scheduling.
*   **Refund Liability:** Group classes have higher coordination friction; a single dissatisfied student can demand a refund for the whole session, creating an undefined liability cap not present in 1-on-1 models.

*Note: The 70% adoption assumption within one month is historically aggressive for behavioral changes; consider phasing the rollout to 20% of tutors first to validate training needs.*
*Note: Ensure the "net revenue" calculation explicitly tracks user IDs to detect cannibalization, as gross revenue alone will be misleading.*
