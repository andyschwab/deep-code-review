**Recommendation: Proceed with a "Go-If" conditional launch, not a full immediate rollout.**

Do not commit the full $500k inventory or hire all three installers immediately. Instead, authorize a **pilot phase** capped at 50 installs over the first 90 days. Hire only one lead installer and a contractor, purchase inventory strictly on a just-in-time basis for confirmed contracts, and allocate only $50k of the licensing budget initially. This approach validates the Dallas labor cost reality and county permitting timelines without exposing the company to the full $1.15M upfront risk if the unit economics collapse.

**Primary Failure Modes:**
1.  **Margin Compression:** Your model assumes Texas labor costs match Arizona's efficiency despite higher Dallas wages. If labor costs per install rise by just 15% (eroding the assumed $2.0k/kW advantage), your gross margin could drop from the projected 40% to below 25%, rendering the expansion unprofitable after fixed overhead.
2.  **Incentive Cliff:** The entire $8M revenue projection relies on the 30% tax credit remaining static. Any legislative reduction to 20% or less before your first quarter ends would immediately invalidate the customer ROI, likely causing deal cancellations mid-pipeline.
3.  **Permitting Bottlenecks:** If average permitting exceeds 6 weeks in target counties, your installer utilization will plummet. Three idle installers costing $255k annually while waiting for permits will burn cash faster than the pilot can generate it.

**Kill Criteria:**
Stop or rethink the expansion immediately if, after the first 50 completed installs, the **net operational margin falls below 15%** (after accounting for actual Dallas labor rates and verified permitting delays). Additionally, if the average time from contract signature to installation start exceeds **45 days** due to permitting, halt hiring and revert to a subcontractor-only model until the bottleneck is resolved. Do not proceed to the full 250-install target unless the pilot demonstrates a sustained net margin above 20% for two consecutive months.
