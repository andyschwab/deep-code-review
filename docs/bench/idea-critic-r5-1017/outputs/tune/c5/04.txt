**Recommendation: Proceed with the pilot, but reclassify the Year 2 revenue as a high-risk aspiration, not a baseline.** The unit economics for the 5-client pilot are sound ($750k revenue vs. $620k direct costs), providing a valid test bed. However, the strategic bet on 80% cross-sell adoption is statistically unsupported and must be decoupled from the go/no-go decision for the pilot phase.

**Three Most Likely Failure Modes:**
1.  **The "80% Fallacy":** Assuming 80% of existing customers will buy an add-on ignores typical SaaS attachment rates (often 10–25% without heavy incentives). If adoption hits only 20%, the Year 2 opportunity collapses from $9.6M to $2.4M, destroying the long-term business case while leaving you with specialized content costs.
2.  **The GDPR Bottleneck:** You identified EU-GDPR content timing as an "unknown." In security training, this is a critical path item, not a variable. If legal review delays this content by 8 weeks, you cannot launch the pilot for European clients, immediately invalidating 20–40% of your pilot revenue target depending on client location.
3.  **Churn Cannibalization:** A 15% churn forecast is aggressive for an *add-on*. If the microlearning is perceived as "extra work" rather than value, it may increase churn on the *core* platform. If churn rises to 20% across the base, the net revenue gain from the add-on is erased by lost core subscriptions.

**Arithmetic & Logic Checks:**
*   **Pilot Math:** $750k (Revenue) - $500k (Dev) - $120k (Marketing) = **$130k Net Pilot Profit**. The pilot pays for itself, but leaves almost no buffer for the GDPR delay or overruns.
*   **Year 2 Logic Flaw:** The $9.6M figure assumes 96 customers (80% of 120) buy at $150k each. This presumes the price point and value proposition proven in a 5-client pilot scale linearly to 96 clients without additional sales friction or discounting, which is a flawed comparison.
