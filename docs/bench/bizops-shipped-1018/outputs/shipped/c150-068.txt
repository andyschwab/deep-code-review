**The Call**
Your $2,500 flat fee is likely compliant as a **technology charge**, not a legal fee, provided your contracts explicitly define the product as a software license unrelated to the outcome or hours of legal work. Do not switch to hourly billing or introduce complexity-based tiers yet; instead, immediately audit your Terms of Service and invoicing language to ensure the fee is legally distinct from the law firm's billing to their clients.

**The Numbers**
The risk is not mathematical but definitional. However, looking at your unit economics:
*   **Current Model:** $180,000 MRR / 40 clients = **$4,500 average revenue per client/month**.
*   **Implied Volume:** At $2,500 per case, your clients average **1.8 cases/month** collectively.
*   **Cost Variance:** Your cost to serve ranges from 2 hours to 40 hours of manual review.
    *   *Assumption (Low Complexity):* 2 hours internal labor. Margin is high.
    *   *Assumption (High Complexity):* 40 hours internal labor. If your internal fully-loaded labor cost exceeds $62.50/hour, you lose money on these specific licenses.
*   **Break-Even Trigger:** If more than **22%** of your cases require the 40-hour review (assuming a $50/hr internal cost), your blended margin collapses under the flat fee.

**What Flips It, and the Trigger**
The single input that changes the call is **contractual language**. If your agreement implies the fee covers "case processing" rather than "software access," regulators may reclassify it as a shared legal fee, triggering ethical scrutiny on reasonableness.
*   **Trigger:** A formal bar complaint or audit requesting your master services agreement.
*   **Rule of Thumb:** Software vendors sell tools (flat fee); law firms sell time/results (hourly/contingency). The moment you market based on "hours saved" rather than "features provided," you blur the line.
*   **This Week's Step:** If your current contract mentions "per case" without defining "case" as a unit of software configuration, consider revising the wording to "per matter license" or "monthly active matter cap" to decouple the price from the attorney's labor effort.
