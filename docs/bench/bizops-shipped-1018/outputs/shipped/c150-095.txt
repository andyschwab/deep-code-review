**The Call:** Do not pivot to a pay-per-completion model immediately; it will catastrophically reduce revenue given your 40% completion rate and likely accelerate your cash burn. Instead, retain the per-seat license but introduce "engagement-based rebates" or tiered packaging (Good-Better-Best) to align price with perceived value without surrendering your revenue floor. Present this to the board as a value-alignment strategy that protects ARR while addressing client churn risk, rather than an admission that the product lacks value.

**The Numbers:**
Your current unit economics show a dangerous disconnect between revenue recognition and value delivery.
*   **Current ARR:** $2,000,000 (200 clients × 50 seats × $200).
*   **Effective Cost Per Completion:** At 40% completion, clients effectively pay $500 per *completed* seat ($200 / 0.40).
*   **Proposed Pay-Per-Completion Risk:** If you switched to charging only for completions at a price point clients deem fair (e.g., $250/completion), your revenue would drop from $2M to ~$1M (assuming volume stays constant), instantly halving your runway.
*   **Burn & Runway:**
    *   Monthly Burn: $150,000.
    *   Cash Remaining: $900,000 (6 months × $150k).
    *   **Formula:** Runway = Cash / Monthly Burn.
    *   **Result:** 6 months.
    *   **Assumption (UNVERIFIED):** This assumes no increase in churn due to low engagement and no additional development costs exceeding current opex. If churn increases by 10% monthly due to dissatisfaction, runway drops to under 4 months.

**What Flips It, and The Trigger:**
The single input that changes this call is **Client Churn Rate**. If your annual churn exceeds 25% because clients refuse to renew due to low completion, the per-seat model becomes unsustainable regardless of pricing structure.
*   **Trigger:** If renewal conversations in the next 60 days indicate >20% of clients are non-renewing specifically citing "low engagement," consider a pilot of outcome-based pricing for *new* logos only.
*   **Rule of Thumb:** Never tie 100% of revenue to a metric (completion) you do not fully control (user behavior).
*   **Step this week:** If churn risk is high, consider offering a "success credit" where clients who fail to hit 60% completion receive a 20% credit toward next year's renewal, rather than lowering the upfront price.
