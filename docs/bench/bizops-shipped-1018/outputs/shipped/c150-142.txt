**The Call**
Adopt a **four-year depreciation schedule** for the combined unit. While a fifteen-year schedule inflates early accounting profits, it creates a "tax trap" where you pay cash taxes on income while sitting on economically obsolete assets. Given the mandatory four-year platform replacement, the asset's *useful life* for tax and accounting purposes is four years, not fifteen. Prioritize cash flow preservation and accurate book value over cosmetic early-year earnings.

**The Numbers**
*Assumptions:* 12 units @ $85,000 = **$1,020,000** total basis. Corporate tax rate **21%** (standard US federal rate, illustrative). No salvage value.

| Metric | 15-Year Schedule (Straight Line) | 4-Year Schedule (Straight Line) | Impact |
| :--- | :--- | :--- | :--- |
| **Annual Depreciation** | $68,000 | $255,000 | +$187,000 deduction/year (Years 1-4) |
| **Annual Tax Shield (Cash Saved)** | ~$14,280 | ~$53,550 | **+$39,270 extra cash/year** (Years 1-4) |
| **Total Cash Tax Savings (Yrs 1-4)** | ~$57,120 | ~$214,200 | **+$157,080 immediate liquidity** |
| **Book Value at Year 5** | ~$748,000 | $0 | Avoids carrying $748k in obsolete tech |

*Note:* Under the 15-year model, you carry $748,000 in assets on your books at the exact moment you must spend ~$1M to replace them, creating a massive write-down risk. The 4-year model aligns the book value with the economic reality of the software cycle.

**What Flips It, and the Trigger**
The deciding input is the **mandatory replacement policy**. If your software architecture changes such that the hardware can operate securely for >8 years without a full platform swap, the 15-year model becomes viable.
*Trigger:* If the FDA or your security audit mandates a hardware-tethered software refresh every 48 months, you must depreciate over 4 years.
*Rule of Thumb:* Depreciation must match the asset's *useful life*, not its *physical life*.
*Step this week:* If your engineering team confirms the 4-year hard stop is non-negotiable for compliance, instruct your CPA to prepare filings based on a 4-year useful life immediately to maximize current-year cash retention.

**Routing Note**
You must consult a CPA to determine the specific classification under **IRS MACRS (Modified Accelerated Cost Recovery System)**. Specifically, ask whether the unit qualifies as "5-year property" (computers/peripherals) or if the medical nature forces a different class, and whether **Section 179** or **Bonus Depreciation** allows you to expense 100% of the cost in Year 1 rather than over 4 years. Additionally, verify **GAAP ASC 360** requirements for "component depreciation," which may allow you to depreciate the mechanical shell over 15 years and the software/compute module over 4 years separately.
