**What Flips It, and the Trigger:**
The determining factor is not the dollar amount ($10,000), but the **nature of the risk transfer**.
*   **The Rule of Thumb:** A "warranty" typically covers the repair or replacement of the *product itself* (the sensor) or direct damages caused *by the product* (e.g., the sensor battery explodes and burns the desk). An "insurance contract" covers loss or damage to *other property* (the building, inventory, drywall) caused by an *external peril* (water) that the product merely failed to warn about.
*   **The Trigger:** If your marketing or contract language promises to "pay for water damage" rather than "replace the defective sensor," you have crossed into insurance territory. Florida Statute § 624.10 broadly defines insurance as any agreement to indemnify another against loss or damage.
*   **Actionable Step:** If you cannot restructure the offer to strictly cover only the cost of the device and direct installation labor, consider partnering with a licensed commercial property insurer to offer an endorsed rider on their policy rather than issuing your own promise.
