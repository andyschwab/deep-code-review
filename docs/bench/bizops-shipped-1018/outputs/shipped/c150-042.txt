**The Call:** Assume your hosts are operating as unregistered public utilities under current California law; do not scale to 1,000 hosts without immediate counsel. Your model of aggregating private sales for a commission likely triggers "electricity service provider" or "public utility" classification for both the platform and potentially the hosts, creating high liability for fines and cease-and-desist orders.

**The Numbers & Regulatory Thresholds:**
California's Public Utilities Code and California Public Utilities Commission (CPUC) precedents define a public utility broadly as any entity delivering electricity to the public for compensation. Unlike simple peer-to-peer marketplaces (e.g., renting a tool), selling a regulated commodity (electricity) often strips away "platform" protections.
*   **Current State:** 200 hosts, 5,000 kWh sold. You are already past the "de minimis" experimental phase in the eyes of a regulator if the activity is continuous and open to the public.
*   **The Trigger:** There is no specific "safe harbor" volume threshold (e.g., "under 10,000 kWh is safe") in California statute for this specific P2P model. The trigger is the *act* of selling electricity to the public for profit without a certificate.
*   **Liability Exposure:** If classified as an unregistered utility, penalties can include disgorgement of all revenue plus significant administrative fines per day of violation.
*   **Host Risk:** Individual homeowners selling power may be reclassified from residential customers to commercial generators, violating their interconnection agreements with their local IOU (Investor-Owned Utility) and voiding their insurance.

**What Flips It, and The Trigger:**
The single input that changes the call is a **CPUC Exemption or Specific Rulemaking** (such as a Distributed Energy Resource Provider exemption) explicitly covering your transaction structure.
*   **Trigger Value:** Without a written exemption letter or a specific CPUC resolution naming your model as exempt, the default status is "unregistered utility."
*   **Stop/Pivot Date:** **Immediately.** Do not onboard the next 800 hosts. The expansion from 200 to 1,000 hosts increases the visibility and regulatory risk exponentially, moving you from "radar blip" to "enforcement target."
*   **Rule of Thumb:** If you touch the money flow for the commodity (taking a commission on kWh) and facilitate the sale to the general public, you are likely the utility.
*   **This Week's Step:** If you have not secured a regulatory exemption, consider pausing all new host onboarding and pivoting to a "membership fee" model (charging hosts for software access only, not taking a % of energy sales) *only* after legal validation, as even this may be scrutinized.
