**The Call:** Immediately implement a hard cap on price increases for any future declared state of emergency in Florida; do not rely on the "automated" nature of your algorithm as a legal defense, as Florida law generally holds businesses strictly liable for the prices charged regardless of the mechanism used.

**The Numbers & Thresholds:**
Florida's price gouging statute (Fla. Stat. § 501.0117) prohibits selling essential commodities or services for a price that "unconscionably exceeds" the average price charged in the 30 days prior to the emergency declaration.
*   **The Rule:** There is no single fixed percentage (e.g., "10%") that is automatically safe. The law uses a rebuttable presumption: an increase of more than **25%** over the pre-emergency average is presumed unconscionable unless you can prove the increase was due solely to additional costs incurred by you (not just market demand).
*   **Your Scenario:** A 300% increase driven purely by demand spikes, rather than your own increased operational costs, almost certainly meets the statutory definition of price gouging. The $50,000 in extra revenue represents potential liability exposure, including civil penalties of up to $1,000 per violation (per ticket) and restitution.
*   **The Trigger:** The moment the Governor declares a state of emergency, your algorithm must switch modes. The trigger is the official declaration date/time; the baseline is your average price from the 30 days preceding that specific moment.

**What Flips It:**
The only factor that might justify a price higher than the 25% presumption is if *your* costs (e.g., licensing fees paid to venues, server costs, payment processing) spiked by a similar amount during those 12 hours. If your costs remained flat while demand surged, the automation provides no shield. The "safe" percentage is effectively **0% to 25%** above your 30-day average, depending on your ability to document cost pass-throughs.
