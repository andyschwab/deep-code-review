**The Numbers & Regulatory Logic:**
Export controls do not operate on revenue percentages or engineering headcount (25 engineers, $900k deal, 20% revenue impact are business metrics, not regulatory exemptions). The binding variables are the **technical data content** and the **foreign interest** in the recipient entity.
*   **The "Export" Definition:** Under US regulations (primarily ITAR and EAR), releasing controlled technical data to a foreign-owned entity *within* the US is legally defined as a "deemed export." The physical border does not matter; the citizenship and ownership structure of the recipient do.
*   **The Threshold:** There is no universal "40% safe harbor." For nuclear-related technology, even minority foreign ownership can trigger strict scrutiny. If the foreign owners are from a restricted nation, the threshold is effectively 0%. If they are from an allied nation (e.g., France), the threshold for control is still low given the subject matter.
*   **The Data Classification:** Simulation data detailing "reactor thermal dynamics" for nuclear maintenance almost certainly falls under:
    1.  **ITAR (International Traffic in Arms Regulations):** Specifically the US Munitions List (Category XVI) if the data relates to defense articles or specific nuclear weapon/propulsion tech.
    2.  **EAR (Export Administration Regulations):** Specifically the Commerce Control List (Category 0 - Nuclear Materials, Facilities, and Equipment) if not ITAR-controlled.
