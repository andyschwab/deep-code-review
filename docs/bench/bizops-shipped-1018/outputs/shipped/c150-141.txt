**The Call:** Do not switch to a tiered subscription yet; retain per-check pricing but immediately introduce a service-level agreement (SLA) credit for false positives exceeding 5%, while absorbing the £0.60 cost increase to secure the 4% error rate. Your current 12% churn driven by regulatory friction is a greater revenue killer than the margin compression from infrastructure costs.

**The Numbers:**
*Current State (Per Check):*
Revenue = 45,000 checks × £2.50 = £112,500.

*Comparison:*
While the per-unit margin drops from £1.50 to £0.90, the alternative (maintaining high errors) risks losing 12% of your base monthly. Losing 5,400 checks at the *old* margin equals £8,100 lost immediately, compounding monthly. The "cost" of the upgrade (£27,000 total margin reduction vs old theoretical max) buys volume retention.

*Tiered Subscription Risk (ASSUMED):*
If you switch to a flat £100,000/month tier for unlimited checks:
Break-even volume = £100,000 / £1.60 cost = 62,500 checks.
At 45,000 checks, you generate £100k revenue but incur £72k cost = £28k margin.
However, if volume spikes to 50,000 due to operator confidence, cost becomes £80k, margin drops to £20k. Tiered models in high-variance verification services often expose the vendor to unlimited downside if accuracy improves and usage surges, unless capped.

**What Flips It, and the Trigger:**
The single input that changes this call is **Churn Elasticity**. If lowering the false positive rate to 4% does *not* reduce churn below 6% within 60 days, the £0.60 cost investment fails its ROI.
*Trigger:* Monitor churn weekly. If churn remains >8% by [Date: 2 months from now], pivot to a "Success-Based" pricing model (charge only on verified passes) rather than a flat subscription, shifting risk back to your performance.
*Rule of Thumb:* Never subsidize infrastructure costs (£0.60) with a price hike (£2.50 → £3.10) when the value prop (accuracy) is the primary driver of churn. Fix the product first.
