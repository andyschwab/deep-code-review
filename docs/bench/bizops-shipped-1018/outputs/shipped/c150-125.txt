**The Call:** Operate under the assumption that your current output is **not copyrightable** by the user, creating an immediate risk to your value proposition; do not promise "ownership" of the draft text in marketing or contracts until a licensed IP attorney reviews your specific generation workflow. There is no numerical "safe harbor" (e.g., 15% human input) that guarantees copyright eligibility under current US practice.

**The Landscape & Numbers:**
The US Copyright Office (USCO) and recent federal court rulings (e.g., *Thaler v. Perlmutter*, *Anderson v. Stability AI*) maintain that copyright protects only human authorship. When AI generates the "traditional elements of authorship" (expression, arrangement, narrative flow), those elements are in the public domain.
*   **Current State:** If your AI generates 85% of the text, including the structure and phrasing of the specification, the user's 15% contribution (technical facts and claim limitations) is likely insufficient to claim copyright over the *entire document*. The user may own the underlying invention (patent rights), but not the copyright in the *text* of the application draft.
*   **The Threshold:** The USCO uses a case-by-case "de minimis" standard, not a percentage. Mere prompting or minor editing is insufficient. The human must exercise "creative control" over the expressive output. If the AI dictates the phrasing, the text is uncopyrightable regardless of the word count ratio.

**What Flips It & The Trigger:**
The call flips only if the user performs **substantive, creative revision** of the AI-generated text (rewriting paragraphs, restructuring arguments, selecting specific phrasing from options) rather than just inputting data.
*   **Trigger:** If a user cannot demonstrate they creatively authored the majority of the expressive text, the copyright claim fails.
