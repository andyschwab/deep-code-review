**The Call:** Adopt a **Good-Better-Best packaging model** with a fixed "Base Rate" and an optional, transparent "Priority/Peak Surcharge." Do not build a hidden buffer into a higher flat rate, as this makes you uncompetitive against tractor services during off-peak times. Do not use fully dynamic pricing on every invoice, as unpredictability scares away farm operators. Instead, productize the risk: sell "Standard Scheduling" (off-peak, fixed price) and "Harvest Priority" (peak window, base + surcharge).

**The Numbers:**
Your current unit economics are fragile because the variable cost (waiver fee) is uncapped while revenue is fixed.
*   **Current Model:** Price = $40/acre. Margin = 15% ($6/acre). Implied Cost = $34/acre.
*   **The Shock:** A 300% spike in waiver fees turns a profitable flight into a loss.
    *   *Assumption (ASSUMED):* If normal waiver cost is $X, peak is $4X. If this spike exceeds your $6 margin buffer, you lose money.
    *   *Scenario A (Higher Flat Rate):* To cover a 300% spike universally, you might raise rates to ~$52/acre (estimated).
        *   *Result:* You lose competitiveness against tractors during 80% of the season when waivers are low.
    *   *Scenario B (Base + Surcharge):* Base Rate covers normal ops + small buffer ($42/acre). Surcharge covers the specific peak waiver cost ($15–$20/acre).
        *   *Result:* You remain competitive for standard jobs. The farmer explicitly pays for the "harvest urgency" value, not just your cost.

**What Flips It, and The Trigger:**
The deciding input is **Price Elasticity of Demand for Peak Timing**.
*   *Trigger:* If more than 40% of your clients refuse the "Priority Surcharge" and reschedule to off-peak times, your fleet utilization during harvest will drop below breakeven.
*   *Rule of Thumb:* Farmers pay for certainty during harvest. If the surcharge is less than the cost of crop loss due to delay, they will pay it.
*   *Action Step:* If you have signed contracts for next season, **consider** adding a "Regulatory Cost Pass-Through" clause capped at 20% for flights scheduled within 48 hours of harvest peak, rather than a hard surcharge, to test resistance.
