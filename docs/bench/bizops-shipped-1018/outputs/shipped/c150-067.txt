**The Rules and Risks:**
Marketplace liability for regulated goods generally hinges on whether the platform had "knowledge" of the violation and whether it took reasonable steps to prevent it. While general intermediary protections (like Section 230 in the US or the E-Commerce Directive in the EU) shield platforms from user content liability, these shields often contain explicit carve-outs for federal criminal law, intellectual property, and specifically regulated goods like controlled substances.

In the context of chemical precursors, regulators (such as the DEA in the US or equivalent bodies in the EU/UK) often impose an "affirmative duty" on entities handling these materials to ensure compliance with licensing. By allowing a listing to exist, even briefly, you may be construed as "distributing" or "facilitating distribution" without the required registration. The fact that your Terms of Service prohibit the act is a necessary baseline but rarely a complete legal shield if your operational practices (manual review of 1,500 transactions/year with only 4 moderators) are deemed negligent given the high-risk nature of the product.
