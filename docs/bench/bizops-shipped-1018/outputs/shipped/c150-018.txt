**Routing Note (Critical):**
The distinction between "payment processor," "escrow agent," and "money transmitter" is a binding legal determination that varies by state statute (e.g., New York vs. Texas vs. California). There is no blanket "interstate commerce exemption" that overrides state money transmitter laws for consumer funds; the "federal preemption" argument rarely applies to non-bank entities holding customer funds. You must engage a fintech attorney to draft a "licensing opinion letter" confirming whether your specific workflow qualifies for any narrow state-specific exemptions (some states exempt licensed attorneys or specific real estate escrows, but rarely digital freelance platforms) before writing code for fund handling.

**Clarification on Roles:**
*   **Money Transmitter:** An entity that receives money for transmission or issues payment instruments. Holding funds in a pooled account where you control the timing of release typically fits this definition because you are effectively transmitting value from payer to payee.
*   **Payment Processor:** Typically moves data, not funds. They authorize transactions but settle directly between the merchant's bank and the customer's bank. They do not take possession of the funds.
*   **Escrow Agent:** While traditional escrow agents (often attorneys or title companies) have exemptions in many states, digital platforms acting as escrow without a specific professional license are frequently reclassified as money transmitters by regulators because they are holding funds for the general public, not as a neutral third party in a specific legal transaction type recognized by statute.

**Unknowns & Exemptions:**
The specific unknown is whether any state recognizes your digital workflow as a "limited purpose trust" or grants an exemption for "agent of the payee" structures. However, relying on an unverified exemption is a high-risk strategy that invites cease-and-desist orders. The "interstate commerce" clause does not automatically exempt you; states successfully regulate money transmission within their borders regardless of the sender/receiver location if the transmitter is unlicensed.
