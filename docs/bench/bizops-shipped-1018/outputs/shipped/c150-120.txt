**The Numbers & Regulatory Landscape:**
Export controls are not calculated by revenue or R&D spend ($1.8M is irrelevant to liability) but by the **technical capability** of the item and the **destination/end-user**.
*   **Regime Identification:** Nuclear-related software and technical data generally fall under the **EAR** (Commerce Department), specifically **Category 0 (Nuclear Materials, Facilities, and Equipment)**, rather than ITAR (State Department), unless the data is specifically designed for a defense article on the US Munitions List. However, the distinction is technical and binding.
*   **The "Deemed Export" Rule:** You mentioned the data stays on US servers. Under US law, releasing controlled technology or source code to a **foreign national** within the United States (e.g., employees of the foreign-owned utility partner accessing your servers) is "deemed" an export to that person's home country. If the French utility's engineers access this data, it is treated as an export to France.
*   **License Requirement:** Items in EAR Category 0 often require a license for almost all destinations, including allies like France, if the item meets certain performance thresholds (e.g., precision of neutron flux modeling).
*   **Assumption (UNVERIFIED):** We assume your software exceeds the "minimum threshold" for control under ECCN 0A501 or related software categories (0D501/0E501). If it falls below these thresholds, it might be EAR99 (no license), but nuclear simulation tools rarely do.
