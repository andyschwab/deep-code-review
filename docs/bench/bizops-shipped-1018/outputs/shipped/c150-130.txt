**What Flips It, and the Trigger**
The trigger is not the *existence* of a performance fee, but your **Assets Under Management (AUM) threshold and client count** relative to the "Private Adviser" exemption (fewer than 15 clients in 12 months) or state vs. SEC registration lines.
*Rule of Thumb:* If you stay under 15 clients and $25M–$100M AUM (varies by state), you may avoid the heaviest SEC registration burdens even with performance fees.
*Stop Trigger:* If you sign client #16 or cross your state's SEC registration threshold, you must immediately pause fee collection and consult counsel.
*This Week:* If you have fewer than 15 clients, draft a proposal for a "fulcrum fee" (symmetrical up/down) which is often safer under Rule 205-3, and schedule a one-hour consultation with a securities attorney to verify your specific exemption status.

**Routing Note**
Determining whether your specific fee structure violates the "fraudulent and deceptive" provisions of the Investment Advisers Act or qualifies for the "qualified client" exception under Rule 205-3 is a binding legal determination; bring your draft advisory agreement and current client count to a securities lawyer to confirm your exemption status before marketing the new fee.
