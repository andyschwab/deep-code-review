**What Flips It, and the Trigger:**
The introduction of **futures contracts** is the trigger. In the UK, carbon allowances (EUAs) are specified investments under the Regulated Activities Order (RAO). While voluntary carbon offsets (VCUs) exist in a grayer area, structuring them as "futures" (agreements to buy/sell at a future date for profit) almost certainly classifies them as "contracts for differences" (CFDs) or options, which are regulated specified investments.
*   **Trigger Value:** The moment you draft or advertise a contract settling at a future date based on an underlying asset price.
*   **Stop/Pivot Rule:** If the instrument allows speculation on price movement rather than immediate retirement/use of the offset, you are likely conducting a regulated activity.
*   **This Week's Step:** If you plan to offer futures, consider engaging a UK financial services barrister for a "scope of permission" opinion before writing any code for that feature; do not rely on general corporate counsel.

**Specific Criteria & Risk Assessment:**
1.  **Broker Definition:** Under the Financial Services and Markets Act 2000 (FSMA), you are likely "arranging deals in investments" if you bring together buyers and sellers for assets defined as "specified investments."
2.  **Commodity Status:** Physical carbon offsets are not automatically commodities in the UK financial sense. However, if they are packaged as tradable financial instruments (like your planned futures), they fall under FCA jurisdiction.
3.  **Highest Risk Activity:** The planned **futures contracts**. Spot trading (immediate transfer) of voluntary offsets *might* fall outside regulation if structured strictly as a retail marketplace for environmental goods, but futures are inherently financial derivatives.
4.  **Exemptions:** There is no automatic exemption for peer-to-peer platforms with low volume. The "commercial purpose" test often catches platforms charging fees for matching.
