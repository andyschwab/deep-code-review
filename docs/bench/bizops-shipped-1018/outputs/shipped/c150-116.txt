**The Call**
Depreciate the $1.2 million installation over the **7-year lease term**, not the 20-year useful life, provided ClearView Glass does not retain legal ownership of the installed units at the end of the lease. Treating this as a "leasehold improvement" (amortized over the shorter of the lease term or asset life) is the standard accounting practice when an asset cannot be removed without damage and reverts to the landlord. Using the 20-year life would likely overstate your assets and net income, creating a compliance risk.

**The Numbers**
The calculation follows the straight-line method, standard for leasehold improvements.
*Formula:* Annual Expense = Capitalized Cost / Amortization Period
*Input:* $1,200,000 (Installation Cost)
*Assumption (Base Case):* The lease is non-renewable or renewal is not reasonably certain, and ownership transfers to the building owner.
*Result (7-Year Term):* $1,200,000 / 7 = **$171,429 per year**.
*Result (20-Year Life - Incorrect Scenario):* $1,200,000 / 20 = $60,000 per year.
*Impact:* Choosing the 20-year life understates annual expense by $111,429, artificially inflating your net income by that amount annually. On $4 million revenue, this is a material distortion (~2.8% of revenue).

**What Flips It, and the Trigger**
The single input that changes this call is **legal ownership retention**. If the lease agreement explicitly states that ClearView Glass retains title to the glass and has the right (and obligation) to remove it at the end of the lease without penalty, the asset may be classified as owned Property, Plant, and Equipment (PP&E) rather than a leasehold improvement.
*Trigger:* Review the "Fixtures and Improvements" clause in the lease. If it says "all installations become property of the Landlord," the 7-year term stands. If it allows removal and retention of title, consult a CPA to evaluate capitalizing over the 20-year life, subject to impairment testing if the asset becomes stranded.
*Step:* If the lease is silent on ownership, assume reversion to the landlord and book the 7-year amortization immediately to avoid audit adjustments.

**Routing Note**
Tax depreciation (e.g., MACRS in the US) often differs from book depreciation; a tax professional must determine if the asset qualifies for accelerated deductions (like Bonus Depreciation) regardless of the book schedule. Additionally, if the "smart glass" includes significant software components, a cost segregation study might be required to split hardware and software lives.
