**The Call**
Treat the initial three-month window as your primary unit economics baseline; do not bank on re-subscription cycles two years out to justify current losses. With an LTV of $147 and a CAC of $65, your unit economics are actually positive ($82 contribution per user), but your total burn suggests your fixed costs (team + infrastructure) are too high for your current volume. The immediate priority is lowering acquisition costs or increasing volume to cover the $18,000 fixed burn, rather than raising prices which could further compress your short usage window.

**The Numbers**
*Formula:* $LTV = ARPU \times Gross Margin \% \times Average Customer Lifespan$
*Formula:* $Contribution = LTV - CAC$

Using your provided figures:
*   **ARPU (Average Revenue Per User):** $49/month
*   **Lifespan:** 3 months (Base Case)
*   **Gross Margin Assumption:** 90% (ASSUMED: Server/API costs are $4k for 1,200 users = $3.33/user. $49 - $3.33 = ~93% margin. We will use a conservative 90% to account for payment processing fees).

*Scenario A: One-time user (Base Case)*
*   Gross LTV: $49 \times 3 = $147
*   Net LTV (90% margin): $132.30
*   **Contribution Margin:** $132.30 - $65 (CAC) = **+$67.30 per user**

*Scenario B: Re-subscription once after 24 months (Optimistic)*
*   Assumption: 20% of users return for another 3-month cycle (ASSUMED).
*   Additional Net LTV: $132.30 \times 0.20 = $26.46
*   **Total Contribution:** $67.30 + $26.46 = **+$93.76 per user**

*Break-Even Analysis:*
*   Monthly Fixed Costs: $18,000
*   Contribution per User (Base): ~$67
*   **Users needed to break even:** $18,000 / $67 ≈ **269 new users/month**
*   Current Acquisition: If you are maintaining 1,200 users with a 3-month life, you are churning 400 users/month. You must acquire 400 just to stay flat, costing $26,000 in CAC alone, which exceeds your total burn. This indicates a cash flow gap unless you have existing capital.

**What Flips It, and the Trigger**
The single input that changes the call is **Churn Rate vs. Acquisition Volume**. Your model flips from "sustainable growth" to "cash burn crisis" if you cannot acquire 400+ users monthly to replace churn while keeping CAC under $65.
*Trigger:* If next month's new signups drop below 350 while churn remains at 33% (3-month life), pause hiring and shift budget from brand building to performance marketing to lower CAC.
*Rule of Thumb:* In short-lifecycle B2B, LTV:CAC > 3:1 is ideal, but >1.5:1 is survivable if velocity is high. You are currently at ~2:1 ($132/$65), which is acceptable, but your *volume* is the bottleneck.
*Step this week:* If your conversion rate on LinkedIn ads is below 2%, consider testing a lower-friction offer (e.g., a one-time $99 "Resume Refresh" package) to capture users unwilling to commit to a subscription, effectively lowering blended CAC.
