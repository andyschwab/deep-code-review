**The Call:** Do not migrate your servers to Germany or France; your current Ireland-based architecture is likely compliant for EU-wide operations, provided you have executed the correct intra-EU data transfer mechanisms and security controls. The deal blocker is not server location, but the absence of a verified Article 28 (processor) agreement and a completed Data Protection Impact Assessment (DPIA) specific to access logs.

**The Numbers & Rules:**
Under the GDPR, there is no general "data residency" mandate requiring personal data of German or French citizens to be stored physically within those specific borders.
*   **The Rule:** Article 44–49 of the GDPR governs transfers. Since Ireland, Germany, and France are all EU Member States, data flowing between them is considered a domestic transfer within the European Economic Area (EEA). No "cross-border" adequacy decisions or Standard Contractual Clauses (SCCs) are needed for the Ireland-Germany/France flow.
*   **The Constraint:** The data must remain within the EEA. If your Irish servers replicate data to the US or UK without safeguards, you violate the *Schrems II* ruling.
*   **The Risk Factor:** Access logs (entry times, duration) constitute personal data. Because this involves "systematic monitoring" of individuals in a private space (rental units), it often triggers a mandatory DPIA under Article 35.
*   **Assumption:** You are not exporting this data outside the EEA for processing. If you are, the risk profile shifts to Tier 3 immediately.

**What Flips It, and The Trigger:**
The single input that changes this recommendation from "proceed" to "halt" is **sub-processing outside the EEA**.
*   **Trigger Value:** If any subcontractor (e.g., cloud analytics, backup services) accesses or processes these logs from a server located outside the EEA (including the US, even via AWS/Azure/Google default regions), you must implement Transfer Impact Assessments (TIAs) and supplementary measures.
*   **Decision Threshold:** If the hotel chain's contract requires proof of a completed DPIA or specific ISO 27001 controls regarding log integrity, you cannot sign until these are documented.
*   **This Week's Step:** If your cloud provider confirms all data replication is restricted to EU regions, consider drafting the Article 28 Data Processing Agreement (DPA) immediately to satisfy the hotel chain's "strict compliance assurance."

**Routing Note:**
While the general rule permits Irish storage, the determination of whether your specific access logs trigger a mandatory DPIA (due to "large scale systematic monitoring") and the final review of the hotel chain's indemnity clauses are binding legal determinations. Engage an EU-qualified data privacy counsel to validate the DPIA necessity and sign off on the DPA before executing the hotel contract.
