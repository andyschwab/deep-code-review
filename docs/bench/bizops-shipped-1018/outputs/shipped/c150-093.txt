**What Flips It, and the Trigger:**
The trigger is not a number; it is the **act of scaling to 1,000 users** without a legal opinion. In California, the Public Utilities Commission (CPUC) looks at whether you control the service and offer it to the public. Aggregating capacity and selling it as a single block often fits the definition of a "electric corporation."
*   **Trigger:** Signing up user #201 without confirming your exemption status (e.g., as a Demand Response Aggregator vs. a Utility).
*   **Rule of Thumb:** If you dispatch the energy yourself rather than letting the grid operator dispatch it via a third-party aggregator, your risk spikes.
*   **Step this week:** If you plan to onboard the next 50 users, consider pausing sales and engaging energy regulatory counsel to file for a specific exemption or confirm your status as a Demand Response Aggregator (DRA) rather than a utility.

**Potential Compliance Burdens & Structural Mitigation:**
If classified as a utility, you face:
1.  **Rate Regulation:** The CPUC sets your prices, likely crushing your 20% commission model.
2.  **Reporting:** Extensive financial and operational reporting (Form 1, etc.).
3.  **Service Obligations:** You may be forced to serve all customers in a territory, not just those who opt-in.

*Mitigation Ideas (for discussion with counsel):*
*   **Contract Structure:** Shift from "selling power" to "providing grid services software." Let a licensed third-party aggregator handle the actual energy sale and dispatch, while you take a SaaS fee.
*   **Pricing Model:** Ensure your $15 fee is clearly for software/access, not a bundled rate for electricity, to avoid "bundled service" triggers.
