**The Call:** Do not move your hosting to Europe immediately; your current US-based setup is likely viable if you implement Standard Contractual Clauses (SCCs) and conduct a Transfer Impact Assessment (TIA), but you must treat video recordings as high-risk personal data requiring encryption and strict access controls. The primary risk is not the server location itself, but the lack of documented safeguards and the potential conflict between US surveillance laws and EU privacy rights post-*Schrems II*.

**The Numbers & Regulatory Scope:**
You process 80 deeds/month × 12 months = 960 transactions annually. Assuming one video file per deed at ~500MB (estimated for 15-min HD video), your annual data volume is roughly 480 GB. While this volume is manageable, the *jurisdictional* exposure is binary: one German client triggers full GDPR applicability.
*   **GDPR Status:** Video recordings of notarizations contain biometric data (voice/face) and special category data (legal proceedings), classifying them as high-risk under Article 9.
*   **Transfer Mechanism:** Storing EU citizen data in Virginia constitutes a "third-country transfer." SCCs are the standard mechanism, but they are not a "set and forget" solution; they require a TIA to verify US law doesn't override them.
*   **Retention Conflict:** US state laws typically mandate notary journals/recordings for 5–10 years (e.g., California requires 10 years). GDPR mandates storage limitation (only as long as necessary). There is no single "international law" retention period; you must adopt the stricter standard for EU clients or segment data by jurisdiction.

**What Flips It, and The Trigger:**
The deal-breaker is a data subject access request (DSAR) or a supervisory authority audit revealing you transferred data without a completed TIA.
*   **Trigger Value:** If you cannot produce a signed SCC and a completed TIA for your US cloud provider within 30 days of an EU client inquiry, you are non-compliant.
*   **Pivot Rule:** If your TIA concludes that US intelligence laws (like FISA 702) pose an unmitigable risk to your specific data type (biometric video), *then* you must migrate hosting to an EU-based region or a sovereign cloud.
*   **This Week's Step:** If you have active German clients, immediately pause new data transfers until you execute SCCs with your Virginia-based host and draft a preliminary TIA documenting that data is encrypted at rest and in transit, with keys managed solely by you.

**Routing Note:**
The determination of whether your specific video content falls under "special category data" requiring heightened protection, the validity of your specific TIA under current EU case law, and the exact retention schedule conflict between specific US states and German law are binding legal determinations. You must engage EU-qualified privacy counsel to finalize the TIA and review your SCCs; do not rely on general templates for biometric data.
