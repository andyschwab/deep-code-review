**The Call:** Bundle mandatory security and safety updates into the base lease to prevent negligence claims, but explicitly exclude liability for feature obsolescence; do not make safety updates a paid add-on.

**The Numbers & Risk Exposure:**
You have 15 units deployed with 10 more coming (25 total). At $800/month, your recurring revenue is $20,000/month (projected).
*   **Current Exposure:** If one machine malfunctions due to unpatched software causing injury or property damage, a single negligence lawsuit could exceed your annual revenue from all 25 units ($240,000/year).
*   **Cost Assumption:** Software maintenance is typically 15–20% of hardware R&D annually. If your R&D burn is unknown, assume a fixed cost per unit (e.g., $50/unit/month) to keep systems safe.
*   **The Trade-off:** Charging separately for safety updates creates a "known defect" scenario if a client declines. This shifts liability squarely to you for failing to ensure safe operation of a robot in a public space.

**What Flips It & The Trigger:**
The deciding factor is the distinction between **safety/functionality** (mandatory) and **features/novelty** (optional).
*   **Trigger:** If a malfunction occurs due to a lack of update, the legal standard shifts from "breach of contract" to "negligence" if you knew the update was critical for safety.
*   **Rule of Thumb:** Safety updates must be automatic and non-negotiable in the lease. Feature updates can be tiered.
*   **Action This Week:** If you plan to scale to 25+ units, consider Y (drafting a "Maintenance and Updates" exhibit that separates "Critical Safety Patches" from "Feature Enhancements").

**Standard Practices & Contract Language:**
In Hardware-as-a-Service (HaaS) models involving robotics, the standard practice is to treat software maintenance as an inherent part of the hardware's "fitness for purpose."
1.  **Bundle Safety:** Leases typically mandate that the provider (you) maintains the system in a safe, working order. Making safety updates a paid extra is risky; if a client opts out and an accident happens, you are likely liable for deploying an unsafe device.
2.  **Limit Obsolescence:** You cannot guarantee a robot will never be "obsolete" in a fast-moving market. You can only guarantee it meets current safety standards and performs the core functions (brewing coffee) as specified.

**Suggested Contract Protections (General Concepts):**
*   **Definition of "Updates":** Explicitly define "Critical Updates" (security, safety, regulatory compliance) vs. "Feature Updates" (new drink recipes, UI changes). State that Critical Updates are included and mandatory; Feature Updates may be optional or billed separately.
*   **Limitation of Liability:** Include a clause stating that while you warrant the robot will perform according to current specifications, you are not liable for "technological obsolescence" or the lack of future features not present at the lease signing.
*   **Indemnification:** Require the building owner to indemnify you against claims arising from their failure to provide necessary power/network conditions required for updates, provided you have notified them of the requirement.
*   **Disclaimer of Consequential Damages:** Exclude liability for lost profits or business interruption of the building owner if a robot goes offline for updates or minor malfunctions, capping liability at the lease value (e.g., 3 months of lease fees).
