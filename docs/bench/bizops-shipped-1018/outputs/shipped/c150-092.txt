**The Call**
Depreciate the hardware over its 12-year mechanical life for both book and tax purposes; do not shorten the schedule to the 3-year software cycle unless the software is legally inseparable from the hardware and renders the unit non-compliant or non-functional without the update. Shortening the life to three years artificially depresses your asset base and inflates expenses, which hurts the "asset longevity" narrative your Series A investors require and likely mismatches IRS recovery periods for medical equipment.

**The Numbers**
*Formula:* Annual Depreciation = Cost Basis / Useful Life.
*User Inputs:* Cost = $120,000/unit; Units = 5; Total Basis = $600,000.
*Assumptions:* Straight-line method; zero salvage value (ASSUMED); software updates are expensed as R&D or maintenance rather than capitalised into the asset base (ASSUMED standard practice unless a major upgrade extends life).

| Scenario | Useful Life | Annual Expense (Total) | 5-Year Cumulative Expense | Book Value (Year 5) |
| :--- | :--- | :--- | :--- | :--- |
| **Hardware Life** | 12 Years | $50,000 | $250,000 | $350,000 |
| **Software Cycle** | 3 Years | $200,000 | $600,000* | $0 |

*\*Note: Under the 3-year model, the asset is fully depreciated by Year 3, resulting in $0 expense in Years 4 and 5.*

**What Flips It, and the Trigger**
The deciding input is whether the software update constitutes a "betterment" that must be capitalised or a "maintenance" item that is expensed immediately. If your legal counsel or tax advisor determines that the robot *cannot legally operate* without the specific software version (making the software integral to the hardware's function), you may need to capitalise update costs, but this still rarely shortens the underlying hardware life.
*Trigger:* If the IRS or your auditor classifies the robot as "medical diagnostic equipment" (typically 5-year MACRS) rather than general manufacturing machinery (7-year), your tax depreciation accelerates naturally without manually forcing a 3-year book life.
*Rule of Thumb:* Investors prefer higher book values ($350k vs $0) to show tangible asset backing.
*Next Step:* If your CPA confirms the hardware qualifies for 5-year MACRS tax depreciation, adopt that for tax filings but keep the 12-year straight-line for investor reporting to maximize shown asset value.
