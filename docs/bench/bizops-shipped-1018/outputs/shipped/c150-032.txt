**The Call**
Capitalize the $1.2M as work-in-progress inventory only if you have high confidence (greater than 50%) of future economic benefit via regulatory approval; otherwise, expense it immediately. Do not write it down to zero solely due to a one-year delay unless approval becomes improbable or the materials have expired.

**The Numbers**
*Accounting Treatment (IAS 2 / IAS 38 logic):*
Inventory is valued at the lower of cost or net realizable value (NRV).
*   **Cost:** $1,200,000 (given).
*   **NRV:** Estimated selling price less costs to complete and sell.
    *   *Scenario A (Approval likely):* NRV > Cost. **Action:** Carry at $1.2M.
    *   *Scenario B (Approval delayed 1 year, still likely):* NRV > Cost (assuming no spoilage). **Action:** Carry at $1.2M.
    *   *Scenario C (Approval unlikely or materials expire):* NRV ≈ $0. **Action:** Write down to $0 (expense the full $1.2M).

*Runway Check:*
*   Cash: $3,500,000
*   Burn Rate: $180,000/month
*   Runway = $3,500,000 / $180,000 = **19.4 months**.
*   *Assumption (ASSUMED):* Burn rate remains constant and excludes any one-time inventory write-down impacts on cash (write-downs are non-cash accounting entries).

**What Flips It, and the Trigger**
The single input that changes the call is the **probability of regulatory approval** combined with the **shelf-life of the biological materials**.
*   **Trigger:** If your internal clinical data or EMA feedback shifts the probability of approval below "probable" (typically >50% likelihood), or if the cold-storage materials reach their expiration date before use, you must impair (write down) the inventory immediately.
*   **Rule of Thumb:** If the delay causes the product to miss its market window or the materials to spoil, the value is zero.
*   **Next Step:** This week, obtain a written assessment from your Chief Medical Officer or Regulatory Lead regarding the likelihood of approval post-delay and confirm the expiration dates of the stored ink; if either is negative, prepare the journal entry to expense the $1.2M.
