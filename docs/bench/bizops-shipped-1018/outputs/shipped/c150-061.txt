**The Call**
Collect sales tax based on the **pickup location** (Alameda County), not the owner's garage (San Mateo County). You likely need to register for a single statewide seller's permit with the California Department of Tax and Fee Administration (CDTFA) rather than registering separately in every county, but you must remit the specific combined rate applicable to each pickup address.

**The Numbers & Logic**
California sales tax on vehicle rentals is generally sourced to the location where the vehicle is delivered to the renter.
*   **Rule:** Tax rate = State base rate + Local district rate (county/city/special districts) of the **pickup location**.
*   **Your Transaction:**
    *   Gross Booking: $120,000 (aggregate example).
    *   Service Fee (15%): $18,000 (this is your revenue; tax usually applies to the total rental charge paid by the consumer).
    *   **Tax Base:** The full amount charged to the renter for the use of the vehicle.
    *   **Application:** If a renter picks up in Alameda County (e.g., Oakland rate ~10.25%), you collect 10.25% even if the owner lives in San Mateo (e.g., ~9.625%).
*   **Assumption (UNVERIFIED):** That LuxRide is deemed the "retailer" responsible for collection. In peer-to-peer models, if the platform facilitates the transaction and processes payment, California often treats the platform as the retailer liable for tax collection.

**What Flips It, and the Trigger**
The one input that changes this logic is the **legal structure of your rental agreement**. If your terms legally structure the transaction as an owner leasing directly to a renter with LuxRide acting solely as an advertising agent (not processing funds or setting terms), the liability *might* shift to the individual owner.
*   **Trigger:** If your legal counsel confirms your current Terms of Service define LuxRide as a mere "introducer" rather than the facilitating retailer, the collection obligation could flip to the 450 individual owners (creating a compliance nightmare).
*   **Rule of Thumb:** Marketplaces that control pricing, booking, and payment are almost always the responsible retailer.
*   **Step this week:** If your platform controls the checkout flow, **consider** hardcoding the tax engine to pull the rate based on the `pickup_latitude/longitude` immediately, rather than the `owner_address`.

**Routing Note**
While the sourcing rule (pickup location) and statewide registration are standard, the determination of whether LuxRide is the "retailer" or if specific local district taxes apply to short-term vehicle rentals is a binding legal and tax determination. You must engage a California tax attorney or CPA to confirm your specific marketplace facilitator status and verify the exact combined rate tables for every potential pickup zone before updating production code.
