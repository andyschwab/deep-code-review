**The Call:** Do not sign the proposed rebate structure in its current form; the $5,000 quarterly cash rebate tied to readmission reductions creates a high risk of violating the federal Anti-Kickback Statute (AKS) because it looks like remuneration for patient referrals rather than a bona fide discount.

**The Numbers:**
Your current unit economics are strong ($300k ARR / 200 patients = $150 average revenue per patient, suggesting your $50 target may be for a specific module or new tier). However, the legal "cost" of a violation dwarfs these figures.
*   **Proposed Rebate:** $5,000/quarter = $20,000/year per hospital.
*   **Trigger:** 15% reduction in readmissions.
*   **Risk Exposure:** AKS violations are felonies. Penalties include up to $100,000 per violation, triple damages, and exclusion from Medicare/Medicaid programs.
*   **Assumption (UNVERIFIED):** That the hospitals you are targeting treat Medicare/Medicaid beneficiaries. If they do, federal law applies regardless of the B2B label.

**What Flips It, and The Trigger:**
The deal flips from "high risk" to "potential safe harbor" only if the remuneration is restructured as a **discount on the price of the goods/services themselves** rather than a cash payment back to the provider.
*   **The Trigger:** If the benefit is provided as a reduction in the monthly per-patient fee (e.g., lowering the $50 rate retroactively) rather than a separate $5,000 check, it may qualify for the "Discount Safe Harbor."
*   **Rule of Thumb:** Cash flowing *back* to a provider for performance is suspect; price reductions *on the invoice* for volume or value are more defensible.
*   **Step this week:** Pause the contract draft. If the rebate mechanism cannot be rewritten as an explicit price discount on the face of the invoice, consider dropping the monetary incentive entirely and replacing it with non-monetary support (e.g., dedicated success managers) that fits within the "Personal Services and Management Contracts" safe harbor criteria.
