**The Call:** Your per-document fee structure does not inherently trigger unauthorized practice of law (UPL) liability; the risk lies entirely in whether your AI provides specific legal advice or creates an attorney-client relationship, not in the billing mechanism itself. **What Flips It, and the Trigger:**
The single input that changes this from a low-risk "legal tech tool" to a high-risk "unauthorized law firm" is **the specificity of the AI's output**.
*   **Trigger Value:** If the AI tells a user *what clause to insert* or *how to interpret a specific liability cap* for their specific situation, you are likely practicing law. If it only highlights missing clauses based on a generic checklist without advising on legal strategy, you are likely providing legal information.
*   **Stop/Pivot Trigger:** If any customer support ticket or user feedback indicates a user relied on your tool to win/lose a legal dispute, **stop scaling immediately**.
*   **Rule of Thumb:** Charging per document is standard for SaaS (like charging per email sent); charging for the *outcome* of a legal strategy is the red flag.
