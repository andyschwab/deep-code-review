**The Call**
Keep your flat-fee model; it is generally permissible for law firms to charge flat fees for immigration matters provided the total fee is "reasonable" and the client consents, but you must immediately decouple your SaaS subscription fee from the attorney's legal fee to avoid the unauthorized practice of law (UPL) and fee-splitting violations. Do not switch to usage-based pricing solely for ethics reasons; instead, build safeguards that empower the *law firm* to determine reasonableness, rather than dictating the price yourself.

**The Numbers**
Your current risk is not mathematical but structural. However, looking at your unit economics:
*   **Current Model:** $2,000/case × 30 firms × (1 - 5% churn).
*   **The Variable:** Case complexity (Simple Renewal vs. Complex Asylum).
*   **Assumption (ASSUMED):** If a "Complex Asylum" case takes 20x the work of a "Simple Renewal" but generates the same $2,000 revenue for the firm, the firm's effective hourly rate may drop below their cost basis, triggering the "unreasonable fee" complaint.
*   **Result:** The flat fee is only ethical if the *law firm* has assessed that the $2,000 covers their costs plus a reasonable profit across their specific mix of cases. If your software forces a price that makes their representation unreasonable, they are liable, but your contract could be voided for facilitating UPL.

**What Flips It, and the Trigger**
The single input that flips this from compliant to non-compliant is **who sets the final price presented to the end client**.
*   **Trigger:** If your platform automatically bills the end-client $2,000 as a "legal fee" without attorney review, you are likely engaging in UPL and illegal fee-splitting.
*   **Rule of Thumb:** Legal ethics rules (ABA Model Rule 1.5) require fees to be reasonable based on time, labor, novelty, and difficulty. Flat fees are allowed, but they cannot be "non-refundable" if the work isn't done, and the lawyer must remain responsible for the fee's reasonableness.
*   **Actionable Step:** If your current contract states "CaseMate charges the client $2,000," consider revising it this week to: "CaseMate charges the Law Firm a $2,000 technology fee; the Law Firm sets the legal fee charged to the client."
