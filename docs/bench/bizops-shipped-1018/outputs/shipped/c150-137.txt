**The Call**
Do not launch the $500 standalone report feature yet; selling pre-formatted documents that interpret specific transaction data against regulations creates a high risk of being classified as the unauthorized practice of law (UPL) in both the UK and Singapore. You must consult a legal ethics specialist in both jurisdictions before defining the product's scope or pricing, as the distinction between a "data tool" and a "legal opinion" often hinges on whether the output tells the client *what* the data means legally versus simply displaying *what* the data is.

**The Numbers**
There is no arithmetic formula to compute legal liability, but the financial exposure can be framed as a risk-weighted decision:
*   **Potential Revenue:** 12 clients × 4 reports/year (estimated) × $500 = **$24,000/year** (illustrative).
*   **Risk Exposure:** Regulatory fines for UPL, cease-and-desist orders, and reputational damage capable of jeopardizing the existing **$500,000/year** recurring revenue.
*   **Assumption (UNVERIFIED):** That regulators will view a static, automated report differently than a lawyer's letter. In many jurisdictions, the *effect* on the consumer matters more than the *method* of generation.
*   **Result:** The potential upside ($24k) is negligible compared to the threat to the core business ($500k). The "cost" of getting this wrong is not a variable you can bracket; it is a binary failure mode.

**What Flips It, and the Trigger**
The single input that changes this call is the **specific wording of the report's conclusion**.
*   **Trigger:** If the report states, "Your transactions comply with Regulation X," you are likely offering a legal opinion. If it states, "Your transactions matched Pattern Y, which Regulation X defines as reportable," you are likely providing a data tool.
*   **Decision Threshold:** If the output requires any human judgment to interpret the regulation against the data before generation, or if the software encodes a legal interpretation rather than a factual mapping, stop.
*   **Rule of Thumb:** Tools show facts; lawyers apply law to facts. If your software does the applying, you are practicing law.
*   **Next Step:** This week, draft two versions of the report output (one purely factual, one interpretive) and take them to a qualified legal ethics attorney in the UK and Singapore for a "scope of practice" review before writing any code.
