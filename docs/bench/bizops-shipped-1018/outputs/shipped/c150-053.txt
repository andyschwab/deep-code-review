**The Call:** Halt the private sale immediately; under current facts, your token distribution is a high-risk unregistered securities offering that cannot be fixed solely by tweaking utility or schedules without a fundamental restructuring of the economic rights and distribution method.

**The Numbers & Risk Triggers:**
Your proposal involves selling 500,000 tokens at $0.50, raising $250,000, with a total fully diluted valuation implied at $500,000 (if only considering the sale) or higher based on the total supply. The specific aspects triggering SEC classification under the *Howey Test* are:
1.  **Investment of Money:** Backers are paying $0.50 cash.
2.  **Common Enterprise:** Funds ($750k raised + new $250k) are pooled to build the platform.
3.  **Expectation of Profit:** You explicitly stated holders expect "value appreciation based on platform adoption." This is the primary failure point.
4.  **Efforts of Others:** Value depends on your team of five developers and the founding team's work, not solely on user participation.

*Assumption (UNVERIFIED):* That the "utility" of voting/verifying is secondary to the investment thesis of price appreciation. If the token price is marketed or expected to rise, utility arguments rarely shield against security classification in a pre-launch sale.

**What Flips It & The Trigger:**
The single input that changes the call is the **removal of profit expectation and the decoupling of token value from company success**.
*   **Trigger:** If you cannot legally guarantee that buyers are purchasing *only* for immediate software access (consumptive use) with no reasonable expectation of resale profit, the sale is a securities violation.
*   **Pivot:** Altering the distribution schedule (vesting) does not mitigate the *existence* of a security; it only changes the terms of the violation. Changing the mechanism to a "free airdrop for verified users" (no money invested) or a "paid subscription for non-transferable access credits" (no resale market) might remove the *Howey* elements, but this fundamentally changes your business model from a token sale to a SaaS or grant model.

**Routing Note (Critical):**
Your Delaware counsel's concern is a binding legal determination (Tier 3). You must engage a securities attorney specializing in digital assets before accepting a single dollar for these tokens. The specific question to bring is: "Can we structure a Reg D 506(c) or Reg S exemption for this specific tokenomics model, or must we abandon the sale entirely and pivot to a non-security utility model?" Do not proceed with the private round without a formal legal opinion letter; the penalties for unregistered sales include rescission rights (returning all money with interest) and federal fines.
