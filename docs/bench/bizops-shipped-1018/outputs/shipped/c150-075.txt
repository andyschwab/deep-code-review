**The Call**
Operate under the assumption that documents with minimal human editing are uncopyrightable in the US and likely Canada, exposing your customers to public-domain risk; immediately revise your Terms of Service (ToS) to disclaim copyright guarantees and limit liability for patent-office rejections based on authorship disputes.

**The Numbers & Legal Reality**
There is no fixed mathematical "threshold" (e.g., 20% vs. 80%) that secures copyright. The legal standard is qualitative, not quantitative.
*   **US Regime:** The US Copyright Office and recent case law (e.g., *Thaler v. Perlmutter*) state that works generated primarily by AI without substantial human creative control are not copyrightable. If the user only provides prompts and light edits to an AI-generated draft, the "human authorship" element is likely insufficient. The result enters the public domain.
*   **Canada Regime:** Canada recognizes "computer-generated works" where the author is the person who made the arrangements necessary for the creation. However, this is untested for AI drafting assistants where the AI determines the expressive content. Reliance on this provision is high-risk without litigation precedent.
*   **Liability Exposure:** Your current ToS claiming "joint ownership" creates a false sense of security. If a customer relies on your guarantee, files a patent, and the application is rejected or the patent invalidated because the specification was deemed uncopyrightable (or the AI contribution was too high), you face breach-of-contract claims.

**What Flips It, and The Trigger**
The deciding factor is the **degree of creative control** the user exerts over the specific expression of the text, not the volume of words typed.
*   **Trigger:** If a user cannot demonstrate they selected, arranged, and modified the AI output with significant creative intent, the work is unprotected.
*   **Actionable Step:** This week, insert a prominent disclaimer in your ToS and UI: *"PatentEase does not guarantee copyright protection for AI-assisted drafts. Users are responsible for ensuring sufficient human authorship to meet US/Canada legal standards. We are not liable for patent office rejections related to AI authorship."*
