**The Call:** Do not pause sales immediately, but you must treat the translation feature as a potential "significant change" to your cleared device rather than general wellness software; proceed with launch only if you simultaneously engage regulatory counsel to validate that the new function does not alter the device's intended use or safety profile.

**The Numbers:**
Your current unit economics are strong, but regulatory risk threatens the entire revenue stream.

**What Flips It, and The Trigger:**
The deciding factor is whether the translation feature changes the device's **intended use** or introduces new risks to hearing health (e.g., latency causing audio distortion, battery drain affecting reliability).
*   **Trigger:** If the software processing alters audio output quality, delays critical alerts, or if marketing claims suggest the translation aids in medical diagnosis or safety (beyond communication), the "general wellness" exemption likely vanishes.
*   **Rule of Thumb:** Features that diagnose, treat, or mitigate a disease are medical; features that simply facilitate communication for quality of life are often wellness. However, combining them in a single cleared hardware unit creates a "hybrid" risk.
*   **This Week's Step:** If your marketing copy uses words like "diagnose," "treat," or implies medical efficacy for the translation, consider rewriting it immediately to strictly limit claims to "communication assistance" while counsel reviews the technical architecture.
