**The Call:** Treat the 10% success fee as a probable compliance violation for foundations with strict 10% administrative caps; do not wait for a legal ruling to pivot your pricing structure for those specific partners. Your current model leaves zero room for the non-profit's internal overhead, effectively guaranteeing a breach if the foundation counts your fee as administrative cost.

**The Numbers:**
*   **Formula:** Total Admin % = (Your Success Fee + Non-Profit Internal Overhead) / Total Grant Award.
*   **Your Figures:** Average Grant = $50,000; Your Success Fee = 10% ($5,000); Foundation Cap = 10%.
*   **The Conflict:** If the foundation counts your fee as administrative, you consume 100% of the allowable cap immediately.
    *   *Scenario A (Strict Cap):* Allowable Admin = $5,000. Your Fee = $5,000. Remaining for Non-Profit Internal Overhead = $0. **Result:** Violation if the non-profit has any internal costs (staff time, reporting, etc.).
    *   *Scenario B (Exception/Exclusion):* Your fee is classified as "direct program service" or "fundraising cost" rather than "administrative overhead." **Result:** Compliant, provided the foundation accepts this classification.
*   **Assumption (ASSUMED):** Non-profits invariably incur internal administrative costs (even 1-2% for staff time managing the grant).

**What Flips It, and The Trigger:**
The single input that changes the call is the **foundation's specific definition of "administrative overhead."**
*   **Trigger:** If a foundation's guidelines define overhead as "any third-party fees paid from grant proceeds," your model is broken for them.
*   **Rule of Thumb:** If the cap is hard (≤10%), a 10% external fee is unsustainable unless explicitly excluded.
*   **Step this week:** If a partner foundation cites a 10% cap, immediately propose reclassifying your 10% success fee as a "direct program expense" (grant writing service) in the budget narrative, or switch that specific client to a higher flat fee ($4,500) with 0% success fee to preserve their internal overhead buffer.

**Routing Note:**
Determining whether your specific fee structure legally constitutes "administrative overhead" under a specific foundation's bylaws or state nonprofit law is a binding legal interpretation. Consult counsel to draft a standard "budget justification" clause that argues your fee is a direct service cost, not general overhead, before signing new agreements with strict foundations.
