**The Call:** Operate strictly as a "decision support tool" where the physician retains final approval authority; do not assume billing agent responsibilities or guarantee coding accuracy until a healthcare attorney validates your specific workflow against federal anti-kickback and false claims statutes.

**The Liability Scenarios:**
Liability turns on the distinction between providing information and submitting claims.
1.  **Tool Model (Lower Risk):** Your software suggests codes, but the licensed physician reviews, modifies, and explicitly approves the final code before submission. The clinic submits the claim. Here, the physician is the "billier," and liability for upcoding generally rests with the provider if they negligently approve incorrect suggestions.
2.  **Billing Agent Model (High Risk):** If your contract implies you guarantee accuracy, if you automatically submit claims without human review, or if you share in the upside of higher reimbursements (e.g., % of collection), regulators may view you as a co-conspirator in False Claims Act violations.
3.  **Algorithmic Negligence:** If your AI is trained to systematically maximize reimbursement rather than clinical accuracy, and you know this, you could face liability for inducing false claims, regardless of physician approval.

**The Numbers (Risk Exposure):**
*   **Current Volume:** 50,000 claims/month × $0.15 = $7,500 monthly revenue.
*   **Potential Penalty Base:** If 1% of claims are upcoded by an average of $50/claim:
    *   Monthly Overpayment: 500 claims × $50 = $25,000.
    *   Statutory Penalty (3x): $75,000.
    *   Civil Fines (approx. $12k–$24k per claim under federal law): 500 × $12,000 = $6,000,000.
*   **Runway vs. Risk:** Your $150,000 cash reserve covers ~20 months of operations but would be obliterated by a single fine on just 10–15 fraudulent claims.

**What Flips It, and The Trigger:**
The trigger is **control and compensation structure**. If you move from a flat per-claim fee ($0.15) to a percentage-of-recovery model, or if you remove the mandatory physician "click-to-approve" step, you flip from a protected tool to a liable billing agent.
*   **Stop/Pivot Trigger:** Before signing any hospital network contract (which increases audit scrutiny), you must halt expansion if your Terms of Service do not explicitly disclaim liability for coding accuracy and mandate physician review.
*   **Rule of Thumb:** If the doctor can say "the software made me do it," you are liable. If the doctor says "I reviewed and accepted the suggestion," they are likely liable.
*   **This Week's Step:** If your current contract lacks a "Physician Review Mandatory" clause, consider pausing new onboarding and inserting an explicit indemnification clause stating the provider assumes all billing liability.
