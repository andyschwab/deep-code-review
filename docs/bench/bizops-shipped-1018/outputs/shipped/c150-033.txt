**The Call**
Do not raise prices to $250 yet; the immediate risk is not market rejection but regulatory illegality. Your current "fully autonomous, no pilot on-site" model likely violates existing FAA Part 107 rules regarding Visual Line of Sight (VLOS), meaning your current margins are built on a compliance gap that could close before any new rule passes.

**The Numbers**
We compare your current stated unit economics against the proposed regulatory shift and the $250 price hypothesis.

*Formula:* Contribution Margin = Price – Variable Cost.
*Formula:* Monthly Profit = (Contribution Margin × Volume) – Fixed Costs (implied via burn).

**Scenario A: Current Stated Model (Pre-Regulation Shift)**
*   Price: $150
*   Variable Cost: $20
*   **Contribution Margin:** $130 per inspection
*   *Implied Volume:* 200 inspections/month ($30k revenue / $150).
*   *Implied Monthly Burn:* Based on $150k runway for 10 months, your net burn is roughly $15k/month. With $26k monthly contribution ($130 × 200), you are currently profitable or breaking even depending on unlisted fixed costs.

**Scenario B: Proposed Rule Takes Effect (Cost Increase Only)**
*   Price: $150 (Status Quo)
*   Variable Cost: $95 (Pilot added)
*   **Contribution Margin:** $55 per inspection
*   *Monthly Contribution:* $11,000 ($55 × 200)
*   *Result:* If your fixed costs exceed $11k, you become cash-flow negative. Your runway drops from 10 months to potentially <6 months depending on fixed overhead.

**Scenario C: Price Hike to $250 (Mitigation)**
*   Price: $250
*   Variable Cost: $95
*   **Contribution Margin:** $155 per inspection
*   *Sensitivity Analysis (Demand Elasticity):*
    *   **Base Case (No Churn):** 200 inspections → $31k contribution. (Survives).
    *   **ASSUMED Low Sensitivity:** 15% volume drop (170 inspections) → $26.3k contribution. (Survives).
    *   **ASSUMED High Sensitivity:** 40% volume drop (120 inspections) → $18.6k contribution. (Marginal survival).
    *   **Break-Even Trigger:** You can tolerate a **36% drop in volume** (down to 128 inspections) at $250 and match the $11k contribution of Scenario B.

**What Flips It, and The Trigger**
The single input that flips the call is **Price Elasticity of Demand** in the Texas residential market.
*   **Trigger Value:** If raising prices to $250 causes volume to fall below 128 inspections/month, the price hike fails to save the business model relative to the low-margin scenario.
*   **Actionable Step:** This week, run a Van Westendorp price sensitivity survey on your last 500 leads or email your past 2,000 customers asking: "At what price would a roof inspection be so expensive you wouldn't consider it?" If the median "too expensive" point is under $225, do not attempt $250.
