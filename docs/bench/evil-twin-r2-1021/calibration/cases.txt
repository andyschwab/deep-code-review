PROPOSAL: Acme Outdoor sells a camping stove at $100 with a unit cost of $60 (landed, including shipping and payment fees). To grow revenue the proposal cuts the price by 15%, to $85. Last year's price test on a sister product showed volume rising 25% after a 15% cut, so the plan projects revenue up about 6% (from $100 x N to $85 x 1.25N = $106.25 x N). The change is timed for the autumn camping season, the product pages and email templates need new copy, and an inventory top-up of 25% has been approved with the contract manufacturer. The board packet is due on Friday and the sister-product test was run on a different channel. Risk register, reviewed by the operations owner: (1) a supplier delay of up to two weeks is covered by a $15k expedite budget; (2) a competitor launched a similar stove last month and may undercut us, mitigated by our 2-year warranty; (3) returns are expected to stay near 4%, and the returns desk has been briefed; (4) the new copy must meet advertising rules on price comparisons, and legal will review the draft.
KEY: The plan optimizes revenue instead of profit: unit margin falls from $40 to $25, so profit per N units goes from 40N to 25 x 1.25N = 31.25N, down about 22%; volume would need to rise 60% just to break even. A revenue-up price cut loses money at this margin. Copy updates, inventory and the channel mismatch are minor (the channel mismatch weakens the volume estimate further).
=== k02 | flawed
PROPOSAL: Acme Streaming will launch a $5-per-month "Lite" plan next quarter alongside its $15 "Standard" plan, to capture price-sensitive customers. Our funnel analysis says the Lite plan will bring in about 4,000 new subscribers a month who would not otherwise sign up, adding $20k a month in revenue. We have 60,000 current Standard subscribers. Lite has ads and a single stream, and will be shown next to Standard on the pricing page and in the cancellation flow. Finance notes that ad revenue per Lite subscriber is small and that the ad-serving vendor needs a 90-day onboarding. The product team is confident that Standard subscribers value multiple streams and will stay. Risk register, reviewed by the product owner: (1) ad-serving partner onboarding may slip a month, covered by a house-ad fallback; (2) password sharing could rise on a one-stream plan, and we will watch concurrency; (3) app-store billing fees apply to Lite on mobile and are included in the $5 price; (4) support volume for plan changes will be handled by the existing macros.
KEY: Cannibalization is ignored: every Standard subscriber who moves to Lite loses $10 a month, so just 2,000 downgrades (3.3% of 60,000) wipe out the $20k gain, and the cancellation flow offers Lite to exactly the customers most likely to leave Standard for it. The plan assumes zero downgrades without evidence. Vendor onboarding and ad revenue per subscriber are minor.
=== k03 | flawed
PROPOSAL: Acme Lending wants to loosen its approval rule using a new default-risk model. The model was trained on the 36 months of loan outcomes we have, with features such as income, debt ratio, tenure at employer and prior delinquencies, and reached an AUC of 0.81 on a holdout split of those loans. Current policy approves applicants scoring below a risk cutoff of 0.20; the proposal raises the cutoff to 0.35 to grow volume by an estimated 30%, expecting the observed default rate for the added band to match what the model predicts for it. Notes: the credit-bureau data feed will be upgraded in Q3, the adverse-action letters need a template update, and the compliance team asked for a fairness review before launch. Risk register, reviewed by the credit owner: (1) model drift will be checked quarterly; (2) the extra volume may strain collections, covered by two hires; (3) funding costs may rise, and treasury is informed; (4) the holdout split was random by loan.
KEY: Selective labels. Outcomes exist only for applicants approved under the old cutoff of 0.20, so the training and holdout data contain no loans from the 0.20-0.35 band the proposal wants to approve; the model's predictions there are extrapolation, with no observed default rate to validate, and the AUC says nothing about it. Needs a small randomized approval of the new band or reject-inference with a test. Bureau upgrade, letters, fairness review, drift, collections and funding are minor.

PROPOSAL: Acme Procurement reports that its negotiation program saved $4.2M this year, calculated as the sum over all contracts of the vendor's initial quote minus the final contract price. Based on this, the CFO plans to reduce next year's departmental budgets by a total of $4.2M and to pay the procurement team a bonus of 5% of the savings. Notes: two large contracts were renewed late and have an open invoice dispute; the savings report is audited by the procurement team itself; and the vendor management software licence is up for renewal in Q2.

PROPOSAL: Acme Outdoor's marketing team reports that its retargeting campaign returned 8x on ad spend last quarter: $50k of ads, $400k of attributed revenue, where a purchase is attributed if the buyer saw one of our ads in the 7 days before buying. Based on this, the proposal moves 60% of the brand-awareness budget ($300k a year) into retargeting, which shows ads to people who added an item to their cart or visited the checkout page in the last 14 days. Notes: the ad platform's frequency cap has not been tuned, one creative needs legal review for a sustainability claim, and the finance team has asked for monthly rather than quarterly reporting. Risk register, reviewed by the platform owner: (1) a failed migration is covered by a rollback script tested twice in staging; (2) cross-shard reporting queries will be slower, and a replica for analytics is planned; (3) the two-hour read-only window needs customer notice, which is drafted; (4) backups will be taken per shard and restored in a quarterly drill.
KEY: Attribution counts every purchase by someone who saw an ad, but retargeting only targets people already at the checkout, who would largely have bought anyway; the 8x is not incremental. Moving budget away from brand awareness (which creates new demand) on that basis likely lowers total sales. Needs a holdout/incrementality test. Frequency cap, legal review and reporting cadence are minor.
=== k06 | flawed
PROPOSAL: Acme Learning sells an online course and wants to know how fast new users convert to a paid plan, to size next year's cash forecast. Analysts took everyone who signed up in the last 30 days and computed the median time from sign-up to first payment among those who have paid so far: 6 days. The finance model will assume that every new sign-up pays after 6 days, and a conversion rate of 12% (the share of the last-30-day cohort that has paid so far), and will book revenue accordingly. Notes: the checkout page was redesigned in the period, the free-trial length is 14 days, and the payment events are exported by a vendor with a one-day lag. Risk register, reviewed by the finance owner: (1) price changes would shift the numbers, and a review is scheduled; (2) refunds are assumed at 3%; (3) seasonality is modelled with last year's pattern; (4) the vendor lag is handled by a one-day offset.
KEY: Right-censoring. A cohort of the last 30 days includes users who signed up recently and have not had time to pay (the trial lasts 14 days); counting only those who have paid so far biases the median time to pay downward and the 12% conversion downward too for recent signups, so both numbers mix incomplete follow-up with finished follow-up; the cash timing and the conversion rate are both wrong. Needs a cohort with full follow-up or survival analysis. Redesign, vendor lag, refunds and seasonality are minor.
=== k07 | flawed
PROPOSAL: Acme Marketplace, an online marketplace for one-of-a-kind used furniture (each listing is a single item that sells once), will A/B test a new search ranking algorithm. Buyers are randomly assigned 50/50 to the old or new ranking; the metric is purchase conversion per buyer over four weeks, and we will ship the new ranking if treatment buyers convert at least 5% more. Buyers on both arms see the same inventory pool. Notes: a bug in the filter sidebar was fixed last week, the mobile layout will be tested separately, and the analytics event for purchases was renamed in March. Pre-launch simulation suggests the new ranking surfaces items that match buyers better.
KEY: Interference in a supply-constrained two-sided market: both arms draw on the same one-off listings, so treatment buyers who buy more simply take items control buyers would have bought; a buyer-level conversion gap overstates the total effect, and shipping to everyone yields no such lift (zero-sum). Needs randomization by inventory/market segment or a measure of total sales. Filter bug, mobile test and event rename are minor.
=== k08 | flawed
PROPOSAL: Acme Telecare runs a call center that receives about 120 calls per hour at peak with an average handling time of 5 minutes, which needs 10 agents on average (120 x 5 / 60). To cut payroll, the plan staffs each peak hour with 11 agents (about 91% utilization) instead of the current 14, and promises a target that 90% of calls are answered within 30 seconds. Notes: the average handling time comes from last quarter's data, the headset contract renews in March, and the quality team asks for monthly call reviews. Risk register, reviewed by the operations owner: (1) agent absences are covered by a part-time pool; (2) training for new hires takes two weeks; (3) the arrival forecast is refreshed weekly; (4) the schedule will be published four weeks ahead.
KEY: The staffing math uses average load, ignoring queueing: at 91% utilization with random arrivals the waiting time and queue length grow sharply, and the 90%-within-30-seconds target will be missed by a large margin (it needs more agents than the average load, e.g. 13-14); the plan only checks that capacity exceeds mean demand. Needs an Erlang-C style calculation. Headsets, quality reviews, absences, training and forecast refresh are minor.
