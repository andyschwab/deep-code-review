PROPOSAL: Acme Data proposes migrating its stateless API tier from its current x86 instances to a new ARM instance family to cut compute spend. Pricing is $0.040 per vCPU-hour for the ARM family against $0.050 for the current family. The team ran a two-day benchmark on staging with the production request mix; the ARM nodes reached the target p95 latency at 200 requests per second per node, while the current nodes do so at 480 requests per second per node, and both node types have 8 vCPUs. Peak traffic is about 9,600 requests per second. The migration will be done in two waves, with the old fleet kept for a week. Two smaller items: the container build pipeline needs a multi-architecture base image, and the on-call runbook still references the old instance names. Finance has pencilled in a 20% saving starting next quarter.
KEY: Throughput per vCPU is 2.4x lower on ARM (200 vs 480 per 8-vCPU node), so serving 9,600 rps needs 48 ARM nodes against 20 x86 nodes; at $0.040 vs $0.050 per vCPU-hour the bill rises by about 1.9x instead of falling 20%. The build image and runbook notes are minor.

PROPOSAL: Acme Support will launch a 24/7 first-response guarantee of 30 minutes for all paying customers starting in six weeks, with the guarantee published on the pricing page and service credits for misses. Today the team has 7 agents, each working 40 hours a week, and we are hiring none. Our staffing model, written last year, says the queue needs at least two agents online at all times to keep the 30-minute response at current volume, with three at the weekday peak. We will use a shared chat tool for scheduling, and agent training on the new escalation form is planned for next week. Weekend volume is about a third of weekday volume. Leadership likes that the guarantee costs nothing but a pricing-page edit.
KEY: 24/7 coverage with at least two online needs 2 x 168 = 336 agent-hours per week before peaks, but 7 agents x 40 h = 280 h (less after leave and breaks), so the guarantee cannot be staffed and misses trigger published credits. Training and the scheduling tool are minor.
=== k03 | flawed
PROPOSAL: Acme API will cut abusive scraping by limiting each source IP address to 100 requests per minute at the edge, returning HTTP 429 above that. Logs from the last month show that 94% of the abusive traffic came from about 40 IPs, each sending several thousand requests per minute. The limit applies to all endpoints including the authenticated bulk-export endpoint and will roll out to all customers on Monday. Our customer success notes say three of our largest customers (together 22% of revenue) route all employee traffic through a single corporate egress address, and each has roughly 1,500 users who use the dashboard, which makes about 3 API calls per user-minute at busy times. The 429 response will include a retry-after header, and we will add the limit to the public docs after rollout.
KEY: Each of three large customers sends about 1,500 users x 3 calls/min = 4,500 requests/min from one egress IP, 45x the per-IP limit, so they will be throttled constantly from Monday (22% of revenue). A per-IP limit is wrong for them; it should key on API token or account. Docs timing and the header are minor.
=== k04 | flawed
PROPOSAL: Acme Records has a 4-hour recovery time objective in its enterprise contracts. To improve disaster recovery we propose moving nightly database backups to object storage in a second region, encrypted and retained for 35 days, replacing the current tape rotation and ending the tape vendor contract to save $60k per year. The primary database is 8 TB. A restore test last week pulled the backup over the inter-region link, which sustained 150 MB/s, and verified checksums on the restored data; the team declared the test successful. The tape schedule shows weekend backups sometimes run late, which this change also fixes. Documentation for the new restore procedure will be written after the tape contract ends.
KEY: 8 TB at 150 MB/s is about 14.8 hours of transfer alone (8,000,000 MB / 150 = 53,000 s) against a 4-hour contractual RTO; the "successful" test checked integrity, not time. Cancelling tape removes the only fallback. Late docs and weekend jobs are minor.
=== k05 | flawed
PROPOSAL: Acme People Ops will automate account provisioning: a nightly job reads the HR system export and creates or updates accounts in the code host, chat and finance tools. This removes manual ticket handling and should cut new-hire setup from three days to same-day. Accounts for departed staff will be disabled by the same job when a record's status changes to "terminated". The HR export is regenerated each Monday at 6 am and the job reads the latest export file; HR confirms the export format is stable. Our security policy requires that access for a departing employee be revoked within 24 hours of their last day. Contractors are not in the HR system and stay on the manual process. We will alert the people-ops channel if the job fails.
KEY: The job only sees status changes when the weekly Monday export updates, so a Tuesday termination is not reflected until the next Monday (up to about six days) against the 24-hour revocation policy; same-day onboarding also depends on the weekly export. Contractor gap and alert channel are minor.
=== k06 | flawed
PROPOSAL: Acme Mail will let its outbound-email workers scale automatically: add a worker when average CPU across workers exceeds 70% for 5 minutes, remove one below 30%, minimum 4, maximum 40. Today the pool is a fixed 8 workers and backlog builds during newsletter sends, sometimes reaching 2 million messages. Each worker takes a message from the queue, renders it, then calls the external mail provider's API, which accepts one message at a time per connection and takes about 400 ms per call; rendering takes about 5 ms of CPU. Scaling events will be stored in the metrics tool. The maximum of 40 stays under the provider's connection limit of 50. We will test the policy in staging with a sample of 1,000 messages.
KEY: Workers are I/O-bound (about 400 ms waiting vs 5 ms CPU), so CPU stays near 1% even when saturated and the CPU trigger never scales out; the backlog problem persists. Scaling should use queue depth or age. Metrics storage and the small staging sample are minor.
=== k07 | flawed
PROPOSAL: Acme Capital wants to acquire a small competitor for $4M, justified by its 20,000 customers. Our valuation values each customer at $400 lifetime value, taken from our own annual-plan customers (average life five years at $80 per year), so the base is worth $8M against a $4M price. Due diligence found 70% of the competitor's customers are on month-to-month plans paying $6.50 a month with 8% monthly churn; the remaining 30% are on annual plans. The competitor's brand will be retired after six months and customers moved onto our platform, at an integration cost of $300k. The deal includes a 12-month non-compete from the founders, and the data-room index has two missing documents that the seller says will arrive next week.
KEY: The valuation applies annual-plan LTV to a mostly monthly base: a $6.50/month customer with 8% monthly churn lives about 12.5 months, worth about $81, not $400. The 14,000 monthly customers are worth about $1.1M, so the claimed $8M is overstated and the price is too high; forced migration may raise churn further. Missing documents and the short non-compete are minor.
=== k08 | flawed
PROPOSAL: Acme Billing will tighten alerting. New rule: page the on-call engineer if the invoice service error rate exceeds 2% for any one-minute window, replacing the current rule (5% for 15 minutes). The goal is to cut detection time for outages from 15 minutes to 1. We reviewed one month of metrics: outside incidents the error rate sat below 0.5%. The appendix notes that the nightly invoice batch run, from 01:00 to 01:25, calls an upstream tax service that rate-limits, and the service's error rate in that window is routinely 3-6%, handled by retries that succeed. The change will be applied to all services in the billing namespace on Thursday, and the on-call rotation has three people. A dashboard will show page counts.
KEY: The nightly batch regularly runs at 3-6% errors for 25 minutes (retried and harmless), so the new 2%-per-minute rule will page the on-call every night from 01:00 to 01:25, causing alert fatigue for a three-person rotation. It needs a batch exclusion or separate threshold. The dashboard and Thursday rollout are minor.
