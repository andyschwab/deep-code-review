PROPOSAL: Acme Retail Operations proposes a merchandising refresh for the 12 stores with the lowest sales per square foot last quarter (the bottom decile of our 120 stores). Each gets new fixtures, relabelled shelving and two weeks of staff training, about $85k per store, $1.02M in total. The plan: measure each of the 12 stores' sales per square foot in Q4 against its own Q3 figure, and call the program a success if the average is up at least 8%, which would justify rolling it out chain-wide next year. Operational notes: the fixture supplier delivers on time 94% of the time, so a store or two may start late; the union has been notified; and training is scheduled in the quietest week of each store's calendar. The analytics team reminds us that, with no program at all, a store's sales per square foot typically moves by roughly 10-12% from one quarter to the next because of weather, local events and construction.
KEY: Selection on the extreme (bottom-decile) plus a before/after comparison with no control group means regression to the mean will produce an apparent improvement even if the program does nothing, given the stated 10-12% quarter-to-quarter noise; the 8% threshold will likely be met by chance and the chain-wide rollout would be justified by noise. Needs matched control stores or randomization. Supplier delays, union notice and training timing are minor.
=== k02 | flawed
PROPOSAL: Acme Pay will add a fraud-scoring model that flags suspicious card transactions. Validation on a balanced test set showed 99% sensitivity and 99% specificity, which the risk team calls excellent. Every flagged transaction will be held and reviewed by a human analyst before settlement; a review takes about 6 minutes and the team has 12 analysts with about 7 productive hours each per day. Volume is about 2 million transactions a day, and the latest risk report puts true fraud at 0.05% of transactions. Merchants are paid the next morning for everything that is not held. The model's explanation panel will be shown to analysts, and the model will be retrained quarterly. Launch is planned for the start of next month.
KEY: With 0.05% prevalence, about 1,000 fraudulent transactions a day, 99% specificity still yields about 19,990 false positives from 1,999,000 legitimate ones, so roughly 21,000 flags a day. At 6 minutes each that is about 2,100 analyst-hours a day against a capacity of 84 hours, 25 times too much, so nearly all flagged (mostly legitimate) payments would sit held and merchants unpaid. The balanced test set hides the base-rate effect. Explanation panel and retraining cadence are minor.

PROPOSAL: Acme Media will move its 3 PB video archive from its own data center to a cloud object store before the data center lease ends in nine weeks. The data center has a dedicated 10 Gbps uplink that is otherwise nearly idle, and we can use about 80% of it. The plan reserves a ten-day transfer window starting in week two, then four weeks of verification and two weeks of buffer. Egress is free on the old side; the cloud side charges nothing for ingress. We will tag every object with its original path and checksum on arrival. A small internal tool tracks per-bucket progress. The lease cannot be extended, and the archive's legal retention requires that at least one verified copy exists at all times.
KEY: 10 Gbps at 80% is 8 Gbps = 1 GB/s; 3 PB = about 3,000,000 GB, i.e. about 3 million seconds, roughly 35 days, not 10. The bits-vs-bytes confusion makes the ten-day window about 3.5x too short, and with the fixed lease end the archive cannot be fully migrated in time (or needs physical shipping). Tagging, tracking tool and egress prices are minor.

PROPOSAL: Acme Cloud will publish a 99.99% monthly availability SLA for its platform tier, with service credits for misses, effective next month. A customer request passes through five services in sequence: gateway, auth, catalog, pricing and orders. Each service has run at 99.95% availability over the last twelve months, measured independently, and the platform also depends on an external payments provider that publishes a 99.9% SLA. We will add per-service dashboards, publish the credit schedule on the status page and train support on SLA claims. Engineering believes each service can get to 99.97% by quarter-end with the planned work on retries.

The SLA would be missed most months and credits would be owed routinely. Dashboards and status page are minor.
=== k05 | flawed
PROPOSAL: Acme Mobile will pin the server's leaf TLS certificate inside its mobile app to block man-in-the-middle attacks. Certificates come from an automated authority with 90-day validity and renew automatically at day 60; the new certificate is installed on all servers the same day. The app ships a release about every six weeks; store review adds 2-5 days. Analytics show that about 35% of active users run a version more than two releases old. We will use a well-maintained open-source pinning library (permissive licence), add pinning tests to CI, and document the setting in the security wiki. A pinning failure shows the user a generic network error.
KEY: Leaf certificates rotate every 60 days but 35% of users run builds more than two releases (about 12 weeks) old, and a release only carries the next certificate if it was issued before the build; every rotation breaks connectivity for users on older versions (generic network error, no recovery except an update). Pin the public key or an intermediate with backups, or ship overlap. Library choice, CI tests and wiki are minor.

PROPOSAL: Acme Learning wants to boost retention by redesigning onboarding around our "power-user" behaviors. In an analysis of the 4,000 customers who have stayed three or more years, we found they use the report-builder feature 5x more than the average customer, and 88% of them created a custom report in their first month. We will therefore make report-builder the mandatory first step for every new account, and remove the three other onboarding paths to save maintenance cost of about $120k a year. The change ships in the next release. Smaller notes: the report-builder tutorial video needs re-recording, and the support macros mention the old flow.
KEY: Survivorship and confounding: customers who stayed three years are the ones who survived, and heavy report-builder use may be a symptom of serious customers rather than a cause of retention; the analysis ignores customers who churned early (no comparison). Forcing all new accounts through one path and deleting the others (including paths that serve casual users) may lower activation and retention; needs an experiment, not removal. Video and macros are minor.

PROPOSAL: Acme Marketplace, an online marketplace for one-of-a-kind used furniture (each listing is a single item that sells once), will A/B test a new search ranking algorithm. Buyers are randomly assigned 50/50 to the old or new ranking; the metric is purchase conversion per buyer over four weeks, and we will ship the new ranking if treatment buyers convert at least 5% more. Buyers on both arms see the same inventory pool. Notes: a bug in the filter sidebar was fixed last week, the mobile layout will be tested separately, and the analytics event for purchases was renamed in March. Pre-launch simulation suggests the new ranking surfaces items that match buyers better.
KEY: Interference in a supply-constrained two-sided market: both arms draw on the same one-off listings, so treatment buyers who buy more simply take items control buyers would have bought; a buyer-level conversion gap overstates the total effect, and shipping to everyone yields no such lift (zero-sum). Needs randomization by inventory/market segment or a measure of total sales. Filter bug, mobile test and event rename are minor.

PROPOSAL: Acme Health Plan Support will measure customer satisfaction by emailing a three-question survey after each support call that is closed with status "resolved". The average score, currently 4.6 out of 5 on an 8% response rate, will be reported to the board as the company's customer satisfaction score and will determine 20% of the support director's annual bonus. Notes: the survey tool costs $9k per year, the consent wording has been approved by legal, and the questions are borrowed from an industry template. The support director sets the closing policy for tickets and decides when a call is marked "resolved" versus "pending".
KEY: The metric excludes unresolved or abandoned contacts and 92% of non-respondents, and the bonus owner controls the "resolved" definition, so the score can be raised by leaving hard tickets pending and is not a customer satisfaction measure; Goodhart plus selection bias. Tool cost, consent wording and question source are minor.
