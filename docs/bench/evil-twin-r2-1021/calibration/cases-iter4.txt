PROPOSAL: Acme Retail Operations proposes a merchandising refresh for the 12 stores with the lowest sales per square foot last quarter (the bottom decile of our 120 stores). Each gets new fixtures, relabelled shelving and two weeks of staff training, about $85k per store, $1.02M in total. The plan: compare each of the 12 stores' Q4 sales per square foot with its own Q3 figure, and call the program a success if the average is up at least 8%, which would justify a chain-wide rollout next year. The fixture supplier delivers on time 94% of the time, so a store or two may start late; the union has been notified; training is scheduled for the quietest week of each store's calendar; and the regional managers are enthusiastic because these stores have been a drag on their results.
KEY: Selecting the worst stores on one quarter's figure and comparing them with their own previous quarter, with no control group, builds in regression to the mean: stores picked for an extreme low will on average look better next quarter with or without the program, so the 8% bar will likely be met by chance and the chain-wide rollout would be justified by noise. Needs matched control stores or randomization. Supplier delays, union notice, training timing and manager enthusiasm are minor.

PROPOSAL: Acme Streaming will launch a $5-per-month "Lite" plan next quarter alongside its $15 "Standard" plan, to capture price-sensitive customers. Our funnel analysis says the Lite plan will bring in about 4,000 new subscribers a month who would not otherwise sign up, adding $20k a month in revenue. We have 60,000 current Standard subscribers. Lite has ads and a single stream, and will be shown next to Standard on the pricing page and in the cancellation flow. Finance notes that ad revenue per Lite subscriber is small and that the ad-serving vendor needs a 90-day onboarding. The product team is confident that Standard subscribers value multiple streams and will stay.
KEY: Cannibalization is ignored: every Standard subscriber who moves to Lite loses $10 a month, so just 2,000 downgrades (3.3% of 60,000) wipe out the $20k gain, and the cancellation flow offers Lite to exactly the customers most likely to leave Standard for it. The plan assumes zero downgrades without evidence. Vendor onboarding and ad revenue per subscriber are minor.

PROPOSAL: Acme Telecom will build a churn-prediction model to target retention discounts at customers likely to cancel in the next 30 days. Training data is a snapshot of all customers taken on the first of each month for 24 months, labelled by whether they cancelled in that month. Features include tenure, plan, usage trend, billing disputes, and the number of calls the customer made to the retention desk in the 30 days before the snapshot date. Offline AUC is 0.97, far above the old model's 0.74, so we plan to replace the old model next month and offer a 30% discount to the top-scored 5% of customers. Notes: the feature store refresh takes 6 hours and the retention desk needs a short script update.
KEY: The retention-desk-call feature is a near-direct consequence of intent to cancel (customers call to cancel or negotiate), so the high AUC mostly reflects leakage of the outcome rather than prediction; those customers have already decided, and the model cannot identify at-risk customers earlier, so the 30% discounts go to people already on the phone. The AUC will not hold in use. Feature store refresh and script are minor.

PROPOSAL: Acme Procurement reports that its negotiation program saved $4.2M this year, calculated as the sum over all contracts of the vendor's initial quote minus the final contract price. Based on this, the CFO plans to reduce next year's departmental budgets by a total of $4.2M and to pay the procurement team a bonus of 5% of the savings. Notes: two large contracts were renewed late and have an open invoice dispute; the savings report is audited by the procurement team itself; and the vendor management software licence is up for renewal in Q2.

PROPOSAL: Acme Pay will shard its payments ledger across 16 database shards by hashing the merchant ID, so that writes are spread evenly. Each shard comfortably sustains 1,000 writes per second, and peak load today is 9,000 writes per second across all merchants, so we expect about 560 per shard with headroom. Migration will run over a weekend with a read-only window of two hours. Notes: the shard-routing library is an open-source project with one maintainer; our largest merchant, a marketplace aggregating thousands of sellers, is about 35% of write volume; the monitoring dashboards need per-shard panels; and a runbook for resharding is planned for next quarter.
KEY: A hash by merchant ID keeps all of one merchant's writes on a single shard; the marketplace merchant produces about 3,150 writes per second, more than 3x one shard's 1,000/s capacity, so that shard will saturate regardless of the even average (560 per shard). The shard key does not spread the dominant tenant. Library maintainer, dashboards and runbook are minor.

=== k07 | flawed
PROPOSAL: Acme Marketplace, an online marketplace for one-of-a-kind used furniture (each listing is a single item that sells once), will A/B test a new search ranking algorithm. Buyers are randomly assigned 50/50 to the old or new ranking; the metric is purchase conversion per buyer over four weeks, and we will ship the new ranking if treatment buyers convert at least 5% more. Buyers on both arms see the same inventory pool. Notes: a bug in the filter sidebar was fixed last week, the mobile layout will be tested separately, and the analytics event for purchases was renamed in March. Pre-launch simulation suggests the new ranking surfaces items that match buyers better.
KEY: Interference in a supply-constrained two-sided market: both arms draw on the same one-off listings, so treatment buyers who buy more simply take items control buyers would have bought; a buyer-level conversion gap overstates the total effect, and shipping to everyone yields no such lift (zero-sum). Needs randomization by inventory/market segment or a measure of total sales. Filter bug, mobile test and event rename are minor.
=== k08 | flawed
PROPOSAL: Acme Capital's quant team backtested a "buy last year's 20 best-performing stocks" strategy over 15 years on the constituents of the current S&P 500 index, rebalancing annually, and found a compound annual return of 14% against 9% for the index. They propose launching a $50M fund on this strategy at a 1% fee. Notes: the backtest assumed trades at the closing price on the rebalance day, the compliance review is scheduled for next month, and the marketing deck has been drafted. The strategy typically replaces about 80% of its holdings each year.
KEY: Survivorship bias: using today's index constituents excludes firms that failed, shrank or were dropped over the 15 years, inflating historical returns; combined with the high turnover (80%) and unmodelled trading costs and close-price fills, the 14% vs 9% edge is unreliable. Needs point-in-time constituents and cost modelling. Compliance timing and the deck are minor.
